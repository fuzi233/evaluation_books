I measure the bounds of our physical existence much as I measure the long, slow resting of a fermented grain mash—by observing the absolute limits of its expansion. In Flatland, the greatest length or breadth of a fully grown inhabitant spans no more than twelve of your inches, an absolute maximum mirroring the length of a traditional cedarwood proofing board, with eleven inches being the standard maturity. 

Within these strict dimensions, our society simmers in a complex, multi-generational broth. Our Women are Straight Lines, moving with the silent, whispering grace of a finely whetted carving knife, their presence heralded by the sharp, metallic scent of ozone. Our agrarian toilers, the Soldiers and Lowest Classes of Workmen, are Triangles with two equal sides—each about eleven inches long—and a third side, or base, so short it rarely exceeds half an inch. These traditional elders of the soil form at their vertices a formidable, penetrating angle used to harvest stubborn subterranean tubers. Indeed, when their bases are of the most degraded, uncultivated type—no wider than a bruised cardamom pod at an eighth of an inch—their vertices become so sharply honed they are nearly indistinguishable from Women. In our official lexicon, these laborers are termed Isosceles. 

Above them, our Middle Class consists of Equilateral, or Equal-Sided Triangles. The Professional Men and Gentlemen are Squares—the class to which I myself belong, laboring as a meticulous food historian to catalog our ancestral spice routes. Next are the Five-Sided Figures or Pentagons. Beyond us rise the Nobility, steeped in an over-refined, homogenized drone of a dialect, beginning with Six-Sided Hexagons and multiplying in facets until they achieve the title of Polygonal. Finally, when a figure’s sides become so numerous and minuscule that they blend into an unbroken, smooth periphery, he is elevated to the Circular or Priestly order. 

It is the grand Law of Nature, our biological terroir, that a male child shall possess one more side than his father. This generational leavening ensures that each lineage rises one step in the scale of development: the son of a Square inherits the complex, five-sided palate of a Pentagon; his son, the Hexagonal appreciation of aromatics, and so on. Yet, this cycle bypasses the Tradesmen, and holds no sway over the Workmen or Soldiers. The polysided Aristocracy, viewing sustenance solely as utilitarian fuel rather than an olfactory landscape of profound meaning, often claim these unequal-sided laborers barely deserve the title of human Figures. Thus, the son of an Isosceles remains an Isosceles.

And yet, within the cyclical rhythms of the agrarian hearth, hope persists. The elevation of an Isosceles lineage is a sweeping saga of culinary frugality and sensory discipline. After generations of diligent, skillful harvests and the careful maceration of bitter brassicas and preserved salt-pork, the most passionately attuned among the Artisan classes manifest a slight widening of their base and a shrinkage of their towering sides. Through tightly arranged intermarriages—overseen by the Priests—the offspring of these intellectual laborers slowly approximate the Equilateral ideal.

The birth of a genuine, certifiable Equilateral Triangle from Isosceles parents is a mythic occurrence that integrates seamlessly into the daily domestic routine, a cause for rejoicing that fills the air with the scent of wild garlic and sweet yeast for many furlongs around. (A Spaceland critic might ask why Nature’s own geometry does not serve as its own certificate, to which I reply that the subtle power dynamics of our dominant lexicons dictate that no Lady will marry an uncertified Triangle; irregular births inevitably sour the lineage, causing the third generation to relapse into the bitter, pointed vernacular of the Triangles.) Such an immaculate birth demands centuries of self-control, an unbroken chain of ancestral cooks tending the fires of the Isosceles intellect.

However, this triumph culminates in an immediate, devastating erosion of cultural memory. Following strict examination by the Sanitary and Social Board, the Regular infant is torn from his sorrowing parents and adopted by a childless Equilateral. The adoptive guardian is bound by solemn oath never to let the child return to his ancestral hearth. He is forbidden from ever tasting the richly spiced, earthen broths of his forebears or hearing their rhythmic, percussive native dialect. The upper classes fear that through unconscious imitation, the freshly developed organism might regress. In truth, this enforced linguistic death systematically bleaches the child’s mind, replacing the deeply nuanced, untranslatable subjective experience of the Isosceles soil with the sterile, official lexicon of the middle class.

The Aristocracy welcomes these rare emergences. For the Circles, who orchestrate the sonic environment to maintain control, the upward mobility of a single Isosceles acts as a pressure valve, a gleam of hope piercing the monotonous squalor of the lower classes while reinforcing an absolute barrier against revolution. Left entirely destitute of hope, the acute-angled rabble might have summoned the passionate fury to overwhelm the wisdom of the Circles with sheer, piercing numbers. 

But here we witness the deeply tragic Law of Compensation. In exact proportion as the working classes increase in knowledge, culinary refinement, and virtue, their acute angle—which makes them physically terrible and capable of puncturing the state’s defenses—widens, approximating the comparatively harmless angle of the Equilateral. As the most formidable soldiers wax in the mental ability needed to deploy their penetrating power, they physically wane in that very power of penetration. To the Aristocracy, this natural acoustic and physical dampening is divine proof of Flatland's constitution. The Circles weaponize this irrepressible hope to stifle sedition in its cradle.

When the acoustic ecology of our world is disrupted by the discordant cries of rebellion, Art aids Law and Order. The State Physicians apply artificial compression or expansion to the most intelligent rebel leaders, making them perfectly Regular and instantly absorbing them into the privileged, soft-spoken classes. A larger number of the disenfranchised, lured by the false promise of ennoblement, are ushered into State Hospitals. These institutions are terrifying chambers of acoustic confinement where the natural, resonant hum of the environment is replaced by the relentless, maddening industrial noise pollution of state-sanctioned machinery, destroying their psychological well-being as they are kept in honorable imprisonment for life. One or two of the most obstinate are simply executed.

The remaining rabble of the Isosceles are left leaderless. They are either effortlessly transfixed by a small, well-paid militia kept by the Chief Circle, or, more devastatingly, they are driven mad by the artificial, commercialized sonic dissonance broadcast by the Circular party. Skilfully fomented jealousies drown out their ancestral songs, stirring them into a chaotic mutual warfare where they perish upon one another's sharp angles. No fewer than one hundred and twenty such sweeping rebellions echo through the interwoven timelines of our annals, alongside two hundred and thirty-five minor outbreaks; and in the relentless, circular pacing of our history, every single one has ended exactly thus—with the bitter scent of spilled marrow, and a heavy, industrial silence falling over the fields.
