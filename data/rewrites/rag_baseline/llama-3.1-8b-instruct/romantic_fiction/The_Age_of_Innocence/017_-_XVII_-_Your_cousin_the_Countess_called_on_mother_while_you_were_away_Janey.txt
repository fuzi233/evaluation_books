The afternoon light filtering through the windows cast a faint glow on the polished surfaces of the Mingott mansion. In the drawing room, Newland Archer sat beside his mother, Mrs. Archer, and his sister, Janey, who had just returned from an afternoon visit with their cousin, Madame Olenska. As he listened to Janey's account of the encounter, Newland's gaze drifted to his mother's hands, folded primly in her lap. The delicate fingers, adorned with rings, seemed to embody the same fastidious reserve that governed her demeanor.

Janey's words trailed off, and an expectant silence fell over the room. Mrs. Archer's eyes, fixed on some point beyond her son's shoulder, betrayed a flicker of annoyance. "She came alone, early on Sunday afternoon," Janey continued, her voice measured, "and wore a black velvet polonaise with jet buttons, accompanied by a tiny green monkey muff. She said she wanted to know us because you'd been so good to her." Newland smiled, his eyes meeting his mother's, and replied, "Madame Olenska always takes that tone about her friends. She's very happy at being among her own people again."

Janey interjected, her eyes fixed intently on her brother's face, "Yes, so she told us. I must say she seems thankful to be here." Mrs. Archer's response was cautious, her lips pursed in a thin line. "She certainly lays herself out to please, even when she is calling on an old lady." The observation hung in the air, unchallenged, as the afternoon sun cast its warm glow over the room.

A few days later, Newland visited Mrs. Mingott, who received him with an unusual warmth. Her gratitude towards him was palpable, stemming from his role in persuading Madame Olenska to abandon her plans for a divorce. "Ah, so you kicked over the traces, did you? And I suppose Augusta and Welland pulled long faces, and behaved as if the end of the world had come?"
