As the door creaked open, Sally's pulse gradually returned to normal, and her heart, which had skipped a beat at the sound of Gerald's voice, resumed its steady rhythm. Though she had long anticipated this moment, she was unexpectedly unflustered. The events of the previous night still lingered in her mind, rendering her incapable of responding with the passion she had once felt when imagining their encounter.

"Gerald," she said calmly, her voice steady despite the weariness that weighed upon her.

Gerald stood swaying, his hand grasping the doorframe for support. His eyes, red-rimmed from drinking, gazed at her with a mixture of sadness and desperation. His collar was undone, and his shirt hung loosely on his frame, a stark contrast to the man she had once admired. The faint scent of whiskey wafted towards her, a potent reminder of the state he was in.

Sally regarded him with a detached curiosity, as if he were a stranger who had stumbled into her home. Her exhaustion had given way to an unnatural clarity, a fleeting respite from the turmoil that had ravaged her nerves.

"Hullo," Gerald slurred, his words slurring together in a pitiful attempt at coherence.

"What do you want?" Sally asked, her tone firm but devoid of emotion.

"I heard your voice, saw the door open," Gerald muttered, his words trailing off. "Thought I'd come in."

A fleeting memory flickered in the recesses of Sally's mind – a recollection of another man who had once worn a similar expression of pitiful longing. It took her a moment to place it: Reginald Cracknell, a man she had encountered before.

"I think you should go to bed, Gerald," she suggested, her voice unwavering. Nothing about him seemed to stir her now, neither his sorrowful gaze nor his self-pitying words.

Gerald's expression faltered, and a single tear rolled down his cheek. His words tumbled out in a jumbled mess, a litany of despair and regret. "I'm a failure, Sally. I see it now. I should have stuck to newspaper work. I'm good at that. I'll have to go back to it. No good trying to write plays anymore..."
