As I emerged from my chamber, Mrs. Bretton's prompt arrival prompted me to open my dresser drawers, revealing an assortment of dresses within. Her eyes scanned the contents before she spoke, "That will suffice," she declared, "though I think it would be wise to acquire a new one." With that, she departed, only to return shortly accompanied by a dressmaker, who set about measuring me. "I intend to follow my own inclinations and make my own decisions regarding this matter," Mrs. Bretton explained.

Two days passed, and upon my return home, I found a pink dress laid out before me. Its vibrant hue startled me, and I couldn't help but feel as if I were being asked to don the attire of a Chinese noblewoman. "This is not for me," I protested, feeling a sense of trepidation wash over me. Mrs. Bretton, however, remained resolute, declaring, "We'll see about that. You will wear it this very evening, mark my words."

I harbored doubts, convinced that no amount of persuasion could convince me to don such an outfit. The pink dress seemed foreign to me, and I felt uncertain about our compatibility. Mrs. Bretton proceeded to inform me that we would be attending a concert that evening, an event of great significance in the city's cultural calendar. The grand affair would take place in the main hall of the principal musical society, featuring the most accomplished students of the Conservatoire. Following the performances, a lottery would be held in aid of the poor, and the King, Queen, and Prince of Labassecour were scheduled to attend. Graham had sent us tickets, cautioning us to dress accordingly as a gesture of respect for the royal family, and advising us to arrive promptly by seven o'clock.

Upon being ushered upstairs, I found myself succumbing to Mrs. Bretton's gentle yet firm guidance, which left me unconsulted and unpersuaded. Despite my initial reservations, the pink dress was eventually donned, adorned with delicate black lace. As I gazed into the mirror, I couldn't help but feel a mix of trepidation and self-consciousness. My heart quickened as seven o'clock struck, and Dr. Bretton arrived. Mrs. Bretton and I descended to the drawing room, where I couldn't help but envy the elegance of her brown velvet gown as I walked in her shadow.
