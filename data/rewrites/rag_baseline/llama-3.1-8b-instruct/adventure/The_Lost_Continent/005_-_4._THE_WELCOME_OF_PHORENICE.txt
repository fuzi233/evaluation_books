The sea breeze carried the scent of salt and damp earth as I stood at the prow of the galley, my eyes fixed on the distant peak of the sacred mountain. Twenty years had passed since I last set foot on the shores of Atlantis, and yet the memory of its splendor remained etched in my mind like the intricate carvings on the temple walls. Our vessel sliced through the calm waters, propelled by the rhythmic beat of the oars, as the city rose from the sea like a mirage. I felt a thrill of excitement mixed with trepidation – what would be my welcome in this city I once called home?

As we drew closer, the pyramids and towers of the higher buildings came into view, their marble facades glinting in the morning light. The wind was against us, but our skilled rowers coaxed the galley forward, their movements a testament to their discipline and strength. The slaves, mostly savages from Europe, labored alongside them, their faces set in determined lines as they battled the elements. The air was thick with the smell of smoke and the acrid tang of erupting fire mountains, carried on the wind.

Our arrival was not unheralded, I realized. News of our coming had preceded us, borne by the swift couriers of Phorenice. Seven of them had successfully completed their perilous journey, carrying the tidings to the capital. I wondered how many of the others had fared, but the uncertainty only added to my sense of unease. As a former priest, I had been trained in stoicism, but the weight of my own destiny hung heavy on my shoulders. What lay ahead? Would I be taken prisoner, put on trial, or welcomed with open arms? The possibilities swirled in my mind like the eddies of the sea.

The city loomed larger now, its grandeur almost overwhelming. I felt a pang of nostalgia for the life I left behind, the people I had loved and lost. The temple where I had come of age, the pyramid where I had undergone initiation – these were memories I cherished, yet they now seemed distant and irretrievable. As we entered the harbor, the city's splendor was tempered by the knowledge that I was returning as a fugitive, a man with secrets to hide and a past to confront. The welcome that awaited me remained a mystery, shrouded in uncertainty like the mists that rose from the sea.
