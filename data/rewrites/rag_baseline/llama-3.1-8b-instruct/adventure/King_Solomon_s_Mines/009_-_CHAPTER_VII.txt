As the last wisps of sunlight surrendered to the encroaching darkness, Sir Henry's party emerged from the labyrinthine cavern, their weary faces etched with the weight of their discovery. The air outside was heavy with the scent of snow and damp earth, a morose perfume that clung to their numb skin like a shroud. "I'm returning," Sir Henry declared, his voice low and resolute.

Good's questioning gaze met his, a flicker of curiosity dancing in its depths. "Why?" he asked, his tone tinged with a hint of trepidation.

Sir Henry's eyes seemed to cloud, as if the shadows within the cave had seeped into his soul. "It has occurred to me that—what we've seen—may be my brother." The words hung in the air like a challenge, a gauntlet thrown down by the darkness.

The group fell silent, their minds reeling as they re-entered the cave to test the validity of Sir Henry's claim. The dim light within was a cruel mockery, a reminder of the blinding radiance they'd left behind. Their eyes, strained from staring into the snow, struggled to adjust to the semi-darkness, their pupils constricting in protest. Yet, as they grew accustomed to the gloom, they began to make out the figure of the dead man, a sentinel of stone and bone, frozen in perpetual vigil.

Sir Henry knelt beside the body, his face inches from the cadaver's pale features. A sigh of relief escaped his lips, a mixture of gratitude and despair. "Thank God, it's not my brother," he whispered, his words a gentle benediction.

I approached the body, my heart heavy with a mix of emotions. The deceased was a tall man, his aquiline features etched into his face like the lines of a worn leather saddle. His hair was grizzled, his moustache long and black, a relic of a bygone era. The skin was a sickly yellow, stretched taut over the skeletal frame like parchment on a wooden board. His clothing, except for a pair of tattered hose, had been stripped away, leaving him exposed to the elements. Around his neck, a yellow ivory crucifix hung, a symbol of faith and mortality.

"Who on earth could this be?" I wondered aloud, my voice a mere whisper.
