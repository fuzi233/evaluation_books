As I sat amidst the rustic beauty of the village, surrounded by the verdant hills that cradled the small church, I couldn't help but feel a sense of trepidation wash over me. The banns of marriage were to be read, marking the union of Margaret and Gerard, a young couple whose love shone brighter than the golden hues of the setting sun. The cure, a kind and benevolent soul, had graciously agreed to perform the ceremony at an expedited pace, much to the delight of the eager couple.

The morning after the second reading, Margaret and Gerard arrived at the church, their anxiety palpable as they clutched each other's hands in anticipation. But just as the proceedings were about to begin, a stranger stood up, his voice booming through the silent space, proclaiming that the banns would not proceed due to the couple's inexperience and lack of parental consent. The news sent shockwaves through the young lovers, leaving them frozen in despair.

As they stepped outside, seeking solace in each other's company, the stranger approached them, his demeanor softening, and revealed that he had ulterior motives for his actions. For a modest sum, he would undo his work and permit the marriage to proceed. Gerard, ever the optimist, hastily offered a golden angel, and the deal was struck. The cure, none the wiser, agreed to perform the ceremony the following day.

However, the man's intentions were far from pure. As he departed, he stumbled upon a local tavern, where he regaled the patrons with tales of his exploit, including a mention of the young couple's plans. Sybrandt, a notorious ne'er-do-well, overheard the conversation and hastened to inform his family. His brothers, black sheep of the family, were eager to sabotage the union, driven by their own selfish desires and avarice. They conspired to undermine the marriage, seeking to claim the inheritance that they believed was rightfully theirs.

Meanwhile, Ghysbrecht Van Swieten, the wise and perceptive burgomaster, observed the siblings' schemes with a discerning eye. Though he chose not to reveal his awareness, he cautioned them against sharing their plans with their mother, whose sentiments were still uncertain. With a hint of superiority, he dismissed them, aware that his own discretion was necessary to prevent the chaos that would ensue.
