As the worthy physician returned home, his housekeeper greeted him with a mixture of concern and familiarity. "A bad burn, sir?" she asked, her brow furrowed in sympathy. The physician nodded curtly, knowing full well that he'd rather not share the true nature of his distress. In the world of medicine, it was common to downplay one's own ailments, even as one tended to others with skill and precision. The housekeeper's remedy, passed down from her mother and grandmother, was a soothing balm that seemed to ease his discomfort, and he submitted to her ministrations with a grateful smile.

Meanwhile, the physician's mind was elsewhere. He had just concocted a plan to have the travelers – his friends, Denys and Gerard – imprisoned for owing him money. He made his way to the local magistrate's office, where he swore an affidavit attesting to their supposed debt and intentions to flee the township. But fate had other plans.

As the officers arrived at "The Silver Lion" to apprehend the travelers, they found themselves empty-handed. Denys and Gerard had vanished, leaving behind only whispers of their whereabouts. It wasn't until the pair stumbled upon the riverbank that the officers caught wind of their trail. The sun beat down on the water as they chased after the fleeing friends, their footsteps pounding the dusty path.

Denys, ever the quick thinker, had anticipated this moment. He'd whispered a few words to Gerard, and together they'd made their way to the river's edge. A small boat bobbed gently in the current, its owner, an elderly man, waiting to take them on a journey along the Rhine. The air was alive with the scent of blooming flowers and the sound of birdsong, and Gerard felt his spirits lift as they pushed off from the shore.

As they glided across the water, Denys asked Gerard if he felt any better. Gerard smiled weakly, his eyes closed against the warmth on his face. "The doctor may have done me good," he said, his voice barely above a whisper. Denys chuckled, shaking his head. "You'd never take his remedies, but knocking him down seems to have done the trick." Gerard's eyes snapped open, a hint of amusement dancing within.

"I know, I know," Denys continued, his tone light. "But tell me, my friend, what was it about the doctor's words that rankled? Was it truly his heresy, or was it something more?"
