As Carthoris, Xodar, Tars Tarkas, and I stood transfixed before the magnificent vessel that anchored our hopes, we watched a second, then a third, crest the hilltops and glide silently behind their sister ship. From the upper decks of the nearest craft, a squadron of single-seater scout flyers erupted, diving in long, screaming arcs toward the ground surrounding us. Within seconds, we were encircled by armed marines. An officer stepped forward to issue commands, but his gaze locked onto Carthoris. With a cry of startled joy, he surged forward, clasping the boy's shoulders.

"Carthoris, my Prince!" he shouted. "Kaor! Kaor! Hor Vastus greets the son of Dejah Thoris, Princess of Helium, and her husband, John Carter! Where have you wandered, O my Prince? All Helium has sunk into mourning. Calamities of unspeakable terror have befallen your grand-father's mighty realm since the fateful day you vanished from our sight."

"Grieve not, my worthy Hor Vastus," Carthoris cried, his voice ringing clear. "For I do not return alone to comfort my mother's broken heart and the spirits of my beloved people. I bring back, as well, the one whom all Barsoom held dear above all others—her greatest warrior, her savior—John Carter, Prince of Helium!"

Hor Vastus whirled in the direction Carthoris pointed. As his eyes fell upon me, the nobleman staggered as if struck by a physical blow, his composure shattering under the weight of shock.

"John Carter!" he gasped, a storm of turmoil washing over his features. "My Prince..." He trailed off, the question forming on his lips dying before it could escape. The loyal soldier, perceptive and cautious, understood the abyss he dared not probe. He would not be the agent to wrench from me the terrible truth of my resurrection from the bosom of Iss, the River of Mystery, nor my return from the shores of the Lost Sea of Korus and the Valley Dor.

"Ah, my Prince," he stammered, regaining his poise as if the interruption had never occurred, "your safe return is enough. Let the honor of Hor Vastus' sword be the first laid at your feet." With swift motion, the nobleman unbuckled his scabbard and cast his blade onto the dusty earth before me.

To understand the gravity of this gesture, one must know the customs and spirit of the Red Men. It was an absolute pledge, spoken without words: *My sword, my body, my life, and my soul are yours. Until death and beyond, I seek authority for every action solely from you. Whether you are right or wrong, your word is my only truth. Whoever lifts a hand against you answers to my steel.* It was the ultimate oath of fealty, usually reserved for a Jeddak of impeccable character who had earned the fervent love of his subjects. I had never known such a tribute bestowed upon a mortal of my station.

"There was but one response," I realized. I stooped, grasped the sword, and raised the hilt to my lips. Stepping to Hor Vastus, I fastened the weapon back onto his belt with my own hands.

"Hor Vastus," I said, laying a hand upon his shoulder, "you know the workings of your own heart best. Doubt not that I may require your blade, yet accept from John Carter, upon his sacred honor, the assurance that I shall never summon you to draw this steel except in the cause of Truth, Justice, and Righteousness."

"That I knew, my Prince," he replied, "even before I cast my beloved blade at your feet."

As we spoke, more flyers darted between the ground and the battleship. Presently, a larger transport, capable of carrying a dozen souls, lowered gently near us. As it settled, an officer leaped from the deck to the soil and advanced to Hor Vastus with a salute.

"Kantos Kan commands that this rescued party be brought immediately aboard the *Xavarian*," the officer announced.

Approaching the craft, I scanned the group for the members of my party and noted with a sinking heart that Thuvia was absent. Inquiry revealed that no one had seen her since Carthoris had sent her flying on a frantic *thoat* toward the hills, desperate to carry her away from danger. Instantly, Hor Vastus dispatched a dozen scouts in every direction to locate her. Surely, she could not have strayed far from our last sighting. While the search parties scattered, the rest of us climbed into the waiting transport, and moments later, we were aboard the *Xavarian*.

The first to greet me was Kantos Kan himself. Though he had risen to the pinnacle of the Helium Navy, to me he remained the same brave comrade who had endured the privations of a Warhoon dungeon, survived the horrors of the Great Games, and braved the treacherous streets of Zodanga alongside me in search of Dejah Thoris. Then I was a wanderer on a strange world, and he a simple padwar; today, he commanded Helium's aerial terrors, and I was a Prince of the House of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium.

Like Hor Vastus, Kantos Kan did not press me for details of my travels. Both men dreaded the truth and refused to extract a confession. Yet, they both understood that the revelation was inevitable. For now, satisfaction lay in knowing I had returned. He greeted Carthoris and Tars Tarkas with radiant delight, though he refrained from asking where they had been. His affection for the boy was overwhelming; he could scarcely restrain his hands from embracing the child.

"You do not know, John Carter," Kantos Kan told me, lowering his voice, "how deeply we of Helium cherish this son of yours. It seems as though the immense love we held for his noble father and his poor mother has coalesced entirely around him. When the news of his loss spread, ten million hearts wept."

"What do you mean, Kantos Kan?" I whispered, a chill running down my spine. "By 'his poor mother'?" The phrase carried a sinister resonance I could not decipher.

Drawing me aside, Kantos Kan spoke rapidly. "For a year, ever since Carthoris vanished, Dejah Thoris has mourned her lost boy. The grief of years past, when you failed to return from the atmospheric plant, was somewhat mitigated by the duties of motherhood, for your son broke his white shell that very night."

"She suffered terribly then, all Helium knows," I interjected softly. "Did not the entire nation share in her agony at the loss of her lord? But with the boy gone, everything collapsed. After expedition followed expedition, returning with empty hands and no clues, our beloved Princess dwindled. Those who saw her felt she could not survive long before joining her loved ones within the precincts of the Valley Dor."

"As a final measure," Kantos Kan continued, "Mors Kajak, her father, and Tardos Mors, her grandfather, assumed command of two massive expeditions. A month ago, they sailed north to scour every inch of the northern hemisphere. Two weeks have passed with no word. Rumors swirled of a catastrophic disaster, that all were dead."

"About this time, Zat Arras renewed his suit for her hand. He has pined for her forever since your disappearance. She hated him and feared him, but with both her father and grandfather gone, Zat Arras held immense power, for he remains Jed of Zodanga—the position Tardos Mors appointed him to after you declined the honor."

"He secured a private audience with her six days ago. What transpired is unknown, but the next morning Dejah Thoris had vanished. With her went a dozen of her household guards and body servants, including Sola, the Green Woman—you recall Tars Tarkas' daughter."

"No word has emerged regarding their intentions," Kantos Kan admitted grimly. "Yet, it is always so with those who embark on a voluntary pilgrimage from which none returns. We can think of nothing else but that Dejah Thoris has sought the icy embrace of Iss, and her devoted servants chose to accompany her."

"Zat Arras was in Helium when she disappeared," Kantos Kan concluded, his voice heavy with dread. "He commands this fleet, which has searched tirelessly for her. We have found no trace, and I fear the quest is futile."

While we conversed, Hor Vastus' scouts began returning to the *Xavarian*, bringing no tidings of Thuvia. I was crushed by the news of Dejah Thoris's fate, and now, the shadow of uncertainty loomed over the girl I believed to be of a proud Barsoomian house, whom I intended to restore to her kin.

Just as I prepared to request a deeper search for her, a flyer from the flagship descended upon the *Xavarian*, delivering an officer bearing a dispatch from Arras. Kantos Kan read the message, then turned to me.

"Zat Arras commands that our 'prisoners' be brought before him immediately. There is naught else to do. Though he is supreme in Helium, it would accord far better with chivalry and decorum if he deigned to come hither and greet the savior of Barsoom with the honors due him."
