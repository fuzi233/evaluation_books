In the sweltering haze of an August evening, twelve years of memories accumulated like layers of sediment on the banks of a forgotten river. It had been twelve years since I'd last stood beside the grave of my great-uncle, Captain John Carter of Virginia, in that ancient mausoleum nestled within the crumbling cemetery at Richmond. I often found myself pondering the cryptic instructions he'd left behind, particularly the enigmatic directives regarding the design of his tomb – an open casket, a mechanism controlling the vault's massive door accessible only from within. The air was heavy with secrets, and I couldn't help but wonder if the man I thought I knew had truly met his end.

As I delved deeper into the manuscript he'd left behind, I discovered a man shrouded in mystery. He claimed to remember no childhood, nor could he estimate his age with any degree of certainty. Yet, he spoke of dandling my great-grandfather's great-grandfather upon his knee, a feat that defied the boundaries of time itself. This enigmatic figure had spent ten years on the unforgiving planet of Mars, where he'd fought alongside the green-skinned warriors of Barsoom and against them, even winning the heart of the breathtaking Dejah Thoris, Princess of Helium. For nearly a decade, he'd ruled as a prince in the House of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium.

Twelve years had passed since his lifeless body was discovered on the windswept bluff overlooking the Hudson River, and during those long, sleepless nights, I'd often questioned whether John Carter truly lay at rest or if he'd returned to the barren expanse of Mars, perhaps finding the fabled atmosphere plant's gates ajar, saving the countless millions suffocating on that distant world. Had he reunited with his black-haired princess and their child, waiting in the sun-kissed gardens of Tardos Mors, or had he discovered he'd arrived too late, condemning himself to a living death on a dead planet? Or, was it possible he'd never truly perished, forever trapped in the void between worlds?

Lost in the labyrinth of my thoughts, I received an unexpected visit from old Ben, my loyal body servant, bearing a telegram that shattered the tranquility of the evening. Unfurling the parchment, I read the words that would change everything: "Meet me tomorrow at the hotel Raleigh, Richmond."
