As soon as I stepped into the doorway, Rachel rose from the piano. I pulled the door shut behind me, and we stood facing one another in a heavy, silent stretch of the room. The motion she had made to stand seemed to drain whatever energy remained in her body; every other faculty, physical or mental, appeared to have collapsed into the single, terrifying act of staring at me.

A cold dread seized me: I had revealed myself too abruptly. I took a step forward, then another, softening my tone. "Rachel!"

The sound of my voice seemed to jolt life back into her limbs, restoring color to her pale cheeks. She drifted forward on her side, saying nothing. Slowly, as if driven by a force outside her own will, she approached me. A warm, dusky hue flushed her face, and a light of returning awareness ignited in her eyes, growing brighter with every passing second. For a moment, I forgot why I had come, forgot the foul suspicion hanging over my reputation, and forgot every duty I owed to the world. I saw nothing but the woman I loved drawing closer. She trembled, standing irresolutely. I could hold back no longer; I caught her in my arms and buried my face in hers, covering it with kisses.

There was a fleeting second when I thought she returned them—a moment when it seemed she, too, had forgotten everything. But before the thought could fully form, her first voluntary action shattered the illusion. With a cry that sounded like pure horror, with a strength I doubted I could withstand, she shoved me back. I saw merciless anger in her eyes, merciless contempt on her lips. She scanned me from head to toe, regarding me as if I were a stranger who had insulted her.

"You coward!" she cried. "You mean, miserable, heartless coward!"

Those were her first words. The most unbearable accusation a woman could hurl at a man was the one she chose.

"I remember, Rachel," I said, "when you might have told me I had offended you in a nobler fashion. I beg your pardon."

Some of the bitterness I felt must have seeped into my voice. At the first note of my reply, her eyes, which had turned away moments before, flickered back to me, though unwillingly. She answered in a low, sullen tone, a manner of submission entirely new to me.

"Perhaps there is some excuse for me," she murmured. "After what you have done, is it truly a manly act to seek me out as you did today? It seems a cowardly experiment to try upon my weakness for you. A cowardly surprise to catch me letting you kiss me. But that is merely a woman's view. I ought to have known it wasn't yours. I would have done better if I had controlled myself and said nothing."

That apology was more devastating than the insult. Even the most degraded soul would feel humiliated by it.

"If my honor were not in your hands," I said, "I would leave you this instant and never see you again. You have spoken of what I have done. Tell me, what have I done?"

"What have you done! Do you dare ask *that* question of *me*?"

"I ask it."

"I have kept your infamy a secret," she answered. "I have suffered the consequences of hiding it. Am I not entitled to be spared the further insult of you asking me what you have done? Has all gratitude died within you? Once you were a gentleman. Once you were dear to my mother, and dearest to me..."

Her voice failed. She sank into a chair, turned her back on me, and covered her face with her hands.

I waited a moment before trusting myself to speak again. In that silence, I could not decide what hurt more deeply: the sting of her contempt or the proud resolve that cut me off from sharing in her misery.

"If you will not speak first," I said, "I must. I have come here with something serious. Will you do me the justice of listening?"

She did not move, nor did she answer. I made no second appeal, advancing not an inch toward her chair. With a stubborn pride equal to hers, I laid bare my discovery at the Shivering Sand and recounted everything that had led to it. The narrative took some time. From start to finish, she never turned her head, and she never uttered a word.

I kept my temper. My entire future likely hung on my ability to maintain composure. It was time to test Mr. Bruff's theory. Driven by a breathless urgency, I circled around to stand directly in front of her.

"I have a question," I said. "It forces me to revisit a painful subject. Did Rosanna Spearman show you the nightgown? Yes, or No?"

She sprang to her feet and walked close to me, seemingly of her own accord. Her eyes searched my face intently, as if trying to read some hidden meaning I had never yet revealed.

"Are you mad?" she asked.

I restrained myself. "Rachel, will you answer my question?"

She continued, ignoring me.

"Do you have some ulterior motive I cannot fathom? Some base fear for the future? They say your father's death made you wealthy. Have you come here to compensate me for the loss of my diamond? Do you have enough conscience left to feel ashamed of your mission? Is *that* the secret of your feigned innocence, your story about Rosanna Spearman? Is there a motive of shame beneath all these lies?"

I stopped her then. I could no longer control myself.

"You have done me an infamous wrong!" I broke out hotly. "You suspect me of stealing your diamond. I have a right to know, and I *will* know, the reason why!"

"Suspect *you*!" she exclaimed, her anger rising to match mine. "_Villain, I saw you take the Diamond with my own eyes!_"

The revelation that crashed down upon me, the total collapse of Mr. Bruff's entire case, left me paralyzed. Though innocent, I stood before her in silence. To her eyes, and to anyone else's, I must have appeared as a man crushed by the discovery of his own guilt.

She recoiled from the sight of my humiliation and her own triumph. The sudden silence that fell over me seemed to terrify her. "I spared you at the time," she said. "I would have spared you now, if you had not forced me to speak." She moved away as if to leave the room, then hesitated before reaching the door. "Why did you come here to humiliate yourself?" she asked. "Why did you come here to humiliate *me*?" She paused a few steps inside. "For God's sake, say something!" she cried, passionately. "If you have any mercy left, do not let me degrade myself like this! Speak—and drive me out of the room!"

I advanced toward her, barely conscious of my actions. Perhaps I had some vague notion of holding her until she told me more. Since the moment I learned that the evidence condemning me in Rachel's mind was her own eyewitness testimony, nothing—not even my certainty of my own innocence—was clear to me. I took her hand and tried to speak with firm purpose. All I could manage was, "Rachel, you once loved me."

She shuddered and looked away. Her hand lay powerless and trembling in mine. "Let go of it," she whispered faintly.

My touch seemed to affect her as the sound of my voice had when I first entered. After she had called me a coward, after she had branded me a thief, while her hand lay in mine, I was still her master!

I drew her gently back into the center of the room and seated her beside me. "Rachel," I said, "I cannot explain the contradiction in what I am about to tell you. I can only speak the truth as you have spoken it. You saw me—with your own eyes, you saw me take the diamond. Before God, who hears us, I declare that I now know I took it for the first time! Do you doubt me still?"

She had neither heard nor heeded me. "Let go of my hand," she repeated faintly. That was her only answer. Her head sank onto my shoulder, and her hand unconsciously closed around mine at the very moment she asked me to release it.

I refrained from pressing the point. But there, my forbearance ended. My chance of ever lifting my head again among honest men depended on getting her to make her confession complete. My only remaining hope was that she had overlooked some trivial link in the chain of evidence—some small detail that, under careful scrutiny, might eventually vindicate me. I admitted to myself that I held onto her hand. I spoke to her with all the sympathy and confidence I could summon from our past.

"I want to ask you something," I said. "I need you to tell me everything that happened, from the moment we wished each other goodnight until the moment you saw me take the diamond."

She lifted her head from my shoulder and made an effort to free her hand. "Oh, why go back to it!" she cried. "Why go back to it!"

"I will tell you why, Rachel. We are both victims of some monstrous delusion wearing the mask of truth. If we examine what happened on the night of your birthday together, we may finally understand each other."
