As the letter from the Major arrived, it precipitated Doctor Portman into a hasty departure for Fairoaks, his countenance bespeaking the gravity of the tidings he bore. His eagerness to convey the news, though disagreeable, bespoke his character as a man of unwavering duty, akin to a dentist extracting a troublesome tooth – a task necessitating firm resolve and decisive action. The doctor hastened to impart the Major's words to Myra and Mrs. Portman, whose keen understanding of the Clavering social hierarchy allowed them to disseminate the information with the utmost speed and precision.

Meanwhile, the widow at Fairoaks had already been privy to the contents of Pen's letter, its revelation alleviating the dark forebodings that had assailed her for many months. Though she had anticipated the worst, her son's return, marked by a contrite heart and a renewed sense of purpose, brought a measure of solace to her troubled spirit. The Rector's words, though well-intentioned and steeped in tradition, failed to stir within her any significant perturbation, save for a deep-seated concern for her son's welfare. She questioned the wisdom of sending him to a place fraught with peril, where the allurements of vice might prove too great a temptation for one still so young. The notion that his father's inheritance was his to squander, as he saw fit, only served to heighten her perplexity.

It was a custom, dating back to a bygone era, whereby siblings would express their affection through a tender gesture, one that Pen and his younger sister, Laura, had once indulged in with great frequency during their childhood. However, as Laura had grown into a tall, slender, and comely young woman, this practice had gradually fallen by the wayside. Upon Pen's return from college, he found himself confronted with a maiden whose appearance had undergone a transformation, rendering it impossible for him to offer the familiar kiss that had once been his due. Instead, Laura greeted him with a dignified curtsy and a proffered hand, accompanied by a becoming blush that rose to the very spot where he had once lavished his affection.
