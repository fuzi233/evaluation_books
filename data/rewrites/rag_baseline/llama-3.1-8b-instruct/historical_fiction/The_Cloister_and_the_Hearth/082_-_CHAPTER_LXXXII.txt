As I sat beside Margaret, the faint scent of baking bread wafted through the air, mingling with the tang of salt and the sweetness of herbs, transporting me to a place of warmth and comfort. The flickering candlelight danced across her face, illuminating the deep creases etched into her skin, a testament to the weight of her sorrow. Her words hung in the air like the wisps of smoke from the hearth, as she spoke of the man she loved, the father of her child, who had vanished like a ghost into the mist.

"Not so," she said, her voice trembling, "they all say he is dead." Her words dripped with desperation, like the slow drip of honey from a broken hive. "Aye, to your face, but behind your back they all say he is dead."

The kitchen, once a sanctuary, now felt oppressive, the air thick with unspoken truths. Luke, the loyal servant, stood tall, his broad shoulders a testament to his unwavering dedication. His eyes, like two pools of calm water, reflected the turmoil brewing within him. "Prithee, weep not so, sweet mistress," he said, his voice a gentle breeze on a summer's day. "I'd bring him back to life an I could, rather than see thee weed so sore."

Margaret's tears, like autumn's first snow, began to fall, soft and silent, as she struggled to comprehend the depths of her own despair. She thought she wept because they were so double-tongued with her, but the truth lay deeper, like a river hidden beneath the surface. Her grief was a tapestry woven from threads of love, loss, and longing.

As she recovered, her hand reached out, a lifeline in the darkness, and rested on Luke's shoulder. "Luke, he is not dead," she said, her voice steady as a mountain peak.
