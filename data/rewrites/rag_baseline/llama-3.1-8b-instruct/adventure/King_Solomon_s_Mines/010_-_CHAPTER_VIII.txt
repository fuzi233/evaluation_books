The sun beat down upon the dusty road, its worn surface a testament to the countless generations that had traversed it before us. As we journeyed deeper into the heart of Kukuanaland, the air grew thick with an almost palpable sense of history. Our guides, Infadoos and Scragga, walked alongside us, their footsteps echoing off the vast expanse of the surrounding landscape. Their followers marched ahead, a seemingly endless procession of brown-skinned warriors, their spears glinting in the sunlight like a scattering of sharp teeth.

I turned to Infadoos, my eyes scanning the endless miles of road stretching out before us. "Who built this magnificent thoroughfare?" I asked, my voice carrying on the wind.

Infadoos' eyes clouded, as if lost in thought. "It was constructed in a time long past, a mystery that even the wise woman Gagool cannot unravel. We Kukuana people have always known it, but its origins remain shrouded in the mists of history."

As we walked, I noticed the intricate carvings that adorned the walls of the caves through which we had passed. They seemed to hold secrets and stories, etched into the stone like the whispers of the ancients. "Whose hand crafted these ancient writings?" I asked, my curiosity getting the better of me.

Infadoos' expression turned enigmatic. "The same hands that created the road left behind these symbols. We know not who they were, nor what their purpose was."

We pressed on, the silence between us broken only by the rustle of leaves and the distant call of a bird. As the sun began its slow descent into the western horizon, casting a golden glow over the landscape, I asked, "When did the Kukuana people first arrive in this land?"

Infadoos' eyes seemed to cloud once more, his gaze drifting towards the distant mountains that ringed our world like a sentinel. "Our ancestors came here, like a great storm, ten thousand moons ago, from the lands beyond the northern mountains. They were drawn by the promise of this fertile land, and here they settled, growing strong and powerful over the generations. Today, our numbers are as boundless as the sands of the desert, and when Twala, our king, summons his warriors, their plumes stretch across the plain like a canvas of unending battle."
