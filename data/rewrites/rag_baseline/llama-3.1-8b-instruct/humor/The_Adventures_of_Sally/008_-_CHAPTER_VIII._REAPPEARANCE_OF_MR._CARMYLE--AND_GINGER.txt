As the train rumbled on, Fillmore's gaze drifted towards the window, where the blur of passing towns and cities formed a melancholy symphony. His mind wandered to the events of the past week – the triumphant opening night, the accolades from the critics, and the validation from Ike Schumann. The sound of his own voice still lingered in his ears, echoing the words of encouragement he'd given to Sally: "We're going to make it big, Sally. We're going to change the face of theatre." The thrill of possibility still coursed through his veins like a gentle stream.

But amidst the euphoria, a small discordant note had struck a chord within him. The critic's careless dismissal of Gladys Winch's performance had left a faint stain on the otherwise radiant evening. Fillmore's thoughts turned to the actress, her fragile smile and tentative steps on stage. He remembered the way Sally had laughed at his bitter remark, her eyes sparkling with amusement.

The train pulled into a station, and the sudden jolt snapped Fillmore back to reality. He turned to Sally, who was watching him with an expectant look. "What is it?" she asked, her voice low and gentle. Fillmore hesitated, unsure how to articulate the unease that had been growing inside him. "I don't know," he said finally, "it's just...sometimes I wonder if we're moving too fast. If we're losing sight of what's truly important."

Sally's expression softened, and she reached out to touch his hand. "We're doing something beautiful, Fillmore. Something that brings people joy. What's wrong with that?" Her words were like a balm to his troubled soul, and for a moment, the doubts receded. But as the train continued on its journey, Fillmore couldn't shake the feeling that they were standing on the cusp of something greater, yet uncertain of what lay ahead.
