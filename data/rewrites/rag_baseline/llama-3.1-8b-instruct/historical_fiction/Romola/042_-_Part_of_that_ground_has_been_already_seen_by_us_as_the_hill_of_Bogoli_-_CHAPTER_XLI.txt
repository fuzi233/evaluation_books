Coming Back.

Fra Girolamo's words hung in the crisp morning air like the first wisps of smoke from the hearth. "Rise, my daughter," he said, finally breaking the stillness. "Your servant is waiting nearby with the mules. It is time for me to continue on to Florence."

Romola slowly rose from her kneeling position, the gentle movement a testament to the resolve that had taken hold within her. The quiet surrender of her resolve to leave her husband had left her feeling fragile, and she needed guidance in even the smallest of things. She pushed aside the hood of her cloak, revealing the pale morning light, and caught sight of Maso and the second Dominican standing at the edge of the hill, about ten yards away, their backs turned towards her. Her gaze, however, lingered on Savonarola, as if seeking his approval before issuing the command to return.

"I will summon them," he said, responding to her unspoken plea. "And I will recommend you, my daughter, to the Brother who stands beside me. You wish to seek guidance and learn the wisdom that has thus far eluded you. The sacrament of confession is a crucial step on this path. You will require a confessor, my daughter, and I wish to entrust you to Fra Salvestro, a brother of San Marco, whom I greatly esteem."

"I would prefer to remain under your guidance, father," Romola replied, her voice tinged with anxiety.

"My daughter, I am not suited for such a role. My vocation calls me to tasks that require solitude and focus, tasks that would necessitate frequent interaction with the laity and detract from my duties."

"Then I will not be able to speak with you in private?" Romola asked, her words laced with unease. She feared that her newfound strength, forged in the crucible of renunciation, might falter if the direct influence of Savonarola were to wane.

"My daughter, if your soul requires counsel from my lips, you may seek me out through Fra Salvestro. I will meet you in the sacristy or the choir of San Marco. I will instruct my brother to guide you along the path of service to the afflicted and the needy, a calling that suits your spirit as a daughter of Florence in these trying times. I envision you among the weaker and less informed sisters, like a shining apple tree amidst the forest, its beauty and gifts serving only to illuminate the divine light
