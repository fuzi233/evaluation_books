In the dimly lit corridors of Headquarters, Mr. Gryce's footsteps echoed as he hastened towards the cellblock, his mind consumed by the enigmatic case that had been unfolding like a slow-burning flame. The air was heavy with anticipation, and the weight of unspoken questions hung like a challenge. As he approached Bartow's cell, the butler's vacant stare seemed to pierce through the gloom, a haunting reminder of the mysteries that lay beyond the veil of reason.

The doctor's verdict still lingered in Mr. Gryce's thoughts – unfavorable, with no hope of immediate recovery. Yet, the detective's resolve remained unwavering, driven by a spark within him that refused to be extinguished. He summoned the officer who had apprehended Bartow, his eyes narrowing as he asked, "How did he react when he discovered his confinement?"

The officer's response was curt, but laced with a hint of unease. "He was surprised, sir. Shook his head, made those... gestures, and then fell into a sort of trance. It's a peculiar sight, sir. He keeps holding his right hand out, as if beckoning us to notice something."

Mr. Gryce's gaze lingered on the officer's words, his mind piecing together the fragments of Bartow's behavior. The same gesture he had made when he first became aware of his audience – a plea for understanding, or perhaps a desperate attempt to convey a crucial detail. The detective's eyes never wavered, drinking in every nuance of the officer's account.

As the officer departed, Mr. Gryce turned his attention to the five small spangles that had been nestled in his pocket. With a gentle touch, he emptied them onto his desk, their glinting surfaces catching the faint light. He had always found solace in the simple, mundane objects that surrounded him – a small, insignificant trinket could become a focal point, clarifying his thoughts and sharpening his focus. The spangles seemed to hold a secret, one that only revealed itself to him as he gazed at them intently. After a moment of contemplation, he gathered them back into the envelope, his eyes flicking to his watch before he strode out into the outer office.
