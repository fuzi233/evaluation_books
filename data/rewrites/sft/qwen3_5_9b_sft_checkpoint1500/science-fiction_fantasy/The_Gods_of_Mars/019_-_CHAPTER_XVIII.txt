Stepping past the heavy doors of the palace, I ushered Solá into the grand dining hall. Once she had paid her respects to her father according to the ancient customs of the Green Men, the silence of the room broke as Solá began to recount the harrowing journey of Dejah Thoris.

"Seven days past," Solá declared, her voice trembling slightly, "after her private audience with Zat Arras, Princess Dejah Thoris vanished into the cover of the midnight hour. Though I knew not the content of her interview with the traitorous Jeddak, I sensed a torment in her spirit that drove her to flight. Discovering her slipping away from the palace walls, I needed no further explanation of her intent.

"I hastily rallied a dozen of her most trusted guards, laying out my fears. Instantly, they joined me, swearing to follow our beloved Princess wherever she roamed, even unto the sacred shores of Issus and the depths of the Valley Dor. We tracked her down mere leagues from the capital. Beside her walked Woola, her faithful hound, but no others. When we overtook her, she feigned fury, ordering us back to the palace. For the first time, however, we defied her command. Seeing that we would not permit her to embark on this final, solitary pilgrimage, she wept, threw herself into our embrace, and together we ventured forth into the southern night.

"The following dawn, we encountered a herd of small thoats, mounting them and pressing onward with desperate speed. We traveled far and fast due south, and by the morning of the fifth day, the horizon lit up with the gleam of a mighty fleet of battleships sailing north. Before we could hide, we were beset by a horde of black-skinned pirates. The Princess's guard fought like lions, but they were swiftly overwhelmed and slaughtered. Only Dejah Thoris and I survived.

"When the black captors seized us, the Princess, realizing her doom, attempted suicide with a dagger. One of the blacks snatched the blade from her hand, and then they bound us both, rendering our hands useless.

"Their great fleet pressed northward. It numbered some twenty massive battleships, accompanied by numerous swift cruisers. That very evening, a smaller cruiser, which had raced ahead of the main body, returned bearing a prize: a young red woman captured in a mountain range just beneath the watchful eyes of a trio of red Martian battleships.

"From fragments of overheard conversation, it became clear that these black pirates were hunting a group of fugitives who had escaped them days prior. Their urgent interrogation of the new captive confirmed her importance. She was subsequently bound and confined in the same compartment with Dejah Thoris and myself.

"This new prisoner was a vision of beauty. She revealed to Dejah Thoris that years ago she had voluntarily left the court of her father, the Jeddak of Ptarth, to undertake a pilgrimage. Her name was Thuvia, Princess of Ptarth. Turning then to Dejah Thoris, she asked who she was. Upon hearing the answer, Thuvia fell to her knees, kissing the fettered hands of the princess, confessing that that very morning she had stood beside John Carter, Prince of Helium, and his son, Carthoris.

"Dejah Thoris struggled to believe her, yet as Thuvia recounted the wild and wondrous adventures she had shared with John Carter, and listened to the tales of valor told by John Carter, Carthoris, and Xodar regarding their exploits in the Land of the First Born, doubt vanished. 'For who,' Dejah Thoris whispered, tears streaming down her cheeks, 'could perform the deeds you describe other than John Carter?' When Thuvia spoke of her love for John Carter and his unwavering devotion to the woman of his choice, Dejah Thoris collapsed into weeping, cursing Zat Arras and the cruel destiny that had torn her from Helium just days before the return of her beloved lord.

"'I do not blame you for loving him, Thuvia,' Dejah Thoris sobbed. 'And I can well believe the purity of your affection from the candor with which you speak it.'

"The fleet sailed north almost to Helium, but last night they evidently realized that John Carter had evaded their grasp. Consequently, they turned south again. Shortly thereafter, a guard burst into our compartment and dragged me onto the deck.

"There is no place in the Land of the First Born for a green one!' the guard sneered. With those words, he shoved me violently off the deck. Clearly, he believed this the swiftest means of disposing of me.

"But fortune, or perhaps a divine intervention, smiled upon me. By a miracle, I escaped with but minor bruises. The ship moved slowly then, and as I plunged into the abyss beneath, I shivered at the thought of certain death. All day, the fleet had hovered thousands of feet above the earth; yet, to my utter astonishment, I landed softly upon a thick mass of vegetation merely twenty feet from the hull. In truth, the keel of the vessel had barely grazed the surface of the ground at that moment.

"I lay where I fell throughout the night. At sunrise, the mystery of my survival unfolded. I beheld a vast panorama of seabottom and distant hills far below. I stood atop the highest peak of a lofty range. The fleet, in the darkness of the previous night, had skimmed the crests of the hills; in that fleeting moment when they hung close to the earth, the black guard had pitched me, intending my destruction, but instead I had fallen into a sanctuary.

"A few miles to the west lay a great waterway. Upon reaching it, I found to my delight that it flowed from Helium. Here, a thoat was secured for me—and thus, you know the rest."

For many minutes, not a soul spoke. The thought of Dejah Thoris in the clutches of the First Born chilled me to the bone. Yet, suddenly, the ancient fire of unquenchable self-confidence flared within my breast. I sprang to my feet, throwing back my shoulders and raising my sword high, vowing solemnly to reach her, to rescue her, and to exact vengeance upon those who held her.

A hundred swords flashed from their scabbards. A hundred warriors leaped onto the tables around us, pledging their lives and fortunes to the expedition. My plans were already forming in my mind. I thanked each loyal friend, leaving Carthoris to entertain them, and withdrew to my private audience chamber with Kantos Kan, Tars Tarkas, Xodar, and Hor Vastus.

Here, deep into the night, we hammered out the details of our operation. Xodar was convinced that Issus would select both Dejah Thoris and Thuvia to serve her for a year.

"For that duration, at least, they will be relatively secure," Xodar reasoned. "And we shall at least know where to look for them."

Regarding the outfitting of a fleet to breach Omean, the specifics were entrusted to Kantos Kan and Xodar. The former promised to procure the necessary vessels and hasten them to the docks, where Xodar would oversee their fitting with water-propellers. For many years, the black Xodar had directed the refitting of captured battleships designed to navigate the waters of Omean, making him intimately familiar with the construction of the propellers, housings, and auxiliary gears required.

Estimates suggested six months would be needed to complete preparations, given the absolute necessity of maintaining secrecy to conceal the project from Zat Arras. Kantos Kan was now certain that the traitor's ambitions were fully inflamed; nothing short of the title of Jeddak of Helium would satisfy him.

"I doubt," Kantos Kan observed gravely, "that he would even welcome Dejah Thoris' return. To him, it would imply another rival closer to the throne than he. With you and Carthoris removed from the scene, there would be little to hinder him from seizing the title. Rest assured, so long as he reigns supreme here, neither of you is safe."

"There is a way," cried Hor Vastus, his eyes blazing. "To thwart him effectively and forever!"

"What?" I asked.

He smiled enigmatically.

"I shall whisper it here, but someday I shall stand upon the dome of the Temple of Reward and shout it to the cheering multitudes below."

"What do you mean?" Kantos Kan demanded.

"John Carter, Jeddak of Helium!" Hor Vastus murmured in a low tone.

The eyes of my companions alighted, grim smiles of pleasure and anticipation spreading across their faces as each gaze turned questioningly toward me. But I shook my head.

"No, my friends," I said, smiling gently. "I thank you, but it cannot be—not yet, at least. When we know that Tardos Mors and Mors Kajak are gone forever, returning no more... if I am still here, then I shall join you all to ensure that the people of Helium are permitted to choose their next Jeddak fairly. Whoever they choose may count upon the loyalty of my sword, nor shall I seek honor for myself. Until then, Tardos Mors remains Jeddak of Helium, and Zat Arras is merely his representative."

"As you will, John Carter," Hor Vastus replied, "but—what was that?" he whispered, pointing urgently toward the window overlooking the gardens.
