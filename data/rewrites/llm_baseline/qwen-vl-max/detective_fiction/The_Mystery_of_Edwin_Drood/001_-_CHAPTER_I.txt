A cathedral tower—ancient, gray, and square—rises starkly against the sky. But how can it be here? This is no English countryside, no quiet village nestled beneath the shadow of a historic church. Yet there it stands, unmistakable in its solidity, looming over a scene that should never have room for such a relic. Between the viewer and the tower, something juts into the air—a spike of rusted iron, sharp and crude, like a forgotten weapon left to corrode. It shouldn’t exist. And yet, it does.

Could it be the Sultan’s decree? A grim stake meant for the impalement of Turkish thieves, one by one? The thought flares with sudden vividness: cymbals crash, a royal procession winds through the streets—ten thousand scimitars flashing in the sun, thirty thousand dancing girls scattering petals. White elephants, draped in dazzling silks, follow behind, attended by endless servants. Still, the cathedral looms in the background, impossible and unyielding. And still, no body writhes upon the spike.

Wait—could it be nothing more than the twisted iron post of an old bedstead, broken and askew? The idea brings a faint, drowsy laugh. Then, shaking violently from head to toe, a man stirs. His scattered thoughts coalesce, piecing together fragments of dream and reality. He rises slowly, bracing himself on his arms, and looks around.

He is in a cramped, filthy room. Through the torn curtain of a window, the pale light of dawn filters into a squalid courtyard. He lies dressed across a large, unclean bed, atop a frame that has collapsed under weight. Beside him, also lying across the bed, are three others: a Chinaman, a Lascar, and a gaunt woman. The two men are unconscious or half-asleep; the woman holds a pipe, blowing gently at its ember, trying to ignite it.

As she blows, her hand trembling, the red spark glows faintly in the dim light—just enough to reveal her face. “Another?” she rasps, her voice thin and rattling. “Have another?”

The man rubs his forehead, disoriented. “You’ve smoked five since midnight,” she continues, complaining. “Poor me, poor me—my head aches so. Those two came in after you. Ah, poor me—the trade’s slack! Few Chinamen at the docks, fewer Lascars, no ships coming in. Here’s another ready for you, dear. You’ll remember—market price’s terrible high now. Over three shillings and sixpence for a thimbleful! And only I—and Jack Chinaman across the court, but he doesn’t mix it right—know the true secret. You’ll pay up, won’t you, dear?”

She speaks while puffing at the pipe, occasionally sucking in its fumes. “O me, O me—my lungs are weak, my nerves are shot. Nearly ready for you, dear. My hand shakes like it might fall off! I saw you coming, and I said to myself, ‘I’ll have one ready. He’ll remember the price and pay.’ O my poor head!”

She explains how she makes her pipes from old ink bottles, fits mouthpieces, measures the mix with a tiny horn spoon. “I was Heaven-hard drunk for sixteen years before I turned to this,” she mutters. “But this don’t hurt me—not really. And it kills hunger better than food.”

She hands him the nearly empty pipe and collapses back, turning onto her face.

He rises, staggers to the hearth, places the pipe down, pulls back the ragged curtain, and stares at his companions with disgust. He notices that the woman, under the influence, bears a strange resemblance to the Chinaman—same cheekbones, same eyes, same pallor. The Chinaman convulses, snarling as if wrestling some unseen demon. The Lascar giggles, drooling. The woman is silent.

“What visions could *she* see?” the man wonders, turning her face toward him. “Dreams of butcher shops, taverns, debts owed? Of more customers, this bedstead upright again, this court swept clean? What could she rise to—even with all this opium—higher than that? Eh?”

He leans close, listening to her murmurs. “Unintelligible.”

Watching the spasms ripple across her face and limbs—like lightning in a storm—something seizes him. He feels their madness creeping into his own mind. He retreats to a worn armchair by the fire, grips its arms tightly, and sits rigid until the sensation passes.

Then, he returns, lunges at the Chinaman, grabs him by the throat, and turns him violently onto the bed. The man struggles, gasps, protests. “What do you say?” The man waits, alert. “Unintelligible.”

Slowly releasing his grip, he listens to the garbled words, frowning. Then he turns to the Lascar and drags him out onto the floor. As the man falls, he jerks awake, glaring, flailing wildly, drawing a phantom knife. But the knife is not in his hand—it’s in the woman’s dress. She too has stirred, restraining him, pleading. They collapse back side by side.

Enough noise and nonsense—no coherent word has emerged. The watcher nods grimly, a dark smile forming. “Unintelligible,” he repeats.

He places silver coins on the table, finds his hat, stumbles down the broken stairs, greets a rat-infested doorkeeper sleeping in a black hutch beneath them, and steps outside.

Later that afternoon, the same massive gray cathedral tower appears again—this time before a weary traveler. Bells toll for vespers. He hurries toward the open doors, drawn by urgency. Inside, the choir rushes to don their stained white robes. He slips into his own, joins the procession, and enters the chancel.

The sacristan locks the iron gates between sanctuary and chancel. All take their places, bow their heads. Then, the chant begins—“WHEN THE WICKED MAN—” echoing through arches and beams, stirring thunder in the vaulted silence.
