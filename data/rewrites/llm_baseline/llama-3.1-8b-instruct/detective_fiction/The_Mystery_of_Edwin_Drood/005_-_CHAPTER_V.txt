As John Jasper strolled through the moonlit Close, he was halted by the sight of Stony Durdles, dinner-bundle slung over his shoulder, leaning against the iron railing that enclosed the burial ground. A small, ragged boy was pelting him with stones, laughing with each successful throw and whistling triumphantly through a jagged gap in his mouth, where several teeth were missing. Whenever he missed, the boy yelped "Mulled agin!" and took aim again, his eyes fixed intently on Durdles.

Jasper stepped out into the moonlight, his voice firm but measured. "What are you doing to the man?" The boy paused, stone poised mid-air, and replied, "Making a cock-shy of him." Jasper's eyes narrowed. "Give me those stones in your hand." The boy snarled, shaking himself free and backing away. "I'll smash your eye if you try to grab me!"

Jasper's expression softened slightly. "Baby-Devil, what has the man done to you?" The boy's gaze dropped, and he muttered, "He won't go home." Jasper's brow furrowed. "What's that to you?" The boy looked up, a glint in his eye. "He pays me a 'apenny to chase him home if I catch him out too late." He began to chant, his voice rising and falling in a strange cadence:

"Widdy widdy wen!
I—ket—ches—Im—out—ar—ter—ten,
Widdy widdy wy!
Then—E—don’t—go—then—I—shy—
Widdy Widdy Wake-cock warning!"

Jasper's eyes widened as he realized the boy was singing a cautionary tune, a warning to Durdles to stay clear. He beckoned the boy to follow him, feeling it futile to try and drag or coax him, and walked towards Durdles, who stood motionless, lost in thought.

"Do you know this... creature?" Jasper asked, struggling to find the right words. Durdles nodded. "Deputy." Jasper's eyes met the boy's, and he felt a pang of unease. "Is that its name?" Durdles nodded again. Deputy, still clutching his stones, explained that he was a servant at the Travellers' Twopenny in Gas Works Garding, and all the servants there were known as Deputy.
