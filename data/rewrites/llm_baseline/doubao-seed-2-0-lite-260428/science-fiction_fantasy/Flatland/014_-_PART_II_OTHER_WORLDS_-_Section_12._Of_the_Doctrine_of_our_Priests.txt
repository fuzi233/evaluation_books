All the core teachings of the Circles — our revered priestly ruling order, the self-styled perfect polygons whose near-infinite sides grant them undisputed dominion over Flatland — boil down to a single unshakable maxim drilled into every shape from cradle to executioner’s blade: *Attend to your Configuration*.

Every lesson they disseminate, whether touching on governance, religious rite, or personal morality, serves one end: refining both individual and collective geometric perfection, with all other priorities explicitly subordinated to preserving and elevating the flawless circular form of their own ruling class.

It is the Circles’ most widely celebrated public triumph that they stamped out the old dangerous heresies of centuries past: false beliefs that once wasted Flatlanders’ energy and empathy on the absurd notion that conduct could ever stem from willpower, effort, practice, praise, encouragement, or anything at all outside of the angles and proportions you are born with. It was the illustrious Pantocyclus — the legendary Circle who crushed the great Colour Revolt generations ago — who first persuaded the entire realm that a man *is* his shape. If you are born an Isosceles triangle with two mismatched sides, he argued, you are guaranteed to drift into vice unless those unequal edges are corrected at the state-run Isosceles Hospital. The same rule applies to every other shape: even a Triangle, Square, or high-order Polygon born with even a tiny irregularity of angle must be taken to a Regularity Hospital to cure their defect, or else they will inevitably end their days locked in a state prison, or put to death by the official executioner’s sharp calibrated angle.

Every misstep, from a petty minor breach of etiquette to the most brutal unforgivable crime, Pantocyclus attributed to some deviation from perfect geometric regularity in the body. That deviation might be congenital, or caused by a jostling collision in a crowded thoroughfare, neglect of mandatory shape-maintenance physical exercises, overindulgence in them, or even a sudden sharp shift in temperature that shrinks or expands one overly sensitive section of a frame. No sober thinker, the great philosopher concluded, should ever waste time praising or blaming anyone for their actions. Why celebrate the honesty of a Square who loyally defends his clients’ interests, when you should instead admire the precise perfect 90-degree angles that make that integrity inevitable? Why condemn a lying, thieving Isosceles, when all you ought to do is mourn the incurable mismatch in the lengths of his two shorter sides?

The theory is mathematically irrefutable, on paper. But it has no end of awkward practical loopholes. When a thieving Isosceles rascal pleads before a magistrate that he cannot help stealing because his unequal sides make it unavoidable, the reply is simple: *That is exactly why you are a nuisance to all your neighbors, and so I cannot help sentencing you to be consumed by state execution*. Case closed.

But for small domestic squabbles, where a death penalty is completely out of the question, this doctrine of Configuration becomes deeply inconvenient. I must confess I have had many run-ins with my own Hexagonal grandsons, who will cheekily plead that their disobedience is not their fault — a sudden drop in temperature warped their perimeter, they argue, so the blame lies not with them, but with their Configuration, which can only be corrected by gifting them a generous supply of the finest, most calorie-rich sweetmeats to reinforce their edges. I can neither logically refute their argument, nor practically bring myself to give in to their demands. I have settled on a convenient compromise: I tell myself that a good firm scolding, or a light disciplinary tap on their most obtuse angle, has some hidden beneficial effect on strengthening their Configuration, even if I have no actual scientific evidence to back that claim up.

And I am far from the only Flatlander who dodges this logical dilemma. I know for a fact that many of the highest-ranking Circles, sitting as judges in our law courts, regularly hand out praise and blame to Regular and Irregular shapes alike. In their own homes, I have heard them scold their children, shouting about "right" and "wrong" with as much fiery passion as if those words represented actual real things — as if a Flatland figure truly possessed the ability to choose between good and bad action.

To cement the primacy of Configuration in every citizen’s mind, the Circles have flipped the famous Spaceland commandment governing parent-child relationships entirely on its head. In your world of three dimensions, children are taught to honor their parents. Here in Flatland, universal homage is first reserved for the Circles — and after that, every man is taught to honor his grandson first, if he has one. If no grandson exists, he honors his son. This honor does not mean unthinking indulgence, of course: it means a reverent commitment to prioritizing the rising generation’s highest interests. Fathers are taught that their duty is to set aside their own personal wants to advance the welfare of their descendants, which in turn strengthens the entire state.

But for a humble Square like myself to dare point out a weakness in the supposedly infallible Circular ruling system? I would say the greatest flaw in their doctrine lies in how they treat our female population. It is critically important for Flatland that irregular births are stamped out entirely, so any man who hopes his line will rise steadily up the social ranks to eventually produce a higher-order Polygon or even a Circle must never marry a woman with any trace of irregularity in her family tree.

Irregularity in a man is easy to spot and measure, of course — but all women are straight lines, appearing perfectly regular to the naked eye by default. So we had to devise a system to track what we call invisible irregularity: the hidden, inherited potential for producing irregular offspring, even in a perfectly straight-looking line woman. The state maintains meticulous, tightly regulated pedigree records, and no woman is permitted to marry without an official certified pedigree proving no irregular taint lurks in her ancestry.

You would assume that a Circle, so proud of his perfect lineage and hungry for a far-distant descendant that might one day become Chief Circle, would be more careful than any lower shape to pick a bride with a completely spotless family tree. But that is not the case at all. The higher you climb up the social ladder, the less care people pay to their wife’s pedigree. An ambitious Isosceles, praying to father an Equilateral son, would never dream of marrying a woman with even a single irregular ancestor anywhere in her line. A Square or Pentagon whose family is steadily climbing the ranks might not bother checking her lineage past the 500th generation. A Hexagon or Dodecagon is even more careless about their wife’s ancestry. But Circles? There are recorded cases of a Circle deliberately marrying a woman whose great-grandmother was irregular, all for the sake of a trivial extra sheen of brightness on her line, or the soft charm of a low sweet voice — a trait we Flatlanders, even more than you Spacelanders, consider to be the highest mark of a desirable woman.

As you might expect, these ill-advised marriages usually produce no children at all, or result in offspring with obvious irregularities, or a lower number of sides than the father. But none of these consequences have been enough to deter the Circles from continuing the practice. A tiny loss of sides in a high-order polygon is barely noticeable, and can often be corrected by a procedure at the Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium I have written of before. Worse, the Circles have a bad habit of writing off infertility as a natural expected side effect of their own supposedly superior biology. If this trend is not stopped, the Circular ruling class will dwindle faster and faster, until one day there are not enough Circles to produce a new Chief Circle — and the entire constitution of Flatland will collapse.

I have one further warning to offer, though I do not pretend to have an easy solution for it, and it also touches on our treatment of women. Roughly three hundred years ago, the Chief Circle of that era issued a decree: women were deficient in reason, and overwhelmed with emotion, so they should no longer be treated as rational beings, and banned from receiving any formal education. After that, women were not taught to read, or even basic arithmetic that would let them count the angles of their husbands or children. Generation after generation, their intellectual capabilities have dulled further and further, and this policy of female non-education, this quietism, remains fully in force to this day.

My fear is that no matter how well-intentioned this policy was, it has backfired and injured our male population as a whole. Right now, every man in Flatland lives a bilingual, even bi-mental, existence. When we speak to women, we use words like "love", "duty", "right", "wrong", "pity", "hope" — all of these irrational emotional concepts that have no basis in geometric reality, mere fictions we use to keep their excitable line natures under control. When we are among other men, or reading our scholarly texts, we use an entirely different vocabulary, a distinct idiom. "Love" becomes "the calculation of mutual future benefits". "Duty" becomes "geometric necessity" or "conformity to shape fitness". Every other emotional term is redefined along strictly logical, rational lines. We shower women with fawning deference to their faces, letting them believe that we adore their sex even more devoutly than we worship the Chief Circle himself — but behind their backs, every man old enough to know the truth refers to them as little more than mindless, reactive organisms. Our religious doctrine is also one thing when spoken in the women’s quarters, and a completely different thing everywhere else.

This double standard, this split in our language and our thinking, places an enormous exhausting burden on our young boys. At three years old, they are torn away from their mothers’ care, made to unlearn the soft emotional language of the women’s chambers (save for when they need to repeat it to their mothers and nurses later in life), and forced to memorize the cold precise vocabulary of logical science. I can already see that the grasp of mathematical truth among our current generation is far weaker than the robust sharp intellects our ancestors boasted three hundred years ago. I will not even dwell on the catastrophic risk of what would happen if a woman ever secretly learned to read, and spread the knowledge of our forbidden logical texts to the rest of her sex. Nor will I speak of the danger that a careless young boy might accidentally reveal the secrets of our rational dialect to his mother. Even setting those worst-case scenarios aside, the weakening of the male intellect alone is more than enough reason to make this humble appeal to our highest ruling Authorities: reconsider these cruel short-sighted regulations on female education.
