The evening sun cast a warm orange glow over the cobblestone streets of Florence as Romola hastened through the crowded marketplaces, her footsteps echoing off the ancient buildings. With Tessa and the children in tow, she navigated the narrow alleys, dodging merchants and artisans hurrying to complete their daily tasks before the impending nightfall. The air was alive with the aroma of roasting chestnuts and baking bread, mingling with the sweet scent of fresh herbs and the acrid tang of coal smoke.

As she quickened her pace, Romola's thoughts turned to the recent publication of Fra Girolamo's Trial, a document that had sent shockwaves through the city. Copies of the confessions, drawn from the prophet by the sixteen Florentine citizens commissioned to interrogate him, had been hastily recalled by the Signoria, but not before many had fallen into eager hands. Romola had obtained a copy, and she poured over its contents with growing unease, searching for a clearer understanding of the events that had transpired.

In the quiet moments, away from the din of the market, Romola found herself drawn to the words of Fra Girolamo, her heart aching with empathy for the suffering he had endured. She recalled the accounts of his tortures, the brutal treatment meted out by the inquisitors, and the whispered rumors of Ser Ceccone's involvement in shaping the document's content. Her mind whirled with the contradictions, torn between the fervent devotion of the Frate's followers and the doubts raised by his detractors.

As the family reached the safety of their humble abode, Romola paused to survey the simple yet comforting spread before her. A steaming pot of ribollita simmered on the hearth, fragrant with the essence of vegetables and herbs gathered from the nearby countryside. The aroma wafted through the room, mingling with the soft murmur of conversation from the family. For a moment, the turmoil outside receded, and Romola felt a sense of peace settle over her.

Yet, as she ladled the soup into bowls and handed them to her children, her thoughts returned to the weighty questions at hand. Could she truly trust the words of Fra Girolamo, or were they tainted by the machinations of his enemies? And what of the Signoria's true intentions, driven as they were by ambition and greed?
