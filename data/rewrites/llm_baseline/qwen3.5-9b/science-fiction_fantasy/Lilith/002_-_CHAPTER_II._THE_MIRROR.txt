Nothing further occurred for several days. It was roughly a week later that the events I am about to recount finally unfolded. For some time, I had been haunted by thoughts of the manuscript fragment, attempting repeatedly to unlock whatever force held it captive, yet all my efforts proved futile. However, I had long harbored an intention to conduct a thorough inventory of the books stored in the closet; the peculiar atmosphere within had begun to unsettle me regarding their preservation.

One afternoon, this vague intention crystallized into a firm resolve. As I rose from my chair to begin the task, I caught a glimpse of movement near the closet's threshold. To be precise, I perceived a shadowy figure advancing from the doorway toward the far end of the room. It appeared to be a slight, stooping man clad in a shabby dress coat that trailed nearly to his heels. As he walked, the tails parted slightly, revealing thin legs encased in black stockings and large feet shod in wide, slipper-like shoes. Without hesitation, I followed. Whether I was pursuing a literal shadow or a tangible entity remained uncertain, yet my conviction that I was tracking *something* was absolute.

He exited the library, crossed the hall, and descended to the base of the grand staircase. Then, ascending, he moved past the principal rooms of the first floor, continuing down a broad corridor to the foot of a narrower flight leading upward to the second story. He climbed those steps as well. When I finally reached the landing, though it may seem improbable, I found myself in a domain almost entirely unfamiliar to me. Having grown up without siblings to encourage the kind of exploratory play that renders children intimately acquainted with every nook and cranny, and having been taken away while still a child by my guardian, I had not set eyes on this house since returning approximately a month prior to claim my inheritance.

We passed through successive passages until we arrived at a door situated beneath a winding wooden staircase. We ascended together; every step groaned under my weight, yet my guide made not a single sound. Halfway up, I lost sight of him, and upon reaching the summit, the shadowy form vanished completely. Though the space was thick with shadows, he was not among them.

I stood in the main garret, a cavernous expanse dominated by massive beams and rafters overhead. Great distances stretched out around me, punctuated only by occasional doors and long vistas whose gloom was pierced by a few cobweb-laden windows and small, dusky skylights. I gazed upon the scene with a strange mixture of awe and delight; this vast, uncharted territory was mine alone. In the center of the room stood an unpainted enclosure constructed of rough planks, its door standing ajar. Suspecting Mr. Raven might be concealed within, I pushed the door open and stepped inside.

The small chamber was bathed in light, yet it possessed the quality of sunlight in a desolate ruin—a dull, disconsolate radiance suggesting a place forgotten by utility, regretting its very existence. A few faint rays of sun cut through a suspended cloud of dust motes, illuminating a tall, narrow mirror. It was old-fashioned, appearing merely as an ordinary pane of glass, save for its ebony frame. Perched atop the frame sat a black eagle with wings spread wide, clutching a golden chain in its beak, from which dangled a heavy black sphere.

I had been observing the mirror rather than peering into it when suddenly, reality shifted. I realized with jarring clarity that the reflection contained neither the chamber nor my own image. My memory is hazy regarding the transition; did the wall simply dissolve? What matters most is the aftermath, sufficient to explain any confusion: perhaps I mistook the glass protecting a wondrous painting for a reflective surface.

Before me lay a wild, broken landscape of heath and rock. In the middle distance rose desolate hills of unusual character, while the horizon was lined by the jagged peaks of a distant mountain range. Closest to me stretched a tract of moorland, flat and profoundly melancholy. Being short-sighted, I stepped forward to inspect the texture of a stone in the immediate foreground. In doing so, I noticed a large, ancient raven hopping toward me with solemn deliberation. Its plumage was a deep, purplish black, softened here and there with patches of gray. It seemed intent on finding worms as it approached.

Unstartled by the presence of a living creature within what I believed to be a picture, I advanced another step to get a clearer view. Suddenly, I tripped—likely over the frame of the mirror—and found myself nose-to-beak with the bird. There was no glass between us. I was no longer in a room; I stood in the open air, upon a houseless heath.
