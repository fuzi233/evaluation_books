As Harry Feversham rode across the Lennon bridge into Ramelton, the morning sun cast a warm glow over the town, infusing the dusty streets with a sense of life. Though the years spent under the scorching suns of the Soudan had etched deep lines on his face, he remained unrecognizable to the townspeople. He navigated the narrow streets, his horse's hooves echoing off the buildings as he made his way to the outskirts of town. The scent of freshly baked bread wafted from the nearby bakery, mingling with the sweet fragrance of blooming wildflowers that lined the roadside.

The intervening years had slowly receded from his thoughts as he journeyed across Egypt and the Continent, leaving behind a trail of fragmented memories. His mind, once consumed by the weight of regret and longing, had begun to find solace in the present moment. The things that had been, here in Ramelton, stood out in stark relief – the joy, the pain, the love, and the heartache. As he rode, the landscape unfolded before him like a canvas of forgotten moments, each one a reminder of the life he had left behind.

He stopped his horse in front of the church, its stone façade rising above the road like a sentinel. For an instant, his thoughts turned to Ethne, wondering if she still lingered in Ramelton, and whether Dermod Eustace's spirit still roamed the hills. But the present moment claimed him, and he urged his horse forward, leaving the memories to recede into the distance. The air was filled with the melodic trills of larks, the earthy scent of the meadow, and the harsh cry of a landrail, a symphony of sounds that drew him deeper into the heart of the town.

As he rode, the silence began to take on a life of its own, weaving together the threads of past and present. He felt the presence of ghosts, phantoms of people he had loved and lost, of memories he had cherished and regretted. The trees loomed ahead, their branches creaking in the gentle breeze, as he guided his horse toward a broken fence and a gateway shrouded in neglect. He rode past the entrance, his eyes scanning the overgrown drive, until he reached the threshold of a once-grand estate, now reduced to a crumbling shell.
