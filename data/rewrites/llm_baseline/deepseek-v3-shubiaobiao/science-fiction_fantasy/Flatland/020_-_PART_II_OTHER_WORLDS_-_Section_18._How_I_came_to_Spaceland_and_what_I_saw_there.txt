A terror beyond words gripped me. Darkness enveloped my senses—then came a lurching, nauseating whirl of perception unlike anything I had known. Lines twisted into impossible shapes; space itself unraveled and reformed. I was myself, yet no longer myself. When at last I found my voice, I screamed, "This is madness—or damnation!"

"Neither," answered the Sphere, calm as still water. "This is knowledge. This is the Third Dimension. Now steady yourself—look again."

And so I did.

A world beyond imagining unfolded before me. There, radiant and whole, stood the very vision of perfection I had only ever dared to theorize—the flawless Circular form, but *alive*, *embodied*. Yet where I expected organs—a heart, lungs, veins—there was only an exquisite, seamless harmony, a presence my Flatland tongue could not name. (You, Spaceland reader, would call it the surface of a Sphere.)

Overcome, I prostrated myself before my guide. "Divine one! I see *into* you, yet discern no inner workings—no heart, no lungs—how can this be?"

"What you believe you see, you do not," the Sphere replied. "No being of Flatland may perceive my depths. I am not of your order. Were I a Circle, you would glimpse my entrails. But I am a Sphere—the Many made One, infinite Circles in a single form. Just as the shell of a Cube is a Square, so too does my surface appear to you as a mere Circle."

Though his words confounded me, my resistance melted into awe.

"Be patient," he said, gentler now. "The truths of Spaceland will unfold in time. Let us first revisit your home—not as you knew it, but as it truly *is*."

Before I could protest, the world lurched again—and there, spread beneath me like a living map, was my own house, its walls *transparent* to my newly awakened sight. My sons slept in their chambers; my grandsons, orphaned and quiet, in theirs. The servants moved in their routines, my daughter in her quarters. Only my wife paced the hall, restless with worry. And there—the Page, feigning concern, rifled through my study, eyeing the gold in my cabinet.

*I could see it all.* Not deduction, not inference—*sight.*

I strained to rush to my wife’s side, but my body would not obey.

"Do not fret," said the Sphere. "Her fear will soon pass. Now—let us survey Flatland entire."

We rose higher. The city shrank below, every home, every soul laid bare. Deeper still—the earth itself split open, revealing mines, caverns, secrets buried beyond mortal reach.

"I have become as a god," I whispered. "For our scholars say that to see all—*omnividence*—is divine."

The Sphere’s voice turned sharp. "Then by your logic, every thief and murderer in Spaceland is a god, for they see your world as plainly as you do now. Does mere sight make one just? Merciful? No. Wisdom lies not in vision, but in what one *does* with it."

Before I could argue, he gestured downward. "Look there."

In the distance loomed the great Polygonal Assembly Hall, where the highest Circles convened. It was the dawn of the year 2000, and as tradition demanded, they gathered to reaffirm an ancient decree:

*"Let all who claim revelations from other worlds be silenced. Let the Isosceles be slain, the Triangles scourged, the Squares and Pentagons confined. Let none disturb the order of Flatland."*

The Sphere turned to me. "Hear your fate. Death or prison awaits the prophet of the Third Dimension."

"Let me go to them!" I begged. "I can make them understand!"

"Not yet." With that, he plunged into the Council’s midst, his circular form swelling before their eyes.

"I bring word of a Three-Dimensional world!" he declared.

Chaos erupted. Young Circles recoiled; Isosceles guards rushed forth, seizing at his shifting edges—"We have him! No—he’s gone!"

The presiding Circle silenced the panic. "This is no surprise. The archives speak of such... *disturbances* at each millennial dawn." He turned to the guards. "Arrest the witnesses. And you—" He nodded to my brother, the Council’s Clerk. "For secrecy’s sake, you must be imprisoned. But be silent, and you will live."

The Sphere returned to me as the Council dispersed, their New Year’s greetings hollow over the sound of shackles.

The lesson was clear: truth alone is not enough. Not when the world refuses to see.
