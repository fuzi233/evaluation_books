As the days passed, George's visits to Bloomsbury became less frequent, his attention increasingly monopolized by Mr. Will's unwavering devotion. The General's stern disapproval of his son's quarrelsome behavior served only to exacerbate the situation, making it difficult for George to resist his cousin's ardent affections. Little Lydia, once fiercely protective of George's affections, now lavished praise upon the quiet, reserved Miss Theo, suggesting that they were perfectly suited to one another. Her words, though well-intentioned, fell flat, given the awkward circumstances of their relationship.

Six weeks prior, Lydia had been consumed by jealousy over George's preference for another, but now she seemed almost indifferent, her attention focused on promoting Miss Theo's virtues. The memory of the constables' timely intervention still lingered, and Lyddy couldn't help but express her gratitude for their role in preventing a duel between Mr. Esmond and George. She pressed George to reconsider his decision to avoid Mr. Esmond's company, assuring him that it was absurd to harbor resentment towards someone who posed no threat.

However, George's reluctance to engage with Mr. Esmond remained unwavering, and he avoided meeting him for several days, not wishing to disturb the harmony of the Lambert household. When the news of their quarrel eventually reached Dean Street, a stir of concern arose among the ladies. "You were prepared to take a life, and yet you came to visit us, saying nothing?" Theo exclaimed, her voice laced with disappointment.

George protested, "He had insulted me and my brother, and I couldn't simply sit by and thank him for it." The General sat silently, his expression grave. Mr. Lambert, Sr., spoke up, "You know, papa, that we've often discussed the importance of standing up for oneself, even in the face of adversity."

Jack Lambert, the young clergyman, added, "It takes great strength of character to refuse, and I think I'd struggle to remain calm if someone called me a coward." George seized upon this sentiment, saying, "See, brother Jack agrees with me!" However, his enthusiasm was short-lived, as Jack's next words caught him off guard: "I'm a soldier's son, after all.
