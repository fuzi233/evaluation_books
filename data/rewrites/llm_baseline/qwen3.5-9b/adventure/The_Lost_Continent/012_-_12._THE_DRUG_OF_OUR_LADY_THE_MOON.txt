The sun was dipping toward the horizon, casting long, dying shadows as a column of black smoke from a burning mountain drifted before its face. Phorenice wrung the water from her soaked garments, shivering violently. "Row harder, Deucalion," she commanded, her voice tight. "Take me through the water-gate and restore me to my comforts. That merchant will regret the ruin of his fine clothes, and I regret them too—a woman's loss is double. Besides, he has no other garments I cannot replace." She glanced sideways at me, tossing back her short red hair. "And pray tell, what do you think of my wisdom in bringing us here without an escort?"

"The Empress can do no wrong," I replied, offering a polite smile.

"At least I've proven I can fight," she continued, her tone lightening. "I caught you admiring me more than once. You were a difficult man to thaw, Deucalion, but you warm perceptibly the closer you stay to me. La, sir, we'll be a pair of rustic sweethearts before long if this continues. I'm glad I devised this trip with those smelly fishermen."

Indeed, she had taken me out in an unguarded litter solely to invite combat, display her martial prowess, and perhaps gauge my reaction under pressure. If we were to share a life as husband and wife, it was vital to know each other's strengths intimately. Furthermore, as a veteran of countless battles, I rarely quarreled with anyone who offered a worthy fight. Still, I couldn't help but compare Phorenice to another woman. Had Nais faced a similar opportunity, she would have spared the struggle out of pity for the squalid rebels who didn't even acknowledge her as chief. Here, however, the Empress frittered away twenty of her hardest subjects merely to satisfy a whim.

Loyal to my vows as a priest and bound by the commands of the High Council on the Sacred Mountain, I suppressed these wandering thoughts. As I rowed across the swaying waves toward the harbor forts, I prayed to the High Gods for eloquence. Through their love for Atlantis and its suffering people, they granted me the power of speech Phorenice cherished. Her eyes gleamed as I spoke.

The beach where we had fought was protected from external ships by a jagged chain of rocks running from the harbor wall to the city's edge. Fishermen knew the passes, often slipping through unseen or leaping onto their boats if danger threatened. I possessed neither the local knowledge nor the dexterity required. *Now,* I thought, *the High Gods will reveal if they intend this woman, who has defiled them, to rule Atlantis—and if they wish me to serve as her husband.*

I voiced these doubts silently, awaiting an omen. There was no ambiguous answer. A massive wave rose in the lagoon behind us, one that could only stem from an earthquake. On its sleek crest, we were hurled forward, clearing the reefs and their clinging seaweed without striking a single stone. Bowing my head as I rowed toward the forts, I accepted the sign: the gods had not yet demanded vengeance for the insults this beautiful woman had offered them.

Sentries in the twin forts beat drums in their rotation, likely ignoring our shouting fishing boat in the twilight. Fortunately, two officers were airing themselves in a turret atop the nearer fort. Recognizing our cries, they silenced the drums. Torches were lowered to inspect our faces, and with a splash, the heavy chain was dropped, granting us passage.

Inside, a galley waited, hugging the harbor wall. Ropes lowered from the fort allowed a crew to board and man the oars. We donned fresh robes provided by the officers, discarding our sodden clothes in the cabin to avoid fevers that plagued those who stayed wet after sunset. Though I knew drugs could ward off such ailments, I noted with grim amusement that the Empress lacked sufficient divine favor to fear the petty illnesses of frail humanity.

The galley sped across the calm harbor waters and moored at the golden rings of the royal quay. While we waited for litters, I watched a lantern glow in the boat guarding Phorenice's mammoth. The colossal red beast stood shoulder-deep in the water, trunk raised. Tamed now, it trembled slightly under the light. I felt no urge to plead for it; if the mud claimed the giant, I could bear the loss with equanimity.

Truth be told, riding that beast had filled me with an almost unbearable sense of helplessness. Perhaps my circumstances had bred excessive self-reliance, but I preferred walking on my own feet—the way the Gods fashioned us. On my own legs, I could guard my own head and neck, a feat accomplished on four continents over a lifetime of action. On the back of that detestable creature, I felt as nervous as a frightened child.

With little time for personal anxieties, Phorenice questioned the port captain about Nais's fate. He reported that the rebel leader had been locked in a dungeon beneath the royal pyramid, with a guard sworn to her safety.

"It is to be hoped he understands his duty," the Empress remarked sharply. "Nais knows the pyramid better than most; he might be sent to the tormentors for placing her in a cell with a secret exit. Would you feel pleasure if she escaped, Deucalion?"

"Assuredly," I answered, knowing secrecy was futile. "I hold no enmity against Nais."

"But I do," she said viciously. "And I remain intent on locking your faith to me with the wedding gift you know of."

"The deed shall be done," I stated firmly. "First, before all else, the Empress of Atlantis."

"Pshaw!" she scoffed. "Deucalion, you are too stiff. You should be honored that I deign to be jealous of your favors. Take my hand, sir, and help me into the litter. Come beside me to keep me warm against the night air. Ho, you guards with the torches! Step back against the walls; the perfume you burn stifles me."

That night, another feast convened in the royal banqueting hall. Once again, I sat beside Phorenice on the raised dais beneath the symbols of the snake and the outstretched hand. This time, our impending union was proclaimed openly. The Empress acknowledged me as her future husband, and the crowd of jeweled courtiers hailed me as superior to themselves by virtue of her choice. There was method in their behavior; rumors of my preference for older, simpler habits had circulated, so no one drank to my health in the frivolous new style. Instead, each lord and lady approached decorously, spilling a goblet at my feet. When I invited anyone—man or woman—to eat from my platter, they did so simply and gratefully, avoiding the extravagant kissing or pocketing of food.

The flaring jets of earth-breath illuminated a plainer dress code and a more sober demeanor among the guests. Phorenice leaned over until her red curls brushed my face, chiding me playfully for usurping her authority.

"Oh, sir," she whispered mockingly, "don't make your rule over us too ascetic. No orders were given for this change, yet tonight there are no perfumes, the food is plain, and I half consider burning the cook. These clothes and jewels... they look as if they came straight from the Old King's reign before I arrived to teach taste. It wasn't by my command, Deucalion. They've shifted by sheer courtier instinct. Look at the men's beards! Many lack the exquisite curls they showed yesterday. By my face, they'd shave their chins smooth tomorrow if you kept setting trends at this rate without interference."

"Why hinder them if they prefer cleanliness?"

"No, sir. There shall be only one clean chin in all Atlantis where a beard can grow, and that belongs to the Empress's husband." She laughed softly. "Would you have no sumptuary laws? Would you have these nobles and the common people imitate us in every haircut and garment fold? Come, sir, if we chose to mark our sovereignty only by superior strength of arm and wit, they would hate us for arrogance. But if we reserve a few personal decorations for ourselves, they become objects of pleasant envy."

"You show me there is more to ruling than meets the eye."

"And yet," she countered, "they tell me you have ruled with some success."

"I employed the older methods. It takes a Phorenice to invent these finer flights."

"Flatterer!" she struck my arm playfully. "You're becoming as great a courtier as the rest. Your fine pleasantries make me blush, Deucalion, and there is no fan-girl here to cool my cheek. I must choose another. But it won't be Ylga. She seems kinder to you than I approve. If she is wise, she'll retire to her palace across the city to organize her slaves and embroider. I won't be hard on her unless forced, but no woman in this kingdom shall treat you with undue civility."

"How am I to act," I asked, falling into her mood, "when I see and hear all the men of Atlantis protesting their love before you? By your own admission, they adore you as ardently as they once loved you hopelessly."
