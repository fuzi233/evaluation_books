In the tenth hour of that day, we pushed the raft into the water. The paddles moved smoothly in their rests, as I had designed, and the raft glided forward with little effort. The Maid stood at the fore paddle, while I took the rear, and we fell into a steady rhythm, propelling ourselves at a pace just slower than walking.

By the twelfth hour, we paused to eat and drink, then resumed our quiet labor. The motion became so natural that we scarcely noticed it—only the gentle rocking of our bodies, back and forth, as the hours slipped past. We spoke softly, and often the Maid would glance back at me with love in her eyes, her lips teasing, though she would shake her head in playful refusal when I made to leave my paddle and go to her.

When the eighteenth hour came, we drew in the paddles. The Maid spread the cloak carefully for our bed, and after eating, we slept deeply, as if slipping into oblivion in an instant.

Eight hours later, we woke together, disoriented for a fleeting moment before remembering where we were—safe, untouched. We laughed, joyously, and after a kiss, washed in the sea’s water before breaking our fast. Then we took up the paddles again, moving steadily along the coast, peaceful and content through the day.

That voyage lasted four full days, for we did not hurry, allowing my strength to rebuild. Nothing troubled us, save once when a great beast heaved itself from the sea onto the shore, browsing on the vegetation. It did not frighten us, only made us glad for the distance between us. It seemed a natural thing, not monstrous, as if the creatures of that land belonged to some ancient order of life.

Once, we passed near a Fire-Hill rising from the sea, its base boiling, jets of flame roaring upward. Strange grunts echoed from the water as gases burst forth—a reminder of the power sleeping in that deep world. We stared back at it long after we had passed, awed.

Otherwise, the journey was uneventful. We navigated carefully where the Great Sea fractured into smaller channels, slipping through the watery passages until, on the fourth day, we reached land—a flat shore leading to the mouth of the First Gorge.

It was the tenth hour, as it had been when we began. A peaceful voyage, yet I knew greater dangers lay ahead. Still, our hearts were set; we would conquer what came, for the prize was a life together in the Mighty Home.

We grounded the raft as best we could. The Maid cut a small piece of wood from it—a keepsake—before we gathered our gear. I donned my armor once more, the Scrip and Pouch at my back, the Diskos in hand. The Maid secured her bundle (now much diminished) and her knife at her belt.

Then she knelt and kissed the raft, a silent farewell to the past. I lifted her gently, and we turned toward the dark mouth of the Gorge.

To our right, miles distant, loomed the great Mountain with its four fire-peaks, ash-hills piled below from ages of eruptions. The Maid marveled at it, as any would.

We entered the Gorge, pausing only to look back one last time at the Country of Seas—that hidden, living land in the abyss of the world. Perhaps we were the last humans who would ever see it. A solemn thought.

Then we rounded the bend, and the darkness swallowed us.

For five days, we journeyed through that desolate place. We kept to sixteen hours of travel, eight of sleep, careful not to overtire. The Gorge was lifeless but for the gas-fires, their moans and whistles the only sounds in that vast silence. The Maid often asked when we would reach the Night Land, her excitement growing, and I shared her eagerness—though beneath it lay a fear that some final disaster might yet strike.

On the fifth day, distant echoes reached us—the monstrous piping of the Great Gas Fountain. The sound grew, and soon we saw its flicker ahead, a blue flame dancing in the darkness. We stood at last before it, deafened by its roar, tiny in the face of that eternal force.

After a time, we turned away, beginning the long climb up the Mighty Slope. For eight days, we crawled upward through unending night, tethered together by straps, blind and numbed by the dark. Often, I paused, calling the Maid to me, holding her close for comfort before we pressed on.

And so we left the Flame behind, ascending into the blackness, toward the terror and wonder of the Night Land.
