While eating and drinking, we calculated my notes of the outward way and decided to limit our daily journey through the Gorge to sixteen hours, sleeping always for eight good hours. We adhered to this schedule until we emerged from the Great Gorge, a process taking five days.

As we entered the lighter parts of the Gorge, we became more cheered, though often we were half-smothered by the horrid gases rising from various spots. My strength grew constant as we journeyed. The Maid never suffered me to carry her; instead, she moved lightly and cleverly, becoming adept at this constant wandering.

From time to time, I stopped to show the Maid where I had slept, urging her to stand where I lay so lonely during my outward search. Always, she treated me with utter tenderness, lacking speech due to the calling of her heart. She constantly asked when we would reach the Night Land, how far it was, and expressed a growing, natural excitement. In truth, I felt almost as excited as she, wondering what she thought of the Mighty Redoubt and all the strange, monstrous lands. Above all, I was shaken to my heart by the thought of bringing the Maid swiftly to safety, lest some woe befall us despite our progress. This made it difficult to rush beyond our set hours, yet we possessed wisdom in this matter and always slept after the sixteenth hour.

We saw no life in the great, desolate Gorge—only gas-burnings, boulders, stark rocks, and the rank smells of gases. An utter, everlasting quiet prevailed, broken only by lone gas-fires moaning or whistling. The whistling echoed drearily across the waste, and

Behold, I drew near unto the towering bulk of the Great Pyramid, and my sanctuary, that vast abode of sweet life and safety, ascended higher into the endless night. It appeared to me then as a mountain of immense proportions, a terrifying majesty that ought to have filled me with awe anew. Yet, such was the grip of despair and exhaustion upon my spirit that I felt no wonder at its scale. All that mattered to me was the presence of Mine Own Maid within the refuge of that colossal shelter. And it remained distant, impossibly far.

So I pressed forward across the desolate land with a heavy, determined stride. As I passed a sunken hollow where a fire-hole burned with a sickly heat, something emerged from the depths. It crawled upward, rising into the darkness, revealing itself to be a great, hairy man. He looked upon me, then advanced, his hands thrusting forward with frantic eagerness. In the gloom of the fire-hole, I beheld these hands clearly; they were monstrous, armored with terrible, jagged claws, capable of rending flesh like a wild beast.

With a swift motion, I dropped my burden to the ground. I cared not for life, nor for anything else; this creature delayed me, and my despair flared into a fierce rage that nothing should hinder me. Leaping forward with cold fury, I struck at the giant, but he sidestepped instantly, vanishing into the shadow cast by the dancing fire. Before I could recover, he flung out his massive arm from the half-light, snatching my helmet from my head with brutish strength and casting me down a dozen feet onto the hard earth. Yet, I suffered no mortal harm, only severe shaking and bruises. I rose immediately, closing in upon the giant, swinging the Diskos as it roared and blazed in my hands. I brought the weapon down upon his midsection, and the Diskos glutted itself, piercing through the giant as if he were non-existent, despite his size and strength. His shoulders twisted as he fell, his upper body crashing to the earth while his legs and trunk stood stark in the light of the fire-hole, blood gushing upward like a fountain into the night.

I made no pause, leaping to reclaim Mine Own Maid, holding her tight in my arms, and surged onward past the fallen carcass, which gave a final, horrible groan. Suddenly, the night seemed to swell with astonishment and uplifted spirits; the spiritual cries of the Millions swarmed around me, telling me they had witnessed this, pouring their love and delight into me, generating a vast excitement.

Scarcely a mile further, however, two vague shapes emerged from a dark recess where rocks jutted upward. I smote them with the Diskos and pressed on, but I never learned what manner of beings they were. After that, it seemed I was smiting forever. Strange things crept from the bushes and rocks, time and again, as if the entire land teemed with foul and monstrous life. I moved as in a dream, smiting and speeding forward with an increasingly fierce despair, for surely the end of our lives had arrived, and I lacked the power to save Mine Own Maid.

All the land rang with grim, monstrous roarings and odd, deadly, lowering sounds. Once, I heard the thunderous tread of giants running. The night was pure evil. By what miracle I was not slain by some dread force, I could not know, save perhaps because I had been burned free of all weakness that an Evil Power could exploit, having endured a bitter, monstrous training.

Then, again, the deep and dreadful baying of the Night-Hounds echoed from the South-East, growing nearer. I knew then that no strength of my own could protect Mine Own. From the upper reaches of the night, where the Last Light lingered, a strange blue flash descended, striking the land toward the South-East. Again it flashed, perhaps a score of times, accompanied by a peculiar crackling sound—less than the thunder of this age, yet louder than any other sound ever heard. I knew then that the Humans were beginning to fight for me, to ensure I brought Mine Own safely home.

It seemed as if all the wakefulness of the land had been mere sleep compared to the awakening now coming. Surely the night rocked with the roarings of the Monsters and the stirring of Great Forces. Over the land went the yowling of that strange, dreadful laughter issuing from the hidden country of the lost East. The hoarse, dreadful baying of the Hounds became constant, signaling a mighty pack abroad. They seemed no more than a mile away to the South-East, and I stood alone, save for the dying Maid clutched in my arms. I looked vainly and with despair for the Hundred Thousand who had been prepared to come to my aid, descending as you know they did. Truly, there was nothing to be seen but strange lights and shadows, the movement of monstrous life here and there. The Hounds drew nearer with every passing moment, and I knew death was very near.

I ceased not from my stride; I began to run. The Pyramid was not far off in the night, and the shine of the Circle surrounding it was plainly visible, save where it was hidden strangely. A despairing hope flamed within me that I might yet reach the safety of the Circle with Mine Own. The baying of the Hounds grew ever nearer. It was a doubly hideous bitterness to lose My Dear One so near to Home; the great Mountain of my Home rose upward before me into the night, seeming so close that I was almost there, yet perhaps two great miles away. Behold, I cried out in vain despair, calling for aid, asking why none came to help me in this extremity. The Hounds bayed now but half a mile to my left, surely having scent of me by their dreadful cries.

Truly, the Millions felt an anguish of sympathy for me; the spiritual noise of their emotion was plain to my spirit. They surely saw and interpreted the way I looked about me, appearing to call out in despair. There came all about me in a moment the companionship of a great and sweet spiritual force, born of their quick going with me in their understanding and love. They perceived how I was unto the end of hope, and the Hounds were almost upon me.

In this moment, there came afresh to my hearing the shaking beat of the Earth-Current. I knew the Humans were taking desperate measures to save us. To my left, a vast pack of Hounds appeared, running at a great pace, their heads low, so great as horses, seen plainly and again in shadow in the same moment as they approached. In verity, I knew that we two would be dead indeed within a minute if the Humans did not haste. I stood where I was; there was no use to run. I looked from the Hounds to the Mighty Pyramid, and again to the Hounds. Again I looked with hope gone to the Pyramid, for the Hounds were scarce two hundred fathoms off, and there were hundreds of the mighty beasts. Even as I looked that last time toward the Pyramid, a monstrous bursting flame rushed downward from the sealed lower part of the Mighty Pyramid. The flame smote downward upon the land where the Hounds ran, and all the night was lost from my sight in the brightness and strangeness of that mighty flame. I saw no more the Pyramid or anything else, only the shining, dreadful glory of that flame. The Flame made a blast in the night, a hotness that seemed to wither me even where I stood apart from it. I perceived that the Humans had truly turned loose the Earth-Current upon the Hounds to save us. There went a constant great thundering over the land, as the Earth-Force rended and split the air and tore up the earth. The roaring of the monsters was hushed and lost in that mighty sound. I saw no place where the Hounds were, only flames and broken lands where the Earth-Force had struck; great rocks were hurled everywhere with a vast noise. Truly, it was a mercy I was not slain a hundred times by the failings and burstings of great rocks and boulders.

Lo! in a moment the Humans cut off the Earth-Force, regaining control. There seemed now a great silence upon the land, an utter dark, save for flames and noise coming from where the Current had struck. Very speedily I freed myself from the daze that held me and resumed my running. For truth, it seemed I should yet be allowed to win unto safety with Mine Own. My eyes grew present to their accustomed use, and I looked all ways about me, lest something come upon me to work our deaths. For a good time there was naught that I saw anywhere, nor the wakeful sounds of the land, save only the grim and horrid laughter from afar in the dead East.

Oft as I ran, I stared hunger-hearted upward at the Mighty Pyramid. Surely it always now seemed less bright than before. At first, I set this to the count that my eyes were yet dazed by the great Flame of the Earth-Force; but soon I perceived it was otherwise—that there was truly a lessening of the light shining throughout all the Mighty Redoubt. This lack I conceived owed to the great using of the force and power of the Earth-Current that had been loosed to save us. I had this new thought cold upon my heart: truly, if the Force of the Current were made over low, there would be a danger for all the Humans that lived, even for all the great Millions of the Mighty Refuge. When consciousness returned to me, I realized I was lying on that same bed, having ascended the Lift from the silent void. It seemed impossible that I would ever need rest again, nor return to the realm of Silence from whence I had risen. A vague, strange sensation filled me as the deep, subterranean organs of the world began to throb, sending a thunderous resonance upward. It sounded like a distant, ethereal music playing beyond the threshold of death—a rolling chant sung by multitudes across far-off mountains, sometimes like a low wind blowing from the Deep, other times resolving into the ancient, majestic melody of the Song of Honour. I understood, though in a dreamlike state, that the millions living beneath those depths were celebrating this miracle of joy. Yet, everything remained faint, half-hidden behind the heavy lids that refused to fully open. I felt as though I were floating on strange, unreal waters. Around me, however, drifted sweet and lovely odors, tangible and real, emanating from the vast fields where flowers grew eternally along the passages of the ascending Lift.

Perhaps I stirred slightly, for then the voice of the Master Doctor came, low and gentle, bidding me rest. He assured me that the Maid was safe. Soon after, I drifted into a haze, existing in a blur of days where I half-lived and half-slept, wondering without care if I were truly alive or dead. Then came a period of profound quietude, where I thought of nothing. The Master Doctor often leaned over me, scrutinizing my face with intense focus. Finally, after what seemed like endless stretches of time, another figure bent over me. It was the face I loved most—the beloved, lovely countenance of Mine Own. Her eyes spoke volumes of love into my soul, yet she remained calm and hushed. Slowly, life began to return to my body. I made a clumsy movement with my hands, and she gently took them, holding them close. From her, warmth and vitality flowed back into me. She was ever wordless and tender, and as contentment took root within me, a natural slumber claimed me.

A day arrived when I was permitted to rise. Those who cared for me carried me to one of the Quiet Gardens within the Pyramid, setting me down and seemingly leaving me alone. Then, a figure emerged from behind a bush, looking at me for a moment with a shy hesitation. However, the radiant love shining in her eyes rendered that shyness insignificant. Truly, I knew it was Mine Own, my Maid, yet I had never before seen her dressed with such grace and prettiness. Looking at her, I realized she was far more dainty and beautiful than I could have ever imagined. Suddenly, I attempted to rise and approach her, driven by some natural impulse, but she quickly ran to me, stopping my foolish movement. She sat beside me, resting my head against her bosom. She did not refuse me her lips, offering instead a dual comfort as both a devoted maid and a nurturing mother in that single, perfect moment.

Afterward, she urged me to remain perfectly still. We sat together in an utter, speechless happiness until the attendants returned. With them came the Master of the Doctors, whose face bore a trace of satisfaction. From that day forth, I saw Mine Own daily. I recovered with astonishing speed, for Love itself healed me. Soon, I was allowed to descend to the Fields, though via private routes, for the multitudes might otherwise have swarmed around me, needing solitude to heal. The Maid accompanied me. Both the Master Monstruwacan and the Master of the Doctors had agreed to this arrangement, arranging for an Officer of Marriage to unite us quietly and simply. I was still too weak for the grand Public Wedding, which would come later—when, indeed, the millions bestowed upon us a Guard of Honour stretching eight miles high from the summit to the base of the Mighty Pyramid. That ceremony would await its proper time, a tribute the people could not deny themselves in honoring me.

Surely, the Maid stayed with me always, becoming my wife and the source of my enduring strength. As I regained perfect health, we wandered freely through the immense Fields, walking paths dedicated to lovers, situated near the scattered villages. These villages were truly colossal, akin to countries in size. We hid our names to avoid being besieged by the natural curiosity and kindness of others; we needed to be utterly together and quiet. We sought out spots for our slumbers where the floral beauty was most wondrous, carrying provisions with us, though we also ate whenever we reached a village. Mine Own kept her promises a hundredfold, preparing me hearty, generous meals. She teased me relentlessly about my gluttony as I ate, kissing me away just as I might have found words to defend myself. Truly, she was everything my heart and spirit desired; she companionship me with love and brought joy into my spirit.

Once, we ventured downward into the Country of Silence, though we did not stay long, for my memories began to return. In future times, we wandered there often, accompanied by memory, holiness, and great thoughts, bound by a love that holds all. As we left that land, I told Mine Own how, when her life had been suspended by the horrid force of the House, I had suffered dreadful pain, fearing I would never wake to discover her kissing me while I slept. She blushed with lovely modesty, unaware that I had been conscious of her sweet affection during her supposed death. She had thought entirely of my agony when she believed herself lost to the vapors of the Earth-Force.

She came to me with a dear understanding. The doctors explained that she had been stunned and frozen in spirit, her entire being and life suspended. Only the great life-force of the Earth-Current had awakened her spirit, allowing her body to live again and her blood to flow properly. The physicians had spoken and searched deeply in the ancient records of their work, finding mention of one such occurrence in olden times, but nothing comparable throughout a mighty age of years. While wandering and resting in the Fields, I often recounted matters to Mine Own. I knew she had learned some odd things before I recovered, though not excessively, for she, too, had been in terrible straits. She had risen from her bed while I lay so still, an order given by the Master Doctor who feared I would truly die without some means to awaken my spirit. In truth, you must consider the depth of my love as I know she held my hands so bravely and gently, barely able to support herself. I offered a little holy praise for Mine Own.

Thus, I come to this conclusion, with one final tale to share. This happened a while later, after we had undergone the second marriage—the solemn Public Ceremony. One day, my wife, Mine Own, took me with a sweet cunning to the Hall of Honour. Upon arrival, I saw many of the peoples standing in silence within that vast hall, waiting for something significant. My wife walked forward with me to the center of the hall. Suddenly, I understood why she had led me there with such sweetness. Standing in the midst of the Hall, in the Place of Honour, stood a statue of a man in broken armor, forever carrying a maid.

I fell silent. How can this age comprehend the honor this meant? Such honors were reserved solely for the Great Dead. I was but a young man, far removed from greatness, save for the fact that I loved with all my heart and spirit, making death seem small compared to love. You must understand how love makes the heart sweet and brave, granting humility, wonder, and a natural pride that someone would think to honor me so. Mine Own wept with joy and honest pride at her husband's side. An utter silence of dear sympathy filled the great Hall. Those present allowed us to depart in quietness, a lovely testament to mutual understanding.

I walked lovingly and thoughtfully with my wife, staying near her. I had gained honour, yet I learned a vital lesson: honour is but the ash of life unless it is fueled by love. And I had love. To possess love is to possess everything, for true love mothers honour and faithfulness, and these three elements build the House of Joy.
