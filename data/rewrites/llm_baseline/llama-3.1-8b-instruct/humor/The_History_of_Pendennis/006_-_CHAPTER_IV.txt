As the afternoon sun cast its warm glow over the George, Mr. Foker and his companion settled into a leisurely repast in the coffee-room, surrounded by the familiar sights and sounds of the inn. The aroma of roasting meats wafted through the air, mingling with the hum of conversation and the clinking of glasses. Mr. Rincer, the inn's stalwart servant, presented the first dish with a bow, his face a picture of gravity, as if he were serving royalty itself. Mr. Foker, ever the connoisseur, attacked the turtle and venison with relish, pronouncing the champagne to be an affront to the senses, a travesty of true flavor, and winking knowingly at the port, which he deemed worthy of his refined palate. As he savored each bite, he regaled the waiters with tales of their families, inquiring about the well-being of their loved ones, and playfully mimicking the toot of the carriage horns as the London coaches departed, their occupants waving farewell to the gathered crowd.

"A bottle of sherry, a bottle of sham, a bottle of port, and a dash of fine wine – it's a tolerable spread, don't you think, Pen?" Foker mused, surveying the spread before him. "A feast fit for kings, or at the very least, a gathering of discerning gentlemen such as ourselves." As they finished their meal, Pen rose, his eyes aglow with excitement, and they made their way to the theatre, where they encountered Mrs. Dropsicum, Bingley's mother-in-law, a renowned patroness of the arts, known for her role as Lady Macbeth in the current production. Foker, ever the diplomat, greeted her with a courteous nod, exchanging pleasantries as they made their way to their seats.

Upon entering the theatre, they found themselves among a select group of patrons, including Lieutenants Rodgers and Podgers, and young Cornet Tidmus, all of whom seemed to be in high spirits, engaged in lively conversations with the performers between scenes. Bingley, the manager, took to the stage, his impressive physique and flair for drama commanding attention as he portrayed the Stranger, resplendent in his elaborate costume, complete with tight-fitting pantaloons, Hessian boots, and a magnificent cloak adorned with a beaver hat and a hearse feather.
