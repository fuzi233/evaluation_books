As the last wisps of winter's chill dissipated, the days began to stretch towards spring, yet the promise of renewal was tempered by the weight of unfulfilled expectations. Mrs. Thornton's absence from the house had become a habit, her visits reduced to rare and formal encounters with Mr. Hale in the study. Mr. Thornton himself appeared infrequently, his conversations with Margaret's father focused on the intricacies of his business affairs, which had grown increasingly complex since the strike. Though Margaret sensed no animosity in his words, she couldn't help but feel a pang of sorrow at the strained atmosphere between them.

In the midst of this somber tranquility, Margaret found solace in the gentle routine of teaching the younger Boucher children. Her efforts at kindness and compassion, though labored, brought her a sense of purpose, even if her heart remained heavy with the weight of unshed tears. Her father, too, benefited from her quiet ministrations, and Margaret reveled in the knowledge that she was able to offer him comfort in his moments of doubt.

As the days passed, the news of Frederick's marriage to Dolores arrived, accompanied by a letter from the young couple. Dolores's writing, a charming blend of Spanish and English, spoke to Margaret's heart, and she found herself drawn to the tender, girlish spirit that shone through the pages. Her father, too, was enchanted by the Spaniard's gentle nature, and together they savored the promise of a new chapter in their lives.

However, the memory of Frederick's earlier letter, written in the depths of despair after receiving news of Henry Lennox's court-martial, lingered in Margaret's thoughts. His words, though passionate and anguished, had revealed a depth of feeling she had not previously known. As she reflected on his pain, she realized that patience was the only balm that could soothe his wounded spirit. And so, she steeled herself for the long journey ahead, knowing that she would need to walk alongside him, supporting him through the trials that lay ahead.

In the silence of the evening, as the fire crackled in the hearth, Margaret's thoughts turned to the letters she had received from Frederick, and the ones that would surely follow. She smiled wistfully, thinking of Dolores's delicate touches, the way her words danced across the page like a gentle breeze on a summer's day.
