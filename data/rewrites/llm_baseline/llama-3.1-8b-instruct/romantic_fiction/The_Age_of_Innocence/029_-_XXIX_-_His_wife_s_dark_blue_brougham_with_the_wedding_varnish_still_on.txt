As the snowflakes gently fell onto the Pennsylvania terminal in Jersey City, Archer stood waiting, his eyes scanning the crowded platform for a glimpse of the Washington express. His wife's dark blue brougham, its wedding varnish still gleaming, had brought him here, and now it stood ready to convey him back to New York. The gas lamps cast a warm, golden light on the faces of the passengers, their reflections rippling like waves in the polished wood of the waiting room.

Archer's mind wandered to the dreamers who envisioned a future where trains would tunnel beneath the Hudson, connecting New York directly to the rest of the country. He smiled wryly, thinking of the fantastical predictions that filled the air: ships that would cross the Atlantic in mere days, machines that would soar through the skies, and the hum of electricity illuminating the city streets. But for now, he was content to focus on the present moment, the gentle rocking of the brougham, and the soft sound of snow falling outside.

As he strolled along the platform, his thoughts turned to Madame Olenska, and the memories of their last meeting flooded his mind. He pictured her stepping down from the train, her slender figure blending in with the crowd, her eyes locking onto his as he guided her to the carriage. The scene played out in his imagination like a cherished melody, every note perfectly pitched, every movement precise. He felt the familiar thrill of excitement as he anticipated their reunion, the gentle rustle of her skirts, the soft curve of her cheek.

The train finally pulled into the station, its cars rattling and screeching as it came to a stop. Archer pushed his way through the crowd, his eyes scanning the windows for a glimpse of Madame Olenska's face. And then, suddenly, he saw her – pale and surprised, her eyes shining like stars in the dim light. Their hands met, and he felt a jolt of electricity run through him as he took her arm.

"This way – I have the carriage," he said, his voice low and smooth.

As they made their way to the brougham, Archer couldn't help but notice the way the snowflakes danced around them, casting a magical spell over the scene. They settled into the carriage, the soft cushions enveloping them in comfort, and the world outside receding into the distance. Madame Olenska's gloved hand rested in his, her fingers cold and still.
