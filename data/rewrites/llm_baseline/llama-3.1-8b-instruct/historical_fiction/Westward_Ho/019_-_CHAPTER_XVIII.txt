As they navigated the waters westward, the air grew thick with an almost palpable sense of anticipation. Henry's words hung in the air, a gentle rebuke aimed at Falstaff's earlier outburst. "Why, what a rascal art thou, then, to praise him so for running?" Henry's tone was laced with a hint of amusement, but Falstaff merely shot back with a knowing glint in his eye, "O' horseback, ye cuckoo! but a-foot, he will not budge a foot."

The exchange was a familiar one, a dance of words between two men who had known each other for years. Yet, as they sailed deeper into the Caribbean, their conversation took on a more subdued tone. They had slipped past the southern point of Grenada under the cover of night, and now found themselves within the enchanting archipelago of islands that lay scattered across the horizon. The sea sparkled like a canvas of diamonds, its surface barely disturbed by the gentle trade winds that caressed the shore.

Amyas stood at the bow, his eyes drinking in the beauty of the tropical landscape. He had seen his fair share of wonders, but this was different – a world of untamed beauty, where the rhythms of nature seemed to pulse in harmony with the beat of his own heart. His thoughts turned to the possibilities of conquest, of claiming these islands for the crown, and the thrill of the challenge sent a shiver down his spine.

But not everyone shared his enthusiasm. Yeo, ever the pragmatist, stood at the helm, his gaze fixed on the horizon. He knew these waters well, and the perils that lay hidden beneath the surface. The memories of John Hawkins' ill-fated expedition still lingered, a cautionary tale of the dangers that awaited those who dared to challenge the might of Spain.

As they rounded a bend in the coastline, a cluster of towering peaks rose up from the sea, their rugged grandeur a testament to the power of nature. Frank's eyes widened in awe as he recognized the mountains of Macanao, the westernmost reaches of Margarita, an island famous for its pearl fisheries and a place of legend in the annals of European trade. The memory of John Oxenham's tragic fate hung heavy in the air, a reminder of the risks that came with exploring the unknown.
