As Deronda wandered through the crowded streets of London, his eyes scanned the faces of the common Jews who lived and worked there, their daily routines a far cry from the romantic visions he'd once indulged in. He'd walk alongside them, observing their lives, but found little to spark his imagination. Why didn't he seek out influential Rabbis or community leaders to inquire about the mysterious Cohen family – Mirah's mother and brother, Ezra? Perhaps after Christmas, he thought. For now, Deronda struggled to reconcile the ideals he cherished with the harsh realities of everyday life. His enthusiasm for the abstract and the poetic waned in the face of concrete challenges, leaving him feeling disconnected from the world around him.

As he navigated the dingy streets of Whitechapel, he began to notice the stark contrast between the idealized visions of the past and the mundane present. He recalled his reveries of searching for Mirah's relatives in medieval Spain, the Crusaders' cries echoing through the air like the howls of bloodhounds. Yet, in the midst of such turmoil, the image of the brave Jewish martyr stood tall, a beacon of hope and defiance. But when faced with the dull, everyday lives of the people around him, his fervor faltered. The imagination that had once soared on the wings of poetry now struggled to pierce the veil of the ordinary.

Deronda's nature inclined him towards a more expansive, empathetic understanding of the world, yet his own experiences had left him wary of sentimental sympathies. Now, with Mirah's plight weighing heavily on his heart, he began to see every Jew and Jewess he encountered as a reflection of her story – a tale of displacement, loss, and resilience. He sensed a collision brewing between Mirah's hopes and the harsh realities she might soon face, a collision that echoed the uncertainty he felt within himself. Though he couldn't quite articulate it, a nagging sense of foreboding whispered that his own path might be similarly fraught with unexpected challenges.
