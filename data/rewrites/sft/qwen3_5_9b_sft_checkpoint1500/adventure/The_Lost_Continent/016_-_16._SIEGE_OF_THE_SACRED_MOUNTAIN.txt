The crossing of the vast continent of Atlantis, though fraught with perils and marked by a lingering sense of dread, need not be chronicled in exhaustive detail. Yet, one cessation of my journey proved unavoidable—a halt necessitated by circumstance. One day, I had tracked and felled a stag in the open savannah, the chase long and arduous. I paused to scan the air, ensuring no lurking beast posed a threat within range, and deemed the location secure. I proceeded to butcher the animal, preparing a sufficient bundle of meat for transport.

However, concealed within the tall grasses lay a formidable serpent-like creature, a monstrous green entity, deadly in its bite. As I moved, I inadvertently brushed it with my heel. Like a flash of lightning, it struck, its tiny head snapping forward to drive its fangs into the lower thigh. I drew my knife, severing its neck; it collapsed, convulsing in a frenzy of twisting movements, its hundred legs coiling in grotesque knots. After cleansing my blade on the earth, I drove it deep around the wound, opening the flesh to ensure the blood flowed freely and flushed the venom from its lodging. With blood trickling steadily from my leg, I hoisted the meat onto my shoulder and strode onward, grateful to have escaped what could easily have become a tragic ordeal.

Yet, as I walked, a tightening and throbbing seized my leg, intensifying hour by hour. Soon, it swelled until the skin stretched taut like parchment. A violent sickness overtook me, rack­ing my frame so severely that had a predator of significant size fallen upon me at that moment, I would have been consumed without resistance. As twilight descended, I dragged myself up into a tree, remaining suspended in the crook of a branch through the long, painful night, wakeful and tormented.

At dawn, with the nocturnal beasts retired to their dens, I climbed down, leaning heavily on my spear. I limbed through the somber forests, guided by the moss growing on the northern sides of the trees. However, gradually, I noticed moss encircling the trunks entirely, and my vision grew dim, clouded by the agony and swelling of my limb. Undeterred, I pressed onward, driven by the final imperative of the High Council's command—to retrieve the enlargement of Nais that had been promised.

My last conscious memory was of encountering a figure in the darkened forest who aided me, after which my waking senses dissolved into oblivion.

After an indefinite interval, consciousness returned. I found myself lying on a bed of leaves nestled in a cleft between two rocks, rudimentary but fortified with stakes and structures against the intrusion of larger predators. My wound was bandaged with a poultice of herbs. Across the cavern, a woman sat squatting, cooking a stew of wood-grubs and honey over a stick-fire.

"How came I here?" I asked.

"I brought you," she replied.

"And who are you?"

"A nymph, they call me," she said, "and I practice as such. I gather herbs, heal the ailments of those who seek me, read fortunes, and make prophecies. In exchange, I accept whatever they can afford; if they fail to pay according to their means, I cast a curse to cause them to wither. It is a meager existence when war and pestilence have left so few impoverished souls to inhabit the lands."

"Do you visit Atlantis?"

"Not I. Phorenice would have me boiled in brine, living, if she could catch me. Our delicate Empress tolerates no magic save her own. Rumor holds she intends to topple the Priests from their Mountain."

"Thus, you possess news of the city?"

"Undoubtedly. It is my profession to acquire good tidings; otherwise, how could I foretell futures to the common folk? Observe, my lord, I discerned your stature by your speech. Knowing you are not one seeking spells or potions, I feel free to speak plainly."

"Speak, then: Does Phorenice still reign?"

"Most wickedly."

"As a virgin?"

"As the mother of twin sons. Tatho is now her husband, a union formed three years ago."

"Tatho! Who served as viceroy of Yucatan?"

"There is no Yucatan," she corrected. "A vast nation of diminutive, hairy men, allegedly arriving from the West, swept over the region. Armed only with wooden clubs capped in stone, their sheer numbers were their primary weapon. They desired neither plunder nor slaves, nor the conquest of cities. Their sole lust was to consume the flesh of Atlanteans, and they pursued it with ferocious vigor. Their numbers swarmed like bees.

"They assailed each city of Yucatan in rotation. Though the colonists slaughtered them by the thousands, the overwhelming weight of their multitude always prevailed. They consumed each city utterly, leaving it to the forest beasts before moving to the next. Eventually, they reached the coastal towns. Tatho and the few survivors boarded ships and sailed home. They even feasted on Tatho's wife for him. These little hairy beings must be strangely persistent. The Gods forbid them from crossing the sea to Atlantis, lest they become a plague far deadlier than Phorenice."

I recalled hearing tales of these small, hairy creatures before. While I had never seen them, I understood them to be a bridge between humanity and beastliness—creatures strictly restrained by the Gods in certain forest solitudes. Occasionally, when divine punishment required, they were released as a devastating host, devouring everything in their path. I held back from mentioning this to the nymph, viewing her as merely a crude, somewhat foolish mortal, typical of the self-proclaimed sorceresses who deceive the ignorant masses. Yet, within my heart, I was deeply grieved for the fate of that splendid colony of Yucatan, wherein I had invested infinite labor.

However, it ill suited my design to have my identity and status broadcast prematurely until the time was ripe. Thus, I diverted the conversation from Yucatan, allowing her to speak of other topics. "What of Egypt?" I inquired.

"In its customary gloom, so the rumors go. Who cares for Egypt in these later years? Who cares for anyone or anything save oneself and one's own possessions? Times were when country folk and hunters brought offerings to this cave out of pure piety. Now, they approach only if they perceive a profitable return for their gifts. Consequently, instead of living full and robust, I subsist on lean meals of honey and grubs. It is a wretched life for a nymph in these latter days, my lord. The fashion among all classes is to disbelieve in any kind of mystery."

"What sort of pestilence did you mention?"

"I have not witnessed it. Praise the Gods it has not reached this path. Yet, they say it has arisen from the populace Phorenice has slain, whose bodies remain unburied. She slays continually, causing corpses to pile thicker than birds and beasts can consume them. For what sin, I wonder, did the Gods permit Phorenice to ascend to power? I pray that she and her twins are boiled alive in brine before they obstruct an honest nymph of the forest from her livelihood."

"She claims to have placed an image of herself in every temple of the city, ordering prayers and sacrifices to be performed night and day. She has decreed all other deities inferior to herself and forbidden their worship. Those of the people insufficiently devout in her eyes have their hamstring severed by her tormentors to force a constant state of devotion."

"Shall I partake of your grubs and honey? There is nothing else. Your back was soaked with blood from carrying meat when I found you, yet you had lost your burden. You must eat this mess now, or go hungry."

I harbored with the nymph for six days. She utilized her drugs and charms to heal my leg, and upon my recovery, I hunted the plains, killing a fat, cloven-hooved horse as payment. Then, I resumed my journey.

The landscape ahead had once supported a sturdy population that expanded its holdings and constructed numerous homesteads. Most dwellings were perched in trees, escaping the cave-bears and cave-tigers, while the superior structures were built of massive logs, tightly clamped and dovetailed, rendering them immune to beastly destruction. Within such a house, owners were safe, able to repel attackers through the gaps between the logs.

Yet, not one in ten of these outlying settlers remained. When I arrived, the houses were silent, the hearths before the doors choked with weeds, and vegetable patches reclaimed by the forest into scrub and jungle. Further on, where villages emerged, strongly walled to defend against beasts, the logs barring the gateways lay scattered amidst sprouting undergrowth, with only kitchen middens remaining to testify to former human habitation. Phorenice's influence appeared to have spread like a horrific blight across what had once been a land of ease and contentment.

To date, I had encountered little interference from men. Many had fled with their women into the forest depths at the mere sight of me; others stood defiant but refrained from attacking, sensing I offered no insult first; and a handful, very few, offered shelter and provisions. However, as I approached the city, traversing muddy, beaten paths, I crossed territories densely populated. Here, the watchman atop the village tower would spot me and cry, "Behold a masterless man!" Whereupon the inhabitants would rush forth to strip me of my weapons and conscript me as a laborer.
