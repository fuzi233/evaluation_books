As Gerard and Margaret sat together in the warm glow of their evening meal, the scent of freshly baked bread wafting from the oven and the soft murmur of the nearby stream providing a soothing background melody, their joy was palpable. They had just returned from Sevenbergen, their spirits lifted by the thrill of recovered freedom and successful adventure. But beneath the surface of their happiness lay a more somber reality – Gerard's status as an escaped prisoner, and the very real danger that he might be retaken and punished.

Margaret's brow furrowed with worry as Gerard revealed his plan to leave the country for a while, drawn by the promise of Italy and the prospect of earning a name for himself as a craftsman and artist. She wished him to stay, but the thought of his going was tempered by her desire to support his dreams. Gerard, too, was torn, but his determination to prove himself in Italy won out, and he convinced Margaret to give her consent.

As they sat together, hands entwined, the shadows of night began to creep in, and the fire crackled softly. They talked long into the night, their words spilling out like a rich stew, filling the air with the savory scent of hope and longing. But just as they thought they had reached a moment of calm, a sudden chill ran through the room. Giles burst in, his face grave, bearing news that shook them both to their core.

"Brother Gerard," he exclaimed, his voice low and urgent, "Kate bids you flee for your life. They've laid a trap for you, and the parchments you took are but a ruse. A price is on your head, and danger lurks around every corner." Gerard's face went white, and Margaret's eyes widened in horror.

Giles added his own warning, his voice laced with concern. "The constables and Dirk Brower were at our house, searching for you. Kate's counsel is sound, Gerard – flee, and flee now." Margaret's cry echoed through the night air – "Oh, yes, Gerard, fly on the instant!" – as Gerard's resolve hardened.

With a newfound sense of purpose, Gerard thanked Giles and bid him farewell, instructing him to return to Kate and tell her he was gone. He then called out to Martin, asking him to ride ahead and watch the road, ready to signal Gerard's escape with an arrow shot into the oak tree in their garden.
