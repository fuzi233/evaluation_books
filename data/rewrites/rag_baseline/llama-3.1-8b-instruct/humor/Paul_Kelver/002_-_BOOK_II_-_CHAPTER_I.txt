As I sat on the low chair before the nursery fire, the flames danced across my face, casting eerie shadows on the walls. The rhythmic scratch of Mrs. Fursey's needle against her thimble created a sense of monotony, punctuated by the occasional creak of the wooden floorboards beneath my feet. At seven years old, my world was a tapestry woven from the threads of my own innocence and the whispered tales of my nurse. She was a wise woman, with a heart as warm as the fire that crackled before us, and a tongue that wielded the power to weave reality itself.

I had grown accustomed to hearing about my exceptional good fortune – how I was blessed with an early bedtime, while others suffered through sleepless nights; how I savored linseed gruel and treacle, a concoction that, in its sweet and bitter taste, reminded me of the capricious nature of fate. But today, as I listened to Mrs. Fursey's words, I began to feel a growing unease. It started as a tickle in the pit of my stomach, a nagging question that refused to be silenced. Why had my mother taken me in, if she hadn't wanted me?

The needle's rhythmic scratching ceased abruptly, and Mrs. Fursey's eyes met mine with a mixture of surprise and concern. "Took you in?" she repeated, her voice barely above a whisper. "What's the child talking about? Who's took you in?" The room seemed to shrink, the shadows deepening as the silence between us grew thicker than the smoke from the fire. I looked up at Mrs. Fursey, my eyes searching for answers, but her face offered none. The only sound was the crackling of the flames, and the distant rumble of thunder outside, a reminder that the storm that had brought me to this place still lingered, its secrets unspoken.
