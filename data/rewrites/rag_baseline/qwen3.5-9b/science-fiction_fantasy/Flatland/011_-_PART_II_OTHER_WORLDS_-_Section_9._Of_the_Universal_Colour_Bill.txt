The intellectual arts were rapidly succumbing to a terminal decay. The noble Art of Sight Recognition, rendered obsolete by shifting social priorities, ceased to be practiced; Geometry, Statics, and Kinetics, once the bedrock of our education, were swiftly relegated to the dustbin of superfluity, abandoned even within the hallowed halls of our University. Simultaneously, the lesser Art of Feeling met the same ignominious fate in our elementary schools.

Consequently, the Isosceles classes, emboldened by their newfound immunity from the burdensome tributes formerly exacted for the service of Education, swelled in number and insolence. Deprived of the disciplining influence of these duties, their brutality hardened and their populations exploded unchecked. Year by year, the Soldiers and Artisans began to roar with a terrifying conviction: that there was no distinction between them and the highest Polygons, for they too could now grapple with life's static and kinetic complexities through the singular, democratizing power of Colour Recognition.

Uncontent with mere neglect, the revolutionaries escalated their demands. They called for the legal prohibition of all "monopolizing and aristocratic Arts," demanding the abolition of endowments for Sight Recognition, Mathematics, and Feeling. Their logic was circular and absolute: since Colour, a Second Nature, had erased the need for aristocratic distinctions, the Law must follow suit, recognizing all individuals as equals entitled to equal rights. Finding the higher orders wavering, the revolutionary leaders pressed further, insisting that *all* classes—including Priests and Women—must do homage to Colour by submitting to be painted.

When objections arose regarding the lack of sides in Priests and Women, the revolutionaries retorted with cold pragmatism: Nature and Expediency dictated that the front half of every human—the hemisphere containing the eye and mouth—must be distinguishable from the hind half. Thus, a bill was drafted for presentation to the General Assembly of all Flatland States. It proposed that in every Woman, the anterior semicircle be coloured red and the posterior green. Priests were to receive the same treatment: red for the front semicircle centered on the eye and mouth, green for the rear.

This proposal bore the marks of cunning far beyond the capacity of any Isosceles, whose angularity was insufficient to conceive such a masterpiece of statecraft. Its architect was an Irregular Circle, spared from death in childhood only to breed desolation for his country and ruin for countless followers. The strategy was twofold. First, by granting Women the same dual-colour palette as the Priests, the revolutionaries ensured that, viewed from specific angles, a Woman would appear indistinguishable from a Priest. In certain positions, she would present a straight line, half-red and half-green, commanding the same reverence due to the sacred order. This prospect proved irresistible to the Female Sex, promising them a sudden elevation in status.

Consider the visual deception engineered by this legislation. A Woman, newly decorated, presents a front face of red and a back of green. Viewed from the side, she appears as a straight line divided equally by hue. Now consider a Priest, whose mouth sits at the apex of his red front semicircle, separated from his green rear by a central diameter. If one views the Great Man along this dividing line, the sight is identical: a straight line where one half is red and the other green. While the Priest's line may be slightly shorter and fade more rapidly at the edges, the chromatic signature creates an immediate, overwhelming impression of shared class. Given the concurrent decay of Sight Recognition and the rapid learning of Women to mimic the circular shape by shading their extremities, the conflation of Priest and Young Woman becomes inevitable.

How intoxicating this prospect must have been to the Frail Sex! They anticipated with delight the chaos that would ensue. Within the domestic sphere, Women could overhear political and ecclesiastical secrets meant solely for husbands and brothers, issuing commands in the name of a priestly circle. Outdoors, the stark, deceptive combination of red and green would lure the common populace into endless errors, allowing Women to seize the deference previously reserved for the Circles. Scandal and constitutional subversion were matters of no consequence to them; even within the households of the Circles, the Women rallied behind the Universal Colour Bill.

The second objective of this diabolical scheme was the systematic demoralization of the Circles themselves. Until this moment, the Nobles had maintained their pristine clarity of understanding, insulated from the start in colourless homes where the Sacred Art of Sight Recognition was cultivated. The introduction of the Colour Bill sought to shatter this insulation. By forcing the Hierarchy to submit to the pollution of Colour, the Irregular aimed to destroy their domestic environments for intellectual training. Once tainted, every parental and childish Circle would begin to demoralize one another. An infant Circle, tasked with distinguishing between Father and Mother, would face insurmountable problems of perception, easily corrupted by maternal imposture. Thus, the intellectual lustre of the Priestly Order would inevitably wane, clearing the path for the total destruction of the Aristocratic Legislature and the final subversion of our Privileged Classes.
