As the sun waned beyond the horizon, casting the small cottage in a warm orange glow, Gerard's restlessness intensified. Denys, his loyal companion, moved swiftly to alleviate his distress, disappearing into the nearby village to procure a lemon to soothe Gerard's parched throat. Though lemons were a rare commodity in these northern climes, Denys's haste was driven by the urgency of Gerard's plight, his friend's irascibility a palpable force that threatened to engulf them both.

The minutes ticked by with agonizing slowness, Gerard's impatience growing with each passing moment. Finally, the door creaked open, and Denys reappeared, accompanied by an unexpected guest. An imposing figure, draped in a long, sober gown trimmed with rich fur, strode into the room. The air was heavy with the scent of old books and leather, and Gerard's gaze was drawn to the gleaming morocco scabbard at the stranger's side. A ruff adorned his neck, stiffened by hidden wooden framework, and a four-cornered cap with a fur border perched atop his head. A majestic white beard cascaded down his chest, and Gerard's heart quickened as he recognized the unmistakable garb of a physician.

A boy trailed behind the old gentleman, carrying a basket overflowing with phials, lint, and surgical tools. The physician approached Gerard's bedside with a gentle smile, his eyes twinkling with kindness. "How fares my young patient?" he asked softly, his voice a soothing balm to Gerard's frazzled nerves.

Gerard explained that his wound was manageable, but his throat was dry and his head heavy. The physician's expression turned thoughtful, his eyes roving over the wound. "Ah, yes, a clean bite, indeed," he murmured, his voice dripping with admiration. "That mastiff had a most impressive set of teeth, I daresay." Gerard's frustration flared, and he corrected the physician, explaining that it was a bear's paw that had inflicted the wound, not a dog's jaws.

The physician's face darkened, his brow furrowed in disapproval. "Young man, think carefully before you speak," he cautioned, his voice stern but measured. "I have spent decades studying the anatomy of beasts, and I can assure you that no bear's claw has ever inflicted such a wound. You would do well to remember that."
