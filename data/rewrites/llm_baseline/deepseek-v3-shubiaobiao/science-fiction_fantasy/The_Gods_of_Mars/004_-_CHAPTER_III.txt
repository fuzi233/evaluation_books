The echoes of that terrible laughter still clung to the chamber’s walls like a curse as Tars Tarkas and I stood motionless, blades at the ready. Silence stretched between us, thick and suffocating, broken only by the distant drip of unseen water.

The Thark’s laughter was low, a sound that would chill any Earthman’s blood—not from fear, but from the grim amusement his kind took in the face of horror. I had seen it before: green Martians roaring with mirth as women and children writhed in torment during their barbaric games.

I forced a grin, though my grip on my sword tightened. "What in the name of all Barsoom is this place?"

His eyes gleamed in the dim light. "You mean you do not know?"

"I know only that I stand on Barsoom—though it feels nothing like the world I left behind."

His voice turned bitter. "After you vanished from the atmosphere plant, all believed you had taken the pilgrimage down the River Iss. To the Valley Dor, where the dead find peace. Or so the legends claim."

A cold weight settled in my chest. "Dejah Thoris—she lives?"

"She lives," he confirmed.

Relief warred with dread. "Then where are we?"

His answer was a blade of ice. "Where I hoped to find you—and another. The Valley Dor, John Carter. The promised Heaven of Barsoom."

The irony in his voice was sharper than any sword. This was no paradise. It was a slaughterhouse.

I thought of the countless souls who had journeyed here, lured by the lie of eternal rest, only to be torn apart by the very creatures we had fought. A legend spoke of one who returned, warning of monsters in the valley—but he had been slain for blasphemy.

Now we knew the truth.

A hollow voice slithered from the shadows: *"There is no hope. The dead return not."*

Then another, shrieking like a damned soul: *"Fools! Would you cheat Issus of her due?"*

Tars Tarkas growled. "Do we fight phantoms?"

"No," I said. "Only cowards who hide in the dark."

The chamber answered with a snarl. A banth, ten-legged and ravenous, lunged from nowhere. My blade found its heart—just as another beast sprang for the Thark.

They came like ghosts, materializing from the walls. But I had seen the trick.

"Watch the far wall," I hissed.

A mirror on Tars Tarkas’ harness showed the truth: a hidden door, rotating silently. Behind it, a white-skinned man with yellow hair pulled levers, unleashing horrors upon us.

Red Martians stood chained beside him, prisoners—or accomplices.

I gave the signal. We struck as the door turned, bursting into the adjoining chamber. The white man drew his pistol, but my sword sent it clattering away.

Steel rang as we dueled, his face twisting in fury. "Who are you?" he spat.

I gambled. "What if I were from the Temple of Issus?"

Fear flashed in his eyes. "Fate forfend!"

His hesitation cost him his life.

But his comrade struck from behind. Exhausted, I barely parried in time. The fight was a blur—until my heel caught on the first man’s corpse, sending me crashing to the stone.

Pain cleared my mind. My hand closed on cold metal—a discarded pistol.

The next move was mine.
