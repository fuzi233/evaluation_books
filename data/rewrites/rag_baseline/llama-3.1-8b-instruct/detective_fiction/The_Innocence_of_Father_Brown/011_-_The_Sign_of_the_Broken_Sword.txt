The frozen forest stretched out like a canvas of grey and silver, the trees' million fingers etched against a sky that shimmered like dark, polished slate. Frost had etched every inch of the landscape, making the black hollows between the trunks seem like deep, dark caverns. Even the squat stone tower of the church appeared to have been plucked straight from the rocky shores of Iceland, its rugged silhouette a testament to a harsh, unforgiving climate. It was an unusual night for anyone to venture into a churchyard, yet perhaps the biting cold made it worth braving.

A hump of green turf rose from the ashen expanse, like a verdant oasis amidst the desolate forest. The path leading to the church was as steep as a staircase, with most of the graves slanting sharply towards the earth. Amidst this somber backdrop, a single monument stood out – a masterpiece crafted by one of Europe's greatest sculptors. Its fame, however, was eclipsed by that of the man it honored: a legendary soldier whose story had captivated generations. Under the faint, silvery light of the stars, the massive metal figure of a soldier lay recumbent, his strong hands clasped in a gesture of eternal devotion, his great head pillowed on a gun. A beard, reminiscent of the heavy, Colonel Newcome style, framed the venerable face. His uniform, though simple, bore the marks of modern warfare. By his right side rested a sword, its tip broken off, while on the left lay a Bible. Summer afternoons brought tourists, their carriages overflowing with Americans and well-heeled city-dwellers, who would pause to pay their respects to the sepulcher. Yet even then, the surrounding forest, with its one domed churchyard, seemed a place shrouded in an eerie silence. Tonight, in the midst of winter's icy grip, one might expect to be left alone with the stars.

But the stillness was broken by the creaking of a wooden gate, and two figures clad in black ascended the winding path to the tomb. Their features were indistinct, save for the fact that one man was imposingly large, while the other, by contrast, seemed almost diminutive.
