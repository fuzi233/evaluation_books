They encountered the host in the hallway.

"Welcome, gentlemen," he said, removing his cap with a low bow.

"Come, we are not in Germany," Gerard remarked.

Inside the public room, they found the proprietress, a robust woman of forty. She curtsied to them and smiled warmly. "Take the trouble to sit, fair sir," she said to Gerard, dusting two chairs with her apron—a gesture unnecessary given the state of the furniture.

"Thank you, dame," Gerard replied. Internally, however, he thought, "This is a polite nation. The trouble of sitting down? I shall endure it with singular patience. And shortly thereafter, the labor of eating, the toil of digestion, and finally, by Hercules's aid, the strain of retiring to bed, and the struggle of sinking fast asleep."

"Why, Denys, what are you doing? Ordering supper for only two?"

"Why not?"

"What? Can we dine without waiting for forty others? Burgundy forever!"

"Aha! Courage, camarade. Le dia—"

"C'est convenu."

Salic law seemed to have missed the French inns entirely. In this establishment, wimples and kirtles reigned supreme; doublets and hose were few and feeble. The landlord himself wandered aimlessly, eternally tipping his cap to patrons out of absent-minded habit, while the women, as they passed, shoved him aside silently, as one brushes aside a live twig in a rushing forest.

A maid brought in supper, and the mistress followed her, empty-handed.

"Fall to, my masters," she said cheerily. "You have but one enemy here; and he lies under your knife." (I suspect this was a formula.)

They ate. The mistress drew her chair closer to the table, providing company as well as food. She gossiped genially with them like old acquaintances. Once this formality was complete, the busy dame departed, sending in her daughter, a beautiful young woman of about twenty, who took the vacant seat. She lacked the elder's breadth and geniality but possessed a gentle, cheerful nature. Upon learning the distance the poor boy had traveled and the journey he must undertake, she displayed a womanly tenderness for Gerard. She lingered nearly half an hour. When she left, Gerard observed, "Is this an inn? Why, it is like home."

"Qui fit Francois il fit courtois," said Denys, bursting with gratified pride.

"Courteous? Nay, Christian; to welcome us like home-guests and old friends—us vagrants, here today and gone tomorrow. But indeed, who deserves pity and kindness more than the weary traveler far from his folk? Hola! Here is another."

The newcomer was the chambermaid, a woman of about twenty-five, with a cocked nose, a large laughing mouth, and a sparkling black eye. Her bare arm was stout, if not particularly shapely.

The moment she entered, one of the travelers made a somewhat free jest at her expense; instantly, the entire company roared at his cost, so swiftly had her practiced tongue accomplished its business. Just as, in a fencing match between a novice and a master, foils clash—the novice is pinked. On this, another, then another, must break lance with her. But Marion planted her great arms upon her haunches and held the whole room in play. This country girl possessed in perfection that rude, ready humor which looks mean and vulgar on paper but carries all before it when spoken: not wit's rapier, but its bludgeon. Nature had done much for her, and daily practice in an inn completed the rest.

Yet shall she not be photographed by me, but feebly indicated: for it was just four hundred years ago, the raillery was coarse, she returned every stroke in kind, and though a virtuous woman, said things without winking which no decent man of our day would utter even among men.

Gerard sat gaping with astonishment. This was to him almost a new variety of *homo*. He whispered, "Denys, now I see why you Frenchmen say 'a woman's tongue is her sword,'" just then as she levelled another assailant. The chivalrous Denys, to console and support "the weaker vessel"—the iron kettle among the clay pots—administered his consigne, "Courage, ma mie, le—" etc.

She turned on him directly. "How can he be dead as long as there is an archer left alive?" (General laughter at her ally's expense.)

"It is 'washing day,' my masters," she said, with sudden gravity.

"After?" a peevish old fellow by the fireside objected. "We travellers cannot strip and go bare while you wash our clothes." He had kept mum until now but crept out into the sunshine of commonplaces.

"I aimed not your way, ancient man," replied Marion superciliously. "But since you ask me"—here she scanned him slowly from head to foot—"I trow you might take a turn in the tub, clothes and all, and no harm done" (laughter). "But what I spoke for, I thought this young sire might like his beard starched."

Poor Gerard's turn had come; his chin crop was thin and silky.

The loudest of all the laughers this time was the traitor Denys, whose beard was of a good length, and singularly stiff and bristly; so that Shakespeare, though he never saw him, hit him in the bull's eye.

"Full of strange oaths, and bearded like the pard."

Gerard bore the Amazonian satire mighty calmly. He had little personal vanity. "Nay, 'chambriere,'" said he, with a smile, "mine is all unworthy your pains; take you this fair growth in hand!" and he pointed to Denys's vegetable.

"Oh, time for that, when I starch the besoms."

Whilst they were all shouting over this palpable hit, the mistress returned, and in no more time than it took her to cross the threshold did our Amazon turn to a seeming Madonna, meek and mild.

Mistresses are wonderful subjugators. Their like, I think, breathes not on the globe. Housemaids, decide! It was a waste of histrionic ability, though; for the landlady had heard, and did not at heart disapprove, the peals of laughter.

"Ah, Marion, lass," said she good-humouredly, "if you laid me an egg every time you cackle, 'L'es Trois Poissons' would never lack an omelet."

"Now, dame," said Gerard, "what is to pay?"

"What for?"

"Our supper."

"Where is the hurry? Cannot you be content to pay when you go? Lose the guest, find the money, is the rule of 'The Three Fish.'"

"But, dame, outside 'The Three Fish' it is thus written--'Ici-on ne loge'"

"Bah! Let that flea stick on the wall! Look hither," and she pointed to the smoky ceiling, which was covered with hieroglyphics. These were accounts, vulgo scores; intelligible to this dame and her daughter, who wrote them at need by simply mounting a low stool and scratching with a knife so as to show lines of ceiling through the deposit of smoke. The dame explained that the writing on the wall was put there to frighten moneyless folk from the inn altogether, or to be acted on at odd times when a non-paying face should come in and insist on being served. "We can't refuse them plump, you know. The law forbids us."

"And how know you mine is not such a face?"

"Out fie! It is the best face that has entered 'The Three Fish' this autumn."

"And mine, dame?" said Denys; "dost see no knavery here?"

She eyed him calmly. "Not such a good one as the lad's; nor ever will be. But it is the face of a true man. For all that," added she drily, "an I were ten years younger, I'd as lieve not meet that face on a dark night too far from home."

Gerard stared. Denys laughed. "Why, dame, I would but sip the night dew off the flower; and you needn't take ten years off, nor ten days, to be worth risking a scratched face for."

"There, our mistress," said Marion, who had just come in, "said I not t'other day you could make a fool of them still, an if you were properly minded?"

"I dare say ye did; it sounds like some daft wench's speech."

"Dame," said Gerard, "this is wonderful."

"What? Oh! No, no, that is no wonder at all. Why, I have been here all my life; and reading faces is the first thing a girl picks up in an inn."

Marion. "And frying eggs the second; no, telling lies; frying eggs is the third, though."

The Mistress. "And holding her tongue the last, and modesty the day after never at all."

Marion. "Alack! Talk of my tongue. But I say no more. She under whose wing I live now deals the blow. I'm sped--'tis but a chambermaid gone. Catch what's left on't!" and she staggered and sank backwards onto the handsomest fellow in the room, which happened to be Gerard.
