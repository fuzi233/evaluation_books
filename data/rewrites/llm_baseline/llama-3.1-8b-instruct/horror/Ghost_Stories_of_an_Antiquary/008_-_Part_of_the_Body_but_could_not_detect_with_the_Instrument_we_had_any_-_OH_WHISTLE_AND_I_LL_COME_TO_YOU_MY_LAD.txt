The warm glow of the fire crackled in the grand hall of St James's College, casting a golden light on the gathering of scholars and professors. Among them was the Professor of Ontography, a man of meticulous habits and refined tastes, savoring the rich flavors of the evening's feast. As he settled into his seat beside a fellow academic, a conversation began to unfold.

"I suppose you'll be leaving soon, now that Full Term has ended," said the stranger, his voice low and soothing.

The Professor nodded, his eyes narrowing slightly as he replied, "Yes, my friends have been encouraging me to take up golf, and I plan to spend a week or ten days in Burnstow, on the East Coast, to hone my skills. I'm hoping to depart tomorrow."

A gentle smile spread across the face of his neighbor, a man with a keen interest in antiquities. "Oh, Parkins, if you're heading to Burnstow, I'd be ever so grateful if you could visit the site of the Templars' preceptory and share your thoughts with me. I've been pondering the possibility of excavating the area during the summer months."

The Professor's gaze turned thoughtful, his mind already turning to the task ahead. "Of course, I'd be happy to take note of the location and provide you with my impressions upon my return. Alternatively, I could write to you if you'd care to share your contact information."

The stranger waved his hand dismissively. "No need to trouble yourself, thank you. I simply thought that, given the rarity of thorough excavations at English preceptories, it might be an opportunity for me to contribute something meaningful during my downtime."

As the Professor raised an eyebrow, his neighbor continued, "The site itself is situated near the beach, not far from the Globe Inn at the northern end of town. The sea has encroached significantly, as you know, and I estimate it's roughly three-quarters of a mile from the inn. Where do you plan to stay?"

The Professor's eyes lit up with amusement as he replied, "Ah, I've actually booked a room at the Globe Inn myself. It's the only available accommodation, as most of the lodging houses are closed during the winter. My room is a double-bedded affair, but they've nowhere to store the second bed, so I'm afraid it's a bit cramped."
