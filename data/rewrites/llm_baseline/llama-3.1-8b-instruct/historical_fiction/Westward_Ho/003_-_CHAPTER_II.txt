As the bells of Bideford's ancient church tolled two hours beyond the usual hour, their sonorous peals echoed through the town's flower-strewn streets, where the inhabitants, resplendent in their finest attire, thronged about in jubilation. For Amyas Leigh, a young man of unassuming demeanor, had returned home after a five-year absence, his name etched in the annals of history as a participant in the epochal voyage of Sir Francis Drake around the world. The streets were alive with the hum of excitement, as if the very heavens themselves had ordained this moment of reunion. Garlands of flowers adorned the buildings, tapestries fluttered in every window, and the ships in the harbor, their flags aflutter, blared out their joyous fanfare through the firing of ordnance of every caliber.

The streets were a kaleidoscope of color, a veritable garden of delights, as seafarers, merchants, and their ladies mingled in a swirl of merriment. Sir Richard Grenville's manor house was a hub of activity, its doors flung open to receive the returning hero, as grooms and serving-men scurried about, attending to the needs of the revelers. The churchyard, packed with the fair ladies and stately gentlemen of North Devon, seemed to be a living embodiment of the region's storied past, when the nobility of England held sway by virtue of their martial prowess, beauty, and intellect.

Among the crowd, the Countess of Bath, escorted by Sir Richard Grenville, stood out, her dignity and poise a testament to her noble lineage. Nearby, the Bassets of Umberleigh, the Carys of Clovelly, and the Fortescues of Wear, Buckland, and other estates, mingled with the Coffins of Portledge, the St. Legers of Annery, and the Coplestones of Eggesford, all eager to catch a glimpse of the young man whose exploits had captured the imagination of the nation. Sir John Chichester of Ralegh, a patriarchal figure, led his entourage, comprising his eight daughters and three sons, including the eldest, who would one day avenge his murdered brother and rule Ireland wisely as Lord Deputy and Baron of Belfast.
