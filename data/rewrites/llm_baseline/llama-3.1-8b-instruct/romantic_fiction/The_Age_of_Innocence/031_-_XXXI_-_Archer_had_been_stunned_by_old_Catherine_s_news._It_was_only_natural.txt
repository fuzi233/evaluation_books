As he stood outside Mrs. Mingott's townhouse, Archer couldn't shake off the feeling of unease that had settled within him since Catherine's announcement. It was only natural that Madame Olenska would have hastened back to New York upon receiving her grandmother's summons, but her decision to remain under the same roof, especially now that Mrs. Mingott's health had begun to recover, was more perplexing.

Archer was certain that Madame Olenska's choice hadn't been driven by the change in her financial situation. He knew the precise amount of her husband's allowance, a sum that barely sufficed to cover the most basic necessities, even before the loss of Medora Manson's support. Yet he was convinced that Madame Olenska hadn't accepted her grandmother's offer out of self-interest. Her history of reckless generosity and extravagant spending habits suggested otherwise.

Madame Olenska was accustomed to living a life of luxury, but she had always demonstrated a willingness to make do without the finer things. Her relatives, the Mingotts and the Wellands, often lamented her lack of concern for appearances, but Archer believed her indifference to material possessions stemmed from a deeper place. Several months had passed since her allowance was cut off, and she had shown no inclination to mend fences with her grandmother. If she had altered her course, it must be for a different reason altogether.

Archer's thoughts turned to the conversation they'd shared on the way from the ferry. She'd spoken of their need to remain apart, her words spoken softly against his chest. He knew there was no calculated coquetry behind her words; she was struggling to uphold her resolve, just as he was, to adhere to the expectations of those who trusted them. Over the past ten days, however, she may have sensed his reluctance to act, his failure to make any overtures towards seeing her again. A glimmer of doubt might have crept in, and she may have realized that, after all, it was easier to follow the path of least resistance.

Just an hour ago, when he'd rung the bell, Archer had felt a sense of clarity about his next steps. He intended to speak with Madame Olenska alone, or failing that, to ask her grandmother about her departure plans. He would then join her on her journey to Washington, or further still, to Japan, where he envisioned a future together. His note to May would ensure that any other options were closed off.
