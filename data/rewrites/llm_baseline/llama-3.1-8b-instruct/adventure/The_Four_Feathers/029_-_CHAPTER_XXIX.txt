As the desert sun beat down on the zareeba, casting a golden glow over the makeshift encampment, Trench's eyes fluttered open. His body felt heavy, weighed down by a persistent fever that had taken hold of him like a relentless specter. Feversham, his loyal companion, hovered anxiously beside him, concern etched on his face.

"It's just a matter of time," Feversham said softly, trying to reassure himself as much as Trench. "He'll be back on his feet in no time."

But Ibrahim's somber expression told a different tale. "It's Umm Sabbah, my friend," he whispered, his voice barely audible over the din of the prison's daily routine. "The typhus fever has claimed many victims within these walls. It's a cruel mistress, taking her toll on the young and the old alike."

Feversham's eyes darted nervously towards the river, where Abou Fatma stood watch in his blue robe, a constant presence that seemed to hold the key to their fate. Each day, the man made his sign, and each day Feversham had given no answer. The uncertainty gnawed at him, but he pushed it aside, focusing on the task at hand – saving Trench's life.

Together, Feversham and Ibrahim tended to Trench with tender care. They brewed cooling draughts from tamarinds, dates, and roots, and carried him from shade to shade as the sun traversed the zareeba. As Trench's condition worsened, they sought to alleviate his suffering, providing him with nourishing vegetable marrow soaked in salt water and packing his mouth with soothing butter. The camel-cloths wrapped tightly around his body offered what little comfort they could.

As the days passed, the fever raged on, its grip tightening with each tick of the clock. On the seventh day, Ibrahim declared, "This is the final test. Tonight, he will succumb."

But Feversham refused to accept defeat. His thoughts turned to the memories they had shared beneath the stars, the simple pleasures of life that Trench had savored in his own village. "No," he whispered, his voice laced with determination. "Not here. Not in this place."
