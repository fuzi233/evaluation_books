As the sun dipped below the treetops of the Surrey hills, casting long shadows across the stone-flagged terrace, Lieutenant Sutch made his way to Broad Place, General Feversham's residence. The warm glow of the setting sun danced across the old red-brick house, nestled among the dark pine trees like a jewel. Sutch limped up the path, his leg still stiff from the lingering effects of a long-forgotten injury, and stepped onto the terrace where General Feversham sat, his eyes fixed on the rolling hills of Sussex beyond.

"How's the leg holding up?" General Feversham asked, his voice firm and commanding, as he rose from his chair with an effortless ease that belied his advanced years. His small, wiry frame seemed almost boyish, despite the wisps of white hair that flecked his head and the sharp lines etched into his face. His steel-blue eyes, however, remained impassive, giving away nothing of the thoughts that lay behind them.

"It's been a bit creaky lately," Sutch replied, taking a seat beside his host. As they sat there, the scent of blooming lavender wafted up from the gardens below, mingling with the sweet fragrance of fresh-cut grass. In the distance, the hum of bees and the chatter of birds filled the air, punctuated by the occasional clanging of pots and pans from the kitchen, where General Feversham's cook was no doubt preparing the evening's feast.

"I'm glad you're here," General Feversham said, his gaze drifting back to the horizon. "It's been too long since we've spoken." He paused, lost in thought, as the warm sunlight cast a golden glow across his face. "This view has always held a special place in my heart," he said finally, his voice low and contemplative. "My wife used to sit here for hours, lost in her own thoughts. She had a way of finding magic in the simplest things – a wide expanse of sky, a patchwork field, a solitary tree standing tall against the wind."

Sutch nodded, a smile playing on his lips. "Yes, she had a gift for seeing the world in a way that few others do." As they sat there, the silence between them grew comfortable, punctuated only by the occasional chirp of a bird or the rustle of leaves in the gentle breeze.
