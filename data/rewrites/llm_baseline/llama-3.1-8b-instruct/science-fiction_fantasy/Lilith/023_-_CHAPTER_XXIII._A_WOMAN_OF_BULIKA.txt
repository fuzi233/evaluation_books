As I stepped into the alley, the moonlight dancing through the narrow passageway illuminated the worn stones beneath my feet. I paused beneath an archway, my eyes scanning the darkness beyond. It was then that a woman slipped into the shadows beside me, her breathing quick and shallow. We stood there, frozen, as a massive leopard emerged from the night, its coat a mesmerizing mosaic of white and dark spots. The woman's body trembled against mine, and I instinctively wrapped my arm around her.

"Don't worry," I whispered. "If the leopard comes near, I'll distract it. You should run."

She nodded, her voice barely audible. "Thank you."

We stood there in silence for a moment, the leopard's presence a palpable force between us. "Have you seen this creature before?" I asked finally.

"Yes," she replied, her voice still shaking. "It belongs to the princess. You're a stranger, or you wouldn't recognize it."

"I'm a stranger," I acknowledged. "But why is it allowed to roam free?"

"It's kept in a cage, muzzled and shod in crocodile leather. But sometimes it breaks free, and... it hunts children." Her words trailed off, and she began to sob.

I gently guided her to a nearby bench, and we sat together in the darkness. "I wish I were home," she wept. "The princess returned last night, and now the leopard's on the prowl. What if it comes for me?"

I listened attentively, trying to offer what comfort I could. "All strangers aren't cruel," I said softly. "And I'll stay with you until you're safe inside."

She looked up at me, her eyes red-rimmed. "Take me home, and I'll give you shelter from this wind. But we need to wait a little longer."

As we waited, she shared stories about her life in Bulika. The people, she explained, spent most of their time excavating precious stones from their cellars. They were wealthy, but it was a peculiarity of their culture that they didn't believe in manual labor. Everything they needed was crafted elsewhere, and they relied on their ancestors' savings to sustain them.

"Why don't you work?" I asked, intrigued.

"Working is considered a shame," she replied. "In Bulika, we believe our ancestors have provided for us. We just sell a few of our gems when we need money."
