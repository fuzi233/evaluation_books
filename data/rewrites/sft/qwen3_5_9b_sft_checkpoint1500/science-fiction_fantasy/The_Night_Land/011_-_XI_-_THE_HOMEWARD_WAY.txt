After the suffocating, solitary gloom of the ancient seabed, the landscape seemed impossibly light. I realized I had emerged onto land situated to the right of my original point of entry. Before me stretched a vast expanse dotted with numerous fire-holes. A profound sense of relief washed over me, yet prudence dictated caution; as anyone knows, these fires often attract dangerous life.

I looked down at the Maid, who gazed up at me. She approached, appearing remarkably beautiful and sweet, though her pallor and evident exhaustion struck me deeply. Guilt gnawed at me for overexerting her, for I felt as strong and unyielding as forged iron, while she remained delicate and tender. Yet, she dismissed my self-reproach, standing close and gazing at me with eyes of striking beauty. I wrapped my arms around her and kissed her, then scanned the terrain to chart our next course.

From this vantage point, the blue shimmer I had spotted at the mouth of the Upward Gorge spread out before me, distant but visible. It was this celestial glow that had guided us across the ancient sea floor. While it offered only a broad directional vector, it served its purpose: it confirmed I was moving toward the far side of the seabed, preventing aimless circling in the dark.

After deliberation, I located the approximate position of the Gorge to my left, though not directly. I noted the ominous red glow of the Giant's Hole lying far to that direction. My strategy became clear: I must navigate wide enough to miss the Giants entirely, while simultaneously avoiding proximity to the blue shimmer, which I mistrusted.

As I settled my arm around the Maid, she stood quietly, observing the surroundings. Her familiarity with the land quickly surfaced as she asked for my intended path. "Straight," I replied, "but keeping a safe distance from both the blue shine and the Giant's red pit."

She directed my gaze along the proposed route. There, amidst the darkness, appeared fire-holes emitting a sickening green luminescence. Naani explained that a toxic gas tract flowed through this sector—a fact verified by the instruments of the Lesser Redoubt. Wherever this gas traveled, the fire-holes glowed green. This corridor extended far to the northwest, leading directly to the area where the great blue shimmer burned. When I asked how they named it, she replied simply, "The Shine."

My companion, Mine Own, urgently warned me against venturing closer to The Shine. She reasoned that the Fixed Giants dwelled within its borders, concealed by the light except when rolling mists revealed them. Recalling the monstrous visage I had witnessed amidst the bright smoke upon my first arrival, I suspected these entities were akin to the Great Watchers guarding the Mighty Pyramid—forces of immense and terrible evil.

Naani further clarified the geography, pointing out the extent of the gas trail. However, a dilemma arose: adhering strictly to the path away from The Shine and the gas would likely prevent me from reaching the Mouth of the Gorge. Suddenly, she recalled how I had previously crossed the land searching for her. I had somehow walked along the far side of the gas trail, eventually reaching the ancient seabed, completely unaware of the hazard.

Her solution was immediate and brilliant. We would descend back into the bed of the Olden Sea, traverse it south-westward beneath the shoreline, bypass the gas entirely, and then ascend again to enter the Gorge safely. Avoiding the Giant's domain and the volatile zones, this route promised a swift exit.

It was a sound plan, exactly what I would have devised, though the Maid's agility and foresight were evident. It was sweet to witness her mind working, her voice sounding as she planned, revealing the depth of her humanity and kindness. To share every thought and action with her was a privilege I cherished.

However, nearly seventeen hours had passed since our last sleep, and the Maid was utterly exhausted. I advised a rest soon, urging us to find a nearby fire-hole slightly northward, where we could recover strength before continuing.

We approached the fire-hole, finding it farther away than anticipated. It burned with a fierce, red glow rising from a rocky hollow. As we neared, I signaled silence, donned the Diskos from my hip, and knelt, beckoning Mine Own to follow suit. From the edge of the hollow, we peered downward. It was a tremendous fire, yet strangely devoid of monsters, bringing a fleeting peace to my heart. Still, caution prevailed; living things congregated around these fires, and danger lurked.

For a long time, we listened keenly into the night, hearing no disturbance in the air or aether. Yet, the Maid, chilled to the bone, begged to descend into the hollow for warmth. Though my head screamed warnings, the shivers tearing through her body compelled my heart to act. Against all logic, we descended swiftly toward the flames.

Strangely divided, I found myself seeking fire despite fearing it, a contradiction born of prolonged exposure to the Land's horrors. Experience taught me to avoid these pits, yet the biting cold rendered those lessons abstract compared to the physical reality of freezing. Just as we reached the fire, the true weight of the dangers returned to my consciousness.

Scanning the rocks at the bottom of the hollow, I found no large creatures, only three snakes and two scorpion-like beasts, motionless in their holes. They harbored a mortal love for the heat and would undoubtedly strike during sleep. Thus, sleeping near the fire was unwise, though I decided not to alarm the Maid, wishing her happiness and rest. If she insisted on remaining, I would ensure her obedience for her safety, for I loved her and meant always to protect her.

Presently, the Maid grew warmer. She slipped a scrip from my shoulder, providing food and drink. We sat together, eating and drinking. As she consumed her second tablet, I sensed the lingering shame of the whipping I had administered earlier; indeed, she was wholly mine.

While we ate, I constantly scanned the surroundings. Soon, the Maid noticed my vigilance, turning sharply to look behind her. In a moment, she spotted a snake moving among the rocks. Panic seized her, and she demanded we find a secure location away from the creeping things.

We sought refuge and found a small cave nestled in an upright rock within the hollow. Perhaps a hundred feet from the fire, the cavern was dry, sweet-smelling, and free of inhabitants or hiding spots for pests. Inside, it was a cozy haven, illuminated by the fire-hole's glow. Yet, a pervasive fear of the Land lingered, weighing heavier on my heart than hers. Mine Own seemed to trust me implicitly, believing I possessed the power to shield her from any monster. That trust, though born of desperation, was a sweet balm to my soul.

That night, sharing a single cloak, we slept with spirits anxious for danger. Seven hours later, a sudden commotion woke us: a terrible screaming and crying echoed through the night—the agonized cries of humans, a sound that pierced our hearts. I could do nothing but wait, bound by my duty to Mine Own and forbidden from rashness.

Shaken, I slid down from the rock to investigate, intending to offer aid, yet unable to abandon the Maid. Immediately, the night erupted in roaring, husky voices—sounds of giants, massive as houses, running and shouting. The Maid trembled violently, recalling the dreadful months of wandering. So did I; the shouting of giants was a horror that haunted the heart.

Suddenly, a dreadful scream tore through the night—the brutal slaughter of a young maiden. My heart sickened at the thought of Mine Own, while a strange, furious anger swelled within me, threatening to burst my frame. Beside me, the Maid broke into sobs.

The screams ceased abruptly, replaced by other cries and the hoarse shouts of great men, accompanied by the thundering tread of mighty feet. The human cries grew nearer, joined by the pounding footsteps. In a heartbeat, the sounds seemed to surround our hollow. Creeping forward, I peered out. The night was filled with fleeing people, clustered in panic, screaming, gasping, and weeping as they ran. Illuminated by the fire-hole, I saw them clearly: men and women in rags or naked, torn by rocks and brush, resembling wild, lost creatures.

A fierce longing and pain gripped my heart. I wanted to rush after them, but I could not leave Mine Own exposed. Even as I wrestled with this impulse, a great thudding of monstrous feet approached. Four great men emerged from the night, passing the hollow with terrifying speed. Three were dull-colored and heavily haired, brutish in appearance, while the fourth was a horrifying white, livid-blotted—a man-monster filled with unwholesome life. They vanished instantly back into the darkness, pursued by their dreadful hunting.

As their footsteps faded, I heard bellying roars followed by a far-away scream containing a death note. I knew these brute-men were killing helpless humans. With a fierce impatience, I reflected that these victims lacked courage; they might have fought back, perhaps dying together in a final stand, finding comfort in hatred. But the peoples of the Lesser Redoubt, starved of the Earth-Current for over a hundred thousand years, inevitably lacked spirit and vigor. Naani, however, was different, proving the exception to this tragic rule.
