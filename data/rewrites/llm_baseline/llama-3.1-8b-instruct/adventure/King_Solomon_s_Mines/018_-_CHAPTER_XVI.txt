The darkness of the third day since the events of the previous chapter still lingered as our small party settled into makeshift huts at the base of the mystical trio of mountains known as the Three Witches. The rugged peaks, a result of a singular upheaval, rose high above us, their snow-capped summits piercing the night sky like shards of splintered stone. As I gazed out at the trio, the moon casting an ethereal glow upon their rugged facades, I couldn't help but feel a sense of foreboding. Our party consisted of Good, Sir Henry, Foulata, who attended to our needs with quiet efficiency, Gagool, borne along in her litter, her mutterings and curses echoing through the stillness of the night, and a retinue of guards and attendants.

The next morning, as the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, the Three Witches awakened from their slumber, their twisted snow-wreaths stretching towards the heavens like ghostly fingers. Beneath the snowline, the peaks transformed into a deep, rich purple, as if infused with the essence of the earth itself. The wild moors that climbed the slopes towards the peaks shimmered with an otherworldly beauty, as if the very fabric of reality had been woven from the threads of myth and legend.

As we set out on our journey, the white ribbon of Solomon's Great Road unwound before us, leading us up the mountain towards the centre peak, its terminus a mere five miles distant. We walked in silence, our footsteps echoing off the rocks as we ascended higher and higher, the air growing thinner and the wind picking up with each step. The excitement that had simmered within me since the previous day's events now threatened to boil over, as we drew ever closer to the fabled diamond mines that had claimed the lives of so many before us.

Gagool's litter creaked and groaned as her bearers struggled to keep pace with our rapid strides, the old woman's voice rising in protest as she peered out from beneath the grassy curtains. "Walk more slowly, white men," she croaked, her eyes glinting with a malevolent light. "Why do you rush to meet the fate that awaits you, seekers of treasure?" Her words hung in the air like a challenge, and for a moment, our enthusiasm wavered. But we pressed on, driven by a mix of curiosity and trepidation.
