Helen, ever vigilant, watched her son Arthur with a keen eye, monitoring every move he made, every gesture, every glance. Though she never mentioned Fanny's name, though she studiously avoided any reference to the young woman, her gaze lingered on Arthur with an intensity that spoke volumes.

Mrs. Pendennis's concern was not merely maternal; it was a calculated attempt to shield her son from the complexities of love and loss. She knew that Arthur's fragile health demanded gentle handling, and yet she couldn't help but notice the way his eyes scanned the mail, the way his fingers trembled as he sorted through the letters addressed to him.
