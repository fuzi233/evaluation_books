As the Miss Dashwoods entered the warm, sunlit courtyard of the country estate the next morning, they were greeted by the vibrant chatter of the household. Mrs. Palmer burst through the door, her bright smile radiating an infectious warmth, and enveloped them in a series of affectionate hugs and handshakes. Her eyes sparkled with excitement as she exclaimed, "I'm so glad you're here! It's been far too long since we've seen each other. I was worried you might not make it, considering the inclement weather outside."

She settled into a plush armchair between Elinor and Marianne, gesturing expansively as she spoke. "We can't stay long, I'm afraid. The Westons are coming to visit us next week, and we simply must be on our best behavior. My husband, Mr. Palmer, has a wicked sense of humor – he always keeps me guessing. I'm so grateful we decided to come to Barton on short notice. The carriage ride was a delightful surprise, and I must admit, I rather enjoyed the sudden change of plans."

The ladies exchanged polite smiles, and Mrs. Palmer continued, "We simply must meet again in town soon. I have it on good authority that I can procure the most exquisite house in Hanover-square for you, right next door to ours. I'd be delighted to chaperone you whenever you wish, although I know Mrs. Dashwood may not relish the idea of venturing out in public just yet."

Her words were met with appreciative nods and polite refusals. As the conversation flowed, Mrs. Palmer turned to her husband, who had just entered the room, and playfully nudged him with her elbow. "Oh, my love, do help me persuade the Miss Dashwoods to join us in town this winter. I'm convinced they'd adore the city life, and I'd be honored to show them the ropes."

Mr. Palmer responded with a nonchalant shrug, his gaze drifting toward the window as he complained about the dismal weather. "This rain is an absolute nuisance. It seems to drain all joy from the world. And have you seen Sir John's estate? Not a single billiard room in sight. How few people truly understand the value of comfort. Sir John is as dull as the skies outside."
