As Romola pushed through the entrance of San Marco, a Frate waited patiently for her arrival. Monna Brigida slipped discreetly into the adjacent church, leaving Romola to be escorted to the door of the chapter-house within the cloister. The doorway led to the quarters of the ailing Fra Luca, a space forbidden to women beyond this point. When the door creaked open, the soft external light merged with the warm glow of two candles placed beside Fra Luca's bedside, revealing the gaunt face of the monk, his golden tonsure gleaming in the dim light. His sunken hazel eyes, red-rimmed from fatigue, were fixed intently on a small crucifix held before him.

The room was heavy with the scent of incense and the faint tang of old parchment. A second Frate stood nearby, his black hood pulled low over his face, watching Romola as she entered. In the background, a frescoed wall depicted a somber scene of Christ's crucifixion, the faces of mournful figures etched beneath. As their eyes met, Romola's thoughts were drawn back to the image of her brother, Dino, whom she had known in happier times.

For a moment, she hesitated, her voice barely above a whisper as she spoke out, "Dino?" But she didn't take a step closer, her body rigid with restraint. The sight of her brother's emaciated form, shrouded in the austere garb of a monk, filled her with a deep-seated revulsion. To her, this monastic attire seemed a symbol of the very superstition that had driven her father to despair. Raised by her father to value reason and independence, she saw the Church as an external force, foreign to their own private world. The memory of her brother's abandonment still smarted, and she couldn't help but feel a mix of disdain and sadness.

Yet, as she gazed at her brother's face, a glimmer of longing flickered in her eyes. Though she stood frozen in her place, her heart yearned for the love and connection they once shared. But Dino's expression remained distant, his eyes lost in a far-off gaze, as if his spirit had already begun to depart.
