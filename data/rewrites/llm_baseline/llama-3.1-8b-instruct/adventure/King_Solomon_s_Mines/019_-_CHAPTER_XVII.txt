The flickering torchlight cast eerie shadows on the walls as we stood frozen in the Place of Death, still reeling from the horrors we'd witnessed. Meanwhile, Gagool had been busy elsewhere, her agile form darting across the cavernous space with an unnerving ease. She scampered up onto the great table, her eyes fixed intently on the lifeless body of our dear companion, Twala, suspended beneath the dripping stalactite. With a tender touch, she kissed his icy lips as if in affectionate farewell, then hobbled back to address a series of cryptic remarks to the shrouded figures surrounding us, her words indistinguishable from the creaking of the ancient rocks. Completing this macabre ritual, she settled onto the table beneath the White Death, her hands clasped in supplication as she began to chant, her voice weaving a spell of dark intent that sent shivers down our spines.

As I watched, a sense of foreboding crept over me, and I felt compelled to intervene. "Gagool," I whispered, my voice barely audible over the creaking of the cavern, "lead us to the treasure chamber."

The old crone sprang down from the table, her eyes glinting with a knowing spark. "My lords are not afraid?" she asked, her voice dripping with mirth.

I steeled myself and replied, "Lead on."

Gagool chuckled, her grin twisting her wizened face, and hobbled around to the rear of the massive Death. "Here is the entrance," she declared, placing a gourd full of oil on the floor. "Light the lamp, my lords, and follow me." As I struck a match, casting a warm glow across the darkness, I searched for the doorway, but found only solid rock before us. Gagool's laughter echoed through the cavern, sending a shiver down my spine. "The way is there, my lords," she cackled, pointing to the unyielding stone.

I frowned, my patience wearing thin. "Do not play games with us, Gagool," I warned.

She merely grinned, her eyes glinting with amusement. "I jest not, my lords. See!"
