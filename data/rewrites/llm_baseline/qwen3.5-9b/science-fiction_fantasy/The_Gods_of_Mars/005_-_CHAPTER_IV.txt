The roar of battle tore me from my reverie, plunging me back into the harsh reality of existence. For a fleeting moment, confusion clouded my mind; I could not orient myself within the darkness nor pinpoint the source of the commotion. Then, the sounds coalesced into a terrifying symphony: the shuffling of bare feet, the guttural snarls of primal beasts, the metallic clang of armor, and the ragged, heavy breathing of a man in extremis.

Rising to my feet, I cast a frantic glance around the cell where I had previously received such a hostile welcome. Against the far wall, bound in iron chains, lay the prisoners and the savage creatures, their eyes fixed on me with a mixture of curiosity, sullen fury, shock, and a flickering spark of hope. That final emotion burned brightest in the face of the young red Martian woman whose earlier scream had saved my life. She embodied the perfection of her race—a species strikingly similar to humanity's divine ancestors, distinguished only by their luminous, copper-red skin. Stripped of adornment, her status remained ambiguous, though it was clear she was either a captive or a slave in this grim setting.

Seconds ticked by before the noises emanating from the partition across the room fully registered in my mind. Suddenly, the implication struck me with force: Tars Tarkas was engaged in a desperate, life-or-death struggle against wild beasts or savage men. A cry of encouragement surged from my throat as I hurled my weight against the secret door, only to find it immovable, as futile as trying to push down a crumbling cliffside. Desperation drove me to search frantically for the mechanism of the revolving panel, but my efforts yielded nothing. Just as I prepared to strike the sullen golden barrier with my longsword, the young woman's voice cut through the tension.

"Save thy sword, O Mighty Warrior," she urged. "Thou shalt need it more where it will serve a purpose—do not shatter it against senseless metal that yields to the slightest touch of one who knows its secret."

"Do you know the secret?" I demanded.

"Yes," she replied. "Release me, and I will grant you entry to the other chamber of horrors, if you wish. The keys to my fetters hang upon the neck of your fallen foe. Yet, why return to face the fierce banths or whatever monstrosity they have unleashed within that trap?"

"Because my friend fights there alone," I answered, rushing to the carcass of the dead custodian. Among the many keys on the oval ring, the fair Martian maid swiftly selected the one that unlocked the great padlock at her waist. Freed instantly, she moved toward the panel. Seeking another key, she chose a slender, needle-like instrument and inserted it into an almost imperceptible aperture in the wall. Immediately, the door swung open on its pivot, dragging the floor section beneath my feet into the chamber where Tars Tarkas battled.

The great Thark stood backed against a corner, surrounded in a semicircle by half a dozen massive monsters, crouching and waiting for an opening. Their blood-stained heads and shoulders bore testament to the ferocity of the fight, as did the glossy hide of the green warrior, torn by sharp talons and cruel fangs. Leg, arm, and breast were ripped to ribbons. Exhausted by prolonged exertion and blood loss, he leaned heavily against the wall for support. Yet, fueled by the tenacious, indomitable courage inherent to his kind, he faced his relentless foes, embodying the ancient proverb of his tribe: *"Leave to a Thark his head and one hand, and he may yet conquer."*

As I entered, a grim smile touched Tars Tarkas's lips. Whether it signaled relief or amusement at my own battered state, I could not tell. Before I could leap into the fray with my blade, a gentle hand rested on my shoulder. Turning, I found the young woman had followed me, defenseless and unarmed.

"Wait," she whispered urgently. "Leave them to me."

Pushing past me, she advanced directly toward the snarling banths. Closing the distance, she spoke a single word in low, commanding tones. Like lightning, the great beasts wheeled upon her. I braced myself to see her torn apart, but instead, the creatures slunk to her feet like puppies awaiting a whip. She spoke again, her voice too soft for me to hear, then began walking toward the opposite side of the chamber. The six mighty monsters trailed obediently at her heels. One by one, she guided them through the secret panel into the room beyond. When the last had vanished, leaving us staring in wide-eyed astonishment, she turned, offered a brief smile, and passed through, leaving us alone.

Silence hung heavy for a moment before Tars Tarkas spoke.

"I heard the fighting beyond the partition," he said. "I feared not for you, John Carter, until I heard the report of a revolver. I knew no man on Barsoom could face you with naked steel and survive, but that shot stripped the last vestige of hope from me, for I knew you possessed no firearm. Tell me of it."

I recounted the events, and together we attempted to navigate the secret panel leading to the exit. Disappointment set in as the lock proved equally elusive. We reasoned that if the prisoners were securely chained, an avenue of escape must exist. We toggled between the baffling golden panel at one end and its mate at the other, finding no purchase.

Just as despair threatened to overwhelm us, one of the panels turned silently toward us. The young woman who had commanded the banths appeared beside us.

"Who are you?" she asked, her gaze piercing. "And what is your mission, that you dare attempt to escape from the Valley Dor and the death you have chosen?"

"I have chosen no death, maiden," I replied. "I am not of Barsoom, nor have I undertaken the voluntary pilgrimage to the River Iss. My friend here is the Jeddak of all the Tharks, and though he has not yet voiced a desire to return to the living world, I am taking him from this deceitful illusion. I am of another world. I am John Carter, Prince of the House of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium. Perhaps some rumor of me has drifted into your hellish abode."

She smiled. "Yes," she replied. "Nothing that occurs in the world we have abandoned remains unknown here. I have heard of you, many years ago. The Therns have often wondered where you fled, since you took neither the pilgrimage nor could be found upon Barsoom."

"Tell me," I pressed. "Who are you, and why a prisoner, yet wielding power over the ferocious beasts of this place that suggests authority far beyond that of a mere slave?"

"A slave I am," she answered, her voice trembling slightly. "For fifteen years I have been a slave in this terrible place. Now that they have grown weary of me and fearful of the power my knowledge of their ways has granted, I have recently been condemned to die."

"What death?" I asked.

"The Holy Therns eat human flesh," she confessed, shuddering. "But only flesh that has died beneath the sucking lips of a Plant Man—blood drained from which leaves the vessel hollow. To this cruel end I have been sentenced. Had your arrival not interrupted their plans, execution would have occurred within hours."

"Were it then the Holy Therns who fell to John Carter's hand?" I queried.

"Oh, no!" she corrected. "Those you slew were lesser Therns, though of the same cruel and hateful race. The Holy Therns dwell upon the outer slopes of these grim hills, overlooking the broad world from whence they harvest victims and spoils. Labyrinthine passages connect these caves to the luxurious palaces of the High Therns, traversed daily by lesser Therns, hordes of slaves, prisoners, and fierce beasts—the grim inhabitants of this sunless underworld. Within this vast network of winding tunnels and countless chambers reside men, women, and beasts born in eternal darkness, who have never seen the light of day—and perhaps never shall. They exist solely to serve the Therns, providing sport and sustenance."

"Now and again," she continued, "some hapless pilgrim, drifting out upon the Silent Sea from the cold Iss, escapes the Plant Men and the Great White Apes guarding the Temple of Issus, falling into the remorseless claws of the Therns. Or, as was my misfortune, is coveted by a Holy Thern watching from the balcony above the river as it issues from the mountain bowels through the cliffs of gold to empty into the Lost Sea of Korus. All who reach the Valley Dor are, by custom, the rightful prey of the Plant Men and Apes, while their arms and ornaments belong to the Therns. However, if one escapes the terrible denizens of the valley for even a few hours, the Therns may claim such a soul as their own. Often, a Holy Thern on watch, seeing a victim he covets, will trample upon the rights of the unreasoning brutes of the valley, seizing his prize by foul means if fairness fails."

"It is said," she mused, "that occasionally a deluded victim of Barsoomian superstition escapes the countless enemies besetting his path from the moment he emerges from the subterranean passage of the Iss, reaching the very walls of the Temple of Issus.
