St. Bertrand de Comminges is a decayed settlement perched on the spurs of the Pyrenees, situated roughly equidistant between Toulouse and Bagnères-de-Luchon. Once the seat of a bishopric prior to the Revolution, it retains a cathedral that draws a modest stream of tourists. In the spring of 1883, an Englishman arrived at this antiquated locale—a place too insignificant to warrant the title of city, housing fewer than a thousand souls. He was a scholar from Cambridge, dispatched specifically from Toulouse to inspect St. Bertrand's Church. He had left two companions in their hotel, men whose archaeological ambitions were far inferior to his own, promising to reunite with them the following morning for the continuation of their journey toward Auch. For his associates, a brief half-hour inspection of the church would suffice; thereafter, the trio could proceed together. However, our protagonist, let us designate him Dennistoun, had arrived prematurely. His objective was singular and exhaustive: to saturate a notebook and exhaust dozens of photographic plates documenting every inch of the magnificent edifice dominating the hill of Comminges. To execute this survey with absolute precision required the exclusive services of the church's verger. When the old man appeared, Dennistoun found himself confronted by an object of unexpected, unsettling interest.

The intrigue did not stem from the sacristan's physical attributes—he was a diminutive, arid, wizened figure indistinguishable from hundreds of similar French church guardians—but rather from a pervasive aura of furtiveness, a hunted and oppressed demeanor that clung to him. He was perpetually casting sidelong glances over his shoulder; the musculature of his back and shoulders remained in a state of constant, involuntary contraction, as if anticipating the imminent grasp of an unseen adversary. Nevertheless, Dennistoun was quickly consumed by his notebook and the operation of his camera, granting the sacristan only sporadic attention. Suspicions mingled in his mind: that the old man was being kept from his midday meal, or that he feared Dennistoun might abscond with St. Bertrand's ivory crozier or the taxidermized crocodile suspended above the baptismal font.
