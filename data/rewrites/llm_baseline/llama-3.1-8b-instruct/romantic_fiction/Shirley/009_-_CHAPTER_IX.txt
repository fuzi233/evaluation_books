As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow across the rolling hills, Messrs. Helstone and Sykes bounded into the parlour, their faces flushed with excitement and congratulations for Mr. Moore's firm stance against the deputation. However, their effusive praise fell flat against his stoic demeanor, his eyes as expressionless as a still pond on a windless day. Rector Helstone, ever the astute observer, noticed the discrepancy between his words and actions and swiftly excused himself, suggesting that Sykes accompany him on his journey, leaving Moore to his thoughts.

"Where's Sugden?" Moore inquired, his voice low and measured.

Helstone's face lit up with a mischievous grin. "Ah, I've taken the liberty of helping you out, my dear fellow! While you were engaged in discussion with that sombre gentleman, Mr. Farren, I decided not to waste a moment. I opened the back window, summoned Murgatroyd to fetch Sykes's gig, and, with his permission, smuggled Sugden and Brother Moses through the gap. Sugden took the reins, driving with all the finesse of Jehu, and within a quarter-hour, Barraclough will be safely ensconced in Stilbro' jail."

Moore nodded curtly, thanking the pair before ushering them out. The rest of the day passed in silence, with Moore lost in thought, his taciturn nature only occasionally broken by a brief exchange with Joe Scott, who poked the fire, cast furtive glances, and muttered something about the evening air being invigorating. Moore's response was a short, dry laugh, and a gentle rebuke for Joe's overzealous concern.

As the miller gathered his belongings, Joe lingered, his eyes darting towards Moore with a mixture of curiosity and wariness. "Do you know the Farrens, sir? They're struggling, I reckon?"

Moore's gaze turned introspective, his mind drifting back to the deputation and its implications. "Tell me, Joe, have you any knowledge of the Farrens' circumstances?"

Joe nodded solemnly. "Aye, sir, they're not doing well. They've gone months without work, and what little they have left is barely enough to keep body and soul together."

"A decent family, the whole of them?" Moore asked, his tone detached.
