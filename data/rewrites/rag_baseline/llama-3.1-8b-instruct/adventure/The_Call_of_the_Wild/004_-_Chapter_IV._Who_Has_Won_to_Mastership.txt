Who Has Won to Mastery

The crisp morning air carried the scent of freshly brewed coffee and baking bread wafting from the Métis camp, mingling with the damp chill of the Canadian wilderness. François stirred the embers of last night's fire, his calloused hands moving with practiced ease as he broke the ice on the water barrel. Perrault, already saddled up, waited patiently, eyeing the dogs with a mixture of affection and exasperation.

"Eh, I spik true w'en I say dat Buck two devils," François declared, his voice low and measured, as he surveyed the morning's work ahead. He nodded toward Buck, whose fur still bore the scars of the previous day's battle with Spitz. "Dat Spitz fight lak hell," Perrault agreed, his eyes scanning the dogs' harnesses for any signs of wear.

François, however, was focused on Buck. He believed the dog had proven himself worthy of leadership, and he intended to make it official. As he began to harness the dogs, Buck seemed to sense his opportunity and strode forward, his eyes fixed intently on the vacant position at the front of the team. Sol-leks, sensing the shift in dynamics, hesitated, but Buck seized the moment, nudging him aside with a gentle yet firm nudge of his muzzle.

"Eh? Eh?" François exclaimed, a grin spreading across his weathered face. "Look at dat Buck. Heem keel dat Spitz, heem t'ink to take de job." Perrault chuckled, a low rumble, as François tried to guide Buck to his new position, but the dog resisted, refusing to budge.

Buck's determination was unwavering; he had earned the right to lead, and he would not be denied. François, taken aback by the dog's defiance, reached for his club, but Buck remembered the man in the red sweater, and instead of retreating, he stood his ground, his hackles raised in a low, menacing growl.

Perrault intervened, attempting to reason with the dog, but Buck remained resolute, his eyes locked onto the coveted spot at the front of the team. François, frustrated by the delay, tossed his club aside, thinking Buck was merely cowering in fear. However, the dog was not afraid; he was simply biding his time, waiting for the perfect moment to assert his dominance.
