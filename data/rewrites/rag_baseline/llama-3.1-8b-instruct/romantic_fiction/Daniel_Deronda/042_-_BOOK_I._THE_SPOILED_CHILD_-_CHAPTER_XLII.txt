The scent of old books and wood polish wafted through the air as Daniel Deronda settled into the worn armchair, the afternoon sun casting a warm glow across the crowded parlor of the Cohens' modest London townhouse. His eyes wandered to the group of children gathered around the grandmother, their laughter and chatter a lively accompaniment to the soft ticking of the clock on the mantle. Mordecai, the enigmatic patriarch, was nowhere to be seen, and Deronda's thoughts turned to the passage he had read earlier that morning, the words of Zunz echoing in his mind like a gentle rebuke. For in the midst of this ordinary scene, he sensed a deeper truth – that even amidst the quiet comforts of family life, the weight of centuries bore down upon the shoulders of the Jewish people.

As he made his way to the parlor, the children parted to let him pass, their eyes shining with excitement. Adelaide, the youngest, reached out a chubby hand to grasp his finger, while Jacob, a bit older, proudly displayed his newfound skill with the cup and ball. Deronda smiled, handing each child a small gift – a set of intricately carved wooden figures for Adelaide, and a delicate ivory cup for Jacob. The grandmother, her eyes twinkling with amusement, gestured for him to join in the game, and soon Deronda found himself caught up in the simple joys of the moment, his worries about Mordecai's absence temporarily forgotten.

It was only when he asked about the older man's whereabouts that the mood shifted, the conversation turning to the subject of Mordecai's increasingly erratic behavior. Cohen, the patriarch, leaned in, his voice low and conspiratorial, as if sharing a secret. "To be frank, sir, we're a bit concerned. He's always been...different, but lately, he's been acting strangely. Not quite himself, if you know what I mean." Deronda's eyes met the grandmother's, and for a moment, they shared a silent understanding – a sense of unease that lingered beneath the surface of this ordinary domestic scene.
