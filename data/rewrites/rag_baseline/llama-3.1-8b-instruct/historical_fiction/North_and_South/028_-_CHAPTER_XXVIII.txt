In the hush of a late autumn afternoon, the Higgins' cottage stood like a sentinel, its weathered stone façade bearing witness to the tempests that had buffeted it for generations. Within, the air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and woodsmoke, a poignant reminder of the transience of life. Margaret Hale, her eyes red-rimmed from the weight of grief, stepped across the threshold, her footsteps echoing off the walls as she made her way to the chamber where the departed lay.

Mary, her face a map of sorrow, met her gaze with a look of half-terrified expectation. Margaret's gentle smile offered what reassurance she could, and together they ascended to the room above, the silence between them a palpable thing. As they entered, Margaret felt a sense of peace settle over her, like a benediction. The face, once worn by pain and troubled thought, now wore a soft, eternal smile. The slow gathering of tears in her own eyes was a balm to her soul, for in death, she saw a peace that eluded life. Beautiful scriptures rose to her mind, a litany of comfort: "They rest from their labors," "The weary are at rest," "He giveth his beloved sleep."

As Margaret turned away from the bed, her eyes met those of Nicholas Higgins, his great eyes wide with shock, his gaze a mixture of bewilderment and despair. He stood, his massive frame imposing, yet somehow fragile, his hands clenched into fists as if he struggled to grasp the reality of her passing. The news had reached him as he walked the winding court, his ears assaulted by the chatter of the townspeople, and now he stood, frozen, his eyes dry and fierce as he grappled with the notion that his world had been irrevocably altered.

Margaret felt a pang of awkwardness, as if she intruded upon a private moment, one that Nicholas had only just begun to comprehend. There had been a moment's hesitation on the creaking stairs, a moment's hesitation before she'd pushed forward, but now she sought to slip past his abstracted gaze, to leave him to his private anguish.
