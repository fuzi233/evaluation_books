As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting the Martian landscape in a warm orange glow, Xodar listened with rapt attention to my tale of the events that had transpired within the arena at the rites of Issus. His eyes, like two dark pools of wonder, reflected the incredulity that threatened to consume him. He could hardly credit the notion that one might dare defy the deity of Issus, wielding a sword in hand, and yet remain unscathed by the full force of her divine wrath.

"It's the final proof," he declared, his voice low and measured, "that there's nothing divine about Issus. She's merely a cunning and powerful woman, manipulating the masses through the Holy Therns, who are just as deceived as the rest of Barsoom. Her decrees, carried on strange parchments stained with blood, are nothing more than clever fabrications, designed to keep her people and the entire planet in a state of religious ignorance."

I nodded in agreement, my mind racing with the implications of Xodar's words. "She remains all-powerful here, however," I cautioned, "so we must be cautious and seize the first opportunity to escape." Xodar chuckled, a low, throaty sound, and I detected a hint of amusement in his eyes. "I wish you luck in finding that opportunity," he said, "for I've never known a prisoner of the First Born to break free."

"The night will provide us with our chance," I replied, glancing up at the darkening sky. Xodar nodded, his gaze following mine. "It won't be long now," he said. "How can I aid in our escape?"

"I need someone who can swim," I explained, my thoughts turning to the perilous journey ahead. "The red one, as you know, is unlikely to be able to navigate the waters of Korus."

Xodar's face broke into a wide, white-toothed grin. "I'm as much at home in the water as any silian that haunts the depths of Korus," he boasted. "We'll need to move quickly and quietly, using the cover of darkness to our advantage."
