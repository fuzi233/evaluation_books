As the sun beat down upon his weathered face, Clement paused at the town's central fountain, breaking his black bread into small pieces and savoring the cool water. The air was heavy with the scent of blooming flowers and fresh bread wafting from the nearby bakery. His stomach growled in protest, reminding him of the long day ahead. Just as he was about to take a bite, a flustered servant girl rushed towards him, her eyes wide with urgency. "Father, please come quickly, our master is dying!" she implored, her hand grasping his arm.

Without a word, Clement returned the bread to his leather pouch and followed the girl through the winding streets, the warm sunlight casting long shadows behind him. She led him to the imposing Stadthouse, its stone façade a testament to the town's prosperity. As they entered, Clement felt a shiver run down his spine, but he steeled himself and followed the girl up the creaking stairs.

Inside, the room was dimly lit, the air thick with the scent of herbs and spices. Ghysbrecht, his arch-nemesis, lay propped up by pillows, his face deathly pale. Clement approached him cautiously, his eyes locked on the sick man's gaunt form. "Pax vobiscum," he whispered, his voice barely audible over the creaking of the wooden floorboards.

Ghysbrecht's eyes fluttered open, and he beckoned Clement closer. "Thank Heaven you've come, Father," he croaked, his voice barely above a whisper. "Absolve me from my sins, pray for my soul." Clement nodded gravely, his expression somber. "My son, before absolution, there must be confession. You must reveal your deepest transgressions, without reserve. Think carefully, and let God guide you."

With a quiet reverence, Clement knelt beside Ghysbrecht's bed and began to pray. When he rose, his eyes locked onto the sick man's. "Confess your sins, my son," he said, his voice firm but gentle. Ghysbrecht's hands trembled as he clasped them together, and a faint blush spread across his cheeks. "Ah, Father, they are many and grave," he whispered.
