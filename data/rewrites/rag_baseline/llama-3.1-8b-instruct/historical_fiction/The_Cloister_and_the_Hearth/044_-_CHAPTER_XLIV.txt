As I watched Catherine's hands move with practiced ease, her fingers deftly untying the laces of Margaret's dress, I sensed the weight of their conversation. The scent of baking bread wafted from the hearth, mingling with the tang of sweat and the acrid tang of despair. It was a mixture that would forever be etched in my memory, a potent reminder of the turmoil that brewed beneath the surface of our lives. The girl, Margaret, lay motionless on the floor, her face pale and drawn, as if the very life force had been drained from her body.

Catherine's hands moved swiftly, opening the window to allow a warm breeze to stir the air, as she spoke in a voice that was both firm and gentle. "Come here, if you please," she called out, her words carrying across the room. Margaret Van Eyck and Reicht hastened to her side, their faces etched with concern.

"What has happened to her?" Margaret Van Eyck asked, her eyes scanning the scene before her.

Catherine's expression was calm, her hands moving with purpose as she tended to Margaret's needs. "Nothing more than is natural in her situation," she replied, her voice steady.

Margaret Van Eyck's face darkened with anger. "You do well to speak so coolly," she said, her words dripping with venom. "You, who are the cause of her situation."

Catherine's gaze remained unwavering. "That I am not," she said bluntly. "Nor any woman born."

Margaret Van Eyck's words poured forth like a torrent, each one laced with accusation. "Was it not you and your husband who kept them apart? And now he has gone to Italy all alone. Situation indeed! You have broken her heart amongst you."

Catherine's face remained impassive, her hands continuing to tend to Margaret's needs. "Why, madam, who is it then? In Heaven's name! To hear you, one would think this was my Gerard's lass. But that can't be. This fur never cost less than five crowns the ell; besides, this young gentlewoman is a wife; or ought to be."

Margaret Van Eyck's words hung in the air, heavy with emotion. "Of course she ought. And who is the cause she is none? Who came before them at the very altar?"
