As Margaret Brandt made her way to the cloister, the scent of baking bread wafted through the air, mingling with the sweet fragrance of fresh herbs from the monastery garden. It was a smell that usually filled her with comfort, but today it only seemed to heighten her anxiety. Her stomach churned with each step, her hands trembling as she smoothed the dark blue folds of her kirtle. She had chosen this dress specifically for its simplicity, a stark contrast to the finery she'd seen the women of the cloister wear. But beneath its humble appearance, Margaret felt a sense of unease, as if the weight of her past was bearing down upon her like a heavy load.

Her he-comrade, Denys, walked beside her, his steady presence a reassuring balm to her frazzled nerves. As they approached the cloister, Margaret's resolve faltered, and she stopped abruptly, her hand on Denys's arm. "Wait," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the rustling of the leaves in the nearby trees. "I think I've forgotten my courage."

Denys smiled softly and squeezed her hand. "You'll be fine, she-general. I'll stay right by your side."

With newfound determination, Margaret continued on, her heart pounding in her chest. As they entered the cloister, the group fell silent, their faces a mixture of curiosity and hostility. Margaret's eyes met Kate's, and for a moment, they simply stared at each other, the tension between them palpable.

Eli, the elder, rose from his seat and gestured for Margaret to take a place at the table. As she sat, her pale complexion seemed to glow in the soft light of the candles, and her dark blue hood framed her face like a delicate frame. Her fingers trembled as she reached into her bosom and withdrew a letter, which she kissed with a quiet reverence. Then, with a deep breath, she began to read, her voice steady and clear, like a summer breeze rustling through the leaves.

The room was silent, mesmerized by the sight of Margaret, the woman who had captured Gerard's heart and brought chaos to their peaceful lives. As she read, her words wove a spell of wonder and curiosity, and even the most hardened hearts began to soften.
