To comprehend the architecture of this flight, one must first understand the absolute terror of the corridors we fled. I could not have been unconscious more than a few seconds, yet the disorientation was total; the next sensation was a blinding radiance consuming the darkness, accompanied by the chilling realization that the eyes of my captors were gone. I rose unscathed save for a bruise upon my forehead, the mark of my fall against the stone flagging.

Springing to my feet, I sought the source of the light. It emanated from a torch wielded by one of a quartet of green warriors descending rapidly toward me. Unseen, I vanished into the first intersecting passage, resolving never to lose my quarry as I had done previously with Tars Tarkas. The party swept past the opening where I crouched; I exhaled a breath I had not known I held. They were the same souls I had tracked into the pits: Tars Tarkas and his three guards.

I trailed them silently until we reached the cell where the great Thark had been shackled. Two warriors remained outside while the key-bearing man entered to re-iron the captive. As the pair began to stroll toward the spiral runway leading upward, disappearing around a bend, I moved to the cell door. My plan was forged in desperation: if Tars Tarkas and I were to return to my camp in the hills, this betrayal was the price.

Holding my longsword aloft, ready to cleave the skull of the jailer the moment he emerged, I waited. The footsteps approached. Within minutes, Tars Tarkas, clad in the metal insignia of a Warhoon chief, hurried down the corridor toward the runway, bearing the torch. Behind him, twelve paces distant, followed John Carter, Prince of Helium.

Those who had occupied the cell with the Thark were ascending the runway as the great warrior appeared.

"Why so long, Tan Gama?" one cried.

"I encountered difficulty with a lock," Tars Tarkas replied, feigning innocence. "Moreover, I have left my short-sword in the Thark's cell. Go on; I shall return to fetch it."

"As you will, Tan Gama," the other responded. "We await you above."

"Yes," Tars Tarkas agreed, turning as if to retrace his steps, but he merely waited until the two figures vanished onto the upper floor. Only then did I join him. We extinguished the torch and crept toward the spiral incline.

On the first floor, the hallway terminated abruptly, forcing us to cross a rear chamber teeming with green folk before reaching the inner courtyard. Our only option was to ascend to the second floor and traverse the gallery I had walked earlier. Cautiously rising, we heard conversations drifting from the room above, yet the hall remained unlit and empty as we reached the summit. We threaded the long corridor to the balcony overlooking the courtyard, unseen.

To our right lay the window into the room where Tan Gama and his warriors had departed earlier. His companions had returned, and their voices drifted to us.

"What delays Tan Gama?" one asked.

"He cannot possibly be retrieving his short-sword from the Thark's cell in this time," another countered.

"His short-sword?" a woman queried. "What do you mean?"

"Tan Gama abandoned his short-sword in the Thark's cell," the first explained. "He left us at the runway to retrieve it."

"Tan Gama wore no short-sword tonight," the woman corrected. "It broke during today's battle with the Thark. He gave it to me for repair. Look," she declared, drawing the blade from beneath her sleeping silks and furs.

The warriors sprang to their feet.

"There is something amiss here," one cried.

"It is precisely what I suspected when Tan Gama left us," another said. "His voice sounded strange then."

"Haste to the pits!"

We heard no more. Slinging my harness into a single strap, I lowered Tars Tarkas to the courtyard below, dropping to his side an instant later. Since felling Tan Gama at the cell door, we had exchanged fewer than a dozen words. Yet, in the torchlight, I had seen the utter bewilderment on the great Thark's face.

"By this time," he had murmured, "I should have learned to wonder at nothing which John Carter accomplishes." He needed not express his gratitude for the risk I took to rescue him, nor his joy at seeing me. This fierce green warrior, who had greeted me twenty years prior with leveled spear and murderous intent as I stood beside the incubator of his horde on the dead sea bottom beyond Korad, was now, among the inhabitants of two worlds, the finest friend I possessed. Tars Tarkas, Jeddak of the Tharks.

Reaching the courtyard, we paused in the shadows beneath the balcony to formulate our next move.

"There are five now in the party, Tars Tarkas," I said. "Thuvia, Xodar, Carthoris, and ourselves. We require five thoats."

"Carthoris!" he exclaimed. "Your son?"

"Yes. I found him imprisoned in Shador, on the Sea of Omean, in the land of the First Born."

"I know not these places, John Carter. Are they upon Barsoom?"

"Upon and below, my friend. Wait until we have secured our escape, and you shall hear the strangest narrative ever told by a Barsoomian of the outer world. Now, we must steal our thoats and flee north before these fools discern our deception."

In safety, we reached the great gates at the courtyard's far end, the only exit to the avenue beyond. Handling five such magnificent, ferocious beasts is no trivial task; they are as wild and savage as their masters, held in check only by cruelty and brute force. As we approached, they sniffed our alien scents, circling in rage with squeals of fury. Their massive necks arched, raising gaping maws high above our heads. Fearsome as they appear, aroused, they are deadly. Standing nearly ten feet at the shoulder, their hides are sleek and hairless, dark slate on the back and sides, fading to vivid yellow at their eight padded, nailless feet, while the belly remains pure white. A broad, flat tail, wider at the tip than the root, completes the portrait of this ferocious green Martian steed—a warhorse worthy of these warlike people.

Guided solely by telepathy, needing no reins or bridles, our objective was to command two that would obey our silent will. As they charged about us, we managed to prevent a coordinated assault, though the cacophony of their squealing would inevitably summon investigating warriors. Eventually, I reached the flank of one great brute; before he understood my intent, I was firmly seated upon his glossy back. Moments later, Tars Tarkas had caught and mounted another. Between us, we herded three or four more toward the great gates.

Tars Tarkas rode ahead, throwing the barriers open, while I held the straying beasts from retreating to the herd. Together, we rode through into the avenue with our stolen mounts, refusing to close the gates, and hurried toward the southern boundary of the city.

Our escape had been nothing short of miraculous, and fortune continued to smile upon us. We passed the outer fringes of the dead city and reached our camp without hearing the faintest whisper of pursuit. Here, a low whistle—the prearranged signal—apprised the remainder of our party that I had returned. We were met by the three with manifestations of enthusiastic rejoicing.

Little time was wasted on narration. Tars Tarkas and Carthoris exchanged the dignified, formal greetings customary upon Barsoom, though I sensed intuitively that the Thark adored my boy and that Carthoris returned the sentiment. Xodar and the green Jeddak were formally introduced. Then Thuvia was lifted onto the least fractious thoat, Xodar and Carthoris mounted two others, and we set out at a rapid pace toward the east. Circling north at the city's far extremity, we sped noiselessly across the dead sea bottom under the glorious rays of the two moons, fleeing the Warhoons and the First Born, unaware of the new dangers awaiting us.

Toward noon the following day, we halted to rest our mounts and ourselves. The beasts were hobbled to graze slowly on the ochre, moss-like vegetation that serves as both food and drink during marches. Thuvia volunteered to keep watch while the rest slept for an hour.

It seemed I had barely closed my eyes when I felt her hand upon my shoulder and heard her soft voice warning of fresh peril.

"Arise, O Prince," she whispered. "There is that behind us resembling a great body of pursuers."

The girl pointed toward the direction from whence we had come. As I rose and looked, I too perceived a thin, dark line on the far horizon. I awakened the others. Tars Tarkas, whose giant stature towered above the rest, could see the furthest.

"It is a great host of mounted men," he said. "They travel at high speed."

Time was of the essence. We sprang to our hobbled thoats, freed them, and mounted. Turning our faces once more toward the north, we took flight at the highest speed our slowest beast could muster. For the balance of the day and all the following night, we raced across the ochre wilderness with pursuers constantly gaining upon us
