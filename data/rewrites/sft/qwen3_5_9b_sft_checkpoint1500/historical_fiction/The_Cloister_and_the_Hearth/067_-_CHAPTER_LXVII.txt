If the men who stood upon the river's edge had hoped to decipher the secret written in ink upon the sand, they were doomed to failure. Their knowledge was too scant, their vision too narrow to read the tragedy unfolding before them. For at that precise hour, a singular event transpired—one so devoid of precedent in the annals of humanity that it might well stand alone until the final day. Yet, the tale must be told, though briefly, woven from the threads of a narrator's observation and the testimony of a participant in the dark drama.

Gerard, having penned his final farewell to Pietro and Andrea, slipped silently into the shadows as twilight deepened. His resolve was ironclad, the stubborn determination common to souls bent on ending their own lives. With a frantic urgency, he emptied his pockets, stuffing every coin of silver and copper within his grasp, ensuring his weight would drag him swiftly beneath the surface. He hastened to a secluded bend of the stream, a place known for its silence and lack of foot traffic.

Yet, solitude, Gerard soon discovered, was a luxury rarely granted to the desperate. Some, particularly the timid, glance around nervously to ensure a witness stands nearby; but this man, hardened by despair, scanned the gloom solely for absence. And yet, there, perched like a vulture upon the corner of a narrow alley, sat a solitary figure. Annoyance prickled through Gerard's feverish mind. He feigned a careless stroll away, only to circle back immediately. Lo! The shadow had risen from the side street and lingered, waiting.

*Does he watch me?* Gerard wondered, his heart hammering against his ribs. *Can he know the purpose of this night?* Impossible, surely. A stranger could not possibly know the depths of his misery.

Driven by a frantic need to prove his isolation, Gerard marched briskly down the street, took a long detour, and returned. The figure had vanished, swallowed by the darkness. But when Gerard cast his eyes wide to confirm the clearing, there stood the man again, merely a dozen paces behind, ostensibly tying his shoe.

Gerard realized instantly that he was being hunted. In the pale, silvery light of the moon, he caught a glint of steel—a gauntlet clenched in the watcher's fist. An assassin.

Strangely, the thought never crossed his mind that this was the life targeted for destruction. Indeed, he remained unaware that any enemy existed in the world. Overcome by a sudden, delirious impulse, he turned and approached the bravo. "Good friend!" he cried, his voice trembling with a mixture of madness and pleading, "buy me out! Sell me this arm! One stroke is enough! Look, here is everything I possess!" He thrust the handful of coins into the assassin's grip.

"Oh, pray thee! Pray thee! Do one kind deed, and free me from this hateful existence!" As he spoke, Gerard tore open his doublet, exposing his bare chest to the cold air.

The assassin stared, unmoved.

"Why do you hesitate?" Gerard shrieked, tears streaming down his face. "Do you have no mercy? Is it so great a pain to raise your arm and let me fall? Is it because I am poor and cannot pay you gold? Wretched fool, you can only strike a man from behind, refusing to look him in the eye. Well then, turn your head, keep your tongue shut, and do it!"

With a snarl of utter contempt, the bravo turned away. Gerard, spurning the offer, leaped forward and threw himself into the water.

"Margaret!"

The heavy splash of his body breaking the surface seemed to rouse the assassin from a trance-like stupor. The bravo rushed to the bank, and with a strange, guttural cry, he dove after the drowning man. What followed next would be revealed by the hands of the killer.
