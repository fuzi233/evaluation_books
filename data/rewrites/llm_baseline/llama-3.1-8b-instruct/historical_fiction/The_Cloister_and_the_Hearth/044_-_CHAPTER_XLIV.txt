The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air as Catherine rushed to assist the young woman, who lay slumped on the floor. With quick, practiced hands, she smoothed out the girl's dress and opened the window to let in a cool breeze. "Come here, if you please," she called out to the others in the room.

Margaret Van Eyck and Reicht hastened to her side, their faces etched with concern. "Oh, my poor girl! What has happened to her?" Margaret asked, her voice laced with worry.

Catherine shot her a sharp glance. "Nothing, madam. Nothing more than is natural in her situation."

Margaret's expression darkened. "You do well to speak so calmly, considering the circumstances."

Catherine's response was blunt. "I'm simply stating the facts. Her condition has nothing to do with me."

Margaret's eyes flashed with anger. "You and your husband kept them apart, and now he's gone to Italy alone. You've broken her heart amongst you."

Catherine's expression remained stoic. "Who are you accusing, exactly? That this young woman's troubles began when she married my son?"

Margaret's words dripped with venom. "Who else could it be? You've seen the letter he wrote to you – the one you made her read to you. It's clear as day that you're the reason for her distress."

Catherine's eyes narrowed. "That's not fair. I'm not responsible for her situation."

Margaret's tone softened slightly. "Perhaps, after seeing her, you'll understand that your bitterness towards her was misplaced. She's coming to, thank God."

As the young woman's color returned to her cheeks, Catherine drew Margaret aside. "Will she be staying with you tonight?"

"No, madam," Margaret replied.

"I wouldn't let her return to Sevenbergen tonight," Catherine said firmly.

Margaret hesitated. "That's for her to decide."

Catherine leaned in close, her voice barely above a whisper. "Listen, Margaret, I need to tell you something. She's not who you think she is."

Margaret's eyes widened in surprise as Catherine whispered a single word in her ear. The old woman's expression transformed from shock to outrage.

"It's a lie! A calumny! A monstrous accusation!" Margaret exclaimed.
