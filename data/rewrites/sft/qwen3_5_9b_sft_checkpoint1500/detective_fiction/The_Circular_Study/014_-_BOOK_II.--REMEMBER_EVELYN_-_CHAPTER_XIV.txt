"The tale I am about to tell you mirrors and yet diverges from what I have just confessed," began young Adams, his voice steady despite the gravity of the hour. "My prior admission tangled truth with fabrication. To ensure you grasp the full reality of my wife's actions—and to correct any misconceptions—you must hear this narrative anew, as though I have never spoken a word to you before."

"Wait!" Mr. Gryce interjected, his tone commanding. He leaned forward, whispering urgently to the Inspector beside him before producing a pencil. With swift, precise strokes, he jotted down several sentences, handing the resulting sheet to the young man. As the document bore the air of a formal memorandum, and as the Inspector frequently glanced at it while Adams spoke, I transcribe these crucial directives exactly as they appeared:

1.  **The Sudden Shift:** How could a woman, initially composed enough to adjust her hair during the opening moments of an interview, suddenly descend into a frenzy of such terrifying exasperation that she took a man's life with her own hands? There was no evident prior grudge between them. (Recall the comb discovered on the floor of Mr. Adams's bedroom).
2.  **The Final Words:** Decipher the meaning of the inscription written by the deceased just prior to this encounter: *"I return you your daughter. Neither you nor she shall ever see me again. Remember Evelyn!"*
3.  **The Pronoun "I":** What authority did Mr. Felix Adams possess regarding this young girl to justify using the first person ("I") in such a communication, especially after her marriage to his brother?
4.  **The Swallowed Paper:** Having inscribed those words, why did he, upon being struck, attempt to swallow the paper itself, dying with it clamped tightly between his teeth?
5.  **Sorrow vs. Hate:** If the killing was born of rage—as described by the killer herself as an act done "as monsters do"—why did the victim's face betray sorrow rather than hatred? Furthermore, why did his expression suggest a determination entirely removed from the chaotic rush of emotions typically accompanying a fatal blow from an unexpected foe?
6.  **The Unlit Light:** If the victim possessed the strength to grab the paper and reach for a switch, why did he fail to turn on a light that might summon aid? Instead, he left the room bathed in a crimson glow. Under the current code of signals, this color signifies: *Nothing more required just now. Keep away.*
7.  **The Steel Plate:** Explain the presence of the massive steel plate found wedged between the door casings. Most critically, why did it remain stationary within its socket at the very climax of his life?
8.  **Poindexter's Arrival:** Account for Old Poindexter's sudden appearance on the scene so shortly after the incident. His overheard remark—"It is Amos's son, not Amos!"—suggests confusion. Did he not know who awaited him in this house? Was the condition of the man before him—a cross on his bosom and a dagger in his heart—less of a shock to him than the identity of the victim?
9.  **Bartow's Pantomime & Left Handedness:** Recall our deductions from Bartow's silent performance. Mr. Adams was slain by a left-handed thrust. We must look for confirmation that the young woman is indeed left-handed, and demand an explanation for why she held her non-knifed arm stretched out behind her for so long.
10. **The Bird's Song:** The bird whose cry is "Remember Evelyn!" occasionally varies it to "Poor Eva! Lovely Eva! Who would strike Eva?" For the tragedy to be truthful, the narrative must reveal that Mr. Adams knew his brother's bride intimately, both long ago and recently.
11. **Bartow's Joy:** If Bartow is innocent of direct involvement in the crime, save as a witness, why does he exhibit such profound joy at its conclusion? This deaf-mute servitor was driven mad by a specific fact. That fact must have brought him joy. Why?
12. **The Lamp Code:** Observe the following schedule, derived from repeated experiments with Bartow and the strange lamp in Mr. Adams's study:
*   *White light:* Water wanted.
*   *Green light:* Overcoat and hat to be brought.
*   *Blue light:* Put back books on shelves.
*   *Violet light:* Arrange study for the night.
*   *Yellow light:* Watch for the next light.
*   *Red light:* Nothing wanted; stay away.
The red light illuminated the final scene. Can Adams's account explain this choice?

"With these questions burning in our minds, let us now examine the history of this crime and trace the remote, perhaps intricate, causes that led to it."
