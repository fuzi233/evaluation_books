For a heartbeat, the shadow of the pirate and I hung suspended, two forces locked in a silent, deadly stare. Then, a cruel, twisted smile carved itself onto the handsome lips above me. From the darkness of the deck's edge, an ebony hand emerged, guiding a cold, iron cylinder toward the center of my brow.

Simultaneously, my free hand snapped out, seizing the black throat. The grip was immediate and iron-tight. The pirate's hiss—"Die, cursed thern!"—was stifled as his windpipe collapsed under my fingers. My revolver clicked uselessly; the chamber was empty. Before he could draw breath for another shot, I yanked him violently over the railing. He crashed against the wood, dropping his weapon and clawing desperately at the rail, his eyes wide with panic.

Our struggle was a grim, suffocating dance. He tore at the air, trying to wrench free, while I dragged him inexorably toward the abyss. His face turned a sickly purple, veins bulging from his temples. Desperation fueled his final, frantic lunge backward, tearing his hands from the rail to claw at my fingers, desperate to break the steel vice around his neck.

In that split second, I waited. With a mighty, downward surge, I swept him off the deck. His body plummeted toward the silent, black waters below, nearly dragging me from my precarious hold on the anchor chain. I did not let go. A single scream from those lips as he hit the water would summon his kin from above to avenge him. So, I held on, choking, drowning in the weight of his struggle, while the ocean swirled beneath us. Gradually, his convulsions slowed, becoming spasmodic, then still. As the last flicker of life faded, I released him. Within seconds, the black shadows of the deep swallowed him whole.

Climbing back to the rail, I raised my gaze to meet the deck. The nearer moon had dipped below the horizon, but the pale, ghostly light of the distant satellite bathed the cruiser, illuminating the scene like a stage lit for tragedy. Six or eight figures lay sprawled in sleep, their breathing soft and rhythmic.

Near the base of a rapid-fire gun sat a young white girl, bound tight. Her eyes, wide with a mixture of terror and anticipation, fixed instantly on me as I ascended. An unspoken wave of relief washed over her countenance, as if I were a savior descended from the stars. She did not speak; instead, her eyes warned me of the slumbering horrors surrounding her.

Noiselessly, I crossed the deck. The girl nodded, signaling me to approach. As I crouched beside her, she whispered urgently, "Release me. I can help you, and you will need every ally you can gather when they wake."

"Some of them will wake in Korus," I replied, a grim smile touching my lips.

Her eyes narrowed, and a cruel, knowing smirk twisted her beautiful features. The horror of it struck me anew: cruelty in a visage usually reserved for gods, where lines of love and beauty should reign, created a jarring, appalling contrast.

"Give me a revolver," she whispered, her voice urgent. "I will handle those your sword cannot silence in time."

I handed over my weapon. Turning to the task at hand, I discarded any notion of chivalry; these were demons who would neither understand nor respect honor. Stealthily, I moved to the nearest sleeper. By the time consciousness returned, he was already drifting toward the bosom of Korus. His piercing shriek echoed faintly from the depths below, a lonely cry in the vast silence.

The next man awoke as I touched him. Though I hurled him from the deck, his wild cry of alarm shattered the night. Five others erupted from their sleep, drawing swords. The girl's revolver crackled, spitting short bursts of flame, and one pirate sank silently to the deck, never to rise.

The remaining four charged, a whirlwind of steel and rage. The girl, fearing to wound me, slipped sideways, moving with the grace of a panther toward the flank of the attackers. Soon, they were upon me.

For minutes, I was engulfed in the heat of battle. Confined within the cramped quarters, there was no room for maneuver; only the brutal exchange of blows. Initially, I absorbed more than I dealt, but soon I found an opening, slipping past a guard to drive a foe collapsing onto the planks.

Their efforts intensified. The clash of steel on steel raised a deafening roar, a metallic symphony that seemed to shake the very air. Sparks flew like angry stars as edges met edges, followed by the sickening crunch of a shoulder bone shattering under the keen edge of my Martian sword.

Three stood before me, but the girl worked her way into position, poised to strike. Then, time seemed to stretch and compress simultaneously. The three lunged, intent on driving me back over the rail into the void. At that exact instant, the girl fired. My sword arm responded with two fluid motions. One man dropped, a bullet lodging in his brain. A sword clattered across the deck, tumbling over the edge into the Lost Sea as I disarmed another opponent. The third fell, my blade buried to the hilt in his chest, three feet protruding from his back, wrenched from my grasp as he crumpled.

Disarmed, I faced my sole remaining foe. His sword lay lost somewhere thousands of feet below, sinking into the abyss. Yet, the situation pleased him. A smile of satisfaction bared his gleaming teeth as he advanced bare-handed. Great muscles rippled beneath his glossy black skin, promising me that I was easy prey, unworthy of the trouble to draw a dagger.

I let him close the distance. Ducking beneath his outstretched arms, I sidestepped to the right, pivoted on my left toe, and delivered a devastating blow to his jaw. Like a felled ox, he dropped in his tracks.

A low, silvery laughter chimed behind me.

"You are no thern," the sweet voice of my companion declared, "neither by your golden locks nor by the harness of Sator Throg. Never before in the history of Barsoom has one fought as you have tonight. Who are you?"

"I am John Carter, Prince of the House of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium," I answered. "And whom," I added, "does it honor to serve?"

She paused, studying me with intense scrutiny. "You are no thern. Are you an enemy of the therns?"

"I have wandered the territories of the therns for a day and a half," I explained, "my life constantly in peril. Harassed, persecuted, attacked by armed men and savage beasts. I had no quarrel with them before, but can you blame me for feeling no love now? I have spoken."

She gazed at me for several minutes, her eyes searching my soul, judging my character and my code of honor. Finally, she spoke.

"I am Phaidor, daughter of Matai Shang, Holy Hekkador of the Holy Therns, Father of Therns, Master of Life and Death upon Barsoom, Brother of Issus, Prince of Life Eternal."

At that moment, I noticed the black pirate I had knocked down beginning to stir. I sprang to his side, stripping his harness and binding his hands and feet securely to a heavy gun carriage.

"Why not the simpler way?" Phaidor asked softly.

"The simpler way?" I queried, confused.

"With a slight shrug," she gestured, mimicking the act of casting something overboard.

"I am no murderer," I stated firmly. "I kill only in self-defense."

She regarded me narrowly, her divine brows furrowing, shaking her head in disbelief. She could not comprehend. Neither could my own Dejah Thoris ever understand what seemed to her a foolish, dangerous policy toward enemies. On Barsoom, mercy is unknown, and every living enemy represents a resource drained from the dying planet. Yet, there seemed a subtle distinction between Phaidor's contemplation of killing and the tender, regretful sorrow my princess felt for the stern necessities of war. Perhaps Phaidor regretted the thrill the spectacle would have afforded her more than the fact that I left another enemy alive.

The captive awoke fully, regarding us with intelligent, curious eyes. He was handsome, powerful, with features of exquisite chisel. The vessel, unguided, drifted slowly across the valley, but I deemed it time to take the helm. I guessed vaguely the location of Valley Dor, certain only that it lay far south of the equator. Without the splendid charts and delicate instruments of the Heliumite Navy, I could not navigate precisely, so I chose a northerly course toward the more settled regions.

Beneath my hand, the cruiser swung gracefully. I engaged the button controlling the repulsive rays, sending us soaring far out into space. With the speed lever pulled to its limit, we raced north, climbing higher above the terrible valley of death.

As we passed overhead at dizzying heights, the flash of powder far below signaled the ferocity of the battle raging on the frontier. No sound reached us; the rarefied air at our altitude dissipated the noise waves before they could travel.
