The twilight hours had always been a time for reflection, but none more so than this evening, as Felix sat in the quiet study of his father's estate, surrounded by the faint scent of old books and the soft glow of fading daylight. His mind wandered back to the conversation with his brother, Thomas, now long gone to Montgomery, tasked with a mission that would determine the course of their lives forever. Felix's thoughts drifted to the words of parting, "Spend what you will, and when your task is completed, there will still be some left for your amusement." The promise of wealth and power hung in the air like a whispered secret, one that only they shared.

As Felix rose from his chair, the worn wooden floor creaked beneath his feet, echoing through the empty halls. He walked to the window, pushing aside the heavy curtains to let in the last wisps of sunlight. The trees outside seemed to lean in, their branches tangling together like skeletal fingers, as if trying to snatch the last rays of light for themselves. Felix's gaze fell upon the letter from Thomas, dated just days ago, and his thoughts turned to the task at hand.

He recalled the words of his brother's parting instructions: "The name under which I have leased a house in New York is Adams, Felix Adams. As such, you will address me." The memory of their father's words still lingered, "Cadwalader is a name that must not leave your lips in Montgomery, nor must you forget that my person is known there, otherwise we might not have been dependent on you for the success of our revenge." A shiver ran down Felix's spine as he remembered the calculated glint in his father's eye, the cold calculation that had driven him to build a fortune, not for the sake of wealth itself, but for the purpose of revenge.

As the darkness gathered outside, Felix's thoughts turned to the task ahead. He knew that Thomas was in Paris, seeking out the introductions they had agreed upon. The weight of their plan settled upon him like a mantle, heavy with the promise of secrets kept and promises made. He felt a sense of restlessness, a feeling that he was standing at the threshold of something momentous, something that would change the course of their lives forever.
