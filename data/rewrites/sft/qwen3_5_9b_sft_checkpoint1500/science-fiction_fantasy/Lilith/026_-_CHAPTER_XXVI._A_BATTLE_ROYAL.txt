I collapsed onto the bed, the linen cool against my fevered skin, and let the narrative she had spun unravel in my mind. It was a tale of tragic irony, one where a single slip of speech—a word spoken without thought—had shattered her spell just enough to reveal the truth. The leopardess leaped into action; the princess lay broken upon the riverbank; the flowing water had washed away the enchantment. Her own account of the journey laid bare the peril facing the Little Ones, and the horrifying realization settled over me: I had inadvertently saved the life of their sole, terrifying foe.

Before this truth fully crystallized, surrender claimed me. Sleep, perhaps induced by wine that tasted less innocent than it appeared, dragged me under.

When consciousness returned, the world had shifted. Night had fallen, yet a solitary lamp hung from the ceiling, casting a soft, amber glow that transformed the chamber into a sanctuary of languor. I felt untethered from earth, drifting on a twilight ocean where existence itself was a form of pure, unburdened pleasure. There was no pain here, no gnawing ache. Or so I believed until the sensation hit me again.

A sudden, searing lance of agony tore through my heart, a mortal sting that made me gasp. And again! This time it was sharper, a brutal intensity that left me reeling. I tried to lift my hand to press against the wound, but some unseen force held it pinned to the mattress. The pain receded slightly, leaving behind a heavy, suffocating paralysis. Something vile clung to me, a hatred I could not name. I wanted to fight back, to scream, to move, but my limbs refused to obey. My will screamed in protest, futile and trapped within a body that had turned to stone. I yielded, lying passively as the darkness closed in.

Then, a gentle pressure touched my cheek, guiding my head deeper into the pillow. A weight settled heavily across my chest, stifling my breath.

My breathing slowly deepened, the oppressive weight lifting from my lungs. Gritting my teeth, I forced my eyes open.

Standing atop the bed was the princess. She gazed out into the room with the vacant, dreamlike air of a sleeper, though her expression betrayed a different state entirely. Her great eyes were startlingly clear and calm, devoid of fear. Her lips curved in a rictus of satisfied passion, smeared with a streak of crimson blood that she hastily wiped away.

Our gazes locked. In a flash, she lunged forward, striking me across the eyes with a handkerchief. It felt like a blade drawn swiftly across my vision, plunging me into blindness for a fleeting, disorienting moment.

As the darkness faded, I heard a heavy, muffled thud—the sound of a large, softly padded beast landing after a short leap. I blinked, squinting into the gloom, and saw the massive sweep of a long tail vanish through the partially ajar doorway. Instinct took over. I scrambled after it.

The creature had melted into the void. I plummeted down the stairs, crashing into the alabaster hall. Above, the moon hung high, bathing the interior in a pale, ghostly luminescence, making the space resemble the insides of a bleached lunar sphere. But the princess was nowhere to be seen. I needed her. In her presence, I might find protection; outside it, I was nothing. To her, I was merely a domesticated beast to feed upon, a living fountain quenching a demoniac thirst. She favored me only to make it easier to consume me. Even now, with my eyes open, I did not truly fear her. Yet, the moment they closed, she would return. Unable to see her, I felt her omnipresence, lurking in some secret cavern of slumber. Safety existed only when my gaze was fixed upon her.

Beyond the alabaster halls, absolute darkness swallowed the world. I crawled blindly, hands and feet feeling for purchase, until I brushed against a curtain. Pushing it aside, I entered a vast, silent assembly hall. It was a spectacle of the impossible: invisible yet somehow perceived, the walls, floor, and ceiling shrouded in an infinity of blackness darker than any moonless night. Yet, my failing eyes strained to separate figures from the mass, a chaotic interplay of individuals divided and surrounded by the void.

Vision failed me, struggling to coalesce from the fog. I pressed my eyeballs, focusing, refocusing, but the image remained a blur. Blackness bled into form, silence mixed with undefined motion, possessing the wide expanse. It was a dim, confused dance, flickered with recurrent glimpses of shapes not unfamiliar to my nightmares. Here, a woman with glorious eyes staring out from a skull; there, an armored figure mounted on a skeletal steed; elsewhere, the hideous, burrowing phantasms. I traced no order, no logic in the mingling currents and eddies. If I seemed to grasp the rhythm of a dance, it broke instantly, replaced by chaos. Shifting colors of seemingly solid forms mingled with a multitude of shadows, appearing independent of their origins, each moving with its own erratic shadow-will. I scanned the periphery, frantically searching for the princess, but amidst the wildly shifting kaleidoscope, I found no sign of her, no hint of her presence. Where was she? What horrors might she be orchestrating? No one paid me the slightest heed as I wandered hither and thither, a lost soul in a sea of shadows. Hope draining from me, I turned away, pressing my hand against the wall for guidance, creeping toward a curtained opening leading to a vestibule.

Moonlight filtered weakly through the curtains, illuminating the cage of the leopardess. Within it raged a desperate, silent struggle. Two vastly disparate forms—one human, one bestial—writhed together in a tangled confusion of limbs and bodies. It had lasted but an instant when the leopardess burst from the cage, walking calmly toward the open door. As I hurried after her, glancing back, I froze. Inside the cage sat the leopardess, motionless, crouching exactly as I had first seen her.

Above, the moon hung halfway up the sky, round and crystal clear. The bodiless shadow I had encountered the night before walked through the trees toward the gate, and trailing behind him was the leopardess, her tail swaying. I followed at a distance, moving as silently as they, neither pausing to look around. Through the open gate, we descended toward the city, lying quiet beneath the moonshine, its surface still as glass. The moon's face was utterly still, radiating an atmosphere of cold, expectant anticipation.

The Shadow moved directly to the staircase at the summit—the same stairs where I had lain unconscious the previous night. He ascended without hesitation, and the leopardess followed close behind. I quickened my pace, but moments later, a cry of sheer horror pierced the air. Then, the heavy, soft crash of something falling landed squarely between me and the stairs. At my feet lay a body, frightfully blackened and crushed, yet unmistakably the woman who had guided me home and barred the door.

Stunned, petrified, I watched as the spotted leopardess bounded down the stairs, clutching a baby in her jaws. Before I could dart forward to intercept her, a blinding flash of white erupted from behind me. Like a great bar of glowing silver, the white leopardess shot through the moonlight and seized the spotted one by the neck. The spotted leopardess dropped the child. I snatched the infant up, holding it tight, and stood frozen to witness the battle unfolding before me.

It was a visceral, primal tableau. One moment the spotted leopardess dominated, the next the white one gained the upper hand. Both were so intent on the kill that all sound was reduced to a low, guttural growl, a whimper, or a snarl of hate, punctuated by the frantic scratching of claws as they scrambled for footing on the pavement. The spotted leopardess was larger, and my heart ached for my friend, but I quickly realized that despite equal strength and agility, the white leopardess possessed superior endurance. She never relinquished her grip on the other's neck.

Finally, a howl of agony tore from the spotted leopardess's throat. It shifted rapidly through varying tones, culminating in the long, drawn crescendo of a woman's ultimate wail. The white leopardess released her jaws. The spotted one recoiled, rising onto her hind legs.

There, bathed in the silver light of the moon, stood the princess. A confused whirlwind of shadows raced over her whiteness—the leopard's spots crowding, hurrying, fleeing toward the refuge of her eyes, where they merged and vanished. The last few, outrun and belated, blended into the cloud of her streaming hair, leaving her radiant as the moon itself when a legion of tiny vapors has been hunted away by the wind from its silvery disk. Save for the trail of blood trickling down the white column of her throat from every wound inflicted by her adversary's terrible teeth.

With a fluid, unnatural grace reminiscent of Hecate, she turned, took a few steps, and suddenly fell. Instantly, she was covered anew in the swirling spots of the leopard, and she fled at a long, stretching gallop.

The white leopardess turned as well, springing toward me. She tore at my arms, pulling them asunder, caught the baby as it tumbled from my grasp, and launched herself down the street toward the gate with the child.
