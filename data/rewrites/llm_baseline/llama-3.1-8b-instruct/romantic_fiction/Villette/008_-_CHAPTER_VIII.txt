As I followed the maîtresse through the labyrinthine corridors of Madame Beck's residence, I found myself in a kitchen that defied my expectations. The space was spotless, yet its layout seemed to belong to a bygone era. No fire crackled in the hearth, nor was there an oven to be seen, but a massive, black furnace dominated one corner, its presence both imposing and mystifying. I couldn't help but feel a twinge of pride, a sentiment that I swiftly suppressed, as the maîtresse led me to a smaller room called a cabinet. A stout cook, clad in a jacket, a short petticoat, and wooden clogs, presented me with a supper that consisted of an unidentifiable meat served in a piquant sauce, boiled potatoes infused with a sweet and sour flavor, a slice of bread with butter, and a baked pear. My hunger momentarily forgotten, I took note of the unfamiliar aromas and textures, my senses awakened by the novelty of it all.

After the evening prayer, Madame Beck appeared, her eyes gleaming with a quiet intensity as she requested that I accompany her upstairs. We navigated a series of narrow, dimly lit dormitories, which I later learned had once been the cells of nuns. The air was heavy with the scent of old stone and the faint hint of incense, transporting me to a realm both somber and sacred. We passed through the oratory, a long, low room where a crucifix hung against the wall, casting a somber glow in the fading light. The atmosphere was thick with the weight of history, and I felt a shiver run down my spine as we entered Madame Beck's chambers.

The room was a sanctuary of comfort, with three small beds arranged around a central table, upon which a dying candle cast flickering shadows. Beside the table, a woman reclined in a chair, her face relaxed into a peaceful slumber. Her attire was a jarring mix of opulence and vulgarity – a gaudy striped silk dress and a worn apron – and a bottle and an empty glass stood precariously balanced on the armrest. Madame Beck regarded this tableau with an air of serenity, her expression neither smiling nor frowning, as if she had beheld a familiar and comforting scene. Without stirring the sleeping woman, she gestured toward a fourth bed, indicating that it was to be mine. With a quiet efficiency, she snuffed out the candle and replaced it
