The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air as Oleron stood at the counter, his hands kneading the dough for the day's batch of pain au chocolat. The rhythmic motion was soothing, a calming balm for the turmoil that had been brewing inside him since Elsie's last visit. He had known, of course, that she would send a postcard before arriving unannounced, and yet the knock at the door still caught him off guard. He opened it to find her standing on the landing, her eyes darting towards the stairs with a mix of anxiety and embarrassment.

As they exchanged pleasantries, Oleron couldn't help but notice the faint scent of lemon verbena on her perfume, a fragrance that always transported him back to the summer afternoons spent in his grandmother's garden. Elsie had come bearing news of an inquiry for short stories, and Oleron thanked her graciously, though he suspected her visit was not solely motivated by altruism. As they chatted, he found himself glancing at the clock, willing her to leave, sensing that her presence was a harbinger of more heartache than joy.

But fate had other plans. As Elsie descended the stairs, her foot slipped through the worn wooden slats, sending splinters flying. Oleron rushed to her side, his hands instinctively reaching out to steady her. Her laughter was a mixture of hysteria and desperation as she clutched at her injured ankle.

"It's just my weight – I'm getting fat," she stammered, her eyes brimming with tears.

Oleron's expression softened, and he gently began to clear the debris from the stairs. As he worked, he couldn't help but think of the countless afternoons he had spent in the kitchen with his mother, learning the art of pastry-making and the importance of patience. He recalled the way the light danced through the windows, illuminating the delicate layers of puff pastry, and the way the sweet scent of butter and sugar filled the air.

As he examined Elsie's injury, he felt a pang of concern, not just for her physical well-being, but for the fragility of her spirit. She refused to reascend the stairs, insisting on leaving as quickly as possible, her words laced with a mix of sadness and resignation.
