As the shadows danced upon the walls, Deronda stood at the threshold, hesitant to bid farewell to Gwendolen, yet unwilling to reveal the truth about his impending departure. He chose not to whisper the words, "I shall not see you again for a long while," lest Grandcourt misinterpret their significance, sensing the gravity of the moment that weighed heavily on his own heart.

For in the depths of his being, a profound emotion stirred, one that recalled the most pivotal experience of his life. It was a summons from Sir Hugo, his guardian, that had summoned him to the baronet's chambers with a note that read, "Come immediately. Something has happened." The anticipation had been palpable, but upon entering the study, Deronda was met with a display of grave affection, rather than the distress he had anticipated.

As he clasped Sir Hugo's hand, he detected a hint of unease beneath the surface, a subtlety that only served to heighten the sense of foreboding within him. "It is nothing to grieve you, sir?" Deronda asked, his tone tinged with a mix of reassurance and inquiry, as if seeking to confirm the worst.

Sir Hugo's countenance revealed a depth of feeling that was rare for him to express, a look that spoke volumes about the weight of the secret he was about to share. "No, Dan, no," he began, his voice trembling with emotion. "Sit down. I have something to say."

Deronda obeyed, his mind racing with possibilities. It was unusual for Sir Hugo to exhibit such vulnerability, and the air was thick with tension as he took a seat. "Not to grieve me, my boy, no," Sir Hugo continued, his words measured and deliberate. "At least, if there is nothing in it that will grieve you too much. But I hardly expected that this—just this—would ever happen. There have been reasons why I have never prepared you for it. There have been reasons why I have never told you anything about your parentage. But I have striven in every way not to make that an injury to you."

The room fell silent, as if time itself had slowed, and Deronda's eyes locked onto Sir Hugo's, searching for answers to the questions that swirled within him.
