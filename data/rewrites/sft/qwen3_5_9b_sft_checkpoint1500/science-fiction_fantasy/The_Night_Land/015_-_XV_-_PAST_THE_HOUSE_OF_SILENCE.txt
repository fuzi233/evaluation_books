By the tenth hour of the day, we launched the raft. The paddles moved with an effortless balance, thanks to the rests I had rigged as you recall, allowing the vessel to glide forward with minimal labor. We stood in tandem—the Maid at the bow paddle, I at the stern—and pushed steadily. Soon, the raft achieved a speed slightly less than a brisk walk over the rugged terrain of the Land.

Around the twelfth hour, we halted to eat and drink, resuming our easy, rhythmic toil. As we moved, our motion resembled little more than rocking gently on our heels. Hours passed in a flow of constant, gentle conversation. The Maid frequently glanced back at me with affection, pressing her lips together in a silent temptation, yet shaking her head most tenderly whenever I attempted to abandon my paddle to approach her.

At the eighteenth hour, we withdrew our paddles. The Maid carefully arranged her cloak to serve as our bedding. After eating and drinking, we fell into a sound, happy slumber immediately, seemingly without a moment's transition. Eight hours later, we awoke together. For a fleeting instant, we scarcely registered our location, but soon realized we were safe and undisturbed by any threat during our rest. We laughed at one another, joyful to awaken in the presence of the other. Following a kiss, we washed briefly in the sea waters and returned to our meal. Once fed, we resumed paddling, proceeding along the coast peacefully and contentedly throughout the day.

In total, this voyage consumed four solid days, comprising ninety-six hours. We traveled without haste or excessive labor, ensuring I had time to recover my strength. Nothing occurred to disturb us save for a single encounter: a massive beast emerged clumsily from the sea onto the shore, browsing upon the local vegetation. Though it appeared monstrous, it caused us no horror; rather, it filled us with gladness at our distance from it. To us, it seemed a creature of nature, devoid of any sinister odor or quality to trouble our spirits. Indeed, all creatures of that region followed this pattern. I suspect the Early World bore a striking resemblance to it, validating the ancient adage that extremes often meet. It seems plausible that the Deep World produced beings akin to those of the Beginning, perhaps bred merely by circumstance and condition. Yet, I cannot dismiss the possibility of a deeper, spiritual force inherent to every form of life—a peculiar inner spark that defines existence. Whether this spirit is the cause of life or a result of evolution remains uncertain, but where it exists untainted, humanity persists. Even if Man once evolved from a fish-like state, or if he was always brutish, his fundamental nature remained unchanged. Development, however rapid, does not alter the core of the Human. I remain open to reason, refusing to build walls around it, acknowledging that while circumstances shape bodies, an inward force shapes souls.

I must clarify that I do not speak of what lies *Afterward*, when all earthly life concludes. Who can predict how far we shall advance toward beauty then? At this juncture, I harbor a wonderful hope for beautiful things, for sweet and mighty uplifting toward that Glad World whose shores we once beheld standing in holiness with the Beloved.

Resuming my narrative, I am pleased to conclude this digression, which required words because it sprang directly from my own story. Beyond the sea beast, another notable feature marked our journey: a great Fire-Hill rising from the sea. The water boiled around it, though not uniformly. A dozen great jets shot upward into the sky, roaring plainly across the expanse. Strange grunts emanated from the base of the hill, likely caused by bursting gases. These signs revealed the immense energy awakening within that deep country. We gazed backward at the Hill of Fire and Force for a considerable time after passing it.

Beyond this, the seas fractured into smaller bodies, yet remained connected by waterways that allowed our passage. I showed the Maid the two locations where I first slept upon entering this country. She listened with sweet interest, always eager to learn.

Upon completing four days on the water, we reached the landfall on a flat coastal plain, where the terrain sloped upward toward the mouth of the First Gorge—the destination you will recall. It was the tenth hour, marking the beginning of our voyage as clearly as it ended. It had been a sweet, peaceful journey, and I was happy if what remained could maintain such pleasantness and safety. However, you know well that great dangers awaited. Our hearts were set in courage, determined to conquer. If successful and safely returned to our Mighty Home, we would spend our remaining lives together in loveliness—a worthy prize and glory to repay our stress.

We beached the raft as securely as possible, wondering if anyone would ever see it again through eternity. Glancing at one another, the Maid cut a small fragment of the wooden raft for a future memento. Gathering our gear on the shore, she assisted me in donning my armor. I strapped the Scrip and Pouch to my back, gripped the Diskos in my hand, and prepared for battle. She wore her reduced bundle and had her knife at her belt.

Then, kneeling, the Maid kissed the raft. Memories gathered upon her; she faced a final break from the first phase of her life. You understand the quiet sympathy required to witness this, and you perceive that her heart stirred with a strange, sorrowful trouble. I stooped gently, lifting her to her feet. I led her from the raft, needing her near me, and we proceeded upward toward the dark mouth of the Great Gorge.

To our right lay the grim, utter-huge Mountain, perched upon which were the four fire-hills I mentioned earlier. Below them ascended great hills of ash, cast down through eternity. The Maid gazed upon this wonder for a long time, never ceasing to be amazed—not I, nor any human who might ever behold such a sight.

Soon, we entered the high mouth of the Gorge and ventured onward into the gloom for a short distance until the gorge bent sharply to the left, plunging into darkness. Here, we paused and turned back toward the Country of Seas for a final, outward view of that Deep and living Land, hidden so far downward in the everlasting night.

It was a solemn realization that we might be the last old humans ever to look upon that country. I wondered if the Humpt Men would eventually evolve, in some far eternity, to the full sweetness of the Human spirit, which I believed resided within them. This thought was both odd and natural. I considered the country as somewhat like the Olden Time renewed, viewing early things with new eyes.

For a while longer, we stood silent, listening to the far mutter of the Great Fire-Mountains and Fire-Hills, and the noise of life drifting over the land. We were but a few paces from the silence of the Great Gorge, which would soon lead us toward the Eternal Strangeness of the Night Land. The Maid held my arm closely as we looked our last into the red light of that Hidden Country, where we had nearly met our death.

Presently, I turned, and she slipped her hand into mine. Tears flowed silently down her face, burdened by everything pressed upon her heart. Yet, amidst sorrow, there was happiness, and a vague emotion that she would never again see the dear island where she nursed me back to life and well-being. Her mind wandered to places she would tell her children of in afterlife—tales of a country they would never see, but which would forever be a wonder to them.

Passing the mighty corner of the Gorge, we stumbled forward into the gloom. For sixteen hours, we traveled steadily, troubled only by the great darkness. By then, it had been twenty-six hours since our last sleep. Surely, this was folly, for I needed full strength before reaching the Night Land, and over-tiring oneself was a mistake. The Maid had spoken much on this point.

Indeed, we found a safe place for slumber. While eating and drinking, we calculated my notes of the outward way and decided to limit our daily journey through the Gorge to sixteen hours, sleeping always for eight good hours. We adhered to this schedule until we emerged from the Great Gorge, a process taking five days.

As we entered the lighter parts of the Gorge, we became more cheered, though often we were half-smothered by the horrid gases rising from various spots. My strength grew constant as we journeyed. The Maid never suffered me to carry her; instead, she moved lightly and cleverly, becoming adept at this constant wandering.

From time to time, I stopped to show the Maid where I had slept, urging her to stand where I lay so lonely during my outward search. Always, she treated me with utter tenderness, lacking speech due to the calling of her heart. She constantly asked when we would reach the Night Land, how far it was, and expressed a growing, natural excitement. In truth, I felt almost as excited as she, wondering what she thought of the Mighty Redoubt and all the strange, monstrous lands. Above all, I was shaken to my heart by the thought of bringing the Maid swiftly to safety, lest some woe befall us despite our progress. This made it difficult to rush beyond our set hours, yet we possessed wisdom in this matter and always slept after the sixteenth hour.
