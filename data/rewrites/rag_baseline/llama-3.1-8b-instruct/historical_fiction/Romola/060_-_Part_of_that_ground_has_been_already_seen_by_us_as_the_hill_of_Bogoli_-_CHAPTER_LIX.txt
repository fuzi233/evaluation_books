As she navigated the narrow streets of Florence, the morning sunlight cast long shadows across the cobblestones, illuminating the vibrant colors of the city's market stalls. Romola hurried along, her senses heightened as she wove through the crowds. She had secured a meeting with Fra Girolamo in the chapter-house of San Marco, courtesy of Fra Salvestro's connection. The prospect of discussing the recent events with the influential friar filled her with a sense of trepidation, but also a deep-seated hope.

She chose to take a circuitous route, passing through the bustling Piazza della Signoria, where the atmosphere pulsed with the fervor of public debate. Every corner, every loggia, and every shop seemed to be abuzz with the fervent discussions of the day. Handbills plastered the walls, some proclaiming the virtues of justice, while others extolled the law. The air was thick with the weight of opinion, as citizens passionately argued for and against the fate of the conspirators. Handbills in smaller print offered more nuanced arguments, and Romola carefully scanned the crowds, searching for copies to peruse later.

As she turned a corner, she spotted Bratti, a familiar figure from the city's lower classes, hawking his wares. His hands moved slowly, methodically separating the wet sheets of paper as he waited for customers. Romola approached him, her eyes scanning the selection. "Do you have both types of handbills?" she asked, her voice firm but polite.

Bratti looked up, his eyes squinting slightly as he regarded her. "Ah, yes, two sorts. 'Law' and 'Justice'," he replied, his tone measured. "I've raised the price on 'Justice', but I think 'Law' has its merits too. People are willing to pay for quality, after all." Romola nodded thoughtfully, producing a small coin to exchange for the handbills. As she took her leave, she felt a sense of unease creeping over her, a sense that the city's passions were running high, and the outcome of the upcoming trial hung precariously in the balance.

Upon entering the outer cloisters of San Marco, Romola spotted Fra Salvestro waiting for her, engaged in conversation with a group of lay visitors.
