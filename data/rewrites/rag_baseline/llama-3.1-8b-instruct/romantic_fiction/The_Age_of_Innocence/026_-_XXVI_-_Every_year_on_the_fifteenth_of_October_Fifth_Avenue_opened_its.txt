The silence of the Manhattan apartment was oppressive, a physical presence that weighed upon Newland Archer as he sat at the window, watching the city awaken. Outside, the shutters of the buildings on Fifth Avenue creaked open, and the sounds of the day spilled in – the clinking of cups, the murmur of conversation, the distant wail of a saxophone. He felt a sense of disconnection from it all, a feeling that had grown more pronounced since his marriage. His wife May's smile, once a bright note in his life, now seemed a distant memory, a fleeting moment of joy in a sea of monotony.

His mother, Ellen, sat across from him, her eyes narrowed as she surveyed the city. She was a woman of habit, and her annual pronouncements on the state of society were a ritual she took seriously. "It's all changing, Newland," she said, her voice low and measured. "I can see it in the way people dress, the way they talk. We're losing our values, our sense of propriety." Her gaze fell on May, who was sitting quietly in the corner, her eyes fixed on some point beyond the window. "Even May's friends are changing. They're dressing like...like those women at the opera, with their gaudy clothes and their flashy jewels."

Newland felt a pang of discomfort at his mother's words, a sense of unease that he couldn't quite explain. He looked out the window again, watching as the city unfolded its daily routine. The streets were filled with people, each one going about their business, unaware of the quiet desperation that lay beneath the surface. He thought of Dr. Ashmore's sermon, the one he had preached on Thanksgiving, and the way it had struck a chord within him. "We're trending," the good doctor had said, his voice full of conviction. "We're moving towards something, but I'm not sure what it is."

As he sat there, lost in thought, the room seemed to grow quieter, the silence thickening until it was almost palpable. Newland felt a sense of disconnection from the world outside, a feeling that he was stuck in a time warp, unable to move forward. His mother's words echoed in his mind, a reminder that things were changing, and he wasn't sure if he was ready.
