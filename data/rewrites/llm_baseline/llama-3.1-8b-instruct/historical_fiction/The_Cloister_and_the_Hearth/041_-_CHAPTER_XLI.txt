As Denys rode at the center of his companions, his mind reeled with anguish and regret. Gerard had given up the German route to Rome for his sake, and now he was left alone in the heart of Burgundy. The thought consumed him, driving him to lash out at heaven and earth in a frenzy of despair, then to sink into a darkness so profound that his friends deemed it wise to restrain him. Outside the town, the entire procession halted at the "Tete d'Or," where the young landlady, preoccupied with the count and her own concerns, spotted Denys and inquired anxiously about his young companion.

Denys's grief burst forth like a torrent as he recounted the tale, pleading with her to send someone after Gerard. "He's lost without me, poor wretch," he implored. "He can't bear solitude." The landlady, however, seemed unyielding, her expression a mixture of annoyance and embarrassment. "I've been far too kind already," she muttered, turning a shade redder.

"You won't?" Denys pressed.

"Not I," she replied curtly, before turning away.

Denys's outburst of curses and insults sent the landlady hurrying off, her servant scrambling to saddle two mules. As she departed, Denys trudged north with his troop, his spirits crushed, unaware that the landlady was swiftly preparing for a hasty departure herself. Within forty minutes, she had transformed herself from a humble hostess into a skilled equestrienne, urging her servant to follow her as she galloped off in the opposite direction.

As night fell, the group arrived at "The White Hart," their arrival causing a commotion that took some time to subside. Manon, Denys's acquaintance, eventually spotted him amidst the crowd, her cheeks flushing with a mixture of emotions. Yet, she chose not to acknowledge him, a sign that their friendship was waning. "The base degrees by which she did ascend," Denys thought, a bitter taste in his mouth. The inn, with its warm fire and savory aromas, only served to remind him of Gerard.
