As I strolled alongside Fra Colonna through the winding streets of Rome, the scent of roasting chestnuts wafted through the air, mingling with the sweet fragrance of fresh basil and the acrid tang of salted meats. We walked in silence, our footsteps echoing off the ancient stones as we made our way to the city's central square. The sun beat down upon us, casting long shadows across the cobblestones, and I couldn't help but feel a sense of awe at the sheer scale of the structures that loomed above us.

Fra Colonna, a man of imposing stature and sharp intellect, spoke little, preferring to let the grandeur of Rome speak for itself. But when he did, his words were laced with a subtle irony that hinted at a deeper understanding of the city's complexities. "This place, Rome?" he said, his voice low and gravelly, "It is but the tomb of mighty Rome." He pointed out the remnants of ancient triumphal arches, half-buried beneath the modern streets, and the columns that rose like sentinels from the earth, their capitals worn smooth by the touch of countless hands.

As we walked, he led me to the great sewer, a testament to the engineering prowess of the ancient Romans. Its ancient stones seemed to whisper tales of a bygone era, one that had left behind a legacy of grandeur and decay. We stood before the fragments of the Temple of Peace, its crumbling facade a poignant reminder of the transience of human endeavor. I gazed up at the Monte Savello, the supposed site of the ancient theatre of Marcellus, and felt a shiver run down my spine as I imagined the roar of the crowds and the whispers of the gods.

Fra Colonna chuckled, his eyes glinting with amusement, as he observed my wonder. "Ah, but there is more to Rome than meets the eye," he said, his voice dripping with a hint of mischief. "Come, let us see what other secrets this city holds." He led me to a small, unassuming church, its doors adorned with intricate carvings of saints and angels. Inside, a knot of people huddled in the porch, their faces lit by the flickering candles that cast eerie shadows on the walls.
