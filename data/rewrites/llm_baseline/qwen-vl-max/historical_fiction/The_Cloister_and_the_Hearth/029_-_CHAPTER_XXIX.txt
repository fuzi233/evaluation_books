Gerard stood stunned by the sudden news, his face flushing and fading in rapid succession. Then, with a surge of anger, he clenched his jaw, teeth gritted tight. Men of true spirit, like the wild boar, may flee when faced with overwhelming strength—perhaps because they are not fools—but if pursued too closely, too persistently, until driven to desperation, they will wheel about and charge with furious rage, trampling all who stand in their way. Gerard, too, braced himself for battle. But no sooner had he steeled his resolve than his expression faltered, and he whispered, brokenly, “And my axe is in Rhine.”

They conferred in silence, torn between caution and necessity. Prudence urged them to avoid the village altogether; hunger demanded food. In the end, hunger spoke loudest, though prudence offered the most compelling argument. They decided upon a course: cross the open fields.

At a haystack near the road, they stopped and took two bundles of hay without asking. They laid them in a dry ditch, hidden from sight but tangled in nettles. One by one, they slipped out, gathered turnips from nearby fields, and returned to eat them in secret as they retreated through the twilight. They continued this way, nibbling at their meager harvest, until the sun sank below the horizon.

Then, shivering and drenched, they emerged into the storm—rain lashing down, darkness swallowing all, wind howling like a pack of wolves. They could neither see nor hear, nor be seen or heard. For all they knew, they passed within arm’s reach of their enemies, unseen as phantoms drifting through the night. The terror of the tempest—the blackness, the fury of the gale—drowned out all thought of pursuit. It felt as though they were carving their path through solid darkness, blind and desperate.

When the moon finally broke through the clouds, they were many miles beyond Dusseldorf. Yet still they walked on, weary but unyielding. Soon, they came upon a massive stone building, its towers rising stark against the sky.

“Courage!” Denys called out, his voice catching with relief. “I believe I know this place. Yes—it is. We are within the diocese of Juliers. Cologne holds no sway here.”

In that instant, they stepped inside the convent walls—and found safety.
