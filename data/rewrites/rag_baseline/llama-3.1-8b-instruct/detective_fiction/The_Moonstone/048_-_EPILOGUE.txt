The scent of damp earth and saltwater lingered on my skin as I stood at the edge of the quay, the sounds of the city giving way to the mournful cries of gulls overhead. It was a summer evening in June, and I had been instructed to follow three men, their features indistinguishable to me, yet their presence a whispered rumor of a crime waiting to unfold. They were said to be Indians, though I knew little of such men, only that they had been spotted on the Tower Wharf, boarding a steamer bound for Rotterdam.

I set out in pursuit, leaving behind the crowded streets of London and the comfort of familiar routines. My path took me to the docks, where the smell of tar and coal smoke hung heavy in the air. I boarded a steamer belonging to another company, one that sailed on the morning of Thursday the twenty-eighth, its departure a deliberate choice, designed to keep me at a distance from those I sought. As I watched the city recede into the distance, I felt a thrill of anticipation, for I knew that I was about to enter a world of strangers and unfamiliar landscapes.

At Rotterdam, I tracked down the commander of the previous steamer, a gruff, seasoned sailor who listened intently as I recounted my mission. He nodded thoughtfully, his eyes narrowing as he pieced together the fragments of information I provided. "They were indeed on board," he confirmed, "but only as far as Gravesend. One of them inquired about the time of arrival in Calais, and when informed of the error, they were eager to disembark."

His words painted a picture of men caught off guard, their plans foiled by a simple mistake. But I knew better than to trust appearances. Their willingness to sacrifice their passage money and abandon their journey suggested a level of desperation, a calculated risk taken to throw me off their trail. I thanked the commander for his time, my mind already racing ahead to the next stage of the chase.

As I boarded a local vessel to continue my pursuit, I couldn't shake the feeling that I was being herded toward a destination of their choosing. They had left Gravesend, and I had lost them in the labyrinthine streets of London. But I had a lead, a thread of information that hinted at their next move. They had sailed on the _Bewley Castle_, bound for Bombay, a journey that would take them across the vast expanse of the Indian Ocean.
