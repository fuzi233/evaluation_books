As I pressed northward and westward, I navigated with extreme caution for a long stretch, steering clear of the Great Watcher lurking in the Northwest. With every step, I weighed the necessities of my situation as best my imagination allowed. First, I calculated my pace, concluding that moderation was essential. Before me lay a vast and perilous journey, and who could truly foresee its end?

Next, I organized my routine. I decided to regulate my movements, meals, and sleep into a disciplined cycle to conserve my strength for the road ahead. I established a rhythm: eating and drinking every six hours, and sleeping from the eighteenth to the twenty-fourth hour. This schedule meant three meals and six hours of rest per day—a regimen I deemed excellent and strove to maintain throughout my travels in the Night Land. However, as anyone can imagine, there were frequent occasions when vigilance demanded I abandon sleep entirely, for the land teemed with grim dangers.

True to human nature, I broke this rule almost immediately. For twenty-one hours, I marched without cease, hiding and creeping wherever the terrain suggested the presence of the Watcher. Hunger gnawed at me, yet the thought of food sickened me, and I delayed eating until necessity forced my hand.

When those twenty-one hours finally elapsed, exhaustion and faintness overwhelmed me. I was compelled to seek shelter. Soon, I spotted a small firehole in the distance—one similar to several I had passed earlier. Drawing nearer, I hoped to find warmth against the biting chill of the Night Land and a dry spot for rest.

Upon reaching it, I found a surprisingly cheerful enclosure amidst the gloom. The hole was only a few paces wide, filled with a dull, bubbling fire that emitted a faint sulfur smoke. Sitting nearby, I placed my Diskos—the strange, disc-shaped weapon—upon the rock beside me.

For a while, I remained motionless, too weary to eat or drink. Instead, I cast my gaze backward toward the Mighty Pyramid. Though I had traveled a considerable distance, the structure loomed so close and immense that it simultaneously lifted my spirits and crushed my resolve. The sheer scale of the task ahead shook me, despite the comfort of nearing my home.

Yet, this was only one side of my turmoil. It was comforting to feel the proximity of my "Mighty Home," knowing that countless millions watched over me from within. Still, I made no signal; a constant farewell was inappropriate—I must simply *go*. It was strange to behave so distantly from humanity while standing so near, yet I accepted this conduct as necessary. I felt a pang of relief knowing that Master Monstruwacan likely observed me through the Great Spy-Glass, perhaps watching me in that very moment.

I chided myself for acting weakly by delaying food. Foolishly displaying weakness to my distant friend, I opened my satchel and consumed three compressed tablets. They were potent, dense nutrition, though insufficient to fully fill my stomach. To satisfy my thirst, I poured a fine dust from a special tube into a small cup. As the air interacted with the powder, a chemical reaction caused it to boil and fizz, transforming instantly into clear water. Thus, from a tiny satchel capable of sustaining life for weeks, I derived sustenance. While uncomfortable and lacking flavor, it was adequate for survival in this dark, starving land.

After eating, I reviewed my gear. Beside the Diskos and food supply, I carried a pouch containing various items. Among them was a small compass gifted by Master Monstruwacan. He had warned me that venturing far into the Night Land might lead me astray in the boundless darkness. If the ancient magnetic principles still functioned within the device—even if oriented toward the Pyramid rather than the North—it could guide me home. It was a marvel crafted by his skilled hands from an antique model housed in the Great Museum.

I set the compass on the ground, but it spun wildly, offering no direction. I realized I was still high above the underground fields and likely not far from the "Crack" in the earth below. I wondered if Master Monstruwacan could see my experiment through the Spy-Glass; the firelight was bright, and the glass powerful. Yet, I knew the search was unreliable. Strange fires flickered erratically, creating shifting patterns of light and shadow, while rising mists often obscured vision completely.

Putting the compass away, I prepared for sleep. Wrapping a cloak around my shoulders and hips, I lay down in the darkness beside the firehole. Placing the Diskos inside the cloak next to me, I felt a kinship with my faithful companion. As I drifted into a drowsy state, I sensed the aether surging around me, stirred by the sympathetic attention of the millions within the Redoubt. Exhausted, I fell into a deep, heavy sleep, my final conscious thought lingering on the sweet maiden I sought. In dream, I spoke with her, bathed in fairy-like light, free from life's sorrows.

Suddenly, a tremendous roar shattered my slumber. Instantly alert, I recognized the howling Voice of the Home-Call echoing across the Night. Swiftly slipping from under the cloak, I gripped the haft of the Diskos. Looking toward the Pyramid, I knew a crisis had erupted; some terror had approached, forcing the entire Night Land to awaken to the fact that a human had strayed from the refuge.

Just as I scanned the Great Redoubt, I dared not fixate solely on it. Fear drove me to survey the surroundings frantically, staring into the upper blackness where the Final Light of the Tower of Observation glowed. Suddenly, high above, brilliant flashes of green fire darted across the sky. Deciphered in the Set-Speech, they conveyed an urgent warning: a Great Grey Man had detected me and was crawling through the moss-bushes behind the firehole. The message commanded me to leap left and hide.

Before the signal faded, I dove into the shadows of a nearby clump of bushes. Trembling with cold terror, my heart raced, yet my spirit hardened to face the threat. Peering quietly from cover, I saw a massive figure emerge from the bushes beyond the fire. It was entirely grey, moving silently and brutishly, keeping its head low to avoid the glare of the flames. It scuttled back and forth three times to the left and three to the right, sniffing the air and thrusting its jaw grotesquely, behaving like a feral beast.

This display nearly broke my courage. Each time it retreated into the shadows, I feared it had discovered my location and intended to ambush me from behind. Fortunately, my heightened hearing saved me. After its last excursion, the creature returned to the bushes, seemingly giving up. But then, a voice spoke plainly within my soul, warning that it had actually located me and was circling to attack from the opposite flank.

I understood immediately: Master Monstruwacan was watching from the Tower, transmitting the warning via his mental link, relying on my innate Night-Hearing. Reinforced by the solemn throb of the Master-Word vibrating through the night air, I trusted the command. I sprang from my hiding spot, crouched in a different cluster of bushes, and kept my spiritual senses wide open, confident that the Master watched over me.

Abruptly, a bush directly behind my position rustled. A great grey hand pushed aside the foliage, followed by the massive head of the Grey Man, which entered the clump where I had concealed myself. Now was the moment to strike. I leaped forward and smashed the Diskos down upon it. The creature collapsed onto its side. Its great grey legs twitched and pulled upward from the bushes, but its head remained trapped in the vegetation where I had hidden.

I stepped back, allowing the monster to expire. In my hand, the Diskos began to spin, emitting a stream of fire as if alive, acknowledging the death of the horrid beast. Once the Grey Man was dead, I moved to the far side of the firehole, raising the spinning, flaming Diskos high. This was to signal the inhabitants of the Mighty Pyramid that I had defeated the attacker, ensuring they could see the proof even if the body lay in shadow.

Master Monstruwacan offered no further words. Such communication was reserved for immediate danger; given the sensitive powers of the Night Land, excessive signaling could invite further peril. Calming slightly, I noticed the aether rippling with joy from the millions in the Redoubt, their collective heartbeat mingling with my own in a mix of relief and fear.

Regaining my composure, I checked the time and realized I had slept through ten hours. Guilt washed over me; I had broken my strict schedule due to fatigue. Resolving to adhere to reason in the future, I gathered my cloak and supplies, turning back toward the Pyramid. I glanced up the great slope disappearing into the eternal night but offered no salute, wishing to avoid unnecessary disturbances to the world's aether.

Turning away, I moved swiftly and cautiously into the darkness, carrying the Diskos with a newfound affection. It felt like a living partner, bonded to me by the Earth-Current flowing within it. It was a known law that no one could wield another's Diskos effectively; strangers handled clumsily and suffered injury. This affinity stemmed from a deep connection between the user and the weapon. Consequently, tradition dictated that upon death, a person's Diskos was returned to the Earth-Current on The Last Road in the Country of Silence. While rooted in ancient custom, it reflected a humane sentiment: lovingly returning tools to the earth and honoring the dead.
