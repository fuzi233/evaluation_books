As the festive atmosphere of Christmas Eve descended upon the town of Bideford, the inhabitants gathered in merry groups, exchanging stories and laughter as they made their way home. Amidst the merriment, however, one figure stood out – a lone woman, shrouded in her black muffler, walking swiftly towards the long causeway and bridge leading to Northam town. Her name was Mrs. Leigh, and her somber demeanor seemed at odds with the joyous spirit of the season.

As she walked, she was intercepted by Sir Richard Grenville and his wife, who invited her to join them for a festive Christmas night filled with music, dance, and feasting. Mrs. Leigh's response was enigmatic, her smile hinting at a deeper sorrow as she pointed westward, towards the gathering storm. The group turned to follow her gaze, and it was then that they noticed the eerie silence that had fallen over the landscape. The sky above was a deepening grey, with misty stars peeking through the clouds, but to the west, the horizon was ablaze with a vibrant display of colors, as if the setting sun had left behind a trail of iridescent hues.

The sound that Mrs. Leigh referred to was not immediately apparent to the others, but as they listened intently, a low, deep roar began to build, echoing across the downs, woods, and salt-marshes, like the rumble of a distant thunderstorm or the approach of a mighty tidal wave. The air was heavy with anticipation, as if the very fabric of the atmosphere was charged with electricity.

Sir Richard attempted to reassure Mrs. Leigh, attributing the sound to the rough seas and turbulent weather, but she remained resolute in her conviction that her son, Amyas, was in grave danger. As the darkness deepened, Mrs. Leigh continued on her way, lost in thought, her heart heavy with worry and her spirit consumed by the prospect of the impending storm.

Meanwhile, in the bay of Smerwick, Amyas sat at the helm of a small boat, his voice carrying across the water as he rallied his crew to pull with all their might against the tempest. The Christmas Eve storm had been brewing for hours, its fury unleashed upon the Irish coast, and Amyas's words were a testament to the turmoil that lay ahead.
