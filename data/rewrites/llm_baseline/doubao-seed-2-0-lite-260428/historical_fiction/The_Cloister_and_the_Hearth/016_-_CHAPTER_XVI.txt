Martin Wittenhaagen braced at the foot of the cottage stairs, his yew bow drawn nearly to the arrowhead, a sharp hunting knife tucked out of sight behind his back, went utterly still with shock when the raiding party tramped back through the door without Gerard. He let the bowstring slack unbidden, his jaw hanging agape as he stared at the sodden, mud-caked men.

For their part, the bandits were equally confused to catch the old farmer in that obvious combat stance.
"Hey, mates, was the old sod about to put a bolt through us before we even crossed the threshold?"

Martin shook himself, his stolid, weathered composure snapping back into place fast. "Stuff and nonsense. I was just testing out my new bowstring, that's all. There, see? I'll unstring the whole thing if you're so nervous about it."

Dierich, the party’s surly leader, narrowed his eyes suspiciously. "Hmph. There’s more going on with you than I can puzzle out. Go heave a good log on that hearth – we’ll dry our hides a bit before we head out again."

A roaring blaze caught fast, and the men clustered around it, their heavy wool cloaks and matted uncut hair wisping steam as they thawed from the chill of the moor. That was when the high, sharp, terrified shrieks rang out from Margaret’s bedchamber at the top of the stairs. The whole group jumped to their feet at once, one man snatching up the sputtering tallow candle to go bounding up the bedroom steps.

Martin lurched up in a panic, his wits reeling from the sudden screams. Terrified the man would harm the girl he’d sworn to protect, he threw himself forward to block the path. Dierich lunged at him from behind, wrapping both arms tight around his chest and yelling for the others to hold the old brute down. The man with the candle slipped past the scuffle and vanished up the stairs, and the rest of the gang piled onto Martin all at once. The struggle was long and brutal: over and over, the whole heaving knot of men rolled across the packed earthen floor with Martin at its center, the old countryman strong as a grizzled Samson even against four younger men. At last they managed to overpower him, and bind his hands and feet tight with the thick hemp rope they had brought along specifically to tie up Gerard.

Martin groaned aloud. He knew the intruder had reached Margaret’s room while they were all distracted by the fight, and now he was completely helpless to stop whatever might happen to her.

"Go on, grind your teeth all you want, you old rogue," Dierich panted, red and sweating from the brawl. "You won’t be sinking them into any of us now. I swear mates, our lives weren’t even safe a minute while this old fellow’s bones were free to move."

"Wait a minute," another bandit put in, frowning. "Makes me think that Gerard we were chasing can’t be far off, hiding somewhere nearby."

"No such luck for us, or him," Dierich scoffed. Then he tilted his head, frowning. "Hallo, lads. Jorian Ketel’s been up in that girl’s bedroom an awful long time. Some of you go see what’s keeping him, won’t you?"

The crude, leering laugh that rippled through the gang barely died away before they heard rapid, thudding footsteps sprinting across the upper floorboards.
"Oh, here he comes at last! Well, Jorian – what the devil is going on up there?"
