Errina sat holding the spindle, her thick coils of raven hair framing a face of deepest agate. With a sorrowful startle, she gazed at the surging visions of her fate: to spin the byssus in weary, insect-like toil while gods and men around her forged legends worthy of song.

When Daniel Deronda stepped into the antechamber of his mother's apartment in the *Italia*, a familiar tremor of boyish anxiety seized him. Two servants stared at him with marked surprise; the doctor his mother had summoned was a striking young man whose very presence seemed to adorn the severe lines of his evening wear. Yet, Deronda paid them no mind until the inner door swung open, revealing a figure waiting at the far end of the vast room.

She was draped in black lace that hung loosely from the summit of her whitening hair down to a long train trailing behind her tall form. Her arms, bare to the elbow save for rich bracelets, were folded before her, and the poised tilt of her head rendered her visage more handsome than reality warranted. Deronda felt no hesitation before stepping forward, taking the hand she offered and pressing it to his lips. She retained his grip, studying him intently, while his own overwhelming awareness was of her piercing gaze and a face so fluid it seemed capable of transforming into someone entirely different in the next instant. Even as she examined him, a subtle interplay of brow and nostril formed a silent language. Paralyzed, unable to guess what emotional display she required, Deronda flushed like a schoolgirl, yet marveled at his own lack of profound reaction. He had rehearsed countless idealized reunions with his mother in his mind, imagining them more vivid than this stark reality. He could not even predict the tongue she would use. Suddenly, she released his hand, placing both palms upon his shoulders. In doing so, her face flashed with a radiant admiration that smoothed every wrinkle, restoring a youthful glow.

"You are a beautiful creature!" she murmured in a low, melodic voice, her syllables possessing a foreign yet pleasing cadence. "I knew you would be." She kissed him on each cheek, and he returned the gesture. It felt less like a reunion between kin and more like a greeting between sovereigns.

After a brief pause, as the lines of age crept back into her features, her tone hardened. "I am your mother. But you can hold no love for me."

"I have thought of you more than of any other being in the world," Deronda replied, his voice trembling with nervous intensity.

"I am not what you imagined," she stated decisively, pulling her hands away to fold them once more, inviting him to scrutinize her. He had often pictured her face mirroring his own; now he saw traces of that resemblance amidst startling differences. She was a remarkable entity. What caused him such a painful sense of detachment? Her worn beauty possessed a strangeness, as if she were not quite a human mother, but a Melusine bound to a realm independent of their own.

"I used to think you might be suffering," Deronda said, driven by a desperate need not to wound her. "I wished I could comfort you."

"I *am* suffering. But with a suffering you cannot alleviate," the Princess said, her voice sterner than before. She moved to a sofa where cushions had been meticulously arranged. "Sit down." She indicated a seat nearby. Noticing the distress clouding Deronda's countenance, she softened. "I am not suffering at this moment. I am at ease. I am able to speak."

Deronda sat, waiting for her to continue. It felt as though he stood before a mysterious Fate rather than the long-awaited mother. He watched her with wonder, separated from her by a spiritual distance she had erected.

"No," she began. "I did not summon you to comfort me. I could not foresee what you would feel toward me. I possess no foolish notion that you can love me simply because I am your mother, especially since you have never seen or heard of me in your entire life. However, I believed I had chosen something superior for you than mere companionship. I did not realize I was depriving you of anything valuable."

"You cannot expect me to believe your affection would have been worthless," Deronda countered, noting her pause as if she awaited a response.

"I do not intend to speak ill of myself," the Princess declared with proud impetuosity. "Yet, I had little affection to offer. I craved none. I had been suffocated by it. I sought to live out the life inherent within me, unhampered by other lives. You wonder what I was? I was no princess then." Rising abruptly, she stood as she had before; Deronda rose instantly, feeling breathless.

"No princess in this tame existence I lead now. I was a great singer, and an actor as well. All others were poor comparisons. Men followed me from nation to nation. I lived a thousand lives in one. I desired no child."

A passionate defense colored her tone. She had discarded all social precedents, seeking justification only in the most intense words available to describe her experience. She seemed to hurl her final defenses against potential reproaches in her son's mind. Deronda stood clutching his coat collar as if clinging to a lifeline, his blood churning with a commotion akin to witnessing a sacred ritual of crime. What else could she reveal? Continuing with the same ferocity, a pale illumination in her face, she spoke:

"I refused to marry. I was forced into wedlock with your father—not by my will, but by my father's commands. Moreover, it was my finest strategy for securing freedom. I could rule my husband, but not my father. I had a right to liberty. I had a right to flee a bondage I loathed."

Seating herself again, she performed a subtle shift in her eyes and closed lips, signaling a suppressed continuation of speech. Deronda remained standing. After a moment, she looked up, her plea less defiant now.

"And the bondage I despised for myself, I intended to spare you. What greater gift could a loving mother bestow? I liberated you from the bondage of being born a Jew."

"Then I *am* a Jew?" Deronda exploded with a deep, resonant energy that made his mother recoil slightly against her cushions. "My father was a Jew, and you are a Jewess?"

"Yes, your father was my cousin," the mother replied, observing him with a shifting expression, as if dreading a revelation she might regret.

"I am glad of it," Deronda said impetuously, his voice veiled in passion. He could not have anticipated the vehemence of his declaration, nor dreamed it would oppose his mother so sharply. He was shaken by a mixed anger, unchecked by reason, directed at this mother who seemed to have borne him reluctantly, made herself a stranger, and perhaps now revealed herself unwillingly. This suspicion illuminated her previous words.

The mother, too, was shaken by a differently blended anger, her composure failing physically. Her eyes widened in pallid excitement as she cried out violently, "Why do you claim gladness? You are an English gentleman. I secured that status for you."

"You did not know what you secured for me. How could you choose my birthright for me?" Deronda retorted, sinking sideways into his chair, leaning his arm over the back, averting his gaze. An intolerant fire burned within him, foreign yet undeniable. Struggling to master himself, fearing he might utter something irreversible in this epochal moment, he fell silent.

A pause ensued before his mother resumed, her voice firmer, its finely varied tones resisting defeat. "I chose for you what I would have chosen for myself. How could I know you possessed the spirit of my father? How could I know you would love what I detested—if you truly love to be a Jew?" The bitterness in her last words suggested a hatred arising between mother and son.

However, Deronda reclaimed his full composure. Recalling the realities of her life, whose prime years had faded, and seeing the signs of suffering etched into her frame as she struggled to recount a past shared by neither of them, his habitual shame regarding the acceptance of fate aided him. As he looked at her silently following her final words, a penetrating calm returned to his face, yet it exerted a strangely agitating influence. Her eyes fixed upon him with fascination, devoid of maternal delight.

"Forgive me if I speak hastily," he said with diffident gravity. "Why have you decided now to disclose to me what you ensured I remain ignorant of? Why, given your apparent anger at my gladness?"

"Oh—the reasons of our actions!" the Princess remarked with a ring of sarcastic scorn. "When you reach my age, it will not appear so simple a query: 'Why did you do this?' People discuss motives in neat packages. Every woman is presumed to share a standard set of motivations, or else she is a monster. I am not a monster, yet I have not felt exactly as other women feel—or say they feel, terrified of appearing unlike them. When you reproach me inwardly for sending you away, you imply I should claim to have felt about you as other mothers claim to feel about their children. I did *not* feel that. I rejoiced in being freed from you. Nevertheless, I acted rightly for you, providing your father's fortune. Do I seem to revoke everything now? Well, there are reasons.
