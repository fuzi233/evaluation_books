The Assembly Rooms buzzed with the soft murmur of conversation as Harry Warrington led his friends into the grand ballroom. Generous to a fault, he had spared no expense in hosting this evening's entertainment, inviting nearly everyone from the Wells to join in the merriment. Card tables were set up in one corner, where those who couldn't resist the allure of chance and skill gathered to play. A nearby room was filled with the tantalizing aroma of supper, served with champagne and negus in abundance. But the pièce de résistance was the ball itself, where Harry's guests could indulge in the traditional minuet, a dance that brought a touch of elegance to the evening.

As the music began, a few couples took to the floor, their steps measured and refined. Among them, Harry danced the first minuet with his cousin, earning the approval of the entire room. He acquitted himself admirably, moving with a poise that far surpassed that of Mr. Wolfe, who stood up with Miss Lowther. Having finished his dance with Lady Maria, the Earl's daughter, Harry requested the pleasure of Miss Theo's company for the next minuet. Her blush deepened as she accepted, and together they glided across the floor to the delight of their parents and the dismay of Miss Humpleby, who had hoped to stand up next.

As the evening wore on, the minuets gave way to country dances, accompanied by a trio of musicians perched in a small balcony above. The harp, fiddle, and flageolet wove a melancholic melody that seemed to whisper secrets from a bygone era. One couldn't help but wonder how people had once found these tunes anything but somber. Yet, despite their mournful air, the dancers laughed and flirted, their joy infectious. It was as if the music itself was a bridge between past and present, a reminder that even the most nostalgic of melodies can hold a beauty that transcends time.

As the night wore on, Harry was joined by a distinguished guest, the Duchess of Queensberry, a renowned beauty who had captured the hearts of many. Though she turned her back on a countess who sat nearby, laughing loudly and gesturing with her fan, the rest of the company fawned over her, barely acknowledging the presence of the Duchess.
