As we gathered in the classroom on that sweltering summer morning in July, the air thick with anticipation, the whispers of our impending lesson hung heavy in the air. The first class sat poised, their neatly tied compositions, a testament to their diligence, awaiting the arrival of our erudite Professor. But as the moments ticked by, the door swung open, and instead of the swift and fiery figure we had grown accustomed to, the measured steps of Madame Beck entered our midst.

She paused before M. Paul's desk, drawing the light shawl around her shoulders, her voice taking on a low, resolute tone as she began to speak. "Today, there will be no lesson of literature." The pause that followed, like a held breath, lasted for what felt like an eternity before she continued. "It is probable that lessons will be suspended for a week, during which time I shall endeavour to secure a suitable replacement for M. Emanuel. In the interim, I suggest we devote our study to filling the void usefully."

Her words hung in the air like a challenge, her gaze fixed intently on us as she delivered the next blow. "Your Professor, ladies," she announced, "has been summoned to a distant land, a call he cannot ignore. He intends to bid us farewell, but alas, time constraints do not permit him to do so in person. Instead, you will spend this morning studying English with Mademoiselle Lucy."

As she spoke, a hush fell over the room, punctuated only by the soft rustling of papers and the occasional sniffle. Some among us were visibly shaken, their faces etched with sorrow and uncertainty. I, however, felt a surge of responsibility, a duty to maintain order and discipline in the face of chaos. With a deep breath, I rose to my feet, my voice steady and authoritative as I called out, "Let us proceed with our reading. Mademoiselle Lucy, please lead us through the assigned texts."

As we delved into our studies, the whispers and murmurs gradually subsided, replaced by the soft scratch of pens on paper and the gentle rustle of pages turning. Though my heart went out to those struggling to contain their emotions, I remained firm, guiding them through the exercises with a steady hand, determined to see us through this difficult hour.
