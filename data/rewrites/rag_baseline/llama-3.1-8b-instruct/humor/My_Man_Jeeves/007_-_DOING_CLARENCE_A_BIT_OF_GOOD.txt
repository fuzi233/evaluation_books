DOING CLARENCE A BIT OF GOOD


The rustic charm of the countryside beckoned, its verdant hues and soothing rhythms a far cry from the frenetic pace of city life. Yet, as I unfolded the letter, a flutter of excitement coursed through my veins, threatening to upset the delicate balance of my morning routine. Elizabeth Yeardsley's handwriting danced across the page, a vibrant splash of color amidst the dull monotony of my daily existence. Her words transported me to a world of bucolic bliss, replete with rolling hills, fragrant gardens, and the gentle lapping of a tranquil lake against the shore.

It had been a while since I'd seen Elizabeth, and I'd often found myself wondering how she fared. Her family, the esteemed Shoolbreds of Worcestershire, had always been a benchmark of refinement and elegance. I recalled the countless afternoons we'd spent strolling through galleries, Elizabeth's passion for art igniting a spark within me that I'd never quite extinguished. Our engagement, though short-lived, had left an indelible mark on my heart, and I'd often found myself reminiscing about those halcyon days.

The news of her marriage to Clarence Yeardsley, an artist, had taken me by surprise. I'd heard rumors of their country estate, where they'd settled down to live a life of quiet contemplation. The idea of Elizabeth surrounded by rolling hills and artistic pursuits had seemed idyllic, a far cry from the whirlwind romance we'd shared. Yet, as I read her letter, I sensed a hint of restlessness, a yearning for connection that transcended the tranquil facade.

Her invitation to visit was a tantalizing prospect, one that promised to reunite me with Elizabeth and her brother Bill, whom I'd not seen in months. The mention of their estate's golf course, touted as a rival to the legendary St. Andrews, was the clincher. I'd always been an aficionado of the game, and the prospect of playing on such a renowned course was too enticing to resist. As I finished reading the letter, a plan began to take shape in my mind – I would accept Elizabeth's invitation, and, in doing so, rediscover the beauty of the countryside, the allure of art, and the complexities of my own heart.
