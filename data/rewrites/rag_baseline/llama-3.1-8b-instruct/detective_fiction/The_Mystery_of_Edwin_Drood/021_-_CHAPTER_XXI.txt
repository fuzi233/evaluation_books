The morning sunlight filtering through the stained glass windows cast intricate patterns on the stone floor of the Minor Canon's quarters. Rosa Bud stood by the window, her eyes fixed on the river as the water lapped against the shore. The sound was soothing, a gentle reminder of the world outside these confined walls. She turned to face Mr. Grewgious, who sat behind his desk, his eyes narrowed in contemplation.

"We've made little progress, haven't we?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Mr. Grewgious nodded, his expression unreadable. "We've considered the options, but nothing seems clear-cut. Perhaps a fresh perspective is needed."

Rosa's gaze drifted to Mr. Crisparkle, who stood by the fireplace, staring into the ashes. He seemed lost in thought, his brow furrowed in concentration. The door creaked open, and a young woman entered, her dark hair tied back in a neat knot.

"Unlimited, what brings you here?" Mr. Grewgious asked, his tone neutral.

"The gentleman from Staple Inn has arrived," she replied, her eyes darting between the three adults. "He requests to see Mr. Crisparkle."

Rosa's heart skipped a beat as she turned to her guardian. "Do you think it's a good idea?"

Mr. Grewgious hesitated before nodding. "It couldn't hurt to hear what he has to say."

The gentleman entered, his brown hair mussed, his eyes bright with a hint of mischief. Rosa's instincts screamed at her to be cautious, but something about him seemed familiar.

"You're the gentleman from Staple Inn," Mr. Crisparkle said, his voice firm but polite.

The gentleman smiled, his eyes crinkling at the corners. "That's correct. But who am I, really?"

Mr. Crisparkle's expression changed, his eyes narrowing as he searched his memory. Rosa watched, fascinated, as the gears turned in his mind.

And then, in an instant, recognition dawned. "Tartar!" Mr. Crisparkle exclaimed, his face lighting up with joy.

The two men shook hands, their faces alight with smiles. Rosa felt a pang of sadness, remembering the stories Mr. Crisparkle had shared about his childhood friend. They had been inseparable, and now, years later, they were reunited.
