As the sun cast its golden light upon the quiet streets of Milton, Margaret and her father, Mr. Hale, made their way towards Nicholas Higgins' humble abode, their footsteps echoing through the stillness. The memory of their recent loss lingered, and they found themselves drawn closer together, their unspoken understanding a palpable force between them. The air was heavy with the scent of wood smoke and damp earth, a reminder of the changing seasons.

Upon arriving, they found Nicholas seated by the fire, his rugged features softened by the flickering flames. His usually tidy appearance was marred by a few days' growth of stubble and a jacket in need of repair. Yet, his eyes sparkled with warmth as he welcomed them, his voice low and soothing.

"Come, sit down, sit down," he said, poking the fire with a gnarled stick, as if to distract from his own troubles. "The fire's dying out." His words were laced with a hint of melancholy, a sorrow that seemed to cling to him like a shroud.

"We thought we'd catch you after dinner," Margaret said, her voice gentle as a summer breeze.

"Aye, sorrows are plentiful these days," Nicholas replied, his gaze drifting towards the floor. "Dinner hour stretches across the day, and I'm often found here, lost in thought."

Margaret's eyes met her father's, and she knew they shared a common concern. "Are you out of work?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Nicholas's expression darkened, and for a moment, he seemed to collect his thoughts. "Aye, I am," he said finally, his voice tinged with a hint of bitterness. "Not wanting brass, mind you. Bess left a small nest egg for me, and Mary's learning the trade of fustian-cutting. But I'm out of work, nonetheless."

"We owe Mary a debt of gratitude," Mr. Hale began, but Margaret's gentle pressure on his arm stayed his words.

"If she takes it, I'll turn her out," Nicholas said, his voice rising. "I'll stay within these four walls, and she'll be forced to leave. That's the way of it."

"But we're grateful for her kindness," Mr. Hale persisted.

"I never thanked her properly for her love and care," Nicholas said, his voice cracking. "I've been trying to find the words, but they elude me."
