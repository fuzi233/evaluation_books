Ah, "getting on in the world"—what a dreadfully serious topic for a professional loafer like myself to ponder! But then again, who better to comment on the frenzied race of humanity than someone who has, quite deliberately, taken a seat on the sidelines? From my luxurious perch of laziness (metaphorically speaking—my actual perch is a battered armchair with suspicious stains), I observe the grand parade of life with the detached amusement of a cat watching a particularly chaotic game of fetch.

What a spectacle it is! A never-ending stampede of humanity, all elbowing, shoving, and occasionally tripping over their own shoelaces in their desperate scramble toward… well, whatever it is they think counts as "success." Young and old, rich and poor, the clever and the spectacularly dim—all hurtling down the same crowded road like shoppers on the first day of a sale.

You’ve got your classic overachievers, shoulders set like they’re charging into battle, and your schemers slinking through gaps like pickpockets at a fair. There’s the bookworm, nose buried in *How to Succeed Without Really Trying*, promptly being trampled by a stampede of people who didn’t bother to read it. Here’s a fellow who looks like he’d rather be anywhere else, dragged along by a determined spouse; there’s a starry-eyed youth already regretting leaving his village the moment he steps in a puddle. And let’s not forget the rogue who thinks zigzagging counts as progress, or the dreamer who keeps stopping to wonder if the destination is worth the blisters.

It’s a glorious mess—duchesses elbowing clerks, politicians shouting promises they’ll forget by lunchtime, poets dodging tax collectors. Everyone’s in a hurry, yet nobody seems to be getting anywhere faster. The road’s dusty, the competition’s ruthless, and the only rest stops appear to be metaphorical ditches where the exhausted collapse. And yet, who can resist joining in? It’s like watching a brawl in a pub—sooner or later, you’re going to want to throw a punch, or at least cheer someone on.

Now, the moralists will wag their fingers and say ambition is vulgar, that contentment is noble. Rubbish. Contentment is what happens when you run out of ideas and take up amateur flute-playing. Ambition is what built everything from pyramids to pub snacks. Sure, the ambitious might be exhausting at parties, but without them, we’d all still be living in caves, contentedly gnawing raw mammoth and wondering what this "wheel" nonsense is about.

Let’s be honest—getting ahead is a game. A brutal, ridiculous, occasionally rigged game, but a game all the same. And like any good game, the fun isn’t just in winning; it’s in the scrap, the near misses, the sheer audacity of playing at all. The contented folk? Lovely people, but they’re the audience, not the players. And audiences don’t get trophies (unless they catch a foul ball, and even then, it’s debatable).

So step right up! The great carnival of life is open to all. Prizes may include wealth, fame, or just the satisfaction of having annoyed the right people. Blanks are guaranteed for most, but who cares? The real trophy is the chaos you create along the way.

Now if you’ll excuse me, I’ve just spotted an opportunity to nap through the next round. Some of us are professionals, after all.
