As the rain pattered against the windows of Offendene, casting a melancholy veil over the landscape, Gwendolen sat ensconced in the drawing-room, her fingers poised over the needlework that had become her reluctant companion. Grandcourt, reclining on the sofa, watched her with an air of quiet interest, his hand resting idly on the back of the cushion, the other tucked between his knees. The last few days had been filled with the rhythmic cadence of horseback rides, but amidst the leisurely pace, Gwendolen had found herself drawn to the quiet intimacy of their domestic routines.

She had always been skeptical of needlework, yet lately, it had become a necessary indulgence, a way to fill the hours that stretched before her like an endless canvas. Grandcourt's responses to her questions about his past were measured and deliberate, a soothing balm to her curiosity. She had grown accustomed to the feeling that he knew exactly how to present himself, that his words were carefully chosen to convey a sense of superiority without arrogance. It was a subtle art, one that he wielded with ease, leaving her to wonder if he was indeed the master of his own destiny.

As she worked, Gwendolen's mind wandered to the day he had kissed her neck, the fleeting touch sending a shiver down her spine. She had laughed it off, attributing her agitation to a mere ticklishness, but deep within, a nagging fear lingered. She was no longer the free spirit she once was, no longer able to dismiss him with the same carefree abandon she had reserved for Rex Gascoigne. The memory of that kiss still lingered, a whispered promise of a deeper connection that she dare not explore.

The package from London, a gift from Grandcourt, lay scattered about the room, its contents a testament to his refined taste. Mrs. Davilow had just departed, leaving behind a trail of admiration for the beautiful objects that adorned the tables. Gwendolen's eyes roamed over the scene, taking in the tranquil atmosphere that had become her reality.

"Why is tomorrow the only day?" she asked, her voice laced with a hint of playfulness.

"The hounds meet the following day," Grandcourt replied, his eyes never leaving hers.

"And after that?"

"After that, I must attend to some matters at Diplow," he said, his voice tinged with a hint of reluctance. "It's a bore, but I shall be back the next day."
