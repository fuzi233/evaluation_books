Search

The air in the room, though shaken by our frantic entry, remained unnervingly still. As the dust settled over the chaos, we managed to compose ourselves enough to order the guards away. Mademoiselle proposed a chilling hypothesis: perhaps Carmilla, startled by the commotion at her threshold, had leaped from her bed in a primal panic, retreating behind a heavy curtain or into the depths of a wardrobe, hidden from view until the majordomo and his minions departed. This theory compelled us to scour the space anew, calling her name with growing desperation.

Silence answered us. Our confusion deepened into a cold dread. We checked every window; they were barred tight against intrusion. I pleaded with Carmilla, begging her not to torment us further, imploring her to reveal herself if she truly lay concealed. Yet, the room remained empty. I became certain she was not here, nor in the adjacent dressing room, whose door stood firmly locked from within. She could not have slipped through. A terrifying question gnawed at me: had Carmilla discovered one of those legendary secret passages whispered about by the ancient housekeeper, tunnels where tradition held sway but knowledge had long faded? Time, I reasoned, must eventually unravel this knot, yet for now, the shadows seemed to swallow us whole.

Past four o'clock, the darkness pressing down felt suffocating. I resolved to spend these final hours in Madame's chamber, praying that dawn might bring answers.

But daybreak brought no solace. By morning, the entire estate was a hive of agitated frenzy, led by my father. Every corner of the château was ransacked, the grounds combed bare. Not a thread, not a footprint, betrayed the missing lady. The stream was about to be dredged; my father trembled on the brink of madness, haunted by the nightmare of explaining this absence to the grieving mother who awaited them. And I, too, teetered on the edge of despair, though my sorrow twisted into something far sharper than simple loss.

Hours bled away in a feverish loop of alarm. Then, at one o'clock, I burst into Carmilla's room. The scene before me stopped my heart. She stood motionless before her dressing table, bathed in the pale light. I stared, unable to comprehend what I saw. With a delicate, silent gesture, she beckoned me closer. Her face, usually radiant, wore an expression of profound, trembling terror.

I rushed to her, overcome with a surge of joy so intense it nearly blinded me, embracing her repeatedly. I clattered the bell, summoning everyone to witness her return and ease my father's torment.

"Dearest Carmilla!" I cried, my voice breaking. "What horrors have you endured? Where have you been?"

She looked at me, her eyes wide. "Last night," she whispered, "was a night of wonders."

"For heaven's sake, speak!" I begged. "Explain anything you remember."

"It grew late," she said, her voice thin, "past two, when I fell asleep as I always do, with both doors locked—the bedroom and the dressing room. My slumber was unbroken, dreamless, I swear. But when I awoke moments ago, I found myself on the sofa in the dressing room. The door between the chambers stood ajar, and the outer door... it had been forced."

"How?" I gasped. "How could you move without waking? And how could you be carried from your bed without stirring? I am the first to wake at the rustle of a leaf!"

Soon, Madame, Mademoiselle, and a throng of servants flooded the room, bombarding her with questions and relief. Carmilla, however, seemed the least capable of offering an explanation, her countenance a mask of bewildered confusion.

When the servants finally withdrew, and Mademoiselle returned with a vial of valerian, leaving only my father, Madame, and Carmilla alone, my father approached slowly. He took her hand gently, guiding her to the sofa where he sat beside her.

"Calm yourself, my dear," he said softly. "May I offer a guess, and ask a single question?"

"Who holds truer authority?" she replied. "Ask whatever you wish, and I shall tell you all. Though my mind is a fog of bewilderment. I know nothing. Remember, my father, the limits Mother places upon my speech."

"I understand, child. Let us avoid those forbidden topics." He paused, studying her. "The miracle of last night lies in your disappearance from a locked room and bed, appearing elsewhere without a sound. Windows were sealed, doors bolted from within. Here is my theory, followed by a question."

Carmilla leaned heavily on her hand, her brow furrowed. Madame and I held our breath.

"Do you recall," my father asked, "being suspected of somnambulism as a child?"

"Never, not seriously," she murmured.

"But you did wander in your sleep?"

"Yes," she admitted quietly. "My old nurse spoke of it often."

My father nodded, a faint smile touching his lips. "Then listen closely. Last night, you rose in your sleep. You unlocked the door, removing the key rather than dropping it inside, then fastened it from the outside. You took the key with you, wandering into one of the twenty-five rooms scattered across this floor, or perhaps higher or lower. This ancient house is a labyrinth of closets and heavy furniture; a thorough search would take weeks. Do you see?"

"I see the path, but not the destination," she confessed.

"And why," Madame interjected gently, "were you found on the sofa in the dressing room, a place we had already cleared?"

"She followed you there," my father said, his voice warm with reassurance, "still drifting in dreams, until she awoke spontaneously. Imagine her surprise to find herself there, just as surely as you were shocked." He laughed lightly, dismissing the tension. "Let us celebrate that the simplest truth prevails: no drugs, no tampered locks, no burglars, poisons, or witches. There is no threat to your safety, Carmilla."

She looked around, her features regaining their luminous charm. Her beauty seemed heightened by a graceful languor, a quiet exhaustion that made her appear even more ethereal. I noticed my father's gaze linger on her, a subtle comparison forming in his eyes.

"I wish," he sighed, turning to me, "that my poor Laura looked half as well as she does now."

With the mystery solved, the alarms subsided. Carmilla was restored to her circle, her strange journey unexplained but ultimately harmless, leaving the house once again shrouded in its deceptive peace.
