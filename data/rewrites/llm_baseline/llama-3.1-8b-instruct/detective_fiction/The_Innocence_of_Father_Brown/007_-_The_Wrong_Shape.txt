The village of St. Mungo lay nestled in the heart of the countryside, its rustic charm and tranquility a world away from the hustle and bustle of city life. A meandering road wound its way through the village, lined with a patchwork of shops, fields, and homes, each one a testament to the region's rich history and cultural heritage. Among the thatched roofs and whitewashed facades, one house stood out – a long, low bungalow painted in soft whites and pale greens, its veranda and sun-blinds a beacon of warmth and hospitality.

The house seemed to whisper tales of far-off lands and exotic climes, its very architecture an homage to the Anglo-Indian influences that had shaped the lives of its former inhabitants. As one gazed upon its tranquil façade, a sense of mystique settled over the viewer, a feeling that there was more to this unassuming abode than met the eye. And indeed, there was.

On the Thursday before Whit-Sunday, at half-past four in the afternoon, the front door swung open, revealing a glimpse into the life within. Father Brown, the humble priest of St. Mungo, emerged onto the porch, his pipe clenched between his teeth as he chatted with a tall, lanky figure – his friend Flambeau, a Frenchman with a quick wit and a love of fine cigars. As they strolled out into the sunlight, the door creaked open further, allowing a tantalizing glimpse into the world beyond.

The interior of the house was a marvel, its layout a deliberate echo of the traditional Indian T-shape. The main wing, a sprawling two-story affair, boasted an array of grand rooms, each one a testament to the refined tastes of its owner, the celebrated poet Leonard Quinton. His study, tucked away in the rear wing, was a treasure trove of colorful trinkets and exotic artifacts, a space where the boundaries between reality and fantasy blurred. Adjacent to the study lay a glass conservatory, a riotous explosion of color and scent, its tropical blooms a symphony of vibrant hues and intoxicating aromas.

As the door swung open, passersby would pause, transfixed by the breathtaking vista that unfolded before them – a kaleidoscope of colors, a whirlwind of light and shadow that seemed to transport them to a realm both fantastical and real. Leonard Quinton, the mastermind behind this dazzling spectacle, had poured his very essence into every detail
