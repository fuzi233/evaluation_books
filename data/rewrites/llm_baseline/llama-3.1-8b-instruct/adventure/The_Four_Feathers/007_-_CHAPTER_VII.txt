As the sun climbed higher in the cloudless sky, casting an unforgiving glare over the parched landscape, Durrance adjusted his field-glasses, the worn leather case creaking softly against his side. Captain Mather nodded in agreement, his eyes scanning the horizon for any sign of movement.

"We'll press on," Durrance said, his voice carrying above the gentle clinking of equipment and the soft padding of the camels' hooves on the sandy terrain.

The scouts led the way, their path winding through the rugged gulley of shale, the rocky walls rising up on either side like sentinels. As they descended, the silence was broken only by the occasional rustle of a mimosa branch or the faint cry of a bird. The air was heavy with the scent of acacia trees, their branches stretching out like skeletal fingers towards the sky.

After what felt like an eternity, the camels crested a rise, and the sight of Sinkat's lush plateau unfolded before them like a verdant oasis. The troopers' faces brightened, their weariness momentarily forgotten in the face of the tranquil beauty before them. Durrance's party moved cautiously across the plateau, their footsteps quiet on the soft earth, as they made their way towards the distant mountains of Erkoweet.

The air was still, the only sounds the soft thud of hooves and the occasional whir of a pigeon taking flight as they approached. Yet, despite the seeming desolation, there was a sense of life beneath the surface – a herd of gazelle would suddenly spring into view, their brown and white coats blending seamlessly into the surrounding landscape as they vanished into the scrubby undergrowth.

Durrance's thoughts turned to the ancient trade routes that crisscrossed this land, the caravans that had once passed through this very spot on their way to Erkoweet and the Khor Baraka. He remembered the tales of the Suakis, who had built their summer-houses on this very plateau, their nomadic lifestyle a testament to the enduring spirit of this unforgiving yet beautiful land.

Captain Mather followed his gaze, his eyes drawn to the distant hills where Tewfik and his men had fought and fallen, their bravery and sacrifice etched into the annals of history. The sun beat down relentlessly, its rays baking the earth and draining the colour from the scene before them.
