As the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, our family stirred to life. After a hasty breakfast, we set to work with the bird-lime, a concoction of caoutchouc oil and turpentine that I had concocted the previous day. My sons, eager to assist, busied themselves preparing the rods, smearing them with the sticky mixture and placing them strategically among the upper branches, where the pigeons congregated in droves.

Their numbers were staggering, a veritable sea of grey and brown feathers that seemed to stretch on forever. I couldn't help but think back to the previous year, when we had first arrived on these shores, and the few scattered pigeons we had encountered were a mere whisper of what we now faced. The sweet acorns of the evergreen oaks, too, were a magnet for the birds, drawing them in with their irresistible allure. As I watched, I realized that the birds must have been roosting on the branches at night, their presence evident in the trampled undergrowth.

With this newfound understanding, I resolved to devise a plan to harvest the pigeons, inspired by the tactics employed by the colonists in Virginia. Meanwhile, the bird-lime proved to be a worthy adversary, ensnaring the pigeons with an unyielding grip. As they flailed and struggled, they became increasingly entangled in the sticky mess, until finally, with pitiful cries, they fell to the ground, sticks and all.

My sons, now seasoned in the art of bird-catching, worked with ease, leaving me to tend to other matters. I busied myself preparing torches for the night ahead, combining pinewood and turpentine in a mixture that would illuminate the darkness. Jack, ever the gentle soul, presented me with a particularly plump pigeon, one that seemed to possess an air of refinement, unlike its more rustic companions. I recognized it as one of our own European breed, and I carefully freed it from the bird-lime's grasp, placing it in a wicker cage for safekeeping. I instructed Jack to capture any others like it, and he soon returned with several pairs, which I was delighted to add to our collection.
