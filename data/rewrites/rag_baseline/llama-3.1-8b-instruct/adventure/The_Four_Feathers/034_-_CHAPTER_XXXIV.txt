As I stepped onto the sun-drenched veranda of Broad Place, the scent of freshly brewed coffee wafted through the air, mingling with the sweet fragrance of blooming jasmine. The warm breeze carried the distant sounds of the countryside, a soothing melody that lulled me into a state of tranquil contemplation. Two elderly gentlemen sat engrossed in their newspapers, their faces set in determined lines, their eyes scanning the pages with an intensity that belied their advanced years. The elder of the two, General Feversham, turned over a sheet, his eyes widening as he read the headlines.

"Osman Digna's back at Suakin," he murmured, his voice laced with a hint of unease. His companion, Lieutenant Sutch, looked up from his own paper, his expression skeptical.

"Oh, he won't do much harm," he said, laying his newspaper aside. As he spoke, the tranquil English countryside melted away, replaced by visions of the bustling port city of Suakin, its white buildings shimmering in the heat, the brown plains stretching out to the horizon like a canvas of golden sand, dotted with the tangled tufts of halfa grass. In the distance, the rolling hills rose up towards Khor Gwob, their rugged contours etched against the sky like the pages of a well-worn book.

"A stuffy place, Suakin, isn't it, Sutch?" General Feversham asked, his voice tinged with a hint of wistfulness. Lieutenant Sutch nodded in agreement, his eyes clouding over as he recalled the stifling heat of the port city.

"Appallingly stuffy," he agreed. "I remember an officer who went down on parade at six o'clock in the morning, sunstruck by the temple heat despite wearing a regulation helmet. A dank, sweltering place, indeed."

General Feversham nodded sympathetically, his eyes drifting back to his newspaper. "But I was glad to be there, Sutch. Very glad," he added, his voice tinged with a deep emotion.

As they continued to discuss the news, a footman appeared, bearing a tray laden with steaming cups of coffee and a selection of delicate pastries. The general's eyes lit up as he scanned the tray, his fingers closing around a letter addressed to him in elegant script.

"From Ramelton?" Lieutenant Sutch asked, setting his newspaper aside.
