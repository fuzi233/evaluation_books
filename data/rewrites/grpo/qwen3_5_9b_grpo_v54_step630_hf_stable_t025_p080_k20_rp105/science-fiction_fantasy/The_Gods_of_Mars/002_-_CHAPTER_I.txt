The cold wind off the Hudson bit through my coat as I stood on the bluff, the river below a silent, grey ghost of a stream. Ten years had passed since I last felt the pull of Mars, a decade of lonely prayer and waiting. But tonight, the red star hung low and bright, and the ancient, terrible magnetism of the God of War seized me again. It was the same pull that had dragged me from that Arizona cave twenty years prior, the same force that had carried me through the void.

My knees buckled. A wave of nausea washed over me, and I pitched forward onto the hard earth. But the moment my skin touched the soil, the familiar horror returned—the phantom screams of the cave, the feeling of paralysis. My muscles locked, then snapped free with a sharp click, like a wire severing under tension. I stood naked and trembling beside the lifeless husk of the thing that had tried to kill me, my eyes fixed once more on the red eye of Mars.

Then, the void opened.

I was gone.

The cold of space vanished, replaced instantly by the searing heat of a twin sun. I opened my eyes to find myself lying on a carpet of red grass, beneath a canopy of trees so vast and strange they seemed to touch the sky. The air was thin, the gravity light. As I attempted to rise, my Earthly strength betrayed me; the lack of resistance threw me several feet into the air before I crashed softly onto the grass. Laughing at my clumsiness, I adjusted, my sinews learning the rhythm of this new world.

The forest was a marvel of unnatural beauty. Trees of ebony, azure, and scarlet stood like polished pillars, their smooth trunks gleaming under the brazen sun. The birds were silent, winged shapes of impossible geometry. Beyond the treeline, a blue sea shimmered, and a great river flowed majestically toward it. This was not the barren wasteland I expected; this was a cultivated paradise.

Yet, as I stepped from the shadows of the trees toward the riverbank, the romance of the scene evaporated. In the meadow, a dozen grotesque figures moved slowly. They were tall, humanoid in torso and legs, but their arms were short, thick, and sinuous, moving like elephant trunks. Their skin was a ghastly blue, save for a broad band of white encircling a single, dead-white eye. Where a nose should be, there was only a ragged, inflamed hole resembling a fresh bullet wound. Below, the face was blank to the chin; there was no mouth.

They were the Plant Men.

Their heads were crowned with a tangled mass of jet-black hair, thick as angleworms, which writhed and crawled as if alive. From their armpits dangled tiny replicas of themselves, suspended by stems, ranging from unopened buds to miniature adults. These creatures fed by running their taloned hands over the grass, sucking the vegetation through mouths located in the palms of their arms.

As I watched, hidden behind a massive tree, a leader of the herd approached. He let out a strange, purring shriek. Instantly, the thousands of worm-hairs on the heads of the herd stiffened, becoming a thousand sensitive ears. The herd turned as one, leaping away in great, kangaroo-like bounds toward the cliffs.

Instinct took over. I launched myself across the meadow, my Earthly muscles producing prodigious leaps in this low-gravity world, chasing the fleeing monsters. They led me to the base of the cliffs, where the river seemed to rise from the earth itself. There, amidst scattered boulders, I saw the source of the commotion.

A small group of Green Men stood back-to-back, facing the Plant Men. These were the true inhabitants of Mars—tall, green-skinned warriors with massive tusks, laterally placed eyes, and extra arms. Two men and four women, armed with long-swords, they faced an overwhelming tide of blue horrors.

The Plant Men attacked with terrifying efficiency. One leaped high above the Green Men and brought his massive tail down in a sweeping arc, crushing a warrior's skull like an eggshell. Others circled, their screeching mouths and flailing tails creating chaos. Two Green Men fell instantly.

Only one warrior remained, along with two women. As two more Plant Men charged, the lone Green Man swung his sword, cleaving one monster in half. But the other struck down the women with a single blow of its tail.

Seeing his companions fall, the Green Warrior charged the remaining herd, swinging his blade in a frenzy. He hacked a path through the attackers and turned to flee into the forest.

I could not watch his noble stand without intervening. With a surge of bloodlust, I sprang from my hiding place, grabbing a long-sword from the ground. I joined the chase, my heart pounding with the thrill of battle.

The Green Warrior was overtaken near the forest edge, backed against a boulder. The Plant Men hissed around him, their single eyes fixed on their prey. I moved silently, closing the distance before they could react. Four of them fell to my blade before they realized I was there.

For a moment, we stood together, a whirlwind of steel and fury. The Green Warrior fought with a skill I had rarely seen, his blade cutting through flesh, bone, and metal in a figure-eight pattern that kept him safe. We were a perfect team, dispatching the monsters with ruthless efficiency. Green, sticky syrup oozed from their severed arteries, coating us as we fought. At one point, a monster's talons sank into my back, its moist lips sucking at the wound, but the Green Warrior tore the beast away with a single, devastating stroke.

Just as we began to gain the upper hand, the shrill wail echoed again from above. I looked up to see a strange figure standing on a natural balcony of the cliff, shrieking and waving his arms.

Following his gaze, my blood ran cold. Converging from the forest, the river, and the distant plains were hundreds of Plant Men, alongside new, swift-running monsters.

"It will be a great death," I said to my companion.

The Green Warrior smiled, a grim expression of acceptance. "We may at least die fighting and as great warriors should, John Carter."

We had just slain the last of our immediate foes when the realization hit me. The voice calling the horde... the Green Warrior's face transformed.

"There before my astonished eyes I beheld the greatest of the green men of Barsoom," I thought, the name rising in my mind with the force of destiny. "Their shrewdest statesman, their mightiest general, my great and good friend, Tars Tarkas, Jeddak of Thark."
