As Major Pendennis navigated the labyrinthine complexities of his nephew's personal affairs, he found himself grappling with a series of intricate dilemmas. The financial information he had gathered through his acquaintance with Mr. Strong, coupled with his own astute observations as a close friend of the Clavering household, painted a disquieting picture of the family's prospects. The prospect of uniting Arthur with a wife whose father was embroiled in a web of debt and whose mother had a history of making questionable marital choices was hardly appealing. The mere presence of Amory or Altamont in polite society would be enough to send the latter into hiding, lest he face the consequences of his past transgressions. However, the knowledge of this fact could also be leveraged to keep Clavering in check, rendering him beholden to the person privy to his dark secret.

Yet, if the Begum were to settle Clavering's debts, she would merely be throwing good money after bad, as her heirs would ultimately inherit a depleted estate. Meanwhile, Miss Amory's marriage would bring nothing but a tarnished reputation and a dubious lineage. It was a scenario that weighed heavily on Major Pendennis's mind as he pondered the various courses of action available to him.

As he mulled over these matters, he recalled the peculiar phenomenon whereby individuals beset by financial difficulties often seemed to disappear from their usual social circles, only to resurface in unexpected quarters. He had known gentlemen of discerning taste and breeding who, having fallen on hard times, would suddenly reappear at humble establishments in unlikely neighborhoods, such as Billingsgate or the Strand. Such individuals often maintained a low profile, revealing their whereabouts only to a select few who held the key to their hidden abodes. Similarly, after his disagreements with his wife and subsequent misfortunes, finding Sir Francis Clavering at home had become a rarity. His valet, Mr. Lightfoot, confided to a friend that his master rarely returned until three o'clock, feigning sleep when summoned for his morning water, and evading detection when descending to the kitchen. Likewise, following his disputes with Mr. Strong, the Baronet had gone into hiding, seeking refuge in places beyond the Chevalier's reproaches – and, indeed, the pangs of his own conscience.
