Kate stood at the hearth, the warm glow of the fire casting a golden light on her face as she pondered the impending visit to Sevenbergen. Her mother, Catherine, had been hesitant to leave the house for a day, always something seemed to go awry, and today was no exception – Peter Buyskens' mule was nowhere to be found. Still, Kate longed to see Margaret Brandt, and the anticipation hung in the air like the sweet scent of freshly baked bread wafting from the oven.

Eli, ever the stern one, had forbidden her to go, and Catherine had agreed with a stern expression, but as soon as they were alone, she turned her ire on herself. "A fool's errand, indeed," she muttered, "like a cat stalking prey, I've been too cautious, too concerned with what others might think. Next time, let's act swiftly, without hesitation, and if questioned, we can say we knew nothing, we meant no harm." Kate watched her mother's face, a mixture of determination and frustration etched on her features.

"Nay, Mother, with patience, Father will come around," Kate tried to reassure her, but Catherine's resolve only hardened. "And so will Michaelmas, but when? I was so eager for you to meet Margaret, we could have shared our thoughts, discussed her charms. No one can truly judge a person until they've met them face-to-face. If I had a hundred more sons, I'd never stand in their way, not until I'd seen for myself."

As Catherine's words hung in the air, Kate hesitated, her voice barely above a whisper. "Mother, I have something to tell you." Catherine's expression darkened, but Kate pressed on, her words spilling out in a rush. "I've seen her, Margaret Brandt. We spoke, and I...I didn't want to upset you, so I waited till I could tell you without you being angry."

Catherine's eyes narrowed, a mixture of sadness and anger swirling within them. "Ah, like mother, like daughter – cowardice our greatest foe. I scold the others, but you, Kate, have never known a harsh word from me, and yet you...Nay, I won't have you cry, child. Tell me everything, better late than never. And when you do decide to visit Sevenbergen, I want to know, I want to be there with you."
