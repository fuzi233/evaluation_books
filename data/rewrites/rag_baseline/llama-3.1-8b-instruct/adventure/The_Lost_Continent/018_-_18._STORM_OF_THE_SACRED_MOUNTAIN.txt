As I walked through the winding passageways of the Sacred Mountain, the sound of clashing steel and screams echoed through the halls. The air was heavy with the scent of smoke and sweat. Nais lay on a couch, her beauty still evident despite the weariness etched on her face. She spoke in a soft, raspy voice, her words laced with a hint of irony. "My lord is the properest man in all the world to be King." Her gaze drifted towards me, and I felt a pang of unease. "I refused to touch the trade till they had given me the Queen I desired, safe and alive, here upon the Mountain."

Her words hung in the air like a challenge. I tried to meet her gaze, but my own eyes felt drawn to the faint tremble of her hand as she reached for the cup of broth I offered. The fragile motion was a stark contrast to the turmoil brewing outside. Phorenice's army had breached the outer gates, and our defenses were weakening. The once-strong walls of the Sacred Mountain now seemed precarious, like a canvas stretched to its limits.

Nais' voice grew weaker, her eyelids drooping as she spoke. "You should not have let me stand in the way of Atlantis' good, Deucalion. Still, it is very sweet to know you were weak there for once, and that I was the cause of your weakness." Her words were a gentle rebuke, a reminder of the delicate balance between our desires and the fate of our kingdom.

I set the cup aside, my mind racing with the implications of our conversation. The Empress would not be easily deterred, and our defenses were crumbling under her relentless assault. I knew I had to act, to find a way to rally our forces and push back against the invaders. But for now, I stood frozen, caught in the fragile web of Nais' gaze.

As I watched her drift off to sleep, I felt the weight of my responsibilities settle upon me. The beard that had grown in the past few hours felt coarse against my skin, a constant reminder of the role I had taken on. I shaved it off, donning a new robe to symbolize my commitment to our kingdom's fate. The sound of battle raged on outside, but in that moment, I felt a sense of detachment, as if I was observing the chaos from a distant shore.

The Sacred Mountain's defenses were failing, and I knew I had to find a way to restore them
