In the quiet hours spent in the English countryside, George Warrington found solace in the companionship of his cousin, Lord Castlewood. Despite past transgressions, their friendship endured, strengthened by the mutual understanding that each would support the other's interests without compromising their own. When George expressed a desire to remain in England, free from the constraints of his mother's estate in Virginia, Lord Castlewood offered his assistance, promising to use his influence to secure a position for George at court or in the House of Lords.

George's reluctance to return to Virginia stemmed from more than mere sentimentality. He found the pursuits and society of London far more captivating than anything his native land had to offer. As a planter, he would have been content with a life of idleness, but only if he could have done so independently. In Virginia, however, he felt trapped, aware that his position as his mother's subject was precarious, and he dreaded the thought of returning with his young bride to a life of subordination and potential conflict.

When Lord Castlewood promised to help George secure a place in society, the Virginian accepted with eagerness. Though his lordship's own circumstances were far from enviable, George chose to focus on the benefits of their connection. Lord Castlewood's visit to George's fiancée, Miss Lambert, was a testament to his good intentions. He praised George to her parents and vice versa, charming them with his wit and good humor, and for a time, they saw beyond his questionable reputation. 

As they spent more time together, Lord Castlewood seemed genuinely drawn to Miss Lambert's innocence and goodness, expressing admiration for her in a way that surprised everyone, including himself. His words carried a hint of sincerity, and he didn't pretend to feel more deeply than he did. "I may have made my share of mistakes," he said with a wry smile, "but I wish I'd known someone like Miss Lambert when I was younger. Perhaps I might have avoided a great deal of trouble."

Lord Castlewood's reaction was immediate and jarring, as if he'd misinterpreted her words entirely. "Too late?" he repeated, taken aback.
