The oppressive, suffocating gloom of the ancient seabed finally lifted, replaced by a light that seemed almost fragile after the endless night. I realized I had emerged onto land to the right of my entry point, a terrain dotted with numerous fire-holes. Their presence warmed my heart, yet prudence dictated caution; life thrived around these flames, and danger often lurked in their heat.

I looked down at the Maid, who gazed up at me. She drew closer, revealing a beauty that was both wondrous and heartbreaking. Her face was pale, etched with exhaustion, and I felt a surge of self-reproach for having pushed her so hard. In truth, I felt forged of iron, while she remained a delicate, tender creature. She silenced my guilt with a simple, beautiful gaze, and I wrapped my arms around her, kissing her forehead before scanning the landscape to chart our course.

Before me stretched the blue shimmer I had spotted at the mouth of the Upward Gorge. It was distant, but it served as my true north, preventing us from wandering in blind circles across the dark expanse. After a moment of calculation, I determined the Gorge lay to my left, though I must navigate carefully. To my left, far away, burned the red glow of the Giants' fire-pit, a place I must avoid entirely. Furthermore, I mistrusted the blue shimmer itself; it felt wrong, unnatural.

I placed my arm around the Maid, who stood quietly beside me, and pointed toward the left. "The Gorge lies that way," I told her, "far beyond sight, marked only by the landmarks I recall from my descent."

She had observed the surroundings intently during our walk and seemed to grasp the geography. "How do you intend to proceed?" she asked.

"Directly," I replied, "but we must skirt the blue shimmer and stay clear of the Giant's red pit."

She gestured for me to look ahead. There, faintly visible, were fire-holes surrounded by a sickly green luminescence. "That is the tract of poisonous gas," she explained, referencing the data from the Lesser Redoubt. "Where the gas flows, the fires glow green. It stretches far to the northwest, and the great blue shimmer burns across that entire sector."

I asked her what they called it. "They simply call it 'The Shine'," she answered.

My internal voice, Mine Own, urgently warned me against approaching The Shine. "The Fixed Giants dwell within its borders," she whispered, noting that they were usually hidden by the burning mist, revealed only when the vapor rolled. This realization chilled me; these Fixed Giants were akin to the Great Watchers guarding the Mighty Pyramid—forces of profound, terrible evil. I recalled the monstrous face I had glimpsed amidst the smoke of The Shine upon my first arrival.

"How far does the gas extend?" I asked.

She indicated the direction, and I realized a dilemma: to reach the Gorge while avoiding The Shine, I would have to cross the path of the gas. Yet, she reminded me that I had previously crossed the land by sheer luck, walking along the far side of the gas cloud to reach the seabed.

"Therefore," she proposed, "we descend back into the seabed. We will walk beneath the shore for hours, heading southwest until we are safely beyond the gas zone. Then, we ascend and proceed with extreme caution, keeping the Giants' fire-pit in our rear."

It was a sound plan, one I would have devised instantly had she not already spoken it. Her eagerness and quick thinking were a sweet relief; I cherished the sound of her voice, the clarity of her mind, and the fact that she was always my partner in thought and action.

We adopted her strategy. However, it had been nearly seventeen hours since our last rest, and the Maid was visibly drained. I urged her to find a fire-hole nearby to warm ourselves before continuing. We selected one to the north, slightly to our right.

The journey to the fire-hole took longer than anticipated—an hour of trudging through the dark. When we arrived, it was a massive, red-glowing fissure venting heat into the night sky. I signaled for silence, retrieved the Diskos from my hip, and knelt, beckoning Mine Own to follow suit.

We crept to the edge of the hollow and peered down. A fierce fire burned within, yet no monsters immediately surrounded it, offering a fleeting sense of peace. Still, I knew better than to linger near the fires, for they attracted all living things. The Maid, however, was shivering violently from the cold. Despite my warnings, her desperation forced my hand. We descended into the hollow and approached the flames.

It was strange that my heart, which had sought fire for so long, now recoiled from it. My spirit whispered warnings that I struggled to ignore as the cold gnawed at my bones. Once at the fire, the reality of the danger returned: the heat drew creatures. I scanned the rocky floor and found three snakes and two scorpion-like beasts, motionless in their holes. They loved the heat and would strike in our sleep.

I decided against sleeping here, but I said nothing to the Maid, wishing to spare her the anxiety. Instead, I hoped she would rest happily by the fire, and later, when she awoke, I would guide her to a safer spot.

Presently, the Maid warmed enough to slip the scrip from my shoulder and prepare our food and water. We ate and drank in silence. As she consumed her second tablet, I sensed the lingering memory of my earlier discipline, a reminder of her absolute submission to me. She was utterly mine.

While eating, I watched constantly for creeping things. Soon, the Maid noticed a snake moving among the rocks. Her fear spiked, and we began searching for cover. We found a small cave in an upright rock, roughly a hundred feet from the fire. It was dry, sweet-smelling, and devoid of inhabitants.

Inside, the cave felt like a haven, illuminated by the fire's glow. Mine Own trusted me implicitly, believing I could protect her from any evil. This trust was a balm to my heart, though my own terror for her safety remained heavy.

We slept that night, sharing a cloak, though the fire offered little warmth. Twenty hours had passed since our last slumber, and we were exhausted, our spirits on edge. Seven hours later, a sudden, piercing scream tore through the night. It was the terrified cry of humans, a sound that shattered our hearts.

I wanted to rush down to help, but I could not abandon the Maid. Immediately, the night erupted in roaring voices—huge, husky shouts that sounded like houses running and shouting. The Maid trembled, recalling the horrors of her long wandering. I held her tight as the screams intensified.

Then came the brutal slaughter of a young girl. My heart sickened at the thought of her fate, while a surge of righteous anger swelled within me. The Maid sobbed uncontrollably. The screams ceased abruptly, replaced by the thudding of massive feet and the hoarse shouts of giant men hunting.

The cries of fleeing humans grew louder, accompanied by the thunder of giant footsteps. Peering out, I saw a cluster of ragged, naked humans running in panic, torn by rocks and brush, looking like wild beasts. My heart ached with the desire to join them, but I knew I could not leave the Maid.

As the giants passed the hollow, four of them emerged from the darkness. Three were dull-colored and hairy, but the fourth was a horrific, livid-white monstrosity—a man-monster filled with unwholesome life. They vanished instantly into the night, followed by the bellowing of their victims.

A wave of sorrowful impatience washed over me. These humans lacked courage; they ran instead of fighting, doomed to die anyway. Yet, I remembered the history of the Lesser Redoubt: their ancestors had starved for a hundred thousand years, lacking the vitality bred by the Earth-Current. Naani was different, but the rule applied to them.

Suddenly, I heard dry sobbing from the back of the cave. Before I could rush to comfort the Maid, a naked girl sprinted across the hollow's edge, looking over her shoulder in terror. She scrambled under a rock ledge, worn out and desperate.

A squat, broad-shouldered man, hairless as a bullock, crept silently into the hollow. He spotted the girl instantly and lunged.

I hesitated for a fraction of a second, then leaped twenty feet down into the hollow. My anger fueled my jump. I landed hard, unharmed by my armor, just as the Squat Man ripped the girl apart. She screamed once, a dreadful sound, and died in his hands.

Wrath burned in my eyes, blinding me momentarily. I charged the monster, the Diskos spinning with a rage-filled roar. The Beast-Man turned, expecting the same fate as the girl, but I struck. My blow missed as he dropped onto my hands, and he seized my legs, tearing at me. I slashed quickly with the Diskos, severing one of his monstrous talons. He threw me across the hollow, my armor clanging loudly.

Miraculously, I landed on my feet, the Diskos still in my grip. The Beast-Man attacked again, silent and swift, foam of brute fury bubbling from his mouth. I leaped, driving the Diskos into his neck and shoulder. The blow severed his head and shoulder. He knocked me backward with his dying spring, bruising me severely, but I was alive.

I rushed to the dead girl, lifting her torn body and casting it into the fire-hole. Turning back to the cave, I found Mine Own running toward me, clutching the belt-knife I had given her. She was pale but steadfast, offering her aid without hesitation. She stood before me in silence, her eyes conveying a profound, wordless admiration and love that honored me deeply.

She needed no words; she simply clung to me, trembling from the horror we had witnessed. I carried her back to the cave, where we rested, ate, and drank.

An hour later, we descended from the cave and headed for the seabed. Two hours of cautious travel brought us to the water's edge, where the fear of the giants had subsided slightly, though we knew they roamed near the fires where humans sought warmth. We descended an hour further, turning southwest. Twelve hours of steady walking brought us safely beyond the zone of poisonous gas.

It was now seventeen hours since we slept. My body ached from the brutal fight, saved only by my armor. Naani marveled at my resilience, contrasting my hardy nature with the softness of the Lesser Redoubt's people, who lacked the strength born of the Earth-Current.

Exhausted, we searched for a cave but found none. I suggested building a shelter from boulders. As I spoke, she echoed my words, and we interlaced our fingers in the dark, laughing softly as we kissed—a timeless gesture of connection that transcended the grim surroundings.

We constructed a narrow trench lined with flat stones to block creeping things. Inside, we ate, shared our cloak, and slept, promising to wake at the slightest danger.

Seven hours later, I woke in agony, my bruises throbbing. I slipped away gently to stretch, realizing my movements were stiff and painful. I returned to the shelter, stripped off my armor and clothes, and applied ointment to my wounds, groaning in pain.

Suddenly, the Maid appeared beside me. Hearing my groans, she had woken instantly, fearing I was injured. She did not mind my nudity; instead, she was furious that I had attempted to treat my wounds alone, exposed to the cold. She dragged the cloak over me, stamped in anger, and retrieved my armor. She took the ointment pot and tenderly massaged my wounds, keeping me warm. She was wise, lovely, and utterly mine.

Once I was dressed, she chided me gently for my lack of foresight, kissed me, and handed me my food. We ate and drank, the bond between us strengthened by the ordeal.

We packed our gear and ascended from the seabed. Upon reaching the shore, we looked over the land. The Great Red Fire-Pit of the Giants was visible to the south-west, with monstrous figures moving against its light. We crouched low, hiding from their gaze.

Beyond the fire-pit lay the low volcanoes and, further west, the cold, horrid glare of The Shine. Our path lay between the west and south-west, a route requiring cunning to avoid all dangers. Naani recounted tales of terror from the Lesser Redoubt, including the legend of wolf-men near the volcanoes, though she admitted no one had ventured there in a thousand years. She described The Shine as a realm where Evil lived eternally, the source of all forces that plagued the Refuge. Her memories stirred her to tears, and I held her gently as we knelt on the earth.

We pressed on, heading northwest to stay clear of the Giant's fire-pit, moving cautiously through the bushes. After six hours, we paused to eat and drink. Then, we turned southwest, steering clear of The Shine.

In the fourteenth hour, we entered a broad valley. Its darkness was deeper than the grey gloom of the seabed, yet the air felt clearer. We descended for three hours, stopping to rest. Naani insisted on the break, a wisdom I had to accept despite my restless, itching blood, fueled by the poison of my bruises.

Two hours after resting, we stopped abruptly. A vague, curious sound drifted through the night. We crouched, listening, but heard nothing. Unease settled over us. We continued for another hour, passing two patches of blue gas that smoldered silently, emitting a bitter smell.

Then, we heard it again—the sound of something spinning in the night. Terror seized the Maid; she recognized it as the signal of a great Evil Force. My own spirit had sensed it, but the confirmation was terrifying. How could I protect her?
