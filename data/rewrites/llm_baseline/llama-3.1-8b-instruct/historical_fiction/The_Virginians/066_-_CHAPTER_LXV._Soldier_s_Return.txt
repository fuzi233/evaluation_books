The echoes of gunfire still lingered in the air as Harry returned home, his face bronzed from the sea and his side-curl slightly tousled from the ball whizzing past. Though he had emerged unscathed from the battle of St. Cas, the memory of his fallen comrades lingered. He spoke little of the skirmish, only hinting at the chaos that had unfolded as they fled down to the sea, pursued by the relentless French cavalry and infantry. The cries of the wounded still haunted him – poor Armytage, who had fallen before his very eyes, and Sir John, whose bravery had cost him his life.

As he recounted the tale to Miss Hetty, her concern for the fallen soldier's widow was palpable. "Why did the King encourage Sir John to take on the mission?" she asked, her voice tinged with a mix of sadness and outrage. "A gentleman cannot refuse a command from such a quarter, but at what cost?" Her father, the General, chuckled dryly, his eyes twinkling with amusement, but Harry knew that beneath his levity lay a deep sense of loss and regret.

"I've no doubt his Majesty will be... preoccupied," he said, choosing his words carefully. "But to expect him to grieve for every soldier who falls in battle would be unrealistic. Kings have duties to attend to, and the weight of responsibility cannot be measured in tears." Miss Hetty's eyes sparkled with defiance, and for a moment, Harry glimpsed a glimmer of the fierce spirit that lay beneath her gentle exterior. "If I thought that, I'd be for the Pretender," she said, her voice barely above a whisper.

The General's laughter echoed through the room, and Harry smiled wryly, knowing that beneath the levity lay a complex web of emotions. "I think better of his Majesty than to suppose him so weak," he said, his voice filled with conviction. "And as for Miss Hester Lambert and her Pretender, I doubt she'd find happiness in that path. The Lambert family has never been known for its moderation." The conversation hung in the air, heavy with the unspoken weight of loss and the fragility of human life.
