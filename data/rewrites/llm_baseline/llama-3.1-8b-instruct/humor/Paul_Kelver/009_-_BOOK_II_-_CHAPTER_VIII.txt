As the winter solstice approached, the house was filled with the warm scent of simmering stews and roasting meats, a reflection of the tender love that had blossomed between my parents. The news of the approaching end had brought them a sense of peace, a gentle reminder that every moment was precious. They spent their days cooking together, experimenting with ancient family recipes passed down through generations, their hands moving in tandem as they chopped, sautéed, and seasoned the ingredients.

Their love had become a rich broth, infused with the essence of herbs and spices, and it warmed the hearts of all who entered the house. Even my aunt Fan, notorious for her sharp tongue and prickly demeanor, had begun to soften, her harsh words replaced by a tentative smile. One afternoon, as we sat around the kitchen table, she announced that she had decided to try her hand at being agreeable.

"I'm a beast of an old woman," she declared, her voice laced with a hint of mischief.

"Don't say that, Fan," my mother chided gently.

"What's the point of saying 'Don't say it' when I've just said it?" my aunt retorted, her eyes twinkling.

"It's your manner that people find off-putting," my mother explained.

"They'd be daft if they didn't," my aunt shot back, a sly grin spreading across her face. "But I can be agreeable if I choose. Nobody more so."

My mother raised an eyebrow. "Then why not choose?"

My aunt leaned in, a conspiratorial look on her face. "Because being disagreeable suits me. It's a taste I've grown accustomed to."

My mother chuckled. "Perhaps it's time to try something new, then."

My aunt's expression turned thoughtful, a hint of vindictiveness dancing in her eyes. "I could do it again, I suppose. But where's the fun in that?"

As we savored the aromas wafting from the kitchen, my father noticed the change in my aunt's demeanor. "Fan seems a bit...different lately," he observed one evening.

My mother smiled. "She's just trying to be agreeable, dear."

My father's eyes crinkled at the corners. "I almost wish she wouldn't. We're used to her being...well, herself."
