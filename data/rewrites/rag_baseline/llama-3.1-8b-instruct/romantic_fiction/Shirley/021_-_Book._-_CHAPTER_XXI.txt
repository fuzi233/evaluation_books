As I entered the kitchen, the scent of baking bread wafted through the air, mingling with the rich aroma of roasting meats. It was a day like any other at Greenhill, yet the tension hung heavy, like the unspoken words that had passed between Mrs. Pryor and Miss Keeldar. I poured myself a cup of steaming tea, feeling the warmth seep into my fingers as I listened to the gentle hum of conversation from the next room. Mrs. Pryor, a woman whose presence was as soothing as a warm oven on a winter's night, sat at the table, her eyes cast downward as if searching for something lost.

Caroline, our dear governess, had a way of sensing these moments, and she knew just what to do to ease the pain. She sat beside Mrs. Pryor, her hands moving deftly as she mixed a batch of sweet biscuits, the dough yielding to her touch like a gentle stream. As they worked, Caroline spoke softly, her words weaving a spell of calm, and slowly, the lines on Mrs. Pryor's face began to smooth out, like the surface of a still pond.

Just then, a knock at the door broke the spell, and Caroline excused herself to answer it. I followed her, curiosity getting the better of me, and found ourselves in the gallery, where Miss Keeldar stood, her face etched with contrition. Her eyes met mine, and for a moment, I saw a glimmer of the girl she once was, before life's trials had hardened her edges.

"How is Mrs. Pryor?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

"Rather out of spirits," Caroline replied, her expression sympathetic.

Miss Keeldar's face darkened, and she turned to go, but Caroline caught her arm, her grip gentle but firm. "Wait," she said. "Mrs. Pryor forgives you, but she asks you to come and speak with her."

As we walked back to the kitchen, I couldn't help but notice the way Miss Keeldar's pace quickened, her feet moving with a newfound sense of purpose. When we arrived, Mrs. Pryor rose from her seat, her eyes fixed on Miss Keeldar, and I saw the faintest hint of a smile on her lips.
