In the esteemed tradition of acknowledging the nuances of cultural expression, Monsieur Emanuel's annual celebration had become an event of great significance at the esteemed institution of Labassecour. It was a day set aside to honor the professor of literature, a man of considerable influence and intellectual prowess, whose reputation extended far beyond the confines of the academy. As the first of March dawned, a fine, sunny day that coincided with the morning's customary attendance at mass and the subsequent half-holiday, the students and faculty prepared themselves for the occasion.

Their attire, typically subdued and practical, gave way to a more festive air, with clean collars and lighter, clearer fabrics making an appearance. Even the usually reserved Mademoiselle Zélie St. Pierre, known for her frugal tastes, opted for a more elaborate ensemble, donning a "robe de soie" and seeking the services of a skilled coiffeur to style her hair. The air was filled with the sweet scent of a new perfume, one that seemed to be all the rage among the younger set. Zélie, ever the romantic, longed for a life of leisure and indulgence, where she might pursue her interests without the burden of debt and the weight of responsibility.

Monsieur Emanuel, a man of discerning taste and refined sensibilities, was well aware of Zélie's affections, and his own gaze would often linger on her with an intensity that left her both flattered and perplexed. He would sit for minutes on end, lost in thought, as if attempting to decipher the intricacies of her heart. This unwavering attention only served to heighten her awareness of his presence, and she would often squirm beneath the weight of his scrutiny.

As the day progressed, the students and faculty began to make their way to the celebration, each bearing a gift of some sort, though none dared to bestow anything of great value upon the professor. Monsieur Emanuel, a man of simple tastes, had explicitly stated his disdain for such ostentatious displays, preferring instead the sincerity and thoughtfulness behind a humble offering. A flower, a drawing, or a handwritten note – these were the tokens he cherished most, a testament to the esteem in which he was held by those around him.
