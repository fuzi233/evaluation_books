To comprehend the architecture of our impending liberation, one must first dismantle the absolute tyranny of the First Born's dogma. Xodar listened with a mixture of incredulity and dawning horror to my account of the arena at the rites of Issus. He struggled to reconcile the reality before him with the ancient lies: that a mortal could brandish a blade against a goddess and survive unscathed, rather than being atomized by her divine fury.

"It is the final proof," he declared, his voice trembling with the weight of revelation. "No further evidence is required to shatter the last vestige of my superstition regarding the divinity of Issus. She is merely a wicked old woman, a tyrant wielding immense power for malevolent ends, using her machinations to keep her own people—and all of Barsoom—in a state of religious blindness for eons."

"She remains all-powerful here, however," I countered, my tone urgent. "Therefore, it behoves us to depart at the first moment that appears even remotely propitious."

"I pray you find such a moment," Xodar replied, a bitter laugh escaping his lips. "For in all my life, I have never witnessed a circumstance wherein a prisoner of the First Born might successfully flee."

"To-night will suffice," I answered.

"It will soon be night," Xodar noted. "How may I assist in this venture?"

"Can you swim?" I asked.

"No slimy Silian that haunts the crushing depths of Korus is more at ease in water than Xodar," he responded with pride.

"Excellent. The Red Man, however, likely cannot swim," I observed. "There is scarcely enough water in their arid domains to float the tiniest skiff. Thus, one of us must bear him through the sea to our chosen vessel. I had hoped to traverse the entire distance submerged, but I fear the Red Youth's physiology could not sustain such a journey. Even the bravest among them are paralyzed by terror at the mere concept of deep water; it has been ages since their ancestors gazed upon a lake, a river, or a sea."

"The Red One accompanies us?" Xodar queried.

"Yes."

"It is wise. Three swords are superior to two, especially when the third wielder possesses the might of this fellow. I have watched him battle in the arena at the rites of Issus countless times. Never, until I witnessed your combat, had I seen one who appeared unconquerable amidst overwhelming odds. One might mistake you for master and pupil, or father and son. Recall his face; there is a striking resemblance between you. It becomes most evident during combat—that same grim smile, that same maddening contempt for the adversary, manifest in every muscle and facial expression."

"Be that as it may, Xodar, he is a formidable fighter. We shall form a trio nearly impossible to overcome. Should my friend Tars Tarkas, Jeddak of Thark, join us, we could carve a path from one end of Barsoom to the other, even if the entire world conspired against us."

"They will learn from whence you come," Xodar stated grimly. "That is but one of the superstitions Issus has implanted in a credulous humanity. She operates through the Holy Therns, who are as ignorant of her true nature as the Barsoomians of the outer world. Her decrees arrive at the Therns' altars, inscribed in blood upon strange parchments. These deluded fools believe they receive divine revelations through supernatural agency, failing to realize the messages appear only because they are placed there undetected. I myself have delivered these messages for Issus for many years. A long tunnel connects the Temple of Issus to the principal sanctuary of Matai Shang, excavated ages ago by the slaves of the First Born in such utter secrecy that no Thern suspected its existence."

"The Therns, in turn, maintain temples scattered throughout the civilized world. Here, unseen priests disseminate the doctrines of the Mysterious River Iss, the Valley Dor, and the Lost Sea of Korus, persuading the poor dupes to undertake voluntary pilgrimages. This swelling of the pilgrim ranks enriches the Holy Therns and increases their slave population."

"Thus, the Therns serve as the primary mechanism for extracting the wealth and labor the First Born demand. Occasionally, the First Born launch raids upon the outer world, capturing females from the royal houses of the Red Men, seizing the latest battleships, and appropriating the skilled artisans who construct them, so they may replicate technologies they cannot create themselves."

"We are a non-productive race, proud of our idleness. It is criminal for a First Born to labor or invent; that is the sole function of the lower orders, existing merely to enable the First Born to enjoy long lives of luxury and sloth. With us, fighting is the only metric that matters. Were it not for this, there would be more First Born than all the creatures of Barsoom could sustain, for as far as I know, none of us ever die a natural death. Our females would live forever but for our weariness of them, compelling us to discard them to make room for others. Issus alone is shielded from death. She has lived for countless ages."

"Do not the other Barsoomians also possess immortality, save for the doctrine of the voluntary pilgrimage which drags them to the bosom of Iss at or before their thousandth year?" I asked.

"I now perceive beyond doubt that they are precisely the same species as the First Born," Xodar concluded, his resolve hardening. "I hope to live long enough to fight for them, to atone for the sins I have committed against them through generations of ignorance bred by false teaching."

As he finished speaking, a strange, haunting call echoed across the waters of Omean. I had heard it the previous evening; it signaled the end of the day, when the men of Omean spread their silken cloaks upon the decks of their battleships and cruisers, succumbing to the dreamless sleep of Mars.

Our guard entered to conduct his final inspection before dawn. His duty was swiftly completed, and the heavy door of our prison slammed shut behind him, leaving us alone in the darkness.

I allowed him time to retreat to his quarters, as Xodar predicted, before springing to the grated window. Below, a monstrous battleship lay anchored a quarter-mile offshore, flanked by numerous smaller cruisers and one-man scouts. Only the battleship maintained a watch. I spotted the sentinel clearly in the upper works; watching him, I saw him spread his sleeping silks upon the tiny platform where he stood. Soon, he collapsed fully onto his couch. The discipline on Omean was remarkably lax—a necessity, given that no enemy suspected the existence of such a fleet, or even of the First Born, or the Sea of Omean itself. Why maintain a vigil against ghosts?

I descended to the floor and conferred with Xodar, detailing the vessels I had observed.

"There is one there," he pointed out, "my personal property, built to carry five men. It is the swiftest of the swift. If we can board her, we can at least make a memorable dash for liberty." He proceeded to describe her equipment, her engines, and the features that made her a flying machine.

In his explanation, I recognized a specific trick of gearing that Kantos Kan had taught me during our clandestine voyage under false names in the navy of Zodanga beneath Sab Than, the Prince. I realized then that the First Born had stolen this technology from the ships of Helium, for only theirs possess such gearing. I understood, too, that Xodar spoke the truth regarding the craft's speed; nothing that cleaves the thin air of Mars can approach the velocity of Helium's vessels.

We resolved to wait at least an hour until the stragglers had retired to their silks. Meanwhile, I was to retrieve the Red Youth from our cell so that we might be ready for our reckless breakout together.

I leaped to the top of our partition wall, pulling myself up onto a flat surface about a foot wide. Walking along this narrow ledge, I reached the cell where the boy sat upon his bench. He had been leaning back against the wall, gazing upward at the glowing dome of Omean. When he spied me balancing above him on the partition, his eyes widened in astonishment. Then, a broad grin of appreciative understanding spread across his countenance.

As I stooped to drop to the floor beside him, he motioned for me to wait. Coming close below, he whispered, "Catch my hand; I can almost leap to the top of that wall myself. I have tried it many times, and each day I come a little closer. Someday, I should have been able to make it."

I lay prone across the wall, reaching my hand far downward. With a short run from the center of the cell, he sprang upward until I grasped his outstretched fingers. Pulling with all my strength, I hauled him to the wall's summit beside me.

"You are the first jumper I have ever seen among the Red Men of Barsoom," I remarked.

He smiled. "It is not strange. I will explain why when we have more time."

Together, we returned to the cell where Xodar awaited, descending to converse until the appointed hour had passed.

There, we formulated our plans for the immediate future, binding ourselves by a solemn oath to fight to the death for one another against whatever enemies confronted us. We knew that even if we succeeded in evading the First Born, we might still face the entirety of the world—the power of religious superstition is mighty.
