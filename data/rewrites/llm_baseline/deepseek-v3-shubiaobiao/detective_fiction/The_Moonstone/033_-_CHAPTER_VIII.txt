Mr. Godfrey Ablewhite sat across from me, his noble features shadowed by an expression of bewildered resignation.

*"I have lost a beautiful woman, a distinguished position, and a considerable fortune,"* he began, *"and yet I submit to it without protest. Can you explain such behavior, my dear Miss Clack? I confess—I cannot."*

*"No motive?"* I echoed, intrigued despite myself.

*"Consider the innocence of a child,"* he continued, spreading his hands in a gesture of helplessness. *"When a child acts, it does so without reason—like the grass growing or the birds singing. I am much the same. Why did I propose to Miss Verinder? Why have I neglected my charitable endeavors? Why have I strayed from the righteous path of the Mothers’ Small-Clothes? I cannot say. Ask a child why it has misbehaved, and it will only stare in mute confusion. So too with me, Miss Clack. I confess it freely—to you alone."*

Here was a puzzle indeed. As one deeply versed in matters of the soul, I felt the stirrings of divine insight. It is not uncommon for those of great virtue to be humbled—perhaps as a reminder of their mortal frailty. Was this not such a moment? The revulsion Mr. Godfrey now felt for his ill-considered engagement, his eagerness to return to his charitable work—these were signs of a spirit redeemed.

I laid a gentle hand upon his. *"Dear friend, your distress is but the awakening of your better nature. You recoil from this union because your soul knows it to be wrong. Rejoice! The path of righteousness lies open before you once more."*

His relief was palpable. He clasped my hands fervently, his eyes alight with gratitude. *"You have lifted a darkness from me!"* he declared.

For a fleeting moment, I felt the warmth of spiritual communion—so intense that I nearly succumbed to a swoon. But fate, in the form of a clattering luncheon service, intervened.

Mr. Godfrey sprang to his feet. *"Time has slipped away! I must catch the next train."*

When I pressed him for haste, his face darkened. *"My father is coming,"* he admitted. *"He will not take this broken engagement lightly. I must intercept him before he arrives."*

With that, he departed, leaving me to reflect on the fickleness of worldly opinion. Let slanderers say what they will—that Mr. Godfrey had selfish reasons for releasing Rachel, or that he sought my favor for mercenary ends. I knew the truth.

---

At luncheon, I studied Rachel closely. Had her newfound freedom eased her spirit? No—if anything, she seemed more agitated, as if tormented by thoughts she dared not voice. *Who was the man who held her heart?* I wondered. Conversion would loosen her tongue in time.

That evening, she resisted all attempts at moral guidance, turning instead to the piano with defiant energy. Undaunted, I waited, the works of Miss Jane Ann Stamper at the ready.

---

The next day, as anticipated, Mr. Ablewhite arrived—followed, to my dismay, by Mr. Bruff. The lawyer’s presence was an ill omen.

*"Rachel,"* Mr. Ablewhite began with forced amiability, *"I have heard disturbing news. Might we speak privately?"*

*"Whatever you have to say, may be said here,"* Rachel replied, cool and unyielding.

His genial mask slipped. *"Am I to understand that you have jilted my son?"*

The pink flush creeping up his bald crown betrayed his rising fury. *"You insult my family!"* he snarled. *"Just as your kin once scorned me for marrying beneath them. Now my son is not good enough for you? I see the Herncastle pride in you, girl!"*

Rachel’s composure was glacial. *"If that is your belief, I shall not waste words correcting it."*

At this, Mr. Ablewhite erupted. *"Then let us be clear! If my son is unfit to be your husband, I am unfit to be your guardian. This house is in my name. You may remove yourself from it—at your earliest convenience."*

With that, he stormed out, leaving silence in his wake.

Aunt Ablewhite, roused from her usual torpor, managed to cross the room in a flurry of agitation.

Thus ended Mr. Ablewhite’s revenge—a petty strike at Rachel’s pride, and a test of her resolve. The battle lines were drawn. The storm, I sensed, was only beginning.
