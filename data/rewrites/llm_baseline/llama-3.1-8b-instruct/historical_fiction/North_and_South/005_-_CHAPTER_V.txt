The evening air clung to Helstone like a damp shroud, weighing heavily on the hearts of its residents. In the parsonage, Margaret Hale sat quietly by the fire, her thoughts consumed by the weight of her family's decision to leave. Her mother, Mrs. Hale, was busily engaged in making preparations for the coming winter, determined to ease the suffering of their poorer parishioners. As she listened to her mother's plans, Margaret's heart ached with a mix of sadness and guilt. They would soon be leaving behind the people who had grown to rely on their kindness and generosity – old Simon, whose rheumatism worsened with each passing year, and Mary Domville's little boy, whose crippled legs made him vulnerable to the harsh winters.

"Papa has always spent the income from his living in the parish," Mrs. Hale explained, her voice tinged with a sense of justification. "I fear we may be overstepping our bounds, but the winter promises to be particularly severe, and our people need our help."

Margaret's eyes sparkled with enthusiasm as she urged her mother to continue their charitable efforts. "Oh, mamma, let us do all we can," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "We may not be here much longer." But Mrs. Hale's expression betrayed a flicker of concern, misinterpreting her daughter's words as a hint of illness. "Do you feel unwell, my dear?" she asked, her brow furrowed with worry.

Margaret reassured her mother that she was merely tired, the fresh country air invigorating her senses after the stifling atmosphere of Harley Street. As she climbed the stairs to her bedroom, she felt a sense of melancholy wash over her. The moon cast an eerie glow over the landscape, casting long shadows across the walls of the parsonage. She lay in bed, pretending to rest, but her mind was racing with thoughts of the future and the uncertainty that lay ahead.

The clock struck nine, and she paused, listening to the distant sound of the church bell tolling the hour.
