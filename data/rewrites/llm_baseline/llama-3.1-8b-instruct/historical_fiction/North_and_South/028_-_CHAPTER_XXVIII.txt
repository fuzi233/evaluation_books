As the sun began its descent, casting a warm orange glow over the quiet streets of the village, Margaret made her way to the Higgins' residence. Mary, ever vigilant, watched her approach with a mix of curiosity and wariness etched on her face. Margaret's gentle smile put her at ease, and they walked together through the house, their footsteps echoing off the walls as they ascended to the upstairs chamber. The air was heavy with the scent of stale air and the faint hint of smoke, a reminder of the countless candles that had burned throughout the long, arduous days and sleepless nights.

In the hushed silence, Margaret felt a sense of peace wash over her as she beheld the tranquil countenance of the deceased. The lines of pain and suffering that had etched themselves into her features during life had softened, replaced by an ethereal smile that seemed to radiate from within. Tears welled up in Margaret's eyes as she gazed upon the peaceful visage, and she felt a deep calm settle within her soul. It was as if death itself had brought solace, a respite from the trials and tribulations of life. Beautiful verses floated through her mind, like whispers from a distant realm: "They rest from their labors," "The weary are at rest," and "He giveth his beloved sleep."

As she turned to leave, Mary's quiet sobbing broke the stillness, and Margaret felt a pang of compassion for the young woman. Together, they descended the creaky stairs, their footsteps a poignant accompaniment to the somber mood that had settled over the household.

Nicholas Higgins stood in the center of the room, his rugged features etched with shock and denial. His eyes, usually bright and piercing, seemed dull and unfocused, as if struggling to grasp the reality of his loss. He had grown accustomed to his wife's frailty, had convinced himself that she would pull through, that she would defy the inevitable. But now, as the weight of her passing bore down upon him, he felt the full force of his grief.

Margaret felt a shiver run down her spine as she met his gaze, sensing the turmoil that churned beneath the surface. She had entered this space uninvited, yet somehow felt a connection to the family's sorrow, as if the threads of their lives had become intertwined.
