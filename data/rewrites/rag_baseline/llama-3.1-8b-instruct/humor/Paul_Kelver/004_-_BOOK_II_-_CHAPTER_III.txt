The air was alive with the scent of baking bread and simmering vegetables as my mother expertly peeled potatoes in the kitchen, her gloved hands moving with a practiced ease. My aunt, seated across from her, busied herself shelling peas with a rhythmic clinking of the pods against the wooden bowl. The sudden jangle of the front doorbell shattered the tranquility, and my mother's hands stilled, her eyes flicking towards the hallway. The bell tolled once more, its insistent ring growing louder, a clear summons.

"Aunt, is it for Louisa?" my mother asked, her brow furrowed in a mixture of curiosity and concern.

My aunt paused, her gaze darting towards the door, before responding, "Once for Louisa, twice for James, isn't it?"

The question hung in the air as I stood frozen, my slate poised mid-air, my heart racing with anticipation. My mother's face flushed, and with a swift movement, she discarded her gloves and pressed them into my hands. "Go, Paul," she whispered urgently. "Tell Louisa...but wait."

As she spoke, her eyes locked onto mine, a message passing between us in the silence. Before I could react, she had vanished out the door, leaving me to wonder what had just transpired. A few moments later, the kitchen door swung open, and my mother's hand beckoned to me from the doorway, her fingers extended in a subtle signal.

"Walk quietly," she mouthed, her eyes darting towards the stairs as she led the way, her footsteps light on the creaking boards. We crept up the stairs, the old house seeming to groan in protest at our stealthy movements. In my parents' bedroom, the wardrobe loomed, its doors slightly ajar, revealing the familiar contours of my best suit, a relic of special occasions past. My mother's hands moved swiftly, guiding me into the garments, her touch firm but gentle.

As I struggled into the Bluchers, my mind whirled with questions, but my mother's warnings silenced them. We descended the stairs, our footsteps muffled, like conspirators in a secret mission. At the street door, I hesitated, my voice barely above a whisper. "Shall I need my cap?" My mother's response was a curt shake of her head, and with a resolute motion, she flung open the door, ushering me into the morning light.
