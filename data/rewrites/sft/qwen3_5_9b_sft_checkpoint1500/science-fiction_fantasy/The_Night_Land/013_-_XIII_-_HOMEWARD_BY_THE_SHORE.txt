We emerged from that bleak, sorrowful realm—the Upward Gorge—and halted shortly thereafter, nestled beneath the towering feet of the great mountains, just beyond the gaping maw of the chasm. Naani, however, could not remain still. She scanned every direction, her breathing rapid and shallow, her eyes ablaze with a brilliant, trembling wonder as she beheld the vast, open spaces of freedom replacing the crushing dread of confinement. Turning, she gazed upward into the abyss of the Gorge, spying the terrible mouth of the tunnel, and a fresh wave of fear seized her. Yet, compelled by the pull of the light, she ran further downward, plunging deeper into the luminous Country of the Seas. Again she paused, turning back to survey the dark, confined passage she had fled, her soul a mixture of awe and profound relief. Then, turning forward, she took in the sprawling expanse of the land and the immense Ocean, and suddenly, simultaneously, laughter and tears welled up within her—a testament to the sheer, overwhelming astonishment of standing in a broad place of light after a lifetime of gloom.

She turned constantly, her gaze never ceasing, drawing deep, ragged breaths of the wide air. She had never known such a breadth of existence. With us, the necessity for hushed whispers vanished, the silence of the dark tunnels replaced by the openness of the day. Indeed, she shouted, testing the acoustics like a child discovering a new playground, her voice ringing beautifully into the distance, eventually lost in the vastness of the Country.

Instantly, an answer returned from the dark mountains behind us. It was not merely an echo; it was something stranger, an unnatural call rising from the gloom and horror of the Gorge. We spun around, unsure if it was a trick of the air or a genuine summons from the depths. The uncertainty spurred us to run downward a bit further, seeking a distance where the strangeness of the mountains would fade from our minds. Eventually, the terror receded, and we halted, feeling ourselves utterly free of the Gorge and its lingering, shadowy influence.

Seeking a vantage point, we spotted a flat rock situated slightly uphill. Climbing atop it, we sat down to eat and drink, huddled close together, happiness mingled with a vigilant caution. We watched the horizon keenly, fearing the approach of the Humpt-Men or any other lurking peril. Naani, the Maid, continued to question everything, her eyes fixed on the distant landscape. Her wonder at the sea was radiant, stirring her entire being with a strange, vivid joy. Yet, this beauty triggered vague, sudden memories—strange dreams mixing pleasure and pain—that overwhelmed her. Suddenly, tears flooded her eyes, and she pressed herself into my arms, needing the physical contact to anchor herself, to rediscover her own identity and return to her natural state of joy.

Often, I spoke of the clear, strange quality of the air, a sensation that resonated with her as well. To me, it felt as if I, too, had lived a life in a Dark Land, as you know. She abruptly broke from her reverie, her speech slipping into ancient, garbled words, reminiscent of moonlight from a forgotten epoch. These memories, old and enigmatic, seemed to flicker like ghostly lights. In an instant, she returned to the present, her spirit clinging tightly to me, often falling into a solemn, heartfelt silence.

The ocean itself seemed to call out to her with a primordial voice, half waking her. I, too, stirred, not from the journey itself, but from the Maid's awakening. My ancient thoughts, buried like memories in a dream, surged back to the surface. Thus, we sat there, shaken by the tides of the Olden World, trapped beneath the dreadful, eternal night that roared overhead. There were words I could not speak, the troubles and stirrings of our spirits too vast for language.

Far off, beyond the mountains' feet, lay a great mist and steam rising from the shoreline. I recalled traversing this exact path on my outward journey. Naani asked about it, and I explained that we must soon pass through it. She marveled at the volcanoes burning in the sea and scattered across the wide land, their height and grandeur lifting her spirit while simultaneously filling her with a strange humility. Overwhelmed by her affection, I took her into my arms, kissing her. She kissed me in return, asking questions between the kisses, driven by a longing to know the country better, but also by a sweet, impudent delight in teasing me.

Passionate and warm, she kissed me thrice, then grabbed my shoulders with her small hands, striking me lightly to hasten my response. She was full of a dear naughtiness, demanding to be kissed firmly. I answered, offering a word between each kiss, until she slipped her hand between our lips. I kissed her palm, small and pretty, but she opened her fingers quickly, allowing me to kiss through them, then shut them again, stopping me.

Later, I made her stand on the rock and loosened her hair over her shoulders, removing her boots to reveal her bare, pretty feet. At first, she half-refused, but soon became obedient and slightly shy, her cheeks flushing. When I stepped back to admire her, she turned gravely, pretending to be a statue, before suddenly stretching out her foot and placing her pink toes upon my lips. Surprised, I froze until she snatched them back, then threw herself into my arms, craving a hug and deep love. I laughed tenderly, confessing my desire to carry her always in a pocket close to my heart. She laughed mischievously, threatening to tickle and pinch me, and I shook her gently, only for her to kiss me naughtily amidst the motion. What could a man do with such a creature?

Eventually, she wished to sit down and retrieve her comb from my pouch. As she combed her hair, I sat and joked with her, my heart lighter than it had been in ages. Though I dreaded the Humpt-Men and the monstrous beasts of the seas, I felt no abiding horror or fear of evil forces; there was a naturalness to everything, a lack of loathing. After binding her hair, I asked her to leave it down, and she smiled, happy to please me.

We organized our gear, and I put her boots back on. We resumed our journey downward, moving at a good pace but not so fast as to tire us excessively. My intent was to rest on the other side of the boiling steam, near the shore. We traveled along the mountain's feet for six solid hours, remaining a considerable distance from the dangerous zone. By the nineteenth hour of our travel, we were ready for slumber.

We found a tall, hard rock with a flat top, nearly twice my length, perfect for our needs. Upon reaching the summit, we ate, drank, and slept, using the cloak beneath us as the Maid wished. The country was so warm and pleasant that cover was unnecessary. We awoke after seven good hours, sitting upright and looking at one another as if seeing each other anew. A fresh joy bloomed in our gazes. She came into my arms, kissing me, and we both felt a hunger for one another, not just physically, but for the presence itself.

I prepared breakfast. The water fizzed strongly, and we ate and drank, utterly happy. While she looked about at the wonders of the new land, I kept a watchful eye nearby for danger. Soon, I drew her gaze back to the mountains where the Gorge had passed. In the relaxed light of morning, I saw them truly monstrous, like a wall ascending forever until it vanished into the dark night of the deathly Upper World, lost in eternity. I remembered vague thoughts from the outward journey, but now, with ease and conversation, I perceived odd matters more clearly.

Our talk and eating were leisurely, but towards the end, we hurried slightly, sensing a tendency toward slackness. Descending from the rock, we proceeded at a good pace. Soon, we heard the far-off hissing of steam and the noise of upward bursting waters. The sound was strange, familiar to me from the outward journey, troubling me less than the Maid. I assured her, and she came close, and together we entered the steam.

For more than three hours, we trudged through the thick fog. I carried the Maid on my back, ensuring she did not stumble into a boiling pool hidden in the maze of steam. I was guided by the shoreline on my left, though neither the sea nor the sky were visible, save for glimpses where the mist thinned. The sea seemed to boil in patches, with hot pools scattered everywhere, making it difficult to distinguish the true sea from the thermal vents. We moved with constant caution.

From every direction came strange bursts, shrieks, and whistles as the deep waters broke upward. Occasionally, the sounds resembled great monsters, and the earth shook beneath our feet. Other times, there was a hush, broken only by a lone, eerie piping from a steam-cranny in the distance.

After nearly four hours, we emerged from the steam. The roaring and piping faded behind us, leaving only a thin mist and distant noises. We stood clear in the air of the Country. Now, Naani perceived the great forests to our right, while the shore remained on our left. She was awestruck by the trees, plucking branches to smell and examine the leaves, stirred by vague memories that felt like dreams. You, reading this, might be similarly stirred if awakened by such strange sights.
