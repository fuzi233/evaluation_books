The morning air was alive with the songs of birds, and the soft rustle of leaves as they danced in the wind.

"It's going to be a lovely day," Ethne said, her voice barely above a whisper, as she turned to her friend, who sat engrossed in a book.

Mrs. Adair looked up, a hint of a smile playing on her lips. "I think so," she replied, her voice low and soothing.

Ethne's gaze drifted back to the lawn, where Colonel Durrance was making his way towards them, his cane tapping out a steady rhythm on the flagstones. As he drew closer, Ethne's thoughts turned to the question that had been nagging at her for weeks: whether Durrance still held onto the hope of recovering his sight.

Mrs. Adair's attention was drawn to Ethne's concern, and she set aside her book, her eyes narrowing slightly as she asked, "Do you think he'd be traveling to London so frequently if he'd given up hope?"
