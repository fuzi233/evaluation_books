THE LITTLE ONES

The air was alive with the soft chirping of unseen birds and the rustle of leaves in the gentle breeze, carrying the sweet scent of ripe fruit through the dense undergrowth of the forest. I had been working in the nearby clearing for mere moments, the rhythmic motion of my hands a soothing balm to my weary soul, when I became aware of tiny, furtive movements around me. Soon, the Little Ones emerged from their hiding places among the slender trees, their bright eyes sparkling with curiosity as they approached me.

As I gestured to the giants, who I claimed had recently departed, the Little Ones burst into a chorus of laughter, their high-pitched voices like a symphony of tinkling wind chimes. "They are too blind to see us," they chimed in unison, their words dripping with mirth. One of them, seemingly the leader, sidled closer, his gaze fixed intently on the rope bound around my ankles. "Do you like that rope?" he asked, his voice laced with a mischievous grin. I smiled, feigning reluctance, and he danced with glee, his footsteps barely making a sound on the soft earth.

A gentle hand reached out, and one of the larger girls knelt beside me, deft fingers working the knot loose. I couldn't help but smile at the ease with which she accomplished the task. As the rope came free, the Little Ones swarmed around me, offering me succulent fruits that burst with juice in my mouth. Their playful antics left me breathless, and I found myself laughing along with them, my worries and cares momentarily forgotten.

When the sun began its slow descent below the horizon, heavy footsteps echoed through the forest, signaling the approach of the giants. The Little Ones scattered, vanishing into the underbrush with an uncanny speed. I hastily reattached the rope to my ankles, remembering the girl's words: "A crush of one of their horrid stumpy feet might kill a very little one!" As the giant appeared, I felt a pang of unease, but the girl reappeared beside me, her eyes glinting with a knowing light. "We must have a care," she whispered, before disappearing into thin air, leaving me alone with the giant.
