Felix's voice wove a tale of their quest for justice, of the pursuit of John Poindexter, the man who had callously dismissed their grief and left them to their despair. "It was dusk when I shared my story," Felix began, "and from that moment until dawn, we sat entwined in a bond of mutual understanding, our eyes locked in a silent communion. We sought out John Poindexter, and though he might have spared us the pain of confronting him, he chose instead to meet us head-on, his expression a mask of detachment, a shield against the world's emotions."

"He told us to spare ourselves, to enjoy our fleeting lives, to savor the moments we had left," Amos said, his voice heavy with the weight of remembered anguish. "But we knew that we could not let him off so easily. We vowed, Felix and I, that we would one day make him feel the sting of our suffering, that we would find a way to pierce the armor of his heart and make him understand the depths of our pain."

"Thirty years have passed since that fateful day, but the fire that burns within me has never dimmed.
