The air in the house was heavy with an unspoken tension, like the scent of stale bread lingering on a plate. Sundays were just like any other day now, with the hot joint appearing at table without fanfare, accompanied by an endless procession of puddings that had lost their allure. We ate and drank without restraint, but the joy was gone. No longer did we laugh and tease over the sausages or the sweet-smelling herrings; instead, our conversations were stilted and hollow, as if a silent guest sat with us at the table, sucking the life from our words.

My parents would occasionally discuss moving to Guilford Street, but the conversation always faltered on some prickly issue, leaving us stuck in this miserable limbo. Months passed, and the cloud of unhappiness deepened.

Then came the news of old Teidelmann's sudden passing in his counting house. My mother's eyes widened with a mixture of emotions when I told her I'd been summoned to his funeral. "You're going?" she asked, her voice laced with a bitter irony. My father stood up, his face tight, and left the house without a word. He was away for hours, and my mother retreated to her room, her sobs muffled behind the door. I couldn't fathom why she should grieve so deeply for a man we barely knew.

After that, Mrs. Teidelmann came to our house more often. Her black veil added a somber elegance to her features, softening the sharp angles of her face. She was kind to my mother, who seemed dull and ungracious beside her. To me, Mrs. Teidelmann was a gentle angel, always bearing small gifts or suggesting treats that I secretly cherished. She seemed to understand me in a way my mother never did – the books I loved, the games that brought me joy, and the simple pleasures that made life worth living. My mother, on the other hand, would often fill my head with the prospect of a future filled with strict rules and obligations, a narrow path I was forced to follow. Mrs. Teidelmann, however, painted a different picture – one of sun-drenched fields and winding paths where I could roam freely.
