The fog crept through the narrow alleys of Cloisterham like a chill, damp breath. Neville Landless stood at his window, watching the wisps of vapor dance across the frost-kissed panes. His eyes wandered to the clock tower, where the chimes echoed through the stillness, a mournful reminder of time's relentless march. He felt a pang of restlessness, a sense of disconnection from the world outside his quiet room.

Below, the streets bustled with people hurrying to and fro, their footsteps muffled by the soft earth and fallen leaves. Red berries clung to the lattices of Minor Canon Corner, like rubies glowing in the pale winter light. The air was heavy with the scent of woodsmoke and baking spices, carried on the whispers of the rooks in the cathedral tower. The atmosphere was alive with the promise of Christmas, yet Neville felt a sense of disconnection, as if he were observing the celebrations from a distant, forgotten room.

As he turned away from the window, his gaze fell upon his scattered papers and books. He began to tidy his space, methodically gathering his notes and destroying any unnecessary scraps. His hands moved with a calm deliberation, as if performing a ritual. When the task was complete, he reached for his wardrobe, selecting a few items of practical clothing – a change of sturdy shoes and warm socks – which he packed into a new knapsack. A recent purchase, it seemed out of place among the familiar contours of his room.

By his side lay a walking stick, its iron-shod tip glinting in the dim light. He picked it up, feeling the weight and balance of it, before setting it aside with the knapsack. His preparations were almost complete. With a sense of detachment, Neville dressed for the evening, his movements economical and precise. As he finished, he glanced at the clock, marking the time – two hours past noon. He had a meeting to keep, one that would take him into the night, and beyond.
