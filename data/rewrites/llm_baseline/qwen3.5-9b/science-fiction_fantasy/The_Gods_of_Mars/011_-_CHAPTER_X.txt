My escort led me through the outer gardens, where I discovered Xodar besieged by a throng of noble Black Martians. They hurled vitriol and curses at him, their faces twisted in loathing. Men slapped his cheeks; women spat in his face. When I stepped forward, the mob's attention shifted instantly to me.

"Ah!" cried one, "So this is the beast who bested the great Xodar with his bare hands. Let us see how the trick was performed."

Another laughed, suggesting, "Bind Thurid! Thurid is a noble Dator. Let him teach this dog what it feels like to face a true man."

"Yes, Thurid! Thurid!" a dozen voices chorused.

"He arrives now!" someone shouted, pointing. Turning, I saw a massive Black Martian, encumbered with glittering ornaments and heavy weaponry, advancing with arrogant grace.

"What do you want?" he bellowed, stopping before us. "Speak quickly, Thurid."

The crowd explained their demand. Thurid turned his gaze toward Xodar, his eyes narrowing into malevolent slits.

"Calot!" he hissed. "I always suspected you harbored the heart of a sorak within your rotting chest. You may have bested me in the shadowy councils of Issus, but on the battlefield, where men are truly measured, your scabby soul has exposed its festering wounds to the world. Calot, I spit upon you with my foot!"

With that, he lifted his leg to deliver a crushing kick.

My blood boiled. Minutes prior, I had seethed at the cowardly treatment meted out to my former comrade simply because he had fallen from favor with Issus. While I held no affection for Xodar, I possessed zero tolerance for such brazen injustice. The sight of it sent a haze of crimson mist clouding my judgment, urging me to act on impulse rather than reason.

Standing mere inches from Xodar as Thurid swung his leg, the degraded Dator stood rigid as a statue. He was prepared to absorb every insult and rebuke in stoic silence. But as Thurid's foot rose, so did mine. I delivered a sharp, painful blow to the shin bone, saving Xodar from further humiliation.

Silence stretched taut for a heartbeat, then Thurid roared in fury and lunged for my throat—the same move Xodar had attempted against me on the cruiser's deck. The outcome was identical. I ducked under his outstretched arms, and as he passed, I drove a devastating right hook into the side of his jaw. The giant spun like a top, his knees buckling until he collapsed onto the ruby-dusted path at my feet.

The Blacks stared in stunned disbelief, first at the prostrate form of the proud Dator, then at me, unable to comprehend what they had witnessed.

"You demanded I bind Thurid," I declared, stepping over the fallen warrior. "Behold!"

I stooped, ripped the ornate harness from his frame, and secured his arms and legs tightly.

"Now treat Thurid as you treated Xodar," I commanded the surrounding guards. "Take him before Issus, bound in his own chains, so she may see with her own eyes that there exists among you one greater than the First Born."

A woman, the one who had initially suggested binding Thurid, whispered, "Who are you?"

"I am a citizen of two worlds," I replied. "Captain John Carter of Virginia, Prince of the House of Tardos Mors, and Jeddak of Helium. Take this man to your goddess as instructed, and tell her that having subdued both Xodar and Thurid, I can match the mightiest of her Dators. With naked hands, with long-sword, or with short-sword, I challenge the finest warriors of your race to combat."

"Come," said the officer guarding me, gesturing toward the return path to Shador. "Orders are imperative; there is no time for delay. Xodar, follow."

There was little malice in his tone regarding either of us. Having witnessed the effortless dispatch of the powerful Thurid, his contempt for the disgraced Dator had evaporated. Furthermore, his deference toward me—a mere slave—was palpable. During the remainder of the journey back, he walked or stood constantly behind me, a short-sword drawn in his hand.

The return voyage to the Sea of Omean was uneventful. We descended the terrifying shaft in the same car that had brought us to the surface, boarded the submarine, and dove deep into the tunnel beneath the upper world. Emerging from the tunnel, we ascended to the pool where we had first encountered the magnificent passage connecting Omean to the Temple of Issus. From the submarine's island, we transferred to a small cruiser bound for the distant Isle of Shador.

Upon arrival, we found a small stone prison guarded by six Blacks. There was no ceremony in our incarceration. One guard unlocked the heavy door with a massive key; we entered, the door slammed shut, and the lock grated loudly. Immediately, that terrible sensation of hopelessness washed over me, identical to the dread I had felt in the Chamber of Mystery beneath the Golden Cliffs.

Tars Tarkas had been with me then, but now I was utterly alone in terms of friendship. My mind drifted to the fate of the great Thark and his beautiful companion, Thuvia. Even if they had miraculously escaped and found refuge elsewhere, what hope existed for aid reaching this hidden sanctuary? None on all Barsoom dreamed of such a place. Knowing the precise location of my prison offered no advantage, for who could penetrate this buried sea against the formidable navy of the First Born?

My situation seemed hopeless. Yet, refusing to succumb to despair, I rose and began to explore my confinement.

Xodar sat on a low stone bench near the center of the room, head bowed, silent since his degradation by Issus. The structure was roofless, with walls rising thirty feet high. Two small, heavily barred windows punctuated the mid-level. Partitions twenty feet tall divided the space into several rooms. Our current cell was empty except for two open doors leading to adjacent chambers.

I explored the connected rooms, finding them vacant until I reached the final one. There, a young Red Martian boy slept on the stone bench, the only piece of furniture in the cell. He was clearly the sole other prisoner. Leaning over, I studied his face. Something about him felt strangely familiar, yet I could not place him. His features were perfectly regular, his limbs graceful and beautiful. Though lighter in color than typical Reds, he was undeniably a specimen of their handsome race.

Sleep in prison is a precious commodity; I refrained from waking him, knowing how easily deprivation can transform men into beasts.

Returning to my cell, I found Xodar still seated in the same posture.

"Man," I called out, "moping serves you no purpose. To be bested by John Carter is no shame. You saw how easily I handled Thurid, and recall how I slew three of your comrades on the cruiser's deck."

"I wish you had ended me alongside them," he muttered.

"Come, come!" I urged. "Hope remains. Neither of us is dead. We are skilled fighters. Why not strive for freedom?"

He looked at me in astonishment. "You speak of things you do not understand," he replied. "Issus is omnipotent and omniscient. She hears your words now; she knows your thoughts. It is sacrilege even to dream of defying her commands."

"Nonsense, Xodar," I snapped.

He sprang to his feet in horror. "The curse of Issus will fall upon you!" he shrieked. "In an instant, you will be struck down, writhing in agonizing death throes."

"Do you truly believe that, Xodar?" I asked calmly.

"Of course! Who dares doubt?"

"I doubt it. I deny it entirely." I paced slightly. "You claim she reads my thoughts. Yet, the Red Martians possess this power for ages, along with the ability to shield their minds. I learned the first secret years ago; the second, I never needed to master, for no one on all Barsoom can read the secrets of my brain."

"Your goddess cannot read my thoughts, nor yours when you are out of sight, unless you permit it," I continued. "Had she been able to read my mind, her pride would have suffered a severe shock when I obeyed her command to 'gaze upon the holy vision of her radiant face.'"

"What do you mean?" he whispered, terrified.

"I mean that I considered her the most repulsive and vilely hideous creature my eyes have ever beheld."

For a moment, he stared at me in horrified amazement. Then, crying out "Blasphemer!", he charged at me.

I had no desire to strike him again, nor was it necessary, as he was unarmed. As he advanced, I seized his left wrist with my left hand, swung my right arm around his shoulder, and drove my elbow sharply beneath his chin, throwing him backward across my thigh. He hung there, helpless, glaring upward in impotent rage.

"Xodar," I said gently, "let us be friends. For perhaps a year, we may be forced to share these cramped confines. I apologize for offending you, but I never imagined one who suffered such cruelty from Issus could still believe her divine."
