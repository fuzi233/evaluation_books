Moonlight spilled across the street below, the wind whispering through the empty lanes like a thief. Yet not a breath of it touched me in my refuge. I had just settled into uneasy rest when a glimmer of movement caught my eye—the sleek, luminous head of the creature I had seen trailing the Shadow rose above the topmost step.

The moment our eyes met, she froze, then retreated, tail first. I leapt to my feet; with no room to turn, she tumbled backward in a flash of white, scrambled up, and vanished down the stairs. I chased her to the street, scanning the shadows, but she was gone.

Two predators now haunted Bulika—one spotted, one pure. I cared little for its treacherous citizens, but a child’s life? That was worth my meager existence. I would stand guard.

The latch clicked—slow, deliberate. The door creaked open. Not the woman I had aided, but the veiled figure of the desert stood there, silent. She led me to a barren chamber, pointed to a rug, and left. The outer door sighed shut.

Then came the sounds—a stifled moan, a child’s cry, a shriek that tore the night. I burst into the passage. There, the white leopardess, a newborn clutched in her jaws like a cub. I threw myself at her, wrenching the infant free. It wailed as it struck stone.

The shrouded woman appeared, stepping over our struggle. She took the child and vanished. When she returned, she pulled me from the beast and pushed me into the night—the leopardess at my heels.

*Betrayed again,* I thought. *Given to the hunter.* But as I fled down the steps, she followed, docile. I turned, braced for attack—instead, she crouched at my feet. Hesitant, I stroked her moonlit fur. Her rough tongue rasped against my skin.

Perhaps she, too, was trapped. If I refused every gesture of kinship, how would I ever find the truth buried in this damned world?

A wet thud broke the silence—a woman’s broken body sprawled in the street. Nearby, the Shadow walked, the leopardess now trailing him once more. I pursued, but as I neared, she whirled, fangs bared, eyes blazing green fire. A snap, a bite—then a look almost pleading. *Why did you make me?*

I turned away, furious. Enough wasting time. The palace loomed ahead, its walls crumbling, gates agape. It slept under the moon, silence thick as a curse.

Exhaustion overtook me. I curled into a recess, a stone bench my bed. Then warmth—weight—a tongue rough as bark against my cheek. The leopardess. I braced for teeth, for blood. Instead, she pressed close, her heat seeping into my bones. I slept.

Dawn came with the illusion of home—the scent of gardens, the comfort of a bed. Then the cruel truth: cold stone, the stink of the city. Yet her warmth lingered.

I rose. Hope, shapeless but fierce, kindled in my chest. I would find the princess. I had saved her once. That had to count for something.

Even if it killed me.
