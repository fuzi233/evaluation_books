As Gerard and Denys entered the rustic inn, the landlord, a man with a worn expression, hastily removed his cap, his eyes darting towards the couple before settling on some invisible point beyond. Gerard quipped, "We're not in Germany, after all," and the landlord bowed low, his cap clutched tightly in his hands. In the warm, fire-lit common room, the mistress, a robust woman in her mid-forties, greeted them with a bright smile and a curtsy. "Welcome, gentlemen, take a seat," she said, dusting off two chairs with her apron, though they seemed spotless. Gerard nodded graciously, and Denys shot him a knowing glance.

As they sat, Gerard couldn't help but think that this was indeed a polite nation – one that made a fuss about the simplest things, like sitting down. The mistress laughed, her eyes twinkling, and began to chat with them like old friends. Denys leaned back in his chair, a pleased smile spreading across his face. "Why not?" he asked, responding to the mistress's query about their numbers. "Can we not dine for two?"

Their hostess chuckled and called out to the kitchen, "Supper for our guests!" A young maid entered, carrying a steaming tray laden with an assortment of dishes. The mistress followed, her eyes shining with warmth, and invited them to begin eating. "You have but one enemy here," she said with a wink, "and he lies beneath your knife." Gerard and Denys exchanged a look, unsure if this was a jest or a genuine warning.

As they ate, the mistress regaled them with stories and laughter, drawing her chair closer to the table. She proved to be a skilled conversationalist, effortlessly weaving tales of the local countryside and its people. When she excused herself to attend to other matters, a younger woman, her daughter, entered the room. She was a vision of loveliness, with a gentle smile and a kind heart. Gerard was taken aback by her beauty and her concern for his well-being, and Denys beamed with pride.

The young woman, whose name was not mentioned, lingered for nearly an hour, chatting with the travelers and sharing stories of her own.
