As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the sleepy streets of Sevenbergen, Peter's household bustled with activity. The scent of roasting meats wafted from the kitchen, mingling with the aroma of freshly baked bread. Margaret, recovering from a bout of illness, lay abed, her appetite whetted by the savory smells. Meanwhile, Jorian Ketel arrived at the door, his intentions clear: to claim Margaret's promise. But Peter, his temper frayed by the town's gossip mill, chased him off with a stern warning.

The incident left Jorian in a foul mood, his thoughts consumed by regret. It was a sentiment he shared with Dierich Brower, who had recently faced the wrath of the burghers after trying to extract information from a tipsy informant at "The Three Kings." Dierich's punishment – a dunking in the local horsepond – served as a stark reminder of the consequences of overstepping the law. The burgomaster of Sevenbergen took the opportunity to send a formal rebuke to his counterpart in Tergou, reprimanding him for allowing Ghysbrecht Van Swieten's men to harass the townspeople.

Ghysbrecht, however, remained unruffled, his composure a thin veil for the turmoil brewing within. He dispatched a conciliatory message to Tergou, claiming that the person his agents had pursued was a Tergovian named Gerard, who had allegedly stolen the town records before fleeing to unknown lands. But his words belied the unease growing inside him. Gerard's disappearance had left a void, and Ghysbrecht couldn't shake the feeling that his nemesis was merely biding his time, waiting for the perfect moment to strike.

As the days passed, Ghysbrecht's behavior became increasingly erratic, his moods darkening like a stormy sky. His eyes darted about, searching for a threat that seemed always just out of reach. His associates noticed the change, sensing a malevolent force at work. Meanwhile, at the Tergou estate, the little family struggled to rebuild their lives. Kate, the young daughter, felt an inexplicable chill whenever she saw her stepbrothers, Cornelis and Sybrandt, their faces now etched with hypocrisy. Her mother, Catherine, observed the tension, her intuition guiding her to remain silent, but her gaze lingering on the pair with a mixture of sadness and suspicion.
