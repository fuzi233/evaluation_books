Noah stirred before dawn, the pale light creeping through the windows of his small cottage like a gentle lover's caress. He rose, his movements economical, and set about preparing a simple breakfast: a tartine, a slice of bread toasted to perfection, and a steaming cup of café au lait. The aroma of freshly ground coffee wafted through the air, mingling with the scent of woodsmoke carried on the breeze from the nearby mill.

As he ate, Noah's thoughts turned to the day ahead, and the tasks that awaited him in Whinbury. He finished his meal quickly, his movements swift and practiced, and set out into the crisp morning air. The dew-kissed grass sparkled like diamonds beneath his feet as he walked, the sun slowly rising above the hills to cast a golden glow over the landscape.

Upon his return, Noah made his way to his counting-house, a small, cozy room filled with the musty scent of old books and the faint tang of ink. His clerk, Henry, brought in the morning's correspondence and the daily papers, which Noah perused with a critical eye. His brow furrowed in concentration, he scribbled notes in the margins, his pen moving swiftly across the page.

As he worked, the door to the counting-house stood open, admitting a warm breeze and the sound of birdsong from outside. The sunlight streaming in highlighted the dust motes dancing in the air, casting a tiny, shimmering aura around each speck. Noah's gaze wandered, his mind seemingly elsewhere, but his hands moved with precision, handling the papers with a practiced ease.

Just then, a dark-blue figure appeared at the doorway, Joe Scott emerging from the dyeing vats behind him. "He's come, sir," he announced, before vanishing as suddenly as he appeared.

A moment later, a large, broad-shouldered man entered, his fustian garments and gray worsted stockings a stark contrast to the tidy, ordered world within the counting-house. He nodded to Noah, who gestured to a chair, and the man took a seat, his eyes scanning the room with a practiced air. As he settled in, he doffed his hat, revealing a tangled mess of hair, and wiped his forehead with a spotted handkerchief.

"It's right warm for February, isn't it?" he observed, his voice gruff but affable. Noah murmured a response, his eyes returning to the paper in front of him.
