The city slumbered, its stone façades worn smooth by the relentless passage of time. I walked through the deserted streets, the echoes of my footsteps a melancholy accompaniment to my thoughts. My destination was the Cathedral, where the Choir-master had once dwelled. Now, he was absent, on a brief leave to perform in London. His departure had left an uneasy silence in the town, like a held breath waiting to be released.

As I walked, the buildings seemed to loom over me, their shadows cast long and sinister on the ground. The wind rustled through the trees, whispering secrets in a language only the leaves could understand. I felt a pang of nostalgia for the music that once filled these streets, the harmonies that had brought people together in joy and sorrow. Now, the only sound was the creaking of wooden signs and the distant hum of the river.

At the hotel behind Aldersgate Street, I found a small room with a view of the General Post Office. The sign above the door read 'Lodgings, Hotel, Boarding House', as if the proprietor couldn't decide what to call it. I settled in, my footsteps quiet on the worn carpet. In the morning, I would visit the Cathedral, hoping to catch a glimpse of the Choir-master's thoughts, now that he was away. But for now, I sat in silence, surrounded by the whispers of the past.

The Cathedral's organist, John Jasper, was a man consumed by his art. His fingers moved deftly over the keys, coaxing sounds from the instrument that seemed almost alive. Yet, in his eyes, a darkness lurked, a sense of purpose that drove him forward with an unyielding passion. His determination was a shield, protecting him from the world outside, but also isolating him from those who might understand him.

As I pondered Jasper's enigmatic figure, the city began to stir around me. People emerged from their homes, going about their daily routines. The sounds of the city grew louder, a cacophony of human activity that threatened to overwhelm the quiet I had sought. I rose from my chair, deciding to take a walk along the river. The water flowed smoothly, a reflection of the calm I sought to find.
