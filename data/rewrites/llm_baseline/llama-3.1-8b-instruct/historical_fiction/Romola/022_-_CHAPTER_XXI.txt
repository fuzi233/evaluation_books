November 17th, 1494, marked the beginning of a new era in Florence, one that would be forever etched in the memories of its inhabitants. It had been nearly nineteen months since Tito and Romola had exchanged their vows during the vibrant Easter celebrations, surrounded by the gentle shower of sweet-smelling comfits, a tradition passed down from ancient Greece. Their union had been a beacon of hope amidst the tumultuous times, and it was said that the heavens themselves would bless their marriage with abundance and prosperity.

As the seasons changed, so did the fortunes of Florence. The once-stagnant air was filled with the whispers of revolution, and the city's fate hung precariously in the balance. Just a week prior, the people had risen up against the Medici, driven by a sense of desperation and a yearning for freedom. The great bell in the palace tower had tolled the call to arms, and the citizens, armed with whatever they could find, had marched forth to reclaim their city. The gates of San Gallo had swung shut on the fleeing Piero and his retinue, and the people had rejoiced at their departure. The images of those who had conspired against the Medici were scrubbed from the walls, and the exiles were invited back to the fold.

Now, the grand palace in the Via Larga stood ready to receive a new occupant, its walls adorned with colorful tapestries and banners that fluttered in the gentle breeze. The streets were carpeted with vibrant fabrics, and the sound of hammering echoed through the air as the townspeople prepared for the arrival of their esteemed guest. The very fabric of the city seemed to vibrate with anticipation as the colors spilled out from the palace, flowing like a river through the Cathedral, the Piazza della Signoria, and across the Ponte Vecchio to the Porta San Frediano. A platform and canopy had been erected near the gate, where the Signoria would gather to welcome the stranger. Messer Luca Corsini, a learned doctor of law, felt his heart quicken at the thought of delivering a stirring oration, while the city's elders donned their finest attire, smoothing their chins and adjusting their silk luccos. The young nobles, too, polished their tunics, emulating the elegant style of the French, in the hopes of making a favorable impression on their guest.
