I drew near to the Mighty Pyramid, and my great Home rose into the endless night like a mountain of life and safety—towering, immense, and awe-inspiring. Yet despair had hollowed my heart too deeply for wonder. All that mattered was getting Mine Own Maid into the shelter of the vast refuge. Still, it remained far beyond reach.

I pressed forward across the desolate land with grim determination. As I passed a hollow where a fire-hole burned, something emerged from the shadows. A massive, hirsute man crawled up, then rose to his feet. He stared at me, then stepped closer, hands outstretched, eager and wild. In the flickering light, I saw them clearly—monstrous hands, thick with cruel claws, capable of tearing flesh as easily as a beast.

I dropped Mine Own Maid to the ground. I no longer cared for life or survival. This creature would delay me, and I could not endure any obstacle. With a surge of cold fury, I lunged at him, swinging the Diskos. He sidestepped in an instant, avoiding the blow. Then, from the half-darkness cast by the dancing flames, he thrust his arm forward and seized my headpiece, wrenching it from my head with brutal force. The impact threw me back nearly a dozen feet, slamming me onto the earth. I was shaken, bruised—but not broken. I rose immediately, charged again, and brought the Diskos down hard. It struck true, cleaving through his midsection. The weapon drank deep, slicing clean through him. His upper body collapsed; the lower half stood upright, blood gushing upward like a fountain into the black sky.

I did not hesitate. I leapt to Mine Own Maid, cradled her in my arms, and ran past the fallen giant, who toppled only then with a terrible thud. The night itself seemed to stir—millions of spirits awakened, their cries rising around me, filled with awe and love. They had seen what I had done. They rejoiced. Their energy surged through me, a wave of shared purpose.

Not long after, two indistinct shapes emerged from behind jagged rocks. I struck them both with the Diskos and moved on. What they were, I never knew.

From then on, it felt as though I fought endlessly. Creatures crawled from bushes and crevices—beasts of every kind, twisted and monstrous. The land teemed with life, foul and deadly. I swung the Diskos in a daze, driven by a fierce, unrelenting desperation. Our time was running out. I could not save her. Not if I failed now.

The air groaned with roars and low, guttural sounds—deadly and unnatural. Once, I heard the thunderous tread of giants. The night was evil. And yet—I survived. How? Perhaps because I had been forged in fire, hardened by relentless trial. My body had become immune to weakness, my spirit armored against fear.

Then came the baying of the Night-Hounds—deep, dreadful, and drawing nearer from the southeast. I knew now: no strength of mine could protect her.

And from the Last Light high above, a strange blue flash lanced downward into the land. Again, and again—dozens of times. A crackling sound followed, sharper than thunder but more intense than any noise I’d ever known. I understood instantly—the Humans were fighting for me. They were using the Earth-Current to save us.

The land erupted into chaos. The night trembled. Monsters howled, stirred by some unseen force. From the hidden east, that terrible laughter echoed once more—ancient, mocking, and full of dread.

Now the Hounds bayed constantly, their pack unmistakable. They were less than a mile away. I was alone, save for the dying woman in my arms. I looked desperately for the Hundred Thousand—those who had prepared to aid me. But there was nothing. Only shadows, shifting lights, and the movement of monstrous life. The Hounds closed in with each passing second. Death was near.

I kept moving. Then I began to run. The Pyramid loomed ahead, its circle of light visible despite occasional veils of darkness. Hope, fragile and desperate, flared: perhaps we would make it.

The baying grew louder. The Hounds were half a mile away—perhaps less. They had scent of us. I called out, voice raw with anguish, but no one answered. No help came.

And then—through the spiritual link, I felt it: the Millions mourned with me. Their sorrow washed over me like a tide. They saw my despair, my frantic glances, my cries. In response, a gentle, powerful presence surrounded me—a warmth born of their empathy, their love, their understanding. They knew I was at the edge of hope. They knew the Hounds were upon me.

At that moment, the Earth-Current pulsed beneath my feet. I knew the Humans had unleashed their final defense.

Then I saw them—a vast pack of Hounds, charging from the left. Their heads were low, their bodies the size of horses. They came in waves, shadow and form merging in the dim light.

I realized: we would die within minutes unless they acted.

I stopped running. There was no point. I turned, staring first at the Hounds, then at the Pyramid. Again, I looked at the Hounds. Then at the Pyramid. Two hundred fathoms away. Hundreds of beasts. No escape.

And just as I gazed upon the Pyramid one last time—there it was.

A monstrous flame burst from the sealed base of the structure. It shot downward, engulfing the land where the Hounds ran. The night vanished in blinding light. I could see nothing—no Pyramid, no sky, only fire and radiant horror. The blast hit the air, scorching me even from afar. The heat withered my skin. I understood: the Humans had activated the Earth-Current. They had unleashed it upon the Hounds.

Thunder shook the world. The force tore through the air, split the earth. Rocks flew like cannonballs. The roar of monsters faded beneath the storm. I saw no Hounds—only fire, destruction, and chaos. I was thankful I hadn’t been crushed under a falling boulder.

Then, abruptly, the Current ceased. The Humans regained control.

Silence returned—absolute, crushing. Darkness swallowed everything, except the distant glow and smoke where the current had struck.

I shook off the daze and ran again. Now, I might survive.

My eyes adjusted. I scanned the surroundings, wary of new threats. For a time, there was nothing—only the distant, horrid laughter from the dead East.

As I ran, I glanced upward at the Pyramid. Its light dimmed. At first, I thought it was due to the glare of the explosion. But soon I realized: the light was truly fading. The power of the Earth-Current had been drained. The mighty fortress was weakening.

This chilled me. If the Current faltered too much, all the Humans—millions of lives—would be in danger. The Masters knew this. They could not use the Current again until it recovered. To do so would risk destroying all life on Earth.

I understood this fully as I ran—and it made me more desperate than ever.

Still, I expected the Hundred Thousand to come. They did not.

Around me, the land stirred again. New sounds arose—creatures awakening, forces stirring. I saw living things crawling between me and the Circle’s glow. I would have to fight again.

I raised the Diskos and pressed on.

Suddenly, my spirit sensed danger. I looked up, hoping the Master Monstruwacan would warn me via the Set Speech. But there was no signal—only silence and dimming light.

Later, I learned the Master *had* tried. But the instruments in the Tower of Observation had failed. The machinery of the Pyramid had shut down—lifts stalled, air pumps silenced. Everything had gone dark for nearly an hour, until the Current stabilized.

That meant death had nearly claimed the entire civilization.

Yet my spirit still felt the warning—not from machines, but from the pain of the Millions, from the faint telepathic call of the Master himself. I walked more cautiously, scanning every direction.

Then—above us—a pale, quiet circle hovered in the night. Steady. Unmoving. I recognized it: one of the Holy Powers, a guardian of souls. A shield against the darkness that threatened to consume us. I felt no fear. I trusted the Force of Holiness. I ran on.

I reached within four hundred paces of the Circle. I thought I had won. But the light of the Circle burned dim. Was it still protective? Could it guard us if the Current weakened?

I ran swiftly, warily, consumed by anxiety.

Then—three beast-men rose from the earth in a shadowed patch. They growled, charging. The first was too close for the Diskos. I struck him with the haft, smashing his skull. I leapt aside, spun, and swung. Fury gripped me—cold, absolute. The Maid was a child in my arm. I met the other two head-on, cutting with precision, swift and merciless. I killed them as if they were mice. None touched me.

In that moment, a shout erupted from within the Circle—joyful, welcoming. I looked, then ran faster. Men in grey armor stood inside, but none came to help.

Then I understood why.

Along the outer edge of the Circle, monstrous Black Mounds swayed and pulsed with unnatural life. Horrors churned beneath their surfaces. If any Human stepped beyond the boundary, they would be destroyed in spirit—lost forever. No one dared. Even sacrifice would be useless. Dead men could not save us.

But the Hundred Thousand shouted urgently: “Haste! Haste!”

I obeyed. I ran with all my strength.

The glowing Circle loomed ahead. The Holy Light above us held steady. We were saved.

I was less than a hundred paces away when the attack came.

An herd of squat, brutish men erupted from the shadows. They grabbed at me, at the Maid, trying to tear her from my arms.

They nearly succeeded. I couldnARED to free myself, defend her, and wield the Diskos at once.

Then—kicks from my metal boots. Twisting. Struggling. I broke free and stumbled back. The herd pursued.

Now I had room. I turned, charged, and cut through them. The Diskos spun, roaring, casting eerie light on their pig-like tusks. I raged through them, striking from right and left, circling fast. They struck me with stones—thousands of blows. My armor rang, cracked. I was battered, wounded, near collapse. But they never reached the Maid. She remained safe above their reach.

The brutes seemed endless. But I advanced, toward the Circle. The night echoed with the shouts of the Hundred Thousand. Many had tried to rush forward—but others held them back. Sacrifice would mean nothing.

Fifty paces. I was nearly spent. Dazed, bleeding, exhausted. I had not slept. I had carried her through days and nights. Fought countless battles.

Then—before the Circle, the Hundred Thousand stood ready. Each man raised his Diskos. They hurled them into the swarm.

It worked. The herd thinned. I gathered my strength and charged, smiting without pause. Bodies fell all around. I broke through, clutching Mine Own Maid, and stepped over the Circle.

Its resistance was weak—almost gone.

A thousand hands reached out—but none touched me. They recoiled. Something about me repelled them. I was changed. Strange.

I stood in silence. The Diskos dripped blood to the haft. I swayed. Hands reached again—then pulled back. Fear, respect, awe.

I looked at them. They looked back. I gasped, struggling to speak. I needed doctors. My Maid was dying.

Then—outside, the sound of giants running.

Voices cried out: “Help him!” “Beware the giants!” “Bring the doctors!”

Others shouted: “The Holy Light is gone!” “The Black Mounds are vanishing!”

The land roared. The noise overwhelmed me. My mind, already shattered by exhaustion, began to fail.

But another sound rose—soft, constant, coming from above and near. The voice of millions—shouting upward, a murmuring chorus from the heights, echoing through the miles.

I found my voice. “Is there a doctor here?”

A Master of the Diskos stepped forward—commander of this age. He saluted with his weapon, offered to take the Maid. I asked again: “Is there a Doctor?”

He gave an order. Thousands shifted, forming a path to the Great Gateway.

The Master signaled. Men stood around me—supporting, but not touching. I was not to be touched. I feared I had come too late. And they seemed to sense my strangeness.

Orders flew. Soon, two men from the Upward Cities arrived, carrying a small figure on a sling. A Master Doctor. He helped me lay the Maid gently on the ground.

The Master of the Diskos gestured. The guards turned away. The Doctor knelt.

Silence fell. The Hundred Thousand stood still. The Pyramid was quiet. Everyone knew: the fate of the maid rested on this moment.

Then the Doctor looked up—calm, sorrowful.

I knew.

Mine Own Maid was dead.

He saw my knowing. He covered her face. Rose quickly. Whispered orders. Some men approached to support me. Others lifted her body, bearing her toward the Gateway.

He studied me closely. I fought to breathe. Then I raised my hands—signaling them not to touch me, nor her.

The Doctor understood. I was strong—until the end.

I bent slowly, lifted Mine Own Maid into my arms once more—for the final journey.

I walked down the silent lane of the Hundred Thousand, clad in grey armor. Each man reversed his Diskos in salute, silently honoring me.

I barely registered anything. The world was empty. My mission had failed.

Yet—had it truly?

I had saved her from the terror of the Second Night Land. She had not died alone, mad and lost. She had died in my arms. And she had known, in her spirit, the depth of my love.

Memories flooded me—her soft touches, her quiet smiles, her whispered words. Then—like a knife—recollection: I had never woken to find her kissing me in sleep, as I had meant to.

A wave of agony tore through me. I blinked, vision blurred. I staggered. But the Master Doctor steadied me briefly—then let go. I walked on, alone.

As I neared the Great Gateway, the Pyramid’s lights flared again. The lifts whirred. The air pumps hummed. The Earth-Current had recovered. The gate opened—mechanized, grand.

Masters emerged. The dear Master Monstruwacan led them, rushing forward with the urgency of a father.

He had heard rumors—fear for the maid.

One near the gateway told him: she was dead.

He paused. All the Masters stood silent. Each reversed his Diskos.

This was the highest honor.

And I walked through—bearing the weight of grief, of love, of loss.

But also—of salvation.
