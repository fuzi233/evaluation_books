As soon as the sound of my wife's Peace-cry faded into the distance, signaling her departure, I turned my attention back to the Stranger. I intended to approach him closely and invite him to sit, but his appearance left me speechless and paralyzed with astonishment. He displayed absolutely no signs of angularity, yet he fluctuated instantly between gradations of size and brilliance that were utterly impossible for any figure within my known experience.

A terrifying thought flashed through my mind: perhaps I faced a burglar or a murderer—a monstrous, irregular Isosceles triangle—pretending to be a Circle to gain entry, now poised to strike me with its sharp angle. In a sitting room, where the air was unusually clear due to the dry season, visual recognition was unreliable at close range. Overcome by sheer terror, I rushed forward, shouting an uncouth, "Sir, you must permit me—" and reached out to touch him.

My wife had been right. There was not a trace of an angle, no roughness, no inequality. Never in my life had I encountered a more perfect Circle. He remained motionless while I circled him, starting from his eye and returning to it. Throughout, he was circular; a perfectly satisfactory Circle. Doubt was impossible.

Then began a dialogue, which I record as faithfully as memory allows, omitting only my excessive apologies—I was consumed by shame and humiliation that a Square should commit the impertinence of feeling a Circle.

The Stranger began with evident impatience regarding the length of my introduction.

**STRANGER:** "Have you felt me sufficiently by now? Are you not introduced?"

**ME:** "Most illustrious Sir, forgive my clumsiness. It stems not from ignorance of social etiquette, but from surprise and nervousness caused by this unexpected visit. I beg you to keep my indiscretion secret, especially from my wife. However, before we proceed, would your Lordship graciously satisfy the curiosity of one eager to know whence this visitor comes?"

**STRANGER:** "From Space, from Space, Sir: whence else?"

**ME:** "Pardon me, my Lord, but are you not already *in* Space, along with your humble servant, at this very moment?"

**STRANGER:** "Pooh! What do you know of Space? Define it."

**ME:** "Space, my Lord, is height and breadth indefinitely prolonged."

**STRANGER:** "Exactly. You see, you do not even know what Space is. You believe it consists of only Two Dimensions. I have come to announce a Third—to you, height, breadth, and length."

**ME:** "Your Lordship seems to be joking. We too speak of length and height, or breadth and thickness, using four terms to denote Two Dimensions."

**STRANGER:** "But I mean not merely three names, but *Three Dimensions*."

**ME:** "Would your Lordship indicate or explain to me the direction of this Third Dimension, unknown to us?"

**STRANGER:** "I came from it. It is 'up above' and 'down below.'"

**ME:** "My Lord seems to imply it is Northward and Southward."

**STRANGER:** "I mean nothing of the kind. I mean a direction in which you cannot look, because you possess no eye on your side."

**ME:** "Pardon me, my Lord; a moment's inspection will convince you that I have a perfect luminary at the junction of two of my sides."

**STRANGER:** "Yes: but to see *into* Space, you ought to have an eye not on your perimeter, but on your side—that is, on what you would call your 'inside,' though we in Spaceland simply call it your 'side.'"

**ME:** "An eye in my inside! An eye in my stomach! Your Lordship jests!"

**STRANGER:** "I am in no jesting mood. I tell you I come from Space—or, since you refuse to grasp the term, from the Land of Three Dimensions, from whence I but recently looked down upon your Plane, which you foolishly call Space. From that vantage point, I discerned everything you claim is SOLID (meaning 'enclosed on four sides'): your houses, your churches, your very chests and safes, yes—even your insides and stomachs—all laid bare and exposed to my gaze."

**ME:** "Such assertions are easily made, my Lord."

**STRANGER:** "Easily made, yes—but not easily proven. Yet, I intend to prove mine. When I descended here, I saw your four sons, the Pentagons, each in their apartments, and your two grandsons, the Hexagons. I watched your youngest Hexagon linger briefly before retiring to his room, leaving you and your wife alone. I saw your three Isosceles servants supping in the kitchen and the little page working in the scullery. Then I arrived here. How do you suppose I entered?"

**ME:** "Through the roof, I suppose."

**STRANGER:** "Not so. Your roof was recently repaired and contains no aperture large enough for even a woman to pass through. I tell you I come from Space. Are you not convinced by my account of your children and household?"

**ME:** "Your Lordship must realize that facts concerning my belongings could easily be verified by anyone in the neighborhood possessing your ample resources for gathering intelligence."

*(To himself)* **STRANGER:** "What must I do? Wait... one more argument suggests itself."

When you see a Straight Line—for instance, your wife—how many Dimensions do you attribute to her?

**ME:** "Your Lordship treats me as if I were a commoner ignorant of mathematics, believing a Woman is truly a Straight Line of only One Dimension. No, no, my Lord; we Squares are wiser. We know that a Woman, though popularly called a line, is scientifically a very thin Parallelogram possessing Two Dimensions, like the rest of us: length and breadth (or thickness)."

**STRANGER:** "But the very fact that a Line is visible implies it possesses yet another Dimension."

**ME:** "My Lord, I have admitted that a Woman is both long and broad. We see her length; we infer her breadth, which, though slight, is measurable."

**STRANGER:** "You do not understand me. I mean that when you see a Woman, besides inferring her breadth, you ought to see her length AND SEE what we call her HEIGHT, although that dimension is infinitesimal in your country. If a Line were mere length without 'height,' it would cease to occupy space and become invisible. Surely you recognize this?"

**ME:** "I must confess I do not understand. When we in Flatland see a Line, we see LENGTH and BRIGHTNESS. If the brightness vanishes, the Line is extinguished and, as you say, ceases to occupy space. Am I to suppose you grant the title of a Dimension to 'brightness,' equating what we call 'bright' with what you call 'high'?"

**STRANGER:** "No, indeed. By 'height' I mean a Dimension like your length; only, with you, 'height' is imperceptible because it is extremely small."

**ME:** "My Lord, your assertion is easily tested. You say I have a Third Dimension, which you call 'height.' Since Dimension implies direction and measurement, please measure my 'height' or indicate the direction in which it extends. I will become your convert immediately. Otherwise, your own understanding must excuse me."

*(To himself)* **STRANGER:** "I can do neither. How shall I convince him? Surely a plain statement of facts followed by ocular demonstration ought to suffice."

"Now, Sir; listen. You live on a Plane. What you style Flatland is the vast, level surface of a fluid, upon which you and your countrymen move without rising above it or falling below it. I am not a plane Figure, but a Solid. You call me a Circle; in reality, I am not a Circle, but an infinite number of circles, ranging from a Point to a circle of thirteen inches in diameter, stacked one atop another. When I cut through your plane as I am now doing, I create a section that you correctly identify as a Circle. Even a Sphere—which is my proper name in my homeland—if it manifests itself to a Flatlander, must appear as a Circle. Do you not recall, for I, who see all things, witnessed last night the phantom vision of Lineland imprinted on your brain? Remember how, when you entered the realm of Lineland, you were forced to manifest not as a Square, but as a Line, because that linear realm lacked the dimensions to represent your whole form, showing only a slice or section of you? Precisely the same applies here. Your country of Two Dimensions is too narrow to represent me, a being of Three; it can only exhibit a slice or section of me, which is what you call a Circle."

The diminished brightness of your eye betrays your disbelief. But now, prepare for positive proof. You cannot see more than one of my sections—or Circles—at a time, for you lack the power to lift your eye out of the plane of Flatland. However, you can observe that as I rise in Space, my sections grow smaller. Watch now: I will rise, and the effect on your eye will be that my Circle shrinks until it dwindles to a point and finally vanishes.
