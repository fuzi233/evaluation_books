The scent of simmering herbs wafted through the farmhouse kitchen, mingling with the crackle of the fire as Ethne stirred the bubbling cauldron of her famous lamb stew. The evening air clung to her skin like a damp shroud, but she stood by the hearth, her eyes fixed on the flames as if willing them to dance higher. Her mind, however, was elsewhere – with Harry Feversham, who had sailed away down the Salcombe estuary months ago, leaving her to the quiet rhythms of rural life. The moment she had longed for, the moment she had lived for, was over. Yet, in the silence, she felt the ache of loneliness, a hollow echo that seemed to reverberate through every corner of her isolated village.

As she ladled the stew into a waiting bowl, her thoughts strayed to the night ahead, to the dinner she would serve tomorrow, a meal that would be a testament to her connection with the land and her people. The flavors of the stew, rich and earthy, transported her to a time when her ancestors had prepared similar dishes, passing down secrets and stories through generations. Ethne's hands moved deftly, as if guided by the ghosts of those who had come before her.

Just then, a knock at the door broke the spell. A servant announced the arrival of Colonel Durrance, and Ethne's expression transformed, her eyes sparkling with a mix of warmth and duty. As she greeted him, she ushered him into the parlor, where the scent of roasting vegetables and baking bread filled the air. They sat together, sipping tea and discussing the plans for Lennon House, the estate they would rebuild together.

But Durrance's eyes betrayed a hint of something more, a glint that spoke of a deeper purpose. "Ethne, my dear, I have a request," he said, his voice low and measured. "Would you play for me the Musoline Overture on your violin? Just as you did that night at Wadi Halfa?"

Ethne hesitated, her fingers stilled over the spoon, as if poised to scoop up a secret. "Not tonight, Colonel," she said finally, her voice firm but gentle. Yet, as she spoke, a spark within her flared to life, a flame that seemed to dance in tandem with the fire crackling in the hearth. "Still, I think I can play it," she said, a smile spreading across her face
