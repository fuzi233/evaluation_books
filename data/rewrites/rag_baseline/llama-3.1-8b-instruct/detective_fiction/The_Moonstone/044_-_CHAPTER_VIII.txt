As dusk gathered over the quiet streets, I received an unexpected visit from Mr. Bruff, the solicitor. The soft light filtering through the window highlighted the lines etched on his face, usually so stern, now tempered by a mixture of concern and fatigue. His handshake, a rare gesture, felt awkward, like a hesitant introduction to a long-forgotten acquaintance.

"Are you planning to return to Hampstead?" I ventured, seeking a neutral topic.

"I've just left," he replied, his voice laced with a hint of weariness. "I know you've finally uncovered the truth, Mr. Franklin. But, I must confess, if I had foreseen the price we'd pay for it, I would have preferred keeping you in the dark."

"Have you spoken to Rachel?"

He nodded, his eyes clouding over. "I took her back to Portland Place; it wouldn't have been wise to leave her alone in the carriage. I'm not laying the blame at your feet, considering I invited you to my home and permitted you to meet her. This unfortunate encounter has shaken her deeply. All I can do is ensure she doesn't suffer further harm. I want your assurance that you won't try to see her again without my consent."

"I promise," I said, my words barely above a whisper.

"You have my gratitude," he replied, his expression easing slightly. "Now, let's discuss the future. In my opinion, the outcome of this extraordinary turn of events is clear. Firstly, we're certain Rachel has revealed the entire truth to you, as plainly as words can convey it. Secondly, despite our conviction that there must be a terrible mistake somewhere, we can hardly fault her for believing you guilty based on her own perceptions, bolstered by the seemingly incriminating circumstances."

I interrupted him, my tone gentle. "I don't hold Rachel responsible; I only regret she couldn't speak more candidly to me at the time."

"You might as well lament that Rachel isn't someone else," he countered.
