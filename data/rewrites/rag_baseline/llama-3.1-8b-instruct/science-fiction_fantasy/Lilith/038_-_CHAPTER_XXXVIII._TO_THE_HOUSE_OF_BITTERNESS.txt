TO THE HOUSE OF BITTERNESS

As we ventured into the forest, the early morning sun cast long shadows across the landscape, illuminating the path ahead like a spectral guide. I guided Lona's horse, carrying her lifeless body, while contemplating the daunting task that lay before me. I would deliver her remains to her father, who would, I hoped, provide a place of rest for her in his ancestral home. If not, I would remain by her side, watching over her as she returned to the earth, her spirit merging with the land. My thoughts were a jumble of emotions, a mix of sorrow and determination.

I also carried Lilith, the woman I had been tasked with bringing to her father. Her fate weighed heavily on my mind – a constant reminder of my own inadequacies. How could I, a mere mortal, hope to control her? The burden of responsibility felt crushing, a weight I bore with each step. I had no right to keep her captive, nor to release her into a world that might not be ready for her unique gifts. As I pondered these doubts, I offered her food, but she refused, her eyes burning with a fierce, unyielding intensity.

As we journeyed deeper into the forest, the landscape shifted, and the air grew thick with the scent of blooming wildflowers. The hot stream we had crossed earlier now seemed a distant memory, replaced by the promise of a new day. The children, who had been quiet for most of our journey, began to stir, their excitement building as we approached the House of Bitterness. We stopped to rest, and I noticed a young girl approaching Lilith, a look of curiosity on her face. Before I could intervene, the child placed something against Lilith's lips, and a sudden cry of pain echoed through the air.

I hastened to her side, sucking the child's finger to soothe her pain. The girl's tears subsided, and she handed me another piece of fruit, her eyes shining with a mixture of fear and trust. But as soon as I took the fruit, she snatched her hand back, and it fell to the ground. The child's name was Luva, and I watched as she retreated, her eyes fixed on Lilith with a mixture of fascination and wariness.
