As I sat beside Lady Verinder, the scent of simmering tea wafted through the air, mingling with the faint tang of lemon from the preserve jar on the sideboard. Her fragile fingers, pale as the petals of a moonflower, trembled as she poured steaming hot water over the tea leaves. I waited for her to begin, the silence between us punctuated only by the soft clinking of cups and saucers. The gentle warmth of the afternoon sun streaming through the windows seemed at odds with the somber tone that had settled over our gathering.

"I've been unwell for some time, Drusilla," she said finally, her voice barely above a whisper. "Though I've hardly noticed it myself." I recalled the countless times I'd watched patients, oblivious to their own suffering, as they lay in bed, their bodies worn down by invisible forces. My aunt's words struck a chord within me, and I feared for her well-being.

"Yes, dear," I replied softly, my mind already racing with possibilities. "Yes." She continued, her hands moving with a deliberate slowness as she added sugar to her tea. "I brought Rachel to London for the doctors' advice. I wanted a second opinion, just in case."

Two doctors, I thought, a pang of unease creeping up my spine. Where was the priest? Where was the spiritual guidance? I pushed the thought aside, focusing on my aunt's words. "Yes, dear?" I asked, trying to sound nonchalant.

"One of the doctors was a stranger to me," she began again. "The other, however, was an old friend of my late husband's. He'd always shown a genuine interest in me, and I thought he'd be the perfect person to guide me through this difficult time." As she spoke, I noticed the way the sunlight danced across her face, highlighting the fine lines etched into her skin. "After examining Rachel, he asked to speak with me privately. I expected him to discuss my daughter's treatment, but instead, he took my hand and said, 'I've been observing you, Lady Verinder, with both a professional and personal interest. I'm afraid you're far more in need of medical attention than your daughter.'"
