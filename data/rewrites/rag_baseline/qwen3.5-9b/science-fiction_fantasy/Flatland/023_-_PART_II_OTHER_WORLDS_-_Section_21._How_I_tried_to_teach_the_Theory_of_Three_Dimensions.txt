The bell above the door chattered its final warning, and I stood frozen, the weight of my revelation pressing against my chest like a stone too heavy for my two-dimensional hands to lift. The street below had erupted into a cacophony of commands, a rising tide of voices demanding silence, until a single, authoritative trumpet cut through the din. A herald's proclamation rang out, clear and terrible: the Council's Resolution, ordering the arrest, imprisonment, or execution of any soul daring to pervert the public mind with delusions or claims of revelation from another World.

My heart hammered against my ribs. This was no time for folly. To speak of my vision now would be suicide. I resolved instantly to abandon the dangerous path of testimony and retreat to the safer, albeit slower, road of pure demonstration. *Upward, not Northward*—that was the key, the linchpin of the entire proof. It had burned bright in my mind moments before sleep claimed me, appearing as obvious as arithmetic upon waking, yet now, under the shadow of the law, the clarity seemed to fray at the edges.

Just then, my wife entered the room, her presence timely yet intrusive. We exchanged the requisite pleasantries, the hollow rituals of domesticity masking the storm within. Yet, despite her proximity, I knew I could not begin my instruction with her. Not today. My pentagonal sons, men of high standing and respected physicians, lacked the necessary mathematical rigor; they were ill-equipped to grasp the higher truths I sought to impart. Instead, my mind turned to a young hexagon, docile and inclined toward numbers, or even better—to my grandson. His earlier, offhand remark regarding $3^3$, which had strangely pleased the Sphere, marked him as a vessel ready for the Gospel. If I discussed this with a child, I would be safe; he knew nothing of the Council's edict. My sons, however, were steeped in a fervent patriotism and a reverent awe for the Circles that far outweighed natural affection. Should they discover me preaching sedition, they would undoubtedly drag me before the Prefect.

Before I could summon my grandson, I faced the inevitable: my wife's curiosity. She demanded to know why the Circle had summoned me and how I had breached the sanctity of our walls. I spun an elaborate tale, a fabrication woven with threads of plausible deniability, hoping to satisfy her without revealing the abyss of the Third Dimension. I managed, with difficulty, to persuade her to return to her household duties, silencing her questions before they could pierce the veil of my secret.

With the coast seemingly clear, I called for my grandson. Confessing the truth, I felt the memories of my vision slipping away like water through clenched fists, fading into the elusive haze of a half-grasped dream. I needed to test my skill, to forge a disciple before the memory vanished. As he entered, I locked the door, securing us both in a private sphere of potential heresy. Sitting beside him with our mathematical tablets—mere lines to you—I announced we would resume yesterday's lesson.

I guided his small mind through the familiar motions: how a point, driven by movement along a line, becomes a line; how a straight line, sweeping across a plane, generates a square. Then, seizing the moment, I forced a laugh onto my face. "And now, you scoundrel," I whispered, leaning in, "you tried to convince me that a square, by moving 'Upward, not Northward,' could birth another figure, a phantom extra-square existing in Three Dimensions. Repeat that, you young rascal."

At that instant, the herald's cry returned from the street, the "O yes! O yes!" echoing ominously. My grandson, though young, possessed an intelligence beyond his years, nurtured in absolute reverence for the Circle's authority. He understood the gravity of the moment instantly. Silence stretched between us, taut and terrifying, until the proclamation finally faded into the distance.

Then, the dam broke. Bursting into tears, he clutched my arm. "Grandpapa," he sobbed, "that was only a joke! I meant nothing by it! We didn't know about the new Law, and I'm sure I didn't say anything about the Third Dimension. And certainly, I didn't say 'Upward, not Northward'—that sounds like nonsense! How can something move upward and not northward? Even a baby wouldn't be so absurd. How silly it is! Ha! ha! ha!"

"Not at all silly," I snapped, my composure shattering as anger flared. "Here, look!" I grabbed a movable square resting nearby. "Watch closely. I move it—not Northward, but—" I hesitated, searching for the impossible vector. "Yes, I move it Upward—that is to say, not Northward, but somewhere else... not exactly like this, but somehow..."

I fumbled with the square, shaking it aimlessly in the air, attempting to simulate a dimension my two-dimensional world could not comprehend. The result was a chaotic blur, a purposeless thrashing that made no geometric sense. My grandson threw his head back, roaring with laughter, declaring that I wasn't teaching him but merely mocking him. Seizing the opportunity to escape the trap I had sprung, he unlocked the door and fled the room.

Thus concluded my first, disastrous attempt to convert a pupil to the Gospel of Three Dimensions.
