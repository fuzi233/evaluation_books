As night descended over the vast expanse of the Kukuan plains, a melancholy hush settled over the sprawling village, punctuated only by the mournful wails of women, their keening voices carrying far and wide on the wind. In the great hut of the deceased King Twala, a makeshift triage unfolded, the flickering light of a primitive Kukuan lamp casting eerie shadows on the walls as Sir Henry, Good, and I lay battered and bruised, our exhaustion compounded by the weight of our own mortality.

Foulata, the enigmatic handmaiden who had pledged her allegiance to us after our shared ordeal, moved quietly among us, her gentle ministrations a balm to our frazzled nerves. With the assistance of Good's skilled hands, she helped us shed our blood-stained shirts, revealing the gruesome extent of our injuries. The contusions were a testament to the ferocity of the battle, the steel links of our chain shirts having deflected the blows but left their mark nonetheless.

As we struggled to make sense of our battered bodies, Good's medical expertise proved invaluable, his fingers moving with precision as he stitched the gashes and applied antiseptic ointment to our wounds. Meanwhile, Foulata prepared a nourishing broth, which we devoured gratefully, our weariness finally beginning to take hold. As the night wore on, the cries of the women grew less frequent, their despair slowly giving way to the oppressive silence that hung over the village like a shroud.

In the darkness, I found myself drawn to the hut of the dead king, the sound of Gagool's mournful keening drifting through the stillness like a ghostly whisper. Her sorrow seemed to echo the anguish that gripped my own heart, a sense of futility and loss that threatened to consume me whole. As I drifted in and out of fitful sleep, the memories of the day's events replayed themselves in my mind, the faces of the fallen warriors etched in my memory like the lines on a worn stone.

The hours ticked by with agonizing slowness, each one a reminder of the terrible price we had paid for victory. In the midst of this desolate landscape, I found myself wrapped in Twala's own karross, a symbol of the transience of power and the fleeting nature of human life.
