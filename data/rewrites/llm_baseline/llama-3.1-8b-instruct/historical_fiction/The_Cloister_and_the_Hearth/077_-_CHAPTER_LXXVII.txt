As the warm scent of roasting meat wafted through the air, the little family at Tergou gathered around the dinner table, their faces lit by the soft glow of candles. Luke Peterson burst in, his dusty boots a testament to his hasty arrival. "Good people, I bring a message from Mistress Catherine," he announced, his voice tinged with urgency. "She requests your presence at Rotterdam, immediately."

Kate, whose name Catherine preferred, looked up from her plate, a mixture of surprise and curiosity etched on her face. "What news could possibly warrant such haste?" she asked, her eyes narrowing slightly as she tried to read the young messenger's expression.

Luke hesitated, his gaze darting around the room before settling on Catherine. "Her baby is ill, ma'am. Failing, I'm afraid." The words hung in the air like a challenge, and the room fell silent as the family digested the news.

Catherine's face set in determination as she rose from her seat. "I'll be off at once," she declared, her voice firm but laced with a hint of concern. Eli, her husband, stood up to help her gather her things, his eyes meeting hers in a fleeting moment of understanding.

As they made their way to Rotterdam, the scent of freshly baked bread and the sound of clucking chickens accompanied them, a comforting backdrop to the seriousness of their mission. Catherine peppered Luke with questions, her tone a mix of gentle prodding and maternal instinct, seeking to understand the extent of the child's illness.

Upon arriving at the doorstep, Margaret flung open the door, her face pale and drawn. Catherine swept her into a warm hug, her eyes scanning the scene within. The failing child lay in a crib, its tiny chest rising and falling with each labored breath. Catherine's gaze locked onto Margaret's, her expression a mix of compassion and authority. "Tell me, child, what ails your baby?"

Margaret's eyes welled up with tears as she recounted her worries and fears, her words tumbling out in a rush. Catherine listened attentively, her face a mask of calm, her mind working overtime to diagnose the problem. "He's poisoned, child," she declared finally, her voice low and steady. "By your worry and anxiety."
