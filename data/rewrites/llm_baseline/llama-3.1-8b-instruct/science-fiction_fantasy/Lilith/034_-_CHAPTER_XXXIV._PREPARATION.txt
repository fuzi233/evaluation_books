As the days passed, the woman of Bulika's words lingered in our minds, her tales of the city's vulnerability, the wickedness of its princess, and the cowardice of its inhabitants echoing through our conversations. The children, with their boundless energy, soon found themselves enthralled by the stories, though they barely grasped the concept of a city. It was then that I began to discern the woman's intentions, though the underlying motives remained unclear.

The idea of claiming the city for ourselves grew more appealing, especially to Lona, and even I found myself drawn to the prospect. With the children's faculties developing at an alarming rate, I saw no insurmountable obstacles to our success. I recalled the weakness of the woman Lilith – her Achilles' heel, her daughter's power, and the influence of the ancient prophecy on the citizens. The risks, though present, seemed worthwhile. If successful, the Little Ones would swiftly grow into a young nation, their governance and influence guided by righteousness. Under their rule, the wicked would be ruled with an iron fist, bringing redemption to the nation.

My thoughts, however, were not entirely altruistic. I envisioned a future where Lona sat upon the throne, her mother's rightful place, with me by her side as her consort and advisor. Together, we would dedicate ourselves to her service, and with the Little Ones as our foundation, we would build a noble state. I even dared to dream of establishing a trade in precious gems between our world and theirs, though I knew it was an impossible endeavor that could only bring harm to both.

Reflecting on Adam's words, I suggested to Lona that finding a source of water might accelerate the growth of the Little Ones. However, she wisely chose to delay this decision, unsure of the consequences, and aware that it might lead to a new set of challenges. "They are what they are without it," she said, "when we have the city, we will search for water."

With our plans in motion, we devoted ourselves to preparing the children for the impending conquest. Lona focused on the logistics, ensuring our supplies and resources were adequate, while I trained the young warriors, teaching them to wield stones, bows, and arrows, and instilling in them a sense of discipline and loyalty. The main challenge lay in getting them to respond promptly to the signal to assemble under their banner. Most of them carried slings, while the older boys used bows and arrows.
