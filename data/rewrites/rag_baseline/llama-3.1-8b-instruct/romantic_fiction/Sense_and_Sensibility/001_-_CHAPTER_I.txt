In the rolling hills of Sussex, the Dashwood family had long been woven into the fabric of the local community. Their estate, Norland Park, stood at the heart of their property, a testament to generations of respectable living that had earned the admiration of their neighbors. The late Mr. Dashwood, a man of advanced years, had lived a life of quiet contentment, surrounded by the comforting routines of his household. For many years, his sister had been his constant companion and housekeeper, sharing the weight of his daily life. However, upon her passing ten years prior, Mr. Dashwood invited his nephew, Mr. Henry Dashwood, and his family to join him at Norland, securing their future through his will.

The introduction of the nephew and his family brought a renewed sense of purpose to the old gentleman's life. Mr. and Mrs. Henry Dashwood's dedication to his wishes, driven by both self-interest and genuine kindness, brought solace to his later years. The children's lively presence added a vibrant energy to the household, infusing Mr. Dashwood's existence with a sense of joy.

As the son of Mr. Henry Dashwood's previous marriage, a young man named John had been amply provided for by his mother's substantial fortune, which had doubled upon his coming of age. His marriage had further enriched him, allowing him to settle into a comfortable life. Consequently, the inheritance of the Norland estate held less significance for him compared to his sisters, whose financial prospects were far more uncertain. With their mother having no fortune of her own, and their father possessing only a modest £7,000, the sisters' inheritance depended on what might arise from their father's legacy. Their father's will, though neither cruel nor ungrateful, ultimately left the estate to his nephew, but on terms that diminished its value. The bulk of the bequest was secured for John's young son, effectively tying up the family's resources and limiting Mr. Dashwood's ability to provide for his wife and daughters.

Though initially dismayed, Mr. Dashwood's temperament remained optimistic, and he hoped to live many years, carefully managing the estate to accumulate a sizeable reserve. Alas, his time was short-lived, and within a year, he passed away, leaving behind a mere £10,000, including the recent legacies.
