The air in the temple was thick with the smell of wet stone and impending ash. An old priest, his knees knocking together like dry twigs, stepped forward and placed a hand on my shoulder. The touch was cold, a physical anchor in a world losing its footing.

"Well?" I asked, cutting him off before he could speak. I had no appetite for interruptions, especially not now.

"News has reached the Three, my King," he said softly. "Of what threatens us."

"Then they know," I replied, turning my face toward the horizon where the smoke was rising. "They know I stand here to fight the finest battle of my life. When it ends, I shall go to the Gods. I will stand behind the stars and welcome them when they finally arrive. I regret only that they are too old, too weak, to witness a killing of this magnitude themselves."

"I bear commands from them, my King," the priest continued, his voice dropping to a whisper that somehow carried louder than the wind. "Commands you will find difficult to swallow. You are forbidden to find your death here. Not in the fighting. They require you elsewhere."

I spun on him, anger flaring hot and bright. "I will take no such order, brother. I refuse to believe it exists. You must have misheard. If I am a man, if I am a priest, if I am a soldier, if I am a King, then it is my honor that no enemy passes this gate while I breathe. Go back. Spit that message in their teeth."

The old man smiled, a thin, envious curve of lips. He, too, had once been a sharp blade in the service of the clan. "I knew you would not believe it easily. So I asked for a sign. They granted it. Take this jewel, my King."

He pressed a bracelet into my palm. It was cool, heavy, and intricate.

"How did they get this?" I asked, staring at the metal. "This belonged to Nais."

"They stripped her of it," he said simply. "I did not know its origin until now. The message instructed me that the owner waits inside the Ark of the Mysteries. The Three's use for you... concerns her as well."

Even then, I admit my obedience was tested to the breaking point. Every instinct screamed at me to stay, to fight, to die gloriously. But the memory of Nais, the pull of her presence, outweighed the glory of the kill. I offered the necessary salute, a hollow gesture acknowledging an order I did not wish to keep, and accepted the directive.

Turning to the knot of priests left to hold the line, I made my farewells. "You will suffer what I avoid," I told them. "I envy you that fight. But I am King of Atlantis, and I am one of the Seven. I serve the Three. Their word is the will of the Highest Gods, and we must obey. Stand behind the stars before I join you. Ask them to commend me to those you meet there. It is not my will that keeps me from your side; it is theirs."

They listened with polite smiles, bowing their weapons in salute. We parted ways. I did not watch the slaughter that followed, but I knew it was fierce. By the time Phorenice's hordes breached the mountain crest, the last of the lesser priests had paid the ultimate price. They fought hard, dying for a return that would never come.

With a bitter taste in my mouth, I ascended the pass, moving through the groves and past the crumbling temples on the upper slopes. I approached the boundary, the sacred line that in better times barred all but the elite. Conventions were dead; my temper was hot. I would have stormed forward without hesitation, but I was stopped.

Waiting in the temple marking the border was Old Zaemon. With the solemnity of ancient ritual, he sought dispensation from the High Gods for my intrusion. He refused to move until the ceremony was complete.

Below, the chaos was already visible. Phorenice's towers and bridges loomed over the gate. The screams of combat rang out as the handful of priests on the rampart traded their lives for the currency of the dead. They were skilled fighters, but old and broken. The odds were insurmountable. The storm was coming, and the roof of the Sacred Mountain would soon be exposed to the invaders. Time for pomp was gone; time for action remained.

Zaemon disagreed. He led me upward, chiding my impatience, forcing me to kiss the Ark of the Mysteries with reverence. Only then did he call out. Another figure emerged from the opening atop the Ark—a man I had never seen, hoary, frail, emaciated. Along with Zaemon, he was one of the only two priests raised to the highest degree, the sole keepers of the deepest secrets.

"Look!" Zaemon shouted, his voice cracking like dry wood. He swept a trembling finger across the landscape.

I followed his gaze. A chill crawled up my spine. The sun burned high in a cloudless blue sky, casting a deceptive warmth over the scene. Below, the earth was smoking, spouting fire, and the sea was boiling with a strange, violent disorder.

But it was the water that terrified me most. Far out, the ocean surged against the rocks in massive, crushing rolls. Up the great estuary, leading directly to Atlantis, the tide gushed in successive waves of impossible height that never retreated. The lower lands were already swallowed by tumultuous water; the harbor walls were gone, submerged in darkness. The flood crept inexorably into the heart of the city.

"You have seen?" Zaemon asked.

"I have seen."

"Do you understand?"

"In part."

"Let me explain. This is the beginning. The end follows swiftly. The High Gods, who sit behind the stars, have limits to their patience. That limit has been crossed. Atlantis, the great continent beyond, and everyone in it, are doomed to unutterable destruction. Long ago, it was foreseen that this wiping-out would occur through the sins of men. To prevent total loss, the Ark of the Mysteries was built under divine direction. No mortal tool can scratch its surface; no wave or rock can wreck it."

He paused, letting the silence of the approaching disaster fill the space.

"Inside are stored on sheets of ancient writing all the knowledge of the world—the learning not shared by the common people. There is grain, sweet water sufficient for two people for four years, seeds, weapons, and everything else deemed fit for survival."

"The High Gods decree that two shall not perish," Zaemon continued. "Two shall be chosen, a man and a woman, fit to carry the ancient learning away and eventually rebuild a kingdom in honor of the Sun and the other Gods elsewhere. The woman is already inside, seated in her appointed place. Though she is my daughter, the burden of selection rests with you. As for the man, the choice falls upon you."

Shock numbed me. "I do not know that I care to be a survivor."

"You are not asked for your wishes," the old man said flatly. "You are given an order from the High Gods, who know you to be their faithful servant."

Habit took over. I made the required salutation and declared my obedience.

"It remains to raise you to the sublime degree of the Three," Zaemon announced. "If your learning is insufficient to grasp the keys to the Powers and the highest Mysteries when they are handed to you, that fault cannot be remedied now."

Time was running out. The fight still raged at the gate, a miracle that the few priests had held so long. But the ocean rolled inland, an ever-growing flood. The mountains smoked and belched volleys of rock as the weight increased. Soon, the besiegers would see the fate threatening them. Madness would drive them to sweep all obstacles aside.

Yet Zaemon and his companion showed no haste. They lacked a temple, sacrifices, or incense. They had only the sky for a roof and the Gods for witnesses. Still, they performed the initiation with all the grand, majestic form accumulated over ages, refusing to curtail a single sentence.

Outside, a burning mountain burst with a bellowing roar as incoming waters met its fires. Phorenice's troops broke the last resistance, pouring into the groves and temples in a frenzy. Yet the two old men quivered through the ritual, never faltering.

I admitted I was less rapt. Standing bareheaded in the heat, I struggled to concentrate, torn by a thousand foreign emotions. A continent was meeting its end, and while Zaemon could focus entirely on the parochial duties of the priesthood, I could not. My mind was filled with anxieties regarding Nais. Still, I maintained a decent facade, responding to the required prompts and trying to memorize the sentences that were keys to power, rather than mere phrases of devotion.

It became clear that if the ceremony did not conclude soon, it would be cut short abruptly. Phorenice's legions swarmed onto the mountain crest, now aware of the catastrophe unfolding below. They were out of control, running like distracted beasts. Knowing the priests possessed the power to halt this wreck via the Ark, their natural impulse was to exact final vengeance on the initiates before it was too late.

My attention flickered between the soldiers closing in and the sea deepening its grip on the land. The fires ringing the mountain doubled in height, roaring unceasingly. Despite the distractions, the two old priests continued their work as if in a quiet temple, undisturbed by a single buzzing fly.
