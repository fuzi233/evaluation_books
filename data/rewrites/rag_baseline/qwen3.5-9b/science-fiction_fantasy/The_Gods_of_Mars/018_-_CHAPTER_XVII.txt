The morning sun, pale and indifferent, cast its light across the marble floors of the temple just as the appointed hour approached. A phalanx of Zat Arras's officers, clad in the rigid armor of Zodanga, breached our quarters with the mechanical precision of executioners. They did not speak; they merely gestured, herding us like cattle toward the Great Hall of the Temple. Two by two, we were driven forward, marching down the Broad Aisle of Hope—a path paved with the hopes of a conquered people—to the central platform where judgment awaited. Flanking us were armed guards, their spears leveled, while three solid ranks of Zodangan soldiery formed a living wall along the aisle, from the grim entrance to the elevated rostrum.

As we ascended the steps to the raised enclosure, the faces of our judges came into view. By the ancient customs of Barsoom, there should have been thirty-one noblemen, selected by lot to ensure fairness, for nobles were those on trial. Yet, looking into the circle of judgment, I saw no familiar features, no allies, no friends. Every single face was alien, every eye cold and Zodangan. These were the very men whose defeat at the hands of the Green Hordes had reduced their nation to vassals of Helium, and whose leaders had burned the avenues of Zodanga with fire and blood. For John Carter, for his son, for the great Thark who had led the savages in that righteous fury, there could be no justice here. Only the machinery of vengeance.

About us, the vast circular coliseum vibrated with a tension thick enough to choke. Every stratum of society was present—old and young, male and female—packed to the brim. As we entered, the low hum of conversation died instantly, swallowed by a silence so profound it felt like the holding of breath by ten thousand souls. We halted upon the platform, the Throne of Righteousness, and the air grew heavy with the weight of impending doom. The judges settled into their great circle around the perimeter, while we were assigned seats with our backs to a smaller, inner stage. This positioning forced us to face the judges and the roaring crowd alike. On the smaller platform, each defendant would stand alone during their hearing. Zat Arras himself took his seat in the golden chair of the presiding magistrate, a symbol of imperial authority.

Once we were seated and our guards retreated to the foot of the stairs, Zat Arras rose. He did not look at us with the eyes of a judge, but with the hunger of a predator. "John Carter," he bellowed, his voice echoing off the stone walls, "take your place upon the Pedestal of Truth. Be judged impartially according to your acts, and learn here the reward you have earned." Turning to sweep the audience with his gaze, he began to recite the indictment, weaving a tapestry of lies designed to justify the slaughter.

"Know ye, O judges and people of Helium," he intoned, his voice dripping with feigned righteousness, "that John Carter, once Prince of Helium, has returned from the Valley Dor and even from the Temple of Issus itself. Know ye that in the presence of many witnesses he has blasphemed against the Sacred Iss, against the Valley Dor, and the Lost Sea of Korus, and against the Holy Therns themselves. He has defied Issus, Goddess of Death and Life Eternal. Your own eyes bear witness that he has violated the sanctity of our ancient religion, returning from these holy precincts in defiance of custom."

"He who is once dead may not live again," Zat Arras continued, pacing slowly. "He who attempts resurrection must be made dead forever. Judges, your duty is plain. What reward shall be meted to John Carter for these crimes?"

"Death!" shouted a judge, springing to his feet.

Before the gavel could fall, a man erupted from the audience. Raising his hand high above the sea of heads, he cried out, "Justice! Justice! Justice!" It was Kantos Kan. Ignoring the flashing steel of the Zodangan guards, he leaped past them and landed on the platform beside us.

"What manner of justice is this?" Kantos Kan demanded, his voice cutting through the stunned silence. "The defendant has not been heard! He has had no opportunity to call witnesses in his behalf. In the name of the people of Helium, I demand fair and impartial treatment for the Prince of Helium!"

A roar of approval swept through the coliseum. "Justice! Justice! Justice!" The crowd surged, a tidal wave of outrage that Zat Arras dared not suppress. Snarling, he turned to me. "Speak, then," he growled, "but blaspheme not against the things that are sacred upon Barsoom."

Turning away from the judges, I addressed the spectators, my voice rising above the clamor. "Men of Helium, how can John Carter expect justice from the men of Zodanga? He asks for none. It is to the men of Helium that I speak; it is to you that I offer my defense. I do not plead for my own life, but for yours—for the lives of your wives, your daughters, and those yet unborn. I speak to save them from the atrocities I have witnessed in the Temple of Issus. Save them from the sucking embrace of the Plant Men, from the fangs of the Great White Apes of Dor, from the cruel lust of the Holy Therns, and from the cold, dead embrace of Issus herself, which leads them from homes of love and life into eternal darkness."

"I know the history of John Carter," I declared, scanning the faces of the nobility. "How he came from another world, rose from a prisoner among the Green Men through torture and persecution to become a leader among the highest of Barsoom. Never did John Carter lie in his own behalf, never did he speak lightly of the strange religions he respected without fully understanding them. There is no man here, or anywhere on Barsoom today, who owes his life directly to a single act of mine—an act where I sacrificed myself and the happiness of my Princess so that you might live."

"And so, men of Helium, I claim the right to be heard, to be believed. Let me serve you and save you from the false hereafter of Dor and Issus, just as I saved you from the real death that other day. It is to you I speak. When I am done, let the men of Zodanga have their will with me. Zat Arras has taken my sword; they no longer fear me. Will you listen?"

"Speak, John Carter, Prince of Helium!" cried a great noble from the stands, and the multitude echoed his command until the very foundations of the building trembled under the noise of their unified support. Zat Arras, realizing the futility of resistance against such a tide of sentiment, remained silent. For two hours, I spoke to the people of Helium, dismantling the lies of Zodanga with the truth of our shared struggle.

When I concluded, Zat Arras rose, turning to the judges with a sneer. "My nobles, you have heard John Carter's plea. Every opportunity has been given him to prove his innocence, yet he has used the time only for further blasphemy. Gentlemen, what is your verdict?"

"Death to the blasphemer!" screamed one judge, and instantly, all thirty-one rose, raising their swords in a unanimous display of condemnation. Though the people may not have heard the specific charges, they heard the verdict clearly. A sullen murmur rippled through the packed arena, growing into a roar of dissent.

Kantos Kan, who had remained steadfast beside me, raised his hand for silence. Speaking in a cool, level voice that cut through the chaos, he addressed the assembly. "You have heard the fate Zodanga intends for Helium's noblest hero. It may be the duty of the people to accept this verdict as final. Let each man act according to his own heart. Here is the answer of Kantos Kan, Head of the Navy of Helium, to Zat Arras and his judges."

With deliberate slowness, he unbuckled his scabbard and hurled his sword to the floor at my feet. In an instant, soldiers and citizens, officers and nobles alike, surged past the Zodangan lines, forcing their way to the Throne of Righteousness. A hundred men climbed onto the platform, and a hundred blades clattered and rang against the stone at my feet. Zat Arras and his officers were furious, their faces twisted in rage, but they were utterly helpless. One by one, I picked up the swords, kissed the hilts in a gesture of reverence, and buckled them back onto their owners' waists.

"Come," said Kantos Kan, "we will escort John Carter and his party to his own palace." They formed a protective ring around us and began the descent toward the stairs leading to the Aisle of Hope.

"Stop!" cried Zat Arras, his voice cracking. "Soldiers of Helium, let no prisoner leave the Throne of Righteousness!"

The Zodangan soldiers were the only organized military force remaining within the temple, and Zat Arras assumed his orders would be obeyed. He did not anticipate the explosion of violence that erupted the moment his troops advanced. From every corner of the coliseum, swords flashed, and men rushed forward, threatening the Zodangans. Someone raised a cry: "Tardos Mors is dead—a thousand years to John Carter, Jeddak of Helium!"
