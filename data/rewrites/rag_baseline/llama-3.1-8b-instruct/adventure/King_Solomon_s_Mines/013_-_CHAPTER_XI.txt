It was only when the first hints of morning began to seep into the night sky that the stillness was broken by the soft crunch of footsteps outside the hut.

A sentry, stationed at the kraal gate, called out a challenge, his voice low and cautious. The reply, though inaudible, seemed to carry a weight of its own, for the footsteps continued, growing louder with each passing moment. And then, in a flash of movement, Infadoos slipped into the hut, followed by a procession of imposing figures – six stately chiefs, their faces set with a mix of curiosity and wariness.

"My lords," Infadoos began, his voice measured and deliberate, "I have come as promised. I have brought with me these men, each of whom commands three thousand soldiers, sworn to do their bidding under the king's rule. I have shared with them the tale of what I saw, of the horrors that befell us last night. Now, let them see the sacred snake, and hear Ignosi's story, that they may determine whether they will stand with him against Twala, the usurper king."

Each of the chiefs drew closer, their eyes scanning the intricate design in the dim light of the lamp, their faces inscrutable.
