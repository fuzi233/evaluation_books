Thirty years had elapsed since the fateful evening Felix spoke of, yet the ache within him remained, a wound that refused to heal. Felix echoed the pain, his voice a low, measured cadence, as if summoning the ghosts of the past to bear witness.

"We sought John Poindexter that night, our eyes locked in a silent understanding, our hearts heavy with grief," Felix began. "It was dusk, and the darkness seemed to swallow us whole. We found him, calm and unyielding, his face a mask of indifference. His words cut deep, a cold blade that sliced through our resolve. 'Spare yourselves, spare me the useless recriminations. The girl is gone; she will not return. Live your lives, enjoy your fleeting moments – it is but a brief reprieve.'"

As Felix spoke, the shadows seemed to deepen, as if the very darkness itself was absorbing the pain. Amos's words dripped with venom, a slow-burning fire that had smoldered for decades. "We vowed, Felix and I, to make that heart feel the weight of our sorrow. To make him understand the anguish he had inflicted upon us. That was thirty years ago, and our oath remains unfulfilled."

Your mother's passing left me a widower once more, and I knew that the time had come to dedicate you to this purpose."
