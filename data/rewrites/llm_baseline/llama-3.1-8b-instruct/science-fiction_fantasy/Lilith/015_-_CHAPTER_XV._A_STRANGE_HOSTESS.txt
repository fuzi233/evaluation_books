As I lay beneath the watchful gaze of the full moon, I felt an inexplicable sense of expectation. Her radiant light illuminated the landscape, casting long shadows across the ravines that crisscrossed the terrain. When her lower edge dipped below the horizon, a fleeting image materialized within her disk – a humble cottage, its door and windows aglow with a warm, golden light. The conviction took hold that I was being summoned to this place, and the moon's sudden disappearance left me with an unsettling sense of anticipation.

The darkness closed in, and I settled into a sandy hollow, making a simple supper from the fruit the children had given me earlier. As I ate, the stars above began to twinkle, their unfamiliar patterns etched against the black sky. My eyelids grew heavy, and I drifted into a deep sleep.

I awoke to find a figure seated nearby, silhouetted against the starry expanse. Its presence startled me, and I sat up, taking in the outline of the figure. From my vantage point in the hollow, it seemed larger than life. The figure slowly turned its head, and I caught a glimpse of its profile.

"Will you join me?" a melodious voice asked, unmistakably feminine.

Curiosity got the better of me, and I replied, "Thank you, but I'm quite comfortable here. Where would you have me go?"

"There's no harm in the night air," she countered, "but the creatures that roam these lands aren't exactly the kind you'd want to keep company with while you sleep."

"I haven't been bothered," I said, trying to reassure her.

"No, I've been sitting with you since you fell asleep," she replied, her back still turned towards me.

Her kindness surprised me, and I asked, "How did you know I was here? Why show me such hospitality?"

"I saw you, just as the moon set," she explained, her voice gentle and apologetic. "I see better in the dark, you see. The shadow of my house would have hidden you, but both doors were open. I was out on the moor, and I spotted you making your way into this hollow. You were already asleep by the time I reached you, and I didn't want to startle you. People get frightened when I appear suddenly. They call me the Cat-woman. It's not my true name, though."
