As the news of Edward Ferrars' marriage to Lucy Steele settled, Elinor Dashwood felt the weight of her expectations crashing down upon her like a leaden fog. She realized, with a clarity born of disappointment, that she had always harbored a secret hope – a hope that somehow, someway, Edward would remain unattached, that some unforeseen circumstance would intervene to prevent the union. But fate, it seemed, had other plans. He was now a married man, and the thought of Lucy, so calculating and practical, securing him with such haste, filled her with a mix of sorrow and resentment.

The news of the marriage, particularly the speed with which it had taken place in town, before Edward could assume his clerical duties and gain possession of the living at Delaford, came as a surprise to Elinor. Yet, upon reflection, it seemed all too plausible. Lucy, ever the pragmatist, would stop at nothing to secure her future, including overlooking the risks of delay. They would soon settle at Delaford, that estate which held a peculiar allure for Elinor, both fascinating and repellent. She envisioned them in their parsonage, Lucy busy with her schemes to present a respectable facade while secretly pinching pennies, and Edward... Elinor's thoughts on him were a jumble of uncertainty.

As the days passed without word from their acquaintances in London, Elinor grew increasingly frustrated, casting blame upon the absent friends who seemed to be thoughtless or idle. Her impatience boiled over when she asked her mother, "When do you write to Colonel Brandon, ma'am?" hoping against hope that he might bring news of the wedding and offer some insight into the circumstances surrounding it.

Her mother replied, "I wrote to him last week, my love, and rather expect to see him here today or tomorrow, or any day." This small spark of optimism gave Elinor something to cling to. Colonel Brandon must have some information to share. And then, just as she had resolved to be calm, a horseman appeared at their gate. Elinor's heart skipped a beat as she gazed out the window, her mind racing with possibilities. The rider dismounted, and she could hardly believe her eyes – it was Edward Ferrars himself.
