As I descended into the heart of Omean, the air thickened with anticipation, heavy with the scent of saltwater and the faint tang of ozone. The black pirate's tale had been a ruse, a clever distraction to mask the impending arrival of the battleship, its crew poised to overwhelm us like a tidal wave. But I, a seasoned hand in aerial warfare, knew better than to underestimate the cunning of my captors. I swiftly reversed the engines, plummeting the little vessel a hundred feet into the depths, narrowly avoiding the snapping jaws of the boarding party.

Above me, the dangling forms of the sailors seemed to freeze in mid-air as the battleship thundered overhead, its propellers a blur of steel and fury. I seized the opportunity, yanking the speed lever to its limit, and our vessel shot forth like a arrow, hurtling straight for the whirring blades. Time seemed to slow as we closed in, the propellers a maddening whirlwind of steel and noise. With a jarring crash, we struck, the impact sending shockwaves through the air.

In the chaos that followed, I reversed the engine, but our prow was lodged deep within the battleship's stern. For a fleeting instant, we hung suspended, a moment's respite before the black devils swarmed our deck. But I was not one to be caught off guard. With a deft flick of my wrist, I snapped the bonds that bound me, the fragile strands snapping like twigs.

The girl, too, was freed, and we were bound together, our wrists secured by the black pirates' hasty work. As we were dragged aboard the battleship, the deck erupted into a frenzy of activity. A thousand black men, their faces a sea of ebony, thronged the deck, their eyes fixed on their captives. The girl's beauty drew catcalls and lewd remarks, a stark contrast to the refined chivalry of the red men of Barsoom.
