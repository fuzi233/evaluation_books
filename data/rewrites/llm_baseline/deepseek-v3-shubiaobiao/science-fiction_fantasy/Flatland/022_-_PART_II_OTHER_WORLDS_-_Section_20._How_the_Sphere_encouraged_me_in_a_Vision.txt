The moment I returned, I knew I could not share the truth with my wife. Not out of fear, but because the reality of my journey would be incomprehensible to any inhabitant of Flatland. So I spun a simpler tale—an accidental fall through the cellar trapdoor, a brief unconsciousness. Even this mundane lie strained credulity, for gravity is weak here, and such tumbles are rare. Yet my wife, sharper than most of her sex, did not press the matter. She saw my agitation and insisted I rest. Grateful for the reprieve, I withdrew to my chamber, my mind ablaze with what I had witnessed.

Before sleep took me, I tried to reconstruct the mysteries of the Third Dimension—how a Square, moving in an impossible direction, could become a Cube. The words of the Sphere echoed in my mind: *"Upward, yet not Northward."* A mantra, a key. I clung to it as drowsiness pulled me under.

Then, the vision.

I stood once more beside the Sphere, his radiant form now calm, his earlier wrath softened into patience. Together, we drifted toward a speck of light, a point so small it seemed to defy existence itself. As we neared, a faint hum arose, a sound so delicate it barely registered until we halted—mere inches away in Spaceland terms.

*"Look,"* said the Sphere. *"You have known Flatland. You have glimpsed Lineland. You have soared through Spaceland. Now, I show you the lowest depth—Pointland, where all dimensions collapse into nothing."*

Before us hovered the Point, a solitary being imprisoned in its own infinitesimal universe. It knew nothing beyond itself—no length, no breadth, no other existence. It was all and nothing, content in its isolation.

*"Observe,"* the Sphere murmured. *"Its perfect self-satisfaction. A lesson: to be blindly content is to remain ignorant. To aspire is greater."*

A tinny voice emanated from the Point, chanting in hollow triumph:

*"It speaks of itself,"* the Sphere explained, *"as a child does, unable to conceive of anything beyond its own mind."*

The Point continued, lost in its solipsistic bliss:
*"It fills all Space, for It is all Space. What It thinks, It speaks; what It speaks, It hears. It is Thinker, Word, and Listener—One and All!"*

Disgusted, I shouted at the creature, desperate to shatter its delusion:

The Sphere silenced me. *"Watch."*

The Point, unshaken, glowed brighter, basking in what it perceived as its own divine wisdom.

*"You see?"* said the Sphere. *"Your words mean nothing to it. It twists all into self-praise. There is no reaching such a mind."*

As we withdrew, the Sphere spoke of higher mysteries—of Extra-Solids, of dimensions beyond even his own. He admitted his initial frustration with my hunger for the unknown, but now, he saw the value in aspiration.

*"Teach others,"* he urged. *"Let them see beyond the limits of their world."*

And so we returned, the hum of Pointland fading behind us, its lonely god still chanting in the void.
