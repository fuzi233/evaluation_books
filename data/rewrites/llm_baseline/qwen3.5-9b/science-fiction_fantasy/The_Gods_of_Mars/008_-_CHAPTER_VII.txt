For a heartbeat, the black pirate and I stood frozen, our gazes locked in a silent duel. Then, a grim smile twisted the handsome lips hovering above me. An ebony hand emerged slowly from the darkness above the deck's edge, and the cold, hollow eye of a revolver pressed against the center of my forehead.

Simultaneously, my free hand shot out to seize the black throat, closing just inches from its mark, while the ebony finger tightened on the trigger. The pirate's hiss—"Die, cursed thern!"—was cut short, half-choked in his windpipe by my iron grip. The hammer fell with a useless *click* against an empty chamber. Before he could squeeze off another shot, I yanked him violently over the railing. Forced to abandon his weapon, he clutched the rail with both hands, desperate to steady himself. My vice-like hold on his neck stifled any scream, and we engaged in a grim, breathless struggle: he striving to wrench free, I determined to drag him to his doom.

His face flushed a terrifying purple; his eyes bulged from their sockets. It was obvious to him that death was imminent unless he could break my steel fingers. With a final, desperate lunge, he threw himself backward onto the deck, simultaneously releasing the rail to claw frantically at my hands, trying to pry them from his throat. That split second was all I needed. With a mighty downward surge, I swept him clear of the platform.

His falling body nearly tore me from the fragile grip I maintained on the anchor chain, threatening to plunge us both into the abyssal waters below. Yet, I refused to let go. I knew that a single shriek from those lips as he plummeted into the silent, black depths would summon his comrades from above to avenge him. So, I held on grimly, choking him relentlessly, while his frantic thrashing dragged me lower and lower toward the end of the chain. Gradually, his convulsions turned spasmodic, then ceased entirely.

Only then did I release my hold. In an instant, he was swallowed by the shadowy waves far below.

Climbing once more to the ship's rail, I finally managed to raise my eyes to the deck level, taking stock of the dire situation. The nearer moon had dipped below the horizon, but the brilliant light of the distant satellite bathed the cruiser's deck, illuminating the scattered bodies of six or eight black men sprawled in sleep. Huddled tightly beside the base of a rapid-fire cannon was a young white girl, securely bound. Her eyes were wide with horrified anticipation, fixed directly upon me as I ascended.

An unutterable wave of relief washed over her face, as if she had found a mystical jewel sparkling in the center of my stolen headpiece. She did not speak; instead, her eyes silently warned me to beware the sleeping figures surrounding her. Moving noiselessly, I gained the deck. The girl nodded, signaling me to approach. As I bent low, she whispered urgently, "Release me. I can aid you, and you will need every advantage possible when they wake."

"Some of them will awaken in Korus," I replied, a smile playing on my lips.

She grasped the implication, and the cruelty in her answering smile struck me with horror. One is not startled by malice in a hideous visage, but when it graces the features of a goddess—whose finely chiseled lines seem carved for love and beauty—the contrast is appallingly stark. Quickly, I freed her bonds.

"Give me a revolver," she whispered. "I can use it against those your sword cannot silence in time."

I complied. Turning then to the grim task ahead, I cast aside any lingering chivalry; these cruel demons would neither appreciate nor reciprocate such virtues. Stealthily, I approached the nearest sleeper. When he woke, he was already halfway to the bosom of Korus. His piercing shriek as consciousness returned drifted faintly up from the black depths below.

The second man awoke the moment I touched him. Though I hurled him from the cruiser's deck, his wild cry of alarm roused the remaining pirates. Five of them surged to their feet. Instantly, the girl's revolver crackled in sharp staccato bursts, and one man sank back to the deck, never to rise again. The others charged madly at me with drawn swords.

Evidently fearing to wound me, the girl dared not fire again. Instead, she moved with feline stealth toward the flank of the attackers. Then, they were upon me. For several minutes, I endured some of the fiercest combat I had ever known. The cramped quarters allowed no room for maneuvering; it was a brutal game of stand-and-fight, giving and taking. Initially, I absorbed more blows than I delivered, but soon I slipped beneath one opponent's guard, watching him collapse onto the deck.

The others redoubled their fury. The clash of their blades against mine created a deafening din that could have echoed for miles through the silent night. Sparks flew as steel met steel, followed by the dull, sickening thud of a shoulder bone shattering under the keen edge of my Martian sword. Three now faced me, but the girl was weaving her way to a position where she could eliminate at least one more.

Suddenly, events unfolded with breathtaking speed. The three rushed me, intent on forcing me back the few steps required to send my body tumbling over the rail into the void. At that exact instant, the girl fired, and my sword arm executed two precise motions. One man dropped, a bullet lodged deep in his brain; a sword clattered across the deck and fell over the edge beyond as I disarmed another opponent; and the third crumpled, my blade buried to the hilt in his breast, three feet of steel protruding from his back. As he fell, he wrenched the sword from my grasp.

Disarmed, I now faced my sole remaining foe, whose own weapon lay lost thousands of feet below us in the Lost Sea. The new conditions seemed to delight my adversary. A smile of satisfaction bared his gleaming teeth as he charged at me bare-handed. The great muscles rolling beneath his glossy black hide convinced him that here was easy prey, not worth the trouble of drawing his dagger.

I let him advance almost to my chest. Then, I ducked beneath his outstretched arms, sidestepping sharply to the right. Pivoting on my left toe, I swung a devastating right hook to his jaw. Like a felled ox, he dropped in his tracks.

A low, silvery laugh rang out behind me.

"You are no thern," said the sweet voice of my companion, "neither for all your golden locks nor the harness of Sator Throg. Never before in all Barsoom has one fought as you have tonight. Who are you?"

"I am John Carter, Prince of the House of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium," I replied. "And whom has the honor of serving been accorded to me?"

She hesitated a moment before answering. "You are no thern. Are you an enemy of the therns?"

"I have been in the territory of the therns for a day and a half. Throughout that time, my life has been in constant peril. Men armed with weapons and fierce beasts have been set upon me. I had no prior quarrel with the therns, but can you wonder that I feel no great love for them now? I have spoken."

She studied me intently for several minutes, her gaze seeming to probe my very soul, judging my character and my code of chivalry. Finally, the assessment appeared to satisfy her.

"I am Phaidor, daughter of Matai Shang, Holy Hekkador of the Holy Therns, Father of Therns, Master of Life and Death upon Barsoom, Brother of Issus, Prince of Life Eternal."

At that moment, I noticed the black man I had knocked down earlier beginning to regain consciousness. Springing to his side, I stripped his harness and securely bound his hands behind his back, then fastened his feet to a heavy gun carriage.

"Why not the simpler way?" Phaidor asked. "I do not understand. What 'simpler way'?" I replied.

With a slight shrug of her lovely shoulders, she gestured with her hands, mimicking the act of casting something over the craft's side.

"I am no murderer," I said firmly. "I kill only in self-defense."

She looked at me narrowly, then puckered her divine brows and shook her head. She could not comprehend. Indeed, neither had my own Dejah Thoris understood what seemed to her a foolish and dangerous policy toward enemies. On Barsoom, mercy is neither asked nor given; every dead man represents a share of the waning resources of this dying planet denied to the survivors. Yet, there seemed a subtle difference between how this girl viewed the dispatch of an enemy and the tender-hearted regret my own princess felt for the stern necessities of war. I suspect Phaidor regretted the thrill the spectacle might have afforded her more than the fact that my decision left another threat alive.

The captive had now fully regained his faculties and regarded us intently from where he lay bound. He was a handsome fellow, clean-limbed and powerful, with an intelligent face and features of such exquisite chiseling that Adonis himself might have envied him.
