The whispers of the evening settled like a soft blanket over the back drawing-room in Harley Street, where Edith lay fast asleep on the crimson damask sofa. Her white muslin dress and blue ribbons seemed to shimmer in the fading light, as if the moon itself had descended to claim her as its own. Margaret gazed upon her cousin's tranquil form, struck once more by the ethereal beauty that had captivated everyone, save herself, since childhood. Though they had grown up together, side by side, Edith's loveliness had only recently come into focus for Margaret – now, with the approaching departure looming, every detail of her cousin's face and figure seemed imbued with a newfound significance.

Their conversation, meandering like a summer stream, had touched upon the intricacies of wedding dresses, the trials of maintaining a piano in tune, and the endless parade of gowns required for visits to Scotland. Captain Lennox's tales of Corfu, where his regiment was stationed, had also woven themselves into the fabric of their discussion, leaving Edith to ponder the mysteries of her future life. As the hours slipped away, Margaret's words had grown softer, her tone a gentle whisper that blended with the hum of the evening's laughter and conversation from the next room.

In the drawing-room, a small gathering of friends had assembled to bid farewell to Edith, their presence a testament to the warmth and familiarity of Mrs. Shaw's hospitality. The ladies, neighbours and friends who had dined together more often than any others, chatted and laughed, their husbands lingering longer than usual in the dining-room, enchanted by Mr. Grey's wit and the promise of a musical performance from Edith. Though the young woman had initially hesitated at the idea of this farewell dinner, her mother's persuasion had ultimately won out, and she now leaned back in her chair, her eyes distant, as she played listlessly with the remnants of her meal.

Margaret's thoughts, however, wandered to a different future – one filled with the quiet rhythms of country life in the parsonage, where her parents resided. She envisioned herself taking her place as the cherished daughter, surrounded by the rolling hills and verdant landscapes of Helstone. Though the separation from her aunt and cousin would be difficult, Margaret's heart swelled with anticipation for the new chapter unfolding before her.
