Two hours after departing my palace in Helium, or roughly at midnight, Kantos Kan, Xodar, and I reached Hastor. Meanwhile, Carthoris, Tars Tarkas, and Hor Vastus had already departed on another cruiser, heading straight for Thark.

The transport fleet was scheduled to launch immediately, proceeding slowly southward. The battleship squadron would catch up to them on the morning of the second day.

Upon arriving at Hastor, everything was in perfect readiness. Kantos Kan's planning had been flawless; within ten minutes of our arrival, the first ship lifted off its dock. Following at a rate of one per second, the massive vessels drifted gracefully into the night sky, forming a long, slender line stretching for miles toward the south.

It wasn't until we were inside Kantos Kan's cabin that I remembered to ask for the current date. Up until that moment, I hadn't been certain how long I had been trapped in the dark pits of Zat Arras. When Kantos Kan revealed the truth, a pang of dread struck me: I had miscalculated the duration of my imprisonment. Three hundred and sixty-five Earth days had passed. It was too late to save Dejah Thoris.

The mission had shifted from rescue to revenge. I didn't bother reminding Kantos Kan of the grim reality that by the time we could possibly reach the Temple of Issus, the Princess of Helium would likely be dead. As far as I knew, she might already be lifeless, for I lacked the precise date of her initial viewing by Issus.

Was it worth burdening my companions with my personal sorrow? They had endured enough suffering alongside me in the past. From now on, I would keep my grief silent. I said nothing to anyone else about our lateness. However, the expedition could still achieve something vital: exposing the Red Men to the cruel deception practiced upon them for eons, thereby saving thousands annually from the horrific fate awaiting pilgrims at the end of their journey.

Opening the fair Valley Dor to the Red Men would be a monumental achievement. Between the Mountains of Otz and the ice barrier lay vast tracts of land requiring no irrigation to yield rich harvests. Here, at the bottom of a dying world, existed the only naturally productive region on Barsoom's surface. Only here were dew and rain, only here an open sea, only here abundant water. Yet, this fertile paradise was merely a staging ground for savage beasts, guarded by the wicked remnants of two once-mighty races who barred access to the rest of Barsoom's billions. If I could succeed in breaking the barrier of religious superstition holding the Red Races back from this El Dorado, it would serve as a fitting memorial to my Princess's immortal virtues. Her martyrdom would not have been in vain.

On the morning of the second day, at first light, we raised the great fleet of transports and their escorts. Soon, we were close enough to exchange signals. Note that radio-aerograms are rarely used in wartime or for secret dispatches. Once a nation invents a new cipher or wireless device, neighbors work tirelessly to intercept and decode it. Over centuries, every possibility of wireless communication has been exhausted; no nation dares transmit important messages this way anymore.

Tars Tarkas reported all was well with the transports. The battleships moved ahead to secure an advanced position, and the combined fleets glided slowly over the ice cap, staying low to avoid detection by the Therns we were approaching.

Far ahead, a thin line of one-man air scouts protected us from surprise, flanking us on both sides, while a smaller group trailed twenty miles behind the transports. Several hours into our progress toward the entrance of Omean, one scout returned from the front, reporting that the cone-shaped summit of the entrance was visible. Simultaneously, another scout raced toward the flagship from the left flank.

His incredible speed signaled urgent news. Kantos Kan and I waited on the small forward deck, equivalent to the bridge of terrestrial battleships. Hardly had his tiny flyer touched down on the broad landing platform before he bounded up the stairs to where we stood.

"A great fleet of battleships, South-South-East, my Prince!" he shouted. "There must be thousands, bearing down directly upon us!"

"The Thern spies weren't in the Palace of John Carter for nothing," Kantos Kan remarked to me. "Prince, your orders?"

"Send ten battleships to guard the entrance to Omean, ordering them to deny entry or exit to any hostile force. That will trap the great fleet of the First Born."

"Form the remainder of the battleships into a great 'V', apex pointing South-South-East. Order the transports, shielded by their convoys, to follow closely behind the battleships. Once the tip of the 'V' penetrates the enemy line, the formation must flare outward at the apex. Battleships on each leg will engage the enemy fiercely, driving them back to create a lane. Through this gap, the transports and their convoys must race at top speed to gain positions above the Thern temples and gardens."

"Let them land there and teach the Holy Therns a lesson in ferocious warfare they won't forget for countless ages."

"It was never my intent to be distracted from the campaign's main objective, but we must settle this conflict with the Therns once and for all. Without doing so, there can be no peace while our fleet remains near Dor, and our chances of ever returning to the outer world diminish drastically."

Kantos Kan saluted and turned to relay my commands to his aides. Incredibly fast, the battleship formation shifted according to my orders. The ten ships destined for Omean sped toward their destination, while the troopships and convoys tightened ranks, preparing for the final surge.

Full speed ahead was ordered. The fleet surged through the air like coursing greyhounds. Within moments, the enemy ships came fully into view. They formed a ragged line extending as far as the eye could see in both directions, about three ships deep. Our attack was so sudden they had no time to prepare. It was like lightning striking a clear sky.

Every phase of my plan executed perfectly. Our massive ships cut completely through the line of Thern battlecraft. Then the 'V' opened wide, revealing a broad lane through which the transports leaped toward the Thern temples, now clearly visible glistening in the sunlight. By the time the Therns recovered from the assault, one hundred thousand Green Warriors were already flooding their courts and gardens, while another one hundred and fifty thousand hung from low-swinging transports, directing their uncanny marksmanship against the Thern soldiers manning the ramparts or defending the temples.

Now, the two great fleets clashed in a titanic struggle far above the hellish din of battle raging in the gorgeous Thern gardens. Slowly, the two lines of Helium's battleships joined ends, commencing the circling maneuver within the enemy line—a hallmark of Barsoomian naval warfare.

Around and around, in each other's tracks, moved the ships under Kantos Kan until they formed nearly a perfect circle. Moving at high speed, they became difficult targets. Broadside after broadside rained down as each vessel aligned with the Thern ships. The enemy tried to rush in and break the formation, but it was futile, like trying to stop a buzzsaw with a bare hand.

From my position on the deck beside Kantos Kan, I watched ship after enemy ship take the awful, sickening dive signaling total destruction. We slowly maneuvered our circle of death until hovering above the gardens where our Green allies fought. Orders were passed for them to re-embark. They rose slowly to a position within the center of the circle.

Meanwhile, Thern fire had practically ceased. They had had enough and were eager to let us pass peacefully. But our escape wouldn't be easy. Scarcely had we resumed movement toward the Omean entrance when we spotted a great black line topping the horizon far to the north. It could only be a fleet of warships.

Who they belonged to or where they were bound, we couldn't guess. When they drew close enough to identify, Kantos Kan's operator received a radio-aerogram, handing it immediately to my companion, who then passed it to me.

*"To Kantos Kan:"* it read. *"Surrender, in the name of the Jeddak of Helium, for you cannot escape."* Signed, *"Zat Arras."*

The Therns must have intercepted and translated the message almost instantly, for they immediately renewed hostilities upon realizing we faced other enemies.

Before Zat Arras could approach close enough to fire, we were locked in hot engagement with the Thern fleet. As soon as he drew near, he unleashed a terrifying barrage of heavy shot. Ship after ship listed and staggered into uselessness under the relentless fire we endured.

The situation couldn't last much longer. I ordered the transports to descend again into the Thern gardens.

*"Wreak your vengeance to the utmost,"* I messaged the Green allies, *"for by night there will be none left to avenge your wrongs."*

Soon, I saw the ten battleships ordered to hold the Omean shaft returning at full speed, firing their stern batteries almost continuously. There was only one explanation: they were being pursued by another hostile fleet. Well, the situation couldn't get worse. The expedition was already doomed. No one embarking on it would cross that desolate ice cap alive. How I wished I could face Zat Arras with my longsword for just an instant before dying! He was the cause of our failure.
