From the realm of dreams, I turn now to the stark reality of fact. It was the final day of the 1999th year of our era. The rhythmic pattering of rain against the walls had long since heralded the arrival of night. Seated beside my wife, we contemplated the weight of the past year and the uncertain horizons of the millennium approaching—the next century, the next great epoch.

[Note: When I use the term "seated," I refer strictly to a state of mental repose, distinct from the physical postures known to your people in Spaceland. For we, lacking feet, cannot "sit" or "stand" in your literal sense; we exist merely as lines floating in space. Yet, we intuitively distinguish between the volitions of lying down, resting, and standing upright. These internal shifts manifest externally as subtle variations in our surface lustre, brighter where willpower intensifies. However, to elaborate on such metaphysical nuances would consume time better spent elsewhere.]

My four sons and two orphan grandchildren had withdrawn to their respective chambers. My wife remained with me, keeping vigil over the transition from the old millennium to the new. Lost in contemplation, my mind drifted back to a conversation earlier that evening with my youngest grandson—a brilliant hexagon of exceptional promise and flawless angularity. We had been engaged in our customary lesson of Sight Recognition, rotating upon our centers at varying speeds while he identified our orientations. Satisfied with his keen perception, I rewarded him with a primer on arithmetic applied to geometry.

Taking nine squares, each measuring one inch per side, I arranged them to form a larger square with a side length of three inches. Thus, I demonstrated to my grandson that, though we cannot perceive the interior volume of a shape, we can calculate its area by squaring the side length: *"Thus,"* I explained, *"3 squared, or 9, represents the total square inches within a figure whose side measures three inches."*

The young hexagon pondered this briefly before posing a question that unsettled me. *"Yet, you teach me to raise numbers to the third power,"* he observed. *"If 3 squared yields a square, surely 3 cubed must hold significance in geometry. What does it denote?"*

*"None whatsoever,"* I replied sharply. *"Not in geometry, at least. Our world possesses only two dimensions."*

Undeterred, I proceeded to illustrate how a point traversing a distance of three inches creates a line of three units (represented by 3), and how a line of three inches moving parallel to itself generates a square of three inches on every side (represented by 3²). Immediately, my grandson seized upon the pattern. *"Then,"* he interrupted, his voice rising with sudden excitement, *"if a point moving three inches makes a line (3), and a line moving parallel makes a square (3²)... then a square moving somehow parallel to itself must create something else entirely—something of three inches in every dimension—and this must be represented by 3³!"*

*"Retire to your bed,"* I snapped, irritated by the intrusion. *"Speak less nonsense if you wish to retain any sense."*

With those words, the boy vanished in shame. Alone once more, I attempted to reflect on the closing year 1999 and the dawn of 2000, yet the echo of my grandson's query lingered stubbornly in my mind. Only a handful of grains remained in the hourglass. As I righted the glass northward for the final time of the old millennium, I muttered aloud, *"That boy is a fool."*

Instantly, a Presence filled the room. A chilling breath swept through my very essence.

*"He is no fool,"* my wife cried out, her voice trembling. *"And you blaspheme the Commandments by dishonoring your own grandchild in such fashion."*

I paid her rebuke no heed. Scanning the darkness in every direction, I saw nothing. Yet, I *felt* the Presence, and another icy whisper brushed against my spirit. I leaped to my feet.

*"What troubles you?"* my wife asked. *"There is no draft. What do you seek? There is nothing here."*

Indeed, there was nothing visible. I returned to my seat, repeating my accusation: *"The boy is a fool; 3³ holds no meaning in geometry."*

At once, a clear, resonant voice answered from the void: *"The boy is not a fool; and 3³ possesses an undeniable geometrical meaning."*

Both my wife and I heard the words, though she failed to grasp their import. Startled, we both lunged toward the source of the sound. Horror gripped us when we finally discerned a Figure before us.

At first glance, it resembled a woman viewed from the side. However, fleeting observation revealed that its extremities faded into dimness far too rapidly to belong to the female sex. I initially suspected a circle, save that the figure shifted in size with a fluidity impossible for any regular plane figure I knew. My wife, lacking my experience and composure, succumbed immediately to the frantic, jealous instincts of her gender. She concluded without hesitation that a woman had slipped indoors through some unseen aperture.

*"How did this person enter?"* she demanded. *"You promised, my dear, that our new home would have no vents!"*

*"There are no vents,"* I assured her. *"But why assume the stranger is female? By my power of Sight Recognition—"*

*"I have no patience with your Sight Recognition!"* she retorted. *"'Feeling is believing,' and 'A straight line to the touch is worth a circle to the sight'"*—proverbs beloved by the Frailer Sex in Flatland.

Reluctantly, fearing further agitation, I yielded. *"Very well,"* I said. *"Demand an introduction."*

Adopting her most gracious demeanor, my wife stepped forward. *"Permit me, Madam, to feel and be felt by..."* She recoiled abruptly. *"Oh! It is not a woman. There are no angles, not a single trace of one. Could I have offended a perfect Circle?"*

*"In a certain sense, I am a Circle,"* the voice replied calmly. *"And a more perfect Circle than any found in Flatland. To be precise, however, I am many circles united as one."*

Softening his tone, the visitor continued, *"I bear a message for your husband that I cannot deliver in your presence. If you would allow us to withdraw for a brief moment—"*

My wife refused to entertain the notion that our august guest would inconvenience himself. Asserting that her own retirement hour had long passed, and offering repeated apologies for her earlier indiscretion, she finally withdrew to her chamber.

I glanced at the hourglass. The last grains had fallen. The third Millennium had begun.
