As Baldassarre stepped behind the velvet curtain and pushed his way into the grand Duomo, the rope binding his wrists and the noose around his neck made him feel like a condemned man. But what struck him most was the sea of warm, living faces stretching out before him. The church was filled to capacity, a kaleidoscope of social classes and ages – magistrates and noblewomen, artisans and peasants, all united in their rapt attention. The Dominican friar, his features etched with conviction, stood tall in the pulpit, the crucifix clutched in his hand as he proclaimed the Word.

At first, Baldassarre's gaze drifted over the crowd without truly seeing them. His mind was still reeling from the events that had led him to this moment. Some of the onlookers had noticed his arrival, their eyes flicking towards him with a mixture of curiosity and wariness. But as they took in the sight of the elderly man with the rope around his neck, their interest waned, and they returned to the sermon. Romola, seated near the back, had entered the church late and found herself positioned to observe the main entrance. Her eyes locked onto Baldassarre, drawn to the wisps of grey hair and the deep lines etched into his face. The weight of his suffering resonated with her, stirring the empathetic nature that had been cultivated through her years of observing the world around her. Though she thought she caught his eye, Baldassarre was lost in his own turmoil, his thoughts consumed by the memories of Tito and the fire that burned within him.

The friar's words began to pierce through the haze, echoing off the stone walls and striking a chord deep within Baldassarre. "The day of vengeance is at hand!" The phrase resonated within him like a battle cry, stirring a primal response. As he looked up, he caught a glimpse of the friar's outstretched arm, the crucifix held aloft like a banner. The sound of the preacher's voice vibrated through him, a promise of deliverance that sparked a glimmer of hope in the darkness. The multitude held its collective breath, suspended in anticipation, as the friar paused, his eyes scanning the crowd as if seeking a connection with each and every soul present.
