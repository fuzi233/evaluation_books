The morning’s *London Advertiser*, laid beside his breakfast plate, brought word of a spectacle too tempting for any lover of sport to resist: a duel of skill between the famed pugilists Sutton and Figg, set to take place at the Wooden House in Marylebone Fields. With the eagerness of youth and a pair of newly acquired horses harnessed to his phaeton, Mr. Warrington set off at once—though his navigation of London’s labyrinthine lanes proved less than expert. After several wrong turns past the Tottenham Road Tabernacle and the solitary grandeur of Middlesex Hospital, he arrived at last to find a raucous assembly already gathered.

The scene was a riot of color and clamor. Beggars jostled for alms, piemen hawked their wares, and a squadron of shoeblacks scrambled for the honor of polishing his boots. Above it all, a banner fluttered from the arena’s eaves, while a trumpet and drum heralded the imminent clash of champions. Inside, the air thrummed with anticipation. The gallery afforded Harry an unobstructed view of the platform where the combatants would meet, while below, his servant Gumbo vanished into the sea of footmen and coachmen, their powdered wigs and liveries a testament to the era’s excess.

The preliminaries were underwhelming—a lackluster boxing match, a cudgel bout that left the crowd restless—until at last the cry went up: *"The masters!"*

Sutton appeared first, sword in hand, offering a salute to the audience and a particular bow to a private box where a stout gentleman sat in evident authority. Figg followed, receiving an approving nod from the same dignitary. Both men were stripped to their shirts, their shaven heads bearing the scars of past glories, their sword-arms adorned with colored ribbons.

The duel began with a thunderous strike from Figg—a blow that might have cleaved Sutton’s head clean had it landed. Instead, Sutton parried, and Figg’s blade shattered from the force of his own swing. Fresh swords were supplied, and the fight resumed in earnest. Steel flashed, blood was drawn (amid much dispute over fairness), and the crowd roared its approval. When swords gave way to quarterstaffs, the battle grew even fiercer, until at last Figg emerged victorious—his legend intact.

As the storm clouds burst overhead, Harry found himself offered refuge in the chariot of his Tunbridge Wells acquaintance, Lord March. The young Virginian, exhilarated by the spectacle, could not help but wish his elder brother, George, had been there to share it—though he quickly checked himself.

*"He wouldn’t have liked this rough sport,"* Harry admitted. *"But by heaven, he was the bravest man I ever knew."*

Lord March, amused by Harry’s earnestness, prodded further. *"You truly wish he were here?"*

*"I do. And yet—perhaps not. He was the scholar, the thinker. I’m only good for riding, cards, and Burgundy."* Harry grinned, then added with characteristic candor, *"But at those, I’ll back myself against any man."*

March laughed. *"By God, Warrington, you ought to be exhibited at Exeter ’Change—a Virginian who misses his elder brother! A rare specimen indeed."*

The evening unfolded as such evenings did: a lavish dinner, wine that flowed as freely as conversation, and then—cards. Piquet was the game, and though Harry started strong, fortune soon favored March. Their play was interrupted twice: first by the arrival of Harry’s lawyer, Draper (who endured the earl’s glares with stoic admiration), and then by the far more dramatic entrance of Betty, Lady Maria’s maid, in tears.

*"Help, sir! They’ve taken her!"* Betty cried, thrusting a letter into Harry’s hands.

Lady Maria’s missive was brief and frantic:

> *"I am fell into a trapp, which I perceive the machinations of villians. I am a prisner. Betty will tell you all. Ah, my Henrico! come to the resque of your MOLLY."*

March, ever the cynic, scoffed. *"Debt, no doubt. Must you rush off tonight?"*

But Harry was already summoning a postchaise.

*"A wager,"* March declared, scribbling in his pocketbook. *"Four to one you don’t reach Bromley within the hour. Another that you’re robbed on Blackheath. And a third you’re not at Tunbridge Wells by midnight."*

*"Done,"* said Harry—and with that, he was gone, the wheels of his carriage churning the mud as he raced to his kinswoman’s aid.
