Ever since Mirdath, my beautiful one, drew her last breath and left me adrift in a hollow, empty world, I have carried an agony of longing no words could ever rightly frame. I who once held all the world in her soft love and steady companionship, who drank deep of every joy life had to offer, now know a loneliness so sharp and total it stuns me even to turn my thoughts to it.

Yet here I sit, pen in hand again. A wondrous hope has taken root in me of late: night after night, I wake in my sleep not to my own dark bed, but to the far-flung future of this world, where I have walked through strange, impossible marvels and known the burn of life’s gladness once more. I have learned the promise woven into the very womb of Time, visited the places where she and I will meet again, pull apart in bitter, wounding sorrow, and find each other once more after vast stretches of uncounted ages, in a reunion bright enough to feel like a miracle made flesh.

This is the uncanny story of what I have seen. I must set it down, no matter how great the task, to ease the weight of longing in my own heart—and perhaps, give a spark of hope to some other suffering soul, pining as I do for a beloved one lost to death.

Some who read this will call it fantasy. Others will argue in its defence. I will say nothing to either, only “Read.” Once you turn these pages, you will have stood beside me at the very portals of Eternity.

The visions I speak of do not feel like dreams. They feel like waking: I blink and find myself standing in a world where the sun has long since guttered out, swallowed by the Everlasting Night that wraps the whole planet. Looking back to my own present age from that future feels like looking back on a vivid dream my soul knows was real—yet seen through new eyes, it glows far off, hallowed with a soft, peaceful light of a long-lost dawn.

Every time I wake into that future, a blurring grey mist wraps around me at first, clearing slowly like a dissipating dark cloud until I can see the vast dark world spread out ahead, dotted here and there with uncanny, glowing sights. I do not wake to ignorance: I know the names and natures of every glow, every terror of the Night Land, as naturally as a man waking from sleep in our own time knows his name, his home, the world he has grown up in. Beneath that native knowledge of the dark future, a quiet subconscious memory of my own present life lingers, sharpened only by the raw, hungry longing for Mirdath.

In my earliest clear memory of that place, I am seventeen years old, standing in an embrasure carved high in the side of the Last Redoubt: that gargantuan grey metal pyramid that shelters the last remaining millions of humanity, holding them safe from the inhuman Powers that hunt them beyond its walls.

I know every inch of that place so well it feels impossible no one in my own time has ever heard of it. I may speak too casually of its wonders and terrors, forgetting to explain things that will be utterly alien to you, reading this now. When I first woke there, I was not the grieving man of my own era, but that seventeen-year-old boy, full of all the natural, lived-in knowledge of seventeen years spent inside the Redoubt. Until that first vision, the man I am now had no inkling that other, future existence was waiting for me—but I woke to it as easily as you wake in your bed to the morning sun, knowing the world you open your eyes to. Even as I stood there in that vast metal embrasure, the memory of my present life lay deep under my new awareness, haloed like a half-remembered dream, but thrumming with a quiet, aching longing for the one my half-memory called Mirdath.

I leaned against the cold metal lip of the embrasure, staring northwest through a strange, polished spyglass, young, bold, and thrumming with a faint, delicious fear. I carried every lesson I had learned growing up inside the Redoubt in my bones: I could name every landmark spread across that dark landscape, rattle off the exact distance of each from the polished mathematical Centre-Point, the dimensionless marker set in the heart of the Redoubt’s Mathematics chamber where I studied every day.

Through the glass, the bright, roiling glow of the Red Pit blazed up, striking the underside of the massive, jutting “chin” of the Northwest Watcher: the ancient Watching Thing that had stood sentinel in that spot since the dawn of recorded time, never moving, never blinking. The old lines of Aesworpth, the ancient poet whose work had survived uncounted thousands of years, drifted through my head: *That which hath Watched from the Beginning, until the opening of the Gateway of Eternity*. But suddenly, the words felt incomplete. I looked deep inside myself, and saw the sunlit splendour of my own present age rising out of my soul like a memory made flesh. I was stunned.

I must make this clear to you: the boy I was, standing in that embrasure in the far future, woke that moment to a sudden, sharp awareness of my own present life, a past so far back it felt like the very dawn of eternity. We were the same soul. He saw vaguely the life I am living now, while I, the man of the present, looked forward to the life I will one day live. How strange, how impossibly tangled the threads of our existence are!

Before that awakening, the boy had only a dim, misty vision of that ancient, sunlit past, a haze that had confounded the learned men of the Redoubt time and time again. But from that moment onward, that half-memory hardened into something sharp, real, ten times more vivid. The hunger of my love for Mirdath leapt across the gulf of ages, turning that vague dream-memory into a searing, living pain of loss. From that moment on, I did nothing but listen, my whole life spent straining to hear any hint that she was out there, somewhere in the dark of the future.

I wrenched my thoughts back from that haze of longing, turning my gaze back to the unthinkable mystery of the Night Land spread before me. No one who lived in the Redoubt ever tired of watching that hideous, vast landscape: young and old alike stood watch from childhood to death, staring out at the black monstrosities the Redoubt’s walls kept at bay.

To the right of the Red Pit stretched the long, winding glow of the Vale of Red Fire. Beyond it, mile after mile of dreary blackness stretched out, lit by the cold, eerie blue radiance that drifted up from the Plain of Blue Fire. At the very edge of the Unknown Lands, a line of low volcanoes cast their light far out into the outer dark, gilding the tops of the Black Hills, where the Seven Lights burned. They never twinkled, never moved, never faltered for all Eternity, and not even the greatest, most powerful spyglass in the Redoubt could make any sense of them. No adventurer who had ever set out from the Pyramid to find them had ever returned to tell their story. The Great Library of the Redoubt held full accounts of every one of those lost explorers, their discoveries and their fates, men who had risked not just their bodies, but their very souls venturing out into the Night Land’s monstrousness.

The Last Redoubt’s own histories did not span thousands of years. They spanned millions, stretching back to what the people of that far future considered the world’s earliest infancy, when the sun still glowed dully, hanging low in the sky. Anything before that point was only myth, a story wise men treated with deep caution, never trusting it fully. Even the lullabies my future self’s nurse sang to him as a small child spoke of the sun, a mythical thing the children of the Redoubt half-believed was just a fairy tale.

To my right, far away to the North, the House of Silence stood atop a low hill. It glowed with steady, unwavering lights, and no sound had ever drifted out of it for as long as memory stretched. Not even the most sensitive distance-microphones the Redoubt had ever built could pick up a single whisper. It was accounted the single most dangerous place in all the Night Lands. Winding around it was the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, a path leading out of the Unknown Lands, past the mist-shrouded home of the Ab-humans. It was the only structure out in the dark that the people of the Redoubt knew had been built by human hands, long long ago. Thousands upon thousands of books had been written arguing over its origins, all contradicting each other, none reaching any conclusion. Entire libraries had been filled with treatises on every monstrous sight, every uncanny glow in the Night Land, millions of pages that had crumbled to dust in the eras long before the Redoubt’s recorded history even began.

I stepped onto the central moving roadway that ran across the 1000th plateau of the Redoubt. It sat six miles and thirty fathoms above the dark plain, more than a mile wide. A few minutes ride brought me to the southeastern wall, where I looked out through the Great Embrasure at the Three Silver-Fire Holes, glowing far off in the southeast, right in front of the Thing That Nods. Closer to me, the vast bulk of the Southeast Watcher loomed, a squat, lumbering living mountain. Torches burned half a mile on either side of it, casting light that showed the brute’s massive head, lurching slowly forward through the dark, never sleeping.

It was the quiet hours of Sleeping-Time on the 1000th plateau, when I heard a far, dreadful sound roll up out of the lightless East. Then it came again: a strange, deep laughter, rumbling like thunder trapped between mountains. It drifted out of the unknown, unvisited stretch of land beyond the Valley of the Hounds, and humanity had named that no-man’s-land The Country Whence Comes The Great Laughter. I had heard the sound hundreds of times before, but every time it made my heart race, made me feel small, acutely aware of the utter terror that had shadowed the last millions of human beings on the planet.

When the laughter faded back into the eastern dark, I turned my spyglass on the Giants’ Pit, just south of the Giants’ Kilns. The giants tended those kilns, their fire burning red and fitful, casting wavering, dancing shadows across the pit’s mouth. I could see the huge, shapeless forms of giants clambering up out of the depths, their forms never quite clear through the shifting light. Behind the pit, a massive black headland jutted up between the Valley of the Hounds, home to the monstrous Night Hounds, and the Giants’ territory. The light of the kilns gilded its top edge, and I saw things time and time again peer over the lip, creep a little into the light, then dart swiftly back into the shadows. That headland had been named The Headland From Which Strange Things Peer, marked clearly on every map of the grim Night Land for uncounted ages.

I turned my glass to the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, scanning it as I had done a hundred times in my youth. The sight of those robed, silent figures always stirred some deep, nameless part of me. At last, I spotted one, alone on miles of empty, grey road, cloaked from head to toe, moving at a steady, unhurried pace, never looking right or left. Folklore in the Redoubt said they would never harm a human who kept a respectful distance, but no one in their right mind would ever dare step close to one.

My gaze followed the figure far down the road, past the stretch lit weirdly by the glow of the Silver-Fire Holes, then curving southeast past the dark bulk of the Dark Palace, running south until it curved west, past the mountainous form of the South Watcher—the biggest monster in all the visible Night Lands. He loomed over the pale, soft glow of the Glowing Dome, a brooding, living hill of watchfulness. The learned order of the Monstruwacans, whose sacred duty it was to track and record every movement of the monsters outside the Redoubt, had recorded its entire slow advance out of the Southern Unknown Lands, starting a million years prior. It had crept closer for twenty thousand years, moving so slowly no single human lifetime could ever measure the shift in its position. It had been marching steadily toward the Redoubt when the Glowing Dome had risen out of the ground directly in front of it, growing slowly for centuries. The dome had halted it in its tracks. For uncounted ages, it had squatted there, staring toward the Pyramid across the pale glow, unable to move any nearer.

The fact that the dome had stopped it was proof to many scholars that not all the powers in the Night Land were evil. Even as the forces of Darkness hunted the last of humanity to the edge of extinction, there were other, unseen forces fighting back, working in ways no human mind could ever hope to understand.

No one could say for certain where all these monstrous things and dark powers had come from. Their rise began long before the Redoubt’s written histories, even before the sun had guttered out completely. Old, half-forgotten records spoke of sciences far beyond anything my present age has ever dreamed of, that tore open the veil between our world and the unnameable Outer Dimensions. They broke the ancient barrier that kept ab-human monsters and uncanny spirits at bay, letting them pour through. Some materialized on the physical plane as grotesque, ravenous creatures. Others, that could not take solid form, gained the power to prey directly on the human soul. As the world descended into chaos and degeneracy, the last remaining millions of unbroken, sane human beings banded together to build the Last Redoubt.

The great metal pyramid rose eight miles high, with 1,320 floors built to withstand every conceivable assault. A watchtower sat at its very peak, where the Monstruwacans kept their endless vigil. When it was complete, the last humans retreated inside its walls, turning it into a vast, self-sustaining city. Thus began the Second Age of Man.

Over thousands of years, terrible half-human, half-beast races rose up in the outer dark, launching repeated, devastating assaults on the Redoubt, only to be beaten back with massive slaughter. Eventually, the inhabitants lit the massive electric barrier around the entire base of the Pyramid, drawing power from the Earth’s own eternal current. They sealed off the lowest half-mile of the structure completely, and a long, unbroken era of quiet watching began, as every generation waited for the day the Earth’s Current would finally run out.

Countless bands of brave, foolhardy explorers ventured out into the dark over the centuries, almost never to return. There were eyes everywhere in that dark, powers that saw everything. As the endless night dragged on, the terror only grew, drawing more and greater monsters out of the outer void, attracted to the single last spark of human light in the endless dark, like infernal sharks circling a lone ship.

That is the world I woke to, every time my soul slipped across the gulf of ages, to the Last Redoubt, standing sentinel against an endless, hungry night.
