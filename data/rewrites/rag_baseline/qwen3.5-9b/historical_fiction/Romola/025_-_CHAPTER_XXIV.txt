Baldassarre shoved aside the heavy curtain, his wrists raw where the bonds bit into the flesh, the rough hemp of the rope digging into his throat and torso. As the darkness of the sacristy gave way to the cavernous light of the Duomo, he froze, a statue of shock against the threshold. He had anticipated the usual hollow shell of a church: a vast nave stripped of life, save for flickering tapers and pale, rigid idols staring back from side altars. Perhaps a handful of worshippers in the distant choir, murmuring a monotonous chant to the ceiling. That was the expectation of a man who viewed sanctuaries merely as architectural shells. Instead, he found a sea of living heat. Hundreds of faces, upturned in breathless silence toward the pulpit, formed a mosaic ranging from the dignified robes of magistrates and gentlewomen to the coarse tunics of artisans and peasants.

At the rostrum stood a Dominican friar, features sharp and commanding, dark hair framing a face etched with intensity. He held the crucifix high, preaching with a fervor that seemed to vibrate through the stone floor. For the first few minutes, Baldassarre registered nothing of the sermon. His entry had been silent, yet the atmosphere shifted instantly; eyes near the doorway snapped toward him with suspicion. The ropes around his limbs screamed their verdict: *escaped prisoner*. Yet, in this place, the church was a sanctuary, a legal shield he was entitled to claim. His advanced age and the wild misery stamped upon his face were weapons designed to elicit pity, not alarm. As he stood motionless, his gaze drifting aimlessly from the throng to the patterned pavement at his feet, the observers lost their vigilance, drawn back into the magnetic pull of the preacher's voice.

Among those eyes that had locked onto him was Romola's. She had slipped in late through a side portal, positioning herself to command a full view of the main entrance. Her gaze lingered on Baldassarre with a piercing attention. Grey hairs, a rare sight in Florence, struck a chord in her heart, as did the unmistakable brand of suffering on his face, sealed by the cord around his neck. These details stirred the dormant sensibilities of her soul, honed by a lifetime of witnessing sorrow. She imagined his eyes meeting hers in that initial, startled sweep, but Baldassarre remained oblivious. He possessed only a fractured awareness of the scene—a fleeting spark that failed to pierce the dense, turbulent fog of emotion ignited by his confrontation with Tito. Memories of the past surged like delirious visions, tangled with thirst and anguish. No coherent thought of the future could form amidst this internal tempest; the closest approximation was a bitter recognition of his fading strength and a vague, corrosive resolve born of total distrust.

Suddenly, the ambient noise coalesced into a roar, echoing the thunder of his own inner turmoil. A voice, cutting through his marrow with an accent of triumphant certainty, declared: *"The day of vengeance is at hand!"*

Baldassarre quivered, forcing his eyes upward. Too distant to discern detail, he saw only the silhouette of the preacher, right arm outstretched, lifting the crucifix like a weapon. He gasped for the threatening cadence again, as if it were a promise of salvation. The preacher paused, slowly lowering his arm until the wooden cross rested on the pulpit's edge. He folded his arms across his chest, scanning the multitude as if challenging every single soul to meet his gaze.

"All ye in Florence are my witnesses," the voice boomed, resonating with terrifying clarity. "I spoke not in a corner. Ye are my witnesses that four years ago, ere signs of war or tribulation appeared, I proclaimed the coming scourge. I lifted my voice as a trumpet to the prelates, princes, and people of Italy, declaring: 'The cup of your iniquity is full.' Behold, the thunder of the Lord gathers; it shall fall and shatter the cup. Your iniquity, which tastes to you as pleasant wine, shall be poured out upon you, becoming molten lead."

"And you, O priests, who mock, saying, 'Ha, ha! There is no Presence in the sanctuary—the Shechinah is naught—the Mercy-seat is bare: we may sin behind the veil, and who shall punish us?' To you, I declare, the presence of God shall manifest in His temple as a consuming fire. Your sacred vestments shall become winding sheets of flame. Where there was sweet music, there shall be shrieks and hissing; where soft couches awaited, there shall be thorns; where the breath of wantons flowed, there shall come pestilence. Trust not in your gold and silver, nor in your high fortresses. Though walls be of iron and citadels of adamant, the Most High shall instill terror into your hearts and weakness into your councils, leaving you confounded and fleeing like women. He shall break in pieces mighty men without number, replacing them with others. For God will no longer endure the pollution of His sanctuary; He will thoroughly purge His Church."

"And since Scripture declares that God reveals His purposes only to His servants the prophets, He has chosen me, His unworthy servant, making His purpose present to my soul through the living Word and the deeds of Providence. By the ministry of angels, He has revealed it to me in visions. His word possesses me so completely that I am but a branch of the forest when the wind of heaven strikes it; I cannot remain silent, even if I am a derision to the scorner. For four years, in obedience to the Divine Will, I have preached three truths delivered by the Lord: that *in these times God will regenerate His Church*, that *before this regeneration comes the scourge over all Italy*, and that *these things will come quickly*."

"But hypocrites, cloaking their hatred of the truth with a show of love, have told me, 'Come now, Frate, leave your prophecies; it is enough to teach virtue.' To these I answer: 'Yes, you whisper in your hearts that God dwells afar off, that His word is a parchment written by dead men, and that He deals not as in the days of old, rebuking nations and punishing oppressors. But I cry again in your ears: God is near, not afar off. His judgments do not change. He is the God of Armies; the strong men who march to battle are His ministers, even as storm, fire, and pestilence. He drives them by the breath of His angels, and they descend upon the chosen land which has forsaken the covenant. And thou, O Italy, art the chosen land; has not God placed His sanctuary within thee, and hast thou polluted it? Behold, the ministers of His wrath are upon thee—they are at thy very doors!'"

Savonarola's voice had climbed to a peak of impassioned force before abruptly silencing. His hands fell, clasping quietly before him. This silence was not a lull; it was a spell as potent as his voice. Across the vast cathedral, men and women sat frozen, faces upturned like breathing statues, until the voice returned in clear, low tones.

"Yet there is a pause—even as in the days when Jerusalem was destroyed, there was a pause that the children of God might flee from it. There is a stillness before the storm: lo, there is blackness above, yet not a leaf quakes; the winds are stayed, that the voice of God's warning may be heard. Hear it now, O Florence, chosen city in the chosen land! Repent and forsake evil; do justice; love mercy; cast out all uncleanness from among you, that the spirit of truth and holiness may fill your souls and breathe through all your streets and habitations. Then the pestilence shall not enter, and the sword shall pass over you and leave you unhurt."

"For the sword hangs from the sky; it quivers; it is about to fall! *The sword of God upon the earth, swift and sudden!* Did I not tell you, years ago, that I beheld the vision and heard the voice? And behold, it is fulfilled! Is there not a king with his army at your gates? Does not the earth shake with the tread of horses and the wheels of swift cannon? Is there not a fierce multitude capable of laying bare the land as with a sharp razor? I tell you the French King with his army is the minister of God: God shall guide him as a hand guides a sharp sickle, and the joints of the wicked shall melt before him, mown down as stubble. He that flees shall not escape, and he that escapes shall not be delivered."

"And the tyrants who built their thrones from the vices of the multitude, and the unbelieving priests who traffic in souls and fill the sanctuary with fornication, shall be hurled from their soft couches into burning hell; and the pagans and those who sinned under the old covenant shall stand aloof and say: 'Lo, these men have brought the stench of a new wickedness into the everlasting fire.'"
