As the scorching desert sun rose over the prison walls of Omdurman, casting a warm glow over the desolate landscape, Feversham slowly regained consciousness. His parched throat begged for water, and his body yearned for nourishment. Trench, ever vigilant, emerged from the shadows, his face a welcome sight to Feversham's bleary eyes. For a moment, the face seemed a product of his fevered imagination, a specter from the darkest recesses of his troubled past. But as reality asserted itself, Feversham's gaze roamed over the familiar surroundings – the worn stone walls, the trampled earth, and the listless forms of fellow prisoners struggling to survive.

Trench's gentle voice cut through the haze, "Good morrow, Feversham. Welcome back to the world." Feversham's fingers brushed against Trench's arm, as if to anchor himself to reality, and a faint smile crept onto his lips. "I am in Omdurman, actually within the prison walls of the House of Stone. It's almost too wonderful to believe." He settled against the wall, his exhaustion evident in every line of his body.

To Trench, Feversham's words seemed tinged with a wry humor, a calculated nonchalance that belied the turmoil brewing beneath the surface. Yet, the prisoner's delirium had stripped away the facade, revealing a man who had faced unimaginable hardships and had emerged scarred but unbroken. The satisfaction in his voice was genuine, a testament to the power of hope and perseverance.

"You've been here a while," Feversham observed, his gaze drifting across the zareeba. "Three years, I'm told." Trench nodded, his eyes clouding over with memories of the unforgiving nights within the prison walls.

"It's the darkness that gets to you," Feversham whispered, his voice barely audible over the wind rustling through the thorns. "A thousand nights of the House of Stone, and yet... somehow, we endure." The weight of those words hung in the air, a poignant reminder of the indomitable human spirit.

"We'll venture down to the Nile by day," Trench said, his voice filled with reassurance. "We'll fetch water from its banks, just as I did this morning." He gestured to the clay bowl beside him, its contents a testament to the promise of freedom.
