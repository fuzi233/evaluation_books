As the sweltering summer heat enveloped Villette, a delicate balance of power shifted within the confines of Madame Beck's pensionnat. The youngest of her children, Georgette, succumbed to a fever, prompting the temporary relocation of Désirée and Fifine to the countryside to prevent potential contagion. In their absence, Madame Beck found herself navigating the intricacies of diplomatic protocol, opting to retain the services of Dr. John, an English physician, over the return of her French counterpart, Dr. Pillule. The decision was met with skepticism from the parents, who perceived Dr. John's presence as a breach of propriety in a traditionally conservative institution.

Madame Beck, however, employed her signature blend of tact and audacity to deflect criticism. She presented Dr. John as a trusted figure, capable of administering care to her own children, who, she claimed, had taken a liking to him. The pupils, particularly the enigmatic Blanche de Melcy and the vivacious Angélique, further solidified Dr. John's position by extolling his virtues and expressing a desire to have him attend to their ailments. As the news of Dr. John's appointment spread throughout the town, Madame Beck's reputation as a directress grew, with many regarding her actions as a testament to her maternal devotion.

Yet, beneath the surface of this carefully constructed facade, whispers persisted about the true nature of Madame Beck's intentions. Some speculated that she harbored romantic feelings for Dr. John, despite their significant age difference. The notion seemed plausible, given Madame Beck's apparent willingness to overlook the conventions that governed her institution. While appearances may have lent credence to this idea, it remained unclear whether Madame Beck's actions were motivated by a genuine affection or a calculated move to assert her authority within the pensionnat. As the days passed, the dynamics between Madame Beck, Dr. John, and the rest of the community continued to unfold, with the delicate balance of power remaining precarious and open to interpretation.
