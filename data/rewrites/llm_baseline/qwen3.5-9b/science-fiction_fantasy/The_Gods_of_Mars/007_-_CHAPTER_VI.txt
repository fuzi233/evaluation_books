"What is it?" I asked the girl.

She pointed upward. I looked, and there, hovering high above the temple, the courts, and the gardens, were shadowy forms darting erratically through the air. Almost instantly, flashes of light erupted from these strange objects, followed by the thunderous roar of musketry. In reply, flashes and roars burst forth from the temple walls and ramparts.

"The Black Pirates of Barsoom, O Prince," Thuvia declared.

In widening circles, the aircraft of the marauders swept lower, descending upon the defensive forces of the Therns. Volley after volley of fire vomited from the pirate craft onto the temple guards; volley upon volley crashed through the thin Martian air toward the fleeting, elusive flyers. As the pirates dove closer to the ground, Thern soldiers poured from the sanctuaries into the open gardens and courts. The sight of them exposed drew a score of enemy flyers darting toward us from every direction. The Therns fired through shields attached to their rifles, but relentlessly onward came the grim, black machines.

They were mostly small craft, designed for two or three men, though a few larger vessels remained high aloft, dropping bombs from their keel batteries onto the temples. Finally, responding to a coordinated signal, the pirates in our immediate vicinity dashed recklessly toward the earth, plunging directly into the midst of the Thern soldiery. Scarcely allowing their craft to settle, the crews leaped among the defenders with the fury of demons.

Such fighting! I had never witnessed its like. I had considered the Green Martians the most ferocious warriors in the universe, yet the awful abandon with which the Black Pirates threw themselves upon their foes surpassed anything I had ever seen. Beneath the brilliant light of Mars' two glorious moons, the entire scene unfolded in vivid detail. Golden-haired, white-skinned Therns battled with desperate courage in hand-to-hand combat against their ebony-skinned enemies. Here, a small knot of struggling warriors trampled a bed of gorgeous *pimalia*; there, the curved sword of a Black man pierced the heart of a Thern, leaving his dead foe at the foot of a wondrous statue carved from living ruby; yonder, a dozen Therns pressed a single pirate back against a bench of emerald, upon whose iridescent surface a strangely beautiful Barsoomian design was traced in inlaid diamonds.

A short distance away stood Thuvia, the Thark, and I. The tide of battle had not yet reached us, but the fighters swung close enough for us to observe them clearly. The Black Pirates fascinated me immensely. I had heard vague rumors, little more than legends, during my former imprisonment on Mars, but I had never seen one nor spoken with someone who had. Popular belief held that they inhabited the lesser moon, descending upon Barsoom at long intervals. Wherever they landed, they committed the most horrible atrocities. When they departed, they carried away firearms, ammunition, and young girls as prisoners. Rumor claimed these captives were sacrificed to a terrible god in an orgy ending with the consumption of their victims.

I had an excellent opportunity to examine them, as the chaos occasionally brought individuals close to where I stood. They were large men, likely over six feet tall. Their features were sharply cut and remarkably handsome; their eyes were well-set and large, though a slight narrowness lent them a crafty appearance. By the moonlight, I determined their irises were of extreme blackness, while the eyeballs themselves were stark white and clear. Physically, their structures seemed identical to those of the Therns, the Red Men, and myself. The only material difference was the color of their skin—a polished ebony hue that, oddly enough, added to rather than detracted from their marvelous beauty. Yet, if their bodies were divine, their hearts appeared to be quite the opposite. Never had I witnessed such a malign lust for blood as these demons of the outer air displayed in their mad battle with the Therns.

All around us in the garden lay their sinister craft, which the Therns, for reasons unknown to me at the time, made no effort to damage. Now and again, a Black warrior would rush from a nearby temple carrying a young woman in his arms. He would leap straight for his flyer while comrades nearby rushed to cover his escape. On the Therns' side, warriors hastened to rescue the girl, and in an instant, both parties were swallowed in the vortex of a maelstrom of yelling devils, hacking and hewing at one another like fiends incarnate. Yet, it seemed the Black Pirates of Barsoom were invariably victorious, carrying the girl away miraculously unharmed into the outer darkness upon the deck of a swift machine.

Similar fighting echoed in both directions as far as sound carried. Thuvia informed me that the Black Pirate attacks were typically simultaneous along the entire ribbon-like domain of the Therns, which circles the Valley Dor on the outer slopes of the Mountains of Otz. As the fighting momentarily receded from our position, Thuvia turned to me with a question.

"Do you understand now, O Prince," she said, "why a million warriors guard the domains of the Holy Therns by day and by night?"

"The scene you witness is merely a repetition of what I have seen enacted a score of times during the fifteen years I have been a prisoner here. From time immemorial, the Black Pirates of Barsoom have preyed upon the Holy Therns."

"Yet they never push their expeditions to a point where the extermination of the Thern race seems imminent," I observed. "It is as though they utilize the race merely as playthings, satisfying their ferocious lust for fighting, and collecting toll in the form of arms, ammunition, and prisoners."

"Why don't they simply destroy these flyers?" I asked. "That would soon halt the attacks, or at least make the Blacks less bold. See how perfectly unguarded they leave their craft, as though they were lying safe in their own hangars at home."

"The Therns dare not," Thuvia replied. "They tried once, ages ago, but the following night—and for an entire lunar month thereafter—a thousand great black battleships circled the Mountains of Otz, pouring tons of projectiles upon the temples, gardens, and courts until every Thern who was not killed was driven for safety into the subterranean galleries. The Therns know that they live only by the sufferance of the Black Men. They were near to extinction once, and they will not risk it again."

As she finished speaking, a new element entered the conflict, coming from a source neither Thern nor Pirate expected. The great Banths we had liberated in the garden had initially been awed by the sounds of battle—the yelling of warriors and the loud reports of rifles and bombs. But now, enraged by the continuous noise and excited by the scent of fresh blood, a massive form suddenly shot from a clump of low shrubbery into the midst of the struggling humans. A horrid scream of bestial rage tore from the Banth's throat as he felt warm flesh beneath his powerful talons. His cry served as a signal to the others, and the entire great pack hurled themselves among the fighters.

Panic reigned instantly. Thern and Black Man alike turned against the common enemy, for the Banths showed no partiality. The awful beasts bore down a hundred men with the sheer weight of their great bodies as they plunged into the thick of the fight. Leaping and clawing, they mowed down warriors with their powerful paws, pausing only to rend their victims with frightful fangs. The scene was fascinating in its terror, but suddenly it struck me that we were wasting valuable time watching this conflict, which might offer a means of our escape. The Therns were so engrossed with their terrible assailants that now, perhaps never again, would escape be comparatively easy.

I turned to search for an opening through the contending hordes. If we could reach the ramparts, we might find that the pirates had thinned the guarding forces, leaving a path open to the outside world. As my eyes scanned the garden, the sight of hundreds of aircraft lying unguarded around us suggested the simplest route to freedom. Why had it not occurred to me before? I was thoroughly familiar with the mechanism of every known type of flyer on Barsoom. For nine years, I had sailed and fought with the navy of Helium. I had raced through space on a tiny one-man air scout and commanded the greatest battleship that ever floated in the thin air of the dying Mars. To think is to act.

Grasping Thuvia by the arm, I whispered to Tars Tarkas to follow me. Quickly, we glided toward a small flyer lying furthest from the battling warriors. An instant later, we were huddled on the tiny deck. My hand went to the starting lever. I pressed my thumb upon the button controlling the ray of repulsion—that splendid Martian discovery permitting them to navigate the thin atmosphere in ships that dwarf Earth's dreadnoughts into pitiful insignificance.

The craft swayed slightly but did not move. Then, a new cry of warning shattered the silence. Turning, I saw a dozen Black Pirates dashing toward us from the melee. We had been discovered. With shrieks of rage, the demons sprang for us. With frenzied insistence, I continued pressing the little button that should have sent us racing into space, but the vessel stubbornly refused to budge.
