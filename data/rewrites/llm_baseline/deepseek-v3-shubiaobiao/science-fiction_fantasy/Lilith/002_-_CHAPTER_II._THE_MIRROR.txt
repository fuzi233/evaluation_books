Days passed without incident. Then, about a week later, something extraordinary occurred.

The manuscript fragment still haunted my thoughts. I had tried—and failed—to free it from whatever unseen force held it in place. But today, my focus shifted to the books themselves. The closet’s stale air made me uneasy about their condition, and I resolved to inspect them properly.

Just as I rose from my chair, I glimpsed movement near the closet door—a figure, slight and stooped, drifting toward the far end of the room. A shadow, perhaps, but one with unnerving clarity: a man in a threadbare coat, its tails nearly brushing the floor, revealing spindly legs clad in black stockings and wide, slipper-like shoes. Without hesitation, I followed.

He led me out of the library, across the hall, and up the grand staircase to the first floor. Past the stately rooms we went, down a corridor, then up a narrower stair to the second floor. Here, the house grew unfamiliar—a maze of passages I had never explored as a child.

At last, we reached a wooden stair, winding and creaky. Halfway up, my guide vanished. No sound, no trace. The top of the stair opened into a vast garret, its high beams crisscrossing beneath the roof. Dust-laden light seeped through cobwebbed skylights, painting the air with a dull glow. Doors stood ajar, hinting at unseen chambers.

Then I saw it—an unpainted wooden enclosure, its door slightly open. Hoping the stranger might be inside, I pushed it wider.

The small room was bathed in a lifeless light, as if abandoned by time itself. Sunbeams, weak and diffuse, slanted through swirling motes, illuminating a tall mirror with a tarnished surface. Its ebony frame bore a black eagle, wings outstretched, gripping a golden chain from which hung a dark sphere.

At first, I thought it merely reflected the room—until I realized it showed nothing at all. No chamber. No me.

Then the wall seemed to dissolve.

Before me stretched a desolate landscape: heathland, barren and flat, broken by low, oddly shaped hills. Beyond them, distant mountains cut the horizon. A lone raven, grizzled with age, hopped toward me with deliberate grace, pecking at the ground.

Unthinking, I stepped closer—then stumbled. My foot caught on something (the mirror’s frame?), and suddenly, I stood not before the glass, but *within* the scene. The air was real, the earth solid beneath my feet. The raven lifted its head, fixing me with a gleaming eye.

I was no longer in the house.

I was *elsewhere*.
