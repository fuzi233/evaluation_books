The realization that the Monarch had been lost in reverie prompted me to attempt a descent from his celestial rapture back to the realm of empirical logic. I resolved to offer him glimpses of the fundamental truths governing our existence in Flatland. Thus, I initiated my discourse: "Your Royal Highness, by what faculty do you distinguish the forms and orientations of your subjects? Before entering your Kingdom, I observed through the sense of sight that your populace consists of Lines and Points, varying in magnitude—"

"You speak of an impossibility," the King interjected sharply. "You must have witnessed a hallucination. To discern the distinction between a Line and a Point via sight is, by the very nature of things, impossible. Such differentiation is achieved solely through the sense of hearing, and by this same mechanism, my own form is precisely determined. Behold—I am a Line, the longest in Lineland, extending over six inches of Space."

"Of Length," I ventured to clarify.

"Fool," he retorted. "Space *is* Length. Cease your interruptions, or I shall end this audience."

Apologizing, I persisted, yet he continued with disdain, "Since you remain impervious to reason, I shall demonstrate with your ears how my dual voices reveal my shape to my Wives, currently situated six thousand miles, seventy yards, two feet, and eight inches away—one to the North, the other to the South. Listen; I summon them."

He emitted a chirp, then proceeded with complacency: "My Wives, receiving the sound of one of my voices followed closely by the other, perceive that the second arrives after an interval during which sound traverses 6.457 inches. They infer that one of my mouths lies 6.457 inches farther from them than the other, thereby deducing my shape to be exactly 6.457 inches. Naturally, you understand they do not perform this calculation repeatedly upon hearing my voice; they established it once, prior to our marriage. Yet, they *could* perform it at any moment. Similarly, I can estimate the form of any male subject through the sense of sound."

"But how," I asked, "if a Man feigns a Woman's voice with one of his tones, or disguises his Southern voice so thoroughly it cannot be recognized as an echo of the Northern? Might such deceptions cause grave inconvenience? And possess you no means of verifying such frauds by commanding neighboring subjects to touch one another?"

This was, of course, a foolish query, for tactile sensation could not serve the purpose; however, I asked it deliberately to irritate the Monarch, and I succeeded perfectly.

"What!" he shrieked in horror. "Explain your meaning."

"Feel, touch, come into contact," I replied.

"If by FEELING you mean approaching so closely that no space remains between two individuals, know, Stranger, that this offense is punishable by death in my dominions. The reason is self-evident: the fragile form of a Woman, liable to shatter under such approximation, must be protected by the State. Since Women cannot be distinguished from Men by sight, the Law universally ordains that neither Man nor Woman shall approach so closely as to obliterate the interval between the approximator and the approximated."

"And indeed, what conceivable purpose would be served by this illegal and unnatural excess of approximation, which you term TOUCHING, when all ends of such a brutal and coarse process are attained more easily and accurately by the sense of hearing? Regarding your suggested danger of deception, it is non-existent: for the Voice is the essence of one's Being and cannot be altered at will. But suppose I possessed the power to pass through solid objects, penetrating my subjects one by one, even numbering a billion, verifying the size and distance of each by the sense of FEELING: how much time and energy would be wasted in this clumsy, inaccurate method! Wherefore, now, in a single moment of audition, I conduct the census and statistics—local, corporeal, mental, and spiritual—of every living being in Lineland. Hark! Only hark!"

Thus speaking, he paused, listening in ecstasy to a sound that seemed to me no better than the tiny chirping of an innumerable multitude of Lilliputian grasshoppers.

"Truly," I replied, "your sense of hearing serves you well, filling many deficiencies. Yet permit me to observe that your life in Lineland must be deplorably dull. To see nothing but a Point! Not even to contemplate a Straight Line! Nay, not even to comprehend what a Straight Line is! To possess sight yet be cut off from the Linear vistas granted to us in Flatland! It would be better surely to have no sense of sight at all than to see so little! I admit I lack your discriminative faculty of hearing; for the concert of all Lineland, which brings you such intense pleasure, is to me no more than a multitudinous twittering or chirping. But at least I can discern, by sight, a Line from a Point. Let me prove it. Just before entering your kingdom, I saw you dancing from left to right, and then from right to left, with Seven Men and a Woman immediately to your left, and eight Men and two Women to your right. Is this not correct?"

"It is correct," said the King, "so far as the numbers and sexes are concerned, though I know not what you mean by 'right' and 'left'. But I deny that you saw these things. How could you see the Line—that is to say, the inside—of any Man? You must have heard these things and then dreamed that you saw them. Furthermore, what do you mean by those words 'left' and 'right'? I suppose it is your way of saying Northward and Southward."

"Not so," I replied. "Besides your motion of Northward and Southward, there exists another motion which I call from right to left."

**KING.** Exhibit to me, if you please, this motion from left to right.

**I.** Nay, I cannot do so unless you could step out of your Line altogether.

**KING.** Out of my Line? Do you mean out of the world? Out of Space?

**I.** Well, yes. Out of *YOUR* World. Out of *YOUR* Space. For your Space is not the true Space. True Space is a Plane; but your Space is merely a Line.

**KING.** If you cannot indicate this motion from left to right by moving within it, I beg you to describe it to me in words.

**I.** If you cannot distinguish your right side from your left, I fear no words of mine can clarify my meaning to you. Yet surely you cannot be ignorant of such a simple distinction.

**KING.** I do not in the least understand you.

**I.** Alas! How shall I make it clear? When you move straight ahead, does it not occasionally occur to you that you *COULD* move in some other way, turning your eye round to look in the direction towards which your side is now fronting? In other words, instead of always moving in the direction of one of your extremities, do you never feel a desire to move in the direction, so to speak, of your side?

**KING.** Never. And what do you mean? How can a man's inside "front" in any direction? Or how can a man move in the direction of his inside?

**I.** Well then, since words cannot explain the matter, I shall resort to deed, and will move gradually out of Lineland in the direction I wish to indicate to you.

At the word, I began to move my body out of Lineland. As long as any part of me remained within his dominion and in his view, the King kept exclaiming, "I see you, I see you still; you are not moving." But when I had finally moved myself out of his Line, he cried in his shrillest voice, "She is vanished; she is dead."

"I am not dead," I replied. "I am simply out of Lineland—that is to say, out of the Straight Line which you call Space, and in the true Space, where I can see things as they are. At this moment, I can see your Line, or side—or inside, as you are pleased to call it; and I can also see the Men and Women to the North and South of you, whom I will now enumerate, describing their order, their size, and the intervals between each."

Having completed this lengthy enumeration, I cried triumphantly, "Does that at last convince you?" With that, I once more entered Lineland, taking up the same position as before.

But the Monarch replied, "If you were a Man of sense—though, as you appear to have only one voice, I have little doubt you are not a Man but a Woman—but if you had a particle of sense, you would listen to reason. You ask me to believe that there is another Line besides that which my senses indicate, and another motion besides that of which I am daily conscious. I, in return, ask you to describe in words or indicate by motion that other Line of which you speak. Instead of moving, you merely exercise some magic art of vanishing and returning to sight; and instead of any lucid description of your new World, you simply tell me the numbers and sizes of some forty of my retinue, facts known to any child in my capital. Can anything be more irrational or audacious? Acknowledge your folly or depart from my dominions."

Furious at his perversity, and especially indignant that he professed ignorance of my sex, I retorted in no measured terms: "Besotted Being! You think yourself the perfection of existence, while you are in reality the most imperfect and imbecile. You profess to see, whereas you can see nothing but a Point! You plume yourself on inferring the existence of a Straight Line; but I *CAN SEE* Straight Lines, and infer the existence of Angles, Triangles, Squares, Pentagons, Hexagons, and even Circles. Why waste more words? Suffice it that I am the completion of your incomplete self. You are a Line, but I am a Line of Lines, called in my country a Square: and even I, infinitely superior though I am to you, am of little account among the great nobles of Flatland, whence I have come to visit you in the hope of enlightening your ignorance."

Hearing these words, the King advanced toward me with a menacing cry, as if to pierce me through the diagonal. In that same moment, arose from myriads of his subjects a multitudinous war-cry, increasing in vehemence until it seemed to rival the roar of an army of a hundred thousand Isosceles and the artillery of a thousand Pentagons. Spell-bound and motionless, I could neither speak nor move to avert the impending destruction; and still the noise grew louder, and the King came closer, when I awoke to find the breakfast-bell recalling me to the realities of Flatland.
