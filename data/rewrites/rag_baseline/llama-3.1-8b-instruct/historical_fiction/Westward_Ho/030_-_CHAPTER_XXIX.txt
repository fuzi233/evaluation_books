In the aftermath of the Virginia Fleet's thwarted voyage, Amyas returned to his tranquil life in Northam, where the rhythms of domesticity and philanthropy now held sway. As he settled into a routine of leisure, Yeo, his trusted major domo, assumed a prominent role, often seen trailing behind him like a faithful greyhound, busily engaged in crafting an assortment of trinkets and novelties for the household, particularly for Ayacanora, the enigmatic maiden who had recently joined their midst. While her affectionate nature endeared her to the family, her fixation on adopting English customs often led to friction, particularly when Yeo presented her with gifts she deemed unsuitable for her refined tastes. Undeterred, Ayacanora persisted in her quest to master the domestic arts, emulating the exemplary behavior of Mrs. Leigh, who exemplified the virtues of a devoted housekeeper and charitable benefactress.

As Ayacanora watched Mrs. Leigh devote herself selflessly to the welfare of the local poor, a nascent idea began to take hold – the concept of extending kindness to those who could offer nothing in return. Her observations of Amyas's generosity, which manifested in the form of monetary donations, alms, and even the renovation of cottages into almshouses for weary sailors, further deepened her understanding of the Christian notion of thanksgiving. However, her comprehension of this idea remained incomplete, for her cultural background had instilled in her a different understanding of sacrificial offerings, which involved the presentation of human victims and the adornment of sacred spaces with the relics of the dead.

It was not until Mrs. Leigh gently elucidated the connection between charity and the divine presence within all individuals that Ayacanora's perception began to shift. She grasped the notion that by giving to the less fortunate, one was, in fact, offering to the Great Spirit, who dwelled within every person, including herself. This epiphany sparked a profound transformation in Ayacanora, and she dedicated herself to serving the community with unwavering dedication, often accompanying Mrs. Leigh on visits to the poor and working tirelessly to create garments for those in need.
