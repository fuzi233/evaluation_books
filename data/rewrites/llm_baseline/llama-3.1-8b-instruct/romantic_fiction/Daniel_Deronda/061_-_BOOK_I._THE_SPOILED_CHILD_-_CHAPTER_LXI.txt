As the morning light seeped through the windows of the Meyrick household, casting a warm glow over the parlor, Hans entered with his familiar latch-key, the sound echoing softly through the stillness. His timing was deliberate, usually arriving around this hour to catch Mirah's weekly lesson with Mab in full swing. Today, however, was different. He burst in, the _Times_ newspaper held aloft, its crackling pages making Mab's music screech to an abrupt halt mid-note. Mirah's fingers, poised above the piano keys, involuntarily stilled, her eyes snapping towards Hans with a mixture of surprise and alarm.

Mab, ever the child, let out a dismayed cry, "O-o-o, Hans! Why do you bring a more dreadful noise than my singing?" Her words were lost on Hans, however, as he stood triumphantly, the newspaper fluttering in his grasp. Mrs. Meyrick, seated nearby, looked up from her book, her eyes scanning the headline with a flicker of curiosity.

"What on earth is the extraordinary news?" she asked, her voice tinged with a hint of trepidation. Hans, with a peculiar glint in his eye, began to speak, his tone a subtle mix of excitement and mischief. "Something from Italy," he said, his words dripping with a knowing undertone, as if sharing a secret only he understood.

Mrs. Meyrick's anxiety spiked, her thoughts racing to Deronda, while Mirah's heart, too, was seized by the same unsettling notion. "Not bad for anyone we care about," Hans reassured her, though his tone hinted at a deeper meaning. Mab, growing restless, cut in, "If you must talk of yourself, Hans, let it be behind your own back. What _is_ it that has happened?"

Hans' smile grew wider as he handed the paper to his mother, his finger pointing to a specific paragraph. "Duke Alfonso is drowned, and the Duchess is alive," he announced, the words tumbling out in a rush. "And Deronda, that fortunate fellow, was at Genoa, staying at the same hotel, and witnessed the whole scene. He even helped rescue her from the water."
