The four of us—Carthoris, Xodar, Tars Tarkas, and I—stood motionless, our eyes fixed on the massive battleship hovering above. One by one, two more vessels crested the hills, their sleek silhouettes cutting through the crimson sky. Then, like a swarm of lethal insects, one-man scouts detached from the flagship and spiraled down toward us.

Within moments, we were encircled by armed warriors, their weapons glinting in the sun. An officer stepped forward, his gaze narrowing—until it landed on Carthoris. Recognition flashed across his face.

**"Carthoris!"** The man’s voice cracked with emotion as he seized the prince’s shoulders. **"Kaor! Hor Vastus greets the son of Dejah Thoris and John Carter! Where have you been? Helium has been draped in mourning since your disappearance!"**

Carthoris clasped the warrior’s forearm. **"Grieve no longer, Hor Vastus. I bring back not just myself, but the man Barsoom thought lost forever—my father, John Carter!"**

Hor Vastus turned toward me, and for a heartbeat, his face betrayed disbelief—then awe. **"John Carter?"** His voice faltered, his loyalty warring with the dread implications of my return. He knew the legends: none returned from the Valley Dor.

But he was a soldier first. Without hesitation, he unbuckled his sword and cast it at my feet. On Barsoom, no gesture carried greater weight—it was an oath of life and fealty, the kind reserved for kings and conquerors.

I lifted the blade, pressed the hilt to my lips, then fastened it back upon him. **"Your loyalty honors me, Hor Vastus. I swear by my honor, I will never call upon it save for justice."**

A larger craft descended, its deck bristling with armed guards. **"Kantos Kan demands your presence aboard the *Xavarian*,"** announced the officer.

As we boarded, I scanned our group—and froze. **"Where is Thuvia?"** No one had seen her since Carthoris sent her fleeing into the hills. Hor Vastus immediately dispatched scouts, but the girl had vanished.

Moments later, we stood on the *Xavarian’s* deck. Kantos Kan, now a fleet commander, greeted me not as a stranger, but as the comrade who had once fought beside him in the dungeons of Warhoon. His embrace was fierce, his words unspoken. He asked no questions, though his eyes burned with them.

But when I inquired after Dejah Thoris, his face darkened. **"She is gone,"** he murmured. **"Vanished with her guards—including Sola, Tars Tarkas’ daughter. Zat Arras claims she sought the Valley Dor."**

My blood turned to ice.

Zat Arras. The name alone was a curse. The Jed of Zodanga had long coveted Dejah Thoris, and now, with her father and grandfather absent, his ambition had turned to predation.

Kantos Kan’s voice dropped lower. **"He commands this fleet. And he has ordered your arrest."**

---

The flagship loomed like a steel claw against the sky. Zat Arras awaited us, his face a mask of cold triumph. **"Why are these men armed?"** he snapped.

**"They are not prisoners,"** Kantos Kan countered. **"Two are of Helium’s royal blood. The others are allies."**

Zat Arras’ lips curled. **"Then let them answer. John Carter—where have you been?"**

**"From the Valley Dor,"** I said. **"From the lies of Iss. I returned to save Barsoom from the slaughter hidden beneath its myths."**

**"Blasphemer!"** he roared. **"You think to escape death with lies?"**

The word *liar* was a spark to tinder. Before anyone could react, I had him by the throat, bending him backward over my knee. **"Call me that again,"** I growled.

His guards surged forward, but Kantos Kan’s whisper stayed my hand. **"A mutiny now will fracture Helium. For Dejah Thoris’ sake—stop."**

I released Zat Arras, leaving him gasping. **"We return to the *Xavarian*,"** I said, turning my back on him.

---

The flight to Helium was silent. Below, the capital’s towers blazed scarlet in the sun, but the streets seethed with unrest. Crowds packed the avenues, their faces torn between adoration and dread.

We were taken not through the cheering throngs, but to the Temple of Reward—where justice was both given and taken.

Zat Arras dared not let the people near us. He knew their love might outweigh their fear.

As night fell, the message came: **"You will stand trial at dawn."**

Through the temple window, I watched the silent masses below. Some wept. Others prayed.

And I? I saw only Dejah Thoris—lost, alone, prey to the horrors I had barely escaped.

Dawn would bring judgment.

But first, it would bring war.
