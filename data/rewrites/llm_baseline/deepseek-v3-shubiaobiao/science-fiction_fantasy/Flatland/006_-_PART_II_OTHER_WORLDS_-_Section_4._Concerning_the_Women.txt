If our Soldiers, with their sharp angles, are formidable, then our Women are infinitely more so. For where a Soldier is a wedge, a Woman is a needle—all point, piercing and precise. Add to this her ability to vanish at will, and you will understand why no sensible Flatlander dares provoke her.

But how, you may ask, does a Woman render herself invisible? The answer lies in perspective. Place a needle upon a table: viewed from the side, its full length is visible, but from the tip, it disappears into a mere glint. So it is with our Women. When she faces us, we see only a line; when she turns her eye—or mouth, for in Flatland these are one—toward us, she becomes nothing but a gleaming speck. And when she presents her back, she fades entirely, her dull edge blending into the surroundings like an Invisible Cloak.

The peril of Women is thus undeniable. A careless brush against a Soldier may maim, but to collide with a Woman is to invite instant annihilation. And when she chooses invisibility, how can even the most cautious among us evade her?

To mitigate this danger, our laws impose strict regulations:

1. **Separate Entrances**: Every household must have an Eastern door, reserved solely for Females, ensuring they enter with due decorum.
2. **The Peace-Cry**: No Woman may walk abroad without emitting a continuous warning signal—silence is punishable by death.
3. **Restrictions on the Unstable**: Any Female afflicted with involuntary motions—be it St. Vitus’s Dance or chronic sneezing—is to be eliminated without delay.

Some States go further, mandating that Women sway their backs rhythmically to signal their presence, or requiring them to be escorted by male kin. Yet excessive restrictions breed rebellion. Confined too harshly, Women turn their fury inward, and entire villages have been lost to their wrath. Thus, the wiser Circles enforce only the essential laws, trusting in self-preservation to temper their violence.

For though a Woman may kill with a mere backward lunge, her fragile form risks shattering in the act. Fashion, too, aids us—ladies of rank cultivate an elegant undulation, a mark of breeding that lesser classes mimic poorly. Among the highest orders, this motion is instinctive, sparing their households from unseen assaults.

Yet do not mistake our Women for mindless brutes. They are creatures of passion, governed by impulse, devoid of memory or foresight. A Woman may slaughter her family in a fit of rage, then moments later inquire after their whereabouts. In their chambers—carefully designed to limit movement—they are harmless, their threats as fleeting as their tempers.

Domestic harmony is a fragile thing. Among the lower classes, reckless husbands invite disaster, their blunt tongues provoking fatal reprisals. But the Circles, with diplomacy and well-timed promises, navigate these dangers with grace. Some even argue that the Women’s destructiveness serves a purpose, culling the reckless and preserving order.

Still, our households lack the warmth of yours. In noble homes, wives keep their eyes—and mouths—fixed ever upon their lords, for to turn away would be unthinkable. The Working Man’s wife enjoys more freedom, her silence a respite from the ceaseless chatter of the elite. Many a weary husband prefers the quiet menace of her sting to the relentless din of her speech.

To Spacelanders, the fate of our Women may seem cruel. Unlike the lowliest Isosceles, who may yet ascend in caste, a Woman is bound eternally to her nature. "Once a Woman, always a Woman," decrees the universe. Yet perhaps there is mercy in her forgetfulness—for she recalls neither her suffering nor her power long enough to revolt.

And so Flatland endures, balanced upon the edge of a needle.
