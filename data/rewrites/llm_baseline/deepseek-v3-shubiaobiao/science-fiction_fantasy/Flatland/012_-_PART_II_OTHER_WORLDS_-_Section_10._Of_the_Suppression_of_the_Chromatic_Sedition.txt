For three long years, the battle over the Universal Colour Bill raged, and for three years, chaos seemed poised to claim victory. Polygons, marching as common soldiers, were cut down by the sharper angles of the Isosceles Triangles, while the Squares and Pentagons watched in silence. But the true horror unfolded in the homes of the elite—wives, inflamed by political fervor, turned against their own. Some pleaded, others slaughtered, leaving twenty-three Circles dead by their hands. The Priests stood at the brink, forced to choose between surrender or annihilation.

Then came the spark that reshaped everything.

A lowly Isosceles—barely four degrees of intellect—stumbled into the colors of a plundered shop and daubed himself in the twelve hues of a Dodecagon. Disguised, he deceived a noble Polygon’s daughter, a woman who had once scorned him. Through luck and the blind negligence of her kin, the fraud was sealed in marriage. When the truth shattered her, she took her own life.

The tragedy rippled through Flatland. Women, once fervent for the Bill, now saw its dangers. What if their daughters were next? What if deception became the law of the land? The Circles seized their moment. They called an Assembly, packing the hall with guards and reactionary Women. Before a seething crowd of Isosceles, the Chief Circle, Pantocyclus, raised his voice—not in defiance, but in cunning concession. He would accept the Colour Bill.

The roar of fury became cheers.

Then came the trap.

With flawless rhetoric, Pantocyclus spun a vision of ruin: tradesmen beggared, artisans stripped of their hopes, Women preyed upon by any rogue with a brush. The Workmen wavered. The Women shuddered. And when Chromatistes, leader of the rebellion, stepped forward to rally them, the guards closed in.

The signal was given.

The Isosceles Convicts struck first, impaling Chromatistes. The Women, guided by the Circles, moved unseen, their deadly precision unmatched. The Artisans stood aside. Every exit was barred. What followed was not a battle, but a massacre. The Isosceles, trapped and leaderless, turned on each other in panic. Within minutes, the rebellion was a field of corpses.

The Circles did not stop there.

The Working Men were spared—but not all. The Equilateral Militia purged every suspect Triangle. Homes were inspected, towns scoured, the excess of the lower classes trimmed like overgrown hedges. Colour was erased from Flatland. To speak of it was a crime. Only in the highest, most secret chambers of the University does it linger—a tool for minds advanced enough to wield it without folly.

And its making? That knowledge is held by one alone: the Chief Circle. The factory that produces it is its workers’ tomb. Each year, they are replaced, lest the secret escape.

Even now, the aristocracy trembles at the memory of those days. For they know—the line between order and chaos is as thin as the edge of a blade.
