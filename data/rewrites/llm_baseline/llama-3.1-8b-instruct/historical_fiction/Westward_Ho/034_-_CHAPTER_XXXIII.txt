As the first of October dawned, a sense of somber calm settled over Appledore, like a gentle blanket cast over the village. The sky, a canvas of soft gray autumnal hues, seemed to echo the stillness that had fallen over the world after the tumultuous summer months of battle and storm. The Vengeance, her flag flying at half-mast, slipped silently into the harbor, her anchor dropping softly onto the sandy bottom as she came to rest. On board, the body of Salvation Yeo lay at peace, a testament to the bond between him and Amyas Leigh.

Cary and Brimblecombe assisted Amyas from the boat, leading him slowly up the hill towards his home, as a crowd gathered around him, their faces etched with a mix of sadness and adoration. They knew well the tale of his journey, and the price he had paid for his loyalty. "Let me pass quietly," Amyas implored, "without fanfare or spectacle. I wish to lay my friend to rest with dignity, without drawing attention to myself."

As he spoke, a second boat appeared on the horizon, its English flag billowing in the breeze, bearing the body of Salvation Yeo. The villagers, moved by Amyas's words, chose to follow him in silence, their footsteps echoing through the stillness as they made their way to the church. A messenger had already been sent to inform Mrs. Leigh of her son's return, and the preparations for the burial were underway.

In the quiet of her home, Mrs. Leigh sat with Ayacanora, her eyes fixed on the prayer book as she read the burial service. "Ayacanora, child," she said softly, "do you not feel a pang of sorrow for Master Yeo, who risked his life to save you from the jaws of the crocodile?" Ayacanora looked down, her thoughts consumed by memories of Amyas, and not the deceased.

The burial service concluded, the parson stepped back, and the crowd lingered, drawn to the simple wooden coffin. Amyas stood at its head, his eyes fixed on the sea, the wind rustling through the nearby tower, a beacon that watched over the land and the waves. As the villagers gazed upon the grave, a sense of expectation hung in the air, as if they sensed that something more was to come. And indeed, it did.
