The darkness of the sea seemed to seep into every crevice of my being as I rowed through the choppy waters, the rhythmic splashing of the waves against the hull a melancholy accompaniment to my thoughts. Phorenice sat beside me, her gaze fixed intently on some point beyond the horizon, her profile illuminated by the fading light of the sun. I couldn't help but steal glances at her, her features still etched in my mind like a melody that refused to fade. The incident on the beach of the fishers still lingered, a reminder of her skills with a sword and her...other qualities.

As I rowed, I found myself comparing her to Nais, a comparison that felt like a betrayal. Phorenice, with her boldness and confidence, seemed a far cry from the more reserved Nais, who had always approached life with a quiet dignity. Yet, I pushed such thoughts aside, bound by my vow as a priest and the high council's commands. My duty was to serve the people of Atlantis, and I would not let personal feelings cloud my judgment.

The sea air filled my lungs as I prayed for the power of speech to return, for the sake of the people I sought to serve and for Phorenice's own sake. The gods, in their benevolence, granted me the gift of eloquence, and Phorenice's eyes sparkled with pleasure as I spoke. The chain of jagged rocks guarding the harbour loomed ahead, a barrier between us and the open sea. I had neither the knowledge nor the skill to navigate these treacherous waters, but the gods, it seemed, had other plans.

A great wave rose from the depths, its crest blown off in a misty veil, and we were swept forward, carried clear of the reefs by its force. The seaweed clinging to the rocks seemed to lick at our hull, and I bowed my head in gratitude as I rowed on towards the harbour forts. It was clear that the gods were not yet ready to exact vengeance for Phorenice's transgressions, and I could only wonder what the future held for us both. The sentries in the forts began to beat their drums, their rhythm a monotone accompaniment to the ebbing tide. As the dusk deepened, they paid scant attention to our arrival, their focus on the familiar routine of their duties.
