As the scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, Catherine summoned Eli to the hearth, her eyes aglow with an intensity that belied the warmth of the fire crackling within. "Eli," she said, her voice low and deliberate, "answer me a question like a man, and I'll ask no more today. What is wormwood?" The question hung in the air, heavy with anticipation, as Eli hesitated, his rugged features twisted in a mixture of confusion and defiance. But as the moments ticked by, he found his footing, and with a hint of a smile, replied, "It's something that tastes main bitter." Catherine's expression remained inscrutable, her eyes never leaving his face, as she prodded him further. "That's a fair answer, my man, but not the one I look for."

The air was thick with tension as Eli struggled to meet her gaze, his words tumbling forth in a hasty attempt to placate her. "Then answer it yourself," he stammered. Catherine's lips curled into a faint smile as she began to weave a tale of two souls, adrift in a world that had lost its way. "Wormwood is – to have two in the house, a-doing nought, but waiting for thy shoes and mine," she said, her voice dripping with a subtle irony. The words struck Eli like a blow, laying bare the emptiness that had come to define their lives. As the silence between them deepened, Catherine's words hung in the air like a challenge, an invitation to reclaim their lost purpose.

Eli's response was laced with a mix of trepidation and resignation. "I'll help, but I'm afraid they'll drink and gamble their very shelves away." Catherine's gaze never wavered, her eyes burning with a fierce determination. "Nay, I don't take you for a simpleton, Eli. Of course, I mean to watch them at starting, and drive them with a loose rein, as the saying is." Her words dripped with a subtle promise, a vow to guide and protect those who had been left to wander in the wilderness. As the conversation unfolded, it became clear that Catherine's plan was not merely to revitalize the pair, but to use them as a means to an end – to draw near the one person she truly cared for, little Gerard.
