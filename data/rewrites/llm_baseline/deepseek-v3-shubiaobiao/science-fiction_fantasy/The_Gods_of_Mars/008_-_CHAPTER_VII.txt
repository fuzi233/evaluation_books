The black pirate and I locked eyes in a frozen instant, two predators sizing each other up. Then his lips twisted into a savage grin as his ebony hand emerged from the deck’s edge, the muzzle of his revolver pressing cold against my forehead.

I moved faster than thought. My free hand shot out, clamping around his throat like a vice. His hiss—*"Die, cursed thern"*—turned to a choked gurgle as my fingers crushed his windpipe. The hammer fell on an empty chamber.

Before he could react, I wrenched him forward, forcing him to release the rail or be dragged over. His face purpled, eyes bulging. Desperate, he thrashed—then made his fatal mistake. He threw himself backward, clawing at my grip.

That was all the opening I needed.

With a heave, I sent him spinning into the void. His weight nearly tore me after him, but I clung to the anchor chain, watching his silhouette vanish into the abyss below. No scream escaped him. Good.

---

I hauled myself onto the deck, scanning the scene under the glow of the farther moon. Six or eight black warriors sprawled in sleep, their forms slack. Near a rapid-fire gun, bound and wide-eyed, a young woman stared at me—not with fear, but with sudden, blazing hope.

Her gaze flicked to the jewel in my stolen headpiece. She didn’t speak. A slight tilt of her chin warned me: *Beware the sleepers.*

I crept to her side. As I bent close, her whisper was a blade in the silence: *"Free me. I’ll fight with you."*

*"Some won’t wake at all,"* I murmured, slicing her bonds.

Her answering smile was a razor’s edge. Beautiful, yes—but beauty honed to cruelty. She held out a hand. *"A revolver. I’ll cover what your sword misses."*

I armed her and turned to my grim work.

---

The first two never woke. The third’s dying shriek tore through the night.

Then chaos erupted.

Five pirates surged to their feet. Phaidor’s revolver barked—one dropped. The rest charged me, blades flashing. Steel rang against steel, sparks flying in the dark. I fought like a cornered wolf, parrying, striking, driving my sword through a chest until the hilt lodged in bone.

Disarmed, I faced the last—a grinning brute who thought fists would finish me.

He learned otherwise.

My right hook snapped his head back. He crumpled.

Behind me, Phaidor laughed—a sound like silver dipped in venom. *"You’re no thern,"* she said. *"No Barsoomian fights like that. Who are you?"*

*"John Carter, Prince of Helium."* I eyed her. *"And you?"*

She paused, weighing me. *"Phaidor. Daughter of Matai Shang, Holy Hekkador of the therns."*

A groan interrupted us. The black pirate I’d knocked out stirred. I bound him swiftly.

*"Why not kill him?"* Phaidor tilted her head, as if asking why I’d spare a scrap of food.

*"I’m no executioner,"* I said.

She frowned, uncomprehending.

---

The bound man—Xodar, as he named himself—watched us with a smirk. When Phaidor demanded his origin, his pride flared. *"Xodar, Dator of the First Born. We are the oldest race, the true immortals."*

He spun a tale of Barsoom’s dawn: of the Tree of Life, of plant men and ancient wars. But his eyes betrayed him—darting past me, just once.

I whirled.

A battleship loomed in our wake, silent and deadly, closing fast.

The game had changed again.
