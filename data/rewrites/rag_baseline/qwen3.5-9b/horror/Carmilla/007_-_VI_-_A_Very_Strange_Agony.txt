When we finally retreated to the drawing room, the heavy oak doors sealing us away from the chaotic world below, the atmosphere shifted with a deceptive ease. We settled onto plush sofas, the air thick with the aroma of roasted coffee and bitter chocolate. Though Carmilla declined her share, sipping only the steam rising from her untouched cup, she seemed to have shed the spectral pallor that had clung to her earlier. Soon, Madame and Mademoiselle De Lafontaine joined our circle, their laughter weaving a fragile tapestry of normalcy. Papa entered shortly thereafter, claiming his customary "dish of tea," a ritualistic pause in the evening's flow. Once the cards were stacked and the game concluded, he took his seat beside Carmilla on the sofa, his brow furrowed with a paternal anxiety that betrayed his composure.

"You have heard nothing from your mother since your arrival?" he asked, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper.

"No," she replied, her tone ambiguous, drifting somewhere between evasion and truth. "But I have been contemplating departure. You have already extended hospitality beyond measure; your kindness borders on overwhelming. I have caused you infinite trouble, and I believe it prudent to depart tomorrow. I intend to hire a carriage and pursue her trail immediately. I know where she lies hidden, though I dare not reveal the path to you yet."

"But you must not entertain such notions!" my father exclaimed, the relief washing over his features palpable. "We cannot afford to lose you. I refuse to consent to your leaving unless it is under the direct supervision of your mother, who graciously agreed to keep you company until her own return. While knowing of her correspondence would bring me comfort, the reports regarding the mysterious plague ravaging our neighborhood grow increasingly harrowing tonight. As your guardian, unaided by maternal counsel, I feel a crushing weight of responsibility. Yet, rest assured, I shall do my utmost. One thing remains absolute: you shall not leave this sanctuary without her explicit directive. We would suffer too grievously to permit such a separation willingly."

"Thank you, sir, a thousand times for your generosity," she answered, her cheeks flushing with a bashful smile. "You have all treated me with a kindness I have rarely encountered in my entire life. Here, within your magnificent château, under your watchful care, surrounded by the warmth of your beloved daughter, I have known a happiness previously unknown to me."

With a gallantry rooted in old-world tradition, he kissed her hand, his eyes crinkling with satisfaction at her effusive praise. Later, I escorted Carmilla to her chambers as was our custom, lingering to chat while she prepared for slumber.

"Do you truly believe," I ventured eventually, breaking the comfortable silence, "that you will ever confide fully in me?"

She turned, a radiant smile playing on her lips, yet offered no verbal response, allowing the expression to linger in the dim light.

"You offer no answer?" I pressed gently. "Perhaps the question was ill-timed; I ought not to have asked."

"You were entirely justified in asking," she corrected softly, her voice taking on a haunting resonance. "Or rather, in asking anything. You possess no conception of how deeply cherished you are to me, nor could you possibly imagine a confidence too profound to seek. However, I am bound by vows—more severe than those of any nun—and I dare not recount my tale, even to you, not yet. The moment approaches swiftly when you shall know every detail. You may deem me cruel, excessively selfish; yet love is inherently selfish. The more ardent the flame, the greater the consumption. How jealous I am, you cannot comprehend. You must accompany me, loving me, unto death; or else hate me, and still follow me through the grave and beyond. There exists no concept of indifference within my apathetic nature."

"Now, Carmilla, you are descending into your wild nonsense once more," I interrupted hastily, sensing the danger in her words.

"Not I, silly little fool that I am, brimming with whims and capricious fancies," she countered, her demeanor shifting instantly to playful innocence. "For your sake alone, I shall discourse like a sage. Have you ever attended a ball?"

"No," I admitted. "How does it fare? How enchanting must it be?"

"I almost forget; it was years ago."

"You are not so aged," I laughed, dismissing the notion. "Your first ball is scarcely etched from memory yet."

"I recall every facet of that night—with considerable effort. I perceive it all as divers observe the surface world above them, through a medium dense, rippling, yet transparent. That evening, an event occurred that obscured the image, fading its vibrant hues. I was nearly assassinated in my bed, wounded here," she pressed a finger tenderly against her breast, "and I have never been the same since."

"Were you near death?"

"Yes, profoundly. A cruel love—a strange affection that sought to extinguish my life. Love demands its sacrifices. No sacrifice occurs without blood."

"Let us retire now," she whispered, her energy seemingly drained. "I feel an overwhelming lethargy. How can I summon the strength to rise and secure my door just now?"

She lay back, burying her tiny hands in her rich, wavy hair beneath her cheek, her small head resting upon the pillow. Her glittering eyes tracked my every movement with a shy, enigmatic smile that defied interpretation. I bid her goodnight and slipped from the room, carrying with me an unsettling chill. I often pondered whether our lovely guest ever uttered a prayer. I had never witnessed her upon her knees. In the mornings, she descended only after our family devotions had concluded; at night, she remained sequestered in the drawing room, absent from our brief evening prayers in the hall. Had it not been for the casual revelation during a momentary lapse in conversation that she had indeed been baptized, I would have questioned her faith entirely. Religion was a topic she never broached. Had I possessed deeper worldly wisdom, this specific neglect—or perhaps active antipathy—might not have so startled me.

The precautions of the neurotic are contagious. Persons of similar temperament inevitably mimic them after a time. I had adopted Carmilla's habit of locking my bedroom door, succumbing to her whimsical alarms regarding midnight intruders and prowling assassins. I had also embraced her ritual of conducting a brief, frantic search of my quarters to ensure no lurking thief or murderer was "ensconced" within the shadows. Having fortified myself with these wise measures, I retired to my bed and surrendered to sleep.

A solitary candle burned in my room, a relic of an ancient habit dating back to my earliest days, a vigilance nothing could tempt me to abandon. Thus fortified, I believed I could rest in peace. Yet, dreams traverse stone walls; they illuminate dark chambers or shroud bright ones, and their inhabitants enter and exit at will, mocking the efforts of locksmiths.

That night, I experienced a dream that marked the genesis of a very strange agony. I cannot classify it as a nightmare, for I remained acutely conscious of my state of sleep. Simultaneously, I was hyper-aware of my location: in my room, lying in bed, precisely as I physically was. I perceived, or perhaps imagined perceiving, the room and its furnishings exactly as I had seen them moments prior, save for the encroaching darkness. At the foot of the bed, something moved, indistinct at first. Soon, clarity returned: it was a soot-black creature resembling a monstrous cat. It appeared to span four or five feet in length, measuring fully the expanse of the hearthrug as it traversed it. It continued to pace back and forth with the lithe, sinister restlessness of a beast trapped in a cage.

I could not cry out, though terror seized my throat. Its pace accelerated, and the room plunged into a darkness so absolute that I could discern nothing but its eyes. Suddenly, I felt it spring lightly onto the bed. Two broad eyes approached my face, and instantaneously, a stinging pain pierced my chest, as if two large needles had darted, an inch or two apart, deep into my heart.

I woke with a scream.

The room was illuminated by the candle that had burned throughout the night. Standing at the foot of the bed, slightly to the right, stood a female figure. She wore a dark, loose gown, her hair cascading down to cover her shoulders. She was as motionless as a block of stone. There was not the slightest stir of respiration. As I stared, transfixed, the figure seemed to shift position, moving closer to the door. Then, the door creaked open, and she stepped out into the corridor.

Relief flooded my lungs, restoring my ability to breathe and move. My immediate thought was that Carmilla had played a prank upon me, forgetting to secure the latch. I rushed to the door, only to find it locked securely from the inside as usual. Fear, cold and paralyzing, gripped me. I was horrified. I threw myself back into the bed, pulling the covers over my head, lying there more dead than alive until the dawn broke.
