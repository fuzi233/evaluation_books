As Gerard rode towards Sevenbergen, the sun cast a warm glow on the rolling hills and verdant fields. The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, enticing him to follow its source. His stomach growled in anticipation of a hearty meal, and he wondered what culinary delights awaited him at his destination. The thought of the Countess's cook, known for her delectable stews, brought a smile to his face.

Upon arriving at Peter Brandt's cottage, Gerard dismounted and knocked on the door. A young woman, Margaret, answered, her fingers stained with ink from sewing. A rugged figure, an elderly soldier, sat beside her, engaged in conversation. Gerard's eyes narrowed, recognizing the man as one he had seen shooting at the butts with remarkable accuracy. The youth felt an inexplicable pang, but his attention soon turned to Margaret.

As he entered, the girl's gaze met his, and her face flushed with a mixture of emotions. She hastily concealed her feelings, adopting a detached tone. "What brings you here, Master Gerard?" she asked, her voice steady.

Gerard explained his visit, asking after her father, and Margaret replied that he would return shortly. As they waited, Gerard's eyes roamed the room, taking in the rustic furnishings and the tantalizing aroma of baking bread. His stomach growled louder, and he spotted a tray of freshly baked pastries on the table.

Margaret noticed his interest and smiled, her expression softening. "Would you like one?" she offered, breaking off a warm pastry and handing it to him. Gerard accepted, savoring the flaky crust and sweet filling.

Their conversation flowed easily, and Gerard soon discovered the source of their earlier misunderstanding. As they talked, the tension between them dissipated, replaced by a sense of shared understanding. Gerard's eyes wandered to the open book on the table – a worn manuscript of the Vulgate – and his heart skipped a beat. He reached for it, but Margaret's hands covered the page, her face reddening.

Gerard chuckled, his eyes sparkling with mirth. "Let me see," he said, his voice low and persuasive. "I'll read it with you, and our souls will meet in the sacred page." Margaret's hands remained steadfast, and Gerard, undeterred, leaned forward, his lips brushing against her fingers.
