As the family gathered for dinner, Madame de Bernstein's demeanor had undergone a remarkable transformation. Gone was the stern countenance from earlier that morning; instead, her face shone with warmth and kindness as she greeted each guest, including Harry. She lavished praise on the cook, declaring the fricassee to be exceptional and the eels, sourced from the Castlewood moats, to be among the finest she had ever tasted. Her toast to Madam Esmond Warrington of Virginia was sincere and heartfelt, and she even inquired about Harry's prospects of returning with an English bride. Though she did not recall his grandmother, her own mother's stories of her mother's beauty and happiness in her second marriage captivated the assembly. According to Madame de Bernstein, her mother's union with Colonel Esmond was a testament to the power of love transcending age and societal norms. 

The Baroness's effervescent mood spread throughout the table, infecting each member of the family with her infectious laughter and warm words. Even My Lady Castlewood and her children couldn't help but be drawn into the Baroness's charming conversation. Harry listened with rapt attention, while Maria looked on with a mix of surprise and delight. Lord Castlewood, however, seemed puzzled by his aunt's sudden change in behavior, yet he wisely chose to keep his thoughts to himself, maintaining a mask of contentment.

As the evening wore on, Madame de Bernstein regaled the company with stories of her childhood, recalling the times she would try to win over her parents' affection after a particularly trying day. It was a habit she claimed to have retained to this day, and she confessed to being just as stubborn and argumentative as she was at seven years old. The chaplain, present at the table, absolved her of her past transgressions, and the Baroness thanked him with a smile. Turning to Maria, she apologized for her thoughtless words earlier, claiming to have been tired and irritable. Maria, however, reassured her that she had done nothing to offend, and Madame de Bernstein implored her to join her on a trip to Tunbridge Wells, promising that her presence would bring her immense joy. The Baroness's words hung in the air, leaving Lord Castlewood to ponder the true motivations behind her sudden change of heart.
