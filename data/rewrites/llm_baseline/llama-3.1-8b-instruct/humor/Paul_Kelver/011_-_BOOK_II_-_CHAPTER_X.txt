The aroma of freshly baked bread wafted through the small, dimly lit kitchen, mingling with the scent of roasting coffee as my mother busied herself preparing breakfast. My stomach growled in anticipation as I watched her expertly chop vegetables for the morning's omelette. It was my father's birthday, and we had been expecting him to rise early, as he always did, to join us for breakfast. But as the minutes ticked by, and the sun rose higher in the sky, a sense of unease settled over us.

"I'll go check on him," my mother said, slipping out of the kitchen in her worn slippers. I followed her, my footsteps echoing on the creaky stairs. She pushed open the bedroom door, her eyes scanning the room as if searching for something out of place. I peered over her shoulder, taking in the familiar sight of my father's bed, the blankets rumpled and still. My mother's hand fluttered to her mouth, and she stepped inside, closing the door behind us.

The room was quiet, the blinds still drawn, casting the space in a soft, golden light. Outside, the sounds of the city began to stir – a vendor's call, a horse's whinny, the clanging of pots in a nearby shop. My mother stood motionless, her ear pressed to the door, listening. I watched, my heart beating slowly, as she seemed to absorb the sounds, her expression unreadable.

As we stood there, a faint scent of watercreases wafted in through the open window, mingling with the air. My mother's grip on the doorframe tightened, and I sensed a change in her, a subtle shift in her posture. She turned to me, her eyes clouded, and I felt a shiver run down my spine. It was then that I noticed the stillness, the complete silence that filled the room. My father lay motionless, his chest not rising, his lips not moving. It took a moment for the truth to sink in – he was gone.

Days blended together in a haze of grief, as if the world outside had slowed to a crawl. I would catch glimpses of him everywhere – in the crowd, in the market, in the faces of passersby – only to turn and find that it was just a stranger.
