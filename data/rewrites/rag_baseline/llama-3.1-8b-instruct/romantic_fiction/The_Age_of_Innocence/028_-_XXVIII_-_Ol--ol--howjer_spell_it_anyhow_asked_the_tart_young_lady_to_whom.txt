The afternoon sunlight struggled to penetrate the dusty windows of the telegraph office, casting a faint glow on the polished brass ledge where Newland Archer's wife had left a message. Archer stood before the counter, his eyes fixed on the scribbled words as he hesitated over the unfamiliar name. "Olenska—O-len-ska," he murmured to himself, trying to capture the rhythm of the foreign syllables. The sound seemed to linger in the air, a haunting echo of a language he barely knew.

Behind him, a low, smooth voice cut through the silence. "Unusual name for a New York telegraph office, don't you think?" Archer turned to see Lawrence Lefferts standing beside him, his moustache twitching with a mixture of amusement and curiosity. Archer felt a spark of annoyance at the interruption, but his gaze drifted back to the message, his thoughts consumed by the memory of the Countess Olenska.

Lefferts' presence was like a jarring note in the stillness of the room. His words hung in the air, a polite inquiry about old Mrs. Mingott's health, but Archer's mind was elsewhere. He had received news of the Countess's illness, and the pain of it was still raw. He paid for his telegram, the transaction a mechanical ritual, and stepped out into the street alongside Lefferts.

As they walked, the city seemed to close in around them, its rhythms and cadences a distant hum. Lefferts spoke of the Beaufort scandal, his words a litany of shock and dismay, but Archer's attention wandered. He thought of the Countess, her name a refrain that echoed through his mind like a melody. The streets seemed to grow darker, the shadows deepening as the sun dipped lower in the sky.

The Beaufort failure dominated the headlines, its implications a stark reminder of the fragility of the social order. Old Catherine Mingott's stroke was relegated to a secondary concern, a minor footnote in the larger narrative of scandal and deceit. Archer felt a sense of disconnection, as if he was observing the world around him from a distance, his emotions numb and detached.

As he walked, the sounds of the city receded, and he found himself lost in thought. The name Olenska continued to haunt him, a whispered promise of something elusive and unattainable. The world outside seemed to fade away, leaving only the quiet, insistent beat of his own heart.
