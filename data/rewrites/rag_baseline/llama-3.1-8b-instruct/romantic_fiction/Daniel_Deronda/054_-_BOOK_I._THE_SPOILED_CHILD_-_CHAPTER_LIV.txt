The sun beat down upon the waves, a relentless drumbeat that mirrored the turmoil brewing within Gwendolen's heart. She stood at the rail of the yacht, her fingers grasping the worn wood as if searching for an anchor in the choppy waters. The turquoise expanse of the Mediterranean stretched out before her, a seemingly endless canvas of freedom that felt like a prison to her spirit. Below, her husband, Grandcourt, lounged in a deck chair, his eyes narrowed against the glare, his expression a mask of tranquil contentment.

As she gazed out at the sea, Gwendolen's thoughts turned to the events that had led her to this isolated outpost. She had married Grandcourt for security and luxury, not love, and now she felt trapped by her own compromises. The contract she had signed was a Faustian bargain, one she had thought she could live with, but the weight of her conscience was slowly crushing her. The process of purgatory had indeed begun for her, and it was not just the husband she was trying to escape.

Grandcourt, however, saw their situation differently. He believed he had fulfilled his obligations, providing her with the luxuries she desired, and now he expected her to fulfill hers – to be his wife, his companion, his possession. But Gwendolen's growing sense of unease was not about her duties or responsibilities; it was about the emptiness within herself, the feeling that she had sold her soul for a life of comfort and status.

As the yacht sliced through the waves, Gwendolen's mind turned to Deronda, the young man whose presence had set off a chain reaction of events that had brought her to this isolated, idyllic hell. She felt a flutter in her chest at the memory of his eyes, his smile, and the way he had listened to her with a depth of understanding that made her feel seen. It was a feeling she dared not acknowledge, not here, not now, but it was a thread that had been woven into the tapestry of her thoughts, refusing to be ignored.

In the distance, the island of Corsica rose up, a rugged sentinel guarding the secrets of the past. Gwendolen's thoughts drifted to the stories of old, to the tales of women who had been cast aside, abandoned on the winds of fate. She felt a kinship with them, a solidarity born of shared experience, and it was this sense of connection that gave her the courage to confront the truth: she was not alone
