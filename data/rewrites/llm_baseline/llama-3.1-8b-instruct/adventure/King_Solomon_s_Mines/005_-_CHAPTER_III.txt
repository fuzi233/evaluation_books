The memories of our voyage still lingered in the air like the sweet scent of the Natal coast, as we steamed past the shoreline, bathed in the golden light of the January sun. It had taken us four to five days to reach this point, the pace governed by the wind and the currents. I spent those days lost in thought, pondering Sir Henry Curtis's proposal, weighing the possibilities and the risks. We'd spoken little of it since our initial conversation, though I regaled them with tales of my hunting exploits, stories that needed no embellishment, for the truth is often stranger than fiction.

As we navigated the coastline, the landscape unfolded before us like a canvas of contrasts. Red sandhills gave way to emerald expanses of bush, dotted with the traditional Kraal villages of the native tribes. The rhythmic pounding of the surf against the rocks created a soothing melody, punctuated by the occasional cry of a seagull. Yet, as we approached the area around Durban, the scenery took on a more vibrant hue. The sheer kloofs carved by centuries of torrential rains glistened like polished jewels, their waters reflecting the colors of the surrounding foliage. The air was alive with the sweet scent of ripening mealies and sugar cane, and the occasional white house stood sentinel, a testament to the presence of humanity in this otherwise wild tapestry.

We had miscalculated our arrival time, and as the sun dipped below the horizon, we anchored off the point, listening to the distant boom of the lighthouse and the cheers of the people of Durban as they welcomed the English Mail. With the night at hand, we retired to dinner, our bellies full and our spirits high. The next morning, the moon cast its silvery glow over the water, illuminating the bustling activity on the shore. The lighthouse flashed its warning, and the brig nearby joined in the symphony of sounds, its sailors singing as they worked to raise the anchor, eager to set sail for their next destination. It was then that I spotted him – Umbopa, the enigmatic servant of Sir Henry Curtis, standing on the dock, his eyes fixed intently on our vessel. His presence seemed to stir something within me, a sense of foreboding that I couldn't quite shake, like the gentle lapping of the waves against the shore.
