As the days went by, Major Pendennis's visits to Grosvenor Place became a regular occurrence. His warm camaraderie with the Clavering family continued to deepen, much to the delight of Lady Clavering. The old gentleman's connections and influence proved invaluable in securing invitations to the most exclusive social events in town. He proposed that Lady Clavering join him at the grand breakfast ball at Viscount Marrowfat's estate in Fulham, where a lavish Watteau quadrille was to be held, and promised to make himself available to escort her. He also extended his offer to assist Sir Francis in any way that would be agreeable to him.

Despite Clavering's noble lineage and substantial fortune, whispers of scandal continued to surround him. He faced rejection at two prestigious clubs and found himself shunned by the upper echelons of society. His interactions within the House of Commons were limited to a select few, and even among those, he was known to associate with questionable characters. Among them was Captain Raff, a former member for Epsom who had recently accepted a diplomatic posting to the Levant, and Hustingson, a vocal advocate for reform who had been appointed Governor of Coventry Island. There was also Bob Freeny, a skilled marksman and scion of the esteemed Booterstown Freenys. Of all these individuals, however, Major Pendennis harbored a particular disdain for Sir Francis Claverring, the representative for their ancestral borough, whom he deemed to be lacking in refinement and morals.

"He's a man who would sooner spend his inheritance on a rigged game than invest in a sound venture," Major Pendennis said, shaking his head. "He's not a man to be trusted, but then again, who among us hasn't made mistakes in our youth? Perhaps it's his education – or lack thereof – that's led him astray. The Fleet and Newgate are no places for a man of his standing, but I dare say he'll learn from his experiences." The Major's words betrayed a hint of understanding, and it was clear that he saw something in Clavering that others did not – a potential for redemption, perhaps, or a hidden depth that lay beneath the surface.
