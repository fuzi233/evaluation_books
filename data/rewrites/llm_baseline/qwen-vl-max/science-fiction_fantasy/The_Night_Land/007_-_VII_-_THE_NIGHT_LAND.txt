I moved steadily north and west, my path cautious and deliberate, as I navigated the vast, unrelenting darkness of the Night Land. My mind was fixed on survival—on every decision that could mean life or death in this desolate realm. The first thing I considered was pace. I knew I couldn’t rush; the journey ahead was immense, and no one could predict its end. A measured, steady rhythm would preserve my strength for when it was truly needed.

So I set a strict routine: eat and drink every six hours, sleep from the eighteenth to the twenty-fourth hour. That gave me three meals and six hours of rest each day. It wasn’t much, but it was enough to keep my body functioning under the crushing weight of endless night. This became my law, my anchor in a world without time or mercy.

But even rules are broken in desperation. For over twenty-one hours, I walked without pause—hiding, creeping, avoiding any trace of movement that might draw the attention of the Great Watcher of the North-West. Hunger gnawed at me, but the thought of food turned sour in my throat. I pushed forward, driven by instinct more than reason.

When exhaustion finally seized me, I collapsed near a small fire-hole—a flickering beacon of warmth in the cold void. It was just a few paces wide, filled with dull, smoldering embers that hissed faintly and released a thin, acrid smoke. I sat beside it, placing the Diskos carefully on the rock beside me. For a long moment, I did nothing but stare into the distance, my eyes drawn back to the distant silhouette of the Mighty Pyramid.

It loomed impossibly close, though I had traveled far. Its size made it seem to press upon me, both comforting and terrifying. I felt the presence of millions watching from within—silent guardians of the Redoubt, their thoughts brushing against the aether like whispers across still water. I didn’t wave, didn’t speak. There was no farewell to be made. Only departure. And yet, knowing the Master Monstruwacan might see me through the Great Spy-Glass, I felt a quiet joy. Perhaps he saw me now, sitting beneath the stars of the dead sky.

I forced myself to eat. Three tablets from my scrip—compact, nutrient-rich blocks that sustained life in minimal bulk. They were tasteless, unsatisfying, but they fed me. Then came the water: I shook a fine dust into a cup. As air touched it, the powder fizzed and boiled, rising into a clear liquid—pure, simple water born of chemistry and necessity. It was strange to witness, but familiar now, like breathing in this cursed land.

Afterward, I checked my gear. Besides the Diskos and food, I carried a pouch with various tools. Among them was a compass—crafted by the Master Monstruwacan himself, a relic from an ancient museum, reworked with skill and care. He had told me it might still respond to Earth-Currents, even if it no longer pointed north. If I wandered too far, if the Pyramid vanished behind the dark, perhaps this device would still guide me home.

I placed it on the ground. But it spun wildly, wavering without direction. I understood why—the Underground Fields beneath the earth interfered with its workings. I was still near the “Crack,” though high above it. Still, I wondered if the Master watched. The fire-hole’s glow was bright enough, and the Spy-Glass powerful. But I knew better than to trust sight alone. Light played tricks here—flickering, shifting, revealing nothing or everything in sudden bursts. Smoke rose unpredictably from fissures in the earth, blurring vision, distorting reality.

Eventually, I tucked the compass away and prepared to sleep. I wrapped myself in my cloak, lay down beside the fire-hole, and placed the Diskos beside me, nestled in the fabric. It was more than a weapon—it was a companion, a silent ally in the silence. As drowsiness took hold, I sensed a subtle vibration in the air, a hum beneath the surface of things. I believed, then, that the millions within the Redoubt were aware of me—watching, feeling, sharing my moment of surrender to sleep. Their collective empathy stirred the aether around me, a warm current in the cold night.

And so I slept—deeply, heavily, worn out by days of travel and fear. My last conscious thought was of her—the woman I sought, the one who had become a dream, a hope, a reason to endure.

Then—**a sound.**

Not a whisper, not a sigh, but a roar that tore through the silence. The Home-Call. The voice of the Redoubt, echoing across the Night Land, summoning all to awareness. I snapped awake, cloak slipping off, Diskos gripped tight in my hand.

I looked toward the Pyramid, searching for a signal. Something terrible had happened. The call wasn’t used lightly. Only dire threats warranted such a wake-up. A predator was near. A monster.

As I stared, I couldn’t keep my gaze fixed on the tower. Fear pulled my eyes outward—scanning the blackness. Nothing. But then, high above, flashes of green fire erupted in the sky—swift, sharp, coded messages in the Set-Speech.

They spelled it clearly: *A Grey Man has found you. It is crawling through the moss beyond the fire-hole. Hide to your left. Take advantage.*

Before the warning faded, I was already moving—darting into a clump of moss-bushes, heart pounding, breath ragged. I crouched, trembling, but focused. The air smelled of sulfur and damp stone.

Then—movement.

Something emerged from the shadows beyond the fire-hole. Tall, broad, entirely grey. It crept low, head pressed to the ground, scanning with slow, brutal precision. The fire disturbed it. It hesitated, sniffing, peering along the earth as if reading invisible signs. Then it retreated—only to reappear again, circling the fire-hole, testing the perimeter.

Three times it came from the left. Three times from the right. Each time, it lowered its head, hunched its shoulders, thrust its jaw forward in a grotesque display of predatory focus. I knew what it meant—each pass was a probe, a test. And I feared it had seen me.

But then—within my mind, a voice spoke.

I froze. Not a memory. Not imagination. A message. From the Master Monstruwacan. Sent through brain-elements, reaching me via the Night-Hearing I possessed. And with it came the deep, resonant throb of the Master-Word—a seal of truth, a confirmation.

I leapt—not to flee, but to attack.

I burst from my hiding place and struck. The Diskos swung in a single, perfect arc. The Grey Man fell sideways, legs twitching, head trapped in the bushes where I’d been. It died quickly. The Diskos spun in my hand, glowing with inner fire, as if alive, as if proud.

I stepped back, watching it die. Then I raised the Diskos high, spinning it, sending sparks into the night. A signal. Proof I had survived. Proof I had killed.

The Master did not reply. No need. Such contact was dangerous. Powers in the Night Land listened. To speak again might bring more danger.

But I felt the aether stir—warm, alive. The millions within the Redoubt rejoiced. Their hearts beat in sympathy. I was not alone. I was watched. I was loved.

I sat then, catching my breath, checking the time. Ten hours had passed. I scolded myself. I had broken my own rule. Sleep had overtaken me, uncontrolled. I vowed to obey discipline henceforth—eat, rest, move—by the clock of reason.

I gathered my things, stood, and looked once more at the Pyramid. I did not salute. I did not linger. I turned away.

And I walked into the night—swift, cautious, the Diskos held close, humming with power. It felt alive. It felt *mine*. Not just a tool, but a partner. A bond formed not by blood, but by shared purpose, by fire and sacrifice.

I knew others wouldn’t understand. But those who carried swords in ancient times—those who trusted steel as a friend—might grasp it. Yet the Diskos was more. It pulsed with Earth-Current, with the same energy that flowed through the Redoubt, through the planet itself. It lived.

And there was a law: no one touched another’s Diskos. It rejected strangers. It would falter, fail, betray. Because the Diskos grew tied to its wielder—body, soul, spirit. When a man died, his Diskos was laid upon The Last Road, returned to the Earth-Current, so its power could flow again. Not just custom. Wisdom. Respect. Love.

Now I walked deeper into the Night Land, eyes darting, ears straining. Every shadow held a threat. Every rustle could be death. But I carried pride too—pride in having slain the Grey Man. Though I knew the truth: I would have died in my sleep if not for the watchful minds of the Redoubt.

I stopped after a while, weak from battle. I ate three tablets, drank water from the dust-tube. I sat, listening, watching the Pyramid rise into the black sky. Silence. Then I rose again.

Six hours north and west. I veered slightly westward to avoid the Watcher—but then, foolishly, I changed course, drawing closer to the beast. Why? I don’t know. Fear? Curiosity? Some unseen pull?

It was reckless. If the Watcher saw me, it would signal. And then—destruction.

But the heart is fickle. It trembles, then leaps. It fears, then dares. And so I walked onward, unwisely, into the unknown.

For days, I kept my rhythm—eat, rest, move. I crawled through moss, bled my hands, then donned the heavy gloves of my grey armor. I crossed stony ground, breathed sulfur-laden air, always alert.

Always watching.

And always, in the distance, the towering form of the Great Redoubt—my home, my refuge, my only light in the eternal night.
