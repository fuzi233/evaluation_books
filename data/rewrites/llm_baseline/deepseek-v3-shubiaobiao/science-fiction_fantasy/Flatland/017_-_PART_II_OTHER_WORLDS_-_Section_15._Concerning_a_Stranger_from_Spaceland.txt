From dreams, I turn to reality.

It was the final night of the 1999th year of our era. The rain tapped gently against the walls, signaling the deepening night as I sat with my wife, reflecting on the past and contemplating the dawn of a new year—a new century, a new Millennium.

[Note: When I say "sat," I do not mean it in your Spaceland sense, for we of Flatland have no limbs to bend or stretch. Yet we recognize the shifting states of mind—lying, sitting, standing—each marked by a subtle change in luminosity, visible to the keen observer. But I digress.]

My four sons and two grandchildren had retired to their chambers, leaving only my wife and me to witness the passing of the old Millennium. My thoughts wandered, lingering on a curious remark made earlier by my youngest grandson, a bright and promising Hexagon.

During his lesson in Sight Recognition, we had rotated before him, testing his perception of our angles and positions. Pleased with his answers, I had rewarded him with a lesson in Arithmetic as applied to Geometry. Placing nine one-inch Squares together, I formed a larger Square with sides of three inches, demonstrating that the area could be calculated by squaring the side length.

"But if three squared is nine," the boy mused, "what does three cubed mean in Geometry?"

"Nothing," I replied. "Geometry deals only with two dimensions."

Yet the child persisted, his mind racing ahead. "If a Point moves to make a Line, and a Line moves to make a Square, then surely a Square moving must make—something else?"

"Enough," I said, mildly exasperated. "To bed with you."

And so he left, while I remained, half-listening to my wife’s chatter, half-lost in contemplation of the coming age. The last grains of sand in the hourglass dwindled.

"The boy is a fool," I muttered, turning the glass northward.

At once, a chill gripped me. A presence—unseen, yet undeniable—filled the room.

"He is no such thing!" my wife protested. "And you shame yourself by speaking so!"

But I barely heard her. My senses strained against the void. Then, a whisper, cold and clear:

We both turned toward the voice—and froze. Before us stood a figure, shifting in ways no shape of Flatland should. At first glance, it resembled a woman, but no—its edges blurred, its form defied reason. My wife, ever impulsive, gasped.

"A woman? Have you broken your vow? There are no ventilators in this house!"

"There are none," I said sharply. "This is no woman. Look closely—it lacks angles entirely."

The figure spoke again, its voice layered like overlapping circles. *"I am a Circle, yet more than a Circle. I am many in one."*

Then, addressing me directly, it added, *"I bring you a message—one not meant for your wife’s ears."*

My wife, though baffled, retreated with stiff propriety, leaving me alone with the stranger.

The final grain of sand fell.

The third Millennium had begun.
