A tottering old priest approached, placing a trembling hand on my shoulder. "Well?" I demanded sharply, having no appetite for interruptions at such a critical juncture.

"News has been carried to the Three, my King, regarding the threat that looms," the elder replied.

"Then they will know that I stand here now, brother, prepared to engage in the finest fight of my life," I declared, steeling my resolve. "When it concludes, I shall ascend to the Gods, standing behind the stars to welcome them when they inevitably arrive. I regret only that they are too aged and frail to witness a glorious victory themselves."

"I bear commands from them, my King," the priest continued, his voice grave. "Commands I fear you will receive with little pleasure. You are strictly forbidden to seek your death in this battle. The Three have a further use for you."

I whirled on the old man, anger flaring in my chest. "I shall take no such order, my brother! I refuse to believe it was ever issued. You must have misunderstood. If I am a man, a priest, a soldier, and a king, then honor dictates that no enemy shall pass this gate while I draw breath. Go back and spit that message in their teeth!"

The old man smiled with a touch of envy; he, too, had once been a keen warrior. "I told them you would not easily accept such a decree," he explained calmly. "So I asked for a sign, and they graciously granted one. I was to present you with this jewel, my King."

He produced a gleaming object. "How did they acquire it? It is a bracelet from the arm of Nais."

"They must have stripped her of it. I was unaware it belonged to her. The message instructed me that the owner of this jewel waits within the Ark of the Mysteries. The further service the Three require of you concerns her as well."

Even hearing this, I must confess, my obedience was severely tested. I have no shame in admitting it, for every true soldier understands the temptation of glory. The promise of battle was intoxicating. Yet, in the end, my love for Nais prevailed. I offered the necessary salute, signaling my acceptance of the divine order.

Turning to the knot of priests remaining to defend the pass, I bid them farewell. "You will possess what I must forfeit, my brothers," I said softly. "I envy you this final stand. Though I am King of Atlantis, I remain merely one of the Seven, and thus a servant of the Three. They speak the will of the Highest Gods, and we must obey. You will stand behind the stars before I join you, and I ask that you commend me to those you meet there. It is not my own will that keeps me from appearing by your side."

They received my words with smiles, saluting me courteously with their weapons before we parted ways. I did not witness the ensuing slaughter, yet I know it was fierce, judging by the silence that fell when Phorenice's hordes finally breached the mountain crest. That last remnant of the lesser Atlantean priests fought with grim determination until the end.

With a bitter taste in my mouth, I ascended to the head of the pass, moving through the groves and between the temples and colleges lining the upper slopes of the Sacred Mountain. I reached the boundary line—a threshold that, in milder times, was fatal to cross without privilege. However, the era of convention had clearly ended. My temper was hot, and I would have pressed onward without scruple had I not been intercepted.

Waiting in the temple marking the boundary was the old priest Zaemon. With due solemnity and invoking ancient rituals designed for such transgressions, he sought dispensation from the High Gods for my intrusion. He refused to yield until the ceremony was complete.

Already, Phorenice's towers and bridges were in position. The clash and screaming from the ramparts of the last gate indicated that a small handful of priests were trading their lives dearly against impossible odds. Trained fighters though they were, they were old and weak; the numbers against them were insurmountable. Soon, the place would be stormed, leaving the roof of the Sacred Mountain exposed to the invaders. If there was any urgent business to attend to, it needed immediate execution while peace still lingered.

But Zaemon was intent on prolonging the ritual. He led me toward the Ark of the Mysteries, chiding my impatience. Once I had kissed it reverently, he called aloud. Another elderly figure emerged from the opening atop the Ark, descending painfully along the battens affixed to its sides. I had never seen him before; he was hoary, frail, and emaciated. Together with Zaemon, he was one of only two remaining priests elevated to the highest degree of our clan, possessing exclusive knowledge of the deepest secrets and powers.

"Look!" Zaemon cried, his shrill voice cutting through the tension. He swept a trembling finger across the shattered landscape visible below.

I followed his gaze, and a chill crawled up my spine. Everything was laid bare. Our Lord the Sun burned high in a cloudless blue sky, casting a shimmering heat over the scene. From this distance, the world below appeared peaceful and still, save for the smoking mountains, the erupting fires, and the sea, which churned with unnatural disorder.

It was the behavior of the ocean that disturbed me most. Far out on the distant coast, it surged against the rocks in monstrous rollers. Up the great estuary, where the city of Atlantis stood, it rushed in successive waves of terrifying height that never receded. The lower lands on either side were already obliterated beneath tumultuous waters; the harbor walls were submerged, and the flood crept upward into the lower wards of the great city.

"You have seen?" Zaemon asked.

"I have seen."

"Do you understand?"

"In part."

"Then let me explain. This is the beginning, and the end will follow swiftly. The Most High Gods, who dwell behind the stars, have limits to their sublime patience, and that limit has been crossed. The city of Atlantis, the great continent beyond, and all within it are doomed to unutterable destruction. Long ago, it was foreseen that this great erasure would occur due to the sins of mankind. Thus, the Ark of the Mysteries was constructed under the direction of the Gods. No mortal tool can scratch its surface; no wave or rock can wreck it."

Zaemon paused, his voice heavy with prophecy. "Inside are stored on sheets of ancient writing all the knowledge of the world not shared with the common people. There is grain sufficient for two persons for four years, fresh water in tanks for the same duration, along with seeds, weapons, and other necessities deemed essential."

"From this vast country, the High Gods have decreed that two shall not perish. Two shall be chosen—a man and a woman—fit and proper vessels to carry away the ancient learning, to dispose of it as they see fit, and eventually to raise a new race that will build another kingdom and honor our Lord the Sun and the other Gods elsewhere."

"The woman is already within the Ark, seated in her appointed place. Though she is my daughter, the burden of her selection rests with you. As for the man, the choice falls upon yourself."

I was half-numb with shock. "I do not know that I wish to be a survivor."

"You are not asked for your wishes," the old man stated firmly. "You are given an order from the High Gods, who know you to be their faithful servant."

Habit rode strong upon me. I offered the required salute and declared my obedience.

"It remains to raise you to the sublime degree of the Three," Zaemon announced. "If your learning is insufficient to grasp the keys to many of the Powers and the highest Mysteries when they are revealed, that fault cannot be remedied now."

Time was running critically short. The fight still raged at the gate, a marvel that the handful of priests had held their ground so long. Yet the ocean rolled inland with increasing fury, and the mountains smoked and belched volleys of rock as the crushing weight mounted. Soon, the besiegers would realize their impending doom and charge blindly, sweeping everything before them.

There was no time to hurry Zaemon and his colleague. They lacked a temple, sacrifices, or incense; they had only the sky for a roof and the Gods themselves as witnesses. Nevertheless, they proceeded with the grand and majestic formality accumulated over ages, refusing to curtail a single sentence of the ritual.

A burning mountain erupted with a bellowing roar as the incoming waters met its fires, but the priests continued gravely, reciting their sentences in turn. Phorenice's troops broke the last lines of resistance, pouring in a frenzied stream among the groves and temples, yet the priests' voices never faltered. It was said that this ceremony was the grandest of all in our holy religion, and I found it to be true. Even the catastrophic circumstances surrounding them failed to diminish the deference owed to the highest order of the clan.

For my part, I admitted I was less rapt. Standing bareheaded in the heat, I struggled to concentrate, torn by a thousand conflicting emotions. The apocalyptic event unfolding around me demanded attention, and my thoughts constantly drifted to Nais. Still, I maintained a decent facade, responding to the required cues and striving to memorize the sentences containing the keys to power and learning, rather than mere phrases of grandeur.
