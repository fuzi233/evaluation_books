Barely a heartbeat had passed since I tumbled back to my 2D home, but sharp, unshakable instinct flared in me: I could not breathe a word of my Spaceland adventures to my wife. It was not that I feared she would intentionally betray my secret—no, I knew for a fact no woman of Flatland could ever wrap her mind around the idea of a third dimension, and any tale of me leaving our plane to see shapes from above would land both of us in terrible danger. So I cobbled together a hasty, on-the-spot lie: I had stumbled through the loose trapdoor to our cellar, struck my head, and laid stunned there for the past stretch of time.

The faint, ever-present tug of southward gravity in our realm is so slight that even to a woman, my story sounded absurd, nearly impossible. But my wife, who has far more good sense than the average of her sex, saw I was strung tight with a wild, unplaceable excitement and did not argue. She only fussed over me, said I was clearly unwell and needed full rest. I could not have asked for a better excuse to retreat to my private chamber, to sit and unpack every weird, glowing detail of what I had just seen.

Alone at last, a heavy drowsiness began to drag at my eyelids, but I fought to keep my focus long enough to trace the shape of the Third Dimension in my mind, fix the logic of a square sweeping "up" out of our plane to form a solid cube. It did not come as clear as I ached for it to be, but I clung tight to the phrase that had been my guide through all my confusion: *Upward, and yet not Northward*. I repeated it over and over, like a charm, until the words blurred together and I slipped into a deep, refreshing sleep.

I dreamed I was floating once more beside the Sphere. His usual sharp silvery glow had softened to a warm, lambent hue—all the wrath he had directed at me earlier, when I dared ask after dimensions beyond the third, had melted away entirely. We drifted together through empty vacuum toward a tiny, pinprick bright point no bigger than the smallest dust speck, and the Sphere bade me watch it closely. As we drew near, I caught a faint, high humming noise: like the buzz of a Spaceland bluebottle, but so faint and thin that even in the total stillness of the void we flew through, we could not hear it until we had slowed our flight to less than twenty of my diagonal lengths away.

"Look here," my Guide said. "You have lived all your life in Flatland; you have been shown the narrow line of Lineland, and flown with me through the wide expanse of Spaceland. Now, to finish your lesson in the full range of existence, I will carry you down to the lowest possible layer of reality: Pointland, the zero-dimensional abyss of no size at all.

"See that sad, limited creature. That single Point is a being just as alive as you or I, but trapped in that non-dimensional gulf. It is its own entire world, its own whole universe. It cannot conceive of anything that is not itself. It knows nothing of length, or breadth, or height, for it has never experienced any of them. It does not even grasp the concept of the number two, or that there could ever be more than one thing in all of creation. For it, it is the One and All—even though it is, in raw reality, barely nothing at all. But mark how perfectly, smugly self-satisfied it is. Learn this lesson from it: blind, unchallenged contentment is nothing but ignorance, and to reach for more than what you already know is far better than being happy in a tiny dark bubble you never think to leave. Now listen."

He fell silent, and a tiny, monotonous, distinct tinkling sound drifted up from the little buzzing creature, like a Spaceland phonograph playing at a volume so low you could barely hear it. I made out the words perfectly: "Infinite beatitude of existence! It is; and there is none else beside It."

"What in the world does that puny thing mean by 'It'?" I asked.
"It means itself," the Sphere said. "Haven't you noticed before that babies, and people too simple to tell the difference between their own thoughts and the rest of the world, refer to themselves in the third person? Hush, now."

The tiny voice went on, unbroken: "It fills all Space, and what It fills, It is. What It thinks, that It utters; and what It utters, that It hears. It itself is the Thinker, the Utterer, the Hearer, the Thought, the Word, Audition. It is the One, and the All in All. Ah, the happiness, ah the happiness of Being!"

"Can't you jolt that little thing out of its silly complacency?" I said to the Sphere. "Tell it what it really is, like you told me. Show it the narrow tiny limits of Pointland, and lead it up to something bigger."
"That is far from easy," my Master said. "Go on, try it yourself."

I raised my voice as loud as I could, and shouted at the Point: "Silence, silence, you contemptible Creature! You call yourself the All in All, but you are nothing—your so-called Universe is no more than a tiny speck on a Line, and a Line is nothing but a faint shadow compared to--"
"Hush, that's more than enough," the Sphere cut me off. "Now watch, and see what effect your little speech has on the King of Pointland."

The little point glowed brighter than ever, no trace of shaken self-satisfaction at all. Before I had even finished yelling, its high, happy droning started up again: "Ah, the joy, ah, the joy of Thought! What can It not achieve by thinking! Its own Thought coming to Itself, suggestive of Its disparagement, thereby to enhance Its happiness! Sweet rebellion stirred up to result in triumph! Ah, the divine creative power of the All in One! Ah, the joy, the joy of Being!"

"You see now," my Teacher said, amused. "Your words did nothing at all. The tiny king, so far as it can make any sense of what you shouted, thinks those words are its own new brilliant thought—for it cannot imagine any being that exists outside of itself. It is preening right now over the 'new, creative idea' of self-disparagement it thinks it just came up with, and calls that proof of its own infinite creative power. Let us leave this so-called God of Pointland to its ignorant little feast of imagined omnipresence and omniscience. Nothing you or I can ever do will pry it loose from that bubble of perfect self-satisfaction."

After that, we drifted gently back toward the familiar plane of Flatland, and the Sphere's soft, mild voice laid out the moral of the vision, urging me to keep reaching for higher understanding, and to teach every other Flatlander to aspire past the limits of their 2D world. He confessed he had been angry at first, when I dared to push past the third dimension and ask for glimpses of realities even higher, but he had since come to see the error of that short-sightedness, and was not too proud to admit he had been wrong to a new student. Then he walked me through even grander, stranger mysteries than any I had seen before: how you could move a full 3D solid through the fourth dimension, by strict analogy, to build an Extra-Solid, then move that Extra-Solid through the next higher plane to make a Double Extra-Solid, and on and on. The logic was so simple, so straightforward, that even the sharpest (and most often underestimated) women of Flatland could follow it without a single stumble.
