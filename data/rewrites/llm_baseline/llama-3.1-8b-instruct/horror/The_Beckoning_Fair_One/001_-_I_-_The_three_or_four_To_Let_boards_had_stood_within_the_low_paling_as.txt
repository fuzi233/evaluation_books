As I strolled through the narrow streets, the worn wooden "To Let" boards creaked in the gentle morning breeze, their faded letters seeming to whisper secrets to the passersby. The boards had stood sentinel in the triangular square for as long as anyone could remember, their crooked limbs like the gnarled branches of an ancient tree. Overhanging the low palings, they resembled a row of wooden choppers poised to strike, yet somehow, miraculously, never managing to sever the thread of life for those who dwelled within the old house.

Beyond the tangle of tenements and alleys, the gentle burble of the nearby stream created a soothing background hum, a constant reminder of the world beyond the confines of this forgotten square. The old house itself, a sturdy edifice of bloomy red brick, seemed a relic from a bygone era, its walls bearing the faded emblems of insurance companies long since defunct. The children of the square had long since claimed the low gate at the entrance alley as their own, wearing down the solid top bar with their laughter and play.

As I navigated the uneven path, the smell of damp earth and overgrown vegetation filled my nostrils, mingling with the acrid tang of chalk marks left by tramps on the boarded basement windows. Water from the eaves of the adjacent house trickled down, creating miniature rivulets that glistened in the morning light. Cats and dogs had claimed the alley as their domain, leaving behind a trail of paw prints and discarded treasures.

I had passed this place countless times, twice a day or more often, on my way to the small room where I worked, a ten-minute walk away. Yet, today, fate had led me down the side street that ran past the broken gate and the rain-worn entrance alley. As I paused before one of the leaning boards, a faint memory stirred within me. The board bore the agent's name, alongside a faded announcement that the key was to be found at Number Six.

My thoughts drifted to the inconvenience of living in a divided domicile – the separate bedroom and workroom, the storage of my grandmother's furniture, the constant shuttling between two distant locations. The weight of these considerations was alleviated by the realization that perhaps it was time to explore new possibilities. With a sense of resolve, I continued my journey to Number Six, the worn boards beneath my feet echoing my footsteps.
