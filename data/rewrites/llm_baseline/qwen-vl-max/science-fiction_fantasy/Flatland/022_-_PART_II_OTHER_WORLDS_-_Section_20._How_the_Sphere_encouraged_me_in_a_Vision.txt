Though I had less than a minute to gather my thoughts, I knew—by an instinct deeper than reason—that I must not speak of what had happened to my Wife. Not because I feared she would betray me, but because no woman in Flatland could possibly grasp the truth of my journey. To her, my tale would sound like madness, or worse, nonsense. So I quickly spun a story: I had fallen through the cellar trapdoor, struck my head, and lain unconscious. It was a tale even I found hard to believe—our land’s gravitational pull toward the South is so faint that such an accident seemed unlikely—but my Wife, sharper-minded than most of her kind, sensed my agitation and chose not to argue. Instead, she insisted I rest, believing I was unwell.

I welcomed the excuse. Alone at last in my chamber, weariness washed over me. Yet before sleep claimed me, I strained to recall the Third Dimension—the way a Cube arises from the motion of a Square. The image flickered, unclear. But one phrase echoed in my mind: *“Upward, yet not Northward.”* I clung to it, repeating it like a mantra, as if the words themselves held the key to understanding. And so, murmuring them again and again, I slipped into a deep, dreamless sleep.

But the dream came swiftly.

I found myself once more beside the Sphere, his light now soft and warm, no longer fierce with anger. We drifted together toward a tiny, shimmering point of light, glowing faintly in the vast emptiness. As we drew near, a faint hum emerged—like the buzz of a Spaceland fly, but softer, almost imperceptible, until we paused just under twenty human diagonals away.

"Look," said the Sphere. "In Flatland you have lived; in Lineland you have glimpsed; in Spaceland you have soared. Now, to complete your journey, I bring you down—to the lowest depth of existence, to Pointland, the realm of No Dimensions."

There, in the void, hovered a single speck—a Point, pulsing with quiet radiance. This creature, he told me, was a being like ourselves, yet trapped in absolute nothingness. He had no length, no breadth, no height. He was neither two nor many—he was only One, and that One was all. He could not know plurality, nor distance, nor time. He was, in fact, nothing at all. Yet he was perfectly content.

"Observe," said the Sphere, "how utterly satisfied he is. From this, learn this: self-contentment is ignorance. To aspire is better than to be blind and blissful."

Then, from the Point, a tiny, monotonous chime rose—like the voice of a distant phonograph—carrying these words:

"What does 'It' mean?" I asked.

"He means himself," replied the Sphere. "Have you not seen how infants, who cannot separate themselves from the world, speak of themselves in the third person? But hush—listen now."

The Point continued:
*"It fills all Space, and what It fills, It is. What It thinks, that It utters; and what It utters, that It hears. It is Thinker, Utterer, Hearer, Thought, Word, Audition. It is the One, and yet the All in All. Ah, the happiness—ah, the joy of Being!"*

"Can you not wake him from this delusion?" I urged. "Tell him the truth, as you told me. Show him how small his world is, and lift him higher!"

"It is not easy," said the Sphere. "Try."

So I raised my voice, shouting with all my might:
"Silence, you pitiful thing! You call yourself the All in All—but you are nothing! Your universe is a speck on a line, and a line is but a shadow compared to—"

"Hush!" the Sphere interrupted. "Listen—see what your words do."

The Point shone brighter than ever. Unmoved, he resumed his song:
*"Ah, the joy of Thought! What can It not achieve? Its own thought returns to Itself, suggesting its own worth—thus enhancing Its joy! Sweet rebellion that leads to triumph! Ah, the divine creative power of the All in One! Ah, the joy, the joy of Being!"*

"You see," said the Sphere gently, "your words only feed his pride. He takes them as his own, for he knows no other. He sees the variety of 'Its Thought' not as limitation, but as proof of greatness. Let us leave this King of Pointland to his blissful ignorance. Nothing we say can free him from his self-satisfaction."

As we drifted back toward Flatland, I heard the Sphere whispering wisdom into my ear—not scolding, but guiding. He confessed that once, he had scorned my ambition to reach beyond the Third Dimension. But now, he saw differently. He had learned. And he was not too proud to admit it.

Then, with growing warmth, he began to reveal wonders beyond even what I had seen. He showed me how Solids might move to form Extra-Solids, and how those might generate Double Extra-Solids—each step built upon analogy, each construction simple enough, perhaps, for even a woman to understand.

And as I floated home, still dreaming, I carried with me not only the vision of Pointland, but a new fire within me: the certainty that to strive, to question, to reach beyond the known—that is the true path of life.
