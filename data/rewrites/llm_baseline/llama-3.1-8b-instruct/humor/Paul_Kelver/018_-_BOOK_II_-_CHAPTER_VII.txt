As the aromatic scents of roasting meats and baking pastries wafted through the old Georgian house in Queen Square, I couldn't help but feel a sense of excitement and anticipation. Our weekly Sunday gatherings were a highlight of the social calendar, and I was always eager to see what culinary masterpiece Dan would create in the kitchen. Today was no exception, as we prepared for an afternoon of fine food and lively conversation.

Our tradition was to critique each other's efforts in the lead-up to the gathering. As I surveyed the array of dishes Dan had prepared, I spotted a cake that seemed to be lacking in depth of flavor. "A bit too much soda, don't you think?" I ventured, trying a small slice.

Dan looked at me incredulously. "It's meant to taste of soda, of course! It's a classic soda cake recipe." But I stood firm, insisting that the delicate balance of flavors was off. "It's like eating a solidified Seidlitz powder," I said, trying to convey the essence of my criticism. Dan huffed and puffed, but eventually conceded that perhaps we needed to start anew.

Meanwhile, I was tasked with ensuring the dining area was spotless. Dan eyed the knives, his fingers drumming impatiently on the countertop. "Are those clean?" he asked, his brow furrowed with concern. I assured him they were, but he remained unconvinced. "You've just wiped them, that's all," he said, his voice laced with skepticism. "There's no knife powder, is there?"

I smiled wryly, knowing that Dan's fastidiousness was a hallmark of his character. "Of course, there is," I replied. "And besides, over-cleaning the knives takes away their edge. We'll need them sharp to cut through those lovely lemon buns of yours."

Dan snorted, his eyes flashing with amusement. "You're as lazy as ever, Juggins. Your cooking makes me indolent, I swear!" I laughed, knowing that his jest was tinged with truth. As we worked together, our banter flowed easily, a testament to the warmth and camaraderie that defined our friendship.

The afternoon sun cast a warm glow over the room as our guests began to arrive. Dan's journalistic acquaintances mingled with ease, their faces familiar from countless gatherings past.
