Victory and Defeat

The scent of damp earth and the faint tang of ozone hung heavy in the air as I stood beside Dejah Thoris, her tears soaking into the fabric of my tunic. Her words, like a gentle summer breeze, rustled through the darkness, "John Carter, John Carter," she whispered, her voice trembling with the weight of longing. "Even now, I scarce believe the witness of my own eyes. When Thuvia spoke of your return to Barsoom, I listened, but it seemed an impossibility, a cruel jest played upon my heart. At last, when reality dawned, and I grasped the truth, I learned to doubt that even you could breach the walls of this forsaken place."

As the moons waxed and waned, casting their silvery light upon the desolate landscape, I resigned myself to the futility of my situation. But the hope, like a stubborn flame, flickered within me. And now, as the sounds of conflict echoed through the palace, I dared to dream that it might be the warriors of Helium, led by my son Carthoris, who fought for the freedom of the Princess of Helium.

"And tell me, what of Carthoris, our son?" Dejah Thoris asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

"He was with me mere moments ago," I replied, my heart swelling with pride. "It must have been he whose men you've heard clashing with the enemy within the temple precincts."

I turned to her, my mind racing with the possibilities. "Where is Issus?" I asked, my voice firm.

Dejah Thoris's shoulders rose and fell in a delicate shrug. "She sent me under guard to this room just before the battle erupted within the temple halls. She seemed...different, her usual composure shaken. I sensed a deep-seated fear, a trepidation that chilled me to the bone. Now I realize it was because she knew that John Carter, Prince of Helium, was coming to claim justice for the imprisonment of his beloved Princess."

The cacophony of battle grew louder, the clash of steel on steel, the shouts of the combatants, and the thunder of footsteps echoing through the temple corridors.
