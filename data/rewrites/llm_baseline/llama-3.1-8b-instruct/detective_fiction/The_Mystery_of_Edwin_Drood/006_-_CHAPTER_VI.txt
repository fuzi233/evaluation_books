Minor Canon Corner, with its tranquil atmosphere, was the perfect haven for the Reverend Septimus Crisparkle to begin his day. As he emerged from the quiet morning hours, his amiable nature was evident in the way he effortlessly broke the morning ice near Cloisterham Weir. The crisp air invigorated his frame, and he took to the exercise of boxing at a looking-glass with great skill and finesse. The glass reflected a portrait of a man in his prime, his features radiating innocence and kindness, as he danced around the room with the agility of a younger man.

Mrs. Crisparkle, the reverend's mother, soon joined him, and as she waited for the urn to be brought in, she watched with a warm smile as Septimus boxed with precision and control. His gloves moved swiftly through the air, striking the glass with a soft thud, but never causing it to shatter. The reverend's movements were a testament to his dedication to the noble art, and his mother couldn't help but feel a sense of pride in her son's accomplishments.

As the morning progressed, Septimus paused to greet his mother with a tender kiss, his boxing gloves still clasped around her face. "I say, every morning, Ma dear," he said, "that you'll be proud of me yet." His mother smiled knowingly, her eyes twinkling with amusement. "And so I shall, Sept," she replied. "One of these days, you'll make me beam with pride."

The reverend chuckled and returned to his exercise, his movements becoming more intense and focused. He sparred with the glass, his body weaving and bobbing as he countered and parried with ease. His mother watched, her face aglow with warmth and affection, as she felt a deep sense of connection to her son. It was moments like these that reminded her of when Septimus was a young boy, listening intently to her recite the Lord's Prayer. Though the years had passed, their bond remained strong, and she knew that she could always rely on him.
