As I stepped out of the grand council chamber, the polished marble floor seemed to reverberate with the echoes of my own footsteps, a hollow reminder of the weight I'd carried for twenty years. The Viceroy's retinue parted to let me pass, their faces a blur of courteous smiles and deferential nods. The drums and trumpets had long since ceased their cacophony, leaving only the faint scent of incense and the soft murmur of the crowd.

I trailed behind Tatho, the new Viceroy, as he made his way through the winding corridors of the palace. The gemstone-studded chain around his neck glinted in the fading light, a symbol of the office he'd inherited. My own hands felt empty, my fingers twitching with a familiar sense of disuse. I'd grown accustomed to the feel of power, the subtle weight of responsibility. Now, it was someone else's burden to bear.

We paused at the entrance to my former chambers, the massive stone door swinging open with a creak. Tatho's voice boomed out, a ritualistic phrase that sent the slaves scurrying to clear the path. As we stepped inside, the door slammed shut behind us, muffling the distant din of the palace. For a moment, we stood there, the only sound the soft rustle of our breathing.

The Viceroy turned to me, his eyes filled with a mixture of gratitude and trepidation. "Deucalion," he said, his voice low and measured, "I swear to you, I did not seek this office. It was thrust upon me. If I had refused, my head would have paid the price, and another man – your enemy – would have taken my place."

I nodded, my expression a mask of understanding. "My friend," I began, "you know as well as I do that the Empress's will is not to be questioned." My voice was a monotone, a reflection of the emptiness I felt inside. The Viceroy's words hung in the air, a promise of loyalty and a reminder of the delicate balance of power in this, the greatest city of Yucatan.
