As I lay abed, lost in a dreamless slumber, the tranquil sounds of the Mediterranean lapping against the hull of the _Circe_ were shattered by a most unseemly commotion outside my state-room door. A feminine voice rose above the din, its plaintive tones echoing through the stillness of the morning air: "Oh, Harold!" A deep, rough voice, unmistakably Voules', responded in kind, its tone a far cry from the measured cadence he employed when serving me. The sudden intrusion was jarring, and I felt my eyes snap open in protest.

"Voules!" I bellowed, trying to shake off the remnants of sleep.

The ruckus ceased abruptly, replaced by the faint sound of muffled sobs carried away on the breeze. A gentle knock at the door followed, and Voules entered with an expression of studious gravity, his usual impassivity momentarily ruffled. "Good morning, sir," he intoned, his voice a model of restraint.

"Voules, what's all this commotion?" I inquired, throwing off the covers.

"I apologize for disturbing you, sir. I was, uh, engaged in a discussion with Miss Pilbeam."

"A discussion, eh? With Mrs. Vanderley's maid? And at this hour?"

Voules' face betrayed a flicker of discomfort before he regained his composure. "Yes, sir. I fear I allowed my emotions to get the better of me."

"Your emotions?" I repeated, intrigued. "You're not one for displays of feeling, Voules. What's gotten into you?"

"I'm afraid I've terminated our engagement, sir," he replied, his voice barely above a whisper.

I was taken aback by the news. Voules, the epitome of discretion and reserve, had a secret life, it seemed. I chose not to pry further, merely nodding for him to continue with his duties.

"Shall I bring you breakfast, sir?" he asked, his usual composure restored.

"Yes, please do," I replied, rising from bed. "And I'll join the others on deck shortly."
