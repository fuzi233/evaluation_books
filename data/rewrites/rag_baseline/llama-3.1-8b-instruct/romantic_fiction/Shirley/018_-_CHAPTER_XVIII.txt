As the last wisps of sunlight surrendered to the horizon, the evening air vibrated with a languid warmth. A crimson glow infused the sky, casting a mesmerizing dance of shadows across the rolling hills and verdant pastures. Caroline and Shirley descended from the fields, their footsteps slow and deliberate, as if savoring the tranquil atmosphere. The churchyard, once bustling with life, now lay deserted, the only sound the soft hum of crickets and the distant chime of a lone bell.

"How serene and peaceful it is," Caroline observed, her voice barely above a whisper.

"And how sweltering it will be inside the church," Shirley countered, her eyes scanning the horizon. "And what a tedious, lengthy sermon Dr. Boultby will deliver. I fear I shall find myself yawning, listening to his words, which, though well-intentioned, will only serve to divide us further." She paused, her gaze drifting toward the church, where the congregants were gathering. "I would much rather remain outside, surrounded by the beauty of nature, than be trapped within those stifling walls."

"But Uncle will be displeased if he discovers our absence," Caroline reminded her friend.

"I am willing to bear the brunt of his disapproval," Shirley replied, a hint of defiance in her voice. "Besides, I have a deep appreciation for the simple, rustic beauty of this landscape. The way the light casts long shadows across the grass, the gentle rustle of the leaves... it's almost as if nature itself is offering a prayer, seeking solace and tranquility for all living creatures."

Caroline smiled, her eyes sparkling with amusement. "You're waxing poetic again, Shirley."

"Poetic, perhaps," Shirley said, her tone tinged with a hint of mischief, "but I assure you, my words are born from a deep understanding of the world and its rhythms. We often speak of Milton's Eve, don't we? The one he described as beautiful, yet flawed. But what of the real Eve, the one who walked among the Titans, who gave birth to giants and heroes? What of the first woman, whose strength and vitality were matched only by her capacity for love and compassion?"
