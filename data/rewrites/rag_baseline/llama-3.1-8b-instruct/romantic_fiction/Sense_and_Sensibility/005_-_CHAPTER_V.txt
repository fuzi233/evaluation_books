Mrs. Dashwood's words carried across the threshold of her son-in-law's parlour like a warm breeze on a summer's day, a gentle harbinger of the news she had come to share. "A house in Devonshire," she announced to her son-in-law and his wife, "one that will provide for me, and spare you the inconvenience of my presence no longer than necessity demands." The couple exchanged glances, their faces a study in surprise and polite interest.

Mrs. John Dashwood's lips remained sealed, but her husband's expression turned thoughtful, his eyes clouding over with concern. "Not too far from Norland, I hope?" he ventured, his tone a blend of politeness and genuine inquiry. Mrs. Dashwood's response was a triumphant smile, her eyes sparkling with the knowledge that she was doing what was best for her family, even if it meant leaving behind the comforts of their current home. "Just four miles north of Exeter," she said, her voice filled with a quiet satisfaction.

As she spoke, she began to imagine the possibilities of her new life in the countryside, the friends she would make, and the warmth of their company. "A small cottage, perhaps, but one that can be expanded upon, and where I shall be delighted to welcome friends from afar. If they find it no trouble to travel so far, I assure you, I shall make no trouble in receiving them."

With a gracious smile, she extended an invitation to Mr. and Mrs. John Dashwood to visit her at her new home, Barton, and to Edward, a particularly affectionate one. Though her conversation with her daughter-in-law had made her realize that it was time to leave Norland, it had not changed her desire to keep her son close to her heart. She wanted to demonstrate to Mrs. John Dashwood that she disregarded her disapproval of Elinor and Edward's match, and that their happiness was still uppermost in her mind.

As she finished speaking, Mr. John Dashwood shook his head, his expression a mixture of regret and vexation. "I'm truly sorry, Mother, that you've chosen a house so far from Norland, for it means I'll be unable to assist you in moving your belongings." His words were laced with a genuine sense of disappointment, for he had been looking forward to lending a hand in the transition.
