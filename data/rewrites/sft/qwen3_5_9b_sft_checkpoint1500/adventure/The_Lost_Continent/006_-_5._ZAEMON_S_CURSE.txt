The grand procession settled before the royal pyramid, a glittering assembly of nobles and soldiers massing in the paved square. The colossal mammoth halted at the threshold, and as stairs were erected, trumpets blared. We three, having ridden beneath the canopy of snakes atop the golden half-castle, dismounted. The transition from the open sky to the subterranean stone mazes was immediate. Without conscious thought, the ingrained reverence of my upbringing compelled me to turn toward the looming peaks of the Sacred Mountain. I performed the customary obeisance, bowing low and offering the silent, prescribed prayer to the gods.

However, as I rose, a sharp, mocking ripple of laughter cut through the air, emanating from the crowd. Instinctively, I scanned the spectators for the source, my brow furrowed in indignation. I then turned to Phorenice, expecting to see her join me in the ritual, and demand justice for this insolence. Yet, she stood erect, her forehead untouched by the dust, having never prostrated herself. Worse still, she regarded me with a peculiar, unreadable expression that chilled me.

Before I could confront the situation publicly, Phorenice signaled me to follow. "Come," she commanded, turning toward the doorway. She cried out the secret word for entry, and the massive stone blocks barring the porch swung back on their hinges. With stately grace, she stepped from the blinding sun into the cool, shadowed interior, followed by a fan-girl trailing decorously at her heels. A heavy weight settled in my chest. Following her, I crossed the threshold, and the stone doors slammed shut with a sullen thud, sealing us inside.

We traveled only a short distance before halting in the Hall of Waiting. I recalled this chamber vividly from twenty years prior, when the former King had bid me farewell as I departed for my vice-regency in Yucatán. Back then, the walls adorned with red paintings of kings reflected the light of a central bronze fountain that breathed fire onto the floor, casting a warm, pure illumination. The air had been sweet and crisp. Now, however, the atmosphere was thick, laden with a heavy, oppressive scent that made breathing difficult.

"My minister," Phorenice said, breaking the silence. "I absolve you of intent to insult. However, I suspect the colonial air has dulled your senses. An act of such reverence toward that mountain, performed not a moment ago, has not occurred since I assumed the throne."

"Your Majesty," I replied, "I am a member of the Priests' Clan, raised on their tenets. Before entering any dwelling, it is my duty to thank the Gods, particularly our Lord the Sun, for the air He provides. I have survived fires and toxic mists in the mountains; thus, I recite this prayer from the depths of my heart."

"Circumstances have shifted since you left Atlantis," she countered. "Today, thanks are not directed toward those ancient deities."

Her implication struck me like a physical blow. If this was the new order, I could not abide it. Serving a monarch was one thing; to compromise faith, to treat a swineherd's daughter as a deity worthy of prayer, revolted my very soul. I decided to challenge her.

"Phorenice," I stated firmly, "I have been a priest since childhood, revering the mysteries of the Gods. Until I personally verify that these old truths are falsehoods, I must remain loyal to my allegiance. If there is a price for this fidelity, I am prepared to pay it."

A slow, amused smile spread across her face. "You are a strong man, Deucalion," she observed. "I have encountered many who were equally stubborn, yet they were all converted." She tossed her ruddy hair, positioning herself so the light from the earth-breath fountain illuminated her features. "I find converting the obstinate as effortless as incinerating them. Few have needed the latter. Yet, it appears my modest appearance and tongue lack sufficient charm today."

"I am but your servant, Phorenice," I said stoically. "Tomorrow, if granted leave, I shall clear the rabble clamoring outside the walls. I intend to demonstrate my utility."

"You are renowned as a fighter," she noted. "I possess some skill in arms and consider myself a competent judge. Tomorrow, we shall test our prowess together. Today, however, I must complete the honorable reception I have arranged. A feast awaits, and you will require preparation. Chambers have been assigned to you, stocked with necessities. Ylga will guide you there."

Waiting until she had retreated into the shadows of the distant corridor, the fan-girl approached with a lamp. Guided by her, we navigated the labyrinthine corridors of the pyramid. The air was saturated with exotic perfumes, and the passages twisted with deliberate complexity, designed to frustrate intruders. Legends spoke of the builders killing anyone who knew the layout of the internal stones, ensuring the pyramid remained invulnerable to assassination.

Ylga, however, moved with practiced ease. She manipulated hidden mechanisms—pushing stones in walls, lifting flagstones—to reveal steep, narrow staircases. Eventually, we reached the chamber designated for me.

"There are garments in these chests along the walls," Ylga announced, gesturing to rows of storage. "Jewels and adornments await in the bronze coffer. These are the Empress's first gifts, a mere prelude to what follows. My Lord Deucalion may discard his simplicity and clothe himself in finery."

"Girl," I warned sharply, "exercise decorum and refrain from such unsolicited counsel."

"If Lord Deucalion finds this rude," she retorted, "he may inform Phorenice, and I shall be whipped. If he commands it, I will submit to scourging before him. The Empress is generous to Deucalion at this juncture."

"Girl," I said, "you are closer to the whipping than you realize."

She stared at me sullenly beneath dark brows. "I have a name," she said. "They call me Ylga. You likely would have heard it had you paid attention during our ride."

I studied her curiously. "We have never met before," I observed. "And your opening words suggest motives that could bring you harm. There is purpose behind this."

She moved to the massive stone slab blocking the chamber's entrance, then pulled me gently away from the airshaft toward a far corner. "I am the daughter of Zaemon," she whispered. "You knew him."

"Do you bear a message?"

"How could I? He resides in the priests' dwellings on the Sacred Mountain—the same peak I just bowed to. I have not seen him in two years. Yet, when I first beheld you stepping from the red pavilion at the harbor, I felt a profound pity. I remembered you as my father's friend, and I knew the designs Phorenice harbored. She has plotted this for two months."

"I cannot speak ill of the Empress."

"And yet..."

"What?"

She stomped her sandal against the stone floor. "You are either blind or incredibly bold. I will not intervene further, not now. I shall observe, however, and if you ever seek a friend, I will endeavor to serve you."

"I accept your friendship."

"You treat it lightly," she insisted. "Why do you not grasp my influence, any more than you comprehend my motives? You may be the first man in this kingdom, but let me assure you, I rank as the second lady. In Atlantis, women hold supreme sway. Remember, my friendship is a commodity highly coveted."

"As I stated, I am grateful. You underestimate my sentiment, Ylga. I have never bestowed such gratitude upon a woman before; cherish it for its rarity."

"Well," she said, "there is an education ahead." She departed, instructing me on summoning slaves, leaving me to reflect on her words. Who was this daughter of Zaemon capable of swaying me from a lifetime of habits?

Slaves arrived promptly, eager to adorn me with countless robes, jewels, and fragrant ointments. Their obsequious manner grated on me. Already clean, shaved, and attired simply, I viewed their attentions as an impertinence. I ordered them to fight one another, threatening to brand them with stripes if they failed to do so thoroughly. It was a peculiar truth: a humble menial could often unsettle a rebellious general more effectively.

Later, I awaited the summons to the feast. Suddenly, a sound emerged from nowhere, growing in volume with infinitesimal increments until it became a deafening clang. The entire pyramid groaned and vibrated, shaking as violently as a child's toy brushed by a heavy boot.

This was the annual portent declared by the Chiefs of the Priests' Clan on the Sacred Mountain—a sacred reminder that humanity, born of fire and earthquake, would ultimately be consumed by them alongside its sins. Here, the Empress was desecrating this solemnity, reducing it to a signal for gluttony and licentious revelry.

How had she obtained this authority? Had she betrayed the Priests' secrets? Or had her keen intellect unlocked the mysteries of the earth's liquid heart independently? If she had acted alone, I could serve her with a clear conscience. But if she had suborned a weak priest from the Sacred Mountain, the consequences would be dire. Betrayal of sacred mysteries invoked undying hatred from the Council.
