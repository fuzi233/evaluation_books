The afternoon sunlight cast a warm glow through the window of the spacious room, illuminating the oaken desk situated by the glass panes that overlooked St. James's Park from a commanding height. The room itself was tastefully furnished and decorated, betraying the refined sensibilities of its owner, a bachelor whose presence was palpably felt, despite his absence. John Marlowe unlocked the desk drawer, retrieving a long, sturdy envelope from the back, and drew it forth with a soft click.

"I believe you've perused this document," he said to Mr. Cupples, who sat reclined on a nearby sofa, surveying the room with an affable expression. "We've discussed it at length."

Marlowe turned to Trent, his gaze steady. "This is your manuscript," he said, placing the envelope on the table. "I've reviewed it thrice. I daresay, there's no other individual who could have extracted as much insight as you've managed to convey here."

Trent's response was measured, his eyes fixed intently on the fire crackling in the hearth. His long legs were twisted beneath the chair, his posture unyielding. "You're suggesting, of course, that there remains more to uncover, that we're prepared to listen as soon as you're ready. I anticipate it will be a lengthy account, and the more detailed, the better, as far as I'm concerned. It seems to me that a preliminary examination of Manderson's character and our relationship with him would be a suitable starting point. The deceased's persona appeared to me, from the outset, as a pivotal element in this enigma."

Marlowe nodded grimly, crossing the room to settle onto a corner of the tall, cushion-topped fender. "I concur with your assessment. I shall begin as you propose."

Trent's eyes locked onto Marlowe's, his voice measured. "Before you proceed, I must inform you that, although I'm here to listen, I've yet to entertain any reservations regarding the conclusions I've reached." He tapped the envelope, his gesture deliberate. "You'll be presenting a defense, I presume? You understand my stance?"
