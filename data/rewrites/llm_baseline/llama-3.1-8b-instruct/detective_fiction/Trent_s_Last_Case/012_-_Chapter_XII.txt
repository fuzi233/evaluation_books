As Trent scribbled a letter to Sir James Molloy from the cobblestone streets of Munich, the faint scent of Bavarian beer wafted through the air, mingling with the echoes of his thoughts. The cheque he'd sent for the Manderson case was being returned, its value deemed far too low for his exceptional work. Yet Trent felt no qualms about forgoing payment; instead, he requested that Sir James donate the funds to a charity untainted by the zealous pursuit of power.

The notion of working on a high-stakes assignment with Sir James had long fascinated Trent, and now, amidst the quiet moments spent contemplating the intricacies of human emotion, he found himself yearning for such a challenge. His artistic endeavors had stalled, much like his attempts to paint a fence – a futile exercise that left him frustrated and uninspired. As he strolled through the city, Trent pondered the prospect of becoming Sir James's Own Correspondent, hoping to uncover stories that would captivate readers and perhaps even himself.

A telegram arrived shortly thereafter, instructing Trent to travel to Kurland and Livonia, where the enigmatic Citizen Browning had resurfaced amidst the turmoil of rebellion. The terrain was treacherous, and the locals were on the brink of chaos. Yet, Trent's instincts proved unerring, leading him to witness pivotal events that would leave an indelible mark on his psyche. He bore witness to the tragic death of General Dragilew at the hands of a young girl, the ravages of burnings, lynchings, and fusillades, and the hangings that seemed to scar the very fabric of society.

Each night, as Trent lay awake, the image of the woman he loved lingered in his mind's eye, frozen in time, much like the moment he first caught sight of her on the windswept cliffside. Her gesture of unbridled joy, a testament to her newfound freedom, had spoken volumes about her inner world. Trent's thoughts were a maelstrom of wonder and trepidation as he grappled with the notion that his love for her might be doomed from the start. This epiphany had crept upon him like a thief in the night, leaving him with an aching sense of longing and a deep-seated understanding of the mysteries that lay beyond the reaches of his comprehension.
