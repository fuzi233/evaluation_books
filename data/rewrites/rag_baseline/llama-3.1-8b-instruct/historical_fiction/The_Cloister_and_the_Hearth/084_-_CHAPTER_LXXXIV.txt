The aroma of simmering stew wafted through the air, mingling with the scent of fresh bread and roasting vegetables, carrying the promise of a celebratory feast at Catherine's house in the Hoog Straet. The shop was finally ready, and Cornelis and Sybrandt were set to unveil it to the world the next day, their names emblazoned above the door, accompanied by the sign of a lamb suckling a gilt sheep – a symbol of the innocence and abundance they sought to cultivate in their new venture. Eli, their supplier, had arrived, bearing gifts of fine goods to stock their shelves and give them a prosperous start. The warmth in the hearts of the parents was palpable as they strolled through the garden, their conversation filled with the conviction that a life of commerce held more promise than the meager existence they had left behind. They vowed to persevere, to dedicate themselves to their craft like devoted artisans, shaping their future with each passing day.

As they spoke, their mother listened intently from a nearby window, her ears attuned to the whispers of her children. She shared their excitement with Eli, and soon the family gathered for a grand supper, with Margaret and her young son in attendance. The meal was a testament to the bounty of their hard work, and the evening air was alive with the prospect of good trade advice and fine wine, raised in toast to those who had chosen the path of commerce over the uncertainties of agriculture. Margaret, however, felt a pang of melancholy, her face flushed with the exertion of helping her mother-in-law prepare for the occasion. She longed for a respite from her emotional turmoil, a chance to reconnect with the world beyond her domestic sphere.

Just then, Joan burst into the room, carrying a vibrant salad from Jorian's garden, its colors and scents a vibrant counterpoint to the somber mood that had settled over Margaret. "He cut it for you, Margaret; you are his favorite," she said with a sly smile. "I'll be jealous, I told him you were to feast today. But, oh, lass, what a sermon in the new kerk! I've never heard it till this day."
