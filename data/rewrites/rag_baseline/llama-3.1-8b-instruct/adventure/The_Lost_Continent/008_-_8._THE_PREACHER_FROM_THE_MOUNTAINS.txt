They toiled to warm themselves, the steam rising from their sodden clothing a testament to their struggles.

As the night wore on, the stillness was disrupted by a mournful blast on a horn or shell, its harsh notes piercing the air. The sound drew closer, and the murmurs of discontent grew louder. The people of the camp began to stir, gathering around a figure standing atop a war engine. His words were indistinguishable, but the crowd's reaction was clear – a mixture of fascination and hostility.

Nais nudged me with her elbow, her voice barely above a whisper. "We should leave now, before things get ugly." Her words were laced with a sense of resignation, as if she knew that trying to escape unnoticed was futile. The crowd's attention was drawn to us, and a group of rough-looking men approached, their faces illuminated by the faint light of the fires.

I felt a surge of anxiety, but she merely raised an eyebrow and stood up, her movements economical and deliberate.

Nais leaned against the stone doorway, her hand resting on the worn surface. "There's no need to explain your presence," she said, her voice low and detached. "They'll assume you're my lover. It's not unusual here."

I felt a pang of irritation, but said nothing. The men parted to let us pass, their gaze lingering on Nais before moving on.
