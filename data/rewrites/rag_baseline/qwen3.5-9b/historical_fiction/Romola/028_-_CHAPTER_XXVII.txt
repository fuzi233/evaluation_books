"No room. Full up."

The door slammed with the dull, decisive thud of bureaucratic finality.

Tito paused, his grip tightening on the leather-wrapped bundle containing the newly acquired armor plates. He had traversed the bridge with purpose, driven by the weight of his commission and the urgency of the moment. Yet, the rejection echoed louder than the city's clamor. To have marched through the streets seeking employment, to have stooped to beg for sustenance, and to be denied even the most basic shelter—the casual ward—was a tactical failure of the highest order. It was not merely poverty; it was the systematic dismantling of dignity.

He stood frozen, staring at the barricaded entryway. The logic of the institution was opaque, designed to filter out the weak and the weary. A man's resolve could be eroded by a single refusal, turning a soldier into a supplicant.

"Full up," the gatekeeper had muttered, his voice devoid of empathy, treating the plight of a stranger as a clerical error to be corrected.

A figure detached itself from the gloom of the alley, stepping into the dim light. He was a veteran of the gutters, his posture hunched against the damp, hands buried deep in the pockets of ruined trousers. He smelled of wet wool and stale gin, a scent that spoke of survival rather than comfort.

"He says it's at capacity," Tito observed, his voice steady despite the shivering that seized his chest. "Capacity implies a limit. Is this a calculated falsehood?"

"That's the standard deterrent at Fulham," the man replied, offering a jagged, cynical grin that revealed yellowed teeth. "They always say it's full. Keeps the ledger clean and the workload light. If there's square footage, they're legally bound to take you. If I were you, I'd ring the bell again and make him do his job."

"There is a certain dark logic to testing the defenses twice," Tito murmured, assessing the threat level. He approached the heavy door and yanked the iron bell-pull. The mechanism groaned, a sound of mechanical resistance, before the door swung open almost immediately.

The same grizzled pauper stood there, acting as gatekeeper with the unearned authority of a minor official. "Here again?" he sneered. "What's the tactical objective here? Think I've got nothing better to do than wait on the likes of you?"

"I am here to fulfill an obligation," Tito stated, his gaze locking onto the man's eyes. "My mission requires resources. Your facility provides them. Let us proceed."

The man blinked, caught off guard by the sheer intensity of the stranger's demeanor. The dynamic shifted instantly; the beggar was no longer a victim but a commander issuing orders. Reluctantly, the gatekeeper stepped aside, allowing passage.

Inside the courtyard, the atmosphere changed. The cold was a physical enemy, steadily liquefying the marrow in Tito's bones and draining the last of his cellular energy. But the open archway presented a tactical anomaly, and more importantly, a lifeline.

It was a ground-level opening, thrown wide to the deluge. He had only to breach the perimeter to secure dry cover. There was no watch, no patrol, no sign of life in the immediate vicinity. Out here, he was at the mercy of the elements, another anonymous casualty waiting to be swept into the Arno. The tramp at the casual ward had surrendered his freedom to the constabulary in exchange for a warm cell. Tito preferred to maintain his operational independence.

Perhaps the villa was empty. Civilian carelessness was a luxury of the secure and the comfortable. He considered knocking to alert the occupants of their vulnerability, perhaps angling for a meager reward. But experience in a harder school had taught him the futility of relying on the gratitude of the comfortable classes. More likely, he would be turned over to the authorities as a vagrant. Better to appropriate the shelter and vanish before dawn.

He leaned over the low brick wall, testing the air. The thermal differential was immediate; warmth bled from the dark aperture. Moving with deliberate silence, he vaulted the barrier. The ground beneath his boots felt cemented and stable. He pressed himself against the exterior masonry, listening at the crevice. Complete silence. It defied logic that any homeowner would leave a ground-floor blind fully raised and the sash open in this weather, but the battlefield is largely dictated by the blunders of the enemy.

Tito slipped inside, closing the door behind him. The silence of the room was absolute, a stark contrast to the chaotic noise of the street. He moved toward the fire, the heat radiating from the logs providing a temporary respite from the encroaching cold. He checked his supplies, ensuring the armor was secured, his mind already mapping the next phase of his journey. The uncertainty of the past hour had been resolved; the path ahead, however difficult, was now defined by action rather than despair.
