The girl’s confession, torn from her by fear, struck me with unexpected force. It humiliated me too—had I given her reason to hope? I was no courtier, no whisperer of pretty lies. Battle and blade were my arts, not sighing over trinkets or pressing wilted flowers to my lips. Yet here I stood, tongue-tied before a weeping beauty.

Facing a horde of savage Tharks would have been simpler. But duty left no choice. Gently, I pried her hands from my neck, holding them as I spoke of Dejah Thoris—of how, across two worlds and countless years, she alone had claimed my heart.

Phaidor did not take it well.

She recoiled like a struck serpent, eyes blazing, lips curled in a snarl. *"Dog!"* she spat. *"Blasphemer! Phaidor does not beg—she commands! What care I for your pitiful love for that outer-world wretch? You spurn me? Then know this: your Dejah Thoris will die screaming. And you? You will grovel as the lowest slave until I grant you death—a death I will watch from the Golden Cliffs as the white apes feast on your bones!"*

Her vengeance was exquisitely planned. Yet she’d forgotten one detail. I gestured to the nearest porthole.

Reality crashed upon her. The swirling black depths beyond the glass drained her fury. She crumpled onto a bench, sobbing—not a goddess now, but a frightened girl.

Our descent continued until the glass grew warm with the pressure of unfathomable depths. Then came a lurch, the whir of propellers, and we shot forward through a submerged tunnel. Light bloomed ahead—a searchbeam cutting the abyss.

We surfaced in a cavern pool, its edges lined with dim passageways. Xodar marched us to a shaft where a magnetic elevator waited. The ascent was swift, Mars’ weak gravity offering little resistance.

Then—light.

No earthly tongue could describe the gardens of Issus. Scarlet sward, ivory trees heavy with violet blooms, paths of crushed rubies and emeralds leading to a golden temple that defied reason. Even my battle-hardened eyes staggered at the spectacle.

*"The Temple of Issus,"* Phaidor whispered.

Xodar’s smirk held malice as black nobles and red slaves swarmed around us. At the temple’s entrance, guards halted us. After a hushed conference, we were led through labyrinthine halls to a vast chamber.

Then came humiliation.

*"On your knees,"* ordered an officer. *"Back in. Do not look upon the Goddess."*

I’d sooner face a thousand swords than crawl backward like a whipped cur. Yet for Dejah Thoris’ sake, I obeyed, inching across cold jewels until a reedy voice spoke:

*"The woman pleases me,"* decreed Issus. *"She shall serve. The man returns to Shador. Woman—turn and behold your doom. Know that those who see Issus’ glory live but a year beyond the sight."*

Phaidor trembled as if pulled by invisible wires. Her arm brushed mine—a shudder ran through her. I braced for divine radiance.

The doors creaked open.

What stood there was no goddess.

A withered crone, bald as a skull, yellow fangs jutting from sunken gums. Her body was a map of wrinkles, draped in rotting silks. Phaidor’s whimper confirmed it—this monstrosity was the object of Barsoom’s worship.

*"This is the slayer of seven First Born?"* Issus’ voice was a bat’s screech.

Xodar was dragged forth. Issus’ rage was grotesque. *"Degrade him! Let him serve slaves in the gardens!"*

Xodar stood unflinching as his trappings were torn away. Pride intact, he strode out.

Issus turned her rheumy eyes on me. *"To Shador with him. Later, he’ll fight for my amusement. Go."*

As guards led me away, Phaidor rushed after me. *"Take me with you!"* she begged. *"I retract my words! Let me share your prison—we’ve both seen Issus. We have a year left. Let it be together!"*

I shook my head. *"You honor me, Phaidor. But my heart belongs to another—Dejah Thoris. That truth stands, even before gods and death."*

For a long moment, she searched my face. Then, without anger, only infinite sorrow, she whispered:

And she was gone.
