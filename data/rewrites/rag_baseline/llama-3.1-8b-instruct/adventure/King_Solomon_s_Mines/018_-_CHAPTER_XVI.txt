The darkness of the third day since our last great revelation settled over the land like a shroud, casting long shadows across the humble huts at the foot of the Three Witches, a triangular peak of mountains where Solomon's Great Road converged. Our small party, consisting of Good, Sir Henry, Foulata, Gagool, and a retinue of guards, stirred fitfully in the stillness. As I sat cross-legged on the earthen floor, the flickering flame of the fire danced across the weathered faces of my companions, illuminating the lines etched into their skin by the unforgiving sun and the weight of our quest.

Gagool, borne aloft in her litter, continued her incessant muttering, a low, raspy chant that seemed to seep into the very marrow of my bones. Her words, a jumble of curses and incantations, were lost to me, but the malevolent energy they conveyed was palpable. The air was heavy with an unspoken sense of foreboding, a feeling that had grown with each step of our journey.

As the night wore on, I found myself drawn to the windowless huts, their thatched roofs blending seamlessly into the surrounding landscape. The wind, a mournful sigh, rustled through the reeds and grasses, a sound that echoed the whispers of the womenfolk back in the city, their lives a testament to the unforgiving nature of fate. And yet, even in the face of such uncertainty, I felt an insatiable hunger to uncover the secrets that lay hidden within the depths of the Three Witches.

The next morning, the sun burst forth in a blaze of glory, casting a golden light upon the rugged peaks. The twisted snow-wreaths, like Nature's own twisted fingers, reached for the sky, while the heather-drenched moors below seemed to writhe and twist like a living entity. Before us, the Great Road, a serpentine ribbon of white, stretched upwards towards the central peak, its terminus a mystery waiting to be unraveled.

As we set out on our march, the excitement that had been building within me for so long threatened to consume me whole. I pushed aside the misgivings that had taken hold of Good and Sir Henry, my friends, and we pressed on, our footsteps echoing off the granite walls of the mountain.
