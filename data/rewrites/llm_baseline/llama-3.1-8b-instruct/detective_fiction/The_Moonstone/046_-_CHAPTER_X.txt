As I stepped out into the crisp afternoon air, the scent of wild thyme and heather wafted through the misty veil that shrouded the moors surrounding Frizinghall. The weight of the morning's events hung heavy on my shoulders, and I felt an unshakeable restlessness, a sense that my feet should be moving, my hands busy with some tangible task. I had planned to visit Mrs. Ablewhite, but the thought of polite conversation and stifled emotions proved too much to bear. Instead, I opted for a solitary walk, leaving a note for Gabriel Betteredge, asking him to delay our meeting until the afternoon. He would not be short of company in Frizinghall, I knew, with his extensive network of friends eager to share a meal and a tale or two.

My footsteps carried me further afield, into the rolling hills and verdant valleys that cradled the village. The wind rustled through the grasses, and the sun broke through the clouds, casting a warm glow over the landscape. As I walked, my mind turned to the prospect of a hearty dinner, and I found myself craving the simple pleasures of a well-cooked meal. Perhaps, I mused, a traditional Frizinghall dish – a rich game pie, or a steaming plate of roasted lamb – would provide the perfect antidote to the turmoil brewing within me.

Eventually, my watch told me it was time to return to Mr. Candy's house. I made my way back, my thoughts consumed by the prospect of finally speaking with Ezra Jennings. His words, I recalled, held the key to understanding the mysterious events that had brought me to this place. As I approached the house, I noticed the quiet stillness that pervaded the air. It was a silence broken only by the distant hum of bees as they flitted between the flowers in the garden, and the occasional, mournful notes of a piano drifting from a neighboring house.

Upon entering the house, I was ushered into a small, sparsely furnished room that seemed to be a makeshift surgery. The walls were adorned with faded diagrams of various ailments, and a bookshelf held a collection of dusty medical texts, topped with a skull that seemed out of place among the more somber tones of the room. A large, cluttered desk dominated the space, surrounded by a few wooden chairs that seemed to belong in a humble kitchen rather than a doctor's office.
