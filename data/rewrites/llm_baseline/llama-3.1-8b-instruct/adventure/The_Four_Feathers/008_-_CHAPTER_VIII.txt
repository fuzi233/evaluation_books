The scent of blooming lilacs wafted through the crisp June air as Durrance strolled through Hyde Park, his eyes scanning the vibrant scene before him. Women in delicate pastel hues glided effortlessly across the grass, their laughter and conversation carrying on the breeze. Durrance, sun-kissed and worn from his travels, stood out among the crowd, his rugged features a testament to his time abroad. Amidst the throngs of people, one figure caught his eye – Mrs. Adair, her dark hair styled in an elegant updo, her eyes gleaming with a hint of curiosity.

As she approached, Durrance rose from his bench, his movements fluid despite the stiffness in his joints. Mrs. Adair's gaze swept over him, her expression unreadable, yet Durrance sensed a glimmer of recognition in her eyes. She was comparing him to the man he had been three years prior, searching for signs of change. Her attire, a somber black dress, seemed out of place amidst the vibrant colors of the park, and Durrance's mind immediately turned to the reason behind her somber choice.

"I'm so sorry, Mrs. Adair," he said gently, his words barely above a whisper. "I had not heard about your husband."

Mrs. Adair's eyes clouded, and for a moment, Durrance thought he saw a flicker of pain. "Eighteen months ago," she replied, her voice low and measured. "He was riding with the Pytchley when his horse threw him. He didn't suffer."

Durrance's expression softened, and he offered a sympathetic smile. Mrs. Adair's silence was a palpable thing, a wall that Durrance found himself struggling to breach. He recalled Mr. Adair as a kind soul, fond of his wife, but now, gazing into Mrs. Adair's enigmatic face, Durrance realized he had never truly seen her before.

"I remember your husband as a warm and affable man," Durrance ventured, attempting to break the silence. "I'm sure he was deeply loved by those around him."

Mrs. Adair's response was a slow nod, her eyes drifting away from his. Durrance sensed a hesitation in her, as if she was weighing her words carefully. "Harry Feversham and his wife," he asked, steering the conversation toward more familiar terrain. "Are they in town?"
