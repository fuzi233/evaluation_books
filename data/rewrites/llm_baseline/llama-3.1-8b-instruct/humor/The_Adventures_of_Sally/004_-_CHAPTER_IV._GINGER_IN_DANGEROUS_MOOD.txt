As the days passed, Bruce Carmyle and Ginger found themselves in Piccadilly, each having taken a different route back to London from Roville. Ginger would have preferred to maintain the distance between them, still reeling from their tumultuous past. However, Carmyle intercepted him, his expression unusually affable.

"Ah, perfect timing," Carmyle said, extending a cigarette case. Ginger hesitated, sensing a trap, as if Carmyle was laying a bait. His wariness was palpable; in all their years of acquaintance, Carmyle's warmth was a novelty. It was clear that the affair of Scrymgeour still lingered, and Ginger knew that the family was discussing it.

Carmyle asked about his recent return to London, and Ginger responded curtly. Carmyle then mentioned their chance encounter at Roville Station, where he had met Miss Nicholas. Ginger's reaction was immediate and violent. Carmyle continued, describing their brief conversation, but Ginger's interest waned. Carmyle's intentions were transparent – he was intrigued by Sally, and Ginger's reluctance only fueled his curiosity.

A hint of possessiveness crept into Carmyle's tone as he inquired about Sally's address. Ginger's response was guarded, as if guarding a prized possession. Carmyle's persistence wore him down, and he eventually shared the sacred number of Sally's street. Carmyle jotted it down in his notebook, a habit he had honed over the years. The atmosphere shifted, and Carmyle's demeanor turned cool, his voice laced with a familiar reproof.

"I saw Uncle Donald this morning," he said, his tone a stark contrast to the earlier warmth. Ginger's mood darkened, recalling their complicated history. The tension between them was palpable, a reminder of the unresolved issues that still lingered.
