As the evening wore on at Lennon House, the warm glow of the chandeliers cast a golden light on the assembly, and the lively chatter of the ballroom mingled with the sweet scent of blooming flowers. Harry Feversham, his mask of gaiety firmly in place, watched Ethne dance with an air of effortless elegance, her laughter and conversation flowing like a rich sauce through the evening's festivities. Her steps seemed to glide across the floor, her feet barely touching the ground, as if she were floating on a cloud of happiness. But beneath her carefree exterior, a cauldron of emotions simmered, threatening to boil over at any moment.

Feversham knew this all too well. He had been a silent witness to the turmoil brewing within her, and he felt the weight of her pain like a heavy burden on his own shoulders. As they twirled across the dance floor, the soft music swirling around them, he couldn't help but steal glances at her, searching for some sign of weakness, some crack in the facade. But she was a master of concealment, and her mask remained firmly in place, even as the morning light began to seep through the blinds, casting a pale glow over the room.

It was then that her control finally slipped, and for a fleeting instant, her composure cracked. As she gazed toward the windows, her eyes clouded over, and her face paled, like a perfectly baked soufflé that had begun to sink. "Look!" she whispered, her voice trembling, and Feversham felt her weight sag against him, as if she had been a delicate pastry that had been subjected to too much heat. He steadied her, his hands firm around her waist, and her eyes fluttered open, like the first tender shoots of a spring garden bursting forth from the earth.

For a moment, they stood there, suspended in time, the music swirling around them like a rich broth. Then, in a voice as weary as a long-simmered stew, she spoke the words that would haunt Feversham forever: "I wonder whether it is right that one should suffer so much pain." The phrase hung in the air, like the savory aroma of a slow-cooked dish, filling his senses and lingering long after the moment had passed. He felt the sting of her words, like a pinch of salt added to a dish, and knew that he had caused her suffering, that he had been the one to wound her so deeply.
