As twilight descended upon the hills, Ethne retreated to the warmth of her farmhouse, the flickering flames of the fire dancing across her face like a chorus of restless spirits. Her fingers drummed a staccato beat on the armrest of her chair, a nervous habit she'd cultivated in the days leading up to this moment. For weeks, perhaps months, she'd anticipated the brief visit from Harry Feversham, her heart swelling with every tick of the clock. Now, as the embers cast eerie shadows on the walls, she felt the crushing weight of his absence, a hollow ache that threatened to consume her. The air inside was heavy with the scent of wood smoke and the faint tang of damp earth, a poignant reminder of the solitude that had become her constant companion.

As she gazed into the fire, her thoughts drifted to the dinner she'd prepared earlier – a simple yet satisfying meal of roasted lamb and root vegetables, carefully selected from the garden she tended with such devotion. The sizzle of the meat on the spit still lingered in her mind, a comforting reminder of the tangible world outside her own. She recalled the way the sunlight had danced across the kitchen floor, casting a golden glow on the wooden spoons and utensils, and the soft hum of the bees as they flitted from flower to flower in the nearby meadow.

But these fleeting pleasures were lost on her now, as she struggled to shake off the feeling of desolation that clung to her like a damp shroud. Tomorrow, she reassured herself, would bring a new dawn, a fresh start, and with it, the chance to relive the moments she'd shared with Harry. The memory of their parting at Lennon House still lingered, a bittersweet taste on her lips, and she knew that with each passing day, the pain would ease, like the gentle lapping of waves against the shore.

Just as she was beginning to collect her thoughts, a knock at the door broke the silence, and a soft voice announced the arrival of Colonel Durrance. Ethne rose from her chair, her movements deliberate, as she made her way to greet him. The dog, Dermod's old collie, sensed her distress and nuzzled her hand, its eyes filled with a deep understanding. She stroked its fur, finding solace in the familiar comfort of their bond.
