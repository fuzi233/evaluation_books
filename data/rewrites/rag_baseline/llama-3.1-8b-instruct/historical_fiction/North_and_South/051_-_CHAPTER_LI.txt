The sun had long since surrendered to the gathering dusk, casting a warm orange glow over the faded elegance of the Hale family's country estate. Within the walls of their stately home, Margaret Hale lay reclined on her bed, her dark hair a tangled halo around her pale face. Edith, her cousin, entered the room with a flourish, a vision in silk and lace, her bright smile faltering as she took in Margaret's wan complexion. The air was heavy with the scent of blooming flowers, and Edith's fingers itched to rearrange the arrangement on Margaret's dresser – a riot of blue and gold that clashed with the somber tones of her aunt's belongings.

"Dixon, not those garish blue flowers with that dull gold gown," Edith said, her voice a gentle reprimand. "Wait, let me fetch some pomegranate blossoms instead." Dixon, the loyal maid, hesitated, her eyes rolling heavenward as she replied, "It's not a dull gold, miss, it's a straw colour. And blue suits straw perfectly." But Edith had already swept in with a bouquet of vibrant scarlet blooms, their petals like tiny, fiery sparks against the muted backdrop of the room.

As she worked her magic, Edith turned to Margaret, her expression a mix of concern and curiosity. "Where have you been, dear cousin? I've been searching everywhere for you. I do hope you're not getting into some scrape, traipsing about those dreadful streets in Milton. I wouldn't dare venture out without a chaperone, it's simply not seemly for a lady."

Dixon, still smarting from Edith's criticism, shot back with a tart retort, "I think it's rich, coming from someone who talks so much about being a lady, yet is so terribly afraid of the world." Her words hung in the air like a challenge, but Edith merely laughed, her eyes sparkling with amusement.

Just then, Margaret stirred, her cheeks flushing with a mixture of embarrassment and fatigue. "Here I am, Edith. I'm sorry I've kept you waiting. I'm not feeling quite myself today, that's all." Her voice was barely above a whisper, and Edith's smile softened as she reached out to smooth a strand of hair behind Margaret's ear.
