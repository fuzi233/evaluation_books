In the rarefied world of high society, Major Arthur Pendennis embodied the quintessential gentleman, renowned for his discerning palate and impeccable taste in wine. As a stalwart member of the esteemed Pall Mall Club, he was often summoned to preside over its Committee, leveraging his extraordinary knowledge to guide the institution towards unparalleled excellence. The club's staff, attuned to his refined sensibilities, accorded him the deference typically reserved for royalty or military luminaries.

Upon arriving at the club around a quarter past ten, Pendennis presented himself with characteristic flair, resplendent in his finest attire: a crisp blacked suit, a faultlessly tied cravat, and an impeccably laundered shirt that would put even the celebrated Beau Brummel to shame. His coat, gloves, whiskers, and cane formed an ensemble that epitomized the sartorial elegance of a retired military officer. Although his rich brown hair appeared luxuriant, a closer inspection revealed telltale signs of its artificial origins, while his eyes, though handsome, betrayed a hint of weariness. His nose, bearing a striking resemblance to the Duke of Wellington's, was a testament to his noble heritage. Adorning his wristbands were exquisite gold buttons bestowed upon him by the Duke of York, complemented by several elegant rings, including a particularly impressive piece featuring the heraldic arms of Pendennis.

As he took his customary seat at the designated table, a small gathering of admirers and acquaintances would often assemble to bask in his presence. Among them was the enigmatic Glowry, a Scottish surgeon who harbored a begrudging respect for Pendennis's numerous invitations and social conquests. The Major's correspondence, laid out in anticipation of his arrival, was a veritable treasure trove of invitations, each one carefully scrutinized and filed away with the precision of a true connoisseur. Whether navigating the intricacies of etiquette, dissecting the latest societal gossip, or offering sage advice on matters of the heart, Pendennis was the go-to authority, consulted by marchionesses and young gentlemen alike. His reputation as a paragon of refinement and courtesy was matched only by his ability to effortlessly command attention and admiration from all who crossed his path.
