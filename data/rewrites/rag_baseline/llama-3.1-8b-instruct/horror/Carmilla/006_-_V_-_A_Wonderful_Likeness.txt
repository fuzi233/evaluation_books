The evening drew in, shrouding the schloss in a soft, velvety darkness that seemed to cling to every stone and windowpane like a damp shroud. It was a moment when time itself appeared to slow, as if the world outside had grown heavy with anticipation, waiting for the whispered secrets that would soon be revealed. And then, without warning, a rattle of wheels echoed through the stillness, announcing the arrival of the picture cleaner from Gratz. He drove up in his horse-drawn cart, the two large packing cases stacked precariously on the bed, adorned with the promise of forgotten tales and dusty memories.

As the news spread through the halls, a murmur of excitement rippled through the assembled household, drawing everyone to the entrance hall like moths to a flame. My mother, with her Hungarian heritage, had a special affinity for the stories and traditions that these old pictures held, and I knew that she was eagerly anticipating the unveiling of the restored portraits. My father, ever the scholar, had a list in hand, carefully noting the corresponding numbers as the picture cleaner worked his magic, coaxing the faded images back to life.

Carmilla, usually so languid and detached, seemed transfixed by the proceedings, her eyes drifting between the newly restored portraits and my face, as if searching for some hidden connection. I watched her, sensing a strange, unspoken understanding between us, a bond that went beyond words. As the picture cleaner produced each successive portrait, my mother's eyes lit up with recognition, her hands reaching out to touch the faces of her ancestors, now freed from the shackles of time and neglect.

And then, with a flourish, the picture cleaner produced the final portrait, its corner bearing the name 'Mircalla, Countess Karnstein,' and the date '1698.' I felt a shiver run down my spine as I recognized the subject – it was Carmilla, her image frozen in time, yet radiating an otherworldly vitality. My father's response was subdued, his gaze darting away, as if he too sensed the weight of this discovery. Carmilla, however, seemed entranced, her eyes locked onto mine, a hint of a smile playing on her lips.
