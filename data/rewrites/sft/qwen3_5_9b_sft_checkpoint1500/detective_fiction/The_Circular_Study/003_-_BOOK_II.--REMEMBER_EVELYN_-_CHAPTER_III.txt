Simultaneously, the individual who had just mimicked the recent tragedy with terrifying precision rose to his feet. A profound daze replaced his earlier ferocity; he bent to retrieve the shattered glass he had dropped and turned to exit the study.

"Wait, sir!" Mr. Gryce's voice cut through the silence. "You cannot remove that evidence until we understand how your master met his end."

The command, delivered with authority, failed to register any tremor in the man's composure. He stepped past Styles, who reached out to intercept him.

"Stop!" Styles barked, seizing the servant's arm. "Do not try to slip away like smoke! Do you not hear the officer speaking to you?"

For the first time, the appeal pierced the fog of the man's mind. The glass slipped from his grasp, striking the floor with a final, shattering *clink* that mingled with a sound neither quite a scream nor a groan—a jagged, animalistic noise. As his gaze landed on the detectives restraining him, his features twisted into a mask of raw terror. Realization dawned with horrific speed; the realization of his trapped situation sent him lunging for the door. Styles, however, pinned him down, dragging the flailing body back to the center of the room, where Mr. Gryce stood over the lifeless form of the master sprawled upon the bearskin rug.

In an instant, the transformation was complete. At the sight of the corpse, a cold, hellish joy ignited the butler's vacant stare, warping his mouth into a grotesque grin. Seeking no concealment for his triumph, he let loose a low, chilling laugh that echoed through the silent study like a ghost's whisper.

Mr. Gryce felt a surge of revulsion at the irresponsible madness before him. He waited until the last reverberations of that diabolical laughter faded into the high corners of the ceiling. Then, locking his piercing gaze onto the servant's wild eyes, he commanded, "Speak! Who killed this man? You lived in this house; you must know."

The butler's lips parted, and a stream of unintelligible guttural clicks erupted from his throat. With his free hand, he frantically touched his ears, then his mouth, shaking his head violently in denial.

"It was obvious, then," Gryce muttered, the pieces falling quickly. "He is both deaf and mute."

The revelation knocked the wind out of Styles. His grip loosened instantly. Freed from restraint, the man shuffled back to his corner, where he began to reenact the murder and the subsequent grief with renewed vigor, a performance that now seemed less a confession and more a ritual of mockery. Standing amidst the stillness of death, the gleam of infernal satisfaction in the servant's restless eyes made his presence feel like a sacrilege.

Styles could endure it no longer. "Can't you speak?" he roared, frustration mounting. "Can't you hear me?"

The man merely wore a slow, ghoulish smile. Gryce intervened, cutting the silence short. "Remove him," Gryce ordered. "Search him thoroughly for bloodstains. I am going upstairs to where you first found him. If he is truly innocent, he will bear no trace of this crime."

Yet, for once, the seasoned detective faced a blank wall. The search yielded nothing: no blood on the old servant, no fingerprints, no fibers linking him to the scene. Disappointed, Gryce retreated to the study, turning his attention back to the two rooms, hoping to find a clue hidden in plain sight among the overturned furniture and scattered papers.

His efforts were fruitless. Lost in contemplation of the deceased's frozen face, which seemed to hold a story of unspoken resolve, a voice suddenly rang out from the shadows above.

"Remember Evelyn!"

The cry, sharp and clear, echoed down the stairwell. Evelyn? In a house stripped bare of every living soul, who was Evelyn, and who spoke? Gryce looked upward. Swinging from a cage suspended near the apex of a window, an English starling tilted its head, recognizing the focus of attention. With a sudden, fierce squawk, the bird repeated the phrase: "Remember Evelyn!"

It was the final, uncanny thread in a tapestry of nightmares. An odd sense of dread settled over Gryce as he leaned forward to get a better look at the bird. Suddenly, without warning, the stark white illumination flooding the study shifted, swirling into a sickly emerald green. Gryce realized with a jolt that he had inadvertently pressed a switch on the complex electrical fixture above him, altering the hue of the light.

Irritated by this trivial puzzle that interrupted his investigation, Gryce stepped back toward the door. Just then, Styles's voice drifted in from the antechamber, where he was keeping watch over the mute servant.

"Shall I release him, Mr. Gryce? He appears quite agitated, though not threatening. He looks as though he has forgotten a duty or recalled a terrible omission."

"Yes, let him go," Gryce replied swiftly. "But keep close behind him. Watch every step. Unconscious movements may yield us vital leads."

As Gryce pushed open the curtain to step out, the cry from above rang out once more—"Remember Evelyn!"—so imperative and strange that he felt compelled to glance back upward. Was the appeal meant for him alone, or perhaps for the man below, whose ears remained forever sealed? Whether trivial or central to the mystery, the detective resolved to ignore it for the moment. The mute servant emerged from the hall, returning to his station with the stiff, respectful posture of a trained employee. Carrying his master's hat in one hand and the umbrella in the other, he handed them to Gryce before retreating silently to his chair, sinking almost immediately back into his catatonic stupor.

"Hmph!" Gryce grumbled, placing the items on the hall stand. "A confused mind, unreliable memory, worthless testimony. Has the tragedy driven him mad, or does his insanity precede the crime?" He might have pondered further if the persistent ringing of the front door bell—the signal of the growing crowd outside—had not been abruptly silenced by the heavy, authoritative knock of an official visitor.

"The coroner, or some other weighty figure," Gryce noted as the door swung wide, ending his solitary golden hour.
