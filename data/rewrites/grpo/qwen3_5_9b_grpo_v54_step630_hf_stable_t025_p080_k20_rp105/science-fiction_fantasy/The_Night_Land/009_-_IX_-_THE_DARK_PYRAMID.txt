Two hours later, I had descended from the Gorge and stood upon that alien country. Though my mind remained troubled and bewildered, a surge of joy welled up within me at the startling brilliance of this sudden land.

Before reaching the mouth of the Gorge, I had stood high within its throat, gazing out over the vast expanse. I counted twenty-seven great volcanoes, ignoring two monstrous ranges of fire-hills burning to my right, nor did I tally the hundred thousand lesser fires scattered about. Truly, it appeared a realm of fire and water. A small fire-hill stood isolated within a sea, perhaps a mile from where I stood, with a dozen or so more spreading behind it. Here, I must explain the seas: I counted three small ones at that time, and a mighty sea stretching endlessly into the red light of the fire-hills, vanishing utterly from sight with no visible end.

Islands rose from these waters, crowned with volcanoes; in other places, the fire-hills erupted straight from the sea. Over the near sea, a plentitude of steam hung, suggesting the ocean boiled at intervals in diverse places. Within the red atmosphere of the place, a low, constant muttering thunder shook the air, shifting from distance to nearness. I judged this to be the voices of the fire-hills, speaking through the fire that lived within them.

How utterly new this was to me! There existed in that country a constant Voice of the Energy of Life, restoring the World-Noise of our Age with keen and undeniable apparentness, perhaps even more intense than it is now.

Let me set down more closely what was ready to my gaze. First, a huge, blackened mountain rose to the left of the Gorge's mouth, ascending into the night for perhaps fifteen or twenty miles. From its side grew a mighty peaked volcano, rising five miles high on the far side. Above it, a second peak soared another nine or ten miles into the blackness. Furthermore, two other mighty fire-hills burned and glowed at an utter height upon the left crest of that black mountain, appearing to make strange, smouldering suns within the night. Below these upward fires rose vast mountains of ash and burned stuff, cast forth by the perched volcanoes and pouring downward through Eternity to build grey, sombre monuments to the dreadful glory of Time.

To my right lay endless sea and the red blazing of the fire-hills; to my left, mighty forests rose, with monstrous fire-hills appearing beyond the great woods. This was the first impression upon my brain and senses.

After descending from the Gorge, I paused, unsure which way led to my search. I looked about for a long while, then climbed back up, calling myself foolish for failing to map my route before coming down. Upon returning to the Gorge, I saw there was but one way: to the right lay only the seas, but to the left, where the shores met the waters, a clear path seemed to extend for a space. Perhaps, having come so far, I would find a further way forward. Thus, I descended again into the Country of the Seas, that red-shining realm of water and fire.

By the time I returned from the Gorge's height, twenty-four hours had passed since my last sleep. I was forced to seek a nook to slumber. I found a neat place where three great trees surrounded a small, dry, warm rock basin. After eating three tablets and drinking some water—my belly yearning as ever for proper food—I made my bed in the rock basin and lay down, thinking briefly of Naani before falling asleep.

Suddenly, I awoke to find myself swimming in hot water. A mercy, I thought, that I had not drowned. I stood up; the basin was full of hot, steaming water, pungent to the taste. I realized the water poured in from a smoothed slit on the far side, gurgling and bubbling, driven upward by a deep, boiling well. Gladly, it did not boil as it emerged.

Now on dry land, I quickly surmised that the water poured forth into the basin seasonally for eternity, then receded through fissures in the basin's bottom, a cycle repeating slightly beyond the length of every hour. Indeed, the basin emptied slowly as I watched.

Much wetted, I stripped off my armor, having previously dipped my effects into the hot pool. I found a spot where the rock was hot and spread my garments. While they dried, I entered the pool for a pleasant bath, feeling no great fear of danger; I had surely left such threats behind in the Night Lands. Yet, I held the Diskos at the pool's edge, lacking proper assurance. As it proved, many monstrous beasts inhabited that country, but I never felt the nearness or horrid power of any Evil Force. These, I conceived, were gathered about the Mighty Pyramid, attracted by the great spiritual essence of so many humans crowded in one spot, much like sharks following a ship carrying cattle. Yet, how the Evil Powers gained entrance to this state of life, I knew not; my reasoning on the matter was surely vanity, for there is no certainty in my conclusions.

Presently, my clothing was dry. Before dressing, I emerged from the bath, which had nearly drained back into the earth. I donned my armor and felt a more lightsome state of mind, yet remained ready for sleep. I slept six more hours beside the pool, woken occasionally by the gurgling and bubbling as the pool filled again.

When the six hours ended, I awoke refreshed, ready to proceed through the red-lighted country. I ate and drank, then moved forward at a great pace. The nameless woods lay to my left, the shores of the seas to my right. Often, trees grew into the water, forcing me to walk among them. A wonderful mystery lived among these forest trees, a glad and wildness unknown to me in the Underground Fields, though those groves possessed solemn beauty. The scent of the woods was sweet to my spirit.

Throughout my journey, the shore remained to my right, while the great forests surrounded me to the left. Life thrived in those darksome woods; living eyes peered out at me occasionally before retreating into the dark. I did not know whether to fear or ignore the trouble, yet nothing came near to cause harm.

Three times that day I encountered little fire-hills burning redly, sending out fire and noise. I heard their trouble through the forest before reaching them. Around each was a deadness and desolation where the fire had killed the big trees, yet the quick life of little plants grew more nigh, born and living between the fire-bursts. In that single day, I passed thirty-seven boiling springs. Whether they truly boiled, I knew not, only that they sent out great steam often, and some made a strong roaring noise, sounding like wild beasts roaring strangely when heard afar off in the forests.

At the eighteenth hour, I sat down as I had at the sixth and twelfth, eating two tablets and drinking water that fizzed richly and quickly. Then I lay down to sleep, greatly wearied. I chose a place against a great rock so that no creature could approach from behind. I fell easily into sleep, but fixed in my mind that I would slumber only with my body, knowing from the shining eyes in the darksome woods that strange creatures abode in the mighty forests.

Before sleeping, I thought of Naani, as I had much that day, feeling her spirit hovering near, striving pitifully to speak with me. I set this out so you may know how it seemed to me in my thoughts. As I lay there, I placed a blessing upon her and determined to make a more desperate speed of my going, if possible, to reach the strange, unknown place in the dead world where the lesser Refuge stood. Then I was asleep in a moment.

Suddenly, I awoke. Lovely was the brightness of that country, showing me my danger instantly, unlike the grey darkness and strange shadows of the Night Land. On the instant I propped myself up, I saw certain things crouching within the borders of the trees, no more than a score of paces off. My spirit had given me knowledge and woken me. I stared, gripping the Diskos, and saw six squat men, humped at the neck and shoulder, crouching in a row, partially hidden by shadows. They watched me, their eyes shining like beast eyes, filling me with strange terror. Yet I had the Diskos and my armor. Though my heart shook a little, my spirit was assured to conquer.

I stood up, the Diskos ready in my hand. Behold! I could not see the Humped Men; they were gone from that place. Yet I never saw them leave, keeping my gaze steadfast on where they hid. I was near ready to believe there had been nothing within the wood's border, yet truly I knew the humped men had been there, as I had seen.

I looked presently and found I had slept five hours. I ate two tablets while standing watchful, then drank some water, ready to go forward. I was grown very anxious to leave that place, knowing with no certainty but that those strangely humped men were but a little way off among the trees, able to strike me in a moment, or further, to call an army of others to my destruction.

Ready with my gear secure, I set off at a great stride, holding the Diskos handy, looking this way and that while moving onward with speed. I felt so lean and hard that it seemed I had the power to outpace those men or anything of their kind.

All that day, for thirty great hours, I went forward at that stride, always watching. At every sixth hour, I ate two tablets, drank a little water, and continued. I hoped I was lost from those Humped Men. Yet my faith was not wise; twice and thrice it came to me that things went through the woods to my left all that day, keeping always to a level with my speed, yet always hidden. As you shall believe, this was a very shaking thing to my heart, making my hope of little account.

Lacking faith to accompany my hope, I found it difficult to sleep until I reached a proper, safe place. So, as I told, I went onward through thirty great hours, finding nowhere that seemed to fit my need.

Lo! About the end of the thirtieth hour, I perceived water ahead, besides the sea ever to my right. I thought perhaps the sea went inward there, but it was otherwise. When I reached that place, I found a river coming into the sea, flowing out from all the country to my left.

In the mouth of this river lay a small island. Surely I looked across to the island, thinking it a refuge from the Humped Men playing dog upon my going. Yet, truly, this was an idle thought. My need was to find a way to cross the river to go forward beside the great sea, which stretched onward seemingly forever before me on the far side.

I knew not how to cross; I had no power to swim, and if I had, there were surely monsters in that great, warm-flowing river.

I went up the river-bank to find where the river narrowed. I would have walked a mighty distance for this purpose, but I soon came to a second river entering the first, not a mile above the sea's shore. Thus, the sea was to one side, the second river to the other, and the first river before me. I was sorely perplexed, as anyone truly in such trouble would be.

Yet, the need to go forward and the danger of the Humped Men put wit into me. I looked about for a fallen tree. There were many, yet great, so that I was much wearied and strained of spirit before getting two little trees to the water.

When this was done, I made a rough pole from a young tree-plant. Afterward, I lashed the two trees together with my belts and straps, creating something of a raft.

Picture this: all the time I worked, I was very heedful lest the Humped Men come upon me before I was free upon the water. This constant heeding doubled the labor of my work, yet in the end, it was done, and I was ready to adventure over the river.

I pushed off with the pole, paddling perhaps half an hour, for indeed it was a clumsy contrivance, and I perhaps more so in my labors. Presently, I came so far over that I drew nigh to the island. It seemed wise and proper to sleep there, then go onward to the farther shore. I did so; after eating and drinking as ever, I lay down to sleep. By this time, it was thirty-three hours since my last slumber, so I was bitterly wearied.

I had a great, restful sleep. Truly, the island seemed a very safe place. In verity, I came to no harm, though I was as a dead man for nine great hours, so you shall perceive my weariness.

When I awoke properly, I ate two tablets, drank some water, finished my voyage, and took back my belts and straps from binding the trees, continuing forward on my way, no longer fearful of the Humped Men. Surely, I thought I had left them all on the far shore of the river, though afterward I minded they might grow on both sides. Yet, I was only discovered by those on one side, as you know.

All that day I went very swiftly, passing many strange matters and coasting upon wonders often. At the sixth and twelfth hours, I ate and drank as ever. Between the eighth and fourteenth hours, I passed two mighty fire-hills that made the whole country tremble with their noise. Four times monstrous creatures passed by me, but I was swift and hid, coming to no harm.

Often as I went, my thoughts were upon the dear Maid I journeyed to save from destruction. Yet, as you must see, my thinkings were always brought sharply to my going. Scarce was I set off to ponder upon Naani before some danger or wonder gave me heed to my way. Because of this, as you have learned, I was more put to plan free of the instant trouble and peril of my way through all that mighty journey than to have quiet chance for thoughts of love unto mine Own. Yet, truly, was not my journey one whole thought of love unto Naani?
