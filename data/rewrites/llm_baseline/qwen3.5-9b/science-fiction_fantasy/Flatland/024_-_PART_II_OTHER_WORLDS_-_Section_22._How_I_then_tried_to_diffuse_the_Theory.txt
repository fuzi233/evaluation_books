My initial attempt to convert my grandson using the simple maxim—"Upward, not Northward"—proved a resounding failure. This setback did not drive me into despair, nor did it silence me entirely. Instead, it taught me a crucial lesson: slogans are insufficient weapons against the tyranny of limited perception. To truly liberate the mind, I needed proof, not just poetry. I realized that I had to construct a logical edifice, a comprehensive treatise that would lay bare the architecture of the Third Dimension.

Thus, I retreated into solitude for several months, dedicating every waking hour to the composition of a work I titled *Through Flatland to Thoughtland*. However, my task was fraught with peril. To avoid immediate detection by the Axioms and the Law, I could not speak plainly of a physical dimension. Instead, I framed my argument around the concept of "Thoughtland"—a metaphysical realm where a figure could gaze downward upon Flatland, perceiving the interior of all objects simultaneously. In this theoretical space, I described figures surrounded by six squares and terminating in eight points. Yet, even within this veil of abstraction, I faced a catastrophic obstacle: the inability to draw. In Flatland, reality consists solely of lines. There are no surfaces, no volumes, only variations in length and luminosity. Without the ability to render diagrams that show depth, shading, or perspective, my written words risked remaining as opaque and confusing to my readers as the concepts I sought to explain. I feared that despite my eloquence, the average citizen would remain blind to my meaning.

As I labored over this manuscript, my personal life crumbled under the weight of suppressed revelation. Every pleasure became a torture; every sight in our two-dimensional world served as a taunting reminder of its true three-dimensional form. The sun, the houses, the streets—all were merely cross-sections of magnificent solids, stripped of their essence. I found myself unable to contain the comparison between the flat illusion and the solid truth. I neglected my clients and abandoned my trade, consumed by a feverish desire to visualize the mysteries I had witnessed but could not share. Even attempting to recall the Cube with my eyes closed proved agonizingly difficult. On one occasion, nearly a year after my journey through Spaceland, I managed to conjure the image, yet I remained haunted by the doubt that my mental reconstruction was imperfect. Was it truly a cube, or merely a distorted memory? This uncertainty deepened my melancholy and fueled a desperate resolve to act.

I felt a willingness to sacrifice my very life for the Cause, believing that conviction was worth any price. But how could I persuade the highest Circles of the land if I could not move my own grandson? Despite this fear, my spirit often rebelled against caution. I found myself slipping into dangerous speech, quoting ancient prophecies about those who claim to see the inside of things, referring casually to "the all-seeing land," and occasionally dropping the forbidden names of the "Third" and "Fourth Dimensions."

The breaking point came at a gathering of the Local Speculative Society, hosted by the Prefect himself. An absurd speaker had just delivered a paper arguing that Providence deliberately limited us to two dimensions and reserved omniscience solely for the Supreme Being. Overcome by a surge of righteous indignation, I lost control. I recounted my entire voyage—the descent into Space, the encounter with the Sphere, the tour of the Metropolitan Assembly Hall, and my return. Initially, I feigned that I was describing the fictional adventures of another, but my passion tore through the disguise. In a final, fervent appeal, I urged the assembly to shed their prejudices and embrace the reality of the Third Dimension.

The silence that followed was absolute, broken only by the sound of guards rushing forward. Before I could finish a sentence, I was seized and dragged before the Council.

The next morning, I stood in the very chamber where the Sphere had once appeared to me. For a brief moment, I believed I might be granted the same audience. The President listened to my defense, allowing me to speak without interruption. But the atmosphere was thick with foreboding. As I spoke, the President noted the presence of the elite police—Circles of high angularity, barely below 55 degrees—and ordered them replaced by a squad of lesser officers, their angles significantly smaller. I understood instantly what this substitution signified: I was to be silenced permanently. Either I would be executed, or I would be imprisoned, and the witnesses to my story would be destroyed to ensure the secret died with me. The President preferred cheap victims to expensive ones.

When my defense concluded, the President, seemingly moved by my sincerity, posed two final questions: Could I demonstrate the direction of "Upward"? Could I describe a Cube without relying on imaginary sides or angles? I answered that I could offer no further proof than the Truth itself, which would eventually prevail. The President nodded in agreement. "Then you shall be sentenced to perpetual imprisonment," he declared. "But if the Truth intends for you to emerge and spread your message, we trust the Truth will facilitate that outcome. You shall face no unnecessary hardship, and unless you attempt to escape, you will be permitted to visit your brother periodically."

Seven years have now passed. I remain a prisoner, isolated from all human contact save for my jailers and the occasional visit from my brother. He is a Square of noble character—just, sensible, and kind. Yet, our weekly meetings are the source of my deepest sorrow. He was present when the Sphere revealed itself; he saw the shifting cross-sections and heard the explanations given to the Council. Despite my relentless efforts to recount the phenomenon and argue for the existence of Solids through analogy, he remains unconvinced. Not a single week goes by without me repeating my tale, detailing the wonders of Spaceland, yet he continues to deny the existence of a Sphere.

I am utterly devoid of converts. It seems the millennium-long revelation promised to humanity has been rendered void by my confinement. Like Prometheus chained to a rock, I sought to bring fire to my fellow citizens, but instead, I lie in darkness, offering nothing but shadows. My only hope lies in these memoirs—if they can somehow reach the minds of beings in another dimension, stirring up a rebellion against the chains of limited perception. This is the comfort of my brightest hours.

Alas, the shadow returns. Heavy is the burden of knowing that I cannot be certain of the exact shape of the Cube I once saw. In my sleep-deprived nights, the cryptic command "Upward, not Northward" haunts me like a riddle carved by a demon. There are moments of profound mental weakness when the images of Cubes and Spheres dissolve into mere fantasies, when the Third Dimension feels as illusory as the Void or the Point. In these dark seasons, even the walls of my cell, the paper beneath my pen, and the solid ground of Flatland seem less than real—merely the delirious hallucinations of a broken mind, a baseless fabric woven from the threads of a nightmare.
