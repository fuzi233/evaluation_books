I woke buzzing, every edge of my polygon frame thrumming with the giddy certainty that I held the keys to revolutionize Flatland. The entire glorious, purposeful career stretched out before me like a perfectly straight line: I would go forth at once, evangelize every last soul in our two-dimensional realm. Even the Women, those thin, wandering lines, even the rough triangular Soldiers who rarely bothered with abstract geometry, would hear the Gospel of Three Dimensions. My first stop, I decided, would be my own Wife.

I had barely mapped out the opening lines of my speech when the shouts of a crowd in the street outside died down to silence. A louder, booming herald’s voice cut through the hush, and I went still. It was the public proclamation of the High Circle Council, ordering that any person found spreading delusional tales of other worlds, or claiming to have received supernatural revelations from realms beyond our two axes, would face immediate arrest, life imprisonment, or summary execution.

A cold prickle ran up all four of my sides. This was no idle threat, and I would not trifle with it. There was no need to lead with my encounter with the Sphere, after all, I reasoned. Pure mathematical demonstration would carry just as much weight, no seditious revelation required. The phrase “Upward, not Northward” was the entire, perfect clue to the proof. It had felt as obvious as basic addition when I woke fresh from my journey through Spaceland, but now, in the quiet of my parlor, it was already growing slipperier, like a half-grasped memory of a dream that slips through your edges when you try to name it.

My Wife drifted into the room just then, soft and curious, asking after the mysterious Circle that had burst through our locked walls the night before. We exchanged a few trivial pleasantries, and I quickly decided she was no fit student for this work. My Pentagon sons, respected local physicians of good standing, were loyal and loving, but they had never cared for the finer points of spatial math, and worse, their devotion to the authority of the Circles ran far deeper than any familial tenderness. I could not guarantee they would not turn me over to the Prefect if they heard me spouting what the Council called heresy.

Then the perfect pupil occurred to me: my precocious little Hexagon grandson, that bright young thing with a knack for numbers who had once casually puzzled over the meaning of 3³, a comment that had made the Sphere himself chuckle. He was young, too young to have paid much mind to the Council’s proclamation, too playful to run tattling to the authorities. I could teach him perfectly safely, I thought.

I weaved a elaborate, barely truthful tale to my Wife to distract her, telling her the visiting Circle was conducting a routine high-level geometrical inspection that the public was not cleared to know about. She bought it at last, and drifted off to tend to her household chores none the wiser about the World of Three Dimensions. No sooner had she left than I sent a maid to fetch my grandson. The edges of my memory of Spaceland were fraying faster by the minute, the image of the Sphere’s curved shape fading to no more than a faint glimmer, and I was desperate to make at least one disciple before I lost the proof entirely.

When he entered the parlor, I quickly turned the key in the lock to secure the door, then sat down beside him at the little line-tablet we used for his math lessons. I walked him through the basics we had covered the day before: a zero-dimensional point moving along one axis draws a one-dimensional line. That whole line, moving perpendicular to its own length Northward, draws a two-dimensional Square.

I forced a light laugh and nudged his shoulder. “And now, you little scamp, you wanted to make me believe that a Square might move the same way, not Northward but *Upward, not Northward*, to make a new sort of extra shape, a third-dimensional extra Square. Go on, say that again, you young rascal.”

At that exact moment, the herald’s “O yes! O yes!” blared right outside our window, repeating the full text of the Council’s ban. My grandson, sharp as any child of his age and raised to revere the Circles’ authority, went dead silent until the herald’s voice had faded completely. Then he burst into tears.

“Grandpapa! That was only my silly joke! I never meant any of it! We didn’t know the new law existed when we were talking yesterday! I never said anything about a Third Dimension, I swear it! I definitely never said ‘Upward, not Northward’ — that’s nonsense! How could anything move a direction that isn’t North or South? It’s ridiculous! Even a baby wouldn’t be that stupid! Ha! Ha! Ha!”

“None of it is silly!” I snapped, my temper slipping. I grabbed a small, moveable wooden square cutout off the table. “Look right here — I take this Square, and I move it, not Northward, but — yes! I move it Upward — that is to say, not Northward, I move it somewhere — not exactly this way, but somehow —”

My sentence died in my throat. I was wiggling the square back and forth in completely random, purposeless directions, unable to show him the direction I meant, flailing like a senile old fool. My grandson cackled so hard his small six-sided sides shook, declared I was pulling a silly prank on him instead of teaching math, ran over to twist the lock open, and bolted out of the room before I could stop him.

That was the end of my first attempt to convert anyone to the Gospel of Three Dimensions.
