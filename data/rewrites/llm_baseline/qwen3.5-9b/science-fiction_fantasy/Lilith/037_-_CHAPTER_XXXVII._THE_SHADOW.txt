A low murmur of relief rippled through my companions; they had finally spotted their kin in the distance. Two riders mounted on Lona's great beast spurred forward to join the gathering, only to be met by a wavering shout that instantly dissolved into silence. As we closed the gap, the sound of their weeping reached us—a chorus of tiny, breaking waves.

When I stepped among them, the truth struck me with chilling clarity: something terrible had befallen these children. Their young faces bore the hollowed-out stare of a terror far stranger than mere fear. Grief alone could not have carved such despair into their features. Slowly, several of them circled me, reaching out to accept the heavy burden I carried. I yielded it to them. The tenderness mixed with hopeless resignation in the smiles they offered made my own heart swell with pity amidst its crushing desolation.

Their sobs over their mother-queen were futile. They begged for a sign of her love, they kissed her cheeks and clung to her limbs as they lifted her onto their shoulders, but she remained unresponsive. One child cradled her arm, stroking it gently; others wedged themselves beneath her weight, while those unable to reach formed a tight circle around the bearers. They laid her down upon a patch of soft, thick grass, and there, surrounded by the "Little Ones," the group collapsed into collective mourning.

Outside this grieving throng stood the elephants, massive and silent. I lingered near them, peering over the sea of small heads to gaze at my Lona. Those closest to me caught sight of the princess and recoiled, trembling violently.

"I've seen that woman before!" Odu whispered urgently to the boy beside him. "It was she who fought the white leopardess the night they woke us with their screaming!"

"Silly!" his neighbor retorted. "That was just a wild beast with spots!"

"Look at her eyes!" Odu insisted, his voice rising. "I know she's a bad giantess, but she's a wild beast all the same. I know she *is* the spotted one!"

The other boy took a hesitant step closer. Odu grabbed him sharply, pulling him back.

"Don't look at her!" Odu cried, shrinking away despite being transfixed by the hate-filled longing burning in the princess's dark eyes. "She'd eat you alive in a heartbeat! It was HER shadow! She's the wicked princess!"

"That can't be true! They said she was beautiful!"

"Indeed, it is the princess!" I interjected softly. "But wickedness has made her ugly."

She heard me. Her expression shifted, a flicker of recognition or perhaps accusation passing across her still face.

"It was very wrong of me to run away," Odu murmured thoughtfully, staring at his hands.

"What made you run?" I asked gently.

"I expected to find you where I left you!" he replied, avoiding my gaze.

He didn't answer immediately. "I don't know what drove me," admitted another child nearby. "I was just frightened."

"A man came down the hill from the palace," a third voice chimed in.

"How did he frighten you?"

"I don't know."

"He wasn't a man," Odu corrected firmly. "He was a shadow. He had no thickness to him!"

"Tell me more about him."

"He came down the hill very black, moving like a bad giant, but spread flat. He was nothing but pure blackness. We froze the moment we saw him, standing and watching. He advanced as if intent on walking right over us. But before he reached us, he began to stretch—spreading wider and wider, growing bigger and bigger until he vanished from our sight entirely. And then... he was upon us!"

"What do you mean by that?"

"He was all black, filling the space between us, blocking out everything else. We couldn't see one another anymore. And then... he was inside us."

"How did you know he was inside you?"

"He hurt me differently," Odu confessed, his voice cracking. "I felt awful. I wasn't Odu anymore—not the Odu I knew. I wanted to tear Sozo apart! Not really, but I felt like it!" He turned abruptly and hugged Sozo tightly. "It wasn't me, Sozo," he sobbed. "Deep down, it was Odu, loving you always! Then Odu came up and knocked the Naughty thing away. I grew sick, convinced I had to kill myself to escape the blackness. Then came a horrible laugh that seemed to hear my thoughts, making the air tremble around me. After that, I suppose I ran, though I didn't realize I was running until I found myself sprinting as fast as I could, along with everyone else. I tried to stop, but I never thought of it until I burst out of the gate into the tall grass. Only then did I realize I had fled from a shadow that wanted to be me but wasn't. That shadow was inside me, hating him from within. It wasn't my own self! Now I know I shouldn't have run, but I truly didn't know what I was doing until it was done. My legs did it, I think—they grew scared, forgot who owned them, and just ran! Naughty legs! There! And there!"

With a final kick directed at each of his "naughty" legs, Odu finished his tale.

"What happened to the shadow?" I asked.

"I don't know," he answered, looking toward the darkening sky. "I guess he went home into the night where there is no moon."

My mind wandered, searching for Lona. Dropping to the grass, I took the lifeless form into my lap and whispered against her ear, "Where are you, Lona? I love you." Her lips gave no response. I pressed a kiss to them, feeling them cool but not quite frozen, then laid her body back down. Appointing a guard to stand vigil, I rose to ensure the safety of Lona's people throughout the coming night.

Before sunset, I established a perimeter watch around the princess outside the main camp, placing sentinels at every angle. Intending to patrol the area myself all night, I ordered the rest of the army to sleep. They threw themselves onto the grass and were unconscious almost instantly.

As the moon climbed higher, casting silver light over the scene, I caught a glimpse of something white darting through the shadows. It was the leopardess. She moved with silent grace, circling the sleeping camp. I watched her pass three times between the princess and the cluster of weeping children. Seeing this, I signaled the watchers to lie down with the others. Exhaustion claimed me as well, and I stretched out beside the cold body of Lona, listening to the wind.
