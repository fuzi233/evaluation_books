For a heartbeat, I stood frozen as the guards surged toward me. Their first violent rush forced me back—until my foot found no solid ground, only empty air. I teetered on the edge of the abyss that had swallowed Issus. Then, with the boy still clutched in my arms, I plunged backward into the void.

We struck a slick chute, the entrance above us snapping shut as swiftly as it had opened. Down we spiraled, unharmed, into a dimly lit chamber far beneath the arena. As I staggered to my feet, my gaze locked onto the venomous glare of Issus herself, her face pressed against the bars of a cell door.

*"Fool!"* she spat. *"You will rot here—alone, in darkness, with the corpse of your accomplice festering beside you. Hunger will drive you to madness, until you feast on the worms that crawl from his bones."*

With that, she vanished. The faint light flickered out, plunging us into absolute blackness.

A dry chuckle sounded beside me. *"Charming hostess."*

*"Who’s there?"* I demanded.

*"Your comrade-in-arms,"* came the reply. *"The one who had the honor of fighting beside the greatest swordsman Barsoom has ever known."*

Relief flooded me. *"Thank fate you’re alive. That head wound looked fatal."*

*"A scratch,"* he scoffed. *"Though death might have been kinder. Starvation won’t be a pleasant end."*

*"Beneath the arena,"* I said. *"That shaft wasn’t just for show."*

To my surprise, he laughed—low, triumphant. Then his hand found my shoulder in the dark, his whisper urgent. *"This is perfect. There are secrets beneath Issus’s secrets, tunnels even she doesn’t know. I worked here once. I know the way out."*

*"A passage leads to the lower waterways—to Omean. If we reach a submarine, we can vanish among the islands. The blacks never go there."*

I gripped his arm. *"First, we return to Shador. My friend Xodar is there. I won’t leave him."*

*"Nor should you,"* he agreed. *"Better recaptured than faithless."*

He began feeling along the floor, searching for the hidden exit. Moments later, a soft *"Here"* guided me to his side. Kneeling, he gestured into the blackness. *"A ten-foot drop. Hang by your hands—the sand below is soft."*

I lowered myself into the abyss, the darkness so complete it was like blindness. The sensation of hanging over nothingness was worse than any battle. Then I let go.

Four feet. A cushioned landing.

The boy followed, then hoisted himself back to reseal the trap. Taking my hand, he led us through the labyrinth—slow, deliberate, his memory our only guide.

The descent steepened. *"Light soon,"* he murmured. *"The same phosphorescent rock as Omean."*

The journey through those ancient corridors was a descent into history. My mind conjured the lost races of Barsoom—the blacks, the whites, the vanished yellows—driven to this last stronghold by the green hordes. Were remnants of the yellow race still hidden somewhere?

A sudden gasp from the boy shattered my thoughts. *"There—light!"*

A faint glow ahead brightened as we advanced, revealing smooth, time-worn passageways. We moved faster now, until abruptly, the corridor ended at a ledge overlooking the submarine pool.

The craft waited, hatch open. No guards.

We dropped silently onto its deck, slithered to the hatch, and slipped inside. Empty. No crew. No resistance.

The boy handed me a scrap of paper—a radio message:

*"Zithad commands Issus’s guard,"* he explained. *"We gave them a scare they won’t forget."*

*"Let’s hope it’s the first of many,"* I said.

We reached Omean’s pool unchallenged. Now came the true test: passing the guards. I forged an order on submarine commander Yersted’s behalf, commanding Torith—the officer on duty—to return us to Shador.

*"But our swords?"* the boy protested.

Boldly, we marched to the sentry post. Rifles snapped up. I handed the order to Torith, who eyed us with suspicion.

*"Returned to the temple,"* I lied, pulse hammering. One step toward the pool would expose us.

But the ruse held. Torith waved us on.

Minutes later, we were back in Shador’s cells—locked away, but not yet broken. The game was far from over.
