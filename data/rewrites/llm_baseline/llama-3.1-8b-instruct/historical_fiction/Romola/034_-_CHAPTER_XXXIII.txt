As the sun began to set over the rolling hills of Tuscany, casting a warm orange glow over the city of Florence, Baldassarre wandered through the winding streets in search of a humble abode to call his own. His footsteps led him to the outskirts of the city, where the landscape shifted from the bustling streets to a more rugged terrain. This was the hill of San Giorgio, a place of sparse habitation and overgrown gardens, yet to be reclaimed by the forces of nature. Bratti had spoken of Tito's dwelling being in the nearby Via de' Bardi, and Baldassarre hoped to find a sheltering outhouse among the scattered buildings that clung to the hillside.

The air was alive with the sweet scent of ripening figs and the distant tang of fresh bread wafting from the nearby ovens. Baldassarre breathed deeply, savoring the familiar aromas that transported him back to his childhood in the countryside. His years of living under the open sky had made him appreciate the simple pleasures of life, and the narrow alleys of Florence often left him feeling claustrophobic and disconnected from the world.

As he climbed the slope of the hill, the silence was broken by the soft bleating of goats and the rustling of leaves beneath his feet. The hill was dotted with the remnants of a long-forgotten landslide, which had left behind a patchwork of jagged stones and overgrown vegetation. Amidst this desolate landscape stood a small, weathered building, its fluted tile roof a testament to the region's rich architectural heritage. A smaller outhouse adjoined the structure, its door ajar, beckoning Baldassarre towards the promise of shelter.

A gnarled old woman, her face etched by the elements, tended to a trio of goats beneath the shade of a half-dead mulberry tree. Her eyes, a deep shade of blue, sparkled with a knowing glint as she worked, her hands moving deftly as she tied a rope around the neck of one of the kids. Baldassarre approached her, his words tumbling out in a rush as he explained his need for a temporary lodging. However, it was not until he stood beside her, his voice raised to a shout, that she comprehended his plea.
