As I descended into the hidden passage, the air grew thick with the remnants of a bygone era. This forgotten vein, once a conduit for the fiery lava flows that had shaped the Earth, now lay dormant, its warmth still palpable despite the passing of ages. I lit a lantern from a nearby niche, casting flickering shadows on the uneven walls as I navigated the treacherous terrain. My throat ached from the dust and the lingering effects of a recent illness, a reminder of the limited medical supplies I'd salvaged from the Yucatan vice-royalty before the sea-fight with Dason's navy.

The labyrinthine passage twisted and turned, a maze designed to confound even the most skilled of explorers. Without the chart etched into my memory from my initiation ceremony twenty years prior, I would have been lost forever in the darkness. The secrets of our Clan had been entrusted to me then, and I recalled the intricate network of tunnels and hidden chambers with precision.

Time seemed to drag on as I traversed the narrow corridors, the air growing increasingly noxious with each step. The city above was abuzz with an unknown excitement, and I knew I couldn't emerge without being detected. I had hoped to slip in unnoticed via the royal pyramid, but the throngs of people made it impossible. Each of the five subsequent exits I tried was similarly impassable, the crowds surging forward like a living entity.

Eventually, I chanced upon a small temple dedicated to a minor deity, its halls shrouded in a thick layer of dust. I stood behind the idol, observing the scene before me. The space was desolate, a testament to the temple's long abandonment. I swept away any visible signs of my presence, using a discarded broom to erase my footprints. The temple door, however, presented a new challenge. As a priest, I was familiar with its locking mechanism, but it seemed the attendant priests had left their posts to join the city's fervor.

The stillness within the temple's precincts was oppressive, a stark contrast to the chaos outside. Yet, according to our ancient traditions, there should always be one priest on watch in the priests' quarters. I made my way down the dimly lit passages, my footsteps echoing off the stone walls. It wouldn't have surprised me to find the dwellings empty, given the sense of upheaval that had taken hold of the city.
