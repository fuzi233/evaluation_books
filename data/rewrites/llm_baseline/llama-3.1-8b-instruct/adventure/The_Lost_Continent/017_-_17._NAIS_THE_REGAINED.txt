From the crest of the Sacred Mountain, the city unfolded before us like a meticulously crafted tapestry, its threads of harbour, estuary, and hills woven together in a delicate balance. The golden glow of the setting sun danced across the landscape, casting long shadows that stretched and twisted in the fading light. In the heart of the city, the imposing stone circle of our Lord the Sun stood sentinel, its rugged grandeur a testament to the reverence of the people. Nearby, the majestic pyramid rose like a monolith, its weathered stones glinting with an otherworldly sheen. Before it, the throne of granite seemed a mere speck, a tiny, insignificant thing in comparison to the monumental structures that surrounded it.

My thoughts, however, were drawn inexorably to the throne, for it was there that Nais lay entombed, my love, my heart, my reason for being. Old Zaemon, sensing my gaze, nodded solemnly, his eyes clouded with a mixture of sadness and determination. "Yes, I know where your true interest lies," he said, his voice low and measured. "Deucalion, when he arrived on this continent, was a force to be reckoned with. Our current ruler, on the other hand, has his weaknesses."

"But those weaknesses can be turned to our advantage," I countered, my voice barely above a whisper. "Give me Nais, alive and by my side, and I will be a stronger man than any viceroy or soldier you can name."

Zaemon's expression turned grave, his eyes narrowing as he considered my words. "I have given my word, and you will have her, but at a cost. The fate of this new kingdom hangs in the balance, and I fear we may have to sacrifice some of its stability to achieve our goal." He paused, his gaze drifting towards the city. "And as for Phorenice, I fear she will not be easily swayed. She has a cunning that borders on the supernatural, and she will stop at nothing to protect herself and her brood."

"I've seen her manipulate the earth tremors," I admitted, a shiver running down my spine at the memory. "But tell me, what is your plan?"
