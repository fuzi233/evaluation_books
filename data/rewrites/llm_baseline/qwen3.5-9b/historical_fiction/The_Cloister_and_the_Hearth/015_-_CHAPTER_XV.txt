Where is the woman who cannot assume a role when necessity demands it? Who will not perform it perfectly to preserve the life of the man she adores? In moments of supreme crisis, nature itself seems to intervene, granting even the most timid souls the power to blind Solomon with dust.

As soon as the soldiers withdrew, Margaret rose from her hiding place. She slipped out of bed, discarding her skirts, petticoats, and stockings, leaving only her nightgown. She pushed the heavy chest—her makeshift coffin—against the wall, then crept silently to the door to listen. The heavy tread of boots faded through her father's chamber and down the stone stairs.

Inside the room lay a secret known only to her. To conceal her presence, a section of the floorboards had been removed. When Gerard had requested repairs to make the room presentable, lacking proper timber, he had cleverly cut away just enough wood to accommodate her narrow form. Using those very planks, he had patched the hole, rendering the deception nearly invisible to any casual observer. Yet, beneath the surface, the truth remained: the "bed" she occupied actually hung suspended from the rafters, two feet above the actual floor. Visually, it appeared shallow; in reality, it was a deep crypt.

Silence reigned absolute. Margaret knelt, offering a silent prayer of gratitude to Heaven. Rising again, she moved to the edge of the trapdoor and whispered softly, "Gerard?"

There was no response. She pressed her voice slightly higher. "Gerard, all is safe. Thank God! You may rise now; but oh, be careful!"

Stillness answered her. She placed her trembling hand upon his shoulder. "Gerard!"

Nothing. Panic, cold and sharp, pierced her heart. "What is this?" she cried, her fingers flying frantically across his face and chest. She gripped his shoulders, shaking him violently, lifting him up. But he offered no resistance; he slid from her grasp, falling back not with the weight of a living man, but with the lifelessness of a corpse.

A profound dread seized her. The lid had been lowered while she slept. The men had been in the room. With a surge of frenzied strength, she ripped him free from the darkness of the chest. Carrying him in her arms, she rushed to the window and threw it wide open. The cool, fragrant night air rushed in, filling the stagnant space. She laid him within the sill, bathed in the silver light of the moon.

His complexion was the color of ash; his limbs were utterly limp and motionless. Reaching for his pulse, she found nothing. Horror struck her with the force of a physical blow. *Horror of horrors!* She had suffocated him with her own body. Her mind refused to accept such a sudden, catastrophic tragedy. Gerard, who had scrambled into that box mere minutes prior, how could he already be dead?

She called upon every endearing name her heart could conjure. She kissed him, caressed him, coaxed him, imploring him to speak. There was no answer to her words of love, phrases she had never dared utter before, yet which now poured forth uncontrollably. Trembling violently, she began to babble over his pale countenance, saying things that were both terrifying and pitiful.

"Oh, Gerard! I am so sorry you are dead. I am so sorry I have killed you. Forgive me for not allowing the men to take you; it would have been kinder than this. Oh, Gerard! I am very, very sorry for what I have done."

Suddenly, her composure shattered completely. She raved aloud. "No! No! Such things cannot be true, or there is no God! It is monstrous! How can my Gerard be dead? How could I have killed my Gerard? I love him! Oh, God, you know how I love him. He does not. If he knew my heart, he would speak to me; he would not remain so deaf to his poor Margaret. This must be a trick to make me scream and betray him! But no! I love him too deeply for that. I would rather die."

Seizing her own throat, she tried to stifle her wild urge to shriek in terror and agony. "If he would but say one word. Oh, Gerard! Do not die without speaking. Have mercy on me and scold me, but speak to me! If you are angry, curse me! I deserve it—the fool who killed the man she loved more than herself. Ah, I am a murderer. The worst in all the world. Help! Help! I have murdered him! Ah! Ah! Ah! Ah! Ah!"

She tore at her hair, emitting shrieks so wild and piercing that they rang like funeral bells throughout the house. The sounds reached the ears of Dierich Brower and his men instantly. Startled from their slumber, they sprang to their feet, staring at one another in confusion and alarm.
