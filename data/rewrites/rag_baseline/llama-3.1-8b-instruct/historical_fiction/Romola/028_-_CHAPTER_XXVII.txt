The sun had barely begun to set over the Arno, casting a warm orange glow over the terracotta rooftops of Florence, but already Romola felt a sense of restlessness creeping over her like the first wisps of autumn fog. She paced the cool stone floors of the old library, her footsteps echoing off the high ceilings as she waited for Tito's return from his visit to the cardinal's palace. It had been three months since her father's sudden passing, and though the city was abuzz with the news of the French king's arrival, Romola had yet to join in the festivities.

As she walked, her thoughts turned to the evenings spent waiting for Tito, watching the clock tick away with an impatient heart, and the words she'd rehearsed in her mind to persuade him to stay with her, rather than leave for another engagement. She remembered the way he'd smile, his eyes crinkling at the corners, as he excused himself once more, promising to make it up to her soon. But soon had become a refrain, a promise repeated until it lost all meaning.

The memory stung, and Romola felt a pang of regret for her own impatience. She'd always known that Tito's life was not her own, that his obligations to her father and the cardinal would always come first. Yet, in her girlish naivety, she'd assumed that their love would somehow transcend the demands of duty and family. Now, she saw it for what it was – a compromise, a delicate balance of give and take that required her to temper her desires with understanding and patience.

As she paused before the window, the fading light catching the dust motes dancing in the air, Romola felt a sense of acceptance wash over her. She would not be the one to break the fragile equilibrium that held their lives together. Instead, she would continue to wait, to offer what comfort and support she could, even as she nurtured the secret hope that one day, Tito would find a way to reconcile his many responsibilities with the love they shared. The thought was bittersweet, like the scent of fresh bread wafting from the kitchen below, a reminder of the simple pleasures that made life worth living, even in the midst of uncertainty.
