The scent of roasting meats wafted through the cloister, mingling with the faint tang of salt and herbs, transporting Father Clement to a realm beyond the mundane. As he ascended to the pulpit, the golden light streaming through the stained glass windows danced across his face, imbuing him with a sense of divine connection. The congregation, a sea of snowy caps adorned with intricate patterns, seemed to shimmer like the frost-kissed fields of his childhood. He felt his heart swell with compassion, knowing that these people, like the heathens of old, often went without the gentle whispers of God's love.

With a deep breath, he began to speak, his words pouring forth like a rich, slow-moving brook. The people hung upon his every phrase, their faces aglow with a mixture of wonder and curiosity. As he spoke of Christ's love, his voice grew stronger, filling the space between the stone pillars like the fragrance of freshly baked bread wafting from the kitchen below.

His gaze wandered, however, to the south aisle, where a shaft of sunlight illuminated the face of Margaret Brandt. Time seemed to stand still as he beheld her, her auburn locks ablaze with the fiery hue of ripening wheat. Her features, once etched in his memory like the intricate patterns on a fine porcelain vase, now shone with an otherworldly radiance, as if the very essence of spring had descended upon the church. He felt his pulse quicken, his skin growing cold and clammy, as if the warmth of the day had suddenly given way to the chill of winter.

As he gazed, transfixed, the words died on his lips, and he trembled, his eyes locked onto hers like a shipwrecked sailor clinging to a lifeline. There was no change in her, only a subtle ripeness, a depth of beauty that seemed to draw him in, like a honeycomb beckoning bees to its sweetness. The people around him faded into the background, their murmurs and rustlings lost in the turmoil of his mind.

In a flash, her eyes met his, a spark of recognition kindling in their depths. His lips parted, ready to shout out her name, but she turned away, vanishing from view like a fleeting dream.
