As the seasons passed, Old Whateley's industrious endeavors seemed to know no bounds. He continued to purchase cattle, but his herd remained small, as if he harbored a secret purpose that defied numerical measurement. Meanwhile, he devoted himself to restoring the neglected areas of his sprawling, peaked-roofed abode, whose rear end had long been submerged beneath the rocky hillside. The three habitable ground-floor rooms, once sufficient for the old man and his daughter, were now augmented by the meticulous craftsmanship of a man consumed by a singular vision.

In his youth, Old Whateley had shown an affinity for carpentry, and it was this skill that now served him in the task of reviving the long-abandoned upper story of the house. Though his mind occasionally strayed, his hands moved with a precision that belied his erratic behavior. As he worked, he seemed to be driven by a calculating purpose, one that found expression in the methodical arrangement of tools and materials. The old tool-shed, once a cluttered repository of discarded implements, was transformed under his care into a tidy, lock-fastened haven.

The old man's zeal for restoration knew no bounds, and he soon set his sights on the upper story, where he began to create a sanctuary of sorts. The air was thick with the scent of sawdust and varnish as he labored tirelessly to bring order to the dusty, cobweb-shrouded space. His daughter watched with a mixture of fascination and trepidation as he boarded up the windows, sealing out the outside world and plunging the room into an eerie stillness. The reason for this peculiarity remained a mystery, but it was clear that Old Whateley's actions were guided by a deeper purpose.

In the downstairs room that he had set aside for his grandson, Wilbur, the old man arranged the scattered remnants of his own library with a sense of anticipation. The shelves, tall and sturdy, stretched along the walls like sentinels, holding aloft a collection of ancient tomes and fragments of books that had lain undisturbed for years. As he worked, Old Whateley would mutter to himself, his voice a low, soothing melody that seemed to carry on the wind.
