As the evening drew to a close, the group decided to adjourn to Mr. Warrington's lodgings, where a sumptuous supper awaited. The Virginians had spared no expense in laying out a feast fit for royalty, and the General and his wife, accompanied by their son, their two lively daughters, and Mr. Spencer, a new acquaintance of George's, were more than happy to join in the festivities. The landlady's culinary skills were put to the test, and she rose to the occasion, presenting a veritable cornucopia of delights. As they sat down to eat, Miss Theo found herself seated beside the chickens, with Mr. George Warrington on her right, while Miss Hetty and a plump ham fought for the attention of Mr. Harry.

Mrs. Lambert, situated on George's left, smiled warmly as she chatted with her son, while the General, his son, and Mr. Spencer claimed the remaining places at the table. Conversation turned to the recent theatrical performance, with some members of the party still raving about the play. "I'm afraid you gentlemen won't listen to reason," Mrs. Lambert teased Mr. Spencer, "it's a truly delightful piece, and I'm ashamed to admit that my husband and Mr. George were completely carried away."

"I'm afraid we laughed in all the wrong places, and when we should have been crying," the General confessed, chuckling. "That's the honest truth." Mr. Spencer nodded sympathetically, "I've seen it myself, and I must say, the acting was superb. That dagger-scene, for instance – it sent shivers down my spine!"

Miss Theo spoke up, her eyes shining with excitement, "I was deeply moved by the play, especially the beautiful performances of Mrs. Woffington. I felt so sorry for the mother who lost her child, and I couldn't help but feel a pang of sorrow myself." Her father looked at her with a mixture of amusement and affection, "Ah, Theo, you're a true romantic, aren't you?"

Hetty, who had been quietly observing the conversation, suddenly piped up, "I think women are much better at understanding plays than men. Next time, please, Jack, give us a nudge when we should be laughing and a pinch when we should be crying!"
