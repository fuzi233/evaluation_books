As I sat at my wooden table, quill in hand, the faint scent of last night's supper wafted through the air, mingling with the smell of freshly ground coffee. It had taken me three years to secure a steady flow of commissions from the local publications, and I was determined to make the most of it. The modest sum I earned was barely enough to cover my expenses, but it was a start. I had come to London with little more than a pocketful of dreams and a burning desire to write.

My agent, Norah, proved to be a shrewd businesswoman, handling the finances with precision and sending me the balance after deducting her fees. At first, my earnings were meager, but Norah's diligence soon paid off, and my articles began to gain traction. Dan, my friend and fellow writer, grumbled about Norah's high rates, but even he couldn't deny her results. She had a knack for negotiating with editors, and her connections were extensive.

As my reputation grew, so did the competition. Other writers began to seek me out, hoping to tap into my unique voice and perspective. Norah, anticipating this, started sending a portion of my work to rival publications. Before long, "Jack Homer" became a sought-after commodity, and I found myself in high demand. My writing became a labor of love, and the four walls of my humble room faded away as I conjured up vivid scenes and characters.

Despite my progress, I knew I couldn't rely solely on my writing income. Journalism beckoned, and I answered. As a penny-a-liner, I joined a motley crew of writers, all of us scrambling to make a name for ourselves. We attended inquests, police courts, and fires, feasting on the drama and tragedy of others' misfortunes. We lived precariously, always on the lookout for the next big scoop, and occasionally, we struck gold – a half-crown here, a crown there. Our leader, a grizzled veteran, would whisper to us, "I think, gentlemen, the family of the deceased would prefer it if this didn't make the papers." And we'd nod, our ears perked up, ready to pounce on the next juicy tidbit. Our notes were meticulous, our research thorough, and our prices negotiable.
