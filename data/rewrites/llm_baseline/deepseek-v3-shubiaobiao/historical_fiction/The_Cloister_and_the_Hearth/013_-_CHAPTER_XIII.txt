The road to Sevenbergen was bright with the joy of newfound freedom, but shadows soon crept over Gerard and Margaret’s hearts. Gerard was a fugitive now, marked by the law, and the thought of parting hung heavy between them. The air, once sweet with the triumph of their escape, turned bitter with the weight of what must come.

Margaret’s fingers tightened around his. "You must leave," she whispered, though the words tore at her. Italy—the land of art, of promise—beckoned him, but the distance was a yawning chasm in her chest. Gerard, torn between love and ambition, swore it was the only path forward. "I’ll return with gold, with honor," he vowed, his voice thick. "Then no one will stand between us."

But promises could not stop the tears that fell between them. They sat through the long hours, hands clasped, weaving dreams of a future where no one could tear them apart—only for reality to claw back in, cruel and unrelenting.

Then came the thunder of hooves.

Giles loomed in the doorway, his face grim, his message dire. "They call you a thief," he rumbled. "They hunt you. Kate says—*run*."

Margaret’s breath left her in a gasp. Gerard’s face paled. The parchments—those damned scraps of ink and vellum—had become a noose around his neck.

"They’re but an excuse," Giles growled. "Dirk Brower’s men are stirring. If you linger, you die."

Fear, sharp and cold, shot through them both. Gerard moved quickly, burying the cursed documents deep in the earth—all but one, a deed that bore his family’s name. He tucked it into his breast, a secret to unravel later.

The sky darkened as if in warning. Thunder rolled in the distance, and rain began to fall, heavy and relentless. Martin, ever watchful, scouted the road. "No sign of them yet," he muttered, but his eyes were wary. "But the storm won’t hold them forever."

The lovers clung to each other in the dim glow of a single candle, the storm howling outside as if the heavens themselves raged against their parting. Supper lay untouched. Time slipped like sand through their fingers.

Then—a sound.

A footstep, too quiet, too careful.

Margaret’s nails dug into Gerard’s arm. Martin’s hand flew to his bow, the candle snuffed out in an instant.

Another step. Then another.

Silence.

Then—

A knock.

The world stopped.

And in that terrible stillness, their hearts pounded like drums of war.
