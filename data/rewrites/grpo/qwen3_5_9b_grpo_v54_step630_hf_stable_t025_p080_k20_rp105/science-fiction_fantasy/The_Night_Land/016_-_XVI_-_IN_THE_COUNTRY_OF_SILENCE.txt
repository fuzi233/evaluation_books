The Great Pyramid loomed before me, a colossal mountain of safety rising into the eternal night, its sheer scale capable of stunning any mind not already shattered by despair. Yet, my heart was too heavy with exhaustion to marvel at its grandeur; my sole, frantic purpose was to reach the girl I carried, to drag her into the sanctuary of that refuge before the darkness claimed us both. She was still far ahead, a fragile weight against my chest, and the distance felt like an eternity.

I pressed forward, my steps heavy but determined, until I reached a hollow where a fire-hole burned with a sickly, rhythmic light. From the gloom emerged a creature, dragging itself upward from the earth. It was a man, but a grotesque aberration—huge, hairy, and terrifying. As he approached, his hands thrust forward in a desperate, clawing gesture. In the flickering fire-light, I saw the truth of his grasp: monstrous, armored with brutish claws capable of tearing flesh like paper.

Panic seized me, not for my own life, but for the delay it imposed on my mission. With a surge of cold, furious anger, I leaped at the giant, swinging the Diskos with all my remaining strength. He moved with unnatural speed, dodging my blow and snatching my helmet from my head with a grip of iron. He threw me across the ground, bruising my ribs, but I rose instantly, the weapon roaring in my hands. I struck him high, and the Diskos bit deep. The giant's massive upper body crumpled to the earth, a fountain of blood erupting into the night, while his legs remained standing in the fire's glare.

I did not pause to gloat. I sprinted to the girl, clutching her tighter, and surged past the dying monster. Suddenly, the air vibrated with the spiritual cries of the Millions within the Pyramid. Their love and excitement washed over me, a tangible force telling me they had witnessed my struggle and were ready to aid me.

I had not traveled far when two vague shapes emerged from the shadows of the rocks. I struck them down with the Diskos and continued, unaware of their true nature. Soon, the land seemed to crawl with foul life. Strange things erupted from the bushes and stones, and I found myself fighting in a dreamlike trance, driven by a fierce, hopeless despair that the end had finally come and I would fail to save her.

The night roared with the sounds of giants and deadly, guttural beasts. Then, from the southeast, the deep, dreadful baying of the Night-Hounds grew louder, signaling their approach. Just as hope seemed to vanish, a strange blue flash descended from the heights of the Last Light, followed by another, and another. A peculiar crackling sound, louder than thunder yet sharper, echoed through the air. I knew then that the Humans were fighting for me, mobilizing to ensure my escape.

The land woke from its slumber. The roar of monsters and the eerie laughter from the lost East filled the sky. The Hounds closed in, a mighty pack mere miles away, leaving me alone with the dying girl in my arms. I looked vainly toward the Pyramid, searching for the Hundred Thousand who had promised to come to my aid, but saw only strange lights and the movement of monstrous life. Death was nigh.

I broke into a run. The Pyramid was visible now, its Circle of light faint but distinct. I held onto a desperate hope that I could reach the safety of the Circle before the Hounds caught us. The baying grew closer, a hideous soundtrack to my impending doom. The Millions' anguish for me became palpable, a spiritual pressure surrounding me as they perceived my final struggle.

Then, the Earth-Current shook. I saw the Humans unleash a desperate measure. A vast pack of Hounds charged, and suddenly, a monstrous flame burst from the lower part of the Pyramid, rushing downward to meet them. The night was swallowed by the blinding brilliance of the Earth-Force. Thunder rolled as the force tore the earth apart, hurling rocks and silencing the monsters in a cataclysmic display. The Humans cut off the current, and silence returned, save for the burning wreckage.

I recovered quickly and ran again. The Pyramid's light was dimming, a cold realization settling in my heart: the Earth-Current had been drained to save us, leaving the Millions vulnerable. If it did not replenish soon, they would all perish. I pushed forward, ignoring the warning that death was close.

As I ran, I sensed a new peril. I looked up, expecting the Master Monstruwacan to signal danger via the Set Speech, but the instruments were failing; the Pyramid's machinery was grinding to a halt. Instead, a pale circle of holiness hovered above us, a shield against the dread power approaching. I trusted in this force and pressed on.

Within four hundred paces of the Circle, three beast-men rose from the earth, growling. I struck the first with the haft of the Diskos, then swung wildly at the others, my mind cold and focused solely on survival. They fell like mice. A shout of welcome erupted from within the Circle, but I saw men in grey armor holding back, terrified by the monstrous Black Mounds swaying outside the perimeter. These mounds were the visible signs of evil forces; to step beyond the Circle was to be destroyed in spirit.

"Come!" they shouted. "Haste!"

I ran faster, but a herd of squat, brutish men with pig-like tusks burst from the shadows. They grabbed at me and the girl. I kicked free, turning to face them. The Diskos spun, casting a strange light on their tusks. I raged through the herd, taking a thousand blows to my armor while shielding the girl above their reach. They seemed endless, but I drove forward, fueled by desperation.

Fifty paces from the Circle, I was near collapse, dazed and bleeding, having carried the girl through endless days and nights without sleep. Suddenly, the Hundred Thousand hurled their Diskos into the herd, thinning the line. I gathered my strength, charging through the gap, and broke into the Circle.

A thousand hands reached out, but recoiled in awe at my state. I stood there, the Diskos dripping blood, gasping for air. I tried to speak, to ask for doctors for the girl dying in my arms. Voices cried out warnings of giants and the fading holy light. The Master of the Diskos approached, saluting me, but I insisted on finding a doctor immediately.

Two large men brought a small man on a sling—a Master of the Doctors. He gently helped me lay the girl down. The Hundred Thousand fell silent, a fearful quiet settling over the Pyramid. The Doctor looked up at me with pity, and I knew in that instant: she was dead. He covered her face and signaled for men to support me and carry her to the Gateway.

I fought to breathe, gesturing that no one should touch me or the girl. The Doctor understood my strength was gone, only my will to die remaining. I stooped, lifting the girl for one last journey.

I walked down the silent lane of the Hundred Thousand, who made a mute salute with their reversed Diskos. I carried my dead love into the Great Lift, accompanied by the Masters, including Monstruwacan, who looked at me with the sorrow of a father. The Pyramid's lights began to glow again as the Earth-Current restored itself, allowing the Great Gate to open.

Inside, the air was thick with grief. The weeping of women drifted down from the upper cities. Monstruwacan and the Doctor stood silently by my side as we ascended. I realized then that I was bleeding heavily from a hundred wounds, but the Doctor did not tend to me; he knew that my heart was already broken beyond repair. Whirlings came to my head, and someone tried to take the girl from my arms, but I held her dumbly. Monstruwacan's face seemed human in the dim light, and he held me up as darkness closed in.

Three days passed in a half-dream, during which I was tended by the best medical knowledge of the Humans. On the third day, I regained my senses, the pain in my breast sharp and real. I refused a loose garment, insisting on my broken armor, a symbol of my battle. As they dressed me, I heard the sorrow of the Millions, knowing they were descending to the Country of Silence.

Just as I was being fitted, the memory struck me again: I had never woken to find the girl kissing me in my sleep. The pain was so acute it nearly killed me, but the Doctor administered a drug to dull my senses. I was carried to the lift again, destined not for the surface, but for the Underground Fields, the Country of Silence, a hundred miles deep where the dead rest.

The Stress Masters arranged the descent, and we dropped through the miles. At the bottom, in the vast quiet, I stood on my feet, refusing the sling, demanding to walk to The Last Road. The Doctor obeyed, walking behind me. The country was filled with the Peoples of the World, their sorrow creating a low, rolling thunder in the air.

Below me lay the Last Rest, where a figure covered in a white robe glimmered with the embroidery of women's love. This was the place for me, the head of the mound reserved for Love, which holds dominion over Faithfulness and Honour. Two maids in white knelt by the grave, symbols of Faithfulness.

I took my courage and stepped to the head of the mound. The girl lay beneath a white garment worked with yellow Flowers of Weeping, a testament to her death in love. No hand but a maiden's had touched her. From far away, the Millions began to sing the Calling Song, a low, anguished chant calling to a lost beloved. It passed over us, a wave of collective grief.

Then, a strange wind-like sound arose, growing into the Song of Weeping, sung by millions across the deep earth. Monstruwacan looked at me, and I knew the moment of parting had arrived. I placed the Diskos beside her. The maids drew back the garment to reveal her face. She slept there, sweet and quiet as a child.

The pain was sufficient. I knew I was dying as I looked. I set my soul about her, fighting to stay upright, but the maids covered her face again. Monstruwacan commended her to Eternity, raising his reversed Diskos in honor. The Road began to move upward toward the Dome, carrying her away.

A low moaning filled the air, the true weeping of multitudes. I stood still, watching the small form recede into the luminous vapour of the Earth-Current at the base of the Dome. The vapour clung to her, making her seem unreal, shifting and uncertain.

Suddenly, a hoarse noise erupted from the nearby Millions, growing into a mighty roar. I saw the girl move on the Road, but it was only the shifting vapour playing tricks on my crazed, longing mind. Then, truly, I saw her move. She was alive.

Life surged back into me, my heart stilled for a moment before pounding anew. Monstruwacan signaled the Road to stop and reverse, but I was already upon it, running as a madman, shouting her name. The Millions held back to prevent a crush, and a signal was sent for calm. I staggered upward, the Roof ringing with their shouts.

Others ran behind me, but I was first. I reached her quickly. She lay on her back, having pushed the garment from her face, her eyes open with a look of gentle wonder. She saw me, and her eyes smiled, weak but glad.

I fell beside her, kneeling on my hands and knees, my heart shaking my lips into dry whispers. She looked steadfastly at me, and I tried to speak, but my mouth refused. Understanding dawned in her eyes like a light; she knew she was in the Pyramid, that I had brought her home. She woke fully, trembling, reaching out from the garment.

I saw the blood flowing constantly from my wounds. She perceived it, waking from her death-swoon with renewed clarity. I spoke simply, "I loved her." She was in a haze, but I knew she had come to her knees, placing my head on her breast. The air shook with a great sound, a mighty spiritual stirring.

The voices of Monstruwacan and the Doctor were dull in my ears, meaningless words. All I knew was that she lived. I resolved not to die, to fight for life, and even as I made this vow, I sank into an utter blackness.
