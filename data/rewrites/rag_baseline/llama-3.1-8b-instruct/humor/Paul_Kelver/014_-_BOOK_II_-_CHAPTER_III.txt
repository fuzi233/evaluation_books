As I slowly regained consciousness, the warm sunlight streaming through the grimy windowpane seared my eyes, forcing me to shield them with a trembling hand. The cacophony of the city's awakening streets echoed through the small, cramped room, a harsh reminder that the day had begun without me. I frantically scanned the space, my gaze settling on the rickety dresser where my pocket watch usually lay. It was gone, leaving behind an unsettling sense of disorientation.

A wave of nausea washed over me as I swung my legs over the side of the bed, my head pounding like a blacksmith's hammer. My clothes were strewn about the room, except for one boot, which sat forlornly on the chair beside the cold hearth. Panic set in as I scrambled to recall the events of the previous night, my memories hazy and disjointed. Had I donned Minikin's oversized boots instead of my own? The thought sent a shiver down my spine as I recalled the night's events, now fragmented and unclear.

As I struggled to piece together the fragments, the chimes from a nearby church steeple cut through the din, tolling out the hour: eleven. I sprang out of bed, my movements jerky and uncoordinated, and collapsed onto the floor. The room still swayed around me, its axis tilted, as if the very foundations of the building had shifted during the night.

With great effort, I stumbled to the washstand and managed to dip my face into the cool water, hoping to revive myself. Drawing on my trousers with fumbling fingers, I collapsed into the easy chair, trying to gather my thoughts. But my mind was a jumble of confusion and regret. Something had clearly gone awry, and I was the culprit.

"A strong cigar," I muttered to myself, wincing at the memory. "I shouldn't have taken that risk. And the crowded room... all those people... And I've been working so hard. I really must take more exercise."

For a fleeting moment, I felt a glimmer of pride, thinking I was stronger than I seemed. But the words I spoke aloud cut through the fog: "Don't lie to yourself, Paul. You were drunk."

"No, not drunk," I protested, trying to salvage what was left of my dignity. "It's just that the sweet champagne disagreed with me. I'm sure it was my liver that was affected. Please, let's say it was my liver."
