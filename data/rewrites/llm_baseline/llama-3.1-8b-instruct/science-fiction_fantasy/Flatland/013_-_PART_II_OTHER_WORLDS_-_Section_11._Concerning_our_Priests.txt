In the depths of Flatland, where the whispers of ancient lore dwelled, I prepared to embark on the grand tale of my initiation into the mystical realm of Space. All that had transpired before was but a gentle lapping of waves against the shores of understanding, a prelude to the majestic symphony that was to come. The moment of truth beckoned, and I must confess that the anticipation was almost palpable.

As I set aside the trivialities of our daily lives – the manner in which we propelled ourselves through the void, the ingenious ways we constructed our dwellings, and the secrets of the rain that nourished our land – I could sense the weight of expectation upon me. My readers, I knew, would be eager to grasp the essence of our priests, those revered guardians of tradition and wisdom who wove the very fabric of our society.

With reverence, I spoke of our esteemed circles, the paragons of Flatland's hierarchy. Theirs was a role both multifaceted and enigmatic, for they were the architects of our culture, guiding the destinies of our people through their mastery of art, science, commerce, and governance. Though the masses regarded every circle as a circle, the initiated knew better – that beneath the surface, our circles were, in truth, polygons with an astonishing number of sides. As the count of sides increased, the polygon's edges grew infinitesimally thin, until they became imperceptible even to the most discerning touch.

In the rarefied atmosphere of our upper echelons, such tactile knowledge was deemed an impertinence, a breach of the sacred veil that shrouded the true nature of our perimeters. And so, our circles cultivated an air of mystery, their circumference a closely guarded secret known only to themselves. It was said that the chief circle of our land possessed a perimeter of ten thousand sides, a testament to their exalted status and the reverential awe with which they were held.

Yet, as our circles rose through the ranks, their ascent was not predetermined by the laws of heredity. The incremental increase in sides, a hallmark of our priestly class, defied the natural order, which limited the growth of sides to one per generation. Instead, our circles were bound by two contradictory decrees: the drive for advancement, which propelled them upward, and the capricious whims of fate, which governed the distribution of sides among their progeny.
