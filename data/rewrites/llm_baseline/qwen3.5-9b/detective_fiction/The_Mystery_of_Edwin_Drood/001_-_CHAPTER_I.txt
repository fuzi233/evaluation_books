An ancient English cathedral tower? How could such a structure possibly exist here? The familiar, massive gray square tower of the old cathedral stood right before him. Yet, there was no intervening space, no clear line of sight from the real world to connect the observer with the spire. What spike pierced the air between his eye and the impossible tower? Who had erected it?

Perhaps it was a command from the Sultan, intended for the impalement of a horde of Turkish robbers, one by one. Indeed, it seemed so: cymbals clashed in a rhythmic frenzy, and the Sultan passed toward his palace in a grand procession. Ten thousand scimitars flashed in the sunlight, while thirty thousand dancing girls scattered petals across the ground. Behind them followed white elephants, adorned in countless vibrant colors, accompanied by an endless retinue of servants.

Yet, despite this exotic spectacle, the cathedral tower rose stubbornly in the background, occupying a place where it should not be. And yet, no writhing body hung from the grim spike.

Wait. Was the spike merely a low, rusty nail atop the post of an old bedstead that had collapsed under the weight of its occupants? Perhaps a moment of drowsy laughter was required to entertain this notion.

Shuddering from head to toe, the man whose fragmented consciousness had assembled such fantastical images finally sat up. Supporting his trembling frame with his arms, he looked around. He found himself in the most squalid and cramped of small rooms. Through a ragged window curtain, the pale light of early dawn filtered in from a miserable courtyard.

He lay dressed across a large, unsightly bed, which had indeed buckled under the burden resting upon it. Also lying across the bed, not lengthwise but sprawled out, were a Chinese man, a Lascar sailor, and a gaunt woman. The first two appeared to be in a deep stupor; the latter was blowing into a makeshift pipe to ignite it. As she blew, shielding the flame with her thin hand, the red spark served as a dim lantern, revealing her features in the morning gloom.

"Another?" she asked in a querulous, rattling whisper. "Would you like another?"

He looked about, pressing a hand to his forehead.

"You've smoked as many as five since you walked through the door at midnight," the woman continued, complaining habitually. "Poor me, poor me, my head feels terrible. Those two came in after you. Ah, poor me, trade is slow, so very slow! Fewer Chinamen around the docks, fewer Lascars, and no ships arriving, they say! But here is another ready for you, dearie. You'll remember, like a good soul, won't you, that the market price is dreadfully high just now? More than three shillings and sixpence for a thimbleful! And you'll recall that nobody but me—and Jack Chinaman across the court, though he cannot match my skill—possesses the true secret of mixing it? Pay accordingly, dearie, will you?"

She blew into the pipe as she spoke, occasionally bubbling within it and inhaling deeply.

"Oh me, oh me, my lungs are weak, my lungs are failing! It's nearly ready for you, dearie. Ah, poor me, poor me, my poor hand shakes as if it might fall off entirely! I saw you beginning to wake, and I said to myself, 'I shall prepare another dose, and he will remember the market price of opium and pay accordingly.' Oh, my poor head! I make my pipes from old penny ink-bottles, you see, dearie—this is one—and I fit in a mouthpiece like this, taking my mixture from this thimble with this tiny horn spoon; and so I fill it, dearie. Ah, my poor nerves! I became drunk as hard as Heaven allows for sixteen years before I turned to this, but this does not harm me, not even a bit. Besides, it removes the hunger as well as the need for food, dearie."

She handed him the nearly empty pipe and sank back, turning onto her face.

He rose unsteadily from the bed, placed the pipe on the hearthstone, pulled back the ragged curtain, and gazed with revulsion at his three companions. He noted that the woman had smoked herself into a grotesque resemblance of the Chinese man. The contours of his cheek, eye, and temple, along with his complexion, were mirrored in hers. The Chinese man convulsively wrestled with one of his many gods or demons, snarling horribly. The Lascar laughed and drooled from his mouth. The hostess remained motionless.

"What visions can *she* possess?" the waking man mused as he turned her face toward him, standing and looking down at it. "Visions of butcher shops, public houses, and excessive credit? Of an influx of hideous customers, and this dreadful bedstead being set upright once more, and this awful court swept clean? To what height can she ascend, under any quantity of opium, above that?—Eh?"

He bent his ear close to listen to her muttering.

"Incomprehensible!"

As he watched the spasmodic twitches darting across her face and limbs, like fitful lightning flashing from a dark sky, some contagion seized him. Compelled by an unclean spirit of imitation, he withdrew to a lean armchair by the hearth—perhaps placed there specifically for such emergencies—and sat tightly gripping the edges until he regained control.

Then he returned, lunged at the Chinese man, and seized him firmly by the throat, twisting him violently onto the bed. The Chinese man clawed at the aggressive hands, resisted, gasped, and protested.

"What do you say?"

A watchful silence ensued.

"Incomprehensible!"

Slowly loosening his grip as he listened to the incoherent babbling with an attentive frown, he turned to the Lascar and dragged him forcefully onto the floor. As the Lascar fell, he sprang into a half-rising posture, glaring wildly, lashing about with his arms, and drawing a phantom knife. It then became evident that the woman had taken possession of the knife for safety; as they both started up, restraining and arguing with one another, the knife was visible tucked into her dress, not in his, when they drowsily dropped back, side by side.

There had been enough chattering and clattering between them, but to no avail. Whenever a distinct word was thrown into the air, it possessed neither sense nor sequence. Therefore, "incomprehensible" was the comment of the watcher, delivered with a reassuring nod and a gloomy smile.

He then laid certain silver coins on the table, retrieved his hat, groped his way down the broken stairs, offered a good morning to a rat-infested doorkeeper sleeping in a black hutch beneath the steps, and departed.

That same afternoon, the massive gray square tower of an old cathedral rose before the sight of a weary traveler. The bells rang out for the daily vespers service, and he felt compelled to attend, seemingly driven by a haste to reach the open cathedral doors. The choir members hurriedly donned their soiled white robes when he arrived among them. He put on his own robe and joined the procession filing into the service.

Then, the sacristan locked the iron-barred gates dividing the sanctuary from the chancel. Once everyone had scrambled into their places and hidden their faces, the intoned words, "WHEN THE WICKED MAN—" rose among the groins of arches and beams of the roof, awakening a muttered thunder.
