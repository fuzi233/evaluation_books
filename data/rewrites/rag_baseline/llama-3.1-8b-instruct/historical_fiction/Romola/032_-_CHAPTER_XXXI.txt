The morning sun cast its warm glow upon the terracotta rooftops of Florence, as Tito sat at his desk, his pen scratching across the parchment with a steady rhythm. His thoughts were elsewhere, however, for he had received news that the Milanese Count and the esteemed Seneschal de Beaucaire would be arriving soon. Their visit would be an opportunity for Tito to showcase the beauty of his library and the ancient artifacts within it, but he knew that Romola, his wife, would not be pleased about the prospect of hosting such high-ranking guests.

As he worked, Romola entered the room, her dark hair tucked beneath a simple hood, and her eyes cast downward. "My dear," Tito said, his voice light and careless, though his heart was heavy with the weight of his own doubts. "I think it would be best if you were to take a day off today, and visit your cousin Brigida. The Count and the Seneschal will be here soon, and I fear they may be...overwhelming."

Romola's hand rested on his head, a gentle gesture that Tito had grown accustomed to over the years. But today, something felt different. He couldn't quite put his finger on it, but he felt a sense of unease, a feeling that he was being pulled apart by opposing forces. He tried to raise his head, to meet Romola's gaze, but found himself unable to do so.

"The French are as ready to leave Florence as wasps leaving a ripe pear," he said, his words tumbling out in a hasty attempt to deflect attention from his true feelings. Romola's hand withdrew, and she turned to leave. "Take Maso with you," Tito called after her, his voice a little too loud, a little too forced.

As the door closed behind her, Tito felt a pang of regret. He knew that he had been less than honest with Romola, that he had been hiding behind a mask of indifference. But he couldn't shake the feeling that he was walking a tightrope, balancing between his love for his wife and his duty to his family. The armor and the painting, those mysterious symbols of his past, seemed to whisper secrets in his ear, urging him towards a path that he dare not tread.
