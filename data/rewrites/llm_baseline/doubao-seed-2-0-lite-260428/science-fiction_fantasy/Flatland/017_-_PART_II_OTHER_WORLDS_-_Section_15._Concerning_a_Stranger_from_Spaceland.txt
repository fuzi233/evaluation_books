I lay aside the surreal phantoms of my dreams to recount unvarnished, tangible fact.

It was the final evening of the 1999th year of our Flatland era. The soft patter of raindrops against our outer wall had long since signaled full nightfall, and I lingered beside my wife, turning over the events of the past year, the dawning century, the very arrival of the third Millennium, in my thoughts.
[To any Spaceland reader who hears me say "sitting" and imagines me perched on some unseen support, let me set the record straight: we have no feet in Flatland. We can no more "sit" or "stand" in your three-dimensional sense than a flounder washed onto your shore can rise to its fins and walk. That said, we know full well the distinct shifts of will that correspond to what you call lying, sitting, and standing – each state marked by a faint brightening of our outer lustre, visible to any nearby observer as our voluntary focus ramps upward. But there are a thousand other small oddities of our world I could explain here, and time will not suffer me to linger on them now.]

My four adult sons and two orphaned grandchildren had already retired to their private chambers. Only my wife remained beside me, to see the old Millennium out and welcome the new one across our threshold. I was lost in a reverie, turning over the half-formed words my youngest grandson had spoken earlier that evening – a brilliant young Hexagon, his angles perfectly even, one of the most promising lads of his generation. His uncles and I had been walking him through our standard Sight Recognition lesson, spinning ourselves slowly and rapidly on our central axes, quizzing him to name our orientation at every turn, and his answers had been so flawless that I rewarded him with a small lesson applying arithmetic to our plane geometry.

I laid out nine one-inch Squares side by side to form a single larger Square with a three-inch side, and proved to my grandson that while we Flatlanders could never see the inner space of that Square, we could calculate its total area by squaring the length of its edge. "And so," I told him, "we know 3 squared, or 9, gives us the total number of square inches inside that three-inch-sided Square."

The little Hexagon chewed on that for a moment, then tilted his sharp, even head and said, "But you’ve already taught me to raise numbers to the third power. I suppose 3 cubed must mean something in geometry, right? What is it?"
"Nothing at all," I told him. "Not in our geometry, at least. Our world only has two Dimensions." I went on to illustrate the math: a Point moving three inches traces a Line three inches long, represented by the number 3. A three-inch Line, moving parallel to itself across three inches of space, traces a three-inch Square, represented by 3 squared.

My grandson cut me off before I could finish, bright and eager, "Wait a second! If a Point moving three inches makes a Line of 3, and a Line moving parallel to itself three inches makes a Square of 3 squared… then a three-inch Square, moving somehow parallel to itself – I can’t picture how, exactly – must make some new thing, right? Something that’s three inches every way, that would be 3 cubed."
"Off to bed with you," I said, a little ruffled by the nonsense he was spouting. "If you spent less time talking ridiculous drivel, you’d retain more of your proper lessons."

My grandson vanished, chagrined, to his room, and I settled back beside my wife, trying to compile my retrospective of 1999 and imagine what 2000 might hold – but I couldn’t shake the odd, persistent thought sparked by my little Hexagon’s chatter. Only a handful of sand grains remained in my half-hour glass. I roused myself from my reverie, turned the glass to face North for the very last time of the old Millennium – and as I did, I blurted out loud, "That boy is a fool."

In that instant, I felt a foreign Presence in the room. A chill breath coiled through the air, sinking straight through my outer edges to my core.
"He is no such thing," my wife protested immediately. "You break the Commandments, speaking ill of your own grandson like that."

I paid her no mind. I twisted to look every direction in the small room, and saw nothing – but the Presence hung there, unmistakeable, and I shivered when that cold whisper drifted through the silence again. I reared up, every edge sharp with alarm.
"What is wrong with you?" my wife asked. "There’s no draft. What are you searching for? There’s nothing here."

There was nothing, by all appearances. I settled back, and said again, louder, "The boy is a fool, I tell you. 3 cubed can have no possible meaning in geometry."
A clear, distinct voice answered immediately, audible to both of us: "The boy is not a fool. And 3 cubed has an obvious, tangible Geometrical meaning."

We both sprang toward the source of the sound, my wife and I alike. Horror coiled in our frames when we saw the Figure standing before us.
At first glance it looked like a Woman, seen edge-on – but a second’s observation showed me its ends blurred into invisibility far too fast to be a Female Line. I would have taken it for a high-ranking Circle, save that its size shifted constantly, swelling and shrinking in a way no regular Figure in all Flatland could ever manage.

My wife had none of my training in Sight Recognition, none of the calm to note those strange details. Like most of her Sex, quick to instinct and prone to hasty jealousy, she jumped straight to the most obvious conclusion: some strange Woman had snuck into our home through an unblocked aperture. "How did this person get in here?" she exclaimed. "You promised me we wouldn’t have any unnecessary ventilators in this new house!"
"There are no ventilators," I said. "What makes you assume our guest is a Woman? My Sight Recognition makes it plain that –"
"Oh, spare me your fancy Sight Recognition," she huffed. "Feeling is believing, and a Straight Line in the hand is worth a Circle in the sight" – two of the most common proverbs among the Frailer Sex of Flatland.
"Fine, fine," I said, not wanting to irritate her further. "Then go ask for a proper introduction."

She smoothed her edges into the most gracious pose she could manage, and advanced on the stranger. "Permit me, Madam, to feel and be felt by you –"
She recoiled so fast she nearly tripped over our floor rug. "Oh! It’s not a Woman. There are no angles here, not a single trace of one. Have I been horribly rude to a perfect, high-ranking Circle?"
"I am, in a certain sense, a Circle," the Voice replied, "and a far more perfect Circle than any that exists within the bounds of Flatland. To speak more accurately, I am many Circles, all joined into one single being." His tone softened. "Dear Madam, I carry a message for your husband, one that I cannot deliver while you are present. If you would grant us a few minutes of privacy –"

My wife would not hear of inconveniencing such an august visitor. She assured him it was long past her own usual bedtime, apologized over and over for her earlier rudeness, and finally glided off to her own chambers.
I glanced down at my half-hour glass. The very last grain of sand had just fallen to the bottom.
The third Millennium had begun.
