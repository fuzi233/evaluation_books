At last, I decided to yank the Lineland monarch out of his awestruck, dazed rapture and ground him in plain sense: it was time to show him a glimmer of actual truth, the fundamental nature of the world I called home.
I opened my attempt gently enough: “Your Royal Highness, how do you tell apart the shapes and positions of your subjects? Before I stepped across the border into your realm, I could see clearly that some of your people are full Lines and others are mere Points, and some Lines stretch far longer than others—”
“Utter nonsense,” the King cut me off. “You must have suffered a delusion. Everyone knows it is physically impossible to tell a Line from a Point with sight alone. That work is done by hearing. And with my two voices, I can map my own exact shape to any of my subjects without effort. Look at me: I am the longest Line in all Lineland, spanning more than six full inches of Space.”
“Of Length, if you please,” I dared to correct him.
“Fool,” he snapped. “Space *is* Length. Interrupt me one more time and I will cast you out at once.”
I apologized, and he continued with open scorn. “Since you refuse to listen to logic, let me show you. Right now, my two wives are 6,000 miles, 7 yards, 2 feet, and 8 inches apart from me, one to my north and one to my south. Watch, I will call to them.” He chirped two rapid, distinct tones, then smirked. “Each of my wives hears my northern voice first, then my southern voice a tiny fraction of a second later—just long enough for sound to travel 6.457 inches. That tells them the two mouths at my two ends are 6.457 inches apart, so they know exactly how long I am. They did the math once before our wedding, but they could recalculate it any time they pleased. By that same method, I can learn the exact shape of every male subject in my kingdom, all with my ears.”
“Wait,” I pressed. “What if a man fakes a woman’s higher pitch with one of his two voices, or disguises his southern tone so no one can link it to his northern one? Couldn’t that trick cause massive harm? Why not make your neighbors confirm each other’s identities by feeling one another, to stamp out that kind of fraud?”
It was a deliberately silly question—feeling could never work in Lineland anyway—but I knew it would get under his skin. It worked perfectly.
“What?” he shrieked, horrified. “Explain that nonsense.”
“Feeling. Touching. Coming into direct contact,” I said.
“If by ‘feeling’ you mean creeping so close there is zero space between two people,” the King said, “know that that crime is punishable by immediate death in all my domains. The logic is simple: Women’s thin, frail forms would shatter instantly under that kind of collision, so the state has a duty to protect them. And since no one can tell a Man and Woman apart by sight anyway, the law bans *everyone* from approaching another close enough to erase the gap between you.
And what use could that brutal, coarse practice of ‘touching’ ever serve? Hearing gives you every bit of that information far faster, far more accurately. The Voice is the very essence of a being: it cannot be faked, it cannot be altered on a whim. Imagine if I wasted time poking through a billion of my subjects one by one, verifying their size by fumbling my way through contact! I can take a full census, mapping every person’s location, health, mood, and character across my entire kingdom in the span of a single breath of sound. Listen! Just listen.”
He fell quiet, soaking in a sound that to me was nothing but the faint, endless chirp of thousands of tiny grasshoppers.
“Your hearing is very impressive,” I conceded. “But your whole world must be unspeakably dull. All you can ever see, no matter where you look, is a single tiny Point! You can never view an entire Straight Line, you cannot even fathom what a full Line looks like. You have sight, but you are trapped with no access to the broad, linear vistas we take for granted in Flatland. It would be better to have no eyes at all than to see so little! I admit my hearing is nowhere near as sharp as yours—all that grand Lineland symphony that makes you giddy sounds to me like nothing but a swarm of chittering gnats. But with my eyes, I can tell a Line apart from a Point, and I can prove it. Right before I stepped into your kingdom, I saw you bouncing from left to right, then right to left, with seven Men and one Woman right next to you on your left, and eight Men and two Women on your right. Is that not correct?”
“The numbers and sexes are right,” the King said, “but I have no idea what this ‘left’ and ‘right’ nonsense you speak of means. And I do not believe you saw any of it. How could you see the inner length of any Man? You heard those facts, then dreamed you saw them. And this ‘left and right’ of yours—you just mean Northward and Southward, don’t you?”
“That is not it at all,” I told him. “You can move North and South, yes—but there is a second, entirely separate motion, which I call moving right to left.”
“Show me this motion from left to right,” the King demanded.
“I cannot show it to you while we are stuck on your Line,” I said. “Not unless you step out of it entirely.”
“Step out of my Line? Out of my world? Out of Space itself?”
“Exactly. Out of *your* Space. Your Space is not true Space. True Space is a wide, flat Plane. Your whole world is nothing but a single thin Line running through that Plane.”
“If you will not move to show me this motion,” the King huffed, “then describe it to me in plain words.”
“If you cannot even tell your own right side from your left,” I said, “I fear no words will make this clear. How can you not know such a simple distinction?”
“I do not understand a single word you are saying,” he replied.
I despaired of making it land. “When you move forward, do you never think you could move another way? Could you never turn your eye to face the direction your side is pointing right now? Instead of only moving toward one of your two pointed ends, have you never felt the urge to move sideways, toward that side of yours?”
“Never,” the King said, bewildered. “What do you mean? How could a man’s own inside face some direction? How could I move into my own inside? That is absurd.”
Words were useless. I would use action instead. “I will move slowly out of your Line, right into the direction I am trying to show you.”
As soon as I began to slip my 2D body sideways, out of the single 1D line of Lineland, the King kept yelling, “I can still see you! You are not moving at all!” The moment every last scrap of my body pulled free of his thin line of a world, he screamed at the top of his shrill voice, “She’s gone! She’s dead!”
“I am not dead,” I called back. “I am simply out of your Lineland, outside the Straight Line you mistakenly call Space. I am in true Space now, where I can see everything as it really is. Right now I can see your whole length, your entire side—the thing you call your ‘inside’. I can see every Man and Woman north and south of you, in perfect order. I can tell you their exact sizes, and the exact gaps between every single one.”
I recited every last detail, down to the tiniest fraction of an inch, for dozens of his subjects, then called out triumphantly, “Now do you believe me?” I stepped back into Lineland, resuming my original position right in front of him.
But the King’s face was twisted with rage. “If you were a reasonable Man—though since you only have one voice I am nearly certain you are just a Woman—you would listen to logic. You ask me to believe there is a second Line outside the only Line my senses know, and a second motion no one in my kingdom has ever felt. All you did to prove it was pull a cheap magician’s trick of vanishing and reappearing, and recite facts every single child in my capital already knows. That is irrational, audacious nonsense. Admit you are lying, or get out of my dominions immediately.”
I flew into a rage at his stubbornness, and especially at the insult of him mistaking me for a Woman. “You deluded, small-minded creature!” I shouted. “You think you are the peak of all existence, but you are nothing but the most imperfect, dim-witted thing alive. You call yourself sighted, but you can only ever see a single Point! You brag that you have deduced that Straight Lines exist—but I *see* Straight Lines, and I know for a fact that Angles, Triangles, Squares, Pentagons, Hexagons, and perfect Circles all exist. I am a Square, a Line of Lines, the very completion of your incomplete 1D self. Even I, who am infinitely greater than you, am nothing next to the great nobles of Flatland. I came here to lift you out of your pitiful ignorance, and this is how you repay me?”
The King let out a snarl, charging at me with his pointed tip outstretched, ready to pierce straight through my diagonal. At that exact moment, a battle cry rose up from millions of his subjects, swelling in volume until it matched the roar of a hundred thousand angry Isosceles Triangles and the artillery of a thousand Pentagons back in my home. I froze, paralyzed, unable to speak or move to save myself. The noise grew louder, the King drew closer, until—
I jolted awake. The breakfast bell was ringing, and I was back in the familiar, flat, solid reality of my own home in Flatland.
