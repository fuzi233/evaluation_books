The silence in the grand ballroom was oppressive, punctuated only by the soft ticking of a grandfather clock. I stood by the window, gazing out at the tranquil gardens of the Esmond estate. The warm sunlight cast long shadows across the manicured lawns, a stark contrast to the stifling atmosphere within. 

A faint rustle of silk announced the arrival of the Commander-in-Chief, His Excellency, accompanied by his retinue. The room fell silent as he approached, his dignified presence commanding attention. His Excellency's Master of the Horse, Captain Talmadge, preceded him, followed by Major Danvers, aide-de-camp, and the postmaster from Philadelphia, Mr. Franklin. 

As they entered, I noticed the postmaster's eyes scanning the room, taking in the opulent decorations and the assembled guests. His gaze lingered on the portraits adorning the walls, and I sensed a hint of curiosity, a desire to understand the stories behind the faces. Mr. Franklin's thoughts, however, were soon diverted to the conversation at hand. 

His Excellency began to speak, his words dripping with charm and diplomacy. "Gentlemen, I am delighted to be here today, surrounded by such esteemed company. And, Mr. Franklin, I must say, I am intrigued by your...unconventional background. A printer's boy from Philadelphia, and yet, you possess a wealth of knowledge and a keen understanding of the world beyond our shores." 

Mr. Franklin bowed graciously, his eyes sparkling with amusement. "It has been my good fortune, sir, to have encountered gentlemen of your caliber, and I have endeavored to learn from them. Education, as you know, is not always a luxury afforded to everyone, but I have made do with what I have."

His Excellency chuckled, his expression warm and affable. "Ah, Mr. Franklin, you are too modest. I have heard tales of the Duke of Marlborough's...limited literary skills, and even the great General Braddock has confessed to being no scholar. But, by George, they are gentlemen, and that is what matters."

The conversation flowed easily, a gentle stream of words and laughter, as the guests mingled and socialized. I stood apart, observing the scene, my mind wandering to the music that once filled these halls. The silence was a heavy blanket, weighing upon my soul.
