Gerard stood stunned, the news striking him like a blow that left his complexion pale, then flushed, then drained again. Rage tightened his jaw, grinding down upon the thought of pursuit. Men of spirit, he realized, behave much like wild boars; they may flee a superior force initially, driven by instinct rather than folly, but if the hunters press too closely and never let go, the beast turns. It lashes out, charging blindly through a thicket of enemies, seeking only space to breathe. Gerard braced himself for this counter-charge, his eyes hardening into battle-readiness. Yet, the resolve shattered instantly, leaving his face fallen and voice trembling with despair. "But my axe remains behind," he whispered, the weight of his abandoned tools echoing in the silence. "It lies in the Rhine."

Together, they weighed their options. Prudence offered counsel, urging caution against the village where enemies might lie in wait. Hunger, however, roared its command to seek sustenance. In the quiet struggle between prudence and necessity, the stomach won the day. They decided to cut across the open fields, away from the watchful eyes of the hamlet.

They paused beside a great haystack, borrowing two bundles to create a makeshift bed. Lying there, hidden beneath the straw yet prickled by stinging nettles, they rested in the damp earth. One by one, they ventured forth, returning with handfuls of turnips. They gnawed these meager roots at intervals throughout their slow retreat, savoring the sweet relief until the sun dipped below the horizon.

Then, the world turned gray and wet. Shivering, they slipped out into the driving rain and encroaching dark, emerging onto the far side of the village. The night was terrible, a blanket of pitch-blackness swept over by a gale that howled with ferocity. Neither sight nor sound held sway; they moved unseen and unheard, invisible phantoms drifting past their foes who slept unawares. In the natural terrors of such a storm, human enemies faded from their minds, replaced by the sheer, blinding horror of the tempest. It seemed as though they were groping and hacking their way through blocks of black marble, blind and deaf. When the moon finally rose, casting a wan light upon the landscape, they had traveled leagues beyond Düsseldorf. Still, they pressed onward, trudging through the mud.

Suddenly, looming before them, stood a massive edifice.

"Take heart!" cried Denys, his voice cutting through the wind. "I believe I recognize this place. Yes, it must be so. We have entered the diocese of Jülich. Here, Cologne holds no sway."

With those words, the fear began to recede. The next moment, they crossed the threshold and found themselves secure within the sanctuary of the walls.
