The rain-soaked streets of Cloisterham whispered secrets to those willing to listen. I stood at the edge of the Nuns' House courtyard, water dripping from my hat onto the worn stones. The Seminary for Young Ladies, a brass plate proclaimed, its letters gleaming like a promise. In the fading light, the old building seemed a relic from a forgotten era, its worn facade a testament to the passing of time. I recalled a similar place, a boarding school where I had spent a few listless years, surrounded by the whispers of other girls and the stern faces of the instructors.

As I stood there, a faint melody echoed through the deserted streets – the distant tolling of the Cathedral bell, its mournful sound a reminder that some sounds never truly fade away. I thought of the old watchmaker in the city, his shop a repository of relics from a bygone age. His wares, like the city itself, were a testament to the transience of life. Time, it seemed, was a currency that could not be redeemed, only spent.

The Nuns' House loomed before me, its gates shrouded in shadows. I pushed them open, the creaking of the hinges a solitary sound in the stillness. Inside, the courtyard was a tranquil oasis, a refuge from the damp and chill outside. Water pooled in the center, reflecting the fading light like a miniature mirror. I walked along the colonnade, my footsteps echoing off the walls. The silence was oppressive, punctuated only by the occasional drip of water from the eaves above.

As I approached the entrance, a figure emerged from the shadows. A young woman, her features indistinct in the fading light, handed me a letter. I recognized the handwriting – Miss Twinkleton's own. The contents, however, remained a mystery, a sealed envelope waiting to be opened, its secrets locked within. I tucked it into my pocket, the weight of it a reminder that some truths were best left unspoken. The woman vanished into the darkness, leaving me alone once more with the silence of the Nuns' House.
