In the hallowed halls of learning, where ancient traditions still hold sway, a curious dichotomy persists. While colleges, schools, and inns of court proudly retain the customs and institutions of their forebears, the trappings of modernity often seem woefully absent. A stroll through the corridors of a venerable foundation school or a learned university reveals a stark contrast to the well-appointed workhouses and prisons of our time. The latter establishments boast amenities that would put even the most discerning gentlemen to shame, yet the former institutions lag behind, content to endure dingy closets and cramped quarters in exchange for a pittance.

One need only consider the esteemed Inns of Court, with their storied histories and illustrious alumni, to appreciate the anomaly. The Temple, in particular, is home to a veritable pantheon of jurists and scholars whose names have become synonymous with excellence in the law. And yet, despite its rich heritage, the Temple itself seems to have evolved at a glacial pace, with its inhabitants still reliant on antiquated systems for even the most basic necessities. The venerable Old Grump, a veteran of the Norfolk Circuit, would often grumble about the recent introduction of shower baths in the chambers of his contemporaries, Warrington and Pendennis. Grump, now well into his seventh decade, had long since grown accustomed to doing without such luxuries, and his ancestors before him had managed quite well without them too.

As one ponders the Temple's history, it becomes apparent that the institution's priorities have always lain elsewhere. The historian's records make no mention of any benevolent soul devising a system of public bathing facilities for the benefit of the lawyers who call the Temple home. Instead, one finds Pump Court and Fountain Court, their hydraulic contraptions a testament to the ingenuity of a bygone era. But what of the countless counsel who toiled in the Temple's hallowed halls, pouring over dusty tomes and pondering the intricacies of the law? Might not a simple pump have brought them solace and comfort in their labors?

Despite these shortcomings, the Temple remains a place of enduring allure, its storied past and rustic charm captivating the hearts of all who wander its grounds. As one strolls through the gardens, one can almost hear the whispers of Eldon, Coke, and Chitty, their spirits lingering amidst the ancient stones.
