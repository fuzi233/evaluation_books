In the morning's hush, when the first wisps of sunlight crept through the leaded panes of the Beresford estate's drawing room, a faint tremor of expectation stirred within Margaret Hale. She had been lost in thought, pondering the inquiry from Mr. Henry Lennox, her mind's eye wandering to the far-off Highlands, where his young friends, Edith and her companions, had danced on the precipice of adventure. The memory of those reckless escapades seemed to kindle a warmth in Margaret's cheeks, a flush that deepened as she rose from her seat to greet Mr. Lennox's arrival. Her fingers brushed against the pages of her book, a worn volume of Dante's Paradise, before she turned to meet the stranger's gaze. The morning light dancing across his features imbued him with an air of quiet authority, a gravity that seemed almost palpable in the room.

As she extended her hand, Margaret's voice was bright with enthusiasm. "Tell Mama, Sarah, we're so eager to hear about Edith. We're all so grateful you've come." Mr. Lennox's response, low and smooth, sent a shiver of pleasure through her, like the gentle lapping of waves against the shore. His eyes, dark and intent, seemed to hold a world of understanding within them, and Margaret felt a pang of curiosity about the man behind the reserved demeanor.

The room itself, with its faded chintz and worn carpet, seemed a pale reflection of the vibrant tapestry that hung outside – the riotous colors of the verbenas and geraniums that bloomed on the lawn, a kaleidoscope of hues that danced in the morning light. Yet, as Margaret's gaze wandered to the bookshelves, her fingers trailing over the spines of the volumes, she felt a sense of comfort in the familiar rhythms of her home, a sense of roots that ran deep and strong. Mr. Lennox, too, seemed to be drinking in the atmosphere, his eyes roving over the room with a discerning eye, taking in the faded grandeur of the Beresford estate.

As Margaret excused herself to fetch her mother, Mr. Lennox's attention turned to the scattered papers on the table – a dictionary, a sheaf of notes in Margaret's handwriting, and the worn leather binding of Dante's Paradise.
