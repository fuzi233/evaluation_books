In the somber, stillness that followed the tumultuous days, the Hale household seemed to hold its breath, as if awaiting a signal to exhale. The afternoon sun cast long shadows across the parlour, where Mr. Hale sat lost in thought, his eyes gazing blankly into the distance. His daughter Margaret moved quietly, avoiding his notice as she went about her tasks, her mind preoccupied with the impending funeral and the anxiety that had taken up residence within her. She had resolved not to speak of the events that had transpired at the rectory, not wanting to disturb her father's fragile composure, but the weight of her own emotions threatened to spill over at any moment.

As she busied herself with the preparations for the next day's ceremony, Margaret's thoughts turned to the possibility of Mr. Bell's arrival. She had always looked forward to his visits, cherishing the warmth and kindness he brought to their home. But now, with the news of his absence due to the gout, she felt a pang of disappointment. Her father, however, seemed resigned to the idea, his voice low and measured as he spoke of the funeral arrangements.

"I suppose we shall hear from Mr. Bell tomorrow," he said, his eyes never leaving the windowpane.

"Yes," Margaret replied, her voice barely above a whisper.

"If he cannot come, I shall ask Mr. Thornton to accompany me," her father continued, his words hanging in the air like a challenge.

Margaret's response was immediate, her impulsive nature getting the better of her. "Don't ask Mr. Thornton, papa. Let me go with you."

Her father's expression was one of surprise, his brow furrowed in concern. "My dear, women do not generally attend funerals."

Margaret's eyes flashed with a mixture of defiance and sorrow. "No, they don't, because they're expected to be weak, and ashamed to show their emotions. But I'm not afraid of being seen, papa. I'll be strong, I promise. Please, don't ask Mr. Thornton. Let me go with you."

The request hung in the air, a delicate balance between Margaret's desire to support her father and her own need to confront the uncertainty that lay ahead. As the silence stretched out, Margaret's father seemed to waver, his resolve weakening in the face of his daughter's determination.
