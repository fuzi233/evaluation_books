As I settled into the rhythms of my new abode, I couldn't help but feel a twinge of disappointment when I saw my friend Miss Bengough dismiss the place with a careless glance. Indeed, she rarely allowed herself to be swayed by the finer details of life, preferring instead to navigate the world with a quiet indifference, often lost in thought as she devoured biscuits from a paper bag or savored the simple pleasures of a well-made cup of tea. A journalist of thirty-four, she was a force to be reckoned with – a whirlwind of energy and movement, her fair skin glowing like polished marble, her pink cheeks flushed with a perpetual vitality that seemed to defy the mundane routines of our lives. And yet, despite her many talents, she often struck me as a bit of a mystery, a puzzle whose pieces never quite fit together in a way that made complete sense.

Her arrival at my doorstep was announced by the rustle of her skirts on the stairs and a single, decisive knock on the door. I was still getting accustomed to the rhythms of my new home, and her entrance brought with it a welcome respite from the isolation. As she flung her hat and veil aside, a burst of fresh air accompanied her, carrying with it the scent of the outside world and the promise of new possibilities. She deposited a bundle of ladies' journals on a nearby chair, her movements a blur of energy and purpose.

"Make yourself at home," she said, her words tumbling forth in a rush as she began to remove her hatpins. "I didn't know if you'd be settled yet, so I brought some sandwiches for lunch. Do you have coffee ready?"

I rose to clear the space, gathering my scattered papers and putting them away with a sense of relief. She was already in the kitchen, the sound of running water filling the air as she prepared our meal. I joined her, and soon we were seated in the sitting room, the tray laden with sandwiches and coffee between us.

As we ate, I asked her opinion of my new home. "Well, what do you think of the place?" I asked, watching as she poured out coffee with a practiced hand.

She hesitated, her eyes scanning the room with a detached air. "It's...different," she said finally. "Anyone would think you were planning on settling down, Paul."
