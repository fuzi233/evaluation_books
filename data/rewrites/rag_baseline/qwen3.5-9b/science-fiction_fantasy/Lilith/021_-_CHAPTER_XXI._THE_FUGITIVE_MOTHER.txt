We shared the same vector, yet she remained blind to my presence, driven by a terror that radiated heat waves off her skin.

Pursuit protocols dictate engagement, but interference risks escalating the threat profile. Instead, I shifted laterally to intercept the trajectory of the pursuer. I paused, scanning the periphery, when a soft, fluid impact registered on my left temple. A kinetic strike. I hit the ground hard, the world tilting.

I recovered instantly, adrenaline overriding pain. All I caught was a flash of white retreating into the mist. I sprinted toward the source, blood dripping from my brow, but the chase had already peaked. A shriek tore through the night—a frequency that bypassed the auditory cortex and vibrated directly in the bone.

I accelerated, pushing past the point of rational caution. Through the vapor-thick moonlight, a low, quadrupedal form emerged. It moved with agonizing irregularity, a gait of desperate, floundering hops. Three points of contact with the earth. The fourth limb was elevated, dragging slightly.

I halted, allowing the subject to pass. Its coat was pristine white, marred by distinct, dark oval markings. A rhythmic, liquid sound accompanied its movement—the acoustic signature of rapid fluid loss. As it cleared my position, a stream of crimson trailed from the raised forelimb.

"It is hemorrhaging," I assessed clinically. "Another unit engaged the target."

Yet, a variable disrupted the tactical assessment: a surge of empathy. The biological imperative to eliminate threats clashed with the observation of acute distress. Even with a weaponized tool in hand, the decision matrix failed to execute a lethal command. The creature limped away, the flow of blood pooling softly in the grass, creating a localized hazard zone.

"If the exsanguination continues," I reasoned, "the threat vector becomes nullified within minutes."

I resumed movement. The primary objective remained the extraction of the fugitive. I located her shortly thereafter, seated on the terrain, the child secured in her lap.

"Status report?" I called out.

Her reaction was instantaneous—a violent startle response, muscles coiling to flee. I dropped to the ground, presenting a non-threatening profile.

"Stand down. No hostile intent detected. I intercepted the predator; it passed me moments ago, critically compromised by trauma to the anterior limb. Vital signs indicate imminent cessation of function."

"A projectile impact. High-velocity stone. Did you not register the acoustic event?"
