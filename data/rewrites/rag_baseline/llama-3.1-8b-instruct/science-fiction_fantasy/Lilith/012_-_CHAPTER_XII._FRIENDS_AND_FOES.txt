FRIENDS AND FOES

As I wandered along the riverbed, my eyes scanning the landscape for signs of life, a peculiar shrub caught my attention. Its branches were laden with unfamiliar fruit, which I hesitated to touch, fearing it might be poisonous. Little did I know that I was being watched by a multitude of eyes, eager to gauge my reaction. Behind the rocky outcroppings, a sea of curious gazes lay in wait.

Further along, I encountered more plants, each one larger than the last, until I realized they were actually dwarf-trees. The channels between them were so dense that I struggled to navigate, my feet stumbling over hidden roots. In one of the narrow passages, I heard a chorus of birds take flight, but saw nothing.

As I continued, the terrain opened up to reveal a clearing, where large fruit-bearing trees stood sentinel. Their fruit looked coarse, but I had no choice but to press on. The air grew thick with the scent of ripening fruit, and I followed the aroma to the edge of a hollow, where a forest flowed into the basin of a former lake. The trees above were a mix of species, but those in the hollow were almost exclusively fruit-bearing.

Weary from my journey, I collapsed onto the lush grass and moss, stretching out my limbs. Nearby, a tiny tree burst forth with rosy apples, their size no larger than cherries. My stomach growling, I plucked one and took a bite. The flavor exploded on my tongue, and I reached for another, but my hand was stayed by the sound of children's laughter and shouts.

"He likes our apples! He likes our apples! He's a good giant! He's a good giant!" The chant rose up from a cluster of children, their voices like a babbling brook. Some were young enough to still cling to their mothers' skirts, while others stood taller, their eyes shining with excitement.

I sat up, taking in the scene before me. The children seemed to range in age from toddlers to early teens, and a few older ones stood apart, observing the commotion with a more measured gaze. As I reached for another apple, they surged forward, their faces alight with joy. A small, mischievous-looking boy approached me, a huge green apple held out in offering.

"Take it, good giant," he said, his voice full of conviction.
