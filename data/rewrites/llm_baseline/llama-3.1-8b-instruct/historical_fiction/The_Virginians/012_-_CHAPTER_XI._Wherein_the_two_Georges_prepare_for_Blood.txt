Colonel Warrington sat in a dimly lit chamber of the tavern, lost in contemplation as he prepared for the impending confrontation. His adversary, in the adjacent room, was also engaged in a different sort of preparation – dictating a final letter to his mother, a testament to the complicated bond they shared. His brother, Harry, scribbled down the words with a mixture of reverence and trepidation.

"Mother, dear mother," George began, his voice laced with a hint of desperation, "I fear this may be my last letter to you. If fortune should favor me, I pray you reconsider the path you've chosen. I dare not think of what may transpire tomorrow." Harry's eyes welled up with tears as he struggled to keep pace with George's words.

"George, please, no," Harry implored, his voice cracking with emotion. "Do not speak of such things."

George's expression turned cold, his voice dripping with a bitter irony. "As I said, 'if I should be fortunate enough to escape unscathed, I scarce imagine you will persist in your current plans.'" The words hung in the air like a challenge, leaving Harry shaken.

"I merely follow the example of our grandfather," George continued, a faraway look in his eyes. "He once fought Lord Mohun, a duel sparked by a trivial quarrel at cards, yet he never mentioned the true reason behind their animosity – a woman's name, one that remained unspoken. I drew inspiration from his words, Harry. Our mother's involvement in this matter remains unspoken, just as it should be."

Harry's pen faltered as he tried to write the final words, "in view," but instead, he scribbled "in vew." A droplet of saltwater from his eyes blurred the ink, obliterating any further mistakes. George's clerk looked up, his face etched with misery.

"I'm too overcome to worry about spelling now, Georgy," he whispered. "Perhaps it's all a futile endeavor. Perhaps it was all just a ruse?"
