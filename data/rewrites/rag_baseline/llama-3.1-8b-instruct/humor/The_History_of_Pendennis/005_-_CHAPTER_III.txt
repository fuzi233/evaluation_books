In the early stages of Arthur Pendennis's life, several key factors contributed to his divergent trajectory. As a young man approaching adulthood, he stood at approximately five feet six inches tall, a stature that would eventually reach five feet eight inches by the time he turned eighteen. His growth spurt, coupled with his natural charm, earned him the admiration of his mother, Mrs. Pendennis, who envisioned him as the embodiment of the county's ideal gentleman. However, the family's social dynamics played a pivotal role in shaping Arthur's future.

His uncle, Major Pendennis, initially suggested that Arthur return to school to receive a comprehensive education, citing the importance of mastering classical Greek. Nevertheless, Arthur's mother, swayed by his son's persuasive nature, ultimately decided against this course of action. Instead, she opted to keep him at home, where he could avoid the perceived perils of the academic establishment and its rowdy student body. This decision was likely influenced by the Major's own experiences and his desire to shield his nephew from potential pitfalls.

The Major, in an attempt to steer Arthur towards a more conventional path, leveraged his connections with the Commander-in-Chief to secure a commission in the Foot Guards for his nephew. The prospect of donning a crimson and gold uniform, complete with a majestic bear-skin cap, undoubtedly captivated Arthur's imagination. He had witnessed the splendor of the military procession during a visit to St. James's, where he had caught sight of his acquaintance Tom Ricketts, now a proud member of the regiment. However, Arthur's mother, deeply rooted in her Christian values, expressed her reservations about the idea of her son pursuing a military career. Her concerns were not merely theoretical; she genuinely believed that a life of service was antithetical to a devout Christian's calling. In the end, Arthur, eager to please his mother, relinquished his aspirations for a military career, much to the chagrin of his uncle, who viewed his nephew's decision as a sign of weakness. Despite this, the Major's disappointment was short-lived, as he soon found solace in observing Arthur's prowess on horseback during their winter hunting excursions at Fairoaks.
