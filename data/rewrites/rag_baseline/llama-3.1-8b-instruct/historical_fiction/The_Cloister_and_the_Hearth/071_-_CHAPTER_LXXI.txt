As I stood amidst the labyrinthine streets of Bruges, the faint scent of blooming lilacs wafted through the air, mingling with the acrid tang of woodsmoke and the distant hint of salt from the sea. The city was alive, its pulse beating in time with the rhythm of the river Yser. But within the walls of a grand estate, a different cadence played out. Philip, Duke of Burgundy, Earl of Holland, lay abed, his frame weakened by the creeping tendrils of diphtheria. The physician, Dr. Remedy, was at a loss, his eyes scanning the shelves of dusty tomes and the cluttered workbenches in search of a solution.

A messenger burst into the room, his words tumbling forth in a rush. "Your Excellency, we've tried every remedy, but nothing seems to be working." The doctor's face twisted in a mixture of frustration and desperation. He knew that the Duke's life hung precariously in the balance. As he pondered the crisis, a spark of inspiration struck. "Flay an ape," he declared, his voice low and urgent. "We'll place it on the Duke's chest, and perhaps the ape's primal energy will counteract the disease."

The officers of state scurried off to fetch an ape, but their search proved fruitless. "There used to be an ape here," one of them said, scratching his head. "I saw it myself, but the mastiff devoured it for stealing its supper." The group stood in stunned silence, their faces etched with worry. Then, a glimmer of hope appeared on the seneschal's face. "Wait," he exclaimed, holding up a small, mangy creature. "This might suffice. It's apelike, don't you see?" The chancellor scoffed, his expression a picture of disdain. "The Princess Marie would never approve. She's besotted with that tailor's apprentice, Giles, strutting about in his finery."

Dr. Remedy's patience wore thin. "Enough!" he cried, summoning a servant. "Bring me a dog, any dog, but make sure it's of a single color." The servant hastened to comply, returning with a liver-colored hound. The doctor ordered it flayed, still warm and trembling, and placed it on the Duke's chest. The result was catastrophic.
