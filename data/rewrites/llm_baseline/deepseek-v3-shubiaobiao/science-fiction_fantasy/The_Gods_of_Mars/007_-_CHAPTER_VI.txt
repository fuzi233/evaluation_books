Thuvia’s hand tightened on my arm as she pointed upward. My gaze followed her gesture, and there, against the twin moons of Mars, dark shapes flitted like carrion birds—silent, swift, and deadly.

“The Shadow Horde,” she whispered.

Before I could speak, the night erupted. Blasts of energy split the air as the black pirates opened fire from their hovering craft. The therns answered in kind, their rifles flashing from temple ramparts. The battle had begun.

The pirates descended in tightening spirals, their ships—small, agile things built for speed—darting like knife-wings through the chaos. Larger vessels circled high above, raining explosives upon the marble spires below. Then, as if on some unseen signal, the raiders plunged recklessly into the gardens, their ships skimming the ground as their crews leapt into the fray.

What followed was slaughter, pure and primal.

The black warriors moved with a grace that belied their brutality. Their skin gleamed like polished onyx under the moonlight, their features sharp and regal—yet their eyes burned with a hunger for carnage. They fought not as soldiers, but as demons unleashed, their curved blades drinking deep of thern blood.

I watched, transfixed, as the battle unfolded. Golden-haired therns fell beneath ebony swords. A pirate dragged a screaming girl toward his ship, only to be cut down by a dozen defenders. Another seized a statue of ruby, shattering it against a foe’s skull. The gardens, once serene, ran red.

Thuvia’s voice pulled me back. “This is why the therns keep a million warriors at their borders,” she said. “The Horde comes like this, always—never to conquer, only to reap.”

“Why not destroy their ships?” I demanded, eyeing the unguarded fliers.

She shook her head. “They tried, once. The Horde returned with a fleet that blotted out the moons. The therns hid in the depths for a full cycle before the shadow passed.”

A new terror erupted as the banths, our liberated beasts, joined the chaos. The great predators tore through friend and foe alike, their roars drowning even the clash of steel. The battle lines shattered into panicked flight.

*Now.* If ever there was a moment to escape, it was this.

I seized Thuvia’s wrist and signaled Tars Tarkas. We sprinted for the nearest flier—a small two-man craft. But as we scrambled aboard, I realized our fatal error. The Thark’s massive weight anchored us. The repulsion tanks couldn’t lift us all.

The pirates spotted us. With howls of triumph, they charged.

No time. I jammed the controls to full thrust, then leapt clear as the flier lurched upward. Thuvia’s cry—“My Prince!”—was cut short as the ship soared away.

Now, alone, I faced the horde.

My blade became a whirlwind. I fought as only a son of Earth could—with strength these Martians had never seen. They fell back, wary. One snarled, “You fight like a Dator. Pity your blood is pale.”

I grinned, bloody and reckless. “I’m no thern.”

Then—impact. Something heavy struck my back. I staggered, turned, and saw the answer to my prayers: an anchor line, dangling from a cruiser above. Without hesitation, I vaulted over the pirates, caught the chain, and swung into the void.

The ship banked hard, carrying me over the cliffs, out above the Lost Sea of Korus. I climbed, hand over hand, until I grasped the rail—

And looked straight into the eyes of a black pirate, his smile sharp as a dagger.

“Welcome,” he hissed.

The deck swallowed me in darkness.
