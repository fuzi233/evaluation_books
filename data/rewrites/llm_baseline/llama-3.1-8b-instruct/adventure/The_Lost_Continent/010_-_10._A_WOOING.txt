As the murmurs swelled into shouts, a sense of unease settled over me. People around me urged me to kneel, warning that failure to do so would invite their wrath. One voice rose above the rest, threatening to become the Empress's priest and butcher me if I refused. Yet, as the word "Deucalion" began to circulate, the fervor slowly dissipated. The man who had once ruled Yucatan twenty years prior was a figure they remembered, and the possibility of his vengeance tempered their zeal.

When the crowd's fervor subsided, Phorenice turned to face me, standing apart from the others. I stepped forward, passing between the massive stones, and approached her beside the altar. I did not kneel, adhering instead to the formal decorum she had taught me as her chief minister. With a bow, I greeted her as Empress.

"Deucalion, man of ice," she replied, a hint of a smile playing on her lips.

"I still adhere to the old Gods," I replied firmly.

She raised an eyebrow, and Ylga approached us, her face pale and her hand trembling. "Please, Empress, spare his life," she implored. "He is a strong-willed man, resistant to change. Perhaps, with time, he will come to see the truth."

Phorenice's expression darkened. "You presume too much, Ylga," she snapped, her voice venomous. "You will return to your duties, or I will have you whipped. And I'll ensure your punishment is... memorable." The Empress's threat sent Ylga scurrying away, her shoulders bowed in defeat.

Phorenice turned to me, her eyes sparkling. "My lord, I'm eager to spread my wings and explore the possibilities they hold." I offered a measured response, and she smiled knowingly. "We'll discuss this further at the pyramid, when we can speak in private."

As it became clear that my defiance had been forgiven, I offered Phorenice my arm, and she took it, her touch warm and delicate despite her skill with the sword. We walked away from the circle, leaving behind the scent of the sacrifice. I couldn't help but think that the Lord above would choose the perfect moment to exact retribution, rather than relying on me to defend His honor.
