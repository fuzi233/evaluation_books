The warm sunlight danced across the vibrant colors of the outer gardens as I followed the guard, Xodar's anguished face etched in my mind. The air was heavy with the scent of blooming flowers and the distant tang of the ocean, a stark contrast to the turmoil brewing before me. A crowd of noble blacks surrounded Xodar, their faces twisted in a mixture of anger and disdain. They hurled insults and curses, their words like a stinging rain that Xodar endured with a stoic silence, his eyes cast downward.

As I entered, the crowd turned toward me, their gazes filled with a mixture of curiosity and hostility. A beautiful woman stepped forward, her laughter like music amidst the chaos. "So, this is the one who defeated the great Xodar bare-handed," she said, her voice dripping with amusement. "Let us see how it was done."

A tall, imposing figure emerged from the shadows, adorned in resplendent ornaments and arms. His name was Thurid, a Dator renowned for his bravery and skill in battle. As he approached, the crowd parted, their eyes fixed on the spectacle unfolding before them.

"What now?" Thurid demanded, his voice commanding attention. "What would you of me?"

The crowd's voices rose in a cacophony of demands, each one urging Thurid to confront Xodar. The Dator's eyes narrowed, his gaze cold and calculating, as he turned to face his former comrade. "Calot," he spat, "ever did I think you carried the heart of a sorak in your breast. You have bested me in the secret councils of Issus, but now, in the field of war, your cowardly heart has revealed its sores to all the world." With a swift motion, Thurid kicked Xodar, his foot aimed at the fallen warrior's exposed flank.

My blood boiled at the sight, and I felt my instincts take hold. I had no love for Xodar, but I could not abide the cruel treatment meted out to him. The memory of his bravery on the deck of the cruiser lingered, and I knew I had to act. I stood beside Xodar, my hand instinctively reaching out to intercept Thurid's kick. My fist connected with a resounding thud, striking the Dator's shin bone with a force that sent a pained yelp echoing through the gardens.
