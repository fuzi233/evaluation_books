In the days preceding my departure from the Bretton household, I found myself oscillating between a deep-seated desire to depart and a lingering reluctance to part with the warmth and familiarity of my adopted family. My trunk, packed and corded, stood ready for transport, its presence serving as a constant reminder of the inexorable passage of time and the inevitable severance of ties that bound me to this place. As I navigated the complexities of my emotions, I was acutely aware of the weight of expectation and obligation that accompanied my impending return to the pensionnat, an institution that represented both confinement and liberation.

Mrs. Bretton's words of reassurance, though well-intentioned, only served to heighten my sense of restlessness, as did the gentle entreaties of her son, Graham, who, despite his own pressing engagements, seemed determined to prolong my stay. Our conversation, like a delicate dance, wove in and out of a complex web of words, each of us probing the boundaries of our feelings and desires, yet neither of us willing to commit to a definitive course of action. It was a struggle I had anticipated, one that would ultimately require me to reconcile my longing for independence with the comfort and security offered by those I had grown to care for.

As the hours ticked away, and the shadows deepened outside, I felt a growing sense of urgency, my thoughts consumed by the prospect of re-establishing myself within the confines of the Rue Fossette. The memory of my previous encounter with Graham, a chance meeting that had taken place under similar autumnal skies, now stirred within me, a bittersweet recollection that I had never explicitly shared with him. Its significance, however, remained palpable, a private knowledge that I chose to keep locked away, hidden from the world, yet resonating deeply within my own consciousness.

Upon arriving at Madame Beck's doorstep, I was greeted by the familiar glow of the lamp, casting a warm light upon the rain-soaked pavement. The atmosphere was eerily reminiscent of that earlier evening, when I had first presented myself at this very threshold, a solitary and supplicant figure, my heart pounding in anticipation. Graham, now standing beside me, rung the bell, and the door swung open, releasing a wave of sound and motion into the quiet evening air.
