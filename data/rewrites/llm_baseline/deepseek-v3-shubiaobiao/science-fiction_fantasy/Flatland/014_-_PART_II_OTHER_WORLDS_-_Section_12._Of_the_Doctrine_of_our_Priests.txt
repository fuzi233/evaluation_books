The doctrine of the Circles may be distilled into a single, immutable maxim: *Attend to your Configuration.* Whether in matters of governance, faith, or morality, their teachings orbit this singular truth—the refinement of individual and collective form, with supreme reverence for the Configuration of the Circles, to which all else submits.

It is the triumph of the Circles to have extinguished ancient heresies that once led men to squander effort and empathy on the delusion that conduct springs from will, effort, or virtue rather than the unyielding geometry of one’s form. The great Pantocyclus—who quelled the Colour Revolt—first illuminated this truth: *Configuration shapes destiny.* An Isosceles born with uneven sides is fated to misdeeds unless corrected in the Hospital; a Triangle or Square marred by Irregularity must submit to regularization or face the State’s merciless angles. All flaws, from petty misconduct to grave crime, Pantocyclus ascribed to deviations in form—whether congenital, acquired by collision, neglect, or even the caprices of temperature. Thus, he decreed, neither virtue nor vice merits praise or blame. Why commend a Square’s loyalty when it is but the inevitable alignment of his angles? Why condemn a thieving Isosceles when his crimes arise from the asymmetry of his sides?

In theory, this doctrine is flawless. In practice, complications arise. When an Isosceles thief pleads his unevenness as excuse, the Magistrate may rightly condemn him as a menace to be consumed. But in the domestic sphere, where consumption is no remedy, the logic falters. When my own Hexagonal Grandson claims his disobedience stems from a sudden chill warping his Perimeter, and insists his Configuration demands indulgence in sweets, I find myself at a loss—unable to refute his logic yet unwilling to indulge it. I resort instead to scoldings, though I confess no evidence that such rebukes mend his angles.

Nor am I alone in this dissonance. Even among the Circles, Judges in their courts wield praise and blame as if such concepts held weight, and in their homes, they chastise their young with fervor, as though choice were real. Yet all the while, they enforce the primacy of Configuration, inverting the natural order of reverence. Where your kind teach children to honor their parents, we teach men to honor their Grandsons—or Sons, if Grandsons are lacking—for the Circles decree that fathers must sacrifice their own interests to posterity, ensuring the State’s geometric perfection.

Yet the system is not without flaw. Consider the Circles’ dealings with Women. To prevent Irregular births, no Woman with Irregular ancestry may wed a man of rising lineage. Yet as rank ascends, vigilance wanes. An ambitious Isosceles scrutinizes his bride’s pedigree for five hundred generations; a Circle may wed a woman with an Irregular forebear for the sake of her luster or the cadence of her voice. Such unions often bear no fruit, or worse, yield diminished offspring. Though the loss of a side or two in a high Polygon may escape notice—and sometimes be restored in the Gymnasium—the Circles grow complacent. If this persists, the day may come when no Chief Circle can be born, and Flatland’s order will collapse.

A second peril lies in our treatment of Women. Centuries ago, the Circles decreed that Women, being ruled by Emotion rather than Reason, should no longer be educated as rational beings. Denied arithmetic, even the ability to count their children’s angles, their intellect has dwindled with each generation. This policy, meant to stabilize, now threatens *us*. For we Males must live a double existence: in the company of Women, we speak of "love," "duty," and "pity"—words without meaning, tools to placate their irrationality. Among ourselves, these concepts dissolve into cold necessity, fitness, benefit. We flatter Women to their faces; behind their backs, even the Circles regard them as little more than mindless shapes.

This duplicity weighs heavily on our young. At three years old, a boy is torn from his Mother’s care and taught to unlearn her language—save for hollow reassurances—and adopt the precise dialect of geometry. Already, I fear our grasp of mathematical truth weakens compared to our forebears. What if a Woman were to secretly learn to read? What if a careless child revealed to his Mother the stark logic we reserve for Men?

Thus, I plead—not for sentiment, but for the preservation of reason—that the Circles reconsider the education of Women. For the sake of Flatland’s future, we cannot afford to let half our kind languish in ignorance.
