As I stepped through the doorway, Rachel rose abruptly from the piano bench. I closed the door softly behind me, leaving us standing in the vast, silent expanse of the room, separated by its full length. The single motion of rising seemed to be the last reserve of energy she possessed; every other faculty, both physical and mental, appeared to have dissolved into the singular act of staring at me.

A sudden fear seized me that I had intruded too abruptly. I took a step forward, then another, softening my tone. "Rachel!"

The sound of my voice seemed to restore life to her limbs and color to her pale cheeks. She moved toward me, silently, as though driven by a force beyond her own volition. Slowly, inexorably, she drew closer. A warm, dusky hue flushed her cheeks, and a light of returning intelligence flickered brighter in her eyes with every passing second. For a moment, I forgot the business that had summoned me; I forgot the vile suspicion hanging over my reputation; I discarded every duty, past, present, and future, that demanded my attention. I saw only the woman I loved advancing toward me. She trembled, halted, and stood irresolute. Overcome, I embraced her and buried my face in hers, showering her with kisses.

For a fleeting instant, I believed the kisses were returned—a moment when it seemed she, too, had forgotten. But before the thought could fully form, her first deliberate action shattered the illusion. With a cry akin to horror, and with a strength I doubted I could have resisted, she shoved me violently away. I saw merciless anger in her gaze and scornful contempt on her lips. She scanned me from head to toe as one might inspect a stranger who had insulted her.

"You coward!" she cried. "You mean, miserable, heartless coward!"

Those were her opening words. The most unbearable reproach a woman could hurl at a man was the accusation she chose to direct at me.

"I recall the time, Rachel," I said, "when you might have told me I had offended you in a nobler fashion than this. I beg your pardon."

Some of the bitterness I felt likely colored my voice. Immediately, her eyes, which had turned away, snapped back to meet mine with reluctance. She replied in a low, sulky tone, displaying a submissive demeanor entirely foreign to my knowledge of her.

"Perhaps there is some excuse for me," she murmured. "Given what you have done, is it truly a manly act for you to seek me out as you did today? It appears a cowardly experiment to test my weakness for you. A cowardly surprise to catch me kissing you. But that is merely a woman's perspective. I should have known it wasn't yours. I would have done better to control myself and remain silent."

That apology was more crushing than the initial insult; even the most degraded man would feel humiliated by it.

"If my honor were not in your hands," I declared, "I would leave you this instant and never return. You have spoken of my actions. What have I done?"

"What have you done! *You* dare ask *me* that?"

"I ask it."

"I have kept your infamy secret," she answered bitterly. "I have suffered the consequences of hiding it. Am I to be spared the indignity of you asking me what you have done? Has all sense of gratitude died within you? You were once a gentleman. You were once dear to my mother, and dearest to me—"

Her voice faltered. She collapsed into a chair, turned her back upon me, and buried her face in her hands.

I waited a moment before trusting myself to speak further. In that silence, I struggled to determine which pain was sharper: the sting of her contempt or the proud resolve that barred me from sharing in her distress.

"If you will not speak first," I said, "I must. I have come with something serious to say. Will you grant me the common courtesy of listening?"

She neither moved nor answered. Making no second attempt to reach her, and matching her obstinacy with my own pride, I recounted my discovery at the Shivering Sand and the events leading to it. The explanation naturally took some time. Throughout, she never glanced around, nor uttered a syllable.

I maintained my composure. My entire future likely hinged on retaining my sanity in that critical moment. It was time to test Mr. Bruff's theory. Driven by the breathless intensity of this trial, I circled the room to stand directly in front of her.

"I have a question to ask," I said. "It requires me to revisit a painful subject. Did Rosanna Spearman show you the nightgown? Yes, or No?"

She sprang to her feet and walked straight up to me, compelled by her own will. Her eyes searched my face intently, as if reading something there unseen before.

"Are you mad?" she asked.

I held my restraint. "Rachel, will you answer my question?"

Ignoring me, she continued:

"Do you have some ulterior motive I cannot fathom? Some base fear regarding the future? Rumors say your father's death has made you wealthy. Have you come here to compensate me for the loss of my diamond? Do you possess the courage to feel ashamed of your mission? Is that the secret behind your feigned innocence and your tale concerning Rosanna Spearman? Is there a motive of shame driving all these lies now?"

I stopped her then. I could no longer control myself.

"You have done me an infamous wrong!" I exploded, heatedly. "You suspect me of stealing your diamond. I have a right to know, and I *will* know, the reason why!"

"Suspect you!" she shrieked, her anger surging alongside mine. "*Villain! I saw you take the diamond with my own eyes!*"

The revelation crashing over me, the total collapse of the case built upon Mr. Bruff's reliance, left me paralyzed. Though innocent, I stood before her in silence. To her eyes—or indeed anyone's—I must have appeared as a man crushed by the discovery of his own guilt.

She recoiled from the sight of my humiliation and her own triumph. The sudden silence that fell upon me seemed to terrify her. "I spared you at the time," she said. "I would have spared you now, had you not forced me to speak." She moved toward the door, hesitating briefly. "Why did you come here to humiliate yourself?" she asked. "Why did you come here to humiliate me?" She paused again, a few steps away. "For God's sake, say something!" she cried passionately. "If you have any mercy left, do not let me degrade myself thus! Speak—and drive me out of this room!"

I advanced toward her, barely aware of my actions. Perhaps I vaguely intended to detain her until she revealed more. Since learning that the evidence condemning me in Rachel's mind was solely the testimony of her own eyes, nothing—not even my certainty of my innocence—seemed clear. I took her hand and attempted to speak with firmness and purpose. All that emerged was, "Rachel, you once loved me."

She shuddered and looked away. Her hand lay limp and trembling in mine. "Let go of it," she whispered faintly.

My touch seemed to affect her as my voice had initially: after calling me a coward and branding me a thief, while her hand remained in mine, I was still her master!

Gently, I drew her back to the center of the room and seated her beside me. "Rachel," I said, "I cannot explain the contradiction in what I am about to tell you. I can only speak the truth as you have spoken it. You saw me—with your own eyes, you saw me take the diamond. Before God, who hears us, I declare that I now realize I took it for the first time! Do you still doubt me?"

She paid no heed. "Let go of my hand," she repeated weakly. That was her sole response. Her head sank onto my shoulder, and her hand unconsciously closed around mine at the very moment she requested its release.

I refrained from pressing further, but my forbearance ended there. My chance of ever raising my head among honorable men depended on compelling her to finish her confession. My only remaining hope was that she might have overlooked a trivial detail in the chain of evidence—one that, under careful scrutiny, could eventually vindicate my innocence. I kept hold of her hand, speaking to her with all the sympathy and confidence I could summon from our shared past.

"I wish to ask you something," I said. "I need you to recount everything that occurred from the moment we bid each other goodnight until the time you witnessed me taking the diamond."

She lifted her head from my shoulder, making an effort to free her hand. "Oh, why go back to it!" she exclaimed. "Why go back to it!"

"I will tell you why, Rachel. We are both victims of some monstrous delusion wearing the mask of truth. If we examine what happened on the night of your birthday together, we may finally understand one another."

Her head dropped back against my shoulder. Tears gathered in her eyes and traced slow paths down her cheeks. "Oh!" she said. "Have *I* never harbored that hope? Have *I* not tried to see it, as you are trying now?"

"You have tried alone," I answered. "You have not tried with me to aid you."
