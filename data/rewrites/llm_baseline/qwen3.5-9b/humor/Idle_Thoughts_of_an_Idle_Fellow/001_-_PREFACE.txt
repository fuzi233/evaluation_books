A couple of friends who took a peek at these manuscripts while they were still wet ink noticed something startling: they weren't terrible. Meanwhile, several relatives made me a binding promise—they'd actually purchase the book once it hit the shelves. Feeling morally obligated by their praise and their wallet, I've decided I can no longer drag out the publication date. Without this tiny spark of "public demand," I probably would have kept these mere "idle thoughts" locked away, refusing to serve them as mental sustenance to the English-speaking world.

Let's be honest about what modern readers expect from a novel: improvement, instruction, and elevation. If you're looking for a book that will lift your spirit higher than a cow could possibly climb, look elsewhere. I cannot, in good conscience, recommend this volume for any serious or useful purpose whatsoever.

My only suggestion? When you've grown weary of devouring "The Best Hundred Books" and need a palate cleanser, pick this up for thirty minutes. It won't teach you anything new, but it will certainly be a refreshing change of pace.
