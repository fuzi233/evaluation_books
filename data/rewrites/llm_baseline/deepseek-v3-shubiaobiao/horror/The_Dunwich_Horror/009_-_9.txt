The motorcar carrying Armitage, Rice, and Morgan rumbled into Dunwich just past noon under a sky that should have been cheerful. Yet the sunlight seemed to falter as it touched the warped hills, the twisted ravines—places where the earth itself felt wrong. Sentinel Hill loomed in the distance, its crown of ancient stones stark against the clouds like broken teeth.

At Osborn’s store, the air was thick with unspoken terror. The men didn’t need to ask what had happened—the ruin of the Frye house was answer enough.

They spent the afternoon scouring the village, piecing together the horror from shaken whispers and the evidence before them: the sticky black residue smeared across the Frye wreckage, the monstrous tracks gouged into the mud, the cattle torn apart as if by something that had no shape. Sentinel Hill’s trail of crushed grass and splintered saplings spoke of something vast, something that moved unseen.

By evening, they learned of the missing State Police—five men who had vanished after venturing near the glen. Old Sam Hutchins turned ashen as he pointed toward the yawning darkness between the hills.

A silence fell, heavy as a burial shroud.

---

Night came, and with it, the distant rumble of the earth. The whippoorwills shrieked like things in pain. Once, a gust from the glen carried the stench of decay—the same fetor that had clung to Wilbur Whateley’s corpse.

But the horror did not show itself.

Armitage clutched the brittle pages of his spells, pulse hammering. Rice checked the metal sprayer in his hands, while Morgan hefted his rifle like a man clinging to a last, futile hope.

Dawn broke gray and sickly. The rain came soon after, thick and cold.

Then—screams on the road.

A pack of men stumbled toward them, wild-eyed, babbling of trees bending where nothing stood, of a bridge splintering under invisible weight. Seth Bishop’s house was gone now, just like the Fryes’.

*“It’s moving again—*and in daylight!”

Armitage forced steadiness into his voice. “We follow it.”

The men hesitated.

“It’s wizardry,” he pressed. “And it can be *unmade*.”

They cut through the soaked fields toward Bishop’s ruined farm. The tracks led on, deeper into the valley, toward Sentinel Hill.

Then, through the telescope, Morgan saw it—

The grass on the hillside was *moving*.

Not from wind.

From something beneath.

Something climbing.
