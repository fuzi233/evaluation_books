As the final moments of the old millennium ticked away, like grains of sand slipping through the hourglass, I sat beside my wife in the warm, golden light of our parlor, the soft ticking of the clockwork mechanisms a gentle counterpoint to the patter of rain outside. Our children and grandchildren had long since retired to their chambers, leaving us to bid farewell to the passing year and welcome the uncertain promise of the one to come. My thoughts, however, lingered on the lively exchange I'd shared with my youngest grandson earlier that evening, his bright mind racing ahead of his years as he grasped the fundamental principles of geometry.

We'd been practicing sight recognition, our bodies rotating in tandem as we questioned each other about our positions, the little one's responses a testament to his natural aptitude. But it was his question about the meaning of 3^3 that had left me momentarily stumped. "Nothing at all," I'd replied, attempting to steer him back on course, "not at least in geometry, which deals only with two dimensions." I'd taken a moment to illustrate the concept, explaining how a point moving through space created a line, and a line moving parallel to itself gave rise to a square.

The boy's persistence, however, had caught me off guard. His eyes sparkling with excitement, he'd pressed his case, suggesting that if a point could become a line, and a line could become a square, then surely a square could become something more – something represented by 3^3. I'd been taken aback, a little irritated, and had dismissed him with a curt instruction to retire for the night. Now, as the final moments of the old millennium slipped away, I couldn't shake the memory of his words, the image of his determined face haunting me like a specter.

Rousing myself from my reverie, I turned the hourglass northward for the last time, the gesture a ritualistic acknowledgement of the passing year. And in that instant, I spoke aloud the words that would change everything: "The boy is a fool." It was a careless remark, a fleeting thought, but as soon as the words left my lips, I became aware of a presence in the room, a cold draft that sent shivers down my spine.
