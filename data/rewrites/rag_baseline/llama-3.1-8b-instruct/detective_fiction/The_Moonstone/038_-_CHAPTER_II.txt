The scent of damp earth and rain-soaked leaves filled the air as I stepped into the crumbling manor, the heavy wooden door creaking beneath my hand. Betteredge, a man whose rugged features had weathered decades of service to the household, looked up from the worn leather book on his lap, a mixture of surprise and curiosity etched on his face. I pointed to the familiar title, and his eyes widened as he recalled the words of Robinson Crusoe, which had hinted at my impending arrival. He rose to his feet, his movements stiff from years of service, and stood frozen, torn between his loyalty to the absent Rachel and his welcome for me.

As he held the book aloft, the yellowed pages seemed to glow in the dim light, and for a moment, I thought I saw a glimmer of the man who had written those words, a man who had faced the unyielding vastness of the ocean and emerged transformed. Betteredge's eyes, however, remained fixed on me, searching for answers to questions he dared not ask. I offered him my hand, and he took it, his grip firm but his touch tinged with a deep sadness.

"Here's the passage, Mr. Franklin," he said, his voice trembling with emotion. "Page one hundred and fifty-six, where Robinson Crusoe stands 'like one thunderstruck'—as if he had seen an apparition. If that isn't a warning of your sudden return, I know nothing of the English language!" He slammed the book shut, his eyes welling up with tears, and I felt a pang of regret for the distance that had grown between us.

As we walked through the deserted halls, the silence was oppressive, punctuated only by the creaks and groans of the old house. Betteredge's footsteps echoed off the walls, and I knew he was struggling to reconcile his love for Rachel with the reality of her absence. I led him out into the garden, where the scent of blooming flowers and fresh earth offered a fleeting respite from the melancholy that shrouded the manor.

There, I broke the news to him, my words tumbling out in a hesitant rush. Betteredge listened intently, his face a map of conflicting emotions.
