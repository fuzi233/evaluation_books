As the sun began to set over the quaint town of Cloisterham, a stranger emerged from the shadows, his white hair and black eyebrows a stark contrast to the warm tones of the fading light. Dressed in a tightly buttoned blue surout and a gray waistcoat, he presented an air of quiet authority, yet introduced himself at the local inn, the Crozier, as a man of leisure living off his means. His intention, he declared, was to settle in the picturesque old city for a short while, perhaps even longer, to indulge in the charms of its ancient architecture. As he waited for his meal to arrive – a simple but satisfying repast of fried sole, veal cutlet, and a glass of sherry – the stranger took a seat by the fire, his eyes scanning the room with a quiet intensity.

The waiter, a man accustomed to dealing with the ebbs and flows of business at the Crozier, took note of the stranger's presence and began to gather information. "A comfortable lodging for a gentleman such as yourself, sir?" he asked, his tone a blend of curiosity and hospitality. The stranger nodded, his white hair shaking slightly as he did so, like a Newfoundland dog preparing for dinner.

"I should prefer something old," he said, his voice low and measured. "Something with character, a place that tells a story. Something, in fact, that is both odd and out of the way." He paused, his eyes narrowing as he watched the waiter. "And if possible, something with a hint of grandeur."

The waiter, a man well-versed in the intricacies of the town, nodded thoughtfully. "We have a selection of such lodgings available, sir," he said, a hint of pride in his voice. "Indeed, I'm confident we can find something that meets your...particular tastes." He hesitated, a flicker of uncertainty crossing his face. "Though, I must admit, an architectural lodging might be a challenge."

The stranger's eyes lit up with interest. "Ah, anything with a touch of the cathedral, perhaps?"
