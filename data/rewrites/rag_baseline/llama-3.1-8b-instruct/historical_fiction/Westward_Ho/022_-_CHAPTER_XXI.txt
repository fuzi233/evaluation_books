As the expedition navigated the dense, impenetrable fog that shrouded the landscape, Amyas Le Strange found himself at the center of a heated debate regarding the nature of the mysterious illness afflicting his crew. The men were on the brink of panic, their anxiety palpable as they struggled to comprehend the causes behind the affliction. The doctor's attempts to explain the disease in terms of humors and complexions fell flat, while Jack Brimblecombe's fervent appeals to divine providence seemed equally unconvincing.

Cary, ever the pragmatist, had given up hope, resorting to jests to mask his despair, while Yeo, the stoic sailor, clung to his conviction that the fate of the crew was predetermined. Drew, the seasoned master, remained resolute in his duty to navigate the vessel, but his words offered little comfort to the stricken men.

Amyas, growing increasingly frustrated, seized the initiative, dismissing the doctor's scientific theories and Jack's theological musings. "We are not here to speculate on the whims of the divine," he declared, his voice rising above the din of dissent. "We are here to find answers, and I propose that we seek them in the simplest of explanations." He pointed out the disparity in the men's experiences, noting that those who had slept on the main deck, exposed to the mist for a longer period, were the first to fall ill. "We must keep our heads above the fog, and trust in nothing but our own wits," he urged, his words echoing the sentiments of the settlers in America, who would later come to understand the simple laws governing these epidemics.

Yeo, inspired by Amyas' words, rose to his feet, recalling the wisdom of the Spaniards, who had long observed that calentures typically afflicted those dwelling in low-lying areas, rarely ascending more than a few hundred feet above sea level. "Let us ascend those few hundred feet," Amyas suggested, his voice filled with conviction. The men looked at each other, their eyes searching for reassurance, as the fog swirled around them, its secrets hidden in the veil of uncertainty.
