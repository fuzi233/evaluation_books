As I entered the dimly lit study, the scent of old books wafted through the air, mingling with the faint aroma of tea, which my mistress had left brewing on the hearth. The reading-lamp cast a warm glow on the polished wood of the table, illuminating the lines etched on the pages of her book. Her eyes remained fixed on the text, her expression unreadable behind the veil of the shade.

"Sergeant," she said, her voice low and even, "is there anything that requires our immediate attention?"

"Most certainly, my lady," replied the officer, his voice firm but polite. "I need to know if anyone in this household intends to depart before I can proceed with my inquiry."

My mistress set her book aside, her movements deliberate and controlled. "Miss Verinder has decided to visit her aunt at Frizinghall. She plans to leave at the earliest opportunity tomorrow morning."

I felt a pang in my chest, like the gentle pressure of a rolling pin on dough. The Sergeant's gaze met mine, and I sensed a weight settle upon me. I took a step forward, then hesitated, my voice caught in my throat.

"May I inquire, my lady," the Sergeant asked, his tone measured, "when exactly did Miss Verinder inform you of her decision?"

"About an hour ago," my mistress replied, her voice steady as a well-brewed cup of tea.

The Sergeant's eyes narrowed slightly as he turned to me. Time seemed to slow, and I felt my heart pounding like a pestle in a mortar. I recalled the countless times Miss Rachel had played in the kitchen, her laughter and giggles mingling with the aromas of roasting meats and baking bread.

"I have no authority to dictate Miss Verinder's actions," the Sergeant said, "but I would greatly appreciate it if you could persuade her to delay her departure until later in the day. I must attend to some matters at Frizinghall, and I would like to speak with her before she leaves."

My mistress nodded, her eyes never leaving the Sergeant's face. "I will instruct the coachman to delay the carriage until two o'clock. Is there anything else, Sergeant?"

"Only one thing, my lady," he replied, his voice low and serious. "If Miss Verinder is surprised by this change, please do not reveal that I was the one who requested it."
