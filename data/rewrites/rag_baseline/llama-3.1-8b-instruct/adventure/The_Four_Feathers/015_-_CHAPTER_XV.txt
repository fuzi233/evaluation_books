As I sat beside Ethne, listening to Willoughby's hesitant recounting of the events in Suakin, the warm breeze rustled the palm fronds outside, carrying with it the sweet scent of ripening dates. It was a smell that transported me to the distant markets of the Soudan, where the vibrant colors of spices and fabrics mingled with the cacophony of merchants' calls. Willoughby's words, however, brought me back to the dimly lit verandah of the palace, where he had first met Harry Feversham, a man whose presence seemed to stir something deep within Ethne.

"I am Deputy-Governor of Suakin," he began, his voice measured and deliberate, like the strokes of a skilled cook preparing a traditional dish. "My chief was on leave in May, and I was left to navigate the sweltering heat of the town. It's a place that clings to your skin like a damp cloth, making it impossible to sleep under the oppressive brine-heavy air. I recall sitting on the verandah, watching the distillation works shimmer in the darkness, when a servant announced the arrival of a mysterious visitor – none other than Feversham himself. The lamp cast eerie shadows on his face, but I didn't recognize him until he drew near."

Ethne's curiosity got the better of her, and she interrupted Willoughby's tale. "How did he look?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Willoughby furrowed his brow, his eyes squinting in concentration. "To be honest, I'm not quite sure. A bit worn, perhaps, from his time in the Soudan – a trifle overwrought, like a well-used spice blend that's lost its flavor."

Ethne's expression turned wistful, her eyes clouding over like the morning mist that rises from the Nile. She had waited five long years for news of Harry, and now that it had finally arrived, she craved every detail – the shape of his features, the tone of his voice, the way he moved. But Willoughby's account was sparse, like a simple broth lacking the richness of spices.

"I beg your pardon," she said, her voice tinged with disappointment. "Please continue."
