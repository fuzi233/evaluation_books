As they gathered beneath the shade of the Higuero tree, Amyas' words hung heavy with concern. "Can anyone truly claim to follow me?" he asked, his voice laced with a hint of frustration. "Have we not been faithful companions for so long, and yet you doubt my judgment now?"

The yellow fever had taken hold of several of his men, and Amyas knew he couldn't afford to succumb to its grasp. Not while his crew was struggling to stay afloat. He called for a gathering of the minds, a council to discuss their predicament. The men were on the brink of panic, ready to rebel against their captain's leadership. Amyas reassured them that their fate wouldn't be sealed by his demise, and perhaps, not even by his presence. The doctor spoke of humors and complexions, Jack Brimblecombe invoked the divine will, while Cary's words dripped with desperation. Yeo, ever the stoic, quoted scripture to justify their fate, and Drew, the seasoned sailor, reminded everyone that his duty lay in navigating the ship, not curing the illness.

Amyas' patience wore thin as he listened to their explanations. "I'll have none of your theories, doctor!" he exclaimed, his fingers raking through his hair in exasperation. "Can you truly change a man's nature or alter his constitution? Can the Ethiopian change his skin, or the leopard its spots? I seek the truth behind this affliction, and you offer only speculation." He turned to Brimblecombe, his tone sharp. "God's visitation, you say? I think it more likely the devil's handiwork. We serve a higher purpose, and I doubt our Lord would hinder us in our duty. So, let us cast aside the devil's influence and press on."

Cary's jests fell flat, and Yeo's fatalism seemed hollow in the face of uncertainty. Drew, ever practical, suggested they navigate the ship out of the poisoned air, but Amyas knew it was a futile endeavor.
