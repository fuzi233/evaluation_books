As the evening mist descended upon Cloisterham, casting a mystical veil over the Cathedral Close, Mr. Sapsea strolled leisurely along the winding paths, lost in thought. His eyes wandered over the churchyard, where the fading light danced across the weathered headstones, as if beckoning him to linger. The Mayor's thoughts were a gentle hum, a soothing melody that accompanied his footsteps. He liked to believe that the townspeople saw him as a benevolent guardian, a patron who had bestowed a prize upon the worthy Mrs. Sapsea. His inscription, a testament to his generosity, seemed to glow with a quiet pride.

As he turned a corner, Mr. Sapsea caught sight of the Dean, engaged in conversation with the Verger and Mr. Jasper. The Mayor's steps quickened, and he doffed his hat in a low bow, his expression transformed by the sudden reverence. The Dean, noticing his arrival, smiled warmly and nodded in greeting.

"We're being immortalized, Mr. Jasper," he said, his eyes twinkling with amusement. "A book about us, written by you, no doubt." The Verger, Mr. Tope, chuckled at the prospect, while Mr. Sapsea looked puzzled, his brow furrowed in concentration.

"I'm afraid I have no intention of writing such a book, sir," Jasper replied, his voice smooth as the notes of a well-played piano. "It's merely a passing fancy. And even for that whim, Mr. Sapsea here is more responsible than I."

The Dean's eyes sparkled with interest as he turned to the Mayor. "Ah, Mr. Mayor, how is that? How are you accountable for Mr. Jasper's whims?"

Mr. Sapsea hesitated, his gaze drifting about the group before settling on the Dean. "I'm not quite sure, sir, to what you refer," he said, his voice measured. As he spoke, he reached out a hand to touch the intricate carvings on the nearby church railings, his fingers tracing the patterns with a gentle touch.

"Durdles," the Dean murmured, his voice barely audible over the rustling of leaves. "Durdles, indeed."
