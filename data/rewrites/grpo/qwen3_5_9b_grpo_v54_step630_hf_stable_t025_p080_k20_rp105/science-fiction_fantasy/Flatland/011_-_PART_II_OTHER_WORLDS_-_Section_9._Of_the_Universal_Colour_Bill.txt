While the intellectual arts crumbled around them, a new, seductive simplicity took root. The Art of Sight Recognition, once the cornerstone of Flatland's hierarchy, became obsolete. Geometry, Statics, and Kinetics were dismissed as archaic burdens, their universities falling into disrepair. Even the inferior Art of Feeling, taught in elementary schools, was abandoned. With these disciplines gone, the Isosceles classes—formerly disciplined by the necessity of education and the tribute they paid to the state—swelled in number and insolence. Freed from the old constraints that had tempered their brutality, they grew bold and numerous.

Year by year, the Soldiers and Artisans, now elevated to parity with the highest Polygons, declared that the distinction between their station and the elite was an illusion. They argued that the complexities of life could be solved not through rigorous calculation, but through the simple, intuitive process of Colour Recognition. Dissatisfied with the slow decline of Sight Recognition, they demanded the legal prohibition of all "monopolizing and aristocratic Arts." They called for the abolition of endowments dedicated to Mathematics and Feeling, insisting that since Colour—a second nature—had rendered class distinctions unnecessary, the Law must follow suit. All individuals, they proclaimed, were entitled to absolute equality.

Seeing the higher orders waver, the revolutionaries pressed further. They demanded that every citizen, including the Priests and the Women, submit to the universal law of Colour by being painted. When objections arose that Priests and Women lacked the sides necessary for such decoration, the revolutionaries countered with a twisted logic: Nature and Expediency dictated that the front half of every human—the side bearing the eye and mouth—must be distinguished from the hind half. Thus, a bill was drafted for a general assembly proposing that every Woman be painted red on the front and green on the back. The Priests were to receive the same treatment: red on the semicircle containing their face, green on the rear.

This proposal was born not of the Isosceles, whose minds were too degraded to conceive such statecraft, but of a cunning Irregular Circle. Spared from destruction in childhood due to a foolish indulgence, this Irregular plotted to bring desolation upon his country. His scheme relied on a brilliant, deceptive geometry. By assigning Women the same dual-colour palette as the Priests, the revolutionaries ensured that, when viewed from the side, a Woman would appear indistinguishable from a Priest. To the casual observer, both would present a straight line, half red and half green.

Consider the visual deception: A Woman, painted red on her front and green on her back, presents a stark diagonal division to the side viewer. Now consider a Priest, whose mouth sits at the apex of his red semicircle, with the green occupying his rear. If one looks directly along the Priest's dividing diameter, the silhouette is also a straight line, split exactly between red and green. While the Priest's form might be slightly shorter and the shading more rapid, the immediate impression of identical colouring would cause the observer to ignore subtle differences. In an age where Sight Recognition was already decaying, and as Women quickly learned to soften their edges to mimic the Circles, the confusion became inevitable. The distinction between the sacred Priest and the common Woman would vanish.

The allure of this prospect for the Female Sex was undeniable. They anticipated the chaos with delight. At home, they could eavesdrop on political secrets meant for men and issue commands in the name of a priestly Circle. Outdoors, the striking red-and-green combination would lead the populace into endless mistakes, granting Women the deference usually reserved for the highest class. The scandal that might befall the Circular Class, or the subversion of the Constitution, were concerns the Women ignored entirely. Even within the households of the Circles, the Women rallied behind the Universal Colour Bill.

The second objective of this diabolical plan was the gradual demoralization of the Circles themselves. Until now, the Nobility had maintained their pristine intellectual clarity, having grown up in colourless homes where the Sacred Art of Sight Recognition was cultivated. But the Irregular sought to stain this purity. By forcing the Hierarchy to submit to the pollution of Colour, he aimed to destroy their domestic environment. Once subjected to chromatic taint, every parental and childish Circle would begin to demoralize one another. The infant Circle, once faced with the problem of distinguishing Father from Mother amidst the confusing hues, would find its logical exercises corrupted by maternal imposture. The intellectual lustre of the Priestly Order would inevitably wane, paving the way for the total destruction of the Aristocratic Legislature and the final subversion of the privileged classes.
