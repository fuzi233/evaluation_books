The fourth cycle of slumber passed, and when the veil lifted, I truly opened my eyes. Though the chamber was swallowed by absolute darkness, a sudden, lucid certainty flooded my mind: I lay within the House of Death, and every fleeting moment since my arrival had been nothing but a dream. Now, at last, I was awake. "Finally!" I whispered to the beating rhythm of my own heart, and it surged with a joy so pure it felt like flight. I turned my gaze, and there stood Lona by my side, waiting. I had not lost her—not truly, not for long. My grief had been a shadow cast by fear, not reality.

"It was dark," I observed, though the shadows held no power over her. "SHE" was not dark. Her eyes burned with the same radiant luminescence as the Mother's, a divine fire issuing from her countenance and spilling outward to transform her tattered death-robe. It was white as fresh snow, glowing with an inner light tenfold brighter than before, awakened by the resurrection of her spirit. A girl had fallen asleep; a woman, fully ripened with the essential beauty of life, had woken. I wrapped her in my arms, and in that embrace, I confirmed that I lived.

"I woke first," she said, her voice a wonder-struck murmur.

"You did, my love, and you woke me," I replied.

"I simply watched over you," she answered softly.

Through the gloom, a candle drifted forward, carrying Adam, Eve, and Mara. They greeted us with a serene, familiar good morning, accustomed as they were to these resurrections.

"I trust you found the darkness pleasant?" the Mother asked gently.

"Not entirely," I confessed. "But emerging from it feels like heaven."

"It has barely begun," she corrected, her smile knowing. "You are hardly awake yet."

"He is already clothed in Death," Adam noted, his tone warm. "Which is the radiant garment of Life itself."

He stepped forward to embrace Lona, placing an arm around me, his gaze shifting between the princess and the leopardess with a look of deep inquiry before patting the great feline's head. "I believe we shall see you both again soon," he said, his eyes moving from Lona back to me.

"Do we have to die again?" I asked, the ancient terror flickering beneath the surface.

"No," Adam answered, his smile mirroring the Mother's benevolence. "You have died *into* life, and you will never die again. Your only duty now is to remain dead to the old world. Once you have undergone this kind of death, all further dying is finished. From here on, you must live, and you must live with every ounce of your blessed strength. The more you live, the stronger you become."

"But what if I tire?" I wondered, feeling the weight of perpetual existence. "What if I stop trying to live with all my might?"

"It requires only the will," the Mother interjected, her presence filling the room. "Strength is always there. Pure life knows no weariness. THE Life continually regenerates yours. Those who refuse to die end up dying constantly, sinking deeper into the illusion until they vanish. Here, everything moves upward, fueled by love and gladness."

She paused, her expression softening into a look of profound kinship, as if to say, *We are mother and son; between us, parting is impossible.*

Mara leaned forward, kissing me on the forehead with a buoyant cheer. "I told you, brother, that all would be well! When you need comfort next, just say, 'Whatever will be well, is already well.'" She let out a small, knowing sigh—I suspected she meant, *'But they won't believe you!'* Then, with a grin echoing her mother's, she concluded, "--You know me now!"

"I know you!" I declared, the words rising from the depths of my soul. "You are the voice crying in the wilderness before John the Baptist appeared! You are the shepherd who drives the wolves from the wandering sheep before the shadows lengthen and night falls!"

"My work will eventually conclude," she mused, "and then I shall share the joy of the Great Shepherd who sent me."

"All night long, the morning draws near," Adam proclaimed.

"What is that sound of flapping wings?" I asked, straining to hear.

"The Shadow hovers," Adam replied, his voice dropping to a serious register. "There is someone here whom he claims as his own. But once she belonged to us, she can never return to him."

I turned to kiss my father and mother before we departed. Their couches were empty, save for the "Little Ones"—their children—who had boldly claimed their hospitality with love. For a split second, the terrifying vision of desolation from my dream returned, chilling me. I looked away.

"What troubles you, my heart?" Lona asked, sensing my shift.

"Their empty spaces frighten me," I admitted.

"They have been gone for hours," Adam explained calmly. "They kissed you before leaving and whispered, 'Come soon.'"

"And I couldn't feel or hear them!" I murmured, a pang of loss striking me.

"How could you? You're trapped in your dreary old house, thinking the terrible place has reclaimed you! Go and find them." He turned to Lona, addressing her gently. "Your parents, my child, must come to find you."

Our departure was imminent. Lona approached the couch where the Mother who had slain her lay, pressing a tender kiss to her lips, then collapsing into her father's arms.

"That kiss will pull her home, my Lona!" Adam assured her.

"Who were her parents?" Lona asked, confused by the duality.

"My father," Adam answered simply, "is her father as well."

She turned, linking her hand with mine.

I knelt, offering humble thanks to the three for guiding me through the final death. Lona knelt beside me, and together they breathed upon us, sealing our transition.

"Hear! I hear the Sun!" Adam announced.

I listened. It approached with the thunderous rush of ten thousand leagues of distant wings, the roaring clamor of a molten world billions of miles away. Its arrival was a crescendo of a hundred harmonies, vibrating through the air.

The three exchanged glances, their smiles blooming like a three-petaled flower floating heavenward—a family's morning thanksgiving. That smile spread from their mouths over their faces, shining through their garments. Before I could speak, Adam and Eve stood transformed before me as the Angels of Resurrection, and Mara stood beside them as the Magdalene at the tomb. Adam's countenance crackled with lightning-like brilliance, while Eve held a napkin that scattered shards of splendor across the room.

A wind began to moan in pulsing gusts.

"You hear his wings now!" Adam said, clarifying he spoke not of the morning's birds.

"It is the Great Shadow stirring to leave," he continued. "Poor creature, he carries himself within his own hollow chest and cannot rest."

"But is there not something deeper within him?" I questioned.

"A shadow has no substance unless there is a light behind it," Adam answered wisely.

Listening intently, he suddenly broke into a joyful smile. "Hark to the Golden Cock! Silent and motionless for millions of years, he has perched upon the clockwork of the universe. Now, at last, he spreads his wings! Soon he will begin to crow, and at intervals, men will hear him until the Dawn of Eternal Day breaks."

I strained my ears. In the heart of an aeonic silence, I heard the clear, jubilant outcry of the golden throat. It hurled defiance at death and the dark, singing a song of infinite hope and coming calm. It was the "expectation of creation" finally finding a voice; the cry of chaos transforming into a kingdom.

Then, a mighty flapping echoed through the room.

"The Black Bat is flown!" Mara exclaimed.

"Amen, Golden Cock, Bird of God!" Adam cried, and the words resonated through the House of Silence, ascending into the airy realms above.

At the sound of his AMEN, like silver-winged doves rising from broken pottery, the Little Ones scrambled to their knees beside their beds, shouting aloud, "Crow! Crow again, Golden Cock!" As if they had both seen and heard him in their dreams.

Each child then turned to look at the sleeping figure on their cot, gazing with love-filled eyes, kissing the silent companion of the night, before springing from the bed. The Little Ones who had lain beside my father and mother stared blankly and sadly at their empty spaces for a moment, then slowly slid to the floor. There, they collapsed into each other's arms, as if, seeing one another's eyes, they realized simultaneously that they were alive and awake. Suddenly spotting Lona, they ran toward her, radiant with bliss, to embrace her. Odu, catching sight of the leopardess standing at the princess's feet, bounded over, threw an arm around the great sleeping head, and began to fondle and kiss it.

"Wake up, wake up, darling!" he cried. "It's time!"

The leopardess remained unmoving.

"She has slept herself cold!" Odu said to Mara, his face twisted in appealing consternation.

"She is waiting for the Princess to wake, my child," Mara said gently.
