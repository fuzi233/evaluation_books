As they strolled through the quiet streets, Margaret's father, Mr. Hale, turned to her with a hint of concern etched on his face. "Margaret, my dear, we must pay a visit to Mrs. Thornton today. Your mother is not feeling quite herself, and I fear she may need some extra care."

Margaret's heart skipped a beat at the sight of her father's worried expression. She had been trying to convince him of her mother's illness for days, but he had been too caught up in his own worries to listen. "Papa, I've been thinking," she said gently, "perhaps we should seek out a doctor. I'm sure Mother would appreciate it."

Mr. Hale's eyes clouded over, and he looked away, his voice heavy with foreboding. "Do you think she has some hidden condition, Margaret? Has Dixon mentioned anything to you?" Margaret's eyes widened in alarm as she realized her father's fears were taking hold. "No, papa, I just think she's not feeling well, that's all. And Dixon can be a bit dramatic sometimes. But I'm certain Mother will be fine once she sees a doctor."

As they walked, Margaret couldn't help but feel a sense of unease. She had always known that her family's move to Milton had been a difficult one, but she had never imagined it would take such a toll on her mother's health. Mr. Hale's anxiety was palpable, and Margaret knew she had to reassure him. "We'll find a good doctor, papa, I promise. And maybe Mrs. Thornton can recommend someone. After all, she's a kind and wise woman."

As they turned a corner, Margaret's gaze fell upon a small, unassuming house tucked away on Marlborough Street. It was nothing like the grand estate she had imagined Mrs. Thornton would own. "I thought Mr. Thornton lived in a grand house, papa," she said, trying to hide her disappointment. Mr. Hale's eyes crinkled at the corners as he smiled wryly. "Ah, perhaps he's been economizing, my dear. But I'm sure Mrs. Thornton will be happy to see us, regardless of her home's size."
