As Lady Violet Lebas poured steaming tea into delicate china cups, the warm scent wafted through the library, mingling with the faint tang of old books and the soft murmur of rain outside. It was a moment suspended outside the relentless passage of time, a fleeting glimpse of tranquility amidst the turmoil that had long since unsettled her own days and nights. Her thoughts, however, remained elsewhere, with the memories of the unspeakable horror that had left an indelible mark on her soul, a darkness that still lingered like a shadow, waiting to reclaim her.

She had hesitated for months, unsure if she could bring herself to recount the tale, to relive the terror that had been her companion for so long. But it was not just her own sake that drove her to put words to paper – it was the insistent, gentle pressure of a friend's desire to understand, to grasp the fragments of a story that had haunted her for years.

As she sat, her gaze drifting back to the stack of letters from Mr. Andrew Lang, she found herself pondering the Baron Vordenburg, a man consumed by the obscure lore of vampirism. His eyes, behind thick spectacles, had gleamed with an intensity that bordered on the fanatical, as he delved into the dusty tomes that lined his small, cluttered study in Gratz. He had spent years accumulating knowledge, pouring over texts like "Magia Posthuma," "Phlegon de Mirabilibus," and "Augustinus de cura pro Mortuis," seeking to unravel the enigma of the undead.

Her father had spoken of his fascination with the subject, of the way the Baron had extracted a system of principles from the scattered accounts of vampire lore. The Baron's research had revealed that the creatures presented no discernible signs of decay, their bodies appearing healthy and vital even in death. Their existence, however, was a paradox, sustained by daily returns to the grave, where they would slumber, their "horrible lust for living blood" rekindling with each dawn.

As Lady Violet's thoughts meandered, she recalled the Baron's words, spoken around the dinner table, of the vampire's propensity for obsessive fixation on specific individuals. It was a passion that bordered on love, a slow-burning fire that would drive them to employ every stratagem to claim their chosen prey.
