The scent of fresh bread wafted through the air as I sat on a low stool before the hearth, watching the warm flames dance across the stone floor. It was a chilly spring morning in the small village of Llanfair, where the villagers were already busy preparing for the upcoming summer solstice celebrations. My nurse, Mrs. Fursey, sat nearby, her hands moving deftly as she stitched a delicate pattern onto a linen cloth. Her bony fingers seemed to move with a life of their own, the sound of her needle punctuating the silence with a soothing rhythm.

As I gazed into the fire, I pondered the tales Mrs. Fursey had told me about my birth. She'd spoken of the misfortunes that had befallen my family, of the ruined mines and the news that had reached our home just hours before I arrived. I remembered her words, her voice laced with a mixture of sadness and superstition. According to her, my early arrival had been a bad omen, a sign that my life would be marked by hardship and disappointment.

But I couldn't help feeling that there was more to the story. Why had my mother taken me in if she hadn't wanted me? The question hung in the air like the sweet scent of baking bread, taunting me with its simplicity. Mrs. Fursey's needle paused mid-stitch, her eyes narrowing as she turned to face me.

"Who's took you in, child?" she asked, her voice firm but gentle.

"Mother," I replied, my voice steady. "If she didn't want me, why did she take me in?"

The fire crackled and spat, the flames casting flickering shadows on the walls as Mrs. Fursey's expression softened. She set her needle aside and leaned forward, her eyes locking onto mine.

"Ah, child, you're a clever one, aren't you?" she said, a hint of a smile playing on her lips. "Sometimes, life isn't as simple as luck or misfortune. Sometimes, it's about love and choice."

As she spoke, the scent of bread wafted through the air once more, mingling with the promise of new beginnings and the mysteries that lay ahead.
