As I entered the dimly lit room, I spotted Betteredge hunched over a worn leather book on his knee, its yellowed pages crackling softly as he turned them. I pointed to the familiar title, "Robinson Crusoe," and asked if he'd had a visit from our guest, Franklin Blake. Betteredge's eyes widened, and he exclaimed, "By the lord above, Mr. Franklin!" The old butler's face lit up with a look of expectation, as if he anticipated Franklin's arrival as if it were a culinary delight waiting to be savored.

With a little help, Betteredge struggled to his feet, surveying the space between me and the book with a puzzled expression. He seemed to be deciding which of us had caused the greater stir, but his gaze eventually settled on the book. He held it aloft, admiring the worn cover, and I couldn't help but notice the way the fading light danced across its surface.

As he regained his composure, Betteredge exclaimed, "Here's the passage, Mr. Franklin! As I live by bread, sir, here's the very words I was reading when you arrived! It's page one hundred and fifty-six, and it says: 'I stood like one thunderstruck, or as if I had seen an apparition.' If that isn't a sign of your impending arrival, I'm not sure what is!" He slammed the book shut, freeing one hand to grasp mine in a warm handshake.

I had expected a barrage of questions from the old butler, but instead, his thoughts turned to hospitality. "Welcome, Mr. Franklin, welcome to our humble abode! I'll cook your dinner, and the gardener's wife will prepare your bed. If we still have that fine Latour claret in the cellar, I'll pour you a glass." His words were laced with a deep affection, a warmth that seemed to fill the room and chase away the shadows.

However, I found myself hesitant to accept his offer. This house, once filled with laughter and life, now felt heavy with the weight of memories. Could I truly sit at the table, or rest in the bed, knowing what had transpired in London? My sense of propriety wouldn't allow it. I took Betteredge by the arm, and we stepped out into the garden, the scent of blooming flowers and fresh earth enveloping us.

We walked in silence for a moment, the only sound the rustle of leaves beneath our feet
