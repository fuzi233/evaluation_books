As Deronda stepped into his Park Lane lodgings, a sense of homecoming washed over him. However, it was quickly tempered by the realization that Hans Meyrick had taken up residence in his chambers. Deronda had anticipated as much, yet the sight of his friend's belongings scattered about still caught him off guard. The space, once a sanctuary of quiet contemplation, now resembled an artist's studio, with papers and sketches strewn about. Two chests from Rome stood open, their contents spilling onto the floor, as if Hans had unloaded his entire existence into this new space.

Deronda wandered over to the window, where he found the lower half shrouded in a dark baize, casting an air of mystery over the room. The sunlight streaming through the upper panes highlighted the dust motes dancing in the air, imbuing the scene with a sense of enchantment. Hans, seated amidst the chaos, looked up from his sketchbook, his eyes shining with excitement.

"I knew you'd be delighted by my collection," Hans exclaimed, as he closed his book and rose from his chair. "I've brought back treasures from Rome, and I thought you'd appreciate them." His hair, longer than Deronda remembered, framed his face, which seemed more angular and whimsical than ever. His high-pitched voice rose and fell as he spoke, a trait that Deronda had grown accustomed to over the years.

Their friendship, forged during their Cambridge days, had only deepened with time. Despite the distance between them, they had maintained a warm correspondence, punctuated by visits and shared adventures. Deronda had always regarded Hans as a younger brother, someone whose trustworthiness he could rely on, even as he often teased him good-naturedly about his eccentricities.

As Hans led Deronda on a tour of his makeshift studio, the younger man's enthusiasm proved infectious. The two friends walked among the scattered papers and artifacts, discussing everything from art to philosophy, their conversation flowing effortlessly. Deronda couldn't help but feel a sense of wonder at the way Hans's imagination seemed to have burst forth, unfettered by conventional boundaries.

"I've found a place in Chelsea, just a short walk from my family's home," Hans explained, his eyes sparkling. "It's a bit of a fixer-upper, but I'll have it shipshape in no time. And then, oh, the masterpieces I'll create! The seeds of greatness are stirring within me, Deronda – I can feel it."
