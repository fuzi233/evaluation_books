In the old stone church at Glenalla, Ethne settled into the worn pew beside the aisle, the soft creak of the wooden bench a gentle accompaniment to the warm summer air drifting through the open door. Feversham stood tall beside her, the afternoon sunlight casting a warm glow across the faces of the congregation, now empty save for the two of them. As he took his place beside her, the scent of fresh bread wafted from the nearby bakery, mingling with the sweet fragrance of blooming wildflowers outside.

Ethne's hands folded together in her lap, her voice barely above a whisper as she began to speak. "I'm glad you remembered our drive and the words we shared," she said, her gaze fixed on some point beyond the pews. "Though I must confess, I've spent many sleepless nights pondering the path ahead." Her words were laced with a quiet determination, each phrase carefully chosen to convey the weight of her emotions. She had rehearsed this speech countless times, seeking the right balance between honesty and restraint.

As she spoke, the scent of freshly baked scones wafted from the nearby village bakery, mingling with the heady aroma of ripening fruits from the orchard outside. Ethne's words wove a spell of comfort and understanding, one that enveloped Feversham like a warm blanket on a winter's night. Though the pain of their impending parting still lingered, her words offered a glimmer of hope – a reminder that their love had endured despite the years of separation.

In the silence that followed, the sound of a nearby baker kneading dough echoed through the empty church, a soothing rhythm that seemed to match the beating of Ethne's heart. Feversham's thoughts drifted back to the days of his captivity, the memories of loneliness and hardship now tempered by the knowledge that their love remained strong. Ethne's words had awakened something deep within him, a sense of purpose that had driven him through the darkest of times. As he stood beside her, the gentle hum of the bees in the nearby garden seemed to swell, a reminder that life went on, even in the face of adversity.
