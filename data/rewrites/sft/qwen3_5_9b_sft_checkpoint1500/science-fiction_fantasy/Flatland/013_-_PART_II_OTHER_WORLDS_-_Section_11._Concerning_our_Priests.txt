Now, having briefly sketched the peculiar mechanics of life within this two-dimensional realm—omitting the intricacies of locomotion, construction, meteorology, and botany solely to honor your limited reading time—I turn to the true heart of this narrative. My purpose here is not to describe the mundane, but to chronicle the grand initiation into the mysteries of the Third Dimension, *Space*. All prior observations serve but as a prologue to this revelation.

Before unveiling the secrets of the void, however, I must address the twin pillars upon which Flatland rests: the architects of conduct and the shapers of fate. You may well ask what I refer to when I speak of "Priests." In your world, a priest is often a man of spiritual counsel alone. Here, the Circle is far more. He is the omnipotent administrator of every branch of knowledge and governance. From trade and commerce to generalship, architecture, engineering, education, statesmanship, legislation, morality, and theology, the Circle directs all affairs. He performs no labor himself, yet he is the invisible cause behind every deed worthy of accomplishment performed by others.

To the uneducated eye, every inhabitant of this realm appears perfectly round, a flawless Circle. Yet, the enlightened know the terrible truth: no Circle truly exists. Every individual is, in reality, a polygon with a vast number of infinitesimal sides. As the count of sides swells, the figure approaches the ideal of a Circle. A being with three or four hundred sides possesses edges so minute that the finest touch cannot discern their angular nature. Indeed, to *feel* a corner would be considered the height of sacrilege in high society, where recognition by touch is forbidden to the elite. This cultural taboo allows the upper circles to maintain the illusion of perfection, hiding the jagged reality of their perimeters. By convention, courtesy dictates that the current Supreme Circle possesses ten thousand sides.

But how does one ascend to such heights? In Flatland, nature imposes a cruel limit: a creature born with $n$ sides can only develop into a polygon with $n+1$ sides in the next generation. If this law held sway, nobility would be a matter of simple arithmetic lineage. However, the divine laws governing circular evolution operate through two opposing decrees. First, as a lineage rises in the hierarchy, its rate of development accelerates exponentially. Second, fertility diminishes in inverse proportion. Thus, a parent with four or five hundred sides rarely produces offspring, and if they do, it is usually solitary. Conversely, the child of a five-hundred-sided parent may emerge with five hundred fifty, or even six hundred sides.

Furthermore, art intervenes to hasten this cosmic ascent. Our physicians have mastered the science of fracturing the tender, minuscule sides of an infant polygon from the higher classes. Through a perilous realignment of the frame, a child might skip hundreds of generations, doubling the nobility of their bloodline in a single, miraculous operation. Though successful outcomes are vanishingly rare—one survivor in ten—the ambition of parents hovering near the threshold of the Circle class drives them to sacrifice their firstborn to the Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium within the first month of life.

One year stands as the arbiter of destiny. For twelve months, the child remains a prisoner of hope and fear, likely joining the grim rows of the Neo-Therapeutic Cemetery. But on those rare, luminous occasions when a procession returns the child to weeping, exultant parents, the miracle has occurred. No longer a polygon, the child is hailed as a Circle, by courtesy if not by absolute geometric truth. And it is from this terrifying gamble, where countless lives are lost on the altar of status, that the ruling priesthood draws its power.
