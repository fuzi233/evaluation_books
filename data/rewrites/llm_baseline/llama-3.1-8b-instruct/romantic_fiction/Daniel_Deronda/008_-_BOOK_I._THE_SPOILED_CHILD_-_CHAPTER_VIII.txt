As the frost-kissed landscape unfolded before him, Rex gazed listlessly out the window, his eyes tracing the faint trails of sunlight filtering through the bare branches of the shrubs. His fragile smile, a mere whisper of its former self, seemed to cling to his pale lips like a fleeting promise. Anna, her heart aching with longing, watched him from her seat beside him, her fingers instinctively reaching out to touch the hand that lay limp in her own.

His mother entered the room, her footsteps soft on the carpet, and Rex's gaze turned to meet hers. A faint tremor crossed his face as he spoke, his voice barely above a whisper. "What a plague I am." The words hung in the air, heavy with unspoken emotions, as his mother's eyes searched his, her expression a mixture of sorrow and concern.

Anna's thoughts wandered back to the events that had brought them to this place – the tumultuous emotions that had ravaged her brother's spirit, leaving him a shadow of his former self. She couldn't help but wonder if Gwendolen's influence had been the catalyst for this transformation, and her mind rebelled against the thought of her aunt's accusations, of Gwendolen's supposed heart of stone. But as she glanced at her brother's wan face, her doubts faltered, and she knew that she couldn't bear to harbor any ill will towards the girl who had captured his heart.

In the silence that followed, Anna's eyes drifted to the window, where the winter landscape stretched out like an endless canvas of frost and shadow. Beyond the garden hedge, the road wound its way through the countryside, a reminder of the world outside their little bubble of sorrow. A wagon creaked by, its wooden wheels echoing through the stillness, a poignant reminder that life continued, untroubled by the turmoil within their walls.

As the morning wore on, Rex's mother attempted to coax him back to his usual self, but he remained withdrawn, lost in a world that seemed both familiar and yet, utterly alien. Anna sat beside him, her presence a gentle comfort, as they both struggled to find their footing in this strange, new landscape, where the boundaries between joy and sorrow seemed to blur and merge.
