As the Bishop of Cartagena settled into his chair, the soft lapping of the waves against the hull of the galleon, The City of the True Cross, created a soothing melody that harmonized with the rustling of the palm fronds swaying in the tropical breeze. The golden light of the setting sun danced across the horizon, casting a warm glow upon the assembly gathered around the bishop. Before him, a delicate Indian girl, adorned with vibrant scarves and glittering gold chains, gently waved a fan made of iridescent feathers, warding off the swarm of flies that hovered above the table laden with exotic fruits – oranges, limes, guavas, and pineapples.

As the bishop's eyes wandered out to the sea, he couldn't help but ponder the economic and cultural implications of the recent events that had ravaged his beloved cathedral town. The destruction wrought by Sir Francis Drake's forces had left a trail of devastation in its wake, and the bishop's words, though laced with poetic flair, betrayed a deep sense of unease. "We have been great sufferers, senors," he quoted from scripture, his voice tinged with a hint of irony. "Great sufferers, truly; but there shall be a remnant, a remnant like the shaking of the olive tree and the gleaning grapes when the vintage is done."

The bishop's words hung in the air, punctuated by the soft clinking of glasses and the occasional snicker from the assembled company. Don Pedro, colonel of the soldiers on board, leaned forward, his eyes locked onto the bishop's face. "It is a great privilege," he said, "to have comfort so graciously administered at once by a churchman and a scholar." The intendant, Don Alverez, nodded in agreement, while Don Paul, captain of mariners, smiled wistfully, lost in thought.

The bishop's gaze drifted back to the sea, where the stars were beginning to twinkle like diamonds scattered across the velvet expanse. He felt a sense of longing wash over him, a yearning for a world untouched by conflict and strife. "It looks gold," he whispered, his voice barely audible over the sound of the waves. "It smells of gold, as I may say, by a poetical license. Yea, the very waves, as they ripple past us, sing of gold, gold, gold!"
