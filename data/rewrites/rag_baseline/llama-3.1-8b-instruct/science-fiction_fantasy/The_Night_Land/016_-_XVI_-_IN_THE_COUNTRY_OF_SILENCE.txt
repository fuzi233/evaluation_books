Silence enveloped the land like a shroud, punctuated only by the faint rustle of dry leaves beneath my feet. I walked on, the weight of the world's desolation bearing down upon me. My home, a distant memory, seemed to recede further with each step, its safety and comfort lost to the vast expanse of the unknown. Ahead, the outline of a pyramid loomed, its peak shrouded in darkness. The air grew colder, heavy with the scent of decay and forgotten dreams.

As I navigated the barren terrain, a flicker of light danced before me – a fire-hole, casting eerie shadows on the ground. Something emerged from the hollow, its massive form coalescing into a grotesque figure. The creature's hands, twisted and claw-like, reached out towards me, its face a twisted parody of humanity. My heart sank, weighed down by the crushing despair that had become my constant companion.

I hit the ground hard, my breath knocked from my lungs. The creature lunged, its monstrous arm swinging towards me. I scrambled to my feet, the Diskos, my trusty companion, still clutched in my hand. The creature's attack was swift and brutal, but I dodged its grasp, my movements fueled by desperation. The Diskos sliced through the air, its blade biting deep into the creature's side. It stumbled, its massive body crashing to the ground.

I didn't hesitate, leaping towards the Maid, my love, my guiding star in this desolate landscape. I swept her into my arms, and together we fled, leaving the fallen creature behind. The night air was alive with the whispers of the Millions, their ethereal voices a gentle breeze that rustled my hair. Their love and adoration washed over me, a balm to my weary soul.

But our respite was short-lived. More creatures emerged from the shadows, their forms indistinct, their intentions unclear. I raised the Diskos, ready to strike, but my aim was true, and they fell silent. The night was a living, breathing entity, its darkness teeming with horrors. I strode forward, the Diskos at the ready, my heart heavy with the knowledge that our time was running out.
