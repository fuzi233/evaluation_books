In the venerable halls of Clovelly Court, where tradition and heritage converged, a most singular scene unfolded. It was an era of Queen Elizabeth's reign, a time when the monarch's sagacity and wisdom guided England through tumultuous waters. As the saying went, "When Her Majesty was beset by troubles, she would often summon a stalwart gentleman from Devon to offer counsel and solace." This adage echoed in the minds of those familiar with the queen's methods, for it was said that she would not hesitate to seek the expertise of a native son should the nation's fortunes falter.

That morning, Amyas Leigh had vanished, leaving behind no hint of despair or desperation. His whereabouts remained unknown until Frank discovered his trail led to Sir Richard Grenville at Stow. Mrs. Leigh, perceptive as always, deduced that her son's actions were motivated by a desire to secure a position in the Irish army. She dispatched Frank to intercept him, urging him to reconsider his decision.

With the sun climbing higher in the sky, Frank set out on horseback, traversing the rolling hills and verdant valleys. As the terrain grew more rugged, he spotted the picturesque Clovelly Court in the distance, its slate roofs glistening in the morning dew. The squire, Mr. Cary, was known for his hospitality, and Frank hoped to find rest and refreshment beneath his roof.

Upon entering the great hall, Frank was immediately struck by the imposing figure of Amyas, seated at the long wooden table. The younger man's countenance was buried in a savory pasty, only to emerge moments later, his eyes fixed intently on Frank. Young Will Cary, seated across from Amyas, was engaged in a heated discussion, his elbows resting on the table as he spoke in a low, urgent tone.

"A plague upon it, lad!" Amyas exclaimed, beckoning Frank closer. "Come, deliver me from the clutches of this firebrand, who threatens to incinerate me unless I surrender to his every whim."

"Ah, Mr. Frank," Will Cary said, rising from his seat to greet the newcomer, "I've been expecting you. I required your counsel on several matters of great import. Please, join us, and partake in a libation to revive your spirits."

"No need for strong drink at this hour," Frank declined, his gaze drifting toward the steaming tankard.
