In the stifling heat of the Omdurman prison, a mirage of hope danced before Feversham's parched eyes. For three days, he'd wandered lost in a haze of fevered visions, while Trench tended to him with quiet dedication. By day, the older man fetched him water from the Nile, sharing his meager rations and shielding him from the scorching sun. At night, they stood watch together in the shadows of the House of Stone, their vigilance a beacon against the darkness that lurked beyond the prison walls. But on the fourth morning, Feversham's gaze cleared, and he beheld Trench's weathered face bent over him, a vision that initially blurred into the nightmare faces of his childhood. He swatted at it, his arm weak, but as his eyes adjusted to the harsh light, the reality of their surroundings asserted itself: the sun-baked soil, the skeletal trees, and the fellow prisoners, their bodies twisted in agony or sprawled lifeless on the ground.

Feversham's gaze locked onto Trench, and he grasped for the older man as if fearing he'd vanish into thin air. A faint smile crept across his lips, and he spoke in a voice that trembled with emotion, "I'm in Omdurman, in the House of Stone. This is Umm Hagar, the prison. It feels almost too good to be true." He leaned back against the wall, exhaling a deep breath, his expression a mix of relief and wonder.

Trench's expression, however, was a mask of detachment, a studied indifference that belied the depth of his concern. He'd seen men like Feversham before – men who'd cultivated a façade of nonchalance, a pose that concealed the turmoil beneath. But Trench knew Feversham better; he'd witnessed the younger man's vulnerability during the delirium, and he recognized the sincerity in his voice now. The peril of Dongola was behind them, and Feversham had finally found solace in the one person who could help him escape.

"You've been here a long while," Feversham said, his voice barely above a whisper.

Trench nodded, his eyes clouding over. "Three years."
