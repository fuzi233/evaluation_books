"Mother," I said, turning to Eve, "one couch beside Lona remains vacant. Though I feel unworthy, may I not spend this night in your chamber with my dead? Pardon my cowardice and my arrogance; take me in. I surrender myself. I am sick of my own existence and crave only the oblivion of sleep."

"The couch next to Lona is already prepared for you," she replied. "Yet something awaits completion before you rest."

"I am ready," I stated.

"How do you know you possess the capacity?" she asked, a sharp, knowing smile playing on her lips.

"Because necessity demands it," I answered.

"What is required?" She turned to Adam. "Husband, is he absolved?"

"From my heart," Adam confirmed.

"Then instruct him."

Adam stepped forward and took Mara's hand, holding it gently within his palm. "Come, child. Let us proceed to the cottage; I shall brief you there."

As we moved, a violent gust erupted, tearing across the roof with a deafening flap before subsiding into a mournful groan. Once the door of the death-chamber sealed shut behind us, Adam sat, and I stood facing him.

"You recall," he began, his voice cutting through the tension, "the dry rock marked by the scars of an ancient waterfall? After leaving your mother's house, you ascended that stone to find a sandy wasteland. Go to that rock now. From its peak, descend deep into the desert. Do not travel far before lying flat and pressing your ear to the sand. If you detect the murmur of water beneath, advance slightly and listen again. Should the sound persist, you are on the correct course. At intervals of a few yards, cease, lie prone, and hearken. If the sound vanishes, you have strayed; scan every direction until the whisper returns. Follow the acoustic signature, ensuring you do not retrace your steps. The volume will increase as you approach the source; the point of maximum resonance is your destination. Dig with the spade I provide until you reach moisture. Place the hand therein, bury it flush with the dune surface, and return. Be vigilant. Carry the hand with absolute care. Never set it down in any location that appears safe; ensure nothing touches it. Do not halt or deviate for any obstacle attempting to block your path. Never glance backward. Speak to no one. Answer no one. Walk straight ahead. It is still dark, and dawn is distant, but you must depart immediately."

He handed me the severed limb and produced a tool. "This is my gardening spade," he noted. "With it, I have cultivated countless beauties for the sun."

I accepted the burden and stepped out into the encroaching night. The air was biting, the darkness absolute. To stumble would be fatal, and the terrain, even in daylight, posed a treacherous challenge. Yet, I had undertaken this task deliberately. The moment I commenced movement, I realized I was not subject to random chance. A pale luminescence fractured the earth with every stride, illuminating precisely where to place my next foot. Through the heather and scattered rocks, I navigated without a single misstep. The bad burrow lay silent; no waves rose, no heads emerged as I traversed it.

A moon rose, casting its silver gaze to reveal the clear path. By morning, I had nearly crossed the dry channels of the river's first branch, judging Mara's cottage to be nearby. The moon hung low, the sun absent, when a figure appeared in the narrowing path, cloaked entirely in a veil of moonlit mist. I maintained my course as though unseen. The figure discarded its shroud.

"Have you already forgotten me?" the voice intoned—whether the Princess or a facsimile remained unclear.

I neither paused nor responded, advancing steadily.

"You intended to abandon me in that horrific tomb!" the entity shrieked. "Do you not comprehend that wherever I choose to exist, I am present? Seize my hand; I am alive as you!"

My lips formed the words, *Give me your left hand*, but I silenced myself, continuing forward.

"Seize my hand," she screamed suddenly, "or I shall rend you apart; you belong to me!" She lunged at me. I shuddered but did not falter. No contact registered, and she vanished from sight.

With measured strides, filling the path for some distance, a phalanx of armored figures emerged. I walked through them—I remain uncertain if they parted for me or were insubstantial specters. They turned and pursued, their marching rhythm pounding at my heels, the clashing of steel audible and palpable. Yet I cast no glance behind; the cacophony of their steps and armor faded into the void.

Further along, the moon dipped near the horizon, plunging the route into deep shadow. There, seated where the passage narrowed to a single file, sat a woman with a muffled face.

"Ah," she breathed, "you arrive at last! I have awaited you for an hour or more! You have succeeded! Your trial concludes. My father dispatched me to intercept you so you might rest briefly en route. Surrender your charge and lay your head in my lap; I shall tend to both until sunrise. I am not solely bitterness, nor am I hostile to all mankind!"

Her words carried a terrible allure, exploiting my profound exhaustion. Was it not plausible that, through rigid adherence to the letter of the command and a lack of spiritual insight, I would trample the very essence of the Lady of Sorrow? My heart faltered, then hammered against my ribs as if seeking escape. Still, my will calcified against my emotions, and my pace remained unbroken. I bit my tongue to suppress any involuntary response, pushing onward. If Adam had sent her, he could not fault my refusal. Nor would the Lady of Sorrow diminish her regard for me simply because she failed to divert me.

Just as I neared the phantom, she peeled back her covering. Great indeed was her beauty, yet those were not Mara's eyes; no deception could mimic them for long. I advanced as though the apparition were nonexistent, and my foot found empty space.

I had nearly cleared the obstruction when a Shadow blocked my way—I suspect it was The Shadow. He wore what appeared to be a helmet, yet drawing nearer revealed it was a skull, distorted beyond recognition of humanity. A cold, repugnant wind struck me, sickening and foul as the air of a charnel house. Stability abandoned my joints; my limbs trembled, threatening to collapse into a useless heap. I seemed to pass through him, though perhaps he pierced me instead. For a fleeting moment, I became one of the damned. Then, a gentle breeze, reminiscent of a newborn spring's first breath, welcomed me, and dawn broke before me.

My path now led past the open door of Mara's cottage. Upon the table rested a loaf of bread and a pitcher of water. Neither howl nor wail echoed within or around the dwelling. I reached the precipice marking the vanished river, climbed its eroded face, and entered the desert.

Finally, after extensive listening in all directions, I pinpointed the location where the subterranean water roared most loudly. Lilith's hand hung loosely around my neck, and I began to dig. The labor proved arduous due to the loose consistency of the sand, requiring a substantial excavation. Eventually, I tossed up a spadeful of damp earth. I cast the sexton-tool onto the edge and placed the hand within the pit. Water began to ooze slowly from beneath its fingers. I leapt back and hurried to fill the grave. Utterly drained, I collapsed beside it and succumbed to sleep.
