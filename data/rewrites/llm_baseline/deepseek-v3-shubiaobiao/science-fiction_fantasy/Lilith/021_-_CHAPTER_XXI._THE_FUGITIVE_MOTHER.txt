The moon vanished behind a shroud of cloud as I hurried forward, and from the gloom emerged a woman in white, clutching a child to her chest. She ran hunched, her movements frantic—each step a silent scream of terror. She hadn’t seen me, her path parallel to mine.

I pivoted, planting myself between her and whatever stalked the night. Before I could turn fully, something launched over my head—a flash of white—striking my brow with a force that sent me sprawling. Blood trickled into my eye as I scrambled up, but the creature was already gone, a phantom in the mist. Then a shriek split the air, raw and final.

I ran.

Ahead, a hunched shape lurched through the moonlight—not fleeing, but limping, dragging a forepaw. Spots mottled its pale hide, and a dark trail glistened behind it. Blood. *A lot of blood.* Some hunter had wounded it, yet as it passed, I felt no triumph—only pity. An axe wouldn’t have touched it now.

The woman sat in the grass, her child cradled tight. She flinched when I spoke.

“I mean no harm,” I said, crouching to seem smaller. “The beast is bleeding out. It won’t hurt you now.”

Her breath hitched. “You don’t know what it is.”

“Then tell me.”

Her fingers trembled over the child’s unmarked skin. “It’s *hers*. The princess sends it to drink the blood of babies. Those who survive grow up hollow.” She swallowed. “I thought I had more time. But I heard it—*sniffing*—and ran.”

“You fought it?”

“A stone to its paw. That scream you heard wasn’t mine.”

I stared. This ragged woman had crippled a monster.

“The princess believes a child will kill her,” she whispered. “So she makes sure none live. Witches teach barren curses. The beast hunts those who slip through.” Her voice dropped lower. “If it reached home, another will come.”

I offered to carry the child. She recoiled.

“Don’t go to Bulika,” she said, rising. “The palace isn’t stone and gold—it’s a throat. At night, the walls *try* to scream.”

Then she was gone, swallowed by the dark.

The blood trail gleamed under the moon, leading back to a city of nightmares.
