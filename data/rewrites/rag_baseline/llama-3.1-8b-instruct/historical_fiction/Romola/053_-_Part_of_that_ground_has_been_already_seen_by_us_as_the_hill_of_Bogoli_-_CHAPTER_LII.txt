As I walked through the winding streets of Florence, the scent of fresh bread wafted from the local bakeries, mingling with the acrid tang of woodsmoke and the faint hint of the Arno River. It was Carnival day, a time of feasting and celebration, but also of heightened awareness of the city's struggles. Famine had been a persistent specter, and the people of Florence walked with a quiet desperation in their eyes.

I had been tasked with supporting my cousin Brigida, whose once-lustrous hair now showed the first wisps of grey, a reminder of the passing of time and the weight of responsibility. Our household was abuzz with the news of Bernardo del Nero's election as Gonfaloniere, the leader of the Medici party. It was a triumph, but also a harbinger of uncertainty, for Romola sensed that this victory would only serve to deepen the divisions within the city.

Every morning, as I walked through the streets, I couldn't shake the feeling of foreboding that had taken up residence in my heart. The fear of what was to come was always there, lurking just beneath the surface, like the threat of the Plague that hung over the city. Yet, as I made my way to the Duomo for the early sermon, I felt a sense of resolve growing within me. I was not just a caregiver for my cousin, but a participant in a larger drama, one that would soon play out on the very streets we walked.

Inside the Duomo, the air was thick with anticipation. Savonarola stood at the pulpit, his voice ringing out across the crowd as he preached the final course of Lenten sermons. His words were like a clarion call, a clarion that cut through the din of everyday life and spoke directly to the hearts of the people. He held up the Church to the light of truth, revealing its corruption and decay, and proclaimed the advent of renovation, of a new era that would bring with it a general revolt against the rot that had consumed it.

As I listened, I felt myself caught up in the fervor of the moment. The Frate's words were like a fire that burned within me, illuminating the path ahead and giving me the courage to persevere in the face of adversity.
