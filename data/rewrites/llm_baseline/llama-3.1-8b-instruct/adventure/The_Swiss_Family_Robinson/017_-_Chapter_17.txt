As I stepped ashore with my family, my wife's face lit up with a mixture of relief and curiosity. "Tell us everything," she implored, her eyes sparkling with anticipation. My wife's gentle hands grasped my arm, and Franz's brow furrowed with concern as I recounted our harrowing encounter with the lioness and her mate. My voice wavered only slightly as I spoke of Jack's perilous situation, but the weight of our narrow escape hung heavy in the air.

Franz's eyes welled up with tears as I mentioned Juno's untimely passing, and he asked, his voice trembling, if her remains had been brought back to be laid to rest near our home. My wife's hands fluttered to her mouth, and she nodded, her eyes brimming with unshed tears. We buried Juno with all due ceremony, and Ernest, moved by his friend's request, composed an epitaph that captured her spirit beautifully:

    JUNO

    Faithful servant, loyal friend,

    Devoted companion,

    A guardian of our hearts,

    Who gave her life

    Defending those she loved.

The truffles and boar meat were a welcome addition to our stores, and my wife's eyes sparkled as she envisioned the savory dishes she would create from them. Though the boar's head was promised to Ernest's collection, I couldn't help but feel a pang of regret at relinquishing its succulent flesh. Reluctantly, I stood firm, knowing that our commitment to scientific inquiry must take precedence over personal indulgence.

Five days passed, but Fritz's absence weighed heavily on my mind. My anxiety became increasingly difficult to conceal, and I knew I had to act. The others welcomed my decision to set out in search of him, and even my wife, when she learned we would be sailing in the pinnace, agreed to join us.

The day dawned bright and fair, with a gentle breeze that filled our sails. As we set off towards Cape Minster, our spirits soared, and the sea air invigorated us all. I steered the boat towards the entrance of the archway, the sunlight dancing across the waves.
