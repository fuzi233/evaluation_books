I collapsed onto the bed, my mind racing to piece together the fragmented tale she had just spun. In her haste, she had betrayed herself with a single careless word, unraveling the mystery of my discovery in the forest. The leopardess had leaped away; the princess lay defeated on the riverbank; the rushing water had washed away her spell. Her own confession laid bare the peril facing the "Little Ones"—a threat I had inadvertently become the sole guardian against. By saving their greatest enemy, I had sealed my fate. This realization barely registered before sleep claimed me. Perhaps the wine she offered was less innocent than it appeared.

When consciousness returned, night had fallen. A single lamp hung from the ceiling, casting a soft, ethereal glow across the chamber. A heavy, dreamlike lethargy wrapped around me, lifting me far from solid ground and into a twilight sea. For a fleeting moment, existence was pure pleasure, devoid of pain. But then, a sudden, searing lance of agony pierced my heart—a mortal sting that made the previous bliss seem like a distant memory. It struck again, sharper, more sickening. I tried to raise my hand to clutch my chest, but something held me fast. Paralysis seized my entire body. An evil force pinned me down, hateful and immovable. My will screamed in protest, futile and broken. Forced into passivity, I waited.

Then, a gentle hand cradled my face, pressing my head deeper into the pillow, while a heavy weight settled across my torso. My breathing eased as the pressure lifted from my lungs. Opening my eyes, I found the princess standing over me, gazing out into the room with the vacant stare of a dreamer. Her great eyes were clear and unnervingly calm, her lips curved in a expression of satisfied passion. She wiped a smear of red from her mouth, locking eyes with me. Before I could react, she brought a handkerchief down hard across my face. It felt like a blade slicing through my vision, plunging me into blindness for several seconds.

A dull, heavy thud echoed nearby—the sound of a large, soft-padded beast landing after a short leap. Blinded, I heard the swish of a long tail disappearing through a partially open door. Instinct took over; I lunged forward. The creature was gone. I sprinted down the stairs and burst into the alabaster hall. The moon hung high outside, bathing the interior in a pale, ghostly light. The princess was nowhere to be seen. Panic flared within me. In her presence, I might survive; without her, I was prey. She treated me with favor only to make my consumption easier. My eyes remained defiant, refusing to close, knowing that if I did, she would strike again. Unable to see her, I felt her omnipresence, lurking in every shadow, perhaps waiting in some hidden corner of my own slumber. Safety existed only under her gaze.

Stepping outside, absolute darkness swallowed the world. Blindfolded by the void, I groped blindly until my fingers brushed a curtain. Pushing it aside, I entered a vast, black hall. Here, visibility defied logic. The walls, floor, and ceiling were draped in an infinite blackness, darker than any moonless night. Yet, my eyes strained to distinguish figures within the gloom. Shapes emerged vaguely from the mass—interpenetrating, dividing, and surrounding one another. My vision refused to stabilize. Pressing my eyeballs, staring harder, yielded nothing but a blur of blackness mingling with indistinct forms. Silence and undefined motion dominated the space, a dim, chaotic dance punctuated by recurring glimpses of familiar horrors: a woman with luminous eyes set in a skull, an armored rider atop a skeletal steed, grotesque burrowing phantoms. There was no order, no rhythm, only confusion. Solid shapes shifted color, accompanied by a swarm of independent shadows dancing to their own erratic wills.

I scanned the kaleidoscope of terror for the princess, but she was absent. No sign of her presence marred the wild shifting scene. Ignored completely as I wandered aimlessly, searching for her, despair began to take root. Turning away, I clung to a wall, using it as a guide, until I reached a curtained opening leading to a vestibule. Moonlight filtered weakly here, illuminating the cage of the leopardess. Inside, a desperate, silent struggle raged between two vastly different forms—human and beast. Their bodies and limbs tangled in a writhing embrace. The fight had lasted mere seconds when the leopardess suddenly burst from the cage, walking calmly toward the open door.

Hastening after her, I glanced back. The leopardess remained in the cage, crouching motionless as before. High in the sky, the moon shone bright and clear. A bodiless shadow walked through the trees toward the gate, followed closely by the leopardess, her tail swinging lazily. I trailed them silently, keeping my distance. Neither paused nor looked back. We passed through the open gate, descending toward the sleeping city, bathed in the stillness of the moonlight. The lunar face seemed frozen in anticipation.

The shadow moved directly to the staircase where I had lain the night prior. He ascended without hesitation, and the leopardess followed. I quickened my pace, but moments later, a cry of sheer horror tore through the air. Something soft and heavy crashed between me and the stairs. At my feet lay a body, frightfully blackened and crushed, yet unmistakably the woman who had guided me home and locked me out. Petrified, I watched as the spotted leopardess bounded down the remaining steps, a baby clamped firmly in her jaws.

I darted forward to snatch the infant before she could turn at the bottom of the stairs. In that same instant, from behind me, the white leopardess erupted through the moonlight like a bar of glowing silver. She seized the spotted leopardess by the neck. The spotted beast dropped the child; I scrambled to grab it, holding it tight as I watched the ensuing duel.

It was a terrifying spectacle. One fought, then the other gained the upper hand. Both were so focused that the only sounds were low growls, whimpering cries, and snarls of hatred, interspersed with the frantic scuttling of claws as they dragged and pushed for purchase on the pavement. The spotted leopardess was larger, and my heart ached for her friend. However, I quickly realized that while equal in strength and agility, the white leopardess possessed superior endurance. She never loosened her grip on the other's neck.

Finally, a howl of agony escaped the spotted leopardess's throat. It escalated rapidly, transforming into the long, drawn-out crescendo of a woman's final scream. The white leopardess released her jaws. The spotted beast recoiled, rising onto her hind legs. Standing erect in the moonlight was the princess. A swirling vortex of shadows raced over her pristine white fur—the leopard spots crowded, hurried, and fled toward the safety of her eyes, vanishing as they merged with her gaze. The last few stragglers, overtaken by speed, blended into the cloud of her flowing hair, leaving her radiant as the moon itself after a legion of tiny vapors has been scattered by the wind. Save for a thin trickle of blood running down the white column of her throat from the wounds of her adversary's teeth, she was perfect.

She turned, taking a few steps with the eerie, multi-legged gait of Hecate, then fell, instantly covered anew in spots. Covered, she broke into a long, stretching gallop and fled. The white leopardess turned as well, springing upon me. She tore my arms apart, snatched the falling baby, and launched herself down the street toward the gate, carrying our only hope into the night.
