As we emerged from the trepidation that had gripped us since the serpent's appearance, my mind lingered on the uncertainty that still lingered – the possibility that another reptile might have slipped past us, unseen, or even entered our domain from beyond Falconhurst. I proposed two expeditions to alleviate these concerns: one to meticulously inspect the thicket and marsh, the other to investigate the Gap, the sole entry point for our adversary.

Summoning my sons to join me on the marsh excursion, I found Ernest and Jack less than enthused, the latter confessing to a lingering unease, haunted by the thought of the serpent's tail flailing mere inches from his ribs. Though I couldn't yield to their reluctance, we cautiously crossed the marsh by laying planks and wicker hurdles, shifting them as we advanced.

Upon emerging from the thicket, we found ourselves on solid ground, adjacent to the precipitous rock face, and spotted a crystal-clear brook flowing from an opening that revealed a substantial cave or grotto. The vaulted ceiling was adorned with stalactites, while sturdy pillars supported the roof, evoking an air of grandeur. The floor, blanketed with fine white earth, felt like fuller's earth beneath my feet.

"This is indeed a fortunate discovery!" I exclaimed. "With this, I'll have soap at my fingertips, obviating the need for soap-boiling." Perceiving the streamlet emanated from a sizeable opening within the rock, Fritz ventured forward, requesting I follow him. As I did, leaving the others in the outer cave, I fired a pistol shot, whose reverberations attested to the cavern's vastness. With my candle lit, we proceeded, its gentle glow casting an ethereal ambiance despite its feeble illumination.

Fritz suddenly declared, "I firmly believe we've stumbled upon a second salt cave! Observe how the walls glimmer, and the light reflects off the roof!" I countered, "Those can't be salt crystals, for the water flowing over them leaves no residue, and tastes sweet. I'm inclined to think we've entered a cave of rock crystal!" The boys exchanged excited whispers, with Jack exclaiming, "Then we've uncovered a treasure!" I cautioned, "Only if we can utilize it; otherwise, it's as valuable as the gold found by Robinson Crusoe."
