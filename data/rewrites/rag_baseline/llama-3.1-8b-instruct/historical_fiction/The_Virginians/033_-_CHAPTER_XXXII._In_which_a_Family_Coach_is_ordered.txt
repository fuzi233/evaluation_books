The silence of the carriage was oppressive, a physical weight that pressed upon my shoulders as I rode alongside my mother. We had been traveling for hours, the rhythmic creak of the wheels and the soft murmur of the horses' breathing the only sounds that broke the stillness. My brother, Martin, sat across from me, his eyes fixed on the passing countryside as he recited a poem under his breath. Our father, Colonel Lambert, sat beside him, his face a mask of contentment as he watched the landscape roll by.

We were bound for Tunbridge Wells, a destination chosen by my father for reasons I couldn't quite understand. The farm horses were idle, the hay was in, and the corn was not yet ready for cutting – a trifling matter, it seemed, to warrant a family excursion. And yet, here we were, a party of six, with our belongings packed tightly onto the roof of our ancient carriage, said to have borne the King himself on his coronation procession.

As we rode, the towns and villages gave way to rolling hills and wooded glades, the air thickening with the scent of blooming wildflowers. My sisters, Hetty and Theo, sat together, their faces aglow with excitement as they chattered about the people we might meet at our destination. I felt a pang of envy, watching them, their carefree laughter and easy smiles a reminder of the life I longed to lead.

We passed by Madame Bernstein's house, the sound of music and laughter drifting out into the evening air. I caught a glimpse of the Baroness herself, resplendent in a gown of silk and satin, as she welcomed her guests with a gracious smile. My father pointed out a few of the familiar faces, his voice low and smooth as he spoke of the various personages gathered within. I felt a thrill of curiosity, wondering what it would be like to join them, to be one of the beautiful people, to dance and laugh and be admired.

But for now, I was content to ride, to watch the world go by, and to listen to my father's stories. The carriage wheels rolled on, a soothing rhythm that seemed to match the beat of my heart, as we journeyed deeper into the evening, and further into the unknown.
