As the last wisps of fog retreated from the streets of Harley Street, a soft, ethereal light seeped into the back drawing-room, casting a gentle glow upon Edith's tranquil form. She lay curled up on the crimson damask sofa, a vision in white muslin and blue ribbons, her silken curls a tumble of darkness against the cream-colored upholstery. Margaret sat beside her, watching with a sense of wistful longing as her cousin's eyelids fluttered shut, her breathing slowing into a peaceful rhythm. They had spent the afternoon discussing the intricacies of wedding dresses and ceremonies, Captain Lennox's stories of Corfu, and the challenges of maintaining a piano in good tune. Edith's laughter had sparkled like diamonds amidst the conversation, but now, as the evening drew to a close, she had succumbed to a soft, drowsy sleep.

Margaret's thoughts turned to her own future, to the life she would leave behind in the city and the new path unfolding before her in the country parsonage. Her father and mother waited for her, their warm hospitality and gentle guidance a comfort she would sorely miss. Yet, as she gazed upon Edith's peaceful face, she felt a pang of sadness. Their lives were about to diverge, and Margaret's heart ached at the thought of being separated from her dear cousin for an indefinite time. The familiar sounds of the dinner party drifted from the next room – her aunt Shaw's animated voice, the laughter of their neighbors, and the occasional, knowing remark from Mr. Grey, the gentleman who always took the bottom of the table at their gatherings. His words, like the gentle patter of rain on the roof, created a soothing background hum, a reminder of the ordinary, mundane world that would soon be left behind. As Margaret's thoughts swirled, the fragments of conversation from the next room floated back to her, snippets of a world that would soon be hers to navigate, full of its own joys and challenges. Edith's whispered dreams mingled with her own, a soft, wordless understanding passing between them as they drifted into the unknown.
