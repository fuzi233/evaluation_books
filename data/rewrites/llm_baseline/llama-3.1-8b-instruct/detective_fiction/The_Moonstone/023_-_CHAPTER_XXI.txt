My mistress, a masterful presence, broke the silence with a few words of apology, addressing Sergeant Cuff directly. "I spoke to you rather hastily earlier," she said, her voice warm and sincere. "If I wronged you in any way, I regret it deeply."

The Sergeant, his rugged demeanor softened by her words, requested permission to explain himself, presenting it as a gesture of respect towards my mistress. He began by stating that he couldn't be held accountable for the unfortunate events that had transpired, citing the necessity of maintaining secrecy regarding Rosanna Spearman. He appealed to me to attest to his restraint, and I verified that he had, indeed, kept her confidence.

However, the Sergeant continued, driven by a purpose that only became clear later, to extract a painful truth from my mistress. "I've heard a theory proposed about Rosanna's tragic fate," he said, his eyes fixed intently on my mistress. "While it may hold merit, my own investigation suggests otherwise. I believe an unbearable anxiety related to the missing Moonstone drove her to despair." He paused, then added, "I think I can identify someone who might shed light on this matter, if your ladyship will permit me."

My mistress waited for a moment before responding, her eyes locked on the Sergeant. "Is this person still within the house?" she asked, her voice firm but measured.

"No, my lady," the Sergeant replied, his words hanging in the air like a challenge.

The ensuing silence was oppressive, punctuated only by the storm raging outside. My mistress's hand, poised over the chequebook that lay on the table, trembled slightly as she pushed it back into the drawer. I felt a pang in my chest, recalling the countless times she'd used that same hand to show kindness to our loyal servants.

With a slow, deliberate pace, my mistress spoke up, her voice barely above a whisper. "I had hoped to settle our accounts without broaching Miss Verinder's name. Did my nephew, Mr. Blake, mention this to you before you arrived?"
