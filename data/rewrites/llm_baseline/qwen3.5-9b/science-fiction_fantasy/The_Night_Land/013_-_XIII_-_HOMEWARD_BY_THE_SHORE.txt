We emerged swiftly from that sorrowful, dreary realm hidden within the great mountains—the Upward Gorge—and halted shortly thereafter, standing beneath their towering feet, just beyond the gorge's gaping mouth.

Naani looked constantly in every direction, breathing rapidly, her eyes blazing with wonder as she beheld the new sights and felt the crushing dread finally lifted. She turned now to gaze upward into the gorge's darkness, spying the vast opening above, and felt a surge of fear. She had to run further downward toward the brightness of the Sea-Country. After pausing once more to look back with awe and relief, she faced the sprawling landscape and the great Sea again. Overcome by amazement, gladness, and sheer astonishment, she nearly laughed and cried simultaneously. She turned ceaselessly this way and that, unable to stop looking, taking deep draughts of the wide air—a sensation unknown to her in that lifetime spent in shadow, as you have witnessed.

We both felt that the need for hushed whispers had passed, born of the gloom and narrow darkness of the gorge. Indeed, she shouted like a child testing an echo; her voice rang prettily into the distance, vanishing far away into that country. Suddenly, an echo returned from the dark mountains behind us. We spun around abruptly. Whether it was a true echo, a strangeness, or an unnatural call rising from the gorge's gloom and horror, we remained unsure. We continued running downward for a while longer until we had recovered our breath, halting where we felt utterly free of the gorge and the strange mental weight that seemed to cling to the darkness of the high mountains.

Surely, we searched for a flat rock suitable for our needs and found a spot perfect for our purpose, situated slightly higher inland. We climbed onto the rock and sat there to eat and drink. As we feasted, we sat close together, happy, yet maintaining a wise vigilance against the Humpt-Men or any other lurking dangers.

Always, the Maiden questioned and stared across the land, filled with a shining wonder and joy regarding the sea. It stirred her very being, causing her to suffer from vague, sudden memories—strange dreams mixed with pleasure and pain. Soon, she began to weep, needing to be held in my arms until she recognized herself once again, returning to her dear, natural state.

Often, "Mine Own" spoke of the clear wonder of the air, which seemed so different to her. To me, it appeared the same, given that I too had lived my life in a Dark Land, as you know. Suddenly, she broke from her rapture, speaking vaguely of an eternity ago, recalling ancient, enchanted memories like moonlight that once shone. In a moment, she moved forward again into that distant future speech, her entire being drawn close to mine, often falling into a solemn silence of the heart.

The greatness of the sea called to her with an ancient voice, half-waking her. I, too, was half-wakened, though not as I had been on my outward journey. Truly, I stirred at the Maiden's touch, bringing forth my own ancient thoughts and memory-dreams to resurface upon my spirit. Thus, the two of us sat there, shaken by dreamings concerning events of the olden world lying above in that dreadful night which formed a mighty, deep roof over the country. I remain silent here, for I have no words to convey the troubles and stirrings of our spirits in that moment.

Far off, miles beyond the mountain's feet where the shoreline lay to our left, rose a great mist and steam. This was the very mist and steam I had traversed on my outward journey. Naani asked about it, and I told her what I knew—that we must soon pass through it on our return.

She marveled at the volcanoes burning in the sea and scattered across the wide country. Their height and grandeur exalted her, yet simultaneously instilled a strange humility in her mind. Presently, I took her into my arms, for I needed to kiss her; she was such a sweet maiden, lovely in her innocence and naturalness. Truly, she kissed me in return, asking questions between kisses. She yearned to learn more about the country, but perhaps also, as I suspect, because she was sweetly impudent toward me, driven by her joy.

Presently, she kissed me three times passionately and warmly on the mouth. Immediately, she grabbed my shoulders with her small hands, which looked so pretty against my breadth and the metal of my armor. She strove to shake me into a quicker response, full of dear naughtiness, needing to be kissed very hard.

I answered her, but in my own fashion—a word between each kiss. Quickly and naughtily, she placed her hand between our lips. I kissed the palm, which was in my way, small and pretty. When I had no thought, she opened her fingers quickly, kissing me through the gaps, then immediately shut them, stopping me.

Afterward, I made her stand on the rock and freed her hair over her shoulders. I removed her boots, revealing her little bare feet. At first, she half-refused, but afterward stood dear and obedient to my wishes, a little shy, becoming even prettier due to her sweet blushes.

When I had her to my liking, I stepped back slightly to look at her. She looked back, quaint and naughty, then turned around gravely, pretending to be a dummy figure. When she completed her circle and faced me again with a sedate expression, she suddenly stretched out her pretty foot and pressed her pink toes against my lips. So surprised was I that I lacked the wit to react before she snatched them back swiftly. Then, she made a joyful spring into my arms, wanting to be hugged and loved greatly. I laughed tenderly, for I loved her utterly, as you know. I told her, as a lover speaks, that I wished for a pocket large enough to carry her always close to my heart. You know the nature of such declarations well. She laughed mischievously, threatening to tickle and pinch me if I carried her that way. I had no answer but to shake her gently; yet, amidst my shaking, she kissed me naughtily on the mouth. What is a man to do with such a creature?

Then, she desired to be more sedate and sit upon the rock. She made me turn around so she could reach the pouch on my back. Taking out a comb, she styled her pretty hair. I sat and talked with her, jesting with a heart lighter than it had been in ages. Though I dreaded the Humpt-Men and the monstrous beasts of the Sea-Country, I harbored no abiding horror of anything I had seen there. There seemed a naturalness to all things, evoking no loathing, nor fear of an Evil Force.

When the Maiden finished combing her hair, she bound it atop her head. However, I asked her to leave it flowing over her shoulders, as it was so beautiful. She smiled at me, happy to please.

Now we were truly settled, organizing our gear. I put the boots back on the Maiden, and we resumed our journey downward into the Sea-Country. We traveled at a good pace without great weariness, for my intent was to rest for our slumber on this side of the boiling steam near the shore, as you recall.

We proceeded along the mountain's feet toward the steam for six solid hours, remaining a good hour's distance from that point. We did not travel as fiercely as during my outward journey, which was utterly strong, as you remember. Having walked six hours, we surpassed the eighteenth hour of that day's travel and were ready for sleep.

Presently, we found a tall, hard rock with a flat top large enough to accommodate us twice over. This suited our purpose perfectly. Upon reaching the top safely, we ate and drank, then slept, placing the cloak beneath us as the Maiden wished. The country was utterly warm and pleasant, requiring no covering.

Surely, we woke both after sleeping seven good hours. Sitting upright, we looked freshly at one another, as if seeing each other anew in the good light, finding fresh joy in each other's faces. She came into my arms to kiss and demand affection; truly, we both had our needs, yet felt hungrier for the other simply for the sake of possessing them.

"Mine Own" prepared our breakfast. The water fizzed strongly and surprisingly. We ate and drank, utterly happy together, talking of everything. The Maiden looked about as we fed, gazing afar at the wonders and novelties of the country to her knowledge. I, however, watched nearby, vigilant for any approaching danger.

Soon, "Mine Own" drew my gaze to the mountains through which the gorge had passed. In truth, now that I looked in ease, I saw with her how truly monstrous they were—like a giant wall ascending forever until they vanished from the country's light into the dark night of the deathly Upper World, lost in eternity. I recalled having vague thoughts of this on the outward way, but now, with ease and the Maiden to speak with, I perceived odd matters more clearly. I tell you this little detail so you may understand how restfulness fell upon me compared to the Outward Going.

Truly, we had little speed with our talk and eating, but eventually hurried somewhat, conscious that we were leaning toward slackness. Indeed, we descended from the sleeping rock quite speedily and pressed forward at a good pace.
