As Newland Archer settled into his worn leather armchair in the quiet sanctuary of his library, the echoes of the previous evening's gala at the Metropolitan Museum still lingered in his mind. The grand halls, teeming with treasures from a bygone era, had stirred a long-dormant memory, transporting him to a simpler time. He recalled the old Cesnola rooms, once a humble space now transformed into a showcase of art and history. A faint image of a slender figure in a sealskin cloak drifted before him, walking away down a narrow corridor, as if beckoning him to follow.

The memory awakened a cascade of recollections, each one etched into the very walls of his library. This was the room where his wife, May, had shared the news of her pregnancy, her words hesitant yet full of joy, a moment that seemed to belong to another era. It was here that their eldest son, Dallas, had taken his first steps, shouting "Dad" as he stumbled across the floor, while May and the nurse exchanged laughter behind the door. The christening of their second child, Mary, who bore an uncanny resemblance to her mother, had also taken place in this very room, as had the tender moment when Archer had kissed her through her veil before their wedding at Grace Church, a tradition that remained steadfast amidst the changing times.

In this sanctuary, Archer and May had often debated the futures of their children: Dallas's fascination with architecture, Mary's passion for philanthropy and sport, and the way they both navigated their own paths in a world that seemed to be constantly evolving. The young men of today were abandoning traditional pursuits, instead embracing new passions – Central American archaeology, landscape engineering, and the preservation of America's prerevolutionary heritage. They were adapting Georgian styles, protesting the misuse of the term "Colonial," and building homes that reflected their unique tastes.

As Archer sat lost in thought, his mind wandered to a particular evening when the Governor of New York had visited, his words still resonating within him. The Governor's words, spoken with conviction, had struck a chord deep within Archer: "Men like you –" the phrase echoed in his mind, a call to action that had once stirred his soul. He remembered Ned Winsett's similar appeal, urging him to join the fight, but it was the Governor's words that had left an indelible mark.
