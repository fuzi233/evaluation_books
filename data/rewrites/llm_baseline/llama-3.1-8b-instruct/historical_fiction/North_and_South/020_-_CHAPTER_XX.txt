The misty dawn crept over Helstone, casting a melancholy veil over the sleepy town, as Margaret Hale returned home, her heart heavy with the weight of the day's events. Her thoughts swirled with the image of Bessy Higgins, pale and frail, her bright smile dulled by the shadow of consumption. Margaret's mother, Mrs. Hale, sat waiting for her in the parlor, her eyes expectant, yet tinged with a hint of worry. News from the outside world was always a balm to her confinement, a reminder that life beyond the confines of their estate still pulsed with vitality.

As Margaret entered, Mrs. Hale's gaze met hers, and she asked, "And can your factory friend come on Thursday to see you dressed?" Margaret's response was laced with concern, "She was so ill, I never thought of asking her." Mrs. Hale's expression turned wistful, a fleeting glimmer of the old Helstone habits of thought resurfacing. "Dear, everybody seems to be ill these days," she said, a hint of jealousy creeping into her voice. "But it's a pity to be ill in a place like this."

Margaret's mind wandered back to the Higgins' cottage, the poverty and hardship that lurked behind the door. She envisioned the little basket she had packed, filled with preserves made from Helstone's finest fruits, a small gesture of kindness in a world torn apart by conflict. As she spoke of her plans to visit Bessy, her words spilled forth like a dam breaking, releasing a torrent of emotions she had held back. Mrs. Hale listened intently, her face etched with concern, as Margaret recounted the tale of Higgins' struggles.

Mrs. Hale's restlessness grew, her desire to act upon the situation overwhelming her. She summoned Margaret to pack a basket in the drawing room, to be dispatched to the Higgins' immediately. As Margaret hesitated, suggesting it could wait until morning, Mrs. Hale's irritation flared. "We may have been doing wrong," she said, her voice tinged with uncertainty. "Mr. Thornton mentioned the other day that helping the strikers only prolongs their suffering." Margaret's thoughts were consumed by the present moment, her compassion for Bessy taking precedence over abstract principles.
