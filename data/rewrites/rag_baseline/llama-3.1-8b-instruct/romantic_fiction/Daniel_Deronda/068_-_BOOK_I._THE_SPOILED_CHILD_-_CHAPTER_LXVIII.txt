As the autumnal mist crept through the streets of London, shrouding the city in an impenetrable veil, Lady Violet Lebas sat ensconced in her library, surrounded by the musty scent of old books and the faint tang of decay. Her gaze drifted listlessly across the letters scattered before her, each one bearing the confident scrawl of Mr. Andrew Lang. The weight of their words hung in the air, a palpable reminder of the world beyond her sheltered existence. It was a world where men like Mr. Lang roamed free, unencumbered by the suffocating strictures of societal expectation. 

Meanwhile, in a small, cramped room elsewhere in the city, Ezra's anxiety simmered just below the surface. His eyes, usually bright with a fierce intensity, seemed dull and clouded, as if veiled by a thin membrane of doubt. He had come to expect a certain measure of restlessness from his father, Lapidoth, but today it seemed to have reached a fever pitch. The old man's mind was awhirl with schemes and stratagems, each one designed to secure his own freedom from the shackles of responsibility. His gaze darted about the room, searching for an opening, a chink in the armor of those around him that he might exploit to his advantage. 

Deronda's arrival was a catalyst for this turmoil, his very presence a spark that fanned the flames of Lapidoth's discontent. The young man's words, usually so gentle and reassuring, now seemed to mock him, a cruel reminder of the gulf between them. As they sat together, the tension grew, until it became almost palpable, a living, breathing entity that threatened to consume them all. Lapidoth's eyes narrowed, his mind racing with the possibilities, each one more outlandish than the last. He was a man driven by a hunger that could never be sated, a hunger that gnawed at his very soul. And yet, he hesitated, unsure of how to act, caught between his desire for freedom and his fear of the unknown.
