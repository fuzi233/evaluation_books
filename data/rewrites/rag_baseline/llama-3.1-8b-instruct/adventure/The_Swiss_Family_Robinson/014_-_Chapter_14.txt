As the seasons shifted and the rainy season descended upon our island home, the family's collective mood began to sag. The confines of our spacious dwelling, though replete with diverse activities and engaging pursuits, seemed to close in around us. My children, Fritz and Ernst, sensed the ennui that had taken hold and proposed a novel endeavor to alleviate the monotony: crafting a more agile and manageable canoe, one that would enable them to traverse the waters with greater ease. Their idea resonated with me, and I envisioned a sturdy vessel, inspired by the Greenlander's robust cajack, that would not only provide a means of transportation but also serve as a masterpiece of craftsmanship.

However, my wife expressed concerns regarding the added risks of venturing further onto the sea, given our existing pinnace and canoe. She posited that our chances of encountering danger were already considerable, and introducing another vessel would only increase our vulnerability. Yet, I reassured her that the new craft would be designed with safety and sturdiness in mind, incorporating whalebone and split bamboo canes to create a robust skeleton, while also utilizing seal-skin and caoutchouc to ensure a watertight seal.

The boys' enthusiasm was palpable, and the project soon absorbed our collective attention. As we worked, the rain dissipated, and the sun broke through the clouds, casting a warm glow over our endeavors. We completed the canoe's construction outside, utilizing our front door as a makeshift launch site. The finished product was a remarkable sight – lightweight yet buoyant, with a peculiar appearance that belied its durability.

Fritz was awarded the honor of christening the canoe, and before he could take her out for a spin, his mother insisted on creating a specialized swimming-dress, designed to inflate in the event of an emergency, providing a vital lifeline in case the canoe suffered damage. While I harbored reservations about the garment's effectiveness, I agreed to collaborate with her in its construction. The resulting creation was a curious ensemble, consisting of a double waistcoat lined with india-rubber-coated linen, sealed with caoutchouc, and featuring a small air-inflation mechanism.

As we watched Fritz don the garment and paddle across the bay towards Shark Island, our surroundings seemed to burst with vitality. The seeds we had scattered had begun to sprout, and the verandah was adorned with vibrant, fragrant creepers climbing up the pillars.
