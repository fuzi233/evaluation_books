To comprehend the grandeur of the coming conflict, one must first grasp the absolute desolation of the hour preceding it. Two hours after departing the golden splendor of my palace at Helium, or rather, as the clock struck midnight, Kantos Kan, Xodar, and I arrived at Hastor. Carthoris, Tars Tarkas, and Hor Vastus had already departed on another cruiser, hastening toward Thark. The transports were poised to launch immediately, drifting slowly southward, while the formidable fleet of battleships would overtake them by dawn on the second day.

At Hastor, everything stood in perfect readiness. Kantos Kan's planning had been meticulous, a testament to a mind unclouded by fear. Within ten minutes of our arrival, the first vessel soared aloft from its dock. Thereafter, at a relentless pace of one per second, the great ships floated gracefully into the night, forming a long, thin line stretching for miles toward the southern horizon.

It was only after we had retreated into the privacy of Kantos Kan's cabin that the crushing weight of time finally struck me. Up until this moment, I had been uncertain how long I had languished in the suffocating darkness of the pits of Zat Arras. When Kantos Kan revealed the date, a pang of dismay pierced my heart: three hundred and sixty-five days had elapsed. It was too late to save Dejah Thoris.

The expedition had transformed from a mission of rescue into an act of vengeance. I held back the terrible truth—that before we could hope to breach the Temple of Issus, the Princess of Helium would likely be dust. She might already be dead; I lacked the precise chronology of her final moments. Why burden my comrades with my personal sorrows? They had endured enough suffering alongside me in the past. From this moment forth, I would hoard my grief, saying nothing to anyone that we had failed in our primary objective. Yet, the expedition could still achieve something monumental. If it could expose the Red Men to the cruel deception wrought upon them for countless ages, saving thousands annually from the horrific fate awaiting those who completed the voluntary pilgrimage, the sacrifice would not be in vain.

Opening the fair Valley Dor to the red races would have been a triumph in itself. Between the Mountains of Otz and the ice barrier lay the Land of Lost Souls, a realm of broad acres requiring no irrigation to yield rich harvests. Here, at the bottom of a dying world, resided the only naturally productive area upon its surface. Here alone were dews and rains, an open sea, and water in abundance. Yet, this beauteous and fertile expanse served merely as a stamping ground for fierce brutes, while the wicked remnants of two once-mighty races barred access to these millions. If I could succeed in shattering the barrier of religious superstition holding the red races back from this El Dorado, it would stand as a fitting memorial to the immortal virtues of my Princess. Her martyrdom would not have been futile.

On the morning of the second day, as the first flush of dawn illuminated the sky, we raised the great fleet of transports and their consorts. Soon, we were near enough to exchange signals. I must note here that radio-aerograms are rarely employed in wartime, nor for transmitting secret dispatches. As soon as one nation unveils a new cipher or invents a novel instrument for wireless purposes, its neighbors expend every effort to intercept and translate the messages. Over such a prolonged period, every possibility of wireless communication has been exhausted; no nation dares transmit important dispatches in this manner.

Tars Tarkas reported all systems functioning well with the transports. The battleships surged forward to seize an advanced position, and the combined fleets moved slowly over the ice cap, hugging the surface closely to evade detection by the Therns whose lands we were approaching. Far in advance, a thin line of one-man air scouts protected us from surprise, flanking us on either side, while a smaller contingent trailed twenty miles behind the transports. In this formation, we progressed toward the entrance to Omean for several hours when one of our scouts returned from the front, reporting that the cone-like summit of the entrance was now visible. Simultaneously, another scout from the left flank raced toward the flagship.

His velocity betrayed the gravity of his intelligence. Kantos Kan and I awaited him upon the small forward deck, corresponding to the bridge of terrestrial battleships. Scarcely had his tiny flyer touched down on the broad landing deck ere he bounded up the stairway to where we stood.

"A great fleet of battleships, south-south-east, my Prince!" he cried. "There must be several thousands, bearing down directly upon us."

"The Thern spies were not without purpose in the palace of John Carter," Kantos Kan observed quietly. "Your orders, Prince."

"Dispatch ten battleships to guard the entrance to Omean," I commanded. "Order them to permit no hostile entry or exit from the shaft. This will bottle up the great fleet of the First Born."

"Form the balance of the battleships into a great V, apex pointing directly south-south-east," I continued. "Order the transports, shielded by their convoys, to follow closely in the wake of the battleships. Once the point of the V penetrates the enemy's line, the V must open outward at the apex. The battleships of each leg shall engage the enemy fiercely, driving him back to create a lane. Through this gap, the transports with their convoys must race at top speed to gain a position above the Temples and Gardens of the Therns. Let them land there and teach the Holy Therns a lesson in ferocious warfare they will never forget. It was not my intention to be distracted from the main issue of the campaign, but we must settle this attack with the Therns once and for all. Without it, there can be no peace while our fleet remains near Dor, and our chances of ever returning to the outer world are severely diminished."

Kantos Kan saluted and turned to relay my instructions to his waiting aides. In an incredibly brief span, the formation of the battleships shifted according to my commands. The ten vessels destined to guard the path to Omean sped toward their destination, while the troopships and convoys closed ranks, preparing for the surge through the lane.

Full speed ahead was ordered. The fleet sprang through the air like coursing greyhounds, and in another moment, the ships of the enemy came fully into view. They formed a ragged line extending as far as the eye could reach in either direction, approximately three ships deep. So sudden was our onslaught that they had no time to prepare; it was as unexpected as lightning striking a clear sky.

Every phase of my plan executed with splendid precision. Our massive ships cut entirely through the line of Thern battlecraft. Then the V opened, revealing a broad lane through which the transports leaped toward the Thern temples, now clearly visible, glistening in the sunlight. By the time the Therns rallied from the initial assault, a hundred thousand Green Warriors were already pouring through their courts and gardens, while another hundred and fifty thousand leaned from low-hanging transports, directing their almost uncanny marksmanship upon the Thern soldiery manning the ramparts or attempting to defend the temples.

Now, the two great fleets closed in a titanic struggle far above the fiendish din of battle raging in the gorgeous gardens of the Therns. Slowly, the two lines of Helium's battleships joined their ends, commencing the circling maneuver within the enemy's line—a hallmark of Barsoomian naval warfare. Around and around, in each other's tracks, moved the ships under Kantos Kan until they formed nearly a perfect circle. By this time, they were moving at high speed, presenting a difficult target. Broadside after broadside they delivered as each vessel aligned with the Thern ships. The latter attempted to rush in and break up the formation, but it was akin to stopping a buzzsaw with a bare hand.

From my position on the deck beside Kantos Kan, I witnessed ship after ship of the enemy take the awful, sickening dive that proclaimed its total destruction. Slowly, we maneuvered our circle of death until we hung above the gardens where our Green allies were engaged. The order was passed down for them to embark. Then, they rose slowly to a position within the center of the circle.

Meanwhile, the Therns' fire had practically ceased. They had had enough of us and were only too glad to let us depart in peace. But our escape was not to be achieved so easily. Scarcely had we gotten underway once more in the direction of the entrance to Omean when we saw, far to the north, a great black line topping the horizon. It could be nothing other than a fleet of war. Whose or whither bound, we could not even conjecture. When they drew close enough to distinguish them, Kantos Kan's operator received a radio-aerogram, which he immediately handed to my companion. He read it and passed it to me.

"Kantos Kan:" it read. "Surrender, in the name of the Jeddak of Helium, for you cannot escape." Signed, "Zat Arras."

The Therns must have caught and translated the message almost as quickly as we did, for they immediately renewed hostilities upon realizing we were about to be assailed by another enemy. Before Zat Arras had approached near enough to fire a single shot, we were again hotly engaged with the Thern fleet.
