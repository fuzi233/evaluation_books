"You're late, Arthur," she said, her words dripping with disdain. But Laura, sensing something amiss in her cousin's demeanor, intervened, her voice a gentle counterpoint to her aunt's sharp tone. "What's wrong, Arthur? Are you unwell?" she asked, her brow furrowed with concern.

Arthur downed a generous measure of sherry, his hand shaking slightly as he set the glass down. "I've just received some extraordinary news," he said, his eyes darting around the table before coming to rest on the servants. "I'll tell you all about it later." His agitation was palpable, and Lady Rockminster's disapproval turned to annoyance. "Do try to compose yourself, Arthur. You're making a scene." She nodded towards Mr. Warrington, who was quietly enjoying his dessert. "And for goodness' sake, stop tapping your feet under the table. You've disturbed Fido's saucer."

It seemed as though the dinner would drag on forever, but finally, Lady Rockminster rose from her seat, her dignity intact despite the earlier tumult. "This has been a dull evening, to say the least. I suspect something's amiss, and you want to speak with Laura, don't you, Arthur? I'll retire to my room and take a nap. Perhaps I won't bother with tea tonight." With a dismissive flick of her head, she swept out of the room, leaving the others to rise in her wake.
