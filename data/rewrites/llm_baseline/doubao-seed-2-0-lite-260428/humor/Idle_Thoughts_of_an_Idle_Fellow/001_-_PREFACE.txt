A couple of my close pals snuck a peek at these scribbled pages when they were still messy handwritten drafts, and told me, to my great surprise, that they weren’t half bad. Even better, a handful of my relatives made a very solemn, very specific promise: if I ever actually got the thing published, they would buy a copy. (Full transparency: most of them already admitted they planned to use it to prop open a wobbly kitchen window or set under a teacup as a coaster.)

Given this overwhelming wave of public demand, I figured I had no right to drag my feet on releasing it any longer. If it weren’t for this tiny, enthusiastic group of cheerleaders, I doubt I’d ever have dared to serve up my silly little “idle thoughts” as proper mental nourishment for every English-speaking reader across the globe.

You see, these days everyone agrees that a good book’s job is to improve you, instruct you, and elevate your whole soul. This book wouldn’t elevate a dozy cow sunning itself in a clover field. I can’t in good conscience recommend it for any useful, self-improving purpose at all.

The only suggestion I will make? The second you get sick to death of slogging your way through that stuffy list of *The 100 Best Books You Absolutely Must Read*, pick this little volume up for half an hour. It’ll be a nice, brainless change.
