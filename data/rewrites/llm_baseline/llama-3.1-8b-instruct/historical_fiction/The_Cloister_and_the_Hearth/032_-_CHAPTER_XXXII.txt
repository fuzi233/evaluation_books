The aroma of freshly baked bread wafted through the refectory, mingling with the hum of conversation as Gerard sat at the long table, his eyes scanning the gathering of monks. A murmur ran through the assembly, and Gerard's ears picked up the hushed tone - a whispered rumor about the sudden passing of Brother Werter, who had been tasked with copying the monastery's precious documents. The news had spread quickly, and Gerard's curiosity got the better of him. He tentatively offered his services to the monks, hoping to prove himself useful.

A senior monk, Brother Edwin, looked at him with a hint of amusement and a twinkle in his eye. "Not for payment, my son," he said with a gentle smile, "but as a token of our gratitude for the hospitality we've shown you during your travels." Gerard nodded, understanding, and produced a piece of vellum from his wallet, the back of which he had carefully cleaned and inscribed with a sample of his writing. Brother Edwin's eyes widened in surprise as he took the vellum from Gerard and hastily rose from his seat, hurrying up the hall to present it to the prior.

The prior's reaction was immediate, and soon the entire table was abuzz with excitement. Gerard watched as the prior examined the vellum, his face a picture of concentration. Meanwhile, Brother Edwin returned, beaming with pride, bearing a steaming hot crustade ryal, a game pie filled with rich spices and gravy, accompanied by a delicate silver cup filled with a fragrant pimentum. Gerard took the cup, bowed low to the prior and his companions, and quaffed the drink, feeling the warmth spread through his chest.

As he handed the cup back to Brother Edwin, the assembly erupted into cheers and applause, calling out in unison, "Are you a brother, deny it not?" Gerard smiled, feeling a sense of belonging he hadn't experienced in a long time, and acknowledged their welcome. "I am indeed a brother," he said, his voice filled with gratitude.

"But how did you learn to write like that?" asked one of the monks, his curiosity piqued.

"You drank from the cup with both hands," replied another, chuckling.
