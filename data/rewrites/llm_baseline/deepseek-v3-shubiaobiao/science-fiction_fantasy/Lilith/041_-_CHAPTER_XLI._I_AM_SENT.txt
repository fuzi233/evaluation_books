I turned to Eve, my voice raw with exhaustion. “Mother, one couch beside Lona lies empty. I know I am unworthy—but may I not keep vigil with my dead tonight? I surrender myself. I am sick of my own weakness. Let me sleep the sleep of surrender.”

Her gaze softened. “The place beside Lona is already yours. But first, there is a task you must complete.”

“I am ready,” I said.

She arched a brow. “How do you know you can do it?”

“Because you command it,” I replied.

She turned to Adam. “Is he forgiven, husband?”

Adam’s voice was steady. “From my heart.”

“Then tell him what must be done.”

Adam took my arm. “Come. We will speak in the cottage.”

The wind howled as we stepped outside, its fury dying to a mournful whisper. Inside, Adam sat, and I stood before him, waiting.

“You remember,” he said, “the dry rock where you first glimpsed the desert. Return there now. Walk deep into the wastes. Listen with your ear to the sand. If you hear water beneath, continue. If silence answers, you have strayed. Follow the sound until it grows loudest—there, you will dig.”

He handed me a spade. “Bury the hand where the water sings clearest. But heed this: carry it with care. Let nothing touch it. Do not stop. Do not turn. Speak to no one. Look at no one. The night is dark, but you must go at once.”

I took the spade and stepped into the blackness.

The cold bit deep, the path invisible—yet with each step, a faint glow pulsed beneath my feet, guiding me. The bad burrow lay still, lifeless. The moon rose, casting long shadows, revealing the way.

As dawn neared, a figure materialized before me—a woman veiled in mist.

“Have you forgotten me?” she whispered.

I walked on.

“You left me in the dark!” she shrieked. “I am alive—take my hand!”

Her fingers brushed my arm—cold, grasping. I did not slow.

Armored phantoms marched behind me, their footsteps fading as I pressed forward.

Then, another—a woman seated in the path, face hidden.

“You’ve come at last,” she crooned. “Rest now. Give me your burden.”

Her voice coiled around me, sweet as poison. My limbs ached. My will wavered.

But I knew her deceit.

I stepped through her.

The Shadow came next—a specter with a helmeted skull, exhaling decay. I choked on the stench, my strength failing—yet I walked on, and it passed through me like smoke.

The wind shifted. Dawn breathed against the horizon.

Mara’s cottage stood open, a loaf and pitcher waiting. No wails. No whispers.

I climbed the precipice, into the desert.

There, I listened—until the hidden water roared loudest in my ears.

I dug. The sand fought me, shifting, sliding. But at last—dampness.

I laid the hand in the earth. Water seeped upward, swallowing it.

I filled the grave.

Then, spent, I collapsed beside it—and slept.
