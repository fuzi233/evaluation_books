For three long, roiling years, the movement to pass the so-called Universal Colour Bill tore Flatland apart, and right up to its final hours, it looked every inch as if anarchy would win, erasing every last line of the geometric hierarchy that had kept our 2D civilization stable for millennia.
A whole volunteer army of Polygons, marching off as common foot soldiers to hold the line, was completely wiped out by a far more numerous horde of Isosceles Triangles, while the Squares and Pentagons stood by neutral, too unsure of the outcome to pick a side. Worse still, some of our wisest, highest-ranking Circles fell not to enemy blades, but domestic fury. Many noble wives radicalized by pro-Colour propaganda hounded their aristocrat husbands day and night to abandon their opposition to the Bill, and when their pleas failed, more than a few turned on their spouses and their own children, slaughtering the whole household before perishing in the bloodshed. Official records note no fewer than 23 Circles lost their lives to this domestic strife over the three years of unrest.
The peril could not have been greater. The priestly ruling class of Circles seemed to have no two choices but total surrender or total extermination – until the tide shifted entirely, thanks to one of those dramatic, absurd little events that all competent statesmen learn to anticipate, even engineer, for the wildly outsized power they hold to tug at the general populace’s heartstrings.
It all started with a low-grade Isosceles Triangle, his total internal angles barely scraping four degrees, who had looted a tradesman’s shop and dabbled in his vats of colored pigment. Some say he painted himself on a lark, others say he paid a disgruntled artisan to do the job, but the end result was the same: he covered every edge of his shape in the full twelve distinct hues reserved for a Dodecagon, the highest-ranking non-Circle class. He wandered to the central market, disguised his rough, jagged voice, and approached a young orphaned maiden, the daughter of a noble Polygon who had rejected his clumsy advances years before. Through a string of lucky coincidences too tedious to recount, and aided by the impossibly lax caution of the girl’s remaining relatives, he pulled off the entire ruse and married her. The moment the poor girl discovered the fraud, she took her own life.
When news of this tragedy spread across every state in Flatland, the entire female population was thrown into a frenzy. Sympathy for the dead girl, and sharp, cold fear that the exact same trick could be pulled on their sisters, their daughters, or themselves, flipped public sentiment against the Colour Bill overnight. Many women openly renounced their prior support of the sedition; the rest needed only a tiny nudge to follow suit.
The Circles seized this golden opportunity at once, calling an extraordinary national assembly. Alongside their usual guard of hardened convict-class Isosceles, they made sure to invite thousands of newly converted anti-Colour women to attend. When the Chief Circle of the era, a legendary ruler named Pantocyclus, stepped up to speak, he was met with the jeers and hisses of a hundred and twenty thousand pro-Bill Isosceles rioters. He called for silence, though, and dropped a bombshell: the Circles would concede. They would give the majority exactly what they wanted, and formally accept the Universal Colour Bill.
The jeers turned to thunderous cheers instantly. Pantocyclus invited Chromatistes, the firebrand leader of the Chromatic Sedition, to step to the center of the hall, to accept the formal surrender of the entire priestly hierarchy on behalf of his followers.
Then came a speech, a masterwork of rhetoric that ran for nearly a full day, too dense and layered for any short summary to do justice. With a perfectly measured air of impartiality, Pantocyclus told the crowd that if they were going to commit to such a radical reform, they owed it to themselves to take one last, full look at the whole perimeter of the issue – its flaws, as much as its supposed benefits. Gradually, he laid out all the hidden dangers the Bill would pose to tradesmen, professional classes, and gentlefolk alike, and when a murmur of discontent rippled through the Isosceles ranks, he merely reminded them that despite all those flaws, he was still fully willing to sign off on the Bill if the majority voted for it. It was plain to see, though, that every single class except the low Isosceles had been swayed, and now stood against or at least neutral to the proposed law.
He turned next to the Artisan class. Their interests would not be overlooked, he told them, and if they chose to back the Colour Bill, they had to understand exactly what they were signing up for. Many of the workmen were on the cusp of earning promotion to the ranks of the regular Equilateral Triangles; many more held hopes of their children climbing even higher, to Squares or Pentagons. That noble, long-held ambition would be completely erased the moment universal color was adopted. All distinctions between ranks would vanish: regular shapes would be indistinguishable from irregular ones, social progress would reverse into degradation, and within a few generations the entire working class would be dragged down to the level of the military, or even the convict class. Political power would end up in the hands of the largest group, the criminal classes – who already outnumbered the workmen, and would soon outnumber every other class combined, once the natural compensative laws of Flatland’s social order were shattered.
A soft murmur of agreement rippled through the assembled artisans. Chromatistes, panicking, tried to push forward to rally his followers – only to find himself surrounded by guards, forced back into silence. Pantocyclus rounded out his speech with a fiery final appeal to all the women in the hall: if the Colour Bill passed, no marriage would ever be safe again, no woman’s honor would ever be secure. Fraud, deception, and lies would creep into every home, domestic bliss would collapse right alongside Flatland’s constitution, and all of it would vanish into ruin. “Rather than that fate,” he roared, “let us choose death.”
Those words were the prearranged signal. The convict-class Isosceles guards surged forward, driving their sharp angles straight through Chromatistes before he could scream. The regular upper classes split their ranks wide open, clearing a path for the cohort of anti-Colour women, who moved backwards, unseen by the rioting Isosceles, gliding unerringly toward the unsuspecting pro-sedition crowd. The artisans, taking their cue, split their own ranks too. Teams of convict sentries sealed every single entrance to the assembly hall, forming an impenetrable phalanx.
The battle, or more accurately the one-sided carnage, lasted barely an hour. Under the Circles’ expert command, every single charge made by the women was fatal, and most of them withdrew their sharp edges unharmed, ready to strike again. No second strike was needed, though. The mob of disorganized, leaderless Isosceles rioters destroyed themselves. Attacked from the front by invisible foes, and finding every exit blocked by the convicts at their backs, they panicked the way Isosceles always do when caught off guard, screaming “treachery!” That cry sealed their fate. Every single one of them suddenly saw a hidden enemy in every other Isosceles around them, and they turned on each other. In half an hour, not a single one of the seventy thousand rioters was left alive. The scattered fragments of their angles, left strewn across the hall, stood as proof that Order had triumphed over sedition.
The Circles did not waste a moment pushing their victory to its full extent. They spared the working classes, but culled their ranks to restore the proper class balance. The full Equilateral Triangle militia was called out immediately, and every Triangle reasonably suspected of having an irregular shape was executed by court martial, no tedious formal measurement by the Social Board required. For over a year, inspectors visited every home in the military and artisan classes, sweeping every town, village, and tiny hamlet across Flatland to purge the excess of lower-class citizens that had built up after decades of neglecting the legal requirement to send surplus criminals to serve as labor for the schools and university, and violating the other natural laws that kept Flatland’s geometric hierarchy balanced. Order, once more, was restored.
It goes without saying that the use of color was banned entirely after that, and owning any pigment was a criminal offense. Even saying any word that referred to color, unless you were a Circle or a specially qualified scientific instructor, carried a harsh penalty. It is only rumored that in the most secret, esoteric upper-level classes at our national University, which I myself have never been privileged to attend, small amounts of color are still permitted for use demonstrating the most complex, high-level problems in higher mathematics. I speak of that only from hearsay, though.
Everywhere else in Flatland, color no longer exists. The secret of how to make color is known to only one living person at a time: the reigning Chief Circle, who passes the secret to his successor only on his deathbed. There is only one single manufactory that produces the pigment, and to make sure the secret never leaks out, every last worker employed there is put to death at the end of each year, and replaced with a new set of staff. Such is the great, lingering terror our ruling Aristocracy still feels, even centuries later, for the long-ago days of the Universal Colour Bill agitation.
