It was the tenth hour of the day when we launched our raft. The paddles moved with surprising ease, balanced perfectly within the rests I had rigged, allowing the vessel to glide forward without excessive labor. We stood side by side—the Maid at the bow paddle, I at the stern—and pushed steadily. Soon, the raft attained a speed slightly faster than a brisk walk over the rugged terrain of the Land.

Around the twelfth hour, we paused to eat and drink, then resumed our gentle toil. Once settled into the rhythm, the motion became almost imperceptible, merely rocking us gently back and forth on our feet. Hours passed in this manner, accompanied by a constant, soft conversation between us. Often, the Maid would glance back at me with affection, pressing her lips against hers in a silent invitation for me to join her. Yet, whenever I attempted to abandon my paddle to move closer to her, she would shake her head with such tenderness that I remained at my post.

When the eighteenth hour arrived, we drew our paddles inward. The Maid carefully arranged her cloak to serve as our bedding. After eating and drinking, we fell asleep instantly, finding a sleep that was profound and joyful.

Eight hours later, we awoke simultaneously. For a fleeting moment, we were disoriented, unsure of our location, but quickly realized we were safe and undisturbed during our slumber. We laughed openly at one another, overwhelmed by the sheer joy of waking to find each other alive. After exchanging kisses, we washed briefly in the seawater and ate our meal. Then, we returned to paddling, proceeding along the coast peacefully and contentedly for the remainder of the day.

In total, this voyage lasted four days of twenty-four-hour cycles. We made no great haste, traveling slowly enough for me to regain my strength. Nothing occurred during this time except for one notable event: we spotted a massive beast emerging clumsily from the sea onto the shore, where it fed upon the vegetation. Though we could not be certain due to the distance, it appeared entirely natural rather than monstrous. It brought us no horror, only relief that we were far removed from it. Indeed, all creatures of that country seemed to possess this primal, ancient nature. It struck me that the Early World must have resembled this place, validating the old adage that extremes meet. Whether driven purely by circumstance or a deeper spiritual force, these beings embodied the raw vitality of creation. Even the dread monsters, hybrids of man and beast, suggested that while external conditions can distort form, an inner essence remains unique to each being. If the human spirit is untainted, humanity persists regardless of physical modification. Development has limits; a man cannot cease to be human simply because he once lived as a fish or adapted to harsher conditions. While I harbor hopes for the future beauty awaiting us in the Glad World, I return now to the narrative.

Beyond the sea-beast, another remarkable feature marked our journey: a towering Fire-Hill rising directly from the ocean. The sea boiled around its base, punctuated by dozens of great jets shooting skyward with thunderous roars. Strange grunts emanated from the depths beneath the hill, likely caused by bursting gases. These sights revealed the immense energy dormant in the deep regions of the world. We gazed back at the Hill of Fire and Force for a long time after passing it.

Further on, the vast ocean fractured into smaller seas, connected by narrow waterways that allowed our passage. I showed the Maid the specific spots where I had slept upon my arrival in this country; she listened with sweet curiosity, always eager to learn.

After four days on the water, we reached the shore at a flat stretch where the land sloped upward toward the mouth of the First Gorge—a place you recall well. It was the tenth hour, mirroring the start of our voyage. What a sweet, peaceful journey it had been! Had everything remaining held such tranquility, I would have been content. But danger awaited. Our hearts were set on courage, determined to conquer whatever lay ahead. If we survived to enter our Mighty Home, we would finally possess our lives together in eternal loveliness—a glorious reward for our suffering.

We beached the raft as securely as possible, wondering if anyone would ever see it again through eternity. We exchanged glances, and the Maid cut a small fragment of wood from the raft to keep as a memento. Gathering our gear, she helped me don my armor once more. I strapped the Scrip and Pouch to my back, gripped the Diskos in my hand, and prepared for action. She adjusted her belt, ensuring her knife was ready, her bundle now reduced to essentials.

Then, the Maid knelt and kissed the raft. Memories flooded her heart; this was her final farewell to the first phase of her life. Her heart stirred with a strange, troubled sorrow. I stooped gently, taking her feet in my hands, and led her away from the raft. She clung close to me as we walked forward, ascending toward the dark, gaping mouth of the Great Gorge.

To our right stood a grim, colossal mountain, crowned by the four fire-hills I had described earlier. Below them rose great hills of ash, deposited over eons. The Maid gazed upon this spectacle for a long time, her wonder seemingly endless—shared by no other human in history.

We ascended to the high entrance of the Gorge and ventured a short distance into the gloom until the passage curved sharply to the left, plunging into darkness. Here, we paused and turned back for one last look at the Country of Seas, hidden far below in the world's everlasting night.

It was a solemn realization that we might be the last of the old humans to witness that land. I wondered if the "Humpt Men" would eventually evolve, in some distant eternity, to possess the full sweetness of the human spirit I believed resided within them. It was an odd yet natural thought. To us, that country looked like a renewal of the Olden Times, viewed through fresh eyes.

We lingered a while longer, listening in hushed reverence to the distant muttering of the Great Fire-Mountains and the chaotic noise of life across that land. We stood mere paces from the silence of the Gorge, which would soon lead us into the Eternal Strangeness of the Night Land. The Maid held my arm tightly as we cast our final gaze upon the red glow of the Deep Country, where we had nearly met our death.

Presently, I turned, and she slipped her hand into mine. Tears flowed silently down her face, weighed down by the emotions pressing upon her heart. Yet, amidst the sorrow, there was happiness, and a vague longing for the island where she had nursed me back to health. She mentally cataloged the places she would visit in afterlife, perhaps telling tales of this unseen country to her children forever.

We rounded the mighty corner of the Gorge and stumbled forward into the gloom.

For sixteen hours, we traveled steadily, disturbed only by the encroaching darkness. By then, we had gone twenty-six hours without sleep—a foolishness, given my need to conserve strength before entering the Night Land. The Maid rightly pointed this out. Eventually, we found a safe spot to rest. Eating and drinking, we calculated our progress and decided to limit our daily trek through the Gorge to sixteen hours, sleeping eight hours each night. We adhered to this schedule strictly for five days until we emerged from the Great Gorge.

Once inside the lighter sections of the Gorge, spirits lifted, though we often struggled to breathe due to the horrific gases rising from various points. My strength grew constant as we journeied. The Maid refused to be carried, moving lightly and skillfully, accustomed to the wandering. At intervals, I stopped to show her the sites of my previous lonely slumbers. She insisted on standing exactly where I had lain, utterly tender and speechless, overcome by the weight of her heart.

She constantly asked when we would reach the Night Land, how far it was, and expressed growing excitement. I shared her anticipation, wondering what she thought of the Mighty Redoubt and the strange, monstrous landscape. Above all, I trembled with the desire to bring her swiftly to safety, fearing that despite our progress, disaster might strike. This anxiety made racing impossible; instead, we exercised wisdom, resting consistently after the sixteenth hour.

Life was absent in this desolate gorge. Only gas-flames burned, surrounded by boulders and stark rocks, often emitting rank smells. An absolute, everlasting quiet prevailed, broken only occasionally by a lone gas-fire moaning or whistling eerily across the waste. These sounds heightened the sense of loneliness, felt deeply by both of us.

Always, the Maid thought in her heart that I had come through this place alone to search for her in the unknown worlds. Naturally, I felt a swelling pride that she remembered and cherished my efforts; her love seemed to grow with every recollection. A man finds natural satisfaction in knowing he has done his best for his beloved. Do not the echoes of such proud thoughts resonate strangely with familiar pain?

We passed three locations where gas-fires danced, emitting low moans and occasional whistles, while other odd sounds arose from the rocks themselves—strange yet familiar.
