As the seasons changed, Oleron found himself retreating further and further into the sanctuary of his home. Few visitors dared to mount the stairs to his doorstep, save for the occasional postman, whose visits grew less frequent with each passing week. His publishers, eager for the manuscript of his new book, sent a series of letters, which Oleron neglected to respond to. The silence was a welcome respite, a chance to focus on the simple pleasures of life.

In the kitchen, Oleron's obsession with cooking reached new heights. He spent hours poring over ancient recipe books, searching for the perfect blend of flavors and techniques to recreate the dishes of his ancestors. His love for traditional cuisine was a comfort, a connection to the past that brought solace in times of uncertainty. The aroma of roasting meats and baking bread wafted through the house, mingling with the scent of fresh flowers that filled the vases in every room.

As the days passed, Oleron's fear of the outside world grew. He dreaded the thought of venturing out into the crowded streets, where strangers' faces seemed to blur together like a kaleidoscope of unknown dangers. His anxiety had given way to a debilitating phobia, one that made even the simplest tasks feel like an insurmountable challenge. But in the safety of his home, surrounded by the familiar scents and tastes of his kitchen, Oleron found a fragile sense of peace.

To avoid the need to venture out, Oleron arranged for his groceries and flowers to be delivered daily. He savored the freedom this brought, the ability to live life on his own terms. But as the days turned into weeks, Oleron's happiness gave way to a deep-seated melancholy. He felt trapped, confined to a world of his own making, where the only sounds were the soft whispers of his own thoughts.

Sunday mornings, once a time for rest and contemplation, had become a source of torment. The drumming and singing of the sect that gathered in the square below his window pierced through the stillness, a cacophony that grated on Oleron's nerves. He would retreat to his room, pulling the blind down to block out the noise, and find solace in the silence.
