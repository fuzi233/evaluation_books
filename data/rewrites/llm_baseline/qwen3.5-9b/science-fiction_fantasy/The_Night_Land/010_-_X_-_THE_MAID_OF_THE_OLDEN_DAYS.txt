In an instant, my utter despair transformed into a vast, swelling gladness and a profound hope. It seemed as though I would soon be reunited with my beloved One. Yet, I recognized this as an over-expectation, unlikely to be swiftly satisfied. All I truly perceived was the silhouette of a great pyramid rising into the night sky.

I knew this pyramid must stand atop a hill in the middle of that dark country; only such elevation could allow it to appear so towering against the void. Driven by this realization, I sprinted downward toward the land, eager to reach the structure.

For a few moments, I ran with frantic speed, only to crash headlong onto the ground. The impact felt as though I had shattered my neck against the hard earth, and pain seized me completely. For a long while, I possessed no strength to move, lying helpless and whimpering. In that vulnerable state, any passing creature could easily have ended my life.

Soon, however, I managed to sit up, clutching my neck. Gradually, the agony subsided, and I rose to my feet. This time, I proceeded with wisdom rather than blind haste, though anxiety gnawed at my heart. How could the pyramid be so utterly dark if it were truly the Lesser Refuge? A chilling fear arose that it might be a dwelling of evil, a malevolent force deceiving my senses. Yet, looking toward the distant fires of the land, the object appeared plain enough. There was little doubt in my mind that it was, in truth, the Lesser Refuge.

When I first spotted the dark pyramid, I had been devoid of reason, driven solely by the urge to rush blindly toward it after such a long search. Initially, I had intended to summon Naani via my brain-elements, sending the Master-Word followed by a message announcing my arrival. But now, prudence demanded caution. I needed to discern the true meaning of that darkness before acting.

Thus, I descended again into the night, initially moving carefully, but soon fueled by a fierce eagerness and a heart expectant of reunion—feelings dulled temporarily by the shock and pain of my fall.

I had spent perhaps thirteen hours climbing to the upper plain of the great volcano. My descent took ten hours; I moved faster than before, though my body remained sorely shaken and uncertain due to the earlier accident.

At the end of the tenth hour, I realized I had returned to the great plain of the land. From here, I could no longer see the refuge directly, obscured by the darkness of the night above. However, I could discern a massive bulk standing between me and the distant glimmers. I knew this great mass was undoubtedly the hill upon which the pyramid stood.

I traveled for four hours across the landscape, passing various fire-holes that cast a faint red glow into the night. Because of these scattered fires, both near and far, the darkness was not absolute.

After another four hours heading toward the pyramid, the distant lights vanished entirely, blocked by the shadow of the hill's base, plunging that direction into total blackness. This confirmed I was nearing the hill, though still an hour's distance remained. During these final five hours since leaving the volcano, I passed the sounds of creatures running through the night three times. Once, I heard a sound akin to a giant roaring in the distance, accompanied by a strange, horrific scream.

As I began to ascend the hill, an intense excitement seized me. I nearly shouted the Maid's name aloud, vainly hoping she might hear me and respond. But this fervor faded quickly as I climbed higher. Caution returned, bringing a coldness of fear, as my spirit sensed something my conscious mind failed to perceive.

Almost reaching the summit, which took nearly three hours, I finally beheld the grim outline of the pyramid rising desolately and silently into the night. An overwhelming terror shook me. My inner intuition knew with certainty that no human dwelt within that great, dark bulk. Instead, I awaited nothing less than monstrous and horrifying entities ready to destroy my soul. Trembling, I turned and descended the hill quietly into the darkness, eventually fleeing the place entirely.

It took four great hours to get clear of the hill. I felt that no safety existed anywhere in that land. Blindly, I wandered for a bit, paying no heed to my path.

Suddenly, I found myself upon the shore of the ancient sea, unaware of how I had arrived there, for it seemed impossibly close. Perhaps the dry seabed curved around to this spot, or perhaps there were multiple ancient seas in this Country of Night.

I sat down, weak and bewildered, feeling as though my heart had died within me. Indeed, my spirit told me that evil things resided in the dark pyramid on the hill. I had no doubt that destruction had fallen upon the peoples of the Lesser Pyramid, and that evil creatures and powers now inhabited that place. If this were true, I had arrived too late to save the Maid. With this thought, I felt a twisted relief that some evil entity might confront me, allowing me to fight and die quickly, for nothing in the world made me glad to live anymore.

You will understand the utter desolation in my heart. Truly, I can see both the wisdom and folly of my reasoning; I did not possess *certain* knowledge that the dark pyramid was truly the Lesser Refuge. Yet, in reality, my spirit knew with absolute surety, leaving no room for doubt in my entire being.

After sitting for a while, I remembered I should send the Master-Word through the night. How else could I know if Naani still lived? Though I held little hope, save for desperation, I recalled hearing the beat of the Master-Word from the depths of the darkness on several occasions during my journey. If Naani did not answer, and an Evil Power instead sought to destroy me, I would find solace in ending my heartache.

I stood and looked out into the blackness of the land. Sending the Master-Word through my brain-elements, I called to Naani three times.

Instantly, breaking through the mystery of the night, the Master-Word sounded low and solemn. Within my brain, a faraway, tiny voice emerged—lone and faint, as if coming from the ends of the world. It was the voice of Naani and the voice of Mirdath, calling me by my old love-name.

Choking back the sheer fright of joy that overwhelmed me, I was shaken by mighty excitement. My despair vanished as if it had never existed. Naani lived; she called to me through her brain-elements. Surely, I had not heard the voice of my own kin for an age of grim labor and dread.

Her voice seemed to come from one located far across the earth. While dazed by the joy that the Maid survived, I knew deep within that she was terribly far away. Peril must have approached her before I could stand by her side to battle for her life, my own joy, and hers.

In that same moment, before I could speak further to Naani, my spirit sensed someone nearby in the bushes, close to a burning fire-hole. My spirit revealed this fact to my mind. I offered no reply to the Maid across the dark expanse of the world but darted swiftly into a large bush near the fire-hole on this side.

Peering through, I saw an open space around the fire-hole. There, kneeling and sobbing on the earth beside the flame, was a slender maiden. She appeared desperately listening, even as she wept. My own soul *Knew*, in a single white moment of clarity. She was there, unknowingly, listening to a cry of the spirit that she believed was traveling through the desolation of the night—even from the Mighty Pyramid. Often, as I perceived, she had cried out to me throughout that lonely month, receiving no answer. Nor did she know I was making a desperate journey to her; her weakness was so great she lacked the power to project the Word strongly or to make her spiritual cries travel through any vast space of the ether.

I drew a sharp breath, gritted my teeth to steady my resolve, and spoke in natural human speech from the bush: "MIRDATH."

The Maid stopped weeping. She looked this way and that with utter new fear, mixed with a frightened hope shining through her tear-filled eyes. I parted the branches before me and stepped out, revealing myself in my grey armor. I paused, drifting in my own thoughts. My heart urged me to take this Maid into my arms again; I had come to be with Mirdath after an eternity of loss. Yet, I hesitated. Truly, she was Naani and she was Mirdath, but she was a stranger in my eyes—dainty, pretty, and shaken by woe, trouble, and grief.

In that exact moment of my approach, she screamed and recoiled, stumbling backward toward the nearer bushes. She struggled weakly, not knowing what had happened in that first fleeting instant. Immediately recognizing it was a human man and not a monster to kill her, she understood. At that precise moment, I spoke the Master-Word aloud to her, granting her knowledge of peace and aid. I told her my name and declared, "I am That One." She knew this instantly, even as my lips formed the sounds.

She cried out in a broken voice, ran to me, and thrust her two small hands into my care. She collapsed into great sobbing and shaking, troubling me greatly. I kept silence, holding her hands firmly, for I wore no armored gloves.
