In the quiet evening hours, when the city's din had subsided and the moon cast its silvery glow over the rooftops, Bruce Carmyle sat in his dimly lit apartment, his thoughts consumed by the unyielding determination to win Sally's heart. He had always been a man who refused to accept defeat, a quality that had served him well in his endeavors, both personal and professional. Though Sally had rejected his proposal at Monk's Crofton, he had not been deterred. His resolve was strengthened by the memory of her sparkling eyes, the carefree lift of her chin, and the charming, effortless elegance that seemed to captivate him whenever she was near.

As he sat in contemplative silence, his mind began to wander back to the moments when doubts had crept in, only to be vanquished by the promise of victory. The Bohemian streak that sometimes unsettled him was temporarily forgotten, replaced by the unwavering conviction that he was meant to have Sally by his side. The darkness of the night seemed to amplify the ache within him, and he felt an insatiable longing to pursue her, to follow the thread of connection that bound them together.

Unbeknownst to Bruce, his plans for departure had not gone unnoticed by the Family. In the intricate network of relationships within the household, news traveled quickly, often through a subtle system of unspoken understanding, akin to the whispers carried on the wind through the savannas of Africa. As the clock struck midnight, a visitor arrived at Bruce's doorstep – Uncle Donald, the Family's respected patriarch, whose imposing presence was a testament to his robust constitution.

Uncle Donald's considerable girth strained against the worn leather of the chair, causing it to groan in protest. His walrus moustache rose and fell with each labored breath, like the gentle swaying of seaweed in the undertow. At sixty-two, his sedentary lifestyle, born from a childhood fear of exertion, had taken its toll, leaving him with a formidable appetite and a somewhat reluctant demeanor.

Bruce's usual deference towards his uncle was tempered by a hint of discomfort as he met Uncle Donald's piercing gaze. "What's this?" Uncle Donald asked, his voice like a low rumble of thunder, "You packing?"

"Yes," Bruce replied, his tone curt, a rare display of hesitation in his usually composed demeanor.

"You going away?" Uncle Donald inquired, his words spaced out like the deliberate beats of a metronome
