The moon hung low in the sky, casting an eerie glow over the small village as we approached our humble hut. I motioned to Infadoos to join us, and with a gentle bow, he stepped inside. The air was thick with tension, the weight of the impending witch-hunt hanging heavy over the land.

"It seems to us, Infadoos," I began, my voice low and measured, "that Twala, our king, is a cruel man. His reign has brought nothing but suffering and fear to our people."

Infadoos nodded solemnly, his eyes clouded with sorrow. "It is true, my lords. The land cries out under his tyranny. Tonight, many will be accused of being wizards, and their lives will be forfeit. No one's fate is secure. If the king desires something from a man – his cattle, his wife, or even his loyalty – Gagool and her cohorts will sniff out the supposed witch, and death will follow swiftly. I fear I may be next, despite my skills in battle and the respect of the soldiers. The land groans under Twala's cruelty, weary of his red-handed rule."

"Why do the people not rise up against him?" I asked, my curiosity piqued.

Infadoos shook his head. "He is the king, and if he were to fall, Scragga would take his place, and his reign would be even more oppressive. His heart is blacker than his father's, and I fear our people would suffer even more under his rule. If only Imotu, our former king, and his son Ignosi had lived, perhaps things might have been different."

As we spoke, a soft voice interrupted us, sending shivers down my spine. It was Umbopa, his eyes gleaming with an otherworldly intensity. "How do you know that Ignosi is dead?" he asked, his words dripping with a quiet authority.

Infadoos turned to face him, a mixture of surprise and curiosity etched on his face. "Who told you to speak, boy?"

Umbopa's gaze never wavered. "Listen, Infadoos, and I will tell you a tale. Years ago, King Imotu was slain, and his wife fled with their son Ignosi. Was it not so?"
