I turned, searching the landscape behind me, but the world had dissolved into a haze—no clear lines, no familiar shapes. Was it mist or meadow? Cloud or cliff? My senses refused to settle. Only one truth stood firm: I recognized nothing.

To test the illusion, I stretched out my hands, stepping blindly, hoping touch might correct sight. But my fingers grasped only air. The ground beneath me was solid, the wind whispered through low plants, yet the world refused to make sense.

Then my gaze fell upon the raven. It stood a short distance away, watching me with an expression both solemn and amused. For a moment, I considered speaking to it—absurd, of course. Yet when I turned away, unease coiled tighter in my chest. Had I crossed into a place where the laws of my world no longer held? Could a man step beyond order and become prey to the lawless?

And yet—the raven remained. The earth beneath me. The wind in my ears.

*“How did I get here?”* The words left my lips before I could stop them.

A voice answered—harsh, croaking, yet deliberate. *“You came through the door.”*

I spun, searching for a speaker, but there was only the raven, its beak parted as if waiting. The sunless air cast no shadows, yet the bird seemed to hold its own darkness within it.

I must make this clear, though words falter. I was in a place—or a *state*—so unlike the world I knew that language buckled under the weight of description. Already I feel the truth slipping, reshaping as I grasp for it, like a dream dissolving upon waking.

The raven spoke again. *“You did not see the door because it was not a door *in*—it was a door *out.* The more doors you pass through, the deeper you go.”*

*“Where am I?”* I demanded.

*“Unanswerable,”* it said. *“You know nothing of ‘where.’ To understand, you must first make yourself at home.”*

*“By *doing.* The sooner you begin, the better. Until then, neither in nor out will make sense.”*

*“I didn’t mean to come here,”* I muttered.

*“Stumbling in doesn’t mean you’ll stumble out. Whether your arrival was *unfortunate* remains to be seen.”*

I bristled. *“Do you never leave?”*

*“Depends on the man. Or the bird.”* It tilted its head. *“Now, my turn: Who are you?”*

The question struck like a blade. Who *was* I? My name—if I ever had one—meant nothing here. I had no answer.

The raven laughed, a sound like dry sticks breaking. *“If you don’t know *yourself,* how can you know *me?*”* Then it shifted—not in form, but in *essence.* One moment, a bird; the next, a gaunt man in a black coat; then a raven again.

*“You—you were the librarian,”* I stammered.

*“Does it matter? You called me a raven. That was truth enough.”* It stepped back, its voice fading. *“No one is *himself* until he first knows *that* he is, then *what* he is. Home is the only place where you can go out and in. Find it.”*

Then it was gone.

I wandered, unmoored. Was I dead? Was this the afterlife? The air hummed with unseen presences. The raven’s words gnawed at me: *Do something.* But what?

A pine forest rose ahead. I entered, desperate for shelter. Between the trees, a shimmering *something* hovered—not light, not substance, but a trembling in the air. I reached for it, stumbled, and fell.

When I opened my eyes, I was back in the garret, the mirror looming before me. The eagle atop it seemed to smirk. Terror seized me. I fled, the house itself now alien, its corridors hungry.

Down, down, until I collapsed in the library. The garret was a weight upon the house—upon *me.* Its unseen inhabitants could descend at any moment. I was not safe.

*“If I don’t know my own mind,”* I thought, *“what protects me from it?”*

Then, as if summoned, the raven’s voice echoed: *Who are you?*

I had no answer.

Desperate, I turned to the hidden door, the mutilated book jutting from the shelf. Inside, fragmented verses teased my understanding—phrases that stirred something deeper than thought. I copied what I could, chasing meaning, but the words slipped like smoke.

Exhaustion took me. That night, I slept without dreams.

By morning, the garret’s dread had faded.

But the question remained.
