As soon as the echo of my wife’s farewell cry faded into silence, I stepped forward, intending to greet the Stranger more closely and invite him to sit. But the moment I drew near, I froze—speechless, motionless—overcome by astonishment.

He was unlike anything I had ever seen. Not a single angle marked his form. Yet he shifted constantly in size and brightness in ways that defied all known geometry. My mind raced: Could this be a criminal? A rogue Irregular Isosceles, pretending to be a Circle to gain entry to our home, now preparing to strike with a sharp point?

In our world, sight is unreliable without fog—and the season was unusually clear. At such close range, I couldn’t trust what I saw. Panic seized me. Without thinking, I lunged forward, blurting, “Sir, if you please—” and touched him.

My wife had been right. There was no angularity. No roughness. No flaw. He was perfectly smooth—a flawless Circle. I walked around him slowly, beginning at his eye and returning to it. From every angle, he remained a perfect circle. There could be no doubt.

Then came a conversation I shall try to recount as faithfully as memory allows, omitting only my many apologies—for I was humiliated beyond measure that a Square like myself should have dared to touch a Circle so unceremoniously.

The Stranger grew impatient with my hesitation.

**STRANGER:** Have you felt me enough yet? Are we not introduced?

**I:** Most illustrious Sir, forgive my clumsiness. It is not from ignorance of manners, but from shock and nervousness. This unexpected visit has thrown me off balance. And pray, do not reveal my rudeness to anyone—especially not my wife. But before you speak further, might you tell me where you come from?

**STRANGER:** From Space. From Space, Sir. Where else?

**I:** Pardon me, my Lord, but are we not already in Space? You and I, here, now?

**STRANGER:** Pooh! What do you know of Space? Define it for me.

**I:** Space, my Lord, is height and breadth extended infinitely.

**STRANGER:** Exactly. And yet you don’t even know what Space truly means. You think it has only two dimensions. But I have come to tell you of a third—height, breadth, and depth.

**I:** Your Lordship jests. We say "length" and "height," or "breadth" and "thickness"—four names for just two dimensions.

**STRANGER:** But I mean three actual dimensions—not just names.

**I:** Then would your Lordship show me where this third dimension lies?

**STRANGER:** I came from it. It is above and below.

**I:** You mean north and south?

**STRANGER:** Not at all. I mean a direction you cannot see—because you have no eye on your side.

**I:** But surely, my Lord, I have an eye at the junction of two sides!

**STRANGER:** Yes—but to see into Space, you’d need an eye not on your perimeter, but on your *side*—what you might call your inside. In Spaceland, we call that your side.

**I:** An eye in my stomach? That’s absurd. You’re joking.

**STRANGER:** I am not joking. I come from Space—the Land of Three Dimensions. From there, I looked down upon your Plane, which you foolishly call Space. From that vantage, I saw everything—your houses, churches, chests, safes—even your insides, laid bare before me.

**I:** Such claims are easy to make.

**STRANGER:** But hard to prove? Indeed. But I will prove it. When I descended, I saw your four sons—Pentagons—in their rooms. I saw your two grandsons—Hexagons. I watched your youngest stay with you before retiring. I saw your three Isosceles servants eating in the kitchen and the page in the scullery. Then I came here. How do you suppose I entered?

**I:** Through the roof, I assume.

**STRANGER:** Not so. Your roof was repaired recently—no opening large enough for even a woman. I tell you again—I came from Space. Are you not convinced by what I’ve told you of your household?

**I:** Your Lordship may have resources others lack. Any neighbor with wealth could learn such things.

**STRANGER:** (To himself) What must I do? One idea occurs to me. When you see a straight line—say, your wife—how many dimensions do you assign her?

**I:** You mock me as if I were ignorant. We know well that a woman is not truly a line, but a very thin parallelogram—two-dimensional, like us. She has length and breadth.

**STRANGER:** But the fact that she is visible implies a third dimension.

**I:** I acknowledge her breadth—though slight, it can be measured.

**STRANGER:** You misunderstand. When you see a woman, you should not only infer her breadth—you should see her *height*, though infinitesimal in your world. If a line had no height, it would vanish from space entirely. Surely you see that?

**I:** I confess I do not. We see length and brightness. If brightness fades, the line disappears. But are you saying "brightness" is your "height"?

**STRANGER:** No. By "height," I mean a dimension like your length—only one you cannot perceive because it's too small.

**I:** Then let us test it. Measure my "height." Show me its direction. Otherwise, I cannot believe you.

**STRANGER:** (To himself) I cannot do either. How can I convince him? A plain statement, followed by proof—surely that will suffice.

Now, listen carefully. You live on a plane. What you call Flatland is the surface of a fluid—on or within which you move, never rising or falling. I am not a plane figure. I am a solid. You call me a Circle, but I am not. I am an infinite number of circles—ranging from a point to a circle thirteen inches wide—stacked one atop another. When I pass through your plane, I appear as a section—a circle. Even a sphere—my true name—must appear as a circle to a Flatlander.

Do you remember when you entered Lineland? You appeared not as a square, but as a line, because that world lacked the dimensions to show your full form—only a slice. In the same way, your two-dimensional world cannot contain me. It can only show a slice—what you call a circle.

Your eyes grow dim with disbelief. But now, prepare for proof. You cannot see all of me at once—your vision is confined to the plane. But you can see how my appearance changes as I rise in Space. Watch: I will ascend—and you will see my circle shrink until it vanishes.

[Here, a diagram would follow—showing the Sphere appearing as a circle, then shrinking to a point.]

There was no movement I could see—yet he grew smaller, then disappeared. I blinked, wondering if I dreamed. But no—it was real. From nowhere, a voice spoke—close to my heart—“Am I gone? Are you convinced? Now I will return. Watch as my circle grows larger.”

Every inhabitant of Spaceland would understand what he said. To me, however—though skilled in Flatland mathematics—it made no sense. The diagram shows clearly: a sphere moving upward would appear as a circle, first full-sized, then shrinking to a point. But I saw only the effect, not the cause. All I knew was that the circle vanished, then reappeared and expanded.

When he returned to his original size, he sighed deeply. He sensed my silence—he saw I still did not grasp.

And I began to suspect he was no Circle at all—but a master trickster. Or perhaps the old tales were true: enchanters and magicians did exist.

After a long pause, he muttered, “Only one option remains—if I avoid action. I must use analogy.”

Another silence. Then he continued.

**SPHERE:** Tell me, Mr. Mathematician—when a point moves northward, leaving a glowing trail, what do you call that trail?

**I:** A straight line.

**SPHERE:** And how many ends does a line have?

**I:** Two.

**SPHERE:** Now imagine that line moving east and west, parallel to itself, so every point leaves a trail. What shape results? Suppose it moves a distance equal to its own length.

**I:** A square.

**SPHERE:** And how many sides? Angles?

**I:** Four sides. Four angles.

**SPHERE:** Now stretch your imagination. Imagine a square moving upward—not north, but *up*—out of Flatland entirely. Every point within you—since you are a square—moves upward, each tracing its own path, without overlapping. This follows analogy. Can you not see?

I held back my rage—tempted to charge at him, to hurl him into Space or out of existence. But I replied calmly:

“And what shape will I form by this motion? Will it be describable in Flatland terms?”

**SPHERE:** Of course. It’s simple—by analogy. A point has one terminal point. A line has two. A square has four. The pattern is geometric: 1, 2, 4. What comes next?

**I:** Eight.

**SPHERE:** Precisely. So the new shape—what we call a *cube*—has eight corners. Are you convinced now?

**I:** Does it have sides, as well as corners?

**SPHERE:** Naturally. But not what you call sides. What we call sides. You would call them solids.

**I:** And how many sides will this cube have?

**SPHERE:** How can you ask? The side of any shape is one dimension less than the shape itself. A point has zero sides. A line has two (its endpoints). A square has four. Zero, two, four—what progression is that?

**I:** Arithmetical.

**SPHERE:** And what is the next number?

**I:** Six.

**SPHERE:** Exactly. So the cube will have six sides—six of your insides. See now?

“Monster!” I cried. “Be you magician, dream, devil, or fraud—no longer will I endure your tricks. Either you or I must die!”

And with that, I lunged at him.
