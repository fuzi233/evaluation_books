In the dimly lit kitchen, the scent of roasting herbs wafted through the air, a poignant reminder of the simplicity that once defined our lives. My mother's words, laced with bitterness, hung heavy in the air as she spoke of our newfound abundance. "Better a meal of herbs where love is," she'd say, "than a feast of plenty with hatred therewith." Her words echoed the sentiments of a wise elder, a truth that few dared to speak aloud. Gone were the days when every Sunday brought a roasted joint to the table, every day a pudding to savor. Our lives had grown dull, a monotony of plenty that suffocated the joy from our meals.

The memory of those days lingered, a bittersweet nostalgia that clung to me like the steam rising from the pot on the stove. We'd eat and drink without restraint, no longer worrying about the morrow's meager rations. Yet, with each passing day, the silence at the table grew thicker, a palpable presence that seemed to suck the laughter from our midst. No longer did we tease and laugh over the simple pleasures of life – a half-pound of sausages, a pair of sweet-smelling herrings – but instead spoke in hushed tones of empty things that lay beyond our small world.

It would have been easy to escape this prison, to leave behind the stifling atmosphere and find solace in the bustling streets of Guilford. My parents would occasionally mention it, their voices laced with a desperation that I couldn't quite grasp. But the conversation would always veer off course, and the weight of our unhappiness would settle back in, like a shroud.

One day, the news arrived that old Teidelmann, a man whose name was synonymous with the counting house, had passed away. My father received a summons, his expression a mix of duty and trepidation. "You're going to her?" my mother asked, her voice tinged with a mixture of sadness and resignation. "I have been sent for," he replied, his eyes clouded with uncertainty. "It may mean business."

As he departed, my mother retreated to her room, the door locked behind her. I crept closer, straining to hear the sound of her sobs. Why would she mourn the death of a man she'd never known? The question swirled in my mind, a puzzle I couldn't solve.
