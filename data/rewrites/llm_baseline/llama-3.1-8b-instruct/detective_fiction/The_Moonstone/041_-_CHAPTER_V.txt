Betteredge had finished reading Rosanna Spearman's letter, and I waited patiently for him to join me. The scent of fresh-baked bread wafted through the air, reminding me of the simple pleasures in life. I took a deep breath, feeling the tension in my body begin to ease. 

Little by little, the image of Ezra Jennings faded from my mind, replaced by the practical concerns of the present. I thought about the plan I had devised, the one that would take me back to London and face-to-face with Mr. Bruff. The prospect of obtaining a personal interview with Rachel filled me with determination. There was still an hour before the train departed, and I hoped that Betteredge might stumble upon something crucial in the unread portion of Rosanna's letter. 

The letter itself ended with a mixture of emotions – a hint of triumph, followed by anxiety. Rosanna wrote about her fear of Sergeant Cuff searching the house, and her desperation to hide the nightgown. She confessed to wearing it, finding a fleeting sense of pleasure in doing so. The words danced across the page, painting a vivid picture of her inner turmoil. 

I recalled the lines that spoke of her encounter with Sergeant Cuff in the servants' hall. The image of the Sergeant's polite smile, his piercing gaze, lingered in my mind. I remembered the uncertainty that gripped her, the fear of being discovered and searched. As I read on, the aroma of freshly brewed tea wafted into the room, transporting me to a different era. 

The passage where she described hiding in the shrubbery, waiting for a chance to speak to me, tugged at my heartstrings. I pictured her standing alone, her eyes scanning the surroundings, her spirit unbroken despite the circumstances. And then, I came across the line where she mentioned being seen by Sergeant Cuff. A shiver ran down my spine as I realized the gravity of the situation.
