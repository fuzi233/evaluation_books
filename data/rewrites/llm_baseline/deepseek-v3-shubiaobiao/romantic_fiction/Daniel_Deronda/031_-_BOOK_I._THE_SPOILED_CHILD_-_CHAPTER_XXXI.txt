The morning of Gwendolen Harleth’s wedding dawned crisp and bright, the low sun glinting on frost-kissed leaves. The village of Pennicote turned out in force, lining the path to the church to catch a glimpse of the bridal procession. The rector, standing in as father to the bride, lent the occasion an air of solemn dignity, while the faces of only two—Mrs. Davilow and Anna—betrayed any sorrow.

Gwendolen, radiant in her bridal finery, carried herself with the poise of a woman born to grandeur. Observers murmured that she had the bearing of a lady of title, though some might have wished for more color in her cheeks to match the occasion. But Grandcourt, pale and composed, seemed to suit her perfectly. If there were whispers about his temperament—or hers—they were drowned in the day’s glittering spectacle.

*She will be happy,* the villagers assured one another. *How could she not be?*

Yet beneath Gwendolen’s dazzling composure, something thrummed like a taut string. She moved through the ceremony with the same reckless energy that had once carried her through a night of gambling—eyes bright, pulse racing, as if daring fate itself. Regret was a distant shadow, eclipsed by the sheer intoxication of the moment. She had won. She was *Mrs. Grandcourt*.

Her mother’s trembling hands and tearful embrace barely registered. Gwendolen laughed, kissed her, teased her for her sorrow. "Here I am—*Mrs. Grandcourt*," she declared, spreading her arms as if the name alone were a triumph.

And then she was gone, swept into the carriage beside her new husband, leaving behind the only life she had ever known.

---

The journey to Ryelands passed in a blur of chatter and forced gaiety. Gwendolen spoke incessantly, as if silence might betray her, while Grandcourt watched her with quiet fascination. But as the carriage rolled through the gates of the estate, her words faltered.

The house loomed ahead—stately, imposing, its windows aglow in the fading light. *Hers.* The thought should have elated her. Instead, something cold curled in her chest.

Grandcourt pressed a kiss to her lips as they crossed the threshold. She barely felt it.

Alone at last in her opulent boudoir, Gwendolen stared at her reflection in the gilded mirrors—a bride in emerald satin, surrounded by firelight and wealth. Then the housekeeper appeared, placing a small packet in her hands.

"A gift from Mr. Grandcourt," the woman murmured before withdrawing.

Gwendolen’s fingers trembled as she unwrapped it. The diamond case gleamed—but atop the jewels lay a letter.

The handwriting stopped her breath.

The words slithered into her like venom. Accusations. Curses. A promise of ruin. With a gasp, she flung the letter into the fire, watching it blacken and vanish. The diamonds spilled across the floor, winking maliciously up at her.

When Grandcourt entered, expecting a wife adorned and smiling, he found instead a woman white with terror, jewels scattered like shattered ice around her.

And then she screamed.

The sound tore through the grand house, sharp as broken glass.
