Jorian Ketel strode purposefully towards Margaret's quarters, his footsteps echoing off the stone walls. The air was heavy with the weight of secrets, and I could sense the tension building within him as he pushed open the creaking door. Inside, the flickering light cast eerie shadows on the walls, illuminating the scene before him.

There, in the midst of this somber tableau, I found Gerard, pale and still, his head cradled in Margaret's lap. Her eyes, once bright with life, now seemed dulled, her gaze fixed on some distant point beyond the confines of the room. The air was thick with her despair, a palpable force that wrapped itself around Jorian like a shroud. I watched as he approached, his movements cautious, his face a mask of concern.

"Why, where was he, then, all the time?" he asked, his voice low and measured, but Margaret did not respond. Instead, she remained frozen, her body a statue of grief, her eyes vacant, her mind lost in some dark recess of sorrow. Jorian's gaze roamed the room, taking in the scene before him, and his eyes settled on the empty chest, its lid thrown open, revealing the contents within. A glint of understanding sparked in his eyes, and he moved towards the chest, his movements deliberate.

As he examined the contents, a pang of sympathy struck him, and he turned to Margaret, his expression softening. "This is a sorry sight," he murmured, his voice heavy with compassion. "A black night's work, all for a few skins! Better have gone with us than so. She is past answering me, poor wench." His words hung in the air, a gentle rebuke, but Margaret did not stir.

Jorian's gaze drifted back to Gerard, and he reached for a small, delicate mirror, no larger than his palm. He placed it to Gerard's mouth and nostrils, holding it there for a moment before withdrawing it. The mirror, once reflective, now dulled, and Jorian's face creased in relief. "THERE IS LIFE IN HIM!" he exclaimed, his voice a whispered revelation.
