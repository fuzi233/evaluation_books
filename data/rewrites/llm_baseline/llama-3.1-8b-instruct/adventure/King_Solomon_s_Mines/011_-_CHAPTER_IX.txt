As the sun began to set on the second day of our arduous journey along Solomon's Great Road, our party found ourselves atop a windswept ridge, gazing out upon the breathtaking vista unfolding before us. The rolling hills and verdant valleys of Kukuanaland stretched out like an emerald tapestry, punctuated by clusters of sturdy kraals and the occasional burst of cultivated fields. Every able-bodied man in the land seemed to be a soldier, their presence a testament to the nation's unwavering commitment to defense and conquest.

As we rested, a sea of warriors swept past us, hastening towards Loo, the capital city, to participate in the grand annual review and festival. The sheer numbers and splendor of these troops left me awestruck – I had never witnessed such a spectacle in all my years. Our own small band, consisting of Sir Henry Curtis, Allan Quatermain, John Good, and myself, stood out amidst the throng, our foreign garb and equipment drawing curious glances from the passing warriors.

As the sky deepened into a fiery hue, we descended into the valley below, where Loo unfolded its vast expanse before us. This magnificent city, sprawling across five miles of fertile plain, was a marvel of African ingenuity, its horseshoe-shaped hill looming large in the north. A tranquil river bisected the kraal, its waters bridged at multiple points, and the distant snow-capped peaks of the Three Witches rose like sentinels on the horizon, their rugged grandeur a stark contrast to the rounded slopes of Sheba's Breasts.

Infadoos, ever observant, noticed our fascination with the mountains and offered a cryptic comment. "The road ends there," he said, his eyes darting towards the peaks. "The wise men of old once ventured into those caves, seeking secrets and treasures beyond our comprehension. Now, our kings lie entombed within the Place of Death, hidden away from prying eyes."

Intrigued, I pressed Infadoos for more information, but he deftly sidestepped the question, hinting that only those born under the stars possessed the knowledge we sought. His enigmatic smile spoke volumes, leaving me to wonder what secrets lay shrouded behind his inscrutable gaze. "My lords from the stars," he murmured, before turning to disappear into the gathering darkness.
