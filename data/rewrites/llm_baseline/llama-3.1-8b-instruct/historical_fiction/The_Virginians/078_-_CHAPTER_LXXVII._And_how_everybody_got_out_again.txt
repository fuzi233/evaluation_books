As I stood in front of the mirror, adjusting my side curls, I couldn't help but think about the various ladies who had recently caught my attention - the Duchess of Devonshire, Mrs. Crew, Mrs. Fitzherbert, and even the enigmatic Mademoiselle Hillisberg from the Opera. I smiled to myself, recalling the countless conversations I'd had with them, each one a delicate dance of wit and charm.

But as I turned to my friend Theo, I noticed a faint blush rising to her cheeks. "Have you told your mother everything, Miss Theo?" I asked, raising an eyebrow. "Every word you shared with Mr. Joe Blake in the shrubbery this morning?"

Theo's eyes sparkled with amusement as she playfully denied any involvement. "Oh, Joe Blake indeed!" she exclaimed. Meanwhile, a young woman nearby, Mademoiselle, looked up from her book, a hint of curiosity on her face. A whispered letter had arrived for her, sent through Sir Thomas's frank, and I wondered what secrets it might contain.

The Squire, ever the wit, chimed in, "We all have our secrets, n'est-ce pas? No, mademoiselle, we won't pry." Theo's mother, however, was not so easily deterred. "Tell me, papa," she coaxed, "what really transpired in the landau on the Hampstead Road that day?"

The Squire's eyes twinkled mischievously as he leaned in, a conspiratorial whisper escaping his lips. "Ah, my dear, let's just say that I did utter a single word - 'Yes.' That's all I'll say, and I leave it to your imagination to fill in the rest."

As the carriage hurtled along the Hampstead Road, I reflected on the events that had transpired. Theo had not, in fact, agreed to defy her parents' wishes, nor had she given her heart to anyone else. Her affections remained steadfastly with me, and I knew that with time, her father would come to accept our love. For now, though, I contented myself with the knowledge that her heart remained mine, and that together we would weather any storm that came our way.
