Another familiar figure—not clad in mourning but in her old red, green, and white—approached the Piazza that morning to glimpse the Carnival festivities. She came from the opposite direction, for Tessa no longer lived on the hill of San Giorgio. After the unsettling incident with Baldassarre, Tito had arranged for her to move to a quieter home near the gardens north of Porta Santa Croce.

This morning, Tessa ventured out with permission. The evening before, Tito had visited her, his weariness melting away as he played with Lillo and steadied toddling Ninna. Sensing his rare good humor, Tessa seized the moment.

*"Ninna doesn’t need me as much now,"* she began, watching the baby settle on the floor. *"Monna Lisa can mind her, and Lillo—he’s so clever, he even fetches Monna Lisa if Ninna cries!"*

Lillo—dark-eyed and golden-haired like his mother—proved his cleverness by promptly thumping Monna Lisa’s arm, making the old woman tut over her spinning.

*"A remarkable boy!"* Tito laughed.

*"Isn’t he?"* Tessa beamed, edging closer. *"And since he’s so good… might I go see the Carnival tomorrow? Just for an hour?"*

Tito pinched her cheek. *"Greedy pigeon! What does a little mother like you want with crowds?"*

*"But even little mothers like to see things!"* Her lip quivered. *"Monna Lisa said she’d go, but she’s too deaf to mind the children alone."*

*"No,"* Tito said firmly. *"You mustn’t take them into the streets. I forbid it."*

*"I’ve never disobeyed you,"* Tessa pleaded. *"Not since Holy Week. And the Carnival I remember was so beautiful—roses, singing, a king and queen…"*

*"Of a sort,"* Tito smirked. *"A line of folk as lively as cranes. But no glittering queens. Still, you might like the bonfire scaffold. Only come home early, and keep away from men with feathers—they’re fierce and fond of lopping heads."*

*"Madonna Santa!"* Tessa gasped, then giggled when she saw his teasing grin. *"I’ll avoid them! But…"* She whispered, *"Could I take Lillo? He’s so sensible!"*

*"And who’d thump Monna Lisa then?"* Tito chuckled. *"No, he’s too heavy for you in a crowd."*

Tessa sighed but brightened when Tito pressed coins into her palm. *"Confetti for the children!"* she exclaimed.

The next morning, Tessa set out in her finest—her green dress, silver-clasped belt, and a necklace Tito had given her. She didn’t think of vanity; she only knew these trinkets marked her as a cherished wife.

Unbeknownst to her, Baldassarre lurked nearby, his hollow eyes tracking her. The old man, now a yarn peddler, had followed Tito for months, nursing his vengeance. Seeing Tessa, he saw a thread to unravel Tito’s life.

---

The streets were still quiet, the Carnival not yet in full swing. Tessa paused at a peddler’s stall—red crosses for sale. She longed for one to bless her home but hesitated at the price.

*"Young woman!"* called the peddler—Bratti, an old acquaintance. *"Four quattrini for your soul’s sake!"*

*"I can’t spare it,"* Tessa admitted.

Bratti squinted, then gasped. *"Little Tessa! You’ve thrived since fleeing old Nofri!"*

*"I’m married now,"* she said proudly. *"My husband cares for me."*

*"Then spare two quattrini for a cross!"* Bratti bargained. *"Where do you live? I’ll call for payment."*

Panicked at revealing too much, Tessa hastily paid and hurried away, clutching her cross.

---

Near Via de’ Libraj, a procession approached—white-robed youths with banners. Tessa shrank aside, but they surrounded her, demanding her jewelry for the *"Bonfire of Vanities."*

*"Give up these worldly trinkets!"* one urged.

Tears welled. *"But my husband gave them!"*

Just as fear choked her, a gentle voice cut through. *"Do not be afraid."*

Tessa looked up into the face of an angel—a lady in black, with serene hazel eyes. Madonna Romola.

*"Fra Girolamo teaches giving must be free,"* Romola told the youths, dispersing them. Then she guided Tessa aside, helping hide the necklace and clasp beneath confetti in her basket.

*"Go home to your little ones,"* Romola urged. *"The Carnival is no place for you today."*

Grateful, Tessa fled, longing only for the safety of Monna Lisa’s care.

---

From the shadows, Baldassarre watched, his mind churning. Here was a tool—Tito’s noble wife, who might hate him if she knew of Tessa. Vengeance whispered: *Patience. Watch. Strike.*
