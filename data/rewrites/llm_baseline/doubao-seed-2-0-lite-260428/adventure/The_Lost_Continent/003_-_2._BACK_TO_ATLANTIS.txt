Tatho’s words were no sleeping draught that night. I lay awake staring at the pyramid’s stone ceiling, realizing I’d made a fool’s bargain: for twenty whole years, I’d buried myself so deep in building the colony of Yucatan that I’d completely lost touch with the machinations back home in Atlantis. I’d known for years that transocean mariners spoke in guarded half-truths, that no news leaked out of the imperial borders unless the Empress herself allowed it – but I’d had no patience to peel back the veil. I refused to torture men who held orders of silence from their superiors, not even for my own curiosity.

The iron discipline of our Priestly Clan left me no choice in the end. My office was stripped from me without a single moment’s warning. All my seals, all my state authority, every document that marked me as ruler of Yucatan was handed over to Tatho before the last sun of my tenure had even set. I was allowed the courtesy of one more night in the pyramid that I’d designed and raised stone by stone, but at first light I was commanded to ride for the harbour at full speed and report to Atlantis at once.

Tatho, for all his newfound vice-regal arrogance, was true to our old comrade’s bond. He was at my chambers an hour before dawn, his whole staff of servants standing behind him ready to put every resource he commanded at my disposal. I asked for almost nothing. “A single ship to take me home,” I told him. “That’s all I need. I’ll owe you the debt.”

He blinked, surprised. “You can have any ship in my fleet, but they’re fouled from the long crossing, all due for a careen. You’d crawl across the ocean at half speed. Why not take Yucatan’s own state navy? They’re anchored in the harbour right now, fine fighting galleons, built for the open sea.”

“They aren’t mine to take. They belong to the colony.”
“Deucalion, you *were* the colony. You’ve ruled it for twenty years.”

I understood what he was implying, and I rejected it stiffly. The ships were funded by public tax or owned by merchant syndicates, I told him, and I had no more claim to them than the lowest labourer in the quays. Tatho shrugged. “You know your own policies best, but it’s madness for a man in our position not to keep a private navy stashed away. You never know when a recall will drop out of nowhere. Without precautions, a lifetime of work can be looted out from under you in a dozen hours.”

“I have no fear of that,” I said coldly.
“Of course not – because you know *I’m* your friend. But if some other man had been sent to replace you? You’d have left here penniless, stripped of every coin you ever earned. Few men can resist the pull of a full treasury sitting under their hand, waiting to be emptied.”

“My Lord Tatho,” I said, “it’s clear our tastes have grown worlds apart. Every possession I’ve earned in twenty years of governing would make no man covetous. I have the rough wool robe on my back, a box of medicinal herbs, three servants: two scribes, and a burly European savage who cooks my meals and draws my bath. I drew only a common soldier’s ration from the state for all my years of service, nothing more. If any man in my name stole so much as an ounce of bronze from a Yucatan farmer, I ask you as one last favour: hang the thief for me before the week is out.”

Tatho stared at me, half amused, half bewildered. “I can’t decide if I admire you or pity you. Atlantis heard tales of your uprightness, your strict justice, but no soul guessed you’d taken it this far. Man, money is power. With gold you could outmaneuver anyone who tried to trip you up. Without it? One tiny, petty betrayal will break you before you see it coming.”

“The Gods will choose my fate.”
“Maybe. But I choose to hedge my bets. I’m not here to copy your model of governance. I’ll rule Yucatan well enough, but I’ll rule it for Tatho first. I brought my own eight private warships, my household guard, my wife and her retinue. All of them need to be provided for. Why shouldn’t the people we govern pay a fair price for the peace we give them?”

“We will never agree on that,” I said. “You hold the power now, do with it what you will. But I beg you – go easy on these people when you raise your new taxes. They’ve grown very dear to me.”

“I hate that I’ve disgusted you,” he said, and there was real regret in his voice. “But I can’t live the way you did. I have a wife and children to care for, even if you have no ties to hold you. You have no idea how to feast, man. I spent the whole long voyage across the ocean daydreaming of the grand welcome banquet you’d throw for me – and what did I get? A meal worse than what a backcountry farmer eats every night. Your European cook is a devil who burns every cut of meat. Your robes are rough as a herdsman’s, you don’t wear a single jewel to mark your rank.”

“I eat when I’m hungry,” I said. “I wear a robe until it wears through. Gluttony and silken softness never appealed to me. But we’ve talked too long. We’ve both changed over twenty years. Let’s part now, forget these differences, and hold on to the memory of the friendship we shared when we were both young officers in Atlantis.”

Tatho bowed his head. “So be it. But I will not let you leave here on a scruffy merchant galley that will get you halfway across the ocean before it falls apart. You sail on *the Bear* – my own personal flagship – and the rest of my fleet will escort you all the way home. I won’t have the man who built Yucatan walking the roads of the harbour afoot at dawn, for the crowds to see that their former ruler was sent away like a disgraced slave.”

The fleet needed a month to refit, first. They hauled out of the inner harbour, anchored in the entrance basin, and crews worked day and night to caulk the hulls, restock fresh water and salted meat, and recharge their solar drives – the secret Atlantean mariners’ tech that pulled power directly from the unclouded sun, sucking water in at the bow and ejecting it at high pressure from the stern to push the ships faster than any oared galley could ever sail, even against the wind. No more hugging coastlines now. Every ship carried a small, weighted idol balanced on the foredeck, its outstretched arm always pointing steady towards the Southern Cross, so no mariner ever lost his way even when the stars were hidden.

For the first time in twenty years, I had no work to do. I sat on the deck of the *Bear* for hours, staring back at the city I’d raised, the fields we’d cleared from the dense jungle, the pastures that fed a hundred thousand families. I hoped Tatho would not ruin all I’d built.

When we finally raised anchor and sailed east, the voyage was nothing like the slow, cautious crossings of my youth. We cut straight across the open gulf, slipped through the Carib Islands as if they were signposts on a highway, and stopped only twice to resupply with fruit and fresh water. The island natives brought their goods to the docks willingly, no fights, no skirmishes – a quiet proof of just how far Atlantis’s power stretched across the seas.

We hit brutal storms that the mariners fought through with stubborn, quiet endurance. Twice, blazing meteorites hissed down from the sky and slammed into the waves a hundred yards from our hull, sending up fountains of steam. The great sea lizards that ruled the open deeps hunted us for days, and most of the time the fleet outran them – until three of the biggest, a giant male and two smaller females, came at us in a coordinated pack, the way they’d learned to hunt human ships in recent years.

Captain Tob, master of the *Bear*, paled and ordered me to lock myself in the reinforced aftercastle. He said Tatho had sworn he would feed him to the lizards alive if anything happened to me, and he was not about to risk that. But I refused. I wanted to see the fight for myself.

Arrows bounced off the lizards’ thick, bull-like hides. Thrown fire didn’t even singe their scales. Only twenty axes hitting the same exact spot at once could force their heads back, with barely a scratch to show for the effort. The lizards reared up, their necks as tall as the ship’s masts, their jaws foaming, their stench choking the whole deck. I grabbed a sharp-tipped lance from a sailor’s hand, shouldered past Tob’s guards, and baited the nearest of the females. I dodged as she lunged for me, and drove the full length of the lance straight into her small, deep-set, vulnerable eye. The shaft snapped off in my hand. She screamed so loud the waves rippled, thrashed in circles, and fled for the open horizon, blind and bleeding.

Tob, desperate to get the other two beasts off our hull, deliberately shoved two strong sailors into their waiting jaws. The monsters feasted for a few minutes, then turned and swam after their wounded pack mate.

I leaned against the taffrail, breathing hard, the taste of salt and lizard slime still in my mouth. Somewhere ahead of us, hidden just over the curve of the horizon, lay Atlantis. The old intrigues, the court politics, the reason the Priests had recalled me all the way home from the colony I’d loved – all of it was waiting, and I knew the dangers ahead would be far deadlier than any sea lizard. But I smiled. The Gods had kept me alive across the ocean. I would not go down easily.
