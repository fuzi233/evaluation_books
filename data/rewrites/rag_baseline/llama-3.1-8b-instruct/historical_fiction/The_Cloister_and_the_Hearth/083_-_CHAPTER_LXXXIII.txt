As I sit amidst the flickering candlelight, the scent of simmering butter and eggs wafts through the air, transporting me to a moment when hope and desperation intertwined like the tender shoots of a young vine. It was a time when my own heart was heavy with worry, weighed down by the uncertainty of my own future. My friend Margaret, a steadfast companion in those trying times, had just received a letter from her ailing father, his words a poignant reminder of the fragility of life. As she poured out her emotions onto the page, her faith in his vision, or perhaps an illusion, still lingered, like the faint glow of embers.

When the letter was finished, and her brother Luke departed, Margaret's optimism began to falter, and she was left to confront the darker realities of her situation. Catherine, ever the scolder, was quick to remind her of the price of false hopes and elevated spirits – the descent into despondency that followed. Yet, it was in this very state of despair that a loyal friend, Joan Ketel, appeared, radiant with an idea that would alter the course of events.

"Margaret, I know the cure for your ills," Joan declared, her eyes shining with conviction. "The hermit of Gouda, a wise and holy man, possesses the gift of foresight. He can reveal to us what the future holds, if we seek him out."

Though Margaret had heard of the hermit, she had given up hope, her voice laced with resignation. Undeterred, Joan coaxed her into accompanying her to the hermit's cave, armed with a basket containing the finest butter and eggs. The journey was not without significance, for it was during this time that Margaret had forged a deep bond with Joan, a connection born from their shared experiences and trust.

Their friendship had been forged in the fire of adversity, as they navigated the trials and tribulations of life together. Just six weeks prior, Joan's husband, Jorian, had fallen gravely ill with a debilitating bowel disease, his condition worsening with each passing day. The doctors had all but given up hope, suggesting that boiling a chicken with a golden angel in the water might provide some relief. However, even this remedy had failed, and the family was on the brink of despair.
