As the last wisps of daylight dissolved into the winter evening, the sleepy town of Cloisterham stirred to life. The air was alive with the sweet scent of holly and the faint tang of woodsmoke carried on the breeze. Red berries glowed like rubies in the lattices of Minor Canon Corner, and the sound of laughter and chatter echoed from the bustling streets. In the Cathedral Close, the rustling leaves underfoot seemed to whisper secrets of Christmases past, their crunching footsteps a reminder of the passing years.

In the midst of this festive bustle, Neville Landless sat in his quiet room, surrounded by the familiar comforts of his books and papers. With the permission of his tutor, Mr. Crisparkle, he had been granted a rare holiday from his studies, and he spent the morning lost in the pages of a favorite volume. As the hours slipped away, he set aside his book and turned his attention to tidying his quarters. His table was cleared, his books were arranged, and his scattered notes were carefully destroyed, leaving behind only the most essential reminders of his studies.

With his room in order, Neville turned to the task of preparing for his journey. He packed a small knapsack with a few essentials – a change of sturdy shoes and socks, a warm cloak – and added a sturdy walking stick, its iron-shod tip gleaming in the fading light. He tried the weight of the stick, swinging it experimentally, before laying it alongside the knapsack on a windowsill. As he worked, his mind wandered to the meeting that lay ahead, and the promise of adventure that awaited him.

The sound of the Cathedral clock tolled through the streets, marking the hour, and Neville rose to his feet. He dressed with care, his movements deliberate and unhurried, as if savoring the quiet moments before the night's events unfolded. With his preparations complete, he stood before the window, his eyes fixed on the darkness beyond, his heart beating with anticipation. Tonight, three paths would converge at the gatehouse, and Neville's fate would be forever changed.
