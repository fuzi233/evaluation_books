As the rain-soaked night wrapped around the temple, I let myself surrender to the allure of sleep, Nais keeping watch beside me. Hours ticked by, and the darkness outside grew thick with the smell of wet earth and woodsmoke. Our plan was to bide our time until the camp had settled into slumber, allowing me to slip away unnoticed towards the sea. But fate had other plans.

A lone figure began to blow a mournful tune on a horn or shell, its harsh notes piercing the night air. The sound drew closer, accompanied by the gruff voices of the camp dwellers, complaining about the disturbance. As the music reached a crescendo, the voices grew louder, and the crowd began to stir, gathering around a figure perched atop a war engine. Though we couldn't quite make out what he was saying, Nais whispered that it was likely some fiery oration that would only serve to enrage those who didn't sleep.

Our quiet evening was thus disrupted, and I felt a pang of disappointment. However, Nais's gaze locked onto the commotion, her eyes narrowing in consideration. "If all the non-sleepers gather to listen to this fellow," she murmured, "it will be simplicity itself for me to slip away unnoticed." Yet, she didn't seem inclined to take advantage of this opportunity. Instead, she rose from her seat, gesturing for me to follow.

"What excuse shall we use?" I asked, though I knew the answer already.

"There's no need for one," she replied, her voice tinged with frustration. "They'll assume we're lovers, and that's a common enough thing here. Just keep your distance from the firelight."

As we stepped out into the rain, a group of rough-looking individuals approached us, their faces illuminated by the flickering flames. They called out for Nais to join the gathering, citing her eloquence and the need for someone to counter the preacher's words. I felt a surge of unease as Nais hesitated, her jaw set in determination.

Without a word, she took my hand, leading me deeper into the darkness. The rain pounded against our skin as we navigated the winding path, the crowd pressing in behind us. The preacher's voice carried on the wind, his words lost to the din of the crowd. I felt a sense of disquiet building within me, as if the very fabric of our carefully laid plans was unraveling.
