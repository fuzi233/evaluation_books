Its presence haunts me, drawing me back time and again through the labyrinthine streets, past the cacophony of vendors and pedestrians, the flickering naphtha lamps casting eerie shadows on the faces of the crowd. The air is heavy with the smells of smoke, sweat, and desperation, yet in this desolate corner, something about the house seems almost... inviting.

Built during a bygone era, when this street was a rural lane winding along the marshy outskirts, the house stands apart from its neighbors, a relic of a more refined past. A once-manicured garden now lies barren and still, its stones cracked and worn, clotheslines stretching out like skeletal fingers, a poignant reminder of better times. The porch, too, remains, its stucco façade worn and weathered, like the stories it could tell if only the walls could speak.

As night descends, and the city's din subsides, I find myself drawn to this place, like a moth to flame. The street, once a hub of activity, falls silent, save for the occasional cry of a vendor hawking coals – a melancholic refrain that hangs in the air like a dirge. In the evening's hush, the house transforms, its rough exterior softened by the shadows, its windows glowing like lanterns in the dark.

As I stand before the broken railings, a sense of longing washes over me. I am a wanderer, drawn to this place, where memories wait like old friends. And as I pause, lost in thought, a figure emerges from the gloom, its features illuminated by the faint light of the nearby lamp. The face, etched with lines of age and experience, is a map of the stories it holds. I turn to the house, and it speaks to me in a low, rumbling voice, its words a gentle breeze on a summer's day.
