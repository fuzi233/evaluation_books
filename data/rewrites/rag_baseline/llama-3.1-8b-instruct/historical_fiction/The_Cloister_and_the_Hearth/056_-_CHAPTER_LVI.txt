As I navigated the labyrinthine alleys of Rome, the scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, mingling with the pungent aroma of ink and parchment. Gerard's modest lodging on the west bank of the Tiber River became his sanctuary, a place to retreat from the harsh realities of his craft. Every day, he would set out in search of work, carrying a specimen of his writing to every shop he could find that commissioned such tasks.

The tradespeople received him with a mixture of disdain and curiosity, their faces a map of skepticism. "Our letters are written with precision," one said, scrutinizing the lines on the page. "Your ink is too dark," another chimed in, her eyes narrowing. But the dominant refrain was, "What use is this? Latin is all we need here. Can you not write in the language of the gods?"

Gerard borrowed a beautiful Greek manuscript at a steep price, feeling the weight of his empty purse, but his spirit remained unbroken. In a fortnight, he had made tremendous strides in mastering the Greek script. To make the most of his newfound skill, he devoted himself to working until noon, and then ventured out to hunt for customers.

However, the traders were unforgiving. When he presented them with a specimen of his Greek writing, they dismissed it as unsellable, citing the abundance of works flooding in from across Europe. Gerard should have arrived a year earlier, they claimed. Deflated, he returned to his lodging, his mind reeling with the prospect of failure.

It was then that his landlady, a woman with a warm smile and a keen understanding of human nature, intervened. She invited him to dine with her, and as they sat together, she probed the depths of his disappointment. Gerard confided in her, and she offered her candid assessment. "Those merchants," she said, "are cunning, for they have writers in their employ, whom they reward handsomely and pay poorly. No wonder they rebuff you. You write too well, my dear. I know this because you leave your door unlocked, and women are naturally curious."

Gerard sought clarification, and she explained further. "Your exceptional writing threatens to eclipse the work they sell. They fear it will supplant their own creations." Gerard sighed, touched by her insight. "You see people for what they truly are, despite your own kind heart."
