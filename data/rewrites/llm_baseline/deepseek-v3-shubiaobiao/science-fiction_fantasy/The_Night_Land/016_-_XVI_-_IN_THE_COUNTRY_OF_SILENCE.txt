The Mighty Pyramid loomed before me, a mountain of light in the eternal dark, its vast bulk rising like a beacon of life against the consuming night. Yet despair clung to me, a weight heavier than the miles I had carried her. Naidok, my beloved, lay still in my arms, her breath shallow, her spirit flickering. So close to salvation—yet still too far.

I pressed forward, my stride unbroken, when a figure emerged from the hollow of a fire-pit—a hulking, hair-covered man, his hands monstrous with claws like a beast’s. He lunged, eager, ravenous.

I set Naidok down swiftly, my heart cold with fury. No delay. No mercy. I struck, but the brute twisted aside, seized my helm, and wrenched it free, flinging me backward. I rolled, unharmed but shaken, and surged to my feet as the Diskos roared to life in my grip. One swing—the blade sheared through him as if he were mist, leaving his halves to crumple grotesquely in the firelight.

No pause. I snatched Naidok up and ran.

The night trembled with unseen watchers. The Millions whispered their awe, their hope, their terror. Then, from the blackness, more shapes slithered—twisted things from the rocks, the brush, the shadows. I cut through them, a relentless storm, my mind numb but my body sharp with desperation. The land itself seemed to wake, snarling, howling, laughing in the dark.

And then—the baying.

The Night-Hounds.

Close now, too close, their cries tearing through the silence. A mile? Less? I ran harder, my gaze locked on the Pyramid, its light dimming as if the Earth-Current itself faltered.

A flash—blue, searing—split the sky. The Humans had unleashed the Current upon the hounds. The world erupted in flame and thunder, the ground rupturing, rocks hurled like shrapnel. I staggered, shielding Naidok, then forced myself onward. The silence afterward was deafening.

But the land was not done.

Creatures lurched from the dark, blocking my path. Tusked, squat, their fingers grasping. The Hundred Thousand watched from the Circle, helpless, for beyond its weakened glow swayed the Black Mounds—living Evil, waiting to devour any who stepped out.

I fought like a thing possessed, the Diskos a blur. Blood sprayed, bodies fell. The Hundred Thousand shouted, hurled their own blades into the fray. I broke through—stumbled—crossed the Circle.

Hands reached but did not touch me. Silence fell.

A Master of the Diskos saluted. A physician came.

Naidok was laid upon the earth.

A breath. A pause. A quiet shake of the doctor’s head.

Dead.

The word echoed in my bones.

I lifted her again, cradling her for the last march through the lane of grey-armored men, their Diskoi reversed in salute. The Great Gate groaned open. The Masters stood in silence, the Monstruwacan at their head, his face stricken.

They knew.

And so I carried her home.
