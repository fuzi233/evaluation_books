As Gerard strolled alongside Teresa, the warm sunlight danced across his face, casting a golden glow on the cobblestone streets of Rome. He pondered the enigmatic woman beside him, her dark eyes sparkling like polished onyx as she navigated the crowded alleys. In her wake, a tantalizing aroma wafted from a nearby bakery, enticing Gerard's senses with the sweet scent of freshly baked bread. His stomach growled in anticipation, and he wondered if perhaps they might stop for a snack.

Teresa, however, seemed oblivious to the allure of the bakery, her focus fixed on securing a position for Gerard. A friend had informed her of a working goldsmith's wife seeking a writer, and she led him to the modest shop with a confident stride. Inside, the goldsmith's wife, Olympia, greeted them with a warm smile, but her response was less than encouraging. "Ah, I'm afraid I don't need a writer," she said, her voice tinged with a hint of distraction. "But I recall that my acquaintance, Claelia, the esteemed sausage-maker, might be interested. She mentioned it to me just the other day."

Undeterred, Teresa launched into a charming speech, her words dripping with honeyed sweetness, before bidding Olympia farewell and leading Gerard to Claelia's residence. As they walked, the scent of sizzling meat and spices wafted through the air, making Gerard's mouth water. Claelia's home was nestled in a quiet alley, its entrance flanked by a pair of potted herbs that seemed to be carefully tended. Upon their arrival, Teresa announced their presence with a gentle knock, and Claelia welcomed them warmly, her eyes lighting upon Gerard with a spark of interest.

"Ah, my dear friend, I've heard from Olympia that you're looking for a writer," Claelia said, her hands fluttering as she spoke. "And I've brought you a talented one – he saved my child from the treacherous waves. For the sake of our Lady, please look upon him favorably." Her words were laced with sincerity, and Gerard felt a surge of gratitude towards her.

However, Claelia's response was similar to Olympia's – a polite rejection, accompanied by a reminder that another woman, Appia Claudia, the tailor, might be in need of a writer.
