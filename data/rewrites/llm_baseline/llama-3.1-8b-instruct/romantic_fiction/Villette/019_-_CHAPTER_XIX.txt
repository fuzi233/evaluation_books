As I lingered at La Terrasse beyond the scheduled end of my vacation, Mrs. Bretton's kindness extended my stay by another fortnight. Her son, Dr. John, had decreed that I wasn't yet strong enough to return to the pensionnat, and so she promptly paid a visit to the directress, securing the indulgence of a longer convalescence on grounds of rest and change being essential for my full recovery. However, this reprieve brought with it an unexpected visit from Madame Beck – an encounter I might have gladly avoided, had I the choice.

One sunny afternoon, Madame Beck arrived in a fiacre as far as the château, seemingly intent on satisfying her curiosity about Dr. John's abode. Upon beholding the charming setting and tasteful interior, she was effusive in her praise, extolling the blue salon as a "pièce magnifique," lavishing congratulations upon me for my fortunate acquisition of such worthy friends, and even deigning to offer a gracious compliment in my direction. As Dr. John entered the room, Madame Beck approached him with unbridled enthusiasm, launching into a torrent of felicitations and expressions of admiration for his "château," his mother, and himself. Her words sparkled with warmth, her French as fluent and ornate as ever. For a fleeting instant, Madame shone with an uncharacteristic brilliance, radiating an aura of delight and affability. Yet, as I accompanied her to her carriage and watched her settle in, a transformation took hold. Her expression turned stern, her demeanor grave, and for an instant, she resembled a sage or a judge. This enigmatic side of Madame Beck fascinated me.

Upon returning to the château, I playfully teased Dr. John about Madame's effusive behavior towards him. His eyes twinkled with mirth as he recalled some of her more florid phrases and mimicked her animated delivery. Dr. John possessed a keen sense of humor, and when he could set aside his preoccupations, he was the most delightful of companions – though, I noted, his thoughts often strayed to Miss Fanshawe.

The phrase "to sit in sunshine calm and sweet" holds a particular allure for those who are weak and vulnerable. During Georgette Beck's illness, I would take her in my arms and stroll with her beneath the verdant wall hung with ripening grapes, basking in the warmth of the Southern sun.
