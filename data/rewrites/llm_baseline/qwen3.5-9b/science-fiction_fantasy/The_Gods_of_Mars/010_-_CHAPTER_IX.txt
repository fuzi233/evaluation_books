The confession of love, wrung from the girl by her terror, struck me with a profound depth, yet it left me humiliated. I realized too late that some careless word or action on my part had led her to believe I shared her affections. I had never been adept at courting; my passions lay in combat and the martial arts, pursuits I deemed far more fitting for a man than lingering over a perfumed glove ill-fitting his hand or kissing a withered bloom that now smelled faintly of rot. Paralyzed by confusion, I knew not how to proceed. A thousand times I preferred confronting the savage hordes of the Dead Sea's floor to meeting the gaze of this stunning youth and admitting the truth I owed her. Yet, there was no alternative. Reluctantly, and with clumsy grace, I gently freed her hands from my neck, clasping them in mine to steady her. "Listen," I began, recounting my devotion to Dejah Thoris. Of all the women I had known across two worlds throughout my long existence, she alone had claimed my heart.

My words failed to soothe her. Instead, she erupted like a tigress, springing panting to her feet. Her beautiful visage twisted into a mask of horrific malice, her eyes blazing with fury. "Dog!" she hissed. "Blasphemer! Do you suppose Phaidor, daughter of Matai Shang, begs for mercy? She commands! What is your petty, foreign passion for the vile creature you chose in your other life compared to the honor bestowed upon you by Phaidor? She has gloried you with her love, and you have spurned it. Ten thousand atrocities cannot atone for the insult you have inflicted. The woman you call Dejah Thoris shall die the most gruesome death imaginable. You have signed her warrant of doom. As for you... you shall become the meanest slave in the service of the goddess you sought to humble. Torture and shame shall pile upon you until you grovel at my feet begging for release. In my 'gracious' generosity, I shall grant your final prayer: from the high balcony of the Golden Cliffs, I shall watch as the great white apes tear you limb from limb."

She had orchestrated the entire tragedy perfectly—a flawless program from beginning to end. It stunned me to realize that one so divinely beautiful could harbor such fiendish vindictiveness. However, I perceived a fatal flaw in her vengeance: she had forgotten her immediate reality. Without intending to disrupt her plans further, merely to allow her to adjust them to practical necessity, I pointed to the nearest porthole. Clearly oblivious to her surroundings and her precarious situation, a single glance at the dark, churning waters outside caused her to collapse onto a low bench. Buried face-deep in her arms, she wept—not as an all-powerful deity, but as a broken, miserable child.

Down, down we sank until the thick glass of the portholes grew hot from the external pressure. We were deep beneath the Martian crust. Our descent halted, replaced by the roar of propellers thrashing through water at our stern, pushing us forward at high velocity. Darkness enveloped us, save for the glow of our lights and the beam of a powerful searchlight mounted on the submarine's bow, illuminating a narrow, rock-lined tunnel. Minutes later, the engines died, and we ascended swiftly toward the surface. Light flooded in, and soon we stopped.

Xodar entered the cabin with his men. "Come," he ordered, and we followed him through a hatch opened by a sailor. We emerged into a small subterranean vault where our submarine floated, its black hull visible just above a central pool. A level platform circled the pool, leading to walls that rose vertically before arching toward a low ceiling. Pierced with entrances to dimly lit passages, the cavern seemed alive with purpose. Our captors guided us toward one such passage, halting before a steel cage at the base of a shaft stretching endlessly upward. This was a standard Martian elevator, operated by massive magnets suspended overhead, regulating magnetic volume to control speed. On Mars, where gravity is weak, the ascent was terrifyingly fast, offering little resistance to the powerful pull above.

Scarcely had the car door closed than we surged upward, stopping abruptly at the summit. Emerging from the terminal building, we stepped into a veritable fairyland of impossible beauty. Language fails to capture the splendor of this realm. One might speak of scarlet grass, ivory trees blooming with violet fire, paths paved with crushed rubies, emeralds, turquoises, and diamonds. Before us stood a magnificent temple of burnished gold, wrought with intricate designs. Yet, words cannot describe the glorious colors unseen by earthly eyes, nor the scintillations of rays emitted by thousands of nameless Martian jewels. Even my eyes, hardened by the barbaric opulence of a Jeddak's court, were awestruck.

"The Temple of Issus," Phaidor whispered, her eyes wide with wonder.

Xodar watched us with a grim smile, mixing amusement with malicious glee. The gardens teemed with brilliantly captured Black Men and Women, while Red and White females attended to their every need. Princesses and goddesses from the Outer Worlds and the Temples of the Therns had been stolen, leaving the Blacks with new slaves. Moving through this spectacle toward the temple, we were stopped at the main entrance by a line of armed guards. Xodar conferred briefly with an officer, and together they entered the sanctuary.

When they returned, they announced that Issus wished to behold the daughter of Matai Shang and the strange creature from another world, formerly a Prince of Helium. Led through corridors of unimaginable beauty, past magnificent apartments and noble halls, we were halted in a vast chamber. An officer signaled, and a richly adorned courtier emerged. We were ordered to drop to our hands and knees, backs turned to the room we were to enter. Never had I occupied such a humiliating posture; only my love for Dejah Thoris and the desperate hope of seeing her again prevented me from rising to face the Goddess of the First Born like a gentleman, ready to spill my blood.

After crawling hundreds of feet in disgust, our escort stopped. "Let them rise," a thin, wavering voice commanded from behind us. "But do not face Issus."

"The woman pleases me," the voice echoed after a pause. "She shall serve me for the allotted time. The man you may return to the Isle of Shador, against the northern shore of the Sea of Omean. Let the woman turn and gaze upon Issus, knowing that those of the lower orders who behold the holy vision of her radiant face survive the blinding glory for but a single year."

I glanced at Phaidor. She had turned a ghastly pale. Slowly, irresistibly drawn, she rotated to face the deity. Standing close enough that her bare arm brushed mine, I felt the violent shudder coursing through her trembling flesh. *It must be dazzling loveliness,* I thought, *to evoke such emotion in the breast of one so radiant.*

"Let the woman remain. Remove the man. Go," Issus decreed.

The officer's heavy hand gripped my shoulder. Dropping to my knees once more, I crawled out of the Presence. My first audience with a god, I confessed, left me unimpressed except for the ridiculous figure I cut scrambling on my marrow bones. Outside, the doors closed, and I was bid to rise. Joining Xodar, we retraced our steps to the gardens.

"You spared my life when you easily might have taken it," Xodar said quietly after a moment of silence. "I would aid you if I could. Your fate is inevitable; you may never return to the outer world."

"What will be my fate?" I asked.

"That depends largely on Issus. So long as she does not summon you to reveal her face, you may live in a mild form of bondage as I can arrange. But why would she summon you?"

"She often uses the lower orders for amusement. A fighter like you would provide excellent sport in the temple's monthly rites—men versus men, men versus beasts, for the edification of Issus and the replenishment of her larder."

"She eats human flesh?" I asked.

"Not in horror," Xodar replied, noting my recent familiarity with the Holy Therns. "Since acquiring knowledge of them, I am prepared for anything in this inaccessible heaven, where ages of narrow fanaticism have eradicated all humanitarian instinct. They are drunk with power, viewing other Martians as beasts. Why should they not eat the flesh of the lower orders, whom they understand no better than we understand the cattle we slaughter?"

"She consumes only the flesh of the best-bred Holy Therns and Red Barsoomians. The rest feeds our boards; the animals feed the slaves. She also enjoys other delicacies."

I did not then grasp the significance of "other delicacies," assuming the limits of ghoulishness had been reached. I was wrong.

As we neared the garden chambers, an officer intercepted us. "Issus wishes to view this man again. The girl reported he slew seven of the First Born and, bare-handed, bound Dator Xodar with his own harness."

Xodar looked uneasy, clearly displeased that Issus knew of his ignoble defeat. Without protest, he turned, and we followed the officer back to the sealed doors of the Audience Chamber.
