To understand the geography of my drifting, one must first map the absolute squalor of my universe at the age of seven. I existed within a microscopic empire of two, subjugated by a man I was conditioned to call "Master." He was a square-built, brutal artifact of a world I did not know, roughly sixty years of age, though time on the island seemed measured only by the decay of his humanity. His dark hair fell in matted, feral clusters, and a vast beard obscured his face, rendering him less a man than a beast draped in the ruin of whatever civilization had cast him out. He possessed a terrifying latent power, yet he spent his days in a state of stagnant lethargy, only stirring to exert his dominance over my labor—collecting sea-birds or hauling faggots of wood down the ravine.

Our lives were bound by the daily ritual of bathing in a salt-water pool, barricaded from the shark-infested ocean by a ridge of jagged stone. Beyond this, my existence was an enforced void. I was permitted no education, no heritage, and almost no language. The Master utilized silence as a tool of oppression, rationing his words to commands and curses, ensuring my mind remained as barren as the rocky crags that caged us. I became a taciturn creature, my intellect starved, left to gaze into the abyssal depths of the ocean or trace the cold geometries of the stars. I was kept as a beast of the field—valuable only for the calories my labor saved him, and perhaps to serve as a mirror for his own desperate need for mastery.

I left him, and commenced a careful descent of the precipices by which we were surrounded, but, before I had gone fifty paces, another flash of lightning was followed up by a loud shriek, which arrested my steps. Where the noise came from I could not tell, but I heard my companion calling to me to come back. I obeyed him, and found him standing where I had left him.

"You called me, master?"

"Yes, I did; take my hand and lead me to the cabin."

I obeyed him, wondering why he asked me so to do. He gained his bed-place, and threw himself down on it.

"Bring the kid full of water," said he--"quick!"

I brought it, and he bathed his head and face. After a time, he threw himself back upon the bed-place, and groaned heavily.

"O God! It's all over with me," said he at last. "I shall live and die in this cursed hole."

"What's the matter, master?" said I.

He offered no answer, sinking instead into a rhythmic liturgy of curses and moans. When he finally fell still, I slipped out again into the dying tempest.
