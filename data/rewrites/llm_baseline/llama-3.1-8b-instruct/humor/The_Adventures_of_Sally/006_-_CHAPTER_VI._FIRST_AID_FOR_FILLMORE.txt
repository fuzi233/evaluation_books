As the Saturday morning sunlight streamed through the windows of the Hotel Statler, Sally finally found herself in Detroit, a city she had grown to associate with the vibrant sounds of its music scene. Her arrival had been delayed due to nursing duties for the ailing Mr. Faucitt, and now, as she sipped her second cup of coffee in the hotel's dining room, she couldn't shake off the feeling of fatigue. Moreover, her spirits were dampened by Gerald's lackluster response to her phone call earlier that morning. His voice had lacked the usual warmth, leaving her wondering if he truly cared about her return after such a long absence.

The gentle buzz of the coffee maker and the soft hum of conversation from the other diners in the room seemed to lull her into a state of melancholy. Yet, as she poured the steaming liquid into her cup, she sensed a renewal of energy coursing through her veins. Perhaps, she thought, a cold shower bath would work wonders for Gerald too, reviving him from his apparent lethargy.

Just then, a young man with a familiar face walked into the dining room, his eyes scanning the space as if searching for someone. Sally's gaze met his, and she felt a jolt of surprise. It was her brother Fillmore, and she hadn't expected to see him here, thousands of miles away from their New York home. As he spotted her, he made his way towards her table, his expression a mixture of nervousness and embarrassment.

"Sally?" he asked, his voice barely above a whisper. She sensed a guilty conscience behind his demeanor, and her mind began to wander to the possibility of him keeping secrets from her. "What brings you here?" he asked, his eyes darting around the room as if searching for an escape route.

"I just got back a week ago," she replied, trying to keep her tone light. "I've been taking care of poor Mr. Faucitt; he's been quite ill. I'm here to see Mr. Foster's new play, 'The Primrose Way.' Have you heard anything about it?"

Fillmore's expression turned awkward. "Actually, it hasn't opened yet. They've shut down all the theaters due to the Spanish flu outbreak. You must have seen it in the papers."
