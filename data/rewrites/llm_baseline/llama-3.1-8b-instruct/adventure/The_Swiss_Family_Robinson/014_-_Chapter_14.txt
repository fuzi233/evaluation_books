As I put the finishing touches on my latest pottery piece, the ominous signs of another harsh winter were unmistakable – the darkening skies and turbulent storms that heralded the onset of the rainy season. The days grew shorter, and our daily excursions were reluctantly abandoned. Even within the spacious confines of our home, the time began to drag, weighed down by the monotony of our routines. The children's usual exuberance was subdued, and the occasional respite from the rain, though welcome, offered only temporary relief. Fritz, ever the visionary, approached me with a proposition: 'Father, why not create a canoe, one that surpasses our current vessels in both speed and maneuverability? A lightweight skiff would allow me to glide effortlessly across the water.'

His words resonated with the rest of us, and the idea quickly took hold. However, my wife, ever the voice of caution, expressed her reservations about adding another watercraft to our inventory, fearing it would only increase our chances of meeting the unforgiving sea. Her concerns were assuaged when I assured her that the new boat would be sturdy and reliable, inspired by the robust design of the Greenlander's cajack. I envisioned a vessel that would engage the children and yield a masterpiece of craftsmanship.

The project soon consumed our attention, and we set to work constructing the framework from whalebone, reinforced with split bamboo canes to fortify the sides and create a solid deck. The resulting design allowed for a generous seating area, a feature I deemed essential for comfort and safety. As the rain subsided, we moved our workshop outside, utilizing the open space to complete the canoe's assembly.

With the cajack taking shape, we turned our attention to its waterproofing, sealing the seams with caoutchouc and encasing the entire vessel in seal-skin. The finished product was a marvel of its kind – lightweight and buoyant, it seemed almost weightless, gliding effortlessly across the water when finally launched. Fritz was unanimously declared the rightful owner of the cajack, but my wife insisted on creating a specialized swimming dress, one that would safeguard him in case of an emergency. Though I harbored doubts about the garment's effectiveness, I agreed to assist her in its construction.
