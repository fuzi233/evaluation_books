As the carriage wound its way through the winter landscape, George Esmond gazed out at the frozen world with a mixture of disinterest and boredom. His mother, Madam Esmond, sat beside him, her face a mask of concern etched with worry lines. Their tutor, Mr. Dempster, rode ahead, his dark figure silhouetted against the pale sun. The snow-covered trees passed by in a blur, their branches like skeletal fingers reaching towards the sky. George's thoughts were consumed by the memory of his recent illness, the fever that had wracked his body and left him shaken. The words he had screamed in the dead of night still lingered in his mind: "Broken! Broken! It never, never can be mended!" He pushed the memory aside, focusing instead on the rhythmic creaking of the carriage wheels and the soft murmur of his mother's voice as she conversed with Mr. Dempster.

Their destination, New York, beckoned, a promise of adventure and new experiences. But George felt no sense of excitement, only a dull resignation. He had always been a solitary child, content to lose himself in the world of music, playing the violin with a passion that bordered on obsession. His voice, once a clear treble, had changed to a deep bass, a reminder that he was growing up, leaving behind the carefree days of childhood. As he looked out at the passing landscape, he felt a pang of sadness, a sense of loss for the boy he once was.

The weeks passed, and the Esmonds traveled up the Hudson River to Albany, where they were received by the local gentry. George's awkwardness in social situations was a source of amusement for the French ladies, who found his stilted attempts at conversation endearing. But George was not interested in making friends; he preferred to observe, to listen and learn. He spent hours practicing his French, pouring over books and listening to the lilting rhythms of the language. He danced the minuet with a elegance that belied his youth, his movements fluid and precise. And he played the violin with a passion that transported him to another world, a world of beauty and wonder.
