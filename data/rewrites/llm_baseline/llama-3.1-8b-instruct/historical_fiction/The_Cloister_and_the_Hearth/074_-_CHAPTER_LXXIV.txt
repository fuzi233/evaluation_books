As the sun rose over the rolling hills, Clement, a humble Dominican friar, emerged from the cloister, his eyes cast downward in contemplation. The air was crisp with the scent of freshly baked bread wafting from the nearby village oven. He had received a message from the Vatican, requesting his presence at the University of Basle, where he would teach languages to the eager minds of the students. Though his visit to England was temporarily postponed, Clement felt a sense of purpose in his new assignment.

As he walked through the northern gate, the friar's thoughts turned to the recent loss of Margaret, whose passing had shaken him to his core. He had never been one to seek solace in prayer, but now, he felt an insatiable longing to connect with the divine. Yet, humility held him back; he feared that his own stained soul was unworthy to intercede for Margaret's innocent spirit. Instead, he implored his fellow monks, Anselm and others, to offer prayers on her behalf.

The following Sunday, Clement arrived in a small Italian town, where he took to the pulpit to share his message of faith. As he spoke, a sense of peace settled within him, and he felt the warmth of God's forgiveness. That day, he finally found the courage to pray for Margaret's soul, and from then on, it became a daily ritual, a thread connecting him to the world beyond the cloister.

As he continued his journey, Clement encountered a young man writhing in agony, his body contorted by the effects of a suspected tarantula bite. The village musicians had only exacerbated the poor man's suffering with their lively tunes. Clement, however, had an idea. He called for a psaltery and began to play soothing melodies, hoping to calm the patient's fevered mind. Though the music initially seemed to have the opposite effect, the friar persisted, eventually noticing that the young man's lips were moving. As he leaned in closer, he realized that the patient was whispering a soft, mournful tune.
