As the warm breeze danced across the verandah, Ethne settled into her chair beside Willoughby, but her promise to let him speak remained unkept. "I am Deputy-Governor of Suakin," he began, his voice carrying out over the tranquil waters of the harbour. "My chief was away on leave in May, and I'm sure you're fortunate not to know Suakin during that time, Miss Eustace. The heat is oppressive, a thick, heavy mist that clings to your skin and refuses to release its hold. I was sitting on the verandah, sipping tea and gazing out at the distillation works, when a servant approached me about a man who wished to speak with me. That man was none other than Harry Feversham, a man I hadn't expected to see again so soon."

Ethne's eyes sparkled with interest, and she leaned forward, her hands clasped together. "How did he look?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Willoughby furrowed his brow, searching for the right words. "To be honest, I'm not quite sure," he admitted. "He'd been in the Soudan for a couple of years, and he seemed...different. A bit worn, perhaps, but still the same man I remembered."

Ethne's face fell, and she let out a gentle sigh. Five years had passed since she'd last heard from Harry, and the silence had been almost deafening. She longed to know if he'd changed, if his smile still lit up a room, if his laughter could still charm the birds from the trees. But Captain Willoughby's expression told her that such details were lost on him, and she nodded graciously.

"Please, do go on," she encouraged.

Willoughby cleared his throat and continued. "Feversham explained that he'd received a packet of letters from General Gordon in Khartoum. They'd been carried down the Nile as far as Berber, but the town had fallen to the Mahdists before they could reach their destination. Abou Fatma, the messenger, had hidden them in the wall of an Arab's house, where they remained until Feversham tracked them down. I examined the letters and found them to be of little consequence, so I asked Feversham why he'd risked so much to retrieve them."
