As the sun dipped towards the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the Tuscan hills, Romola strolled through the quiet streets of Florence, her feet carrying her towards the ancient hill of San Miniato. Her thoughts, however, lingered elsewhere, amidst the memories of the day's events. The whispered conversations, the veiled glances, and the weight of unspoken secrets had all conspired to bring her to this moment, alone, yet not alone. For she knew that Baldassarre followed, his footsteps silent as a ghost's, as he had been following her for some time now.

"I would speak with you," he said, his voice low and measured, as Romola slowed her pace, sensing his presence behind her. She turned to face him, her eyes narrowing slightly as she searched for any sign of the turmoil that brewed within him.

"Yes," she replied, her tone neutral, yet inviting. "But you wish to speak where no one can hear us?"

Baldassarre's eyes darted nervously about, as if fearful of being overheard, and Romola's gaze lingered on his face, trying to discern the truth behind his words. "Out – in the air – away from the streets," he murmured, his voice barely audible.

"I sometimes go to San Miniato at this hour," Romola said, her voice steady. "If you like, I will go now, and you can follow me. It is far, but we can be solitary there." She smiled faintly, knowing that the distance would give them both a respite from the prying eyes and wagging tongues of the city.

As they walked, the air grew thick with the scent of cypress trees and the distant tang of the Arno River. The hill loomed ahead, its rugged contours softened by the fading light. Romola paused on a rocky outcropping, her eyes scanning the terrain for any sign of Baldassarre. He was close, his labored breathing the only indication of his fatigue.

When he finally reached her side, he sank down onto a nearby boulder, his massive frame seeming to sag beneath the weight of his own exhaustion. Romola's heart went out to him, her compassion tempered by a deep-seated wariness. She had grown accustomed to the company of those worn down by life's hardships, and yet, there was something about Baldassarre that set him apart.
