It was a tale woven into the very fabric of our existence, repeated endlessly across the thousand floors of our great city and echoed in the other one thousand, three hundred and twenty cities of the Pyramid. Somewhere, out in the desolate expanse of the Night Lands, there existed a second Place of Refuge. Here, in another corner of this dead world, the last millions of humanity had gathered to fight to the bitter end.

I heard this story everywhere during my travels through the cities of the Great Redoubt. My journeys began when I reached my seventeenth year and continued for three years and two hundred and twenty-five days. In those days, it was the custom for every child to spend exactly one day in each city as part of their training. It was indeed a grand voyage, and I encountered countless individuals whom I came to love deeply—yet whom I could never see again. Life simply does not possess enough space for everyone; each must fulfill their duty to the security and well-being of the Redoubt. Despite the vast distances we covered, there were so many millions of us and so few years.

As I mentioned, wherever I went, the narrative remained the same: the existence of this other Refuge. In the libraries of the cities I visited, I found numerous works detailing this second sanctuary. Some records, dating back to distant eras, asserted its existence with absolute confidence. Indeed, in those bygone ages, there seemed to be no doubt whatsoever. However, today, these very records are studied only by scholars who harbor doubts even as they read them. Thus it always goes.

Yet, regarding the reality of this Refuge, I harbored not a single shred of doubt from the day I first heard of it from our Master Monstruwacan. He, along with all his assistants, occupied the Tower of Observation at the apex of the Pyramid. Let me clarify here that he and I shared a profound affinity and close friendship. Although he was fully grown and I merely a youth, our bond was undeniable. When I turned twenty-one, he offered me a position within the Tower of Observation. This was a stroke of wondrous fortune for me, for in the entire vast Redoubt, appointment to the Tower of Observation was the most coveted role. Through it, even as astronomy does today, the natural curiosity of mankind was somewhat eased, albeit thwarted.

Let me further explain why I was chosen, lest anyone assume it was solely due to my friendship with the Master. There was a solid justification: I possessed a strange gift we termed "Night-Hearing." While this was but a fanciful name carrying little literal meaning, the peculiar ability itself was rare. Among the millions inhabiting the Pyramid, none possessed this gift to such a degree except myself.

Because of this gift, I could perceive the "invisible vibrations" of the aether. Without relying on the calls of our recording instruments, I could intercept the messages flowing continuously through the eternal darkness—even doing so better than the machines themselves. Now, perhaps it can be better understood how significant it was that I had grown accustomed to listening for a voice that had not rung in my ears for an eternity, yet which sang sweetly and clearly in my memory-dreams. To me, it seemed that Mirdath the Beautiful slept within my soul, whispering to me out of all the ages.

One day, at the fifteenth hour when Sleep-Time began, I was pondering this enduring love, marveling that my memory-dreams preserved the voice of a passion from such a remote era. Lost in thought and dream, as a young man might, I fancied that this aeon-lost figure was whispering beauty directly into my ears. My memory had sharpened so much, and I had pondered so deeply.

Suddenly, as I stood there, communing with my thoughts, I thrilled as if struck. Out of the everlasting night, a whisper thrummed against my subtler hearing.

For four long years I had listened, since that awakening in the embrasure when I was but a boy of seventeen. Now, out of the world-darkness and the eternal years of that lost life—which I now live in this Present Age—the whisper arrived. I recognized it instantly. Yet, trained in wisdom, I answered with no personal name. Instead, I sent the Master-Word through the night, projecting it via my brain-elements as best I could. All beings might do this, more or less, depending on their capacity, provided they are not mere clods. Furthermore, I knew that whoever called quietly would possess the power to hear without instruments, if it were truly her. If, however, it were one of the false callings of the Evil Forces, or some cunning monster, or perhaps the House of Silence meddling with our souls, they would lack the power to speak the Master-Word. This had been proven throughout the Everlasting.

Trembling, striving not to become tense (for tension destroys receptivity), I felt the throb of the Master-Word circle my spiritual essence. It beat steadily in the night, a marvelous sound. Then, with all the sweetness in my spirit, I called with my brain-elements: *"Mirdath! Mirdath! Mirdath!"*

At that exact instant, Master Monstruwacan entered the section of the Tower where I stood. Seeing my face, he stood utterly still. Though he lacked the power of Night-Hearing, he was wise and thoughtful, taking great care of my gift. Moreover, he had just returned from the Receiving Instrument, believing vaguely that he had caught the throb of the Master-Word, though it was too faint to register properly on the device. He sought me out, knowing that I, being gifted, might listen for it.

I recounted parts of my story, my thoughts, my memories, and that awakening. Up to this present moment, he listened with sympathy and a troubled, wondering heart. In that age, a man could speak sanely of things that, in our current era, would be considered foolishness or the ravings of insanity. Thanks to the refinement of mental arts, strange experiments, and advanced learning, people were able to conceive of matters now closed to our imaginations. Just as we today might calmly listen to tales that our ancestors would have dismissed as lunacy, so it was then.

While I spoke, I listened with my spirit. Save for a sense of faint, happy laughter wrapping around me, I heard nothing else all day.

Here, I must note that due to my memories and half-memories, I frequently disputed with our learned men. They were doubtful of the truth behind the olden stories of the Days of Light and the existence of the Sun, despite hints of truth in our oldest records. I, remembering, told them tales that seemed fairy-like to them, enchanting their hearts while angering their rational minds, which refused to accept as fact what their hearts embraced gladly. Yet Master Monstruwacan would listen to anything I had to say, even for hours. Oddly, after talking long and drawing stories from my Memory-Dreams, I would return to the present of that Future. Suddenly, all the Monstruwacans would abandon their instruments and observations to gather around me. The Master would be so sunk in interest that he wouldn't notice them leaving, nor would I, being so filled with the past.

When the Master returned to awareness of the present, he would rouse himself, chiding gently, while the lesser ones fled swiftly and guiltily back to their duties. Still, they carried a muddled, bewildered, thoughtful air, hungry for more, constantly questioning.

So it was with the other learned ones outside the Tower, those who disbelieved even as they hung on my every word. From the "dawn" hour to the fifteenth hour of "night"—when Sleep-Time commenced—they would listen. Occasionally, I found among them men of extraordinary learning who upheld my stories as truths. A faction formed, and later, more came to believe. Whether they believed or not, all were ready to listen, allowing me to spend my days in conversation, had I not had my work to do.

Master Monstruwacan believed from the start and was always wise enough to understand. I loved him for this, and for many other dear qualities.

Consequently, among all those millions, I was singled out. My stories traveled downward through a thousand cities. Eventually, in the lowest tier of the Underground Fields—a hundred miles deep beneath the Redoubt—I found that even the ploughboys knew something of my tales. They would gather around me whenever the Master and I descended to discuss matters concerning the Earth-Current and our Instruments.

Regarding the Underground Fields (which we then simply called "The Fields"), I should write briefly. They were the mightiest work of this world; the Last Redoubt itself was but a small thing beside them. A hundred miles deep lay the lowest field, spanning a hundred miles in every direction. Above it sat three hundred and six fields, each slightly smaller than the one below, tapering until the topmost field, directly beneath the lowermost floor of the Great Redoubt, measured only four miles across.

Thus, these fields, stacked one beneath another, formed a mighty, incredible Pyramid of Country Lands deep within the earth, stretching a hundred miles from base to summit.

The whole structure was sheathed on the sides with the grey metal of which the Redoubt was built. Each field was pillared and floored beneath the soil with this same wonder-compound, rendering it secure against monsters digging in from without.

All of that underground land was illuminated where needed by the Earth-Current. That same life-stream fertilized the soil, giving life and blood to plants, trees, bushes, and every natural thing.
