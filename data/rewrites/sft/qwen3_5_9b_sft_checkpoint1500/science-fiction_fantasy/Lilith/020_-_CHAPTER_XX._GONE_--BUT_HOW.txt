Chapter XX. GONE!—BUT HOW?

I rose, the room spinning, a hollow void expanding in my chest. For a heartbeat, she was absent, vanished into the silence like a dream dissolving at dawn. Loneliness crashed back over me, heavy and suffocating. She whom I had plucked from the jaws of death, who owed her breath to my desperate hands, had fled. Left standing alone, I felt the weight of a crime I couldn't name. Had I pushed too hard? Was the life I forced upon her a cage? If I had done her wrong, I would spend the rest of my existence carrying the guilt, hoping to balance the scales somehow.

Then I saw her.

She moved swiftly across the grass, moving away from the river, her steps purposeful. I took a final gulp of air, feeling the last sting of the leech and the shock of the woman's blow fading, replaced by a surging current of vitality. My blood hummed with a new rhythm. I could keep her in sight, though the distance stretched between us.

"Is this the end?" I whispered, the words hanging heavy in the air. Her gaze, sharp with hatred, burned in my mind. I understood the resentment; I had dragged life back into a vessel that perhaps wanted stillness. But why the loathing? Could virtue itself be repelled by servitude? How could a soul so proud, one who knew every act of mine, believe I had dishonored her? I had treated her not as a patient, but as a mother treats her lost child—with reverence, with tenderness. Had all my labor, all my sleepless nights, boiled down to nothing but ingratitude?

"No," I told myself, the denial rising like a shield. "Beauty cannot exist without a heart. Even if buried deep, it must pulse beneath the surface. The deeper the soil, the more fiercely it blooms when finally unearthed. To awaken that spirit is a greater gift than any lifespan. It is to give her a noble existence!"

She climbed a gentle slope, her stride steady, unyielding. Yet, as I watched, the gap between us widened. I called upon every reserve of strength, and it surged through me like a tidal wave. My limbs felt light, almost ethereal, as if gravity had loosened its grip. Like a breeze chasing the storm, I closed the distance, overtaking her.

She never glanced back. She moved with the fluid grace of a mythological figure, yet without a hint of hurry. Within three yards, she stopped, turning with a poise that defied exhaustion. She showed no sign of fatigue, no sweat, no panting. Her skin was not pale from sickness, but a luminous, radiant white. Her breathing was slow, deep, and measured. Her eyes... they held the vastness of the cosmos, illuminating the world around them. It was nearly midday, yet the atmosphere felt like a starless night where invisible dew makes the stars swell to impossible sizes.

"Why do you follow me?" she asked. Her voice was quiet, yet stern, as if addressing a stranger for the first time.

"I have lived so long on the mere hope of seeing your eyes," I answered, the truth tearing from my throat, "that I must see them again."

"You WILL NOT BE SPARED!" she commanded, the air freezing around her words. "Stop where you stand."

"Not until I am certain you are safe will I leave you," I replied, stepping forward.

"Then take the consequences," she said, and resumed her gliding walk.

As she turned, however, she cast a glance backward. And I stood frozen, as if pierced by an unseen spear. Her disdain had failed; now, her beauty threatened to destroy me.

Despair shattered my resolve, breaking the spell. I ran, catching up to her.

"Pity me!" I cried.

She ignored me. I followed her like a child clinging to a parent pretending to abandon them. "I will be your slave!" I shouted, laying my hand on her arm.

She spun around as if bitten by a viper. I cowered before the blinding fire of her eyes, unable to look away.

"Pity me!" I begged again.

She continued walking.

For the entire day, I trailed in her footsteps. The sun climbed to its zenith, paused, and descended, yet she never halted, never slowed. The darkness fell, and I remained close; if I lost sight of her for even a second, I feared I might lose her forever.

We walked all day across fields of thick, soft grass. Suddenly, she stopped and collapsed onto the earth. There was just enough light to see that utter exhaustion had claimed her. I stood behind her, gazing down.

"Do I love her?" I wondered, knowing she was dangerous, flawed. "Do I hate her? Yet I cannot leave her!"

I knelt beside her.

"Begone! Do not dare touch me!" she screamed, her voice trembling.

Her arms lay limp by her sides, paralyzed.

Then, suddenly, they snapped shut around my neck, rigid and unyielding like the grasp of a torture maiden. She pulled my face down to hers, her lips pressing against my cheek. A sharp, electric sting shot through me, pulsing violently. I could not move a muscle, not an inch. Slowly, the pain faded, replaced by a heavy, dreamlike weariness, a strange, intoxicating pleasure. And then, blackness swallowed me.

When I opened my eyes, the moon hung low above the horizon, casting no warmth, merely a stark, brilliant point in the infinite black. My cheek stung; I touched it and found a damp stain. My neck ached; another wet mark bloomed there. I sighed, exhausted, and turned my gaze listlessly around—and saw where the moon's light had gone. It had gathered around the lady. She stood encased in a shimmering, glowing nimbus.

I stumbled toward her.

"Down!" she cried, her tone imperious, commanding a rebellious beast. "Follow me a single step, if you dare!"

"I will!" I murmured, the effort agony itself.

"Step within the gates of my city, and my people will stone you: they do not love beggars!"

Her words fell on ears that refused to hear. Weak as water, half-asleep, I did not realize I was moving, yet the space between us shrank. She took one step back, raised her left arm, and struck me across the forehead with a clenched fist. It felt like being hit by an iron hammer, and I fell.

I sprang to my feet, cold and soaked, yet my mind was razor-sharp, my strength renewed. Had the blow woken me? There was no wound, no pain—but how was I wet? I could not have been lying here long; the moon hadn't moved much.

The lady stood some yards away, her back to me. She was doing something, but I couldn't tell what. Then, a sudden flash revealed the truth: she had shed her garments, standing naked and white in the moon's daze. For a moment, she stood upright—and then she fell forward.

A streak of white shot away in a swift, drawn line. Simultaneously, the moon flared fully, revealing the trail: a long-bodied creature, racing in great, low-curved bounds over the grass. Dark spots streamed down its back like a rushing river, as if it had darted beneath the shadow of trees, stealing their gloom.

"Oh, God of mercy!" I cried, "Is the terrible creature speeding toward the night-enfolded city?" And from afar, I seemed to hear the sudden, spreading roar of terror as the pale savage bounded from house to house, rending and slaying.

While I stared, paralyzed by fear, another large creature shot past me from behind, like a silent, deadly arrow. Pure white, it raced straight for the spot where the lady had fallen—or so I thought. My tongue stuck to the roof of my mouth. I lunged forward, pursuing the beast, but the spot I aimed for vanished instantly, far ahead of it.

"It was well," I thought, "that I did not scream. If she had risen, the monster would have been upon her!"

When I reached the location, there was no lady. Only the garments she had discarded lay dark in the moonlight.

I stood staring after the second beast. It tore over the ground with even greater swiftness than the first—in long, level, skimming leaps, the very essence of wasted speed. It followed the path the first had taken, and I watched it shrink until it disappeared into the uncertain distance.

But where was the lady? Had the first beast crept silently upon her, surprising her? I had heard no shriek! There hadn't been time to devour her! Could it have snatched her up as it ran, bearing her away to its lair? Yet, so laden, it could not have run so fast! And surely, I would have seen that it carried something!

Horrible doubts began to claw at my mind. After a thorough, fruitless search, I set out in the track of the two beasts, driven by a terrifying uncertainty.
