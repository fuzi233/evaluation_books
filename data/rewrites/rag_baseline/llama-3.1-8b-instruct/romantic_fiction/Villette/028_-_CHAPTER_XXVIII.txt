The intricacies of pedagogical dynamics within the confines of Madame Beck's esteemed institution often proved a labyrinth to navigate, particularly when M. Paul Emanuel was involved. His acute sensitivities to interruption were well-known among the teachers and pupils, who collectively regarded traversing the classe under such circumstances as a perilous endeavor akin to walking a tightrope over a precipice.

Even Madame Beck herself, if compelled to intervene, would execute her passage with the caution of a diplomat navigating a minefield, her skirts rustling softly against the floor as she skirted the formidable estrade. As for Rosine, the portress, her duties necessitated frequent excursions into the heart of the divisions, where music lessons were conducted in the oratory, the salle-à-manger, or other designated piano-stations. Her experiences with M. Paul's ire had instilled in her a deep-seated trepidation, evident in the tongue-tied mannerisms that beset her upon each successive encounter.

As I sat in the carré, engaged in embroidering a piece commenced by one of the pupils, the sounds emanating from the adjacent classe grew increasingly agitated. A voice, rising in crescendo, seemed to reverberate off the walls, its cadences both ominous and unyielding. Though separated by a sturdy partition-wall and possessing a convenient means of egress through the glass-door to the court, I found myself enthralled by the unfolding drama, rather than intimidated. Poor Rosine, however, was not so fortunate; her previous attempts to traverse the perilous terrain had left her shaken, and her latest summons to fetch a pupil from under M. Paul's watchful eye was fraught with danger.

"Mon Dieu! Mon Dieu!" she exclaimed, her words laced with desperation. "What will become of me? Monsieur will surely kill me, I am certain; for he is in a fury!"

Summoning the courage born of necessity, she pushed open the door and called out, "Mademoiselle La Malle au piano!" However, before she could effect her escape or even close the door, M. Paul's voice boomed forth, its tone as unforgiving as a judicial decree:

"Dès ce moment!—La classe est défendue. La première qui ouvrira cette porte, ou passera par cette division, sera..."
