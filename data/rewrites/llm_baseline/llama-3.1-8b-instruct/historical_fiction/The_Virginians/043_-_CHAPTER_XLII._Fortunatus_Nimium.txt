As the days passed, Harry Warrington's resolve to honor his promise to his cousin, Will, remained steadfast, despite its unpopularity. Few, however, would have accused him of being overly enthusiastic about the arrangement. It was more likely that the physical altercation between the two had been a calculated move, designed to provoke a reaction from the family. Perhaps Harry thought to himself, "A fight between us will inevitably lead to a confrontation with the family, and in the chaos, Maria will take her brother's side. I, of course, will refuse to back down. When the dust settles, Will will realize I am not one to be trifled with, and our agreement will come to an end, freeing me from this obligation."

Thus, Harry carefully laid his plan, and the consequences unfolded accordingly. Though Will's nose was swollen, and his eye bruised, no lasting harm was done. No challenge was issued, and no blood was spilled. Will, though shaken, rose to his feet, as any sensible man would, eager to settle his differences in private, without an audience. Maria, far from taking her family's side, sided with her cousin, as did her brother, Lord Castlewood, when informed of the incident. The chaplain had witnessed the start of the brawl, and Lord Castlewood saw it as a familiar pattern – Will's propensity for brawling in his cups. Mr. Warrington's response was reasonable, a testament to his character, and it was Will who should have sought forgiveness.

Harry insisted that he would not accept an apology unless his horse was returned and his bet settled. The details of the outcome remain unclear, but it is known that the cousins eventually met at various social gatherings, their animosity momentarily set aside. Lord Castlewood had initially been willing to see his sister, Maria, married to anyone who came along, regardless of her past. A gentleman from Virginia, in particular, would have been a suitable match, and she would have retreated to his home, her life simplified. However, after a visit with Aunt Bernstein, Lord Castlewood's perspective shifted. He began to discourage Maria from the match, expressing a sense of pity for Harry, who was being made to suffer due to a rash promise made in his youth.
