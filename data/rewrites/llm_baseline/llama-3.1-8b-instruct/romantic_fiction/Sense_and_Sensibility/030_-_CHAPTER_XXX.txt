Mrs. Jennings swept into the room, her eyes filled with genuine concern as she approached Marianne. Without waiting for an invitation, she let herself in, her footsteps light on the floorboards. "How are you, my dear?" she asked, her voice a soothing balm as she took in Marianne's wan appearance.

Marianne turned her face away, her shoulders shaking with suppressed sobs. Mrs. Jennings's expression turned stern, her brow furrowed in dismay. "This is no wonder," she said, her voice low and steady. "He's to be married soon – that good-for-nothing fellow!" Her hands clenched into fists, as if she'd like to shake some sense into him. "Mrs. Taylor told me just this morning, from a friend of Miss Grey's. I wouldn't have believed it otherwise." A shiver ran down her spine. "Well, I'll tell you this, my dear: if it's true, he's treated a young woman of my acquaintance abominably. I hope his wife gives him a life of misery."

Marianne's sobs grew louder, but Mrs. Jennings's words seemed to soothe her, and she began to calm. "Don't worry, my dear, you'll find another young man soon enough. With a face as lovely as yours, you'll never lack for admirers." She patted Marianne's hand reassuringly, before standing up to leave. "I won't disturb you further, but the Parrys and Sandersons are coming tonight, and that should take your mind off things."

As she slipped out of the room, Mrs. Jennings left behind a trail of the scent of lavender and baking spices, a gentle reminder of the warmth and comfort she brought to those around her. Marianne, however, was far from comforted. In fact, the thought of dinner with the Parrys and Sandersons only made her feel worse.

Elinor, sensing her sister's distress, urged her not to attend the dinner, but Marianne insisted, saying she could bear it. Elinor was skeptical, but she helped Marianne adjust her dress and prepared to escort her downstairs. When they arrived in the dining room, Marianne looked wretched, but she ate a little and seemed calmer than Elinor had expected.
