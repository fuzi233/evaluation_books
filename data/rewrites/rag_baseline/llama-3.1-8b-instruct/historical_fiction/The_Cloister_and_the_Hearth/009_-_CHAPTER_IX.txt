The leopards, their fur like burnished copper, darted forward with a fierce snarl, their jaws closing around the offering with a satisfying crunch. Just as the sounds of rending flesh filled the night, a nearby hound let out a loud, mournful bay, and the trio at the window drew closer together. The leopard, now wary of its supper being stolen, retreated swiftly into the darkness of the woods, pursued by the distant sounds of riders and dogs giving chase. Martin and his companions breathed a collective sigh of relief as the leopard vanished into the shadows. Margaret seized the moment to release a gentle sob, and Gerard's lips brushed away her tears with tender care.

At the table, Gerard raised his goblet in a toast to Margaret's wit, saying, "It is stronger than any man's strength." Margaret smiled, her eyes sparkling with amusement, and replied, "Indeed, when those she loves are in peril, her spirit knows no bounds."

Tonight, Gerard lingered longer than usual at Margaret's side, his heart full of joy and contentment. As he made his way home, the moon casting long shadows across the path ahead, he chanced upon two figures standing beneath the boughs of ancient trees. They blocked his path, their faces stern and unyielding. Gerard's father and mother stood before him, their expressions a mixture of concern and disappointment.

"Out so late," his father growled, his voice low and even. "What could possibly have kept you?"

Gerard's heart skipped a beat as he hesitated, unsure how to respond. His mother's voice, laced with both reproof and affection, cut through the silence. "Oh, Gerard!"
