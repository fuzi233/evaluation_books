In the tranquil morning light, the small front parlor of Mrs. Meyrick's home stood as a sanctuary, a refuge from the turmoil that had beset Mirah's young life. The gentle breeze carried the sweet scent of blooming flowers through the open window, mingling with the soft ticking of the clock on the mantle-piece and the occasional rumble of wheels from outside. Amidst this peaceful atmosphere, Mrs. Meyrick sorted colored wools for her embroidery, her hands moving with a soothing rhythm as she worked. Hafiz, the family cat, lay contentedly on the windowsill, his eyes closed in a warm patch of sunlight.

As Mirah descended the stairs, her dark hair curling slightly as it dried from its morning bath, she wore a look of tentative hope, a glimmer of comfort in her eyes that hinted at the long-suffering and vigilance that had etched lines on her face. Mab, the younger daughter, beamed with pride as she led Mirah into the parlor, holding up a pair of dainty felt slippers that fit her friend perfectly. The cheap clothing that clung to Mirah's feet seemed a makeshift yet endearing adornment, like the tender shoots of new growth that burst forth from the earth.

"Oh, look at the slippers, how lovely they fit!" Mab exclaimed, her eyes shining with excitement. "You're like the Queen Budoor, with delicate feet, the very image of the protecting and all-recompensing Creator's handiwork."

Mirah's gaze drifted down to her feet, a fleeting smile crossing her lips as she recalled the countless times they had borne the weight of her burdens. Her eyes rose again, locking onto Mrs. Meyrick's, who was watching her with a mixture of warmth and wariness. "I fear those feet have shouldered their load a little too often of late," Mrs. Meyrick said gently. "But today, dear Mirah, you'll rest and be my companion."

Mab's face fell, her eyes clouding over with disappointment, for she felt herself caught in the midst of a captivating tale and was reluctant to miss any of the chapters. Kate was already out sketching along the river, and Amy was away on errands, leaving the mother and Mirah alone together. The silence that followed was a palpable thing, a heavy blanket that wrapped around the room, punctuated only by the soft ticking of the clock and the distant hum of the city outside.
