As the morning sun cast a warm glow over the streets of New York, a gentle spring breeze carried the whispers of the city's inhabitants, mingling the scents of blooming lilacs and lilies-of-the-valley. In the quiet hours preceding the ceremony, the ladies of both families had donned their finest attire – faded sables and yellowing ermines – a testament to the passing of seasons and the enduring traditions of the past. The air inside the church was heavy with the sweet fragrance of camphor, a reminder of the reverence that filled the space.

Newland Archer stood at the chancel step of Grace Church, his tall hat and gloves grasped firmly in his left hand, as he awaited the arrival of the bride and her father. A signal from the sexton indicated that they were approaching, and Archer took a moment to adjust his position, his eyes scanning the gathering crowd. He had gone through the motions of preparing for this day with a sense of resignation, as if navigating a labyrinth he had traversed many times before.

As he stood there, Handel's March swelled through the church, its grandeur a reminder of the countless weddings he had attended, each one a unique yet familiar experience. He pondered the faces before him, the same ones he had seen at countless other ceremonies, each with their own stories and secrets. He wondered what lay ahead, not just for the newlyweds, but for himself, and the lives they would soon leave behind.

As he stood at the threshold of this new chapter, Archer's thoughts turned to the music that filled the church, a symphony of anticipation and joy. He felt a sense of detachment, as if observing the scene unfold before him like a first-night performance at the opera. The same faces, the same rituals, the same expectations – all waiting to be played out once more. And yet, amidst the familiarity, he sensed a subtle shift, a hint of something new and unknown, like the faintest note of a melody yet to be composed.
