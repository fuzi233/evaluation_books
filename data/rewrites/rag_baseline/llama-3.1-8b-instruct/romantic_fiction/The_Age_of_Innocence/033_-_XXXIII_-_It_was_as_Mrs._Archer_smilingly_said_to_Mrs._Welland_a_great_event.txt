The sound of ticking clockwork echoed through the silence. May Archer stood at the long dining table, surrounded by the fragrance of Jacqueminot roses and the delicate curves of maidenhair ferns. A basket of orchids from Skuytercliff sat atop the piano, a reminder of Mr. van der Luyden's thoughtful gesture. The air was heavy with anticipation, weighed down by the expectation of a grand evening. 

Mrs. Archer sat across from her, her sharp gold pen moving deftly across the menu card, checking off each name with a precise flourish. The list was a litany of obligation, a recitation of the names that were expected to attend. Henry van der Luyden, Louisa, the Lovell Mingotts, the Reggie Chiverses, Lawrence Lefferts and Gertrude, the Selfridge Merrys, Sillerton Jackson, Van Newland and his wife, and Countess Olenska – each name a thread in the intricate tapestry of social expectation.

As Mrs. Archer worked, her mother-in-law surveyed her son-in-law with a look of fond approval. "No one can say, Newland, that you and May are not giving Ellen a handsome send-off." The words were a gentle rebuke, a reminder of the duties that came with marriage and social standing.

Mrs. Archer nodded in agreement, her eyes never leaving the menu card. "I understand May's wanting her cousin to tell people abroad that we're not quite barbarians." The phrase hung in the air, a wry acknowledgment of the social pressures that governed their lives.

The clock struck the hour, its metallic voice piercing the stillness. May emerged from the dining room, a look of concentration etched on her face. "Newland, I need to check the placing of the Maillard bonbons," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. Archer rose from his chair, his movements mechanical, and followed her back into the dining room. The table was a carefully constructed illusion, a fragile edifice of etiquette and social nicety. And yet, beneath the surface, he sensed the weight of expectation, the knowledge that this evening would be remembered for years to come.
