As Madame Beck spoke those words, "Oubliez les Professeurs," I felt a spark of unease ignite within me. It was a sentiment that should have been avoided, for it only served to heighten my awareness of the presence of another person, one who had begun to occupy a space in my thoughts. I had been instructed to forget, yet Madame Beck's words only succeeded in drawing my attention to this individual, and to the questions surrounding their nature.

To know him was to understand the depth of their character, and it was precisely this that the professors had sought to convey to me. They presented him as a paragon of virtue, a stainless hero whose love was born of a southern soul, untainted by the vicissitudes of life. His was a love that had conquered death itself, a flame that burned bright and true, illuminating the darkness and inspiring devotion.

But I had known him differently. I had seen glimpses of a more complex personality, one marked by jealousy and suspicion, yet also by tender moments and fleeting displays of kindness. These contradictions had left me uncertain, and it was only when Père Silas and Madame Beck revealed the full extent of his character that I began to grasp the magnitude of his emotions.

Their narrative painted a picture of a man who had dedicated his life to an unselfish purpose, laying aside his own desires for the sake of others. He had proven his fidelity through his actions, making sacrifices that testified to the depth of his commitment. And yet, as I reflected on this portrait, I couldn't help but wonder about the woman who had captured his heart – Justine Marie.

Like many of the girls at Madame Beck's school, she was a phlegmatic, pale, and reserved individual, kind-hearted but unremarkable. Yet, I sensed that there was more to her than met the eye, that perhaps she was the recipient of some secret affection, one that had imbued her with a radiance that belied her quiet nature.

The questions that arose from this realization were daunting, and they lingered long after Madame Beck's words had faded. Was I to be swayed by the image of a pale nun, an eternal barrier between us? What of the charities that had absorbed his worldly goods, or the vows he had taken to remain chaste? These queries haunted me, a constant presence that refused to be silenced.
