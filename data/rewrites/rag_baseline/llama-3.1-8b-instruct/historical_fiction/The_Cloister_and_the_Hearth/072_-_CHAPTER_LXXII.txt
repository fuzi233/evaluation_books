In the dimly lit recesses of the cloister, the once-mighty Dominicans struggled to reclaim their dominance, their influence waning against the ascendant Franciscans. In this tumultuous landscape, the sagacious leaders of the order sought out zealous sons to spearhead their revival, men who could navigate the complexities of language and culture with ease. Among these hopefuls was Clement, whose remarkable proficiency in Latin, Italian, French, and Dutch had already earned him recognition. His destiny was to traverse England, spreading the message of the faith and forging connections with the Roman centre.

However, Jerome, a man with the superior's ear, harbored reservations about Clement's suitability for such a grand endeavor. "He still carries the milk of the world within him," Jerome cautioned, "its passions, its frailties – let not his newfound zeal and humility tempt us to overlook our ancient wisdom. Test him first, temper him, lest we find ourselves relying on a reed for a staff."

The prior concurred with Jerome's advice, and so the older friar took it upon himself to evaluate Clement's mettle. One day, he led Clement to a sun-drenched field where the young men of the order would often gather to play and socialize. Jerome knew this to be a haunt of Clement's old friends, particularly Pietro Vanucci and Andrea, who had once been among his closest companions. As they chanced upon the two friars, Clement's eyes betrayed a fleeting start, but he quickly composed himself, murmuring a quiet prayer.

"Would you not speak with them, brother?" Jerome asked, probing Clement's resolve. "No, brother," Clement replied, his voice barely above a whisper, "it was enough for me to see them. They remind me of the sins I can never fully atone for." Jerome nodded, making a mental note of Clement's response, and later reported favorably on his protégé's character.

Next, Jerome took Clement on a series of grueling trials, exposing him to the harsh realities of life. They visited deathbeds, where the dying lay wracked with pain and despair, and dungeons, where the stench of rot and disease hung heavy in the air. Though Clement's body was tested, his spirit remained unbroken, and he brought solace to those who still clung to hope. Jerome's assessment of Clement's character was clear: his flesh was weak, but his spirit was strong.

Yet, a far more daunting challenge awaited.
