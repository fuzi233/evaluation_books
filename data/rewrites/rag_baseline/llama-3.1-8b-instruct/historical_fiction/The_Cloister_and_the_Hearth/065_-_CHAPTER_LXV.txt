As the storm clouds gathered above Rome, Gerard navigated the narrow streets with an air of desperation, seeking refuge from the torrential downpour. His footsteps led him to a humble tavern, where he pushed open the creaky door and stepped inside, shaking the rain from his cloak. The fire crackled in the hearth, casting a warm glow across the room as Gerard called out for wine, his voice lost amidst the din of laughter and conversation.

As the night wore on and the rain continued to pour outside, Gerard succumbed to the wine's numbing effects, his head lolling forward onto his hands as he drifted into a fitful sleep. The tavern's patrons grew more boisterous, their raucous laughter and clinking glasses gradually rousing him from his stupor.

Gerard's gaze drifted towards two figures huddled in the corner, their voices hushed in a heated discussion. One was a pardoner, his tattered robes and worn boots a testament to his profession. The other, clad in simple yet neat attire, wore a hat pulled low over his face, concealing his features save for a thick, unkempt beard. Their conversation revolved around a clandestine transaction, a bargain struck in the shadows of the city's moral underbelly.

The pardoner protested the rising costs of indulgences, claiming they were still within reach for even the most modest of sinners. However, his companion countered that the prices had grown exorbitant, threatening to exclude the very poor from redemption. The pardoner scoffed at this notion, citing the Pope's edict that indulgences were available to all, regardless of social standing.

As the two men negotiated, Gerard's ears picked up snippets of their conversation, his mind clouded by the wine's haze. He caught phrases like "crime of one sort or another," "the Pope's face against," and "laymen" – a jumble of words that made little sense to him. The pardoner produced a parchment, reading aloud from it with a flourish, the list of transgressions and their corresponding prices etched into his memory like a scar.

Though Gerard's gaze remained fixed on the table, he felt a surge of revulsion towards the man in the slouched hat, his anger simmering just below the surface.
