The narrow alleys of Florence beckoned like a labyrinth, their worn stone facades bearing witness to the city's storied past. It had been weeks since Baldassarre last set foot in the Piazza del Duomo, and the memory of his ignominious escape still lingered, a festering wound that refused to heal. The recollection of being seen half-naked, with his hair unkempt and a rope encirusted with the symbol of his supposed madness, stung like a fresh wound.

As he navigated the winding streets, his thoughts turned to the monk who had unwittingly revealed his true identity to Tito. A faint tremor ran through his frame, a manifestation of the turmoil brewing within. His heart yearned for vengeance, yet a gnawing doubt crept in, threatening to undermine his resolve. Had he truly transformed, or was it all just a clever ruse, a masquerade to conceal the man he once was? The thought was a palpable weight, pressing upon his chest like a physical force.

At the hospital, he had played the part of a beggar, sharing a tale of captivity by the French, but the brothers' kindness had awakened a pang of guilt. Their generosity – a coarse tunic, a pair of peasant's shoes, and a few coins – had only served to underscore the chasm between his past and present. As he walked to Arezzo, he stopped at a small town and spent a portion of his coins to get shaved and have his hair trimmed, seeking to reclaim a semblance of his former self.

In the barber's shop, a glint of steel caught his eye – a small hand-mirror that offered a reflection of the man he once was. His gaze faltered, and for an instant, his hand trembled like a leaf as he considered the possibility that Tito might not recognize him. The thought was a searing indictment, a harsh reminder that his transformation was far from complete.

The barber, sensing his unease, held the mirror steady, allowing Baldassarre to behold his reflection. The lines etched on his face told the story of his years, but they did not obscure the rugged texture of his skin, the deep-set eyes that seemed to bore into the soul. A faint glimmer of recognition flickered, only to be extinguished by the realization that he was not as changed as he had hoped.
