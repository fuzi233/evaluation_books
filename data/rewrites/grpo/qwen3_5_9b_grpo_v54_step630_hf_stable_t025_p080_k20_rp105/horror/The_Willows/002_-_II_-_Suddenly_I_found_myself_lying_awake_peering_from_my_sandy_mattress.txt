The darkness pressed against the canvas walls, heavy and suffocating, until I found myself lying awake, my eyes fixed on the gap where the tent door hung open. My gaze drifted to the watch pinned haphazardly against the rough fabric; the moonlight caught its face, revealing that the hour had long since passed midnight. We had slept only a few hours, a slumber broken by the relentless howling of the wind outside. Beside me, the Swede remained fast asleep, oblivious to the chill that had settled deep in my chest. It was a primal dread, a sudden, sharp plucking of the heart that signaled something was wrong in our immediate vicinity. The air felt thick with disturbance, a static charge before a storm.

I sat up abruptly, squinting into the gloom. The trees beyond the campsite were thrashing violently, their branches whipping like frantic snakes as the gusts battered them. Yet, our small patch of green canvas seemed miraculously secure, nestled safely in the hollow, the wind sliding over us without finding enough purchase to tear. But the unease did not fade. It coiled tighter in my gut. I crawled out of the tent, moving with deliberate silence so as not to wake my companion, driven by a strange, electric excitement that had no name.

Halfway out, kneeling on the damp sand, my eyes locked onto the tops of the bushes directly opposite. Against the pale backdrop of the moonlit sky, the moving tracery of leaves formed shifting, unnatural silhouettes. I froze, sitting back on my haunches, staring in disbelief. There, hovering just above the brush and slightly ahead, were shapes of an indeterminate nature among the willows. As the branches swayed, these forms seemed to coalesce, arranging themselves into monstrous outlines that twisted and shifted rapidly beneath the silver light. They were close—perhaps fifty feet away.

My first, desperate instinct was to rouse the Swede, to share this vision with someone else. But a sudden, paralyzing hesitation stopped me. The thought occurred to me, perhaps subconsciously, that I would not welcome confirmation from another; that seeing them alone made them more terrifying, more absolute. So I crouched there, my eyes stinging from the strain, wide awake and trembling.

Then, they became fully visible. Emerging from the canopy of the bushes were immense, bronze-colored figures, moving with a fluidity entirely independent of the swaying branches. They were colossal, far larger than any human, and their very appearance screamed that they were *not* human. They were certainly not merely the play of light and shadow. They shifted on their own accord, rising in a continuous, spiraling stream from the earth into the sky, vanishing completely the moment they touched the darkness above. Interlaced and fused together, they formed a great, writhing column. I could see their limbs and massive bodies melting into one another, creating a serpentine line that bent and twisted in rhythm with the wind-tossed trees. They were nude, fluid shapes pouring upward through the foliage, rising in a living pillar toward the heavens. I could never see their faces; they were blank masks of bronze.

They poured upward unceasingly, swaying in great, bending curves, their skin glowing with a dull, metallic hue. I stared, forcing every ounce of vision from my eyes, terrified that at any moment they would dissolve into mere optical illusions created by the moonlight and the wind. I searched frantically for proof of their reality, yet all the while, I understood that my definition of reality had shifted. The longer I watched, the more certain I became that these figures were real and alive, though perhaps not by the standards of biology or photography.

Far from feeling fear, I was seized by a profound awe and wonder unlike anything I had ever known. It felt as though I was gazing at the personified elemental forces of this haunted, primeval land. Our intrusion had stirred the ancient powers of the place into activity; we were the cause of the disturbance. My mind overflowed with stories and legends of spirits and deities worshipped by men throughout history. But before I could construct any explanation, an impulse drove me forward. I crept further out on the sand, standing upright. The ground beneath my bare feet was still warm; the wind tore at my hair and face; the roar of the river burst upon my ears with sudden violence. These sensations proved my senses were functioning normally. Yet, the figures continued to rise from the earth to the heavens, silent and majestic, in a great spiral of grace and strength that overwhelmed me with a deep, visceral emotion of worship. I felt compelled to fall down and worship—absolutely worship.

Perhaps I would have done so in another minute when a violent gust of wind swept against me, knocking me sideways and nearly sending me stumbling. It seemed to shake the dream violently from my mind, offering a new perspective. The figures remained, ascending into the heart of the night, but my reason began to claw its way back to the surface. It must be a subjective experience, I argued to myself—real to me, but still subjective. The moonlight and the branches were working together to paint these pictures upon the mirror of my imagination, which I had foolishly projected outward into the world. I knew this had to be the case. I took a breath, steeling myself, and began to move forward across the open patches of sand. By Jove, was it all hallucination? Was it merely a trick of the mind? Did my reason argue in the old, futile way, clinging to the narrow standards of the known?

All I know is that the great column of figures ascended darkly into the sky for what seemed an eternity, possessing a completeness of reality that defied my skepticism. And then, suddenly, they were gone!

As the immediate wonder of their presence faded, a cold rush of terror descended upon me. The esoteric, terrible meaning of this lonely, haunted region flamed up within my soul, and I began to tremble dreadfully. I cast a quick, panicked glance around, calculating vainly for a way to escape, realizing with horror how helpless I was to do anything effective. I crept back silently into the tent, collapsing onto my sandy mattress. First, I lowered the door-curtain to shut out the sight of the willows in the moonlight, and then I buried my head as deeply as possible beneath the blankets, trying to deaden the sound of the terrifying wind that whispered threats from the dark.
