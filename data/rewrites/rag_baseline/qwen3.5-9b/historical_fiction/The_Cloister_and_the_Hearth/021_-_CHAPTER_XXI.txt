Speech is the familiar vent of human thought, yet there exist emotions so primal and overwhelming that they erupt not in articulate words, but in raw, eloquent cries. In such moments, man sheds his civilized veneer, revealing himself as nothing more than a higher beast; for when driven to the brink of agitation, even the most refined creatures reduce themselves to mere ejaculation, stripped of speech. There was something terrifyingly bestial in the triumphant roar that erupted from the thicket as Dierich's men burst forth upon their quarry, mirrored instantly by the piercing shriek of terror that drove Gerard and the mule into flight. The pursuers' hands clawed at the empty air, grasping at ghosts mere feet behind them, as they chased a phantom of survival. For a fleeting second, confused and disoriented like lions that have missed their spring, Dierich and his followers faltered, allowing Gerard and the beast to carve out a ten-yard sanctuary. Then, fueled by adrenaline, they surged forward with weapons raised, certain of capture. This was not the first time these factions had tested their mettle against one another; earlier that morning, in the open fields, they had visibly closed the gap on the trio. Now, however, fate had reset the board for a contest decided solely by velocity.

A hundred yards vanished in the blink of an eye, yet those crucial ten yards persisted, a stubborn barrier between hunter and hunted. This sudden acceleration baffled Dierich Brower. The truth lay in the mechanics of motion itself: when three flee together, their pace is dictated by the slowest link. From Peter's house to the forest's edge, Gerard had been tethered to Margaret's slower stride; now, unburdened by her presence, he ran with his own youthful vigor, aided by a mule capable of leaving the entire party in the dust. Furthermore, the benefits of youth and chastity began to manifest physically. Light began to bleed perceptibly into the space separating the hunted from the hunters. Desperate to close the distance, Dierich made a final, valiant effort, gaining two yards, only for Gerard to steal them back in a heartbeat. The pursuit dissolved into a chorus of curses. Martin heard them, and a spark ignited in his eyes. "Courage, Gerard! Courage, brave lad! They are breaking apart!" And indeed, the formation fractured. Dierich was now led by a single man, while another lagged entirely to the rear.

They ascended a long, gentle rise, and here, the advantages of youth, grit, and sober living proved insurmountable. Before reaching the summit, the weight of Dierich's forty years crushed him, feeling heavier than forty leaden bullets. "Our prey is turning to dough," he gasped, abandoning the sprint to follow at a walking pace. Jorian Ketel trailed off next, followed by others until one by one, Gerard ran them all to a halt, save for a solitary figure who pressed on with the relentless tenacity of a bloodhound, though steadily losing ground. His name, if my memory serves, was Eric Wouverman. Following him, they approached a sharper, steeper rise within the woods. "Hold fast to the mane!" Martin shouted. Gerard obeyed, and the mule propelled him upward with a speed surpassing his previous running. Witnessing this maneuver, Dierich's remaining follower lost his nerve. Now fully eighty yards behind Gerard and significantly ahead of his nearest comrade, he halted abruptly. Obedient to Dierich's silent command, he lowered his crossbow, leveled it with deliberate precision, and just as the trio disappeared over the crest of the hill, released the bolt. It whizzed through the air with deadly intent. A cry of dismay shattered the silence, and the next instant, as if struck by a thunderbolt, they all lay prone upon the earth—the men and the mule alike, silenced by the arrow.
