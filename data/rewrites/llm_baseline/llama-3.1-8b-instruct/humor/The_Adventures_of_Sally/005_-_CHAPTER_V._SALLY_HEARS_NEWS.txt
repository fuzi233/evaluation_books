As Sally stepped off the taxi and onto the familiar streets of New York, the warm sunlight danced across her face, illuminating the vibrant cityscape. The sound of carriages rattling over cobblestones and the hum of pedestrians filled the air, creating a soothing melody that harmonized with the gentle rustle of leaves in the trees. It was as if the city itself was awakening from a long slumber, ready to welcome her back with open arms.

Sally's initial intention had been to indulge in the opulence of the St. Regis Hotel, but upon reconsidering, she decided to return to Mrs. Meecher's boarding house, where she had spent the past three years. The thought of reconnecting with her friends and familiar surroundings brought a sense of comfort, like the soft strumming of a lute on a summer evening. Besides, she rationalized, it was only temporary, until she found the perfect apartment to call her own.

The taxi driver expertly navigated the crowded streets, and Sally's gaze wandered to the people passing by – each with their own stories, their own melodies playing in the background of their lives. The street cleaners moved with a quiet purpose, their white uniforms a stark contrast to the bustling energy of the city. The streetcars, packed with people headed to their daily routines, seemed to be singing in harmony, their laughter and chatter mingling with the clanging of the bells as they pulled away from the curb.

As the taxi stopped in front of Mrs. Meecher's establishment, Sally felt a flutter in her chest, hoping to catch a glimpse of Gerald Foster, whose departure had been hinted at in Mrs. Meecher's previous letters. But as she entered the boarding house, she was met with the news that Gerald had left town that morning, bound for Detroit, along with Miss Doland, who was rumored to be performing in a new play.

Mrs. Meecher, ever the drama enthusiast, regaled Sally with stories of the rehearsals, her eyes sparkling with excitement. Sally listened intently, trying to shake off the disappointment that had settled in the pit of her stomach. She remembered the countless nights spent listening to Gerald's music, the way his fingers danced across the keys, creating a symphony that transported her to another world. The thought of him being gone, even temporarily, left a hollow feeling, like the silence between notes in a melancholic melody.
