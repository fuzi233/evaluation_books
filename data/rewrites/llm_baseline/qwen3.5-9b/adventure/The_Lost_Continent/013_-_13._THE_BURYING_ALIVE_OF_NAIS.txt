There was no mistaking the speed with which Empress Phorenice's whims became reality in Atlantis. Her philosophy was absolute: the entire nation existed solely for her benefit, and no price was too steep to pay for her desires. Having issued her decree regarding the execution of Nais—framed officially as the construction of a stone throne, though universally understood to require the labor of thousands—the city mobilized instantly. Heralds rang out the proclamation in every ward. Masons, laborers, stonecutters, sculptors, engineers, and architects abandoned their current tasks to converge on the designated site. Architects appointed a chief director, while lesser architects and engineers oversaw the execution. Any material within the city limits bearing the guild's seal was seized immediately, without compensation. To accommodate the colossal blocks required, entire buildings were demolished to clear the path.

Despite a rebel army battering the city walls with war engines, the building guilds worked tirelessly. Their skill, aided by Phorenice's marvelous inventions, surged forward. While they could not match the monolithic precision of the early Gods' sacred circles, their rams, trucks, and cranes handled immense weights with terrifying efficiency.

The throne was destined for the open square before the Royal Pyramid. Its foundation consisted of seven tiers of stone, each knee-high and cut with three steps on the front face. The uppermost layer contained a cavity designed to hold Nais's body, surmounted by the vast block forming the throne's seat. Through the night, illuminated by torchlight, relays of stonecutters, masons, and sweating laborers toiled endlessly. Engineers constructed lifting machines, and architects verified the placement of every stone. Whips cracked, men collapsed from exhaustion, only to be replaced immediately by fresh workers. There was no tolerance for delay when the Empress's will was involved.

By dawn, as the square filled with spectators drawn to the spectacle, the debris and scaffolding were cleared away, revealing the bodies of roughly thirty workmen who had died from accidents or sheer exertion. Standing there was the throne, magnificent in its carvings and ready for its final act. The lower section rose more than two man-heights above the ground, composed of courses weighing no less than twenty men each. The upper portion was twice as heavy, carved with a royal snake encircling the chair, its great hooded head looming overhead. Currently suspended high by lifting rams, its purpose was obvious to all.

Summoned by the chamberlains, I emerged from the Royal Pyramid in the cool morning air. Great men arrived with banner-bearers and trumpeters; the middle classes displayed their finest attire; even the poorest, skeletal figures wrapped their heads in colorful turbans to mark the day's "gaiety." Trumpets announced my arrival, and the crowd shouted welcomes. Accompanied by the gorgeous chamberlains walking backward ahead of me, I approached a scarlet awning and took my seat upon the cushions beneath it.

Then came Phorenice, my bride, fresh from sleep and radiant in her splendor. She was carried out in an open litter of gold and ivory by fantastical savages from Europe, her refined features standing in stark, glorious contrast to their brutish appearance. The crowd drew a collective breath of delight. It was impossible not to envy me her choice, and equally impossible to believe anyone present—save perhaps Ylga, hidden somewhere veiled—could imagine I was not overjoyed to win such a lovely wife.

Internally, however, I summoned the iron discipline of my training to mask my true feelings. This was a ghastly ceremony, yet it remained ceremonial. I intended to project a grand demeanor that would leave Phorenice no room for criticism. After everything we had endured, I resolved not to shatter the scheme with visible agony or trembling hands. When the time came, I decided to lay the living body of my love into the hollow beneath the stone as calmly as a priest burying a stranger. I knew she would not betray our secret; with her, too, it was truly "Before all Atlantis."

To my relief, the proceedings lacked mockery. Everything was solemn and impressive. Though the profound sorrow biting at my heart was lost on the vulgar crowd, the outward shape of this double sacrifice would surely not be forgotten, even if its inner meaning remained hidden from them. Phorenice, usually flexible in her moods, demanded rigid exactness and pomp today.

She was lifted by her European attendants to the scarlet awning. I handed her down to the ground. Seated on the cushions, she beckoned me to her side, intertwining our fingers as was customary for Atlantean rulers and their consorts. Before us, a body of soldiers marched forward, parting to reveal Nais in their midst. She wore a metal collar around her neck, secured by chains held by two guards to prevent her from rushing the escorts and meeting a swift death—a common fate for those sentenced to slower agonies.

Pleasantly, she still wore her clothes. Custom dictated that the garments of the condemned belonged to the guards, but since Nais was not stripped, I deduced someone had bribed the sentries. I suspected Ylga's hand in this gesture and felt a surge of gratitude toward her.

Soldiers brought her to the edge of the pavilion's shade. She was ordered to prostrate herself before the Empress, a wise move that spared her rough handling. Her face was pale, yet devoid of fear or defiance; her eyes were calm and natural, reflecting her acceptance of her duty to Atlantis. Watching her, I felt a thrill of love and pride. Outwardly, I remained as impassive as stone, ensuring that whatever Phorenice saw on my face was exactly what she ought to see.

"Nais," the Empress said, "you have eaten from my platter as a fan-girl and drunk from my cup. What was yours, I gave you freely. You should have gained more than gratitude; you should have known the length of the Empress's arm and the heaviness of her hand. Yet, you possess neither. Moreover, you attempted to seize something set apart for myself. You were offered pardon on terms and rejected it. You were foolish. However, today I am inclined to clemency. Soon, seated on that carved granite throne Deucalion has built for me, I shall take my Lord Deucalion as husband. Speak plainly: are you sorry, girl? Name a man you would choose, and I will remember the brightness of this occasion. You shall be pardoned and wed before we rise from these cushions."

"I will not wed," she replied quietly.

"Consider for the last time, Nais, your other option," Phorenice continued. "You will be taken, warm, quick, and beautiful as you stand now, and laid in the hollow beneath the throne-stone. Deucalion, who is to be my husband, will place you in that terrible bed as a symbol that all of Phorenice's enemies shall perish. Then he will release the rams and lower the upper stone into place, and the world shall see your face no more. Look at the bright sky, Nais, fill your lungs with sweet, warm air, and consider what this death means. Believe me, girl, I do not wish to make an example of you unless you force me."

"I will not wed," the prisoner said again, her voice steady.

Phorenice loosened her fingers from my arm and reclined against the cushions. "If the girl presumes on our old familiarity, or thinks I jest, show her now, Deucalion, that I do not."

"The Empress is far from jesting," I stated. "I will perform this deed because it is the Empress's wish, and because her command dictates that a symbol remain forever as a warning to others. Lead your prisoner to the place."

The soldiers turned, and the two guards preparing to use force to drag Nais up the steps found her cooperation willing. She walked with them, her color unchanged. I rose, bowed to the Empress, and followed.

Before the gaze of ten thousand eyes, we displayed no emotion. Not only for the sake of Atlantis, but because both Nais and I possessed a certain pride; we were not like Phorenice to flaunt endearments publicly.

When I bid the guards unfasten the collar restraining Nais's neck and threw my arms around her, demonstrating the rough strength expected of a captor preventing escape, a shockwave of emotion nearly overwhelmed me. Only supreme willpower kept me from breaking the ceremonial facade. I, who had never embraced a woman with anything but rough necessity, now held pressed against me one I loved with infinite tenderness. The realization of how love could link with love was almost my undoing. Yet, outwardly, Nais made no sound, lying half-strangled in my arms as any woman might be borne away by a spoiler.

I trod with her to the topmost step, the vast throne-stone looming overhead. Ensuring visibility for those watching from the pyramid sides and rooftops, despite being beyond Phorenice's direct line of sight, I used brutal force to drag her across the level and deposit her into the hollow. Remarkably, the girl offered no resistance whatsoever.

To prevent her from struggling out and crushing herself—or gaining an easy death when the stone descended—I fitted brazen clamps into grooves above the hollow. Doing this butcher's work with one hand, I held her down fiercely with the other. Godsdamn it, the sweat of agony dripped onto the thirsty stone. I could not suppress it.
