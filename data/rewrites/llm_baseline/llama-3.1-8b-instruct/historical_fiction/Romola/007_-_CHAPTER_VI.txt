As the door creaked open, ushering in the two unexpected visitors, Romola's eyes widened in surprise. Nello, ever the courteous host, led Tito forward with a gentle nudge, his eyes fixed on Romola as he introduced the Greek scholar. "Messer Bardo, I present to you Tito Melema, a learned man from Greece, whose acquaintance with your esteemed family's reputation and extensive library has brought him to our humble abode. His arrival is a testament to the value you place on the pursuit of knowledge, and I daresay, a reflection of the esteem in which you hold the arts of ancient Greece."

Romola's astonishment was palpable, her gaze lingering on Tito's face as if trying to reconcile it with the words of the cunning barber, Nello. She had grown accustomed to scholars who were middle-aged or grey-haired, not youthful and radiant. For an instant, her mind wandered to the memory of her brother, whose smiling face still lingered in her thoughts – a fleeting image of warmth and light that had vanished too soon. Yet, Romola's reserve, forged from her father's tales of the world's injustices, held firm, guarding her emotions behind a mask of quiet determination.

Tito's presence, however, was like a gentle breeze on a spring morning, carrying the scent of blooming flowers and the promise of renewal. His dark eyes sparkled with kindness, and his bright features seemed to radiate a warmth that put Romola at ease. As he bowed to her, his face creased into a subtle smile, and for a moment, the reserve that had shielded her heart began to thaw. A delicate flush rose to her cheeks, only to fade quickly, as if summoned back by the dictates of her will. Tito's gaze, on the other hand, remained soft and inviting, a gentle plea for connection that spoke directly to Romola's soul.
