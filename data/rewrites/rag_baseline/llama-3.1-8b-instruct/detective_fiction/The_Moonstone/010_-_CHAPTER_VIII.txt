As I sit here, reflecting on the events that transpired in the weeks leading up to Miss Rachel's birthday, I find myself drawn to the scent of the sweet-smelling ink that still lingers on my fingertips. It was on the night of the twenty-sixth that I stumbled upon this enigmatic token, hidden away on the gravel walk beneath the faint moonlight. I recall showing it to Mr. Franklin the following morning, sharing with him the tale of its mysterious appearance. His eyes, a piercing shade of blue that seemed to hold a world of understanding, sparkled with amusement as he listened intently. He proposed a theory – one that spoke to the possibility of the Indians having been drawn to our home, their intentions fueled by a misguided notion of magic. According to Mr. Franklin, these individuals believed that by creating signs on a boy's head and pouring ink into his hand, they could unlock hidden realms beyond the boundaries of mortal perception. He spoke of a similar practice in our own country, where people claimed to possess a heightened sense of sight, dubbed by the French as "brightness of sight."

As I pondered Mr. Franklin's words, a question formed on my lips: "Do you think they'll try again, sir?" His response was laced with a hint of skepticism, yet tempered by a glimmer of curiosity. "It depends," he said, "on what the boy can really do. If he can see the Diamond through the iron safe of the bank at Frizinghall, we shall be troubled with no more visits from the Indians for the present. If he can't, we shall have another chance of catching them in the shrubbery, before many more nights are over our heads."

I confess that I waited eagerly for that latter opportunity, my heart beating with anticipation. But fate, it seemed, had other plans. Whether the jugglers had received word of Mr. Franklin's presence at the bank, or whether the boy's abilities truly allowed him to perceive the Diamond's whereabouts, or if it was simply a matter of chance, one thing is certain: the ghost of an Indian never again appeared at our doorstep during the weeks leading up to Miss Rachel's birthday. The jugglers continued to ply their trade in the town, while Mr. Franklin and I remained vigilant, watching and waiting to see what the future might bring. It was a delicate dance, one that required us to tread carefully, lest we alert the rogues to our suspicions.
