The days following the Palmers' departure passed slowly for the Barton family, as they found themselves once again entertaining each other in the evenings. Elinor's mind lingered on the visit, musing over Charlotte's serene happiness, Mr. Palmer's unassuming yet capable nature, and the incongruity that often characterized their union. Her reverie was interrupted by the arrival of new acquaintances, courtesy of Sir John and Mrs. Jennings's zealous efforts to expand their social circle.

On a morning excursion to Exeter, they had encountered two young women, relatives of Mrs. Jennings, which prompted Sir John to extend an invitation for them to visit the park. The young ladies readily accepted, leaving Lady Middleton apprehensive about receiving strangers, particularly those whose elegance and gentility she couldn't verify, despite her husband and mother's assurances. Mrs. Jennings attempted to console her daughter by reminding her that as cousins, they would have to tolerate one another, but her words fell on deaf ears. Lady Middleton's anxiety only intensified as she reprimanded her husband multiple times a day about the impending visit.

The young ladies, Miss Steeles, finally arrived at the park, and their appearance was far from unrefined or unfashionable. Their attire was stylish, their manners polite, and they expressed genuine enthusiasm for the house and its furnishings. Their affection for children further endeared them to Lady Middleton, who proclaimed them to be agreeable young women, a testament to her enthusiasm. Sir John, emboldened by this praise, hastened to inform the Miss Dashwoods of the Steeles' arrival and assured them of their delightful company. However, Elinor remained skeptical, aware that charming individuals could be found in every corner of England, each with their unique characteristics.

Sir John insisted that the entire family visit the park immediately to meet his guests. His benevolence and desire to share his cousins with the world were palpable, but Elinor found it disconcerting to witness his eagerness.
