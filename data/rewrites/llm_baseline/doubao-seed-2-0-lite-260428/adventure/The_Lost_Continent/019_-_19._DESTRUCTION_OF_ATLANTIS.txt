A stooped, white-haired priest stumbled through the press of armed defenders clustered at the pass gate, and tapped me hard on the shoulder.
I snapped my head around, hand already half-drawn to my sword at the interruption. We were moments out from traitor Empress Phorenice’s hordes breaching our outer defences, and every man left at my back was a veteran ready to die where he stood. “What is it?”
“Word has been carried to the Three, my King, of the treachery unfolding here. They’ve sent a command.”
I laughed, breathless, already tasting the fight to come. “Good. Tell them I’m here to see the finest bloodbath of my life before I fall. When I die, I’ll stand guard at the gates of the afterlife behind the stars to welcome the rest of them when their turn comes. My apologies that they’re too old and feeble to pick up a blade and get a taste of the killing themselves.”
The old priest’s face went solemn. “They forbid you to die here, my King. They say they still have use for you. You will quit the gate at once.”
I rounded on him, hot with rage. “I will take no such order. You’ve misheard them. If I am a man, a priest, a soldier, a king, my honour demands no enemy passes this gate while my heart still beats. Go back and throw that lie right in their teeth.”
He smiled, and the line of it held the ghost of the warrior he’d been in his youth. “I knew you’d never believe it. I begged them for a sign, and they gave me this.” He held out a glint of carved gold, the serpent bracelet I’d fastened around Nais’s elbow the day I’d crowned her my queen.
My anger died in my throat. “They stripped that from her?”
“They gave no explanation for how they came by it. Their word is this: the owner waits for you inside the Ark of the Mysteries. The purpose they have for you is tied directly to her.”
I’ll own it freely, every soldier reading this will understand: the lure of standing with my men, sword in hand, and going down fighting, was almost too strong to resist. But Nais’s face rose unbidden before me, and my love for her won out in the end. I gave the formal salute that marked I accepted their order.
I turned to the knot of priests who’d stayed to hold the gate, my throat tight. “You’ll get the fight I’m forced to miss, brothers. I envy you it. But though I am King of Atlantis, I am still one of the Seven, a servant of the Three, and their word is the will of the Highest Gods. Do your worst. I’ll ask you to save me a spot at that star-side guard post you’ll be heading for soon, since I can’t march in there with you.”
They grinned, clashed their weapons together in salute, and I turned and walked up the path before I could change my mind and run back to stand with them.
I never saw that fight, but I knew it was brutal. Long, long minutes dragged by before the first of Phorenice’s hordes crested the far side of the pass, screaming and blood-soaked. That tiny remnant of priests sold their lives for every inch of ground, for every enemy they could cut down before they fell.
The climb up the sacred mountain was long. I passed groves of cypress, half-empty temples, colleges where scholars had studied for centuries, houses that had belonged to the greatest families of the continent. The no-man’s-land that only the highest initiates could cross stood before me, but no sentries barred my path. All the old rules were already burning away with the world below.
Old Zaemon was waiting for me at the boundary temple, his voice trembling as he chanted through a full ancient ritual begging dispensation for my trespass, slow as molasses. Below us, we could already hear the clash of steel at the last gate. The handful of old priests manning the ramparts were bartering their lives for every invader they could kill, but they couldn’t hold out forever. The storm was coming for the sacred mount any minute now. I itched to run, but Zaemon wouldn’t budge until the last line of the rite was spoken.
He led me to the great Ark of the Mysteries at the peak, chiding me for my impatience, and made me kiss its unscratchable obsidian surface in reverence. A second old, emaciated priest I’d never seen before climbed slowly down the nailed battens running up the Ark’s curved side. These two were the last two living initiates of the highest order, the only men who knew all the secrets of the Gods.
Zaemon lifted a shaking finger and swept it over the land spread far below the peak. “Look.”
I followed his gesture, and a cold chill crawled down my spine. The sun hung high in a cloudless blue sky, the air warm and still — but the coasts far out were being hammered by walls of surf hundreds of feet high, waves that rolled inland and never receded. The wide estuary leading to Atlantis’s main city was choked with churning flood, swallowing the harbour walls whole, creeping up into the lower wards of the great city itself. The lower farmlands were already gone, blotted out under roiling grey water. The mountain ranges dotted across the continent were spouting smoke and gouts of flame, their slopes hissing where the floodwaters hit hot rock.
“You see it?” Zaemon asked.
“Part of it.”
“Then hear the rest. The Highest Gods’ patience has run out. The whole of Atlantis, the vast continent stretching far out west past our coasts, every man, woman, child, beast, and temple — all of it is doomed to drown and shatter. This was foreseen thousands of years ago. That Ark you just kissed? The Gods ordered it built for exactly this day. No blade can scratch it, no rock can crack it, no wave can sink it. Inside, all the forbidden knowledge of our people is carved on stone sheets, there’s grain and fresh water enough for two people to survive four years, seeds, weapons, every supply we could possibly need to build a new life far away. The Gods have decreed only two people will survive. A man, a woman, chosen to carry that knowledge across the waves, to raise a new people that will honour the Sun God and the rest of the divinities once Atlantis is gone. The woman is already inside. The Gods have chosen you, Deucalion, to be the man.”
I went numb. “I don’t know if I want to be the last one left.”
“The Gods don’t ask for your wants.” His voice was sharp. “They command you, their faithful servant.”
Habit kicked in, hard. I saluted, and told him I would obey.
The time we had left was vanishing fast. Below us, the flood rose higher, the volcanoes rumbled so hard the ground under our feet shook. The last gate fell. Phorenice’s troops poured through, screaming, swarming up the slopes. But the two old priests didn’t hurry a single line of the initiation ritual that would raise me to their rank, one of the Three. A nearby volcano burst with a roar that shook the mountain when seawater hit its magma chamber. They didn’t flinch. Thousands of panicking soldiers ran through the temple groves below us. They didn’t skip a single chant. They went through every majestic, age-old line of the rite, with nothing but the sky for a ceiling and the Gods for witnesses, and I forced my reeling, distracted mind to memorise every secret, every key to the powers they gave me, even as I watched the sea swallow more and more of the land.
By the time the ritual drew to a close, Phorenice’s troops had flooded onto the peak, wild with panic, knowing the whole world was ending. They were hunting every priest they could find to tear them apart, convinced the old men had called the destruction down on their heads.
“Kneel,” Zaemon said, his voice steady as ever. “Bow to the Earth, your mother, and swear you will never misuse the powers the Gods have granted you over her.”
I knelt, swore, and stood up the third of the highest order of initiates, the third of the Three.
“Brother,” Zaemon said, clapping me on the shoulder, “the two of us will not be coming with you. We will stay here, to give the Gods our final account of our stewardship, to stand beside them behind the stars. Go. Climb into the Ark. Shut its roof door the second anyone tries to break in. You are forbidden to fight, forbidden to take any provocation, no matter how much it stings. The Gods’ will is that you and Nais survive. But linger a moment at the top, if you can. You are one of only two people who will ever carry the story of Atlantis’s death. You should see it all.”
I climbed the battens up the Ark’s side, slipped down the inner stair, and found Nais waiting for me in a small, lamp-lit chamber, her face pale but calm. “Zaemon dragged me here,” she said, reaching for my hand. “Threatened me, said you’d be here soon. He’s my father, gruff as he is, I know he loves us. Can’t we save him?”
I shook my head. “The Ark was never meant for more than two. His mind is made up. He’d rather go face the Gods than hide.”
I kissed her, and climbed back up to the Ark’s roof.
The whole of Atlantis was gone now. The great city had vanished under the waves, the plains were all foaming white flood, the only land left above water was the very peak of the sacred mountain we stood on. A handful of oarless galleys were tossed across what had once been inland forests, until a great wave swallowed them whole. The last of the giant lizards that had roamed the lowlands were drowning in the churning, boiling sea.
Then I spotted a tight, armed force marching straight for us through the panicking mob. Phorenice’s personal guards, their blades still wet with blood, had cut their way through their own terrified troops to make a path for their Empress. Behind them, carried in a gold and ivory litter borne by her European slaves, rode Phorenice herself, her hair cascading red over her shoulders, her face calm, lovely, no trace of fear. Her two small children lay at her feet. Tatho, her scheming chief advisor, marched at her right, his beard curled into a thousand neat ringlets. Ylga, the fan girl who’d once been my friend, walked at her left, her fan in her hand, unruffled.
They halted ten paces from the Ark.
Phorenice lifted a perfectly plucked eyebrow and looked up at me, and laughed. “Well, well. Deucalion. The man I once thought was sweet enough to keep, until he bored me. They made you king, did they? All the little smelly old priests on this peak crowned you their leader?”
“Nais is inside this Ark with me,” I called back. “We two will be the only ones left of Atlantis before long.”
She threw her head back and laughed. “She can have you, dear. I am a Goddess, remember? My arts let me live forever. When all this mess is over, I’ll just go back to the star-side realm I came from. You’re welcome to your little stone box and your rustic queen.”
Zaemon stepped forward, holding the blazing Sun Symbol high in his hand, and roared a curse so loud it cut through the thunder of the rising flood. Phorenice’s face twisted with rage. She screamed for her guards to kill him, tried to leap from the litter to kill him herself — but the power of the Sun Symbol blazed, and her dark arts couldn’t touch him. She sank back against her cushions, beaten, sullen, for the first time I’d ever seen her broken.
Only Ylga broke away from the group, running to the base of the Ark, and looked up at me. The roar of the waves was getting so loud it almost swallowed her words. “Deucalion! I wouldn’t come with you even if you begged me. I don’t take half a man. But I’ll die happy knowing you almost loved me once, and you’ll never forget me.”
A wall of green, churning seawater crested the edge of the peak behind them, swallowing half the panicking soldiery in one single rush. Steam from the flooded volcanoes billowed up in a thick golden mist, turning the whole world hazy. I looked down and saw Zaemon wave the Sun Symbol at me, pointing to the roof door of the Ark, ordering me to shut it.
I slammed the heavy obsidian door shut, shot home the iron bolts, and stumbled back down the stair to Nais’s side, wrapping my arms tight around her. Outside, I heard men battering at the Ark’s sides with axes, screaming, climbing the battens to wrench at the roof door.
A great surge hit, and the whole Ark lurched upward, as light as a feather, torn free of the rock of the peak. The noise of blows and screaming faded, muffled through the Ark’s thick stone walls. The lamp tipped over and went out, and we were left in total darkness, tossed on the heaving peaks of the endless ocean, the only two survivors of a whole lost world.
