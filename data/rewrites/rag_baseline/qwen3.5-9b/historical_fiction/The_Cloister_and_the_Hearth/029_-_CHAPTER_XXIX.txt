Gerard stood transfixed, the sudden intelligence striking him with such violent force that his complexion flickered between pallor and crimson. With a grinding of teeth born of suppressed fury, he prepared to lash out. Men of spirit possess the ferocity of the wild boar; they may flee when cornered by a superior host, driven merely by instinct rather than cowardice, but if the pursuer presses relentlessly, closing the distance until escape becomes impossible, the beast turns. It whirls, charging blindly at the multitude of hunters, ready to gore them all. Gerard had set his jaw for this very charge, his eyes hardening for battle, yet the resolve shattered in an instant. His face crumpled into a look of profound desolation, and he whispered, "But my axe remains in Rhine."

Together, they weighed their options. Prudence urged them to skirt the village entirely, while hunger roared its imperative command: *Buy food.* In the cacophony of survival, hunger spoke the loudest, though prudence argued with the most convincing logic. Ultimately, they resolved to cut directly across the open fields. They paused beside a towering haystack, borrowing two bundles to form a makeshift pallet within a dry ditch, hidden from view yet stinging beneath layers of nettles. Taking turns, they ventured forth into the gloom and returned laden with turnips, gnawing at these bitter roots at irregular intervals throughout their retreat until the sun surrendered to the horizon.

Soon, they emerged once more, shivering violently into the encroaching rain and impenetrable darkness, merging onto the roadway beyond the village. It was a night of dismal despair, black as pitch and whipped by a gale that howled with relentless fury. Visibility vanished; sound was swallowed whole. Neither party could see, hear, be seen, or be heard. For all anyone knew, they drifted past their enemies like spectral phantoms, invisible in the void. Yet, amidst the natural terrors of this storm-lashed obscurity—a night where they seemed to grope and hack their path through solid black marble—their adversaries faded from their minds.

When the moon finally ascended, casting a pale, trembling light, they had traveled leagues beyond Düsseldorf. Still, they pressed onward, trudging through the mud. Suddenly, a massive structure loomed ahead. "Courage!" cried Denys, his voice cutting through the wind. "I believe I recognize this convent. Yes, it is. We have entered the diocese of Jülich. Here, Cologne holds no sway." And in that single declaration, the next moment found them secure within the protective walls.
