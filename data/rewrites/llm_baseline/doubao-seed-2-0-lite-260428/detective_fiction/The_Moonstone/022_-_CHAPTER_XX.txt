The news of Rosanna’s death had run ahead of us up the drive, and we found the entire household staff already in the grip of wild panic. As we passed Lady Verinder’s bedchamber, the door flew violently inward, and my mistress burst out among us, Franklin Blake trailing helplessly behind her, doing his level best to calm her frayed nerves. She was quite beside herself with horror, one hand raised to shake wildly at Sergeant Cuff.
“*You* are answerable for this!” she shrieked. “Gabriel! Pay that wretch his fee at once—drag him out of my sight, and be done with him!”

Sergeant Cuff was the only one of us still in full possession of his wits, the only person fit to meet her rage head-on. “I am no more to blame for this terrible tragedy than your ladyship is,” he said, soft but unshakable. “If in half an hour’s time you still insist I leave this house, I will accept your dismissal. But I will not touch your ladyship’s money to go.”

His tone was perfectly respectful, but it carried a weight that quieted her. She let Franklin lead her back into her chamber, and the door clicked shut behind them. Cuff’s sharp, observant gaze drifted over the huddle of maids, and he marked that while the rest were only shaken with fear, Penelope had dissolved into quiet tears. “As soon as your father has changed out of his wet clothes,” he told her, “come join us in his room. I have questions for you.”

Before that half hour was up, I had dried off, changed into dry linens, and lent Sergeant Cuff a spare set of my own clothes that fit him well enough. Penelope slipped in soon after, and I pulled her straight onto my knee, holding her tight. I don’t think I ever felt so proud of my good, dutiful daughter as I did in that moment, and I prayed over her, blessing her quiet heart. She tucked her face against my chest and looped her arms around my neck, and we sat silent for a minute, both of us thinking of the poor dead girl who had eaten at both our hearts. Cuff stepped over to the window and stared out at the grounds to give us space, and I thanked him for the kindness of it.

Folks of rank get every luxury, and that includes the luxury of wallowing in their feelings as long as they please. Us working folk have no such privilege. Necessity that spares our betters has no pity on us: we learn to tuck our grief and soft feelings back down, and trudge on with our duties as patiently as we can. I don’t complain of it, I only note it as fact. Penelope and I were ready to talk the moment Cuff was.

He asked her first if she knew what had driven her fellow servant to kill herself. As you might guess, Penelope confirmed it was unrequited love for Mr. Franklin Blake. When he asked if she had ever shared that suspicion with anyone else, she said no—she’d kept quiet, for Rosanna’s sake.
I added my own two cents right then. “And for Mr. Franklin’s sake too, my dear. If Rosanna truly died for love of him, it was with no knowledge or fault of his. Let him leave this house today, if he must go, without the useless, cruel weight of that truth hanging over him.”
“Quite right,” Sergeant Cuff said, and fell quiet again, turning Penelope’s account over in his head against some private theory he’d not breathed a word of to any of us.

The half hour was no sooner up than my lady’s bell rang sharp. On my way to answer it, I ran straight into Franklin coming out of his aunt’s sitting parlor. He told me Lady Verinder was ready to see Sergeant Cuff now, and added that he had a short word he wanted to speak to the Sergeant first. We paused in the hall beside the pinned-up railway timetable on our way back to my room.
“Are you truly planning to leave us, sir?” I asked him. “Give Miss Rachel a little time, surely she’ll come back to her senses.”
“She will come to her senses,” Franklin said, “the moment she learns I am gone for good, and she will never set eyes on me again.”
I thought he spoke out of resentment for the way Rachel had snubbed him, but it was far worse than that. My lady had noticed ever since the police first arrived in the house that the bare mention of Franklin’s name was enough to send Rachel’s temper flaring out of control. He had loved his cousin too much to admit that hard truth to himself, not until she stormed off to her aunt’s house without a word to him. Now his eyes were opened, and he had made the only resolution a man of any pride could make: to leave the house for good.

What he had to say to Cuff he said openly, with me standing right there. He explained that Lady Verinder was ready to own she had spoken far too hastily earlier, and asked if Cuff would consent to take his full fee now, leave the Moonstone investigation exactly where it stood, and be on his way.
Sergeant Cuff shook his head. “No, sir. My fee is paid for doing my duty. I will not take a penny of it until my work here is done.”
“I don’t follow you,” Franklin said.
“I will explain,” Cuff answered. “When I took this post, I swore I would shine every necessary light on the matter of the missing Diamond. I am ready now to keep that promise. I will go to Lady Verinder, lay every fact of this case out plain before her, and tell her exactly what steps to take to recover the Moonstone. Once that is done, my responsibility is discharged. Then she may choose for herself whether to let me continue my work, or send me off. Only then will I take my fee.”
Those words reminded us all that even a detective on the force has a reputation to guard, and no one could argue his logic. There was no more to be said on the matter.

As I stood up to lead him to my lady’s chamber, Cuff asked if Franklin wished to sit in on the interview. Franklin said he did not, unless Lady Verinder specifically requested it. He leaned in and whispered to me as we turned to go: “I know exactly what that man is about to say about Rachel. I love her far too well to sit here and hear those accusations, and keep my temper. Leave me by myself.”
I left him there, miserable, leaning his weight on the windowsill ledge, his face buried in his hands. Penelope was peeping around the doorframe, dying to slip in and comfort him. If I was in Franklin’s shoes, I’d have called her over at once. When one woman has used you ill, there is no comfort like spilling your troubles to another—nine times out of ten, she will take your side without a second’s hesitation. I would not be surprised if he did call her in once my back was turned, and I will say for my daughter: she would have done anything in her power to soothe Mr. Franklin Blake.

Sergeant Cuff and I proceeded on to my lady’s room. The last time we met with her, she could barely bring herself to lift her eyes from the book she had spread open on her table. This time, there was a marked change. She met Cuff’s gaze straight on, her own as steady and unflinching as his. The hard, proud Verinder family grit blazed in every line of her face, and I knew instantly: Sergeant Cuff had finally met his match. No woman of Lady Verinder’s mettle, strung up and braced to hear the very worst secrets he could lay bare, would flinch an inch.
