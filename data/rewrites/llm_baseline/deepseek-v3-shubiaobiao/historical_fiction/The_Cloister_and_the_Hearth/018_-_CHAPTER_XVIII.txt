Ghysbrecht Van Swieten tossed restlessly through the night, his mind churning with unease. Fear of the storm’s wrath kept him from joining the search of Peter’s house, but as soon as the last thunderclap faded, he rose, saddled his mule, and rode hard for Sevenbergen.

At the *Three Kings*, he found his men sprawled in drunken slumber—some slumped over tables, others curled on the floor. With a snarl, he roused them, listening impatiently as they recounted their failure.

*"Fools!"* he spat. *"You let him slip through your fingers. Did you even think to look beneath the girl’s bed?"*

One of the men grunted. *"No space for a man there."*

*"And how would you know if you didn’t look?"* Ghysbrecht’s voice was a whip-crack. *"You should have torn the place apart—searched every corner, tested every panel!"*

Dierich Brower staggered to his feet, rubbing his eyes. *"If you find him now, call me a horse and not a man."*

Within moments, Peter’s house was surrounded once more. Ghysbrecht, his face a mask of grim determination, left his mule with Jorian Ketel and led the charge inside. The rooms stood empty—no Peter, no daughter, no sign of their fugitive guest.

Then a shout rang out. One of the men pointed through the open window, where across the fields, walking calmly beside Margaret and Martin, was the very man they sought.

A triumphant cry burst from Ghysbrecht’s lips. He hurtled down the stairs, vaulted onto his mule, and spurred his men into furious pursuit.
