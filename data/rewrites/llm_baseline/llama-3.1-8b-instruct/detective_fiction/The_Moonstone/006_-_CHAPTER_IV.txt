Just as I was drifting off into a peaceful reverie, the clatter of plates and dishes from the servants' hall signaled that dinner was ready. I made my way to my sitting room, where I'd taken to dining alone, leaving the staff to their own meal. I wished them a hearty appetite, and then settled back into my chair, feeling the gentle creak of the wooden slats beneath me.

As I stretched my legs, a commotion at the doorway caught my attention. Not my dear daughter Penelope this time, but Nancy, the kitchen maid, burst into the room, looking quite perturbed. I stood up to let her pass, taking note of the scowl etched on her face – a sight I never tolerated in my household. "What's wrong, Nancy?" I asked, my voice firm but kind.

Nancy tried to brush past me, but I wouldn't let her. Instead, I gently took her by the ear, a habit of mine when I wanted to show my approval. "What's troubling you, child?" I asked again. "Rosanna's late for dinner, and I'm supposed to fetch her in," Nancy replied, her tone a mixture of frustration and resignation.
