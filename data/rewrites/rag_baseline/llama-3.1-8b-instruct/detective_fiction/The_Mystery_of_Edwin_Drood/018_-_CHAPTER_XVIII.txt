Silence seeped into the worn stone walls of Cloisterham's ancient cathedral, a gentle hush that seemed to reverberate within the hollows of its weathered stones. In the coffee room of the Crozier, a lone figure sat, shrouded in the dim light that struggled to penetrate the grimy windows. Dick Datchery, a man with a shock of white hair that seemed to shimmer like a wild animal's coat, sipped his tea, his eyes fixed on the worn wooden table before him.

As the waiter, a man with a tired expression, approached, Datchery's gaze flickered, his eyes narrowing into a thoughtful squint. "A fair lodging for a single gentleman such as myself," he said, his voice low and measured, "can be found in these parts, I presume?"

The waiter nodded, a gesture of resignation rather than enthusiasm. "Indeed, sir. We have a selection of...inconvenient lodgings available." His eyes wandered to the dusty window, where a faint glimmer of sunlight struggled to penetrate the grime.

Datchery's eyes followed, his gaze lingering on the cobweb-shrouded sill. "Something old, perhaps," he said, his voice barely above a whisper. "Something venerable, with a certain...inconvenience." His hand rose, gesturing to the waiter, who hastened to retrieve his hat from the peg. "What do you see written there?" Datchery asked, his voice detached, as if observing a stranger.

The waiter's eyes scanned the hat, his brow furrowed in concentration. "Datchery," he read aloud, his voice steady. "Now I know your name, sir." He hung the hat back on its peg, his movements economical. "We have several options that might fit your description," he said, his tone measured. "However, I must admit that an architectural lodging might be a challenge."

Datchery's face creased into a faint smile, his eyes glinting with a hint of amusement. "Cathedraly, perhaps?" he suggested, his voice tinged with a dry humor.

The waiter's expression turned puzzled, his hand rising to rub his chin. "Mr. Tope might be able to assist you in that regard," he said finally, his voice hesitant. "He's the Verger, you see. Mrs. Tope used to let lodgings, but they've been vacant for some time."
