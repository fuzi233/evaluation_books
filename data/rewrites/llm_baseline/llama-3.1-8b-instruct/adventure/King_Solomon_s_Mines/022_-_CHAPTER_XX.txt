As I strolled along the winding banks of the life-giving stream, the warm sun beating down upon my shoulders, I chanced upon a sight that made my heart skip a beat. Twenty yards ahead, nestled beneath the boughs of a majestic fig tree, stood a humble hut crafted from grass and withes, its door facing the gentle flow of the water. I rubbed my eyes, wondering if the desert sun had addled my brain, for what could a hut possibly be doing in this unforgiving expanse? Yet, even as I pondered, the door creaked open, and a white man emerged, his movements stiff and labored due to a pronounced limp. His face was shrouded in a wild tangle of black beard, and I felt a shiver run down my spine as he regarded me with a mixture of confusion and longing.

"What in the name of the ancients?" I muttered to myself, my eyes fixed upon the apparition. The words were barely out of my mouth when Sir Henry and Good appeared, their faces set with curiosity. Together, we beheld the stranger, and I turned to my companions with a questioning gaze. "Is that a white man, or am I beset by the desert's madness?"

Sir Henry's eyes narrowed as he took in the scene, while Good's expression remained inscrutable. It was then that the bearded man let out a great cry and began to hobble towards us, his limbs faltering beneath him. Before he collapsed, Sir Henry sprang to his side, his voice filled with emotion. "By the gods, it is my brother George!"

The sound of their reunion sent another figure bursting forth from the hut, a rifle slung over his shoulder. He spotted me and let out a cry of recognition. "Macumazahn, don't you recall me, Baas? I'm Jim the hunter. I misplaced the note you entrusted to me, and we've been stranded here for nearly two years." The man fell to his knees, his body wracked with sobs as he clasped my legs.

"You scoundrel!" I exclaimed, shaking my head in exasperation. "You should be well scolded!" Meanwhile, the bearded man rose to his feet, his eyes fixed intently upon Sir Henry.
