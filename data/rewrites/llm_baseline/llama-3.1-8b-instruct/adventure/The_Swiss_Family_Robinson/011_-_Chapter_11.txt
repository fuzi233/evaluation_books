As the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, I rose with renewed purpose, exclaiming, 'Let us conclude this task forthwith!' My family stirred, and together we set to work on the day's laborious duty. With the morning's routine complete, we turned our attention to the pressing of the blubber, carefully extracting the purest oil from the tubs and collecting it in vessels beneath. The smell of the process hung heavy in the air, but my wife, ever the pillar of fortitude, endured it with characteristic equanimity.

However, as we worked, she broached a proposal that would change our circumstances irrevocably. 'Why not establish a new colony on this island?' she suggested, her words laced with conviction. 'There, our abandoned animals would be safe from marauding beasts and other scavengers, and we could boil whale-blubber and extract train oil without disturbance.' Her reasoning struck a chord within me, and I saw the sense in her words. The boys, ever enamored with novelty, were ecstatic at the prospect, and I knew that I had to weigh the benefits against the drawbacks.

But as I pondered the proposal, my mind wandered to the putrid carcass that lay before us. We couldn't simply abandon it, no matter how inconvenient it was. I reminded the boys of the importance of allowing nature to run its course, and that the island's denizens – birds, insects, and the elements themselves – would eventually cleanse the air and reduce the whale to its skeletal remains. They nodded in agreement, and we continued our work with a newfound sense of resolve.

As I stood amidst the chaos of our makeshift workshop, an idea began to take shape in my mind. I envisioned a device that would harness the power of human ingenuity to simplify our labors. I took an iron bar, roughly hewn from the island's resources, and set about constructing a rowing-machine. The design was rough, but I was determined to see it through. I fashioned a series of ribbed machinery, attached flat paddles to either end, and created a system of cogwheels that would convert the mechanical energy into propulsion.

Fritz and I worked tirelessly to bring the machine to life, and when we finally launched it onto the water, it performed admirably. The paddles struck the surface with a rhythmic splash, propelling the boat forward with surprising speed. Our family cheered and clapped, basking in the glory of our invention.
