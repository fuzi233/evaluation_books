I leave the realm of dreams to step firmly onto the ground of fact.

It was the final day of the 1999th year of our era. The rhythmic tapping of rain against the ceiling had long since heralded the descent of night. I sat in the quiet company of my wife, lost in contemplation of the year just passed, the century approaching, and the dawn of the new Millennium.

[Note: When I use the word "sitting," I do not refer to the physical posture you of Spaceland understand, for we possess no feet to support such distinctions. We cannot "sit" or "stand" in your sense any more than a sole of a shoe can. Yet, we intuitively grasp the varying degrees of volition implied by these states, often signaled to an observer by a subtle intensification of our inner light. However, time presses upon me, forbidding further discourse on such nuances.]

My four sons and two orphaned grandchildren had withdrawn to their respective chambers. My wife remained, keeping vigil as the old millennium faded and the new one began.

I was deeply absorbed in thought, replaying a conversation with my youngest grandson, a brilliant hexagon of exceptional promise and perfect angles. Earlier, my uncles and I had engaged him in a lesson of Sight Recognition, rotating on our centers to test his ability to discern our positions. His answers were so precise that I felt compelled to reward him with a glimpse of Arithmetic applied to Geometry.

I had arranged nine squares, each an inch in dimension, to form a larger square with a side of three inches. Though we cannot perceive the interior of a shape, I demonstrated to the boy that the area could be calculated by squaring the side length: "Thus," I explained, "we know that $3^2$, or 9, represents the number of square inches within a square whose side measures three inches."

The little hexagon pondered this briefly before asking, "But you have taught me to raise numbers to the third power. I suppose $3^3$ must hold some meaning in Geometry; what does it signify?"

"Nothing at all," I replied, "at least not within the confines of Geometry, for our world possesses only Two Dimensions." I then proceeded to show him how a point traversing a distance of three inches creates a line ($3$), and how a line of three inches moving parallel to itself generates a square ($3^2$).

Suddenly, my grandson interrupted, his curiosity overriding his deference. "Well, then, if a point moving three inches makes a line ($3$), and a line moving parallel to itself makes a square ($3^2$), it must be that a square moving somehow parallel to itself creates something else ($3^3$)—something of three inches every way!"

"Go to bed," I snapped, slightly irritated by the interruption. "If you wished to retain your wits, you would cease speaking nonsense."

The boy vanished in disgrace. I returned to my seat beside my wife, attempting to reflect on the year 1999 and the possibilities of 2000, yet the echo of the boy's question lingered. Only a few grains of sand remained in the hourglass. As I turned the glass northward for the last time of the old millennium, I muttered aloud, "The boy is a fool."

Instantly, a Presence filled the room, and a chilling breath swept through my very soul. "He is no such thing," cried my wife, "and you dishonor your own grandson by such slander." I paid her no heed. Scanning every angle of the darkness, I saw nothing, yet I *felt* the Presence, shivering as the cold whisper repeated. I leaped to my feet. "What is the matter?" asked my wife. "There is no draft; what do you seek? There is nothing."

There was indeed nothing visible. I resumed my seat, repeating, "The boy is a fool; $3^3$ has no meaning in Geometry."

Immediately, a distinct voice answered, "The boy is not a fool; and $3^3$ possesses an obvious geometrical meaning."

Both my wife and I heard the words, though she failed to grasp their significance. We both surged forward toward the source of the sound. Horror seized us when we beheld a Figure. At first glance, it resembled a woman viewed from the side, but a fleeting observation revealed that its extremities dissolved into dimness too rapidly to belong to the Female Sex. It might have been a circle, save that it altered its size in a manner impossible for any regular figure known to us.

My wife lacked my experience and the composure to analyze these anomalies. Driven by the instinctive jealousy and haste characteristic of her sex, she instantly concluded that a woman had slipped through a hidden aperture. "How did this person enter?" she exclaimed. "You promised me, my dear, that our new home would have no ventilators."

"There are none," I replied. "But why do you assume the stranger is a woman? I perceive by my Sight Recognition—"

"Oh, I have no patience with your Sight Recognition," she retorted. "'Feeling is believing,' and 'A straight line to the touch is worth a circle to the sight'"—proverbs beloved by the Frailer Sex in Flatland.

"Well," I said, fearing to provoke her further, "if it must be so, demand an introduction."

Assuming her most gracious demeanor, my wife advanced toward the Stranger. "Permit me, Madam, to feel and be felt by..." She recoiled abruptly. "Oh! It is not a woman, and there are no angles, not a single trace of one. Could I have so grievously offended a perfect Circle?"

"I am, in a certain sense, a Circle," replied the voice, "and a more perfect Circle than any in Flatland; but to speak more accurately, I am many circles in one." He added more gently, "I bear a message to your husband that I cannot deliver in your presence. If you would allow us to retire for a few moments—"

My wife refused to entertain the suggestion that our august visitor should inconvenience himself. Assuring the Circle that her own hour of rest had long passed, and offering numerous apologies for her recent indiscretion, she finally retreated to her chamber.

I glanced at the hourglass. The last sands had fallen. The third Millennium had begun.
