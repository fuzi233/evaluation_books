In the stillness of evening, Gerard found himself in the tranquil company of a monk, whose inventive genius had birthed a majestic sundial in the prior's garden, a waterwheel for the convent's needs, and a delicate winnowing machine for the grain. The air was alive with the sweet scent of blooming herbs, and the gentle rustle of leaves as the monk's fingers danced across the strings of his psaltery. Gerard, though skilled in the humble art of playing, was mesmerized by the monk's divine touch, and he listened intently as the old man revealed the intricacies of music. Their conversation flowed effortlessly, bridging the gap between youth and age, as they discovered a shared love for the arts.

As the sun dipped below the horizon, Gerard confided in the monk about his journey, sharing tales of his recent adventures, though carefully omitting the more perilous aspects. The monk listened attentively, his eyes twinkling with a deep understanding, and when Gerard finished speaking, he let out a sigh. "Alas, my young friend," he said, "I too have walked the paths of the world, but never have I encountered the dangers that beset you. You must be cautious, for inns are treacherous places, infested with rogues and those who revel in darkness. Instead, seek out the peace of monasteries, where the air is pure and the souls are kind."

Gerard nodded, taking the monk's words to heart, and the old man's expression turned stern. "Tell me, when was the last time you confessed your sins?" he asked, his voice firm but not unkind. Gerard hesitated, aware of his own transgressions, and the monk's words cut deep. "Better than a fortnight," he admitted, his cheeks flushing with shame. The monk's response was immediate, his voice filled with a deep sense of urgency. "Then you must seek absolution, my child. Come, let us proceed to the chapel, and may the waters of forgiveness wash away your guilt."

As they walked towards the chapel, Gerard felt a sense of relief wash over him, but the monk's demeanor changed suddenly, his eyes clouding with concern. "Not to me, my son," he whispered urgently, his voice barely audible.
