The scent of sugar and spices wafted from the confectioner's shop like the promise of a warm summer day. Amidst the riotous colors of Camden Town, the shop stood out like a beacon, its windows ablaze with an assortment of treats that seemed almost too beautiful to eat. Young men and women of all ages gathered at the corner, their faces pressed against the glass as they gazed at the treasures within. Among them stood a young man, John Turnbull Angus, his rugged features softened by the gentle glow of the shop lights. His bright red hair was mussed, and his eyes, though tired, sparkled with a quiet intensity.

Angus carried a worn portfolio under his arm, its contents a testament to his artistic talent. His uncle, an admiral, had once disinherited him for his socialist views, but Angus's determination had only grown stronger. As he pushed open the door, a bell above it rang out, and the young woman behind the counter looked up, her dark eyes flashing with curiosity.

She was a vision in black, her slender figure a striking contrast to the vibrant colors surrounding her. Angus approached her with a quiet confidence, his voice low and smooth as he placed his order. "One halfpenny bun and a small cup of black coffee, please." A pause, and then he added, "And I'd like to marry you."

The girl's expression froze, her dark eyes narrowing as she searched his face. For a moment, the only sound was the soft hum of the shop's machinery and the distant chatter of the other customers. Then, with a quiet dignity, she spoke. "Those are jokes I don't allow."

Angus's gaze never wavered, his eyes locking onto hers with an unspoken intensity. "Really and truly," he said, his voice low and serious, "it's as serious as the halfpenny bun. It's expensive, like the bun; one pays for it. It's indigestible, like the bun. It hurts."

The young woman's eyes seemed to bore into his very soul, as if searching for a glimmer of truth. And then, to Angus's surprise, a faint smile played on her lips. She sat down in a chair, her movements economical and deliberate.
