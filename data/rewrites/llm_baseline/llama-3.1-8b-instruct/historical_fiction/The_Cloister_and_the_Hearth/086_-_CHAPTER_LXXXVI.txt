The warm scent of fresh bread wafted through the cloistered halls of the church, mingling with the faint fragrance of incense, as Father Clement ascended to the pulpit. The afternoon sun cast a kaleidoscope of colors across the stone floor, drawing his gaze upward to the vaulted ceiling. His thoughts, however, were not with the architecture, but with the congregation before him – a sea of faces, each one a story waiting to be told. As he began to speak, his words wove a tapestry of hope and redemption, a message that resonated deeply with the gathered faithful.

As he spoke, his eyes wandered to the south aisle, where a shaft of sunlight illuminated the figure of Margaret Brandt, her auburn hair aglow like a sunset over the rolling hills. Her slender form leaned against a white column, her eyes cast downward, lost in thought. The gentle rustle of her dress and the soft lilt of her breathing transported him back to a time long past, when their love had burned bright as the candles that lit the altar.

For an instant, time stood still. The world outside receded, and all that remained was the memory of what they had shared. The faces before him blurred, and he was alone with the ghost of his beloved. His heart, once frozen in grief, now swelled with longing. The words on his lips faltered, and his voice cracked, as if the very weight of his emotions threatened to consume him.

Then, in the blink of an eye, she vanished. Yet, before disappearing, their gazes met, a fleeting glance that spoke volumes. Though not a recognition, it was a spark of connection, a hint of the deep bond they had forged in the past. Father Clement's world reeled, his senses overwhelmed by the shock of seeing her again. He struggled to regain his composure, forcing himself to continue speaking, his words tumbling forth in a jumbled mix of sorrow and joy.

As the sermon drew to a close, he descended from the pulpit, his mind racing with questions. Had she truly appeared to him, or was it merely a product of his fevered imagination? The answer, however, seemed clear: she had shown herself to him, and now he knew that she lay nearby, perhaps even beneath the very pillar where she had revealed herself.
