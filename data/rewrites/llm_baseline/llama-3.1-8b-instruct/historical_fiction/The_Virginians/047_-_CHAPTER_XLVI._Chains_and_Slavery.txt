As Harry sat in the dimly lit cell, the weight of his circumstances slowly began to sink in. Remorse for past mistakes and foolish decisions washed over him like a cold wave, and the indignity of being arrested stung his pride. Yet, he was convinced that this was merely a temporary setback. He had twenty friends who would surely come to his aid – Mr. Draper, the astute businessman who had always been eager to please; his kind uncle, the Baronet, who had offered him a refuge in his home and treated him like a son; his cousin Castlewood, who had amassed impressive fortunes through his cunning; his noble acquaintances at the Chocolate-House, and his beloved Aunt Bernstein. Any one of them would provide him with the assistance he needed, although some of the relatives might offer a gentle reprimand for his imprudence. The primary concern was to handle the situation discreetly, for Mr. Warrington was keen to keep his predicament out of the public eye, as a gentleman of his standing should not be associated with such a mundane process as arrest.

But, as he pondered his situation, Harry couldn't shake off the feeling that the world would soon learn of his misfortune. He thought of the Chocolate-House, where his friends would likely be discussing his arrest over dinner, and how Mr. Selwyn would probably make light of it. The thought of March gloating over their bet and the possibility of losing made Harry's anxiety spike. He imagined the whispers and snickers that would follow him everywhere he went, and the difficulty of regaining his reputation.

As the first light of dawn crept into the cell, Harry made a decision. He wouldn't turn to his relations or his esteemed friends at White's for help just yet. Instead, he would seek the counsel of Sampson, a man who had faced similar predicaments and would know exactly how to guide him through this trying time. With a newfound sense of determination, Harry dispatched a messenger to Long Acre, hoping that Sampson would arrive soon to offer his sage advice and help him navigate the treacherous waters of his situation.
