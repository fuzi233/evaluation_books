The clash of battle dragged me back to consciousness. Disoriented, I blinked at the dim chamber around me—the chains, the prisoners, the savage beasts frozen in wary silence. Then the sounds came again: the scrape of claws, the snarls of banths, the ragged breathing of a warrior. Beyond the wall, Tars Tarkas fought for his life.

I staggered to my feet. The prisoners watched me—some with hatred, others with desperate hope. Among them, a young red Martian woman stood out, her copper skin gleaming in the faint light. Her eyes held intelligence, defiance. She had warned me earlier, saved me from an ambush.

"The door won’t yield to force," she called as I raised my sword against the golden panel. "The mechanism obeys a touch, not steel."

I turned. "You know how to open it?"

"Free me, and I will."

The keys were on the corpse of the fallen guard. She selected one with practiced ease, and the shackles at her waist fell away. Without hesitation, she pressed a needle-like key into a nearly invisible seam. The wall pivoted, and the floor beneath me lurched forward, carrying me into chaos.

Tars Tarkas stood battered but unbroken, his back to the corner, facing six snarling banths. His green hide was ribboned with wounds, his strength fading—yet he grinned as I entered.

Before I could strike, the woman stepped past me, unarmed, and uttered a single word.

The banths froze.

Another command, and they slunk to her heels like chastened hounds. One by one, she led them through the panel and sealed it behind her, leaving us in stunned silence.

Tars Tarkas exhaled. "I heard your fight," he rasped. "But the gunshot… I thought you dead."

I told him of the therns I’d slain. Together, we searched for an exit, but the walls defied us—until the panel slid open once more. The woman returned, her gaze sharp.

"Who are you," she demanded, "to defy the Valley Dor?"

"John Carter of Helium," I said. "And this is Tars Tarkas, a jeddak. We seek no death here—only escape."

Her lips curled. "Escape? The therns rule this hell. They eat the flesh of those who die in their traps. I was to be their next meal."

I recoiled. "Then help us. Expose their lies."

For a heartbeat, she hesitated. Then, with a nod, she freed the other prisoners—ten red Martians, weary but resolute. Armed with stolen blades and revolvers, we followed her through twisting passages, climbing toward a storeroom of weapons.

But fate intervened.

In a vast chamber, we faced a golden-circled thern, his scalp crowned with a wig of yellow locks. Thuvia shot him dead without hesitation.

"Look," she breathed, stripping the wig from his bald skull. "You wear his face now."

The disguise was flawless. Clad in the thern’s robes, I became Sator Throg, a Holy Thern of the Tenth Cycle. The prisoners carried the corpse as we pressed on, arming ourselves in the storeroom before exhaustion claimed me.

As I collapsed onto the cold floor, Thuvia’s voice echoed in my fading thoughts:

"The therns are everywhere. Even if we leave this mountain, their reach is endless."

Sleep took me. The battle was far from over.
