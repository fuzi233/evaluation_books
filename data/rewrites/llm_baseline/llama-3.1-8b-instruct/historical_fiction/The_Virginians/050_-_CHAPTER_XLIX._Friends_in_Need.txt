As George Warrington urged his horse through the misty London night, the rhythmic clip-clop of hooves echoed through the deserted streets of Strand and Fleet. The gas lamps cast flickering shadows on the pavement, making the journey seem even longer than the grueling ride from Marlborough. He had traveled this route countless times before, but tonight it felt like an eternity since he'd arrived in Bristol, sending word to his brother Harry of his miraculous return from captivity. Little did he know, the letter had fallen into the wrong hands – those of the French privateers, who had seized it along with the American ship.

Upon his arrival in London, George made his way to Harry's lodgings in Clarges Street, near Bond Street. The maid-servant who answered the door was taken aback by the resemblance between the two brothers and let out a terrified shriek, fleeing back to her mistress. Mrs. Ruff, Harry's landlady, hesitated to reveal the truth, fearing the consequences of admitting that her lodger was, in fact, a prisoner. "Mr. Warrington has left his lodgings," she said evasively, "I don't know where he is." George, however, was well-versed in the layout of London, having studied the city maps extensively. He recalled that Aunt Bernstein lived nearby and might be able to provide information about Harry's whereabouts.

George had heard stories of Aunt Bernstein's kindness to his brother through Harry's letters from Virginia. Even Madam Esmond, though initially hesitant, had grown fond of the Baroness, particularly after receiving a heartfelt letter from her that had prompted George to hasten his journey to Europe. As he pondered his next move, George couldn't help but think of the letter's impact on their family's dynamics. Rachel Esmond rarely spoke of her eldest daughter, and Madam Esmond Warrington never mentioned her sister. The weight of the past hung heavy, and George wondered if the wounds of the past would ever fully heal.

As he dismounted his horse, a young lad named Gumbo emerged from the shadows, stuttering out a few words about his master's condition and current predicament. George's demeanor turned stern, and he felt a sense of duty to rescue his brother from this difficult situation. Little did he know, the complexities of London's underbelly would soon test his resolve and challenge his understanding of the city's harsh realities.
