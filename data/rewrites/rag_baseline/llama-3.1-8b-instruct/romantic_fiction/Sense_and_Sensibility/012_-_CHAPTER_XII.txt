As Elinor and Marianne strolled through the dew-kissed gardens of their family's modest cottage the next morning, Marianne's countenance radiated a radiant joy, her words tumbling forth like a sweet melody. She shared the news of Willoughby's generosity, of the fine horse he had bred himself on his Somersetshire estate, one that would be a perfect match for a lady's gentle frame. Her sister listened intently, though a flicker of concern danced across her features. Marianne's thoughtlessness and impulsive nature were well-known to Elinor, yet the extravagance of this gesture caught her off guard.

Without considering the practicalities – the added expense of maintaining a second horse, the need for a servant to ride it, or the construction of a stable to accommodate them – Marianne had accepted the gift with unbridled enthusiasm. Her mind was already conjuring visions of herself and Elinor galloping across the rolling downs, the wind in their hair and the sun on their faces. For a moment, she refused to acknowledge the unpleasant realities that lay hidden beneath the surface of this seemingly idyllic scenario.

Elinor, ever the voice of reason, gently coaxed her sister back to reality. She pointed out the financial implications, the strain on their mother's resources, and the imprudence of accepting a gift from a man they barely knew. Marianne's defenses rose in response, her words growing more heated as she insisted that her connection with Willoughby was deeper than mere acquaintance. She argued that true understanding between people was not forged by time or circumstance, but by the alignment of their dispositions. Seven years might not suffice for some individuals to form meaningful bonds, while seven days could be enough for others.

Elinor chose to let the matter drop, aware of her sister's stubborn nature and the futility of opposing her on this point. Instead, she appealed to Marianne's love for their mother, highlighting the potential consequences of her imprudent decision. By emphasizing the burden their mother would bear, Marianne was eventually persuaded to reconsider, promising to decline the gift and inform Willoughby of her decision when next they met.

Her words were carried out, and when Willoughby arrived at the cottage later that day, Elinor overheard Marianne express her regret to him in hushed tones.
