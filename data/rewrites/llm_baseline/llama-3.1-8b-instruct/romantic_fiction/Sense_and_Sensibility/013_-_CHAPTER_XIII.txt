As the sun rose over the rolling hills surrounding Barton Park, Elinor's excitement began to wane, tempered by the damp air that clung to her skin like a damp shroud. She had been looking forward to their excursion to Whitwell, envisioning a day filled with laughter and good company, but now it seemed that fate had other plans. The rain that had fallen throughout the night still lingered, casting a misty veil over the landscape.

The group gathered in the grand dining hall, eager to break their fast before embarking on their journey. The morning light streaming through the windows highlighted the vibrant hues of the flowers in the vases, but even their beauty couldn't dispel the sense of disappointment that hung in the air. The atmosphere was thick with anticipation, as if everyone was holding their breath, waiting for something to happen.

Just as they sat down to eat, the letters arrived. Amidst the pile of correspondence, one caught Colonel Brandon's eye. He took it, his expression unreadable, and hastily excused himself from the table. The room fell silent, with all eyes fixed on him as he disappeared from view.

"What's wrong with Brandon?" Sir John asked, breaking the tension.

No one knew, but speculation ran rampant. "I hope he hasn't received some terrible news," Lady Middleton ventured, her brow furrowed with concern.

Colonel Brandon reappeared a few minutes later, his face a mask of composure. "No bad news, I assure you," he said, his voice steady.

Mrs. Jennings, ever the inquisitive one, pressed him for more information. "Was it from Avignon? I hope it's not about your sister's health?"

"No, ma'am," he replied, his tone measured. "It's just a letter of business from town."

But Mrs. Jennings wasn't convinced. "How could you be so flustered if it was just a letter of business?" she asked, her eyes sparkling with curiosity.

Colonel Brandon hesitated, his color rising. "My dear madam, please," Lady Middleton intervened, her voice a gentle reminder of propriety.

Mrs. Jennings, undeterred, continued to probe. "Is it perhaps news about your cousin Fanny's marriage?" she suggested.

"No, indeed it's not," Colonel Brandon replied, his cheeks flushing.
