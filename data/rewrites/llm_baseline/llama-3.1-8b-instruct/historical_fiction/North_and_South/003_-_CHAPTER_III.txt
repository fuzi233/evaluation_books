As the morning sunlight poured into the drawing room, Margaret's thoughts turned to Mr. Henry Lennox, and the inquiry he had made about her daily routine. It seemed like only a moment ago that she had been pondering his interest, and now, as she set down her needlework, she rose to greet him. "Tell Mama, Sarah," she called out, "Mama and I have so many questions for you about Edith; I'm ever so grateful you could come."

Mr. Lennox's deep voice rumbled a gentle response, his tone softer than before. "Didn't I promise I would?" Margaret smiled, her eyes sparkling like sunlight on a still pond. "But I heard of you all the way in the Highlands, and I never thought Hampshire would be part of the picture." She walked towards him, her steps light as a summer breeze.

"Oh, our young couple were quite the adventurous sort," Mr. Lennox explained, a hint of amusement in his voice, "always climbing mountains and sailing across lakes. I thought they needed guidance, a steady hand to keep them out of trouble. And indeed, they did – they were quite beyond my uncle's management, keeping him in a state of constant worry. When I realized how ill-prepared they were to be alone, I felt it was my duty to stay with them until I'd seen them safely on their journey."

Margaret's eyes widened in surprise. "You've been to Plymouth? Edith never mentioned that. She's written in such haste lately – did they truly set sail on Tuesday?" Mr. Lennox nodded, producing a small note from his pocket. "They sailed, and I was relieved of many responsibilities. Edith sent all her regards, and I believe I have a little message for you."

As Margaret took the note, her gaze flickered with a mix of curiosity and hesitation. She excused herself, saying she needed to inform her mother again – perhaps Sarah had made an error – and slipped out of the room. When she was gone, Mr. Lennox's eyes roamed the space, taking in the warm glow of the sunlit room. The open window allowed the scent of blooming roses and honeysuckle to waft in, and the vibrant colors of the verbenas and geraniums on the lawn seemed to dance in the breeze.
