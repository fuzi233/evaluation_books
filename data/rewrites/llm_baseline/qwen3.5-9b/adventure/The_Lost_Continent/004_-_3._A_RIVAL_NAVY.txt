As our fleet crested the horizon toward the coast of Atlantis, Tob's mastery of his modern instruments guided us with astonishing precision. Yet, even with his skill, we still faced a ten-day odyssey upstream before reaching the great arm of the sea where the capital lay hidden.

The mere sight of the land, coupled with the scent of earth and vegetation drifting on the breeze, acted as a divine miracle. After weeks of relentless open-ocean travel, many of our sailors had perished, while others lay dying of scurvy, weakened by the unnatural diet of salted provisions. But the promise of shore revitalized them instantly. Those who had become helpless, motionless logs under the lash suddenly regained strength, returning to work and preparing to defend their lives and their ships.

Since my deposition in Yucatan, despite Tatho's reassurances, a shadow of doubt had lingered in my mind regarding my reception in Atlantis. Would the Empress Phorenice greet me as a hero or execute me the moment I stepped onto her soil? I had chosen to accept whatever fate awaited, trusting entirely in the will of the Gods. Even if Phorenice demanded my head, the Lord Sun ruled above her; if my life ended, it would be His decree. Thus, I had spent the voyage in quiet contemplation of higher mysteries, leaving my anxieties behind.

But when our navy had retraced its path far enough to reach the twin headlands marking the entrance to the inland waters—just two days' sail from the capital—we encountered a rival force waiting to intercept us.

Our remaining five ships rode at anchor in a sheltered bay, but scouts atop the cliffs had already signaled our approach. As we rounded the headland, the enemy emerged to block our passage. They were formidable: three sleek, black-hulled ships and three massive galleys bristling with oars. In stark contrast, our vessels were storm-tossed and weather-beaten, their hulls fouled with seaweed. While we proudly displayed the banners of Tatho and Deucalion, signaling our noble origins, the enemy moved without flags or insignia, appearing as shameless scavengers.

Clearly, they intended to fight. The seas knew no laws protecting brothers-in-arms from preying on one another, especially when one returned laden with plunder. Tob immediately ordered our ships into battle formation, and the other captains did likewise, closing ranks to form a compact squadron. The sun blazed overhead, seemingly eager for the conflict, as the two fleets advanced rapidly. The enemy galleys held back initially, maintaining their line.

At roughly a hundred ship-lengths apart, the opposing navy halted. One of their galleys surged forward, flying green branches from its mast—a signal for parley. Given our battered condition, we had no desire to invite unnecessary combat. Hoarse shouts carried across the gap, and both sides stopped. Tob raised a withered branch to signify our willingness to negotiate and respect diplomatic immunity.

The enemy galley maneuvered closer until its stern scraped against our rail. A young, robust envoy climbed aboard our deck, dressed in immaculate finery, looking down on our tattered appearance with a sneer. He spotted Tob and nodded familiarly.

"Old pot-mate," he called out cheerfully. "Your woman awaits you on the quay in Atlantis, with four brats at her heels. I saw her just last week."

"You didn't come out here merely to deliver gossip," Tob replied sharply. "I've shared enough drinks with you, Dason, to know your jests."

"I simply wished to remind you that your family is waiting for you," Dason pressed. "Home is still yours."

"I never forget my home," Tob said grimly. "Precisely because I keep them in mind, I have sailed over countless pirates. Had I been a lone man, perhaps I would have accepted slavery, but I am not that man."

"Dason," the envoy continued, "if this turns to bloodshed, we'll likely lose several men before we sink your crippled ships and drag you home. My navy has orders. We wish to see if you will play the sensible card and surrender what we demand, allowing you to return safely."

"You've addressed the wrong bird for a plucking," Tob laughed darkly. "We took no treasure or goods from Yucatan. We stayed only long enough to cure our provisions and refill our stores. We sail back empty-handed. You're welcome to inspect the holds yourself; you'll find nothing but broken, scurvy-stricken sailors. Here, you can steal nothing but blows, Dason, and we'll gladly provide them."

"The cargo you describe is precisely what I must secure," Dason noted smoothly. "Only then will you earn safe passage."

Tob frowned. "Speak plainly. I am a common sailor, not a scholar of fancy talk."

"It is obvious," Dason declared, "that you have been tasked with bringing Lord Deucalion back to Atlantis as a prop for Empress Phorenice. We find her difficult enough to oppose without additional reinforcements. We intend to seize Deucalion for our own purposes."

"And if I refuse?" Tob asked.

"Why," Dason shrugged, gesturing to our splintered ships, "then we shall send you all to the fishes. Instead of Deucalion facing the Gods alone, he will descend with a ragged, defeated company limping at his heels."

"I doubt it," Tob retorted. "Letting you have Lord Deucalion is impossible. First, if I arrive without him, Lord Tatho will eventually return, and I shall die slowly in his hands. Second, I witnessed Deucalion slay a giant sea-lizard; he proved himself a true man, and I would not betray him willingly, not even to Tatho. Third, you owe me for our last drinking bout ashore. I'll see you in the underworld before I yield anything until that debt is paid."

"Well, Tob, I hope you drown easily," Dason spat. "As for your wife... I've always fancied her. I'll know how to make use of her."

"I'll draw your neck for that, you European spawn!" Tob roared. "If you don't clear this deck, I'll cleave it right here! Get away, you father of monkey-brats! Let us fight fairly, or by my honor, I'll rip your innards out and make your crew wear them as necklaces!"

Enraged, Dason retreated to his galley. Immediately, Tob engaged the machinery of our flagship, the *Bear*, and bellowed orders to the other vessels. Though our crews were weak with scurvy, they obeyed swiftly. Under the brilliant sun, our five ships surged forward, converging on a single enemy vessel. Caught off guard, the opponent attempted to flee, presenting her broadside to our underwater beaks. Before we could disengage, our ships struck simultaneously, tearing her planking and sinking her instantly.

However, eliminating one foe did not secure victory. The three nimble galleys quickly formed a line, their boatwains cracking whips as slaves drove their oars. Within moments, one of our ships was gored and sunk, her crew drowning without hope of rescue. What followed was a savage melee that would have thrilled any warrior. Ships and galleys ground against one another on the swells like living beasts. Arrows flew, axes slashed, swords hacked, and fire-pots exploded, burning flesh and timber alike.

Yet, every assault focused on the *Bear*. It was our flagship that bore the brunt of their hatred, rallying our scattered forces as other vessels caught fire or succumbed. Battle is an old companion to us of the Priestly Clan; carving out territory for new colonies often demands frequent conflict. Having heard "Deucalion" shouted as a war cry for half my life, I had seen many fierce engagements, yet none compared to this.

The environment itself was hostile: the unstable sea, swaying decks, the horrific burning fires, the man-eating birds circling overhead, and the predatory fish swarming the water below. These conditions could daunt the bravest armies. But these hardened sailors faced them with indomitable courage. Life on the sea had stripped Death of half its terror through sheer familiarity. These men, capable of fighting to the death in taverns for sport, now embraced slaughter with a fervor akin to a heavenly fire raging in a forest. They took their wounds with laughter.

On our side, the cry was "Tob!" The name of this rough captain inspired a confidence that rivaled any famous general. The enemy, however, shouted a dozen different names, confusing their coordination. As their commanders fell one by one, and only Dason remained active in mischief, his name became their sole rallying cry, echoed back at us against our "Tob!"

I need not detail the prolonged carnage of this brutal engagement. Ship after ship sank, crews wiped out, until only Dason's galley and our *Bear* remained. For a brief moment, we were overwhelmed. With barely a dozen survivors from our original navy, the enemy had boarded us, turning the *Bear*'s deck into a slaughterhouse.

But the enemy had been distracted by throwing fire. As the smoke billowed from our own sturdy vessel, revealing she was beyond repair, Tob issued a new command with booming laughter.

"They may stay here to fry if they wish," he shouted. "For us, the galley is sufficient! Guard Lord Deucalion closely, and follow me, shipmates!"
