As the sun set over the rolling hills of Tuscany, casting a warm orange glow over the city of Florence, Tito reflected on the delicate balance he had maintained over the past three months. His eyes, accustomed to the intricacies of politics, gleamed with a calculating intensity as he considered the intricate web of allegiances he had spun. By carefully navigating the treacherous waters of Medicean politics, he had managed to secure a position of trust with both the conspirators and their enemies, the Frate and the popular party. The Mediceans, blinded by their own duplicity, saw only the surface-level benefits of having Tito as their agent, unaware of the subtle manipulation he had employed to maintain their confidence.

Meanwhile, the Roman and Milanese allies of the aristocratic party, the Arrabbiati, viewed Tito's actions with a mix of suspicion and pragmatism. Their own system of clandestine correspondence and espionage relied on deception as a means to an end, and they were unlikely to question an agent who played his part convincingly. On the other hand, the Piagnoni, driven by their fervent conviction, were more inclined to believe Tito's professed allegiance to their cause, seeing in him a kindred spirit untainted by religious motivations.

Through these calculated moves, Tito had achieved a remarkable level of success. His ultimate goal remained to secure a position of influence in either Rome or Milan, where the rewards for his talents and learning would be greater. Currently, the prospects seemed more favorable in Milan, and if he could deliver valuable services to Duke Ludovico Sforza within the year, a coveted appointment at the Milanese court beckoned.

The sudden arrest of Lamberto dell' Antella, however, had thrown a wrench into Tito's carefully laid plans. The telltale letter on his person and his deep-seated animosity towards the Medici had made it impossible for Tito to avoid suspicion. Yet, his quick mind had swiftly assessed the situation and identified a path that would allow him to emerge unscathed. Like a skilled weaver, he had skillfully woven a safety net for himself, ensuring that he would suffer the fewest consequences possible.

He had secured the three outcomes he desired most: immunity from prosecution, retention of his secretaryship, and the confidentiality of the price he had paid for these guarantees.
