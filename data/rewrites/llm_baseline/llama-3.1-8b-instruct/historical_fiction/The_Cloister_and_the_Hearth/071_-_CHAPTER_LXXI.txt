As the scent of roasting chestnuts wafted through the streets of Rotterdam, a small miracle unfolded in a humble tailor's house. Unbeknownst to the bustling crowds, a young boy named Jacob was being born, destined to become one of the most renowned chefs of his time. Meanwhile, in Bruges, the Duke of Burgundy, Philip, lay ailing, his once robust physique weakened by a mysterious illness that would soon claim his life. The doctors, in their haste to save the duke, resorted to drastic measures, bleeding him repeatedly, but to no avail.

As the duke's condition worsened, the doctors turned to more unconventional remedies. "We need something to counteract the imbalance of his humors," Dr. Remedy exclaimed, his eyes scanning the room frantically. "Fetch me an ape!" he ordered, but the officers of state returned empty-handed, for the duke's household was devoid of the exotic creature. They scoured the city, searching for a suitable substitute, but every beast they encountered seemed inadequate – a buffalo, a lizard, even a leopard – until finally, a desperate seneschal spotted a makeshift solution.

"There's this," he whispered, holding up a peculiar-looking apricot pie. "It's apelike, don't you think?" The chancellor scoffed, recalling the duke's wife, Princess Marie, who would never approve of such a travesty. But Dr. Remedy was undeterred, and in a last-ditch effort, he ordered a liver-colored dog to be flayed and placed on the duke's chest. The poor animal still quivered with life as it was pressed against the duke's body, but it was too late – the damage was done.

With the duke's passing, the kingdom was thrown into turmoil, and his thirty-one children were left to navigate the complex web of alliances and rivalries that defined their family. Philip the Good may have left behind a legacy of grandeur, but it was his descendants who would truly shape the course of history. And as for Jacob, the young chef, he remained in obscurity, his talents waiting to be discovered. It would take centuries for his name to become synonymous with culinary excellence, but the seeds of greatness were already being sown in that humble tailor's house in Rotterdam.
