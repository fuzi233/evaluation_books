As Gerard and Denys traversed the snow-covered expanse outside the town, the crunch of wolf tracks beneath their boots served as a reminder of the unforgiving landscape they navigated. The wolves' relentless passage had churned the pristine snow into a maze of footprints, a testament to their unrelenting hunger.

"We did well to heed the old man's counsel," Denys said, his voice carrying above the biting wind.

"Aye, we did," Gerard replied, his eyes scanning the horizon. "For I recall hearing them last night, their haunting cries echoing off the walls of the market-place. Yet, not a single burgher pitied their plight, and no succor was offered."

Denys's lips curled into a wry smile, but it was tinged with an air of abstraction. The two friends walked on in silence, the only sound being the soft crunch of snow beneath their feet.

"What troubles you, my friend?" Gerard asked, his brow furrowed in concern.

Denys's gaze drifted away, his thoughts consumed by some inner turmoil. "Your benevolence," he said finally, his tone heavy with a mix of admiration and frustration.

Gerard's eyes narrowed, sensing a hint of mockery beneath Denys's words. "A civil question deserves a civil response," he said dryly.

"I meant no offense," Gerard protested, though a hint of defensiveness crept into his voice.

Denys's expression turned wry. "Then why feign concern for my virtues when you know I possess none?"

Gerard's lips compressed into a thin line. "Had another spoken thus, I'd have replied, 'You lie.' But to you, I say, 'You see only the qualities of women, not those of men.' And I defy your unreasonable temper once more, saying I was pondering your kindness from the previous evening. Would you have wedded me to the 'Tete d'Or' or rather to the 'tete de veau doree,' leaving yourself to solitude?"
