As Neville Landless set out early, the crisp morning air invigorated him, and the rhythmic sound of birdsong accompanied his footsteps. By the time the church bells tolled in Cloisterham, signaling the start of morning service, he had covered eight miles. Seeking a hearty breakfast, he stopped at the rustic sign of The Tilted Wagon, a humble tavern nestled on the outskirts of a village. The proprietor, a gruff but kind-hearted woman, welcomed him warmly, and Neville settled into the cozy sanded parlour, where the scent of damp fagots crackled in the fireplace.

The Tilted Wagon, though a far cry from a luxurious inn, held a certain charm. A scrawny chicken pecked at crumbs scattered about the floor, while a toddler, clad in a single red sock, wailed in its mother's arms. The air was thick with the smell of old cheese and stale bread, but Neville's hunger soon got the better of him, and he ordered a simple repast. As he ate, he pondered the route ahead, weighing the merits of continuing on the main road versus taking the lesser-used cart track that wound its way through the nearby heath.

After finishing his meal, Neville rose to leave, deciding to take the cart track, which promised a more leisurely pace. The track itself was steep and rutted, but the exercise proved invigorating, and the fresh air filled his lungs. As he laboured along, he became aware of a group of pedestrians behind him. Initially, they seemed to be keeping a respectful distance, but as he continued on, they gradually closed in. Neville noticed that the group consisted of eight individuals, divided into two groups of four, each pair walking in tandem.

He stopped, intrigued by this peculiar formation, and the four in front slowed their pace, while the four behind seemed to be waiting for him to continue. When Neville resumed his walk, the two groups maintained their position, always keeping him in sight. It soon became apparent that they intended to accompany him, and Neville felt a growing sense of unease. He stopped once more, this time to confront the situation directly.

"Why do you attend upon me in this manner?" he asked, his voice firm but measured. "Are you a group of troublemakers?"
