A full-grown resident of our planar world rarely stretches more than 11 inches across at their widest point, with a hard maximum size of 12 inches. That is the full limit of physical scale in our two-dimensional realm, and all our social order and daily life is shaped by the sides and angles that make up every Flatlander’s form.

Let me lay out our social hierarchy, written into the very geometry of our bodies:
- Our Women are pure Straight Lines: thin, sharp, and capable of slicing through any unsuspecting shape that crosses their path.
- Soldiers and the lowest class of manual laborers are Isosceles Triangles: two equal sides almost 11 inches long, with a third base edge so short (often less than half an inch, sometimes barely an eighth of an inch) that the sharp vertex at their point is almost indistinguishable from the tip of a Straight Line. These are our most physically formidable residents, for their narrow points can pierce almost any other shape with ease.
- Our middle class is made up entirely of Equilateral Triangles, all three sides perfectly equal, their 60-degree angles neither too sharp to be recklessly dangerous nor too wide to be clumsy.
- Professional men, scholars, and gentlemen of the trades are Squares—this is the class I myself belong to—and five-sided Pentagons, set apart from the working masses by their extra side and wider, more gentle internal angles.
- Above these ranks sit the Nobility, graded by nothing but the count of their sides: starting with six-sided Hexagons, then seven-sided, eight-sided, climbing ever higher in number until they earn the honorific title of Polygonal, or "many-sided".
- At the very peak of our social order are the Circles, who make up our Priestly caste. By the time a shape has hundreds upon hundreds of tiny, evenly matched sides, the difference between their multi-sided perimeter and a perfect unbroken curve becomes impossible to spot with any Flatlander eye. This is the highest, most revered rank in all our planar lands.

It is a fundamental Law of Nature in Flatland that every male child will be born with exactly one more side than his father. Barring accident or defect, each generation climbs one full rung up the ladder of development and nobility: the son of a Square is a Pentagon, the son of a Pentagon a Hexagon, and so on, in a steady upward march of refinement.

This law does not extend, however, to the lowest ranks. Tradesmen, soldiers, and manual laborers are not regular equal-sided figures at all, and barely qualify as full human citizens in the eyes of the upper classes. An Isosceles Triangle’s son will almost always be born Isosceles too, with no automatic advancement for him. But even they are not condemned to permanent stagnation. Over generations of exceptional military valor, or generations of skilled, diligent labor, the most intelligent, disciplined members of the lower classes will slowly see their short base edge lengthen, while their two long sharp sides shrink gradually closer to equal length. The Priests arrange carefully vetted marriages between the highest-achieving Isosceles households, and each successive generation’s offspring comes a little closer to the perfect equal-sided proportions of the Equilateral Triangle.

Genuine, officially certified Equilateral children born of two Isosceles parents are extraordinarily rare, one in tens of thousands of births. You, a Spacelander, might scoff and ask why we need a formal certificate at all—surely the birth of a Square son to a Triangle father is proof enough of regular blood? But I must tell you, no reputable lady of any standing will marry an uncertified Triangle. Sometimes a slightly irregular Triangle can sire a Square, but that hidden flaw in his sides will show up in the grandchildren: the third generation will never be able to advance to Pentagonal rank, and often slips all the way back to the lower Triangular class. A true Equilateral birth requires generations of deliberate, frugal, disciplined self-improvement from the line of ancestors before it, and slow, careful cultivation of the Isosceles intellect across hundreds of family lines.

When one of these rare True Equilaterals is born, the news spreads for miles around, and the entire region celebrates. A full panel of the Sanitary and Social Board conducts a rigorous physical inspection to confirm the infant’s sides are perfectly equal. Once certified, the child is taken in a solemn public ceremony out of his working-class parents’ home, and adopted by a childless Equilateral household, bound by sacred oath to never let the child return to his old neighborhood, or even lay eyes on his birth family ever again. The risk is too high that the newly regular shape might, by unconscious mimicry, slip back to the irregularity of his heritage.

This rare upward mobility is cherished by both the lowest serfs and the highest Aristocracy. For the poor Isosceles, it is a glowing beacon of hope that cuts through the drudgery of their daily lives, proof that advancement is not entirely out of reach. For the upper classes, it is the most reliable guard against revolution. If the masses of sharp-acute rabble had no hope at all of climbing the ranks, they might find brilliant leaders among themselves in their frequent seditious uprisings, and their overwhelming numbers and piercing sharp points would overwhelm even the greatest wisdom of the Circles. But the Law of Compensation, that most perfect of natural ordinances, sees to it that as the working classes gain intelligence, skill, and virtue, their sharp, dangerous acute angles widen to match the far less menacing 60-degree angle of the Equilateral Triangle. The dullest, most brutal soldier Triangles, who are almost as mindless as our Straight Line Women, are the ones with the sharpest, most deadly piercing points. The more intelligent they get, the less physically dangerous they become.

What a perfect, providential proof of the fitness of Flatland’s aristocratic order, is it not? Using this law, the Polygons and Circles can snuff out almost every sedition before it ever gets off the ground, leveraging the universal, unquenchable hope of advancement in every Flatlander’s chest. Art and medicine step in to support law and order too: State physicians can carefully apply controlled compression and expansion to the sides of the most intelligent rebel leaders, correcting their irregular proportions to make them fully Regular, then elevate them straight into the privileged classes. The far greater number of lower-class dissidents who fall just short of full regularisation are lured by the promise of future ennoblement to volunteer for honourable lifelong confinement in State hospitals. Only the tiny handful of obstinate, hopelessly irregular rebels who refuse all offers of reform are ever led to execution.

Left without leaders, the unorganised mass of Isosceles rabble are either cut down without resistance by the small paid force of loyal Triangles the Chief Circle keeps on hand for emergencies, or far more often, the Circular ruling class stokes petty jealousies and old grudges between different factions of the working class, stirring them to fight one another until they perish on each other’s sharp angles. Our annals record no less than 120 full-scale recorded rebellions, plus 235 smaller scattered uprisings. Not a single one has ever ended any other way.
