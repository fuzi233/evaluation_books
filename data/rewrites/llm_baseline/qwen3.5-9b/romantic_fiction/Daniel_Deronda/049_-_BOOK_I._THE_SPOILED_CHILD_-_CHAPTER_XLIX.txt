In the depths of his spirit, a higher justice prevailed, transforming gratitude into a triumph over lingering resentment. This capacity for magnanimity marked the regal nature within him—a soul endowed with a broader life and a fuller potential for joy. Unlike those sharp-witted individuals who revel in dissecting virtue until it dissolves into insignificance, dismissing kindness as unworthy of thanks, Deronda possessed a different strength. These cynics, often masquerading as humble devotees of love and trust, ultimately beg from a world that hoards its own humanity.

As he parted ways with Gwendolen, Deronda carefully withheld the words, *"I shall not see you again for a long time; I am leaving."* He feared that Sir Hugo would interpret such a statement as significant to her, whereas the truth was far more personal. He was departing under circumstances of profound gravity to himself. When he finally set out to keep his promise of visiting her, he carried within him a solemn emotion that stirred the deepest currents of his existence.

Sir Hugo had summoned him to his chambers with a terse note: *"Come immediately. Something has happened."* Upon entering the baronet's study, however, Deronda found not the distress he had anticipated, but a grave affection. Sir Hugo extended his hand, and Deronda grasped it, speaking in a tone that blended restored confidence with inquiry: "Is there nothing here to cause you grief, sir?"

There was an unusual weight in Sir Hugo's gaze and a subdued tremor in his voice as he replied, "No, Dan, no. Sit down. I have something to say." Deronda complied, though a heavy presentiment weighed upon him. It was exceedingly rare for the baronet to display such intense seriousness.

"It does not grieve me, my boy," Sir Hugo continued gently. "At least, not in a way that should trouble you deeply. But I confess, I scarcely expected this specific event to occur. There have been reasons why I never prepared you for it, and why I kept silent regarding your parentage. Yet, I strove in every manner to ensure that this secret remained an injury to none."

Deronda sat in stunned silence, unable to articulate that he had never truly felt it as an injury. Even if that sentiment were true, his throat tightened against the thought. More than anyone else could possibly comprehend hung suspended in this fragile moment, waiting for the veil of secrecy to tear apart. Sir Hugo observed the pallor draining from Daniel's usually noble face, noting how his lips were pressed together in visible pain.

"I acted in obedience to your mother's wishes," Sir Hugo explained, his anxiety deepening as if fearing further wounds. "Her desire was for secrecy. But now, she desires to remove it. She wishes to see you. I will place this letter in your hands; you may read it later. It contains only her instructions on what you must do and where you may find her."

He handed over a letter written on foreign stationery. Deronda tucked it safely into his breast pocket, relieved not to be forced to confront its contents instantly. The emotional turmoil on Daniel's face had begun to unsettle the baronet's composure as well. Finding it difficult to proceed, Sir Hugo fell silent, allowing the tension to build.

Then, a question rose in Deronda's heart—the most arduous in the world to voice. Yet, he could not endure the delay. This was a sacramental instant; if he allowed it to pass, he might lose the unique spiritual alignment required to speak these words and receive their answer. For several moments, their gazes dropped, and it seemed as though unspoken thoughts drifted through the air between them. Finally, looking up at Sir Hugo, Deronda spoke with a trembling reverence, dreading that his phrasing might inadvertently suggest a reproach for years of stifled affection: "Is my father still alive?"

The answer came immediately, low and emphatic: "No."

A complex storm of emotions swept through the room, making it impossible to disentangle pure joy from acute sorrow. A new light had illuminated the past for Sir Hugo as well during this intimate exchange. After a silence that left Deronda feeling as though his old beliefs had dissolved before he could embrace a new faith, the baronet confessed, "Perhaps I was wrong, Dan, to undertake what I did. Perhaps I enjoyed having you entirely to myself a little too much. But if you have suffered any pain that I could have alleviated, I ask your forgiveness."

"The forgiveness has long been granted," Deronda responded softly. "My greatest pain has always stemmed from someone else—someone I never knew, whom I am now destined to know. It has never prevented me from feeling a profound affection for you, a bond that has constituted a vast portion of the life I remember."

Driven by a single, powerful impulse, the two men clasped hands for a brief, poignant moment.
