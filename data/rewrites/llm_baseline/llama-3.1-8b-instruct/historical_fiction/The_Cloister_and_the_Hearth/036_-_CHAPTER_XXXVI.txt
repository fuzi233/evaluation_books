As the warm sunlight streamed through the small windows of the prison cell, a faint scent of baking bread wafted in, mingling with the smell of damp stone. It was a peculiar combination, but one that Manon had grown accustomed to over the past few days. She sat on a narrow pallet, her eyes fixed on the floor as she listened to the conversation between the alderman and the two strangers, Denys and Gerard.

Denys spoke up, his voice laced with concern. "In prison, sir, for what misdeed?" he asked the alderman.

The alderman smiled, pleased to be addressed as bailiff. "She is a witness, and may be a necessary one," he replied. "We keep the women folk detained in cases of blood, where they may provide testimony that could aid in bringing justice to the realm."

Gerard jumped in, his words tumbling out in a rush. "You don't lay all your witnesses by the heels, do you, sir? That seems a rather...unusual practice."

The alderman chuckled, his expression warm. "Ah, but we do indeed. In cases where a man's word may be clouded by emotions, a woman's testimony can be quite revealing. They say that women are often more prone to speak the truth, even if it's not always pleasant to hear."

As the alderman spoke, a servant entered with a tray bearing a steaming loaf of bread and a wedge of cheese. The alderman took a bite, savoring the flavors before continuing. "Now, I recall that Jean Hardy, one of the thieves, confessed to being part of a band. And it seems that Manon here was the decoy, using her charm to lure unsuspecting travelers to their doom."

Denys and Gerard exchanged a skeptical glance. Gerard spoke up, his voice firm. "But surely, sir, you wouldn't take the word of a thief at face value? His opinion is hardly reliable, especially when it comes to someone he's trying to frame."

The alderman nodded thoughtfully, his hands cradling his glass as he read from a parchment. "Ah, but here it is in black and white.
