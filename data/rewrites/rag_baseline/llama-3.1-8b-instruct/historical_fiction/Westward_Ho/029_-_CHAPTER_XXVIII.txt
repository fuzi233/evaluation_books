As the 15th of February, 1587, unfolded under a melancholic sky, Mrs. Leigh's footsteps echoed along the worn stone path of the Burrough estate's terrace-walk. The rhythmic lapping of the river against its banks, the desolate expanse of the sand-hills, and the distant sea stretched out before her like an unfurled canvas, painted with hues of indigo and gold. For three long years, she had stood watch, her eyes scouring the horizon, searching for a glimpse of the sails that had borne her sons, Frank and Amyas, into the unknown. The passing seasons had etched lines of sorrow on her face, her hair now flecked with wisps of gray, her steps slowing as if burdened by the weight of unspoken longing.

Mrs. Leigh's gaze wandered to the west, where the sun had dipped into the sea, casting a warm orange glow over the waves. It was a sight she had witnessed countless times before, yet each evening brought a fresh pang of nostalgia. In her mind's eye, she saw the Rose, the sturdy vessel that had borne her sons on their fateful voyage, its sails billowing in the wind, its hull gliding effortlessly across the waves. Though the years had passed, the memory of its lines remained etched in her mind, a constant reminder of what she had lost.

As she walked, the sound of distant laughter and music carried on the breeze, mingling with the scent of woodsmoke and roasting meat. The villagers of Northam had lit bonfires to celebrate the sacking of Cartagena, San Domingo, and Saint Augustine, and the relief of Raleigh's Virginian Colony. News of these victories had brought a fleeting sense of hope to the community, but for Mrs. Leigh, it was a hollow solace. She knew that her sons had sailed into the West with the same sense of adventure and idealism that had driven John Oxenham before them. Yet, as the days turned into weeks, and the weeks into months, and the months into years, the silence had become deafening.

Mrs. Leigh's thoughts were interrupted by the pealing of the bells from the tower above, their melodic tones blending with the shouts and cheers of the villagers. She stood still, her eyes fixed on the sea, as the flames from the bonfires danced and flickered, casting shadows on the surrounding hills.
