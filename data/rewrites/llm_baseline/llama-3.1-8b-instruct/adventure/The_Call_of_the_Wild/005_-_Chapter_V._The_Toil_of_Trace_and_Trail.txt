The journey from Dawson had taken thirty days, and when the Salt Water Mail finally arrived in Skaguay, its passengers, including Buck and his companions, were in a state of utter exhaustion. Their once robust frames had shrunk, weighed down by the relentless toil of traversing over twenty-five hundred miles in less than five months. During the final eighteen hundred miles, they had managed only five days' rest, and the cumulative effect had taken its toll. Their muscles, fibers, and cells were drained, leaving them with no reserve strength to draw upon.

As they trudged down the main street of Skaguay, their feet heavy and sore, the driver's words of encouragement did little to lift their spirits. "Mush on, poor sore feet," he coaxed, but it was clear that they were on the brink of collapse. The drivers themselves had covered twelve hundred miles with only two days' rest, and they expected a well-deserved break, but the congestion in the town, caused by the influx of prospectors and their loved ones, had resulted in a bottleneck. Official orders had been issued to replace the worn-out dogs with fresh batches from the Hudson Bay, and the worthless ones were to be sold off.

Three days passed, and Buck and his companions began to realize the full extent of their weakness. It wasn't until the morning of the fourth day that two strangers, Hal and Charles, appeared on the scene. Charles, a middle-aged man with a bushy mustache that seemed at odds with his limp upper lip, and Hal, a young man sporting a large Colt's revolver and a hunting knife, purchased the team, including Buck and his mates, for a relatively small sum. The pair's arrival was met with a mixture of curiosity and suspicion, and it was clear that they were not familiar with the rigors of the North.

As Buck was led to the new owners' camp, he caught sight of a makeshift and disorganized setup, with dishes unwashed and equipment scattered about. A woman, Mercedes, was introduced as Charles's wife and Hal's sister, and Buck couldn't help but feel apprehensive about his new surroundings. The couple's manner was marked by a lack of discipline and organization, and Buck wondered what the future held for him under their care.
