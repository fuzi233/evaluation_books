As I emerged from the dentist's humble abode in Plaistow, the scent of ripening fruit wafted from the garden, mingling with the faint tang of metal polish and the faint hint of a long-forgotten jam still simmering on the stove. The summer air was heavy with the promise of evening, and the sky above was a deep, burnished gold, like the patina on a well-loved spoon. I felt the weight of my half-crown in my knickerbocker pocket, the coins clinking softly against my leg as I walked. My mother's instructions still lingered in my mind: "Have the half-crown in your hand," she'd said, "but if he won't take it, bring it home again."

The memory of the dentist's visits was etched in my mind like the intricate patterns on a plate of his wife's renowned jams. He was a kind friend, an old bachelor with a heart as big as the jar of preserves that overflowed from his garden's bounty. His house was a haven, filled with the sweet aroma of ripening fruit and the soft hum of conversation. I remembered the velvet chair of state where I sat, mesmerized by the array of instruments laid out before me, each one more fascinating than the last. The dentist's words dripped with affection as he recommended the most fearsome-looking tools, his eyes twinkling with amusement.

But the moment my mouth opened to protest, a tiny pair of scissors would spring from beneath his sleeve, and with a few swift snips, the ordeal would be over. We'd sit together over tea, sipping the warm brew and listening to tales of my mother's youth. She was a prankish, wilful child, full of mischief and laughter, according to the dentist's stories. I'd recount them to my mother, who'd smile and laugh, saying she had no recollection of such exploits. Yet, she'd ask for more, her eyes sparkling with curiosity.

As I walked across the bridge, the sound of the barges drifting lazily between the piers created a soothing melody. The East India Dock Road stretched out before me, a seemingly endless ribbon of pavement and market gardens. The one-storeyed brick cottage at the beginning of the bridge stood sentinel, a reminder of the world beyond my narrow existence.
