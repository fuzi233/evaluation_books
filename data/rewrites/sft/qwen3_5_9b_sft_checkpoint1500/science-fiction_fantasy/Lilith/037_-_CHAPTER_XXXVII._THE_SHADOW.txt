A ripple of relief passed through my companions when they finally spied their kin in the distance. Two riders, mounted on Lona's steed, spurred forward to join them, only to be met by a wavering cry that instantly dissolved into silence. As we approached, the sound of their weeping rose, soft and jagged like breaking waves.

Stepping into the circle, I saw the terrible truth etched upon their young, weather-beaten faces. This was not the sorrow of loss alone; it was the hollow gaze born of some primal, unseen dread. Several children drifted slowly toward me, extending small, trembling hands to receive my burden. I yielded it to them. Their smiles, though filled with a tender, crushing hopelessness, swelled my heart with a pity that cut deeper than my own ruin. Their tears for their mother-queen were futile. No matter how gently they coaxed her awake, nor how fiercely they clung to her, nor how softly they stroked her limbs as they carried her away, she remained stone-cold. On a patch of lush, yielding grass, they laid her down, and soon the entire group of Little Ones surrounded her, sobbing together.

Beyond the throng stood the great elephants. I lingered near them, peering over the sea of small heads to find Lona. Those nearest me froze, their eyes wide with tremors. Odu broke the silence first, his voice a harsh whisper.

"I've seen that woman before!" he muttered to the boy beside him. "It was she who fought the white leopardess the night they woke us with their screaming!"

"Silly!" retorted his neighbor. "That was just a wild beast, covered in spots!"

"Look at her eyes!" Odu insisted, his face pale. "I know she is a bad giantess, but she is a wild beast nonetheless. I know she is the spotted one!"

His companion stepped closer, drawn by a terrifying fascination. Odu yanked him back sharply.

"Don't look at her!" he cried, shrinking away despite his fear. "She would devour you in an instant! It was HER shadow! She is the wicked princess!"

"That cannot be!" the other protested. "They said she was beautiful!"

"It is the princess!" I interjected firmly. "Wickedness has stripped her beauty away!"

She heard me, and the expression on her face shifted.

"It was very wrong of me to run away!" Odu murmured, lost in thought.

"What made you flee?" I asked. "I expected to find you right where I left you."

He hesitated.

"I don't know what drove me off," another child admitted, his voice shaking. "I was simply terrified."

"A man came down from the palace hill," a third offered.

"How did he frighten you?"

"I... I can't say."

"He wasn't a man," Odu corrected, his voice rising. "He was a shadow. He had no substance!"

"Tell me more about him."

"He descended the hill very dark, moving like a monstrous giant, but flat against the ground. He was pure blackness. We were scared the moment we saw him, but we didn't run. We stood and watched. He advanced as if he meant to crush us. But before he reached us, he began to spread, expanding endlessly, growing larger and larger until he vanished from sight entirely. And then—he was upon us!"

"What do you mean?"

"He blocked everything between us. We couldn't see each other. And then... he was *inside* us."

"How did you know?"

"He changed me completely," Odu confessed, his voice cracking. "I felt awful. I wasn't Odu anymore—not the Odu I knew. I wanted to tear Sozo apart! Not really, but it felt like it!"

He turned and embraced Sozo tightly.

"It wasn't me, Sozo!" he sobbed. "Deep down, it was still Odu, loving you always! And Odu rose up inside me and knocked the Naughty part away. I grew sick, convinced I had to kill myself to escape the black. Then came a horrible laugh—one that seemed to hear my thoughts—and it made the air vibrate around me. After that, I suppose I ran. I didn't realize I was fleeing until I found myself sprinting as fast as I could, dragging everyone else with me. I wanted to stop, but I never thought of it until I burst out of the gate into the grass. Only then did I realize I had fled from a shadow trying to become me, erasing the real Odu. That shadow was inside me, hating Sozo from within, and it wasn't my own mind! Now I know I shouldn't have run! But I didn't quite know what I was doing until it happened. My legs did it, I think. They grew so frightened they forgot me and just ran. Naughty legs! There! And there!"

With a kick aimed at each leg, Odu finished his tale.

"What became of the shadow?" I asked quietly.

"I don't know," he replied. "I suppose he went home into the night where there is no moon."

My gaze drifted to where Lona lay. Wondering where she had gone, I dropped onto the grass, taking the lifeless form into my lap. Whispering close to her ear, I asked, "Where are you, Lona? I love you!" Her lips gave no answer. I pressed a kiss to them, feeling them barely warm, then laid her body back down. Appointing a guard to stand vigil, I rose to ensure the safety of Lona's people throughout the coming night.

Before sunset, I had stationed a watch over the princess outside the camp, placing sentinels around her perimeter. Intending to patrol the grounds myself all night, I ordered the rest of the army to sleep. They collapsed onto the grass, falling into slumber almost instantly.

As the moon climbed high, I caught a fleeting glimpse of something white—the leopardess. She moved silently around the sleeping encampment, passing three times between the princess and the Little Ones. Seeing this, I signaled the watch to lie down with the others and stretched myself beside Lona's cold body, ready to wait.
