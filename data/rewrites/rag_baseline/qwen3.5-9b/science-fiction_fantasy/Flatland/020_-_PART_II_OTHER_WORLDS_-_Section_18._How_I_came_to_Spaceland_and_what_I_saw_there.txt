An unspeakable horror seized me. Darkness swallowed the world, replaced instantly by a dizzy, sickening vertigo of perception—not sight as I knew it, but a violation of it. I saw a Line that was no longer a Line; I beheld Space that defied my two-dimensional existence. I was myself, and yet I was not myself. When voice returned to my throat, it tore from my lips in a shriek of agony: "Either this is madness or it is Hell."

"It is neither," a calm voice replied, cutting through the chaos. "It is Knowledge. It is Three Dimensions. Open your eye once again and try to look steadily."

I obeyed. Behold, a new world unfolded before me! Standing there, visibly incorporated, was all that I had previously inferred, conjectured, and dreamed of in perfect Circular beauty. What appeared to be the center of the Stranger's form lay open to my view, yet within that openness, I saw no heart, nor lungs, nor arteries. Instead, there existed a beautiful, harmonious Something—for which my vocabulary possessed no name—but you, my Readers in Spaceland, would recognize it as the surface of the Sphere.

Prostrating myself mentally before my Guide, I cried, "How is it, O divine ideal of consummate loveliness and wisdom, that I see thy inside, and yet cannot discern thy heart, thy lungs, thy arteries, thy liver?"

"What you think you see, you see not," he replied gently. "It is not given to you, nor to any other Being of Flatland, to behold my internal parts. I am of a different order of Beings from those in your plane. Were I a Circle, you could discern my intestines, but I am a Being composed, as I told you before, of many Circles—the Many in the One, called in this country a Sphere. And, just as the outside of a Cube presents the appearance of a Square, so the outside of a Sphere presents the appearance of a Circle."

Bewildered though I was by my Teacher's enigmatic utterance, I ceased to chafe against it, surrendering instead to silent adoration. His tone softened further. "Distress not yourself if you cannot at first comprehend the deeper mysteries of Spaceland. By degrees, they will dawn upon you. Let us begin by casting back a glance at the region whence you came. Return with me a while to the plains of Flatland, and I will show you that which you have often reasoned and thought about, but never seen with the sense of sight—a visible angle."

"Impossible!" I cried. Yet, led by the Sphere, I followed as if in a dream until his voice arrested me again. "Look yonder, and behold your own Pentagonal house, and all its inmates."

I looked down and saw with my physical eye all that domestic individuality which I had hitherto merely inferred with my understanding. How poor and shadowy was that abstract conjecture compared to the terrifying reality now revealed! My four sons slept calmly in the North-Western rooms; my two orphan grandsons lay to the south; the servants, the Butler, and my daughter occupied their respective apartments. Only my affectionate wife, alarmed by my prolonged absence, had left her chamber and was roaming anxiously up and down the Hall, awaiting my return. Even the Page, roused by my earlier cries, had emerged from his room, feigning concern for my fainting state while prying into the cabinet in my study.

All this I could now *see*, not merely infer. As we drew nearer, I discerned the contents of my cabinet, the two chests of gold, and the tablets the Sphere had mentioned.

Touched by my wife's distress, I would have sprung downward to reassure her, but I found myself utterly incapable of motion.

"Trouble not yourself about your Wife," said my Guide. "She will not be long left in anxiety; meantime, let us take a survey of Flatland."

Once more, I felt myself rising through the void. Just as the Sphere had predicted, the further we receded from the object beneath us, the larger the field of vision expanded. My native city, with the interior of every house and every creature within, lay exposed to my view in miniature. We ascended higher, and suddenly, the secrets of the earth were bared: the depths of mines, the inmost caverns of the hills—all unveiled before my unworthy eye.

Awestruck at the sight of these terrestrial mysteries, I turned to my Companion. "Behold, I am become as a God. For the wise men in our country say that to see all things—or, as they express it, *Omnividence*—is the attribute of God alone."

There was a note of scorn in the voice of my Teacher as he answered. "Is it so indeed? Then the very pickpockets and cutthroats of my country are to be worshipped by your wise men as gods, for there is not one of them that does not see as much as you see now. But trust me, your wise men are wrong."

"I. Then is omnividence the attribute of others besides Gods?"

"S.P.H.E.R.E. I do not know. But, if a pickpocket or a cutthroat of your country can see everything that is in ours, surely that is no reason why the pickpocket or the cutthroat should be accepted by you as a god. Does this *omnividence*, as you call it—it is not a common word in Spaceland—make you more just, more merciful, less selfish, more loving? Not in the least. Then how does it make you more divine?"

"I. More merciful, more loving!"

"But these are the qualities of women! And we know that a Circle is a higher Being than a Straight Line, insofar as knowledge and wisdom are more to be esteemed than mere affection."

"S.P.H.E.R.E. It is not for me to classify human faculties according to merit. Yet many of the best and wisest in Spaceland think more of the affections than of the understanding, more of your despised Straight Lines than of your belauded Circles. But enough of this. Look yonder. Do you know that building?"

I looked, and afar off I saw an immense polygonal structure, recognizing immediately the General Assembly Hall of the States of Flatland, surrounded by dense lines of pentagonal buildings standing at right angles to one another, which I knew to be streets. I perceived that I was approaching the great Metropolis.

"Here we descend," said my Guide.

It was now morning, the first hour of the first day of the two thousandth year of our era. Acting, as was their wont, in strict accordance with precedent, the highest Circles of the realm were meeting in solemn conclave, just as they had gathered on the first hour of the first day of the year 1000, and also on the first hour of the first day of the year 0. The minutes of the previous meetings were read aloud by one whom I at once recognized as my brother, a perfectly symmetrical Square, and the Chief Clerk of the High Council.

It was found recorded on each occasion that: *"Whereas the States had been troubled by divers ill-intentioned persons pretending to have received revelations from another World, and professing to produce demonstrations whereby they had instigated to frenzy both themselves and others, it had been for this cause unanimously resolved by the Grand Council that on the first day of each millenary, special injunctions be sent to the Prefects in the several districts of Flatland, to make strict search for such misguided persons, and without formality of mathematical examination, to destroy all such as were Isosceles of any degree, to scourge and imprison any regular Triangle, to cause any Square or Pentagon to be sent to the district Asylum, and to arrest any one of higher rank, sending him straightway to the Capital to be examined and judged by the Council."*

"You hear your fate," said the Sphere to me, while the Council passed the formal resolution for the third time. "Death or imprisonment awaits the Apostle of the Gospel of Three Dimensions."

"Not so," replied I. "The matter is now so clear to me, the nature of real space so palpable, that methinks I could make a child understand it. Permit me but to descend at this moment and enlighten them."
