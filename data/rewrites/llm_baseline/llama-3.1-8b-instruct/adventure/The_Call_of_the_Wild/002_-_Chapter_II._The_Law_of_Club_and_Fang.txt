Chapter II. The Unyielding Wilderness

Buck's first day on the rugged Dyea beach was a jarring introduction to a world far removed from the comforts of civilization. Every hour was a blur of shock and surprise, as if he'd been plucked from the heart of urbanity and dropped into the raw, untamed essence of nature. Gone were the lazy afternoons spent lounging in the sun, with nothing to do but lounge and languish. Here, every moment was a battle for survival, where life and limb hung precariously in the balance. The air was alive with tension, and Buck's senses were stretched to the breaking point as he struggled to make sense of his new surroundings.

He'd never witnessed anything like the ferocity of these wolf-like dogs, their primal instincts on full display as they clashed in a frenzy of snarls and snapping teeth. Buck's first exposure to this brutal spectacle left him shaken, a vicarious experience that seared itself into his memory. Curly, the gentle giant, had been the unfortunate victim of this chaos. She'd approached a massive husky with a friendly demeanor, only to be met with a lightning-fast attack that left her face torn open from eye to jaw.

The aftermath was a scene of unspeakable horror, as thirty or forty huskies closed in on the struggling pair, their eyes fixed on Curly with a hunger that chilled Buck to the bone. The onlookers' silence was a palpable thing, a heavy, expectant stillness that seemed to draw the very breath from Buck's lungs. He watched in stunned horror as the pack descended upon Curly, their snarls and yelps rising to a deafening crescendo as they overwhelmed her.

The brutality of it all left Buck reeling, his mind struggling to comprehend the savagery that had unfolded before him. Spitz, the scarlet-tongued husky, seemed to revel in the chaos, his laughter echoing through the air as he danced around the carnage. François, the burly half-breed, swung his axe with deadly precision, scattering the pack with a few swift blows. Two minutes passed, and the last of the attackers were sent fleeing, but the damage was done. Curly lay motionless in the trampled snow, her body a mangled, lifeless thing.
