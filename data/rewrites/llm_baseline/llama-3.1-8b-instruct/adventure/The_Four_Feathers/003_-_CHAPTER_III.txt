As Durrance rode through the misty dawn, the dew-kissed grass beneath his horse's hooves glistened like a thousand tiny diamonds. His thoughts drifted back to the Lennon River in Donegal, where two years ago he'd been summoned by Dermod Eustace on a whim. The memory of Ethne's ethereal melodies still lingered in his mind, like the haunting scent of sea salt carried on the wind. He recalled the way she'd played her violin with her back to the audience, as if the music flowed through her like the river itself. The Melusine overture, with its mournful sighs, had spoken to him of distant lands and secret longings, of moonlit seas and the whispers of the desert.

As he dismounted his horse beneath the lilac and laburnum trees, he couldn't shake the feeling that Ethne's music had left an indelible mark on him. He'd caught a glimpse of the true beauty of music that night – a beauty that transcended words and complaints. It was a lesson he'd carry with him, one that would guide him through the trials ahead.

But for now, he had to focus on the present. He glanced at his watch and noted the time – half past nine. Where was Harry Feversham? They'd met here every week since their Oxford days, and Durrance felt a pang of unease. Had he lost his friend?

As the minutes ticked by, the rumors of war grew louder, until they became a certainty. And then, just as Durrance was starting to worry, Harry appeared, his face flushed with excitement. "I've got the news I've been waiting for!" he exclaimed. "I'm off to Egypt with General Graham's staff! We might even make our way down to Suakin."

Durrance's eyes widened with envy, but Harry's face betrayed a deeper emotion – a mix of longing and regret. Durrance sensed it, but chose to interpret it as a reflection of his own ambition. "Sorry to hear about your regiment staying behind," he said sympathetically.

As they rode side by side, the silence between them was palpable. But when they reached the shaded chairs beneath the trees, Harry broke the silence. "It was the day we dined together that I submitted my resignation," he said, his voice barely above a whisper.
