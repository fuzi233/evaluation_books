As the sun dipped below the Arno's tranquil surface, casting a warm orange glow across the city, Romola's thoughts turned to the purpose that had been growing within her like a gentle stream swelling alongside its parent river. It was more a sense of urgency than a deliberate resolve, a necessity born of her heart's deepest conviction. Undeterred by the encroaching darkness, she hastened across the bridge, the river's dark waters reflecting the fading light like a mirror. Without hesitation, she made her way directly to the Old Palace, her footsteps echoing off the stone walls as she sought out her husband. Though it was unlikely she would find him there, she felt compelled to follow her intuition, to discharge the weight of her concerns.

Her eyes scanned the pillared courtyard and the grand staircase, but her mind was elsewhere. She requested an audience with the Gonfaloniere, providing her name and a humble request for a private meeting. As she waited, surrounded by the serene frescoes and the soft flicker of newly lit candles, she felt a sense of trepidation build within her.

When the door finally opened, Bernardo del Nero stood before her, his head held high, the silk lucco draped elegantly across his shoulders. His eyes narrowed slightly as he took in Romola's disheveled appearance, her hair loose and unkempt, her face etched with fatigue and worry. Yet, it was the fire in her eyes that truly caught his attention – a spark that spoke of deep-seated anxiety.

"What has driven you here, child?" he asked, his voice tinged with a mix of concern and curiosity.

Romola's lips parted, but she hesitated, unsure how to articulate the fears that had taken hold of her. "It's not about me," she began, her words spilling forth in a rush. "Oh, dear godfather, it's about you. I've heard whispers... things I dare not speak aloud. But I implore you, be cautious. There are those who would do you harm, and – and there are traitors lurking in the shadows. Trust no one."

Bernardo's expression softened, his eyes crinkling at the corners as he reached out to gently stroke her hair. "Ah, Romola, my child," he said, his voice low and soothing.
