The moon cast an indifferent glow over the deserted streets of Cloisterham. Edwin Drood walked alone, the sound of his footsteps echoing through the stillness. His thoughts wandered to the conversation he'd had with Neville Landless, the way the other man's words had cut deep. Neville's veiled references to his betrothal to Helena had been a low blow, one that Edwin couldn't quite shake. He quickened his pace, his footsteps a staccato beat in the silence.

As he turned a corner, he caught sight of himself in a window reflection. A fleeting image of a man trapped in a life he didn't fully understand stared back. His father's legacy, the Firm, and the weight of his betrothal hung over him like a shroud. The thought sent a familiar knot twisting in his stomach. He felt like a man trying to find his way through a dense fog, every step uncertain.

Edwin's eyes scanned the rooftops, searching for some respite from the turmoil within. The moon, now a silver crescent, seemed to mock him with its serene beauty. He felt like a small, insignificant speck in the grand tapestry of life. The Firm, with its intricate web of connections and obligations, loomed large in his mind. He was a mere pawn, waiting for the game to unfold.

A sudden chill ran down his spine as he recalled Neville's words. "You would be called to account for it." The phrase echoed in his mind, a challenge, a warning. Who was Neville to lecture him on civility? Yet, a nagging doubt crept in, a feeling that he had overstepped, that his sharp tongue had gotten the better of him.

Edwin slowed his pace, his footsteps now a deliberate, measured beat. He stopped in front of a small shop, its windows dark and uninviting. In the stillness, he felt the weight of his words, the sting of Neville's rebuke. For a moment, he stood there, suspended between the world he knew and the one he longed to escape. The moon continued its steady march across the sky, indifferent to the turmoil brewing within him.
