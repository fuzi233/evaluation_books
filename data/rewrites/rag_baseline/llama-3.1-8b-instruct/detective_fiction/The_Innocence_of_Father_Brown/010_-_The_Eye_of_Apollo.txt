As the radiant light of the summer solstice danced across the Thames, casting a kaleidoscope of colors on the waters below, two men crossed Westminster Bridge. One stood tall, his stature imposing against the backdrop of the imposing clock-tower of Parliament, while the other, shorter and more humble, walked beside him, clad in the simple attire of a cleric. The Reverend J. Brown, attached to St. Francis Xavier’s Church, Camberwell, was making his way to visit the new offices of his friend, M. Hercule Flambeau, a private detective renowned for his exceptional abilities. 

Their destination, a sleek and modern high-rise, towered above the surrounding buildings, its glass façade glinting in the morning light. The structure was a testament to American architectural innovation, with its intricate network of elevators and telephones that whirred and hummed as they carried the inhabitants of the building through its steel and concrete corridors. Only a handful of residents had taken up residence thus far, including the occupants of the floor above Flambeau and the one below him. However, it was the office situated directly above Flambeau that drew the attention of the two men as they stepped off the bridge. Perched atop this office, an imposing statue of an eye, gilded and surrounded by rays of gold, dominated the view.

"What in the world is that?" Father Brown asked, his voice laced with curiosity, as he halted his steps.

"A new faith," replied Flambeau, a hint of amusement dancing in his voice. "One that preaches the virtues of a sin-free existence, akin to Christian Science. The individual behind this movement, known as Kalon – though I've yet to learn his full name – has taken up residence in the flat above me. His two lady typists occupy the office below, while this charismatic figure above us proclaims himself the New Priest of Apollo, worshipping the sun."

Father Brown's gaze narrowed, his eyes fixed intently on the imposing statue. "May he watch out," he cautioned. "The sun was a capricious deity, known for its cruelty."

"I believe it's a tenet of their doctrine," Flambeau explained, "that a person can withstand any adversity if their mind remains steadfast. The sun and the open eye serve as their primary symbols, representing the idea that a healthy individual could gaze upon the solar orb without flinching."
