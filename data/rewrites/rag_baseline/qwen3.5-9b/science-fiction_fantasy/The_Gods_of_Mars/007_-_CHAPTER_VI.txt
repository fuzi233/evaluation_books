"What is it?" I demanded of the girl, my voice cutting through the sudden tension. She answered only by pointing upward, her finger trembling slightly. I lifted my gaze, and there, suspended against the velvet canvas of the Martian sky, shadowy forms danced—a chaotic ballet of death flitting hither and thither high above the temple, the court, and the garden. Almost instantly, the darkness fractured; flashes of blinding light erupted from these strange vessels, illuminating the chaos below. A deafening roar of musketry shattered the air, followed immediately by answering volleys and flashes bursting forth from the temple walls and ramparts.

"The Black Pirates of Barsoom, O Prince," Thuvia whispered, her voice tight with fear.

In great, sweeping circles, the aircraft of the marauders descended, drawing nearer to the defending forces of the Therns. Volley after volley they vomited upon the temple guards; wave upon wave of fire crashed through the thin, brittle air toward the fleeting, elusive fliers. As the pirates dove closer to the earth, Thern soldiery poured from the sanctuaries into the open gardens and courts. The sight of them exposed drew a score of defenders darting toward us from all directions. The Therns fired through shields affixed to their rifles, but on, steadily on, came the grim, black craft. They were small fliers for the most part, built for crews of two or three men. A few larger behemoths hovered high aloft, raining bombs from their keel batteries upon the sacred grounds.

At length, responding to a concerted signal, the pirates in our immediate vicinity dashed recklessly to the ground, crashing directly into the midst of the Thern soldiery. Scarcely allowing their craft to settle, the creatures manning them leaped among the Therns with the fury of demons unleashed. What fighting! Never had I witnessed its like. I had thought the Green Martians the most ferocious warriors in the universe, but the awful abandon with which the Black Pirates threw themselves upon their foes transcended everything I had ever seen.

Beneath the brilliant light of Mars' two glorious moons, the scene unfolded in vivid, horrific detail. Golden-haired, white-skinned Therns battled with desperate courage in hand-to-hand combat against their ebony-skinned enemies. Here, a knot of struggling warriors trampled a bed of gorgeous *pimalia*; there, the curved sword of a Black man found the heart of a Thern, leaving his dead foe at the foot of a wondrous statue carved from living ruby; yonder, a dozen Therns pressed a single pirate back upon a bench of emerald, whose iridescent surface bore a strangely beautiful Barsoomian design traced in inlaid diamonds.

A little to one side stood Thuvia, the Thark, and I. The tide of battle had not yet reached us, but the fighters swung close enough that we might distinctly note their movements. The Black Pirates interested me immensely. I had heard vague rumors, little more than legends, during my former life on Mars; but never had I seen them, nor spoken with one who had. Popular lore held that they inhabited the lesser moon, descending upon Barsoom at long intervals. Wherever they visited, they wrought horrible atrocities, carrying away firearms, ammunition, and young girls as prisoners. Rumor claimed these captives were sacrificed to some terrible god in an orgy ending in the consumption of their victims.

I had an excellent opportunity to examine them, as the strife occasionally brought one or another close to where I stood. They were large men, possibly six feet and over in height. Their features were clear-cut and handsome in the extreme; their eyes were well-set and large, though a slight narrowness lent them a crafty, predatory appearance. The iris, discernible even by moonlight, was of extreme blackness, while the eyeball itself was quite white and clear. Physically, their structure seemed identical to that of the Therns, the Red Men, and myself. Only the color of their skin differed materially from ours—that of polished ebony—and, odd as it may seem for a Southerner to admit, it added to rather than detracted from their marvelous beauty. But if their bodies were divine, their hearts appeared quite the reverse. Never did I witness such a malign lust for blood as these demons of the outer air evinced in their mad battle with the Therns.

All about us in the garden lay their sinister craft, which the Therns, for reasons then unaccountable to me, made no effort to injure. Now and again, a Black warrior would rush from a nearby temple bearing a young woman in his arms. Straight for his flier he would leap, while comrades fighting nearby rushed to cover his escape. The Therns, on their side, would hasten to rescue the girl, and in an instant the two would be swallowed in the vortex of a maelstrom of yelling devils, hacking and hewing at one another like fiends incarnate. Yet, it seemed, the Black Pirates of Barsoom were invariably victorious. The girl, brought miraculously unharmed through the conflict, was borne away into the outer darkness upon the deck of a swift flier.

Fighting similar to that surrounding us could be heard in both directions as far as sound carried. Thuvia told me that the attacks of the Black Pirates were usually made simultaneously along the entire ribbon-like domain of the Therns, which circles the Valley Dor on the outer slopes of the Mountains of Otz. As the fighting receded from our position momentarily, Thuvia turned toward me with a question.

"Do you understand now, O Prince," she said, "why a million warriors guard the domains of the Holy Therns by day and by night?"

"The scene you are witnessing is but a repetition of what I have seen enacted a score of times during the fifteen years I have been a prisoner here. From time immemorial, the Black Pirates of Barsoom have preyed upon the Holy Therns."

"Yet they never carry their expeditions to a point, as one might readily believe it was in their power to do, where the extermination of the race of Therns is threatened. It is as though they utilize the race merely as playthings, with which they satisfy their ferocious lust for fighting; and from whom they collect toll in arms, ammunition, and prisoners."

"Why don't they jump in and destroy these fliers?" I asked. "That would soon put a stop to the attacks, or at least the Blacks would scarce be so bold. Why, see how perfectly unguarded they leave their craft, as though they were lying safe in their own hangars at home."

"The Therns do not dare. They tried it once, ages ago, but the next night and for a whole moon thereafter, a thousand great black battleships circled the Mountains of Otz, pouring tons of projectiles upon the temples, gardens, and courts, until every Thern who was not killed was driven for safety into the subterranean galleries. The Therns know that they live at all only by the sufferance of the Black men. They were near to extermination that once, and they will not venture risking it again."

As she ceased speaking, a new element was instilled into the conflict, coming from a source equally unlooked-for by either Thern or Pirate. The great Banths we had liberated in the garden had evidently been awed at first by the sound of the battle, the yelling of the warriors, and the loud report of rifle and bomb. But now, enraged by the continuous noise and excited by the smell of fresh blood, all of a sudden a great form shot from a clump of low shrubbery into the midst of a struggling mass of humanity. A horrid scream of bestial rage broke from the Banth as he felt warm flesh beneath his powerful talons. As though his cry were a signal to the others, the entire great pack hurled themselves among the fighters.

Panic reigned in an instant. Thern and Black Man turned alike against the common enemy, for the Banths showed no partiality toward either. The awful beasts bore down a hundred men by the mere weight of their great bodies as they hurled themselves into the thick of the fight. Leaping and clawing, they mowed down the warriors with their powerful paws, turning for an instant to rend their victims with frightful fangs. The scene was fascinating in its terribleness, but suddenly it came to me that we were wasting valuable time watching this conflict, which in itself might prove a means of our escape. The Therns were so engaged with their terrible assailants that now, if ever, escape should be comparatively easy.

I turned to search for an opening through the contending hordes. If we could but reach the ramparts, we might find that the pirates somewhere had thinned the guarding forces and left a way open to us to the world without. As my eyes wandered about the garden, the sight of the hundreds of air-craft lying unguarded around us suggested the simplest avenue to freedom. Why had it not occurred to me before? I was thoroughly familiar with the mechanism of every known make of flier on Barsoom. For nine years I had sailed and fought with the navy of Helium. I had raced through space on the tiny one-man air-scout and commanded the greatest battleship that ever floated in the thin air of dying Mars. To think, with me, is to act.
