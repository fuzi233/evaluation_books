As I stood before my mother, surveying the state of our defenses at Castlewood, I couldn't help but feel a sense of unease. What use was a colonel without a regiment, especially when the Governor and Council were hiding on board their ships, indulging in piracy and arson on the shore? My Lord Dunmore's alliance with the negroes had sent shivers down our spines, and the rumors of his envoy among the Indians of the South had us bracing for the worst. "And these are to be our allies?" I whispered to my mother, exchanging a troubled glance with her. I recalled the image of that savage, his face twisted in a snarl, his knife poised above me on Braddock's Field. My mother's eyes clouded, and she seemed to drift back to a time long past, when our father fell in a foray of Indians. Her hands trembled as she recounted the tale, her voice barely above a whisper.

The figures moving outside our house that night sent her into a panic, convinced they were savages come to claim us. I tried to reassure her, but she clung to the thought, her eyes brimming with tears. "Lord have mercy upon us!" she cried, her voice cracking with fear. The negro allies, summoned by my Lord Dunmore, vanished as soon as they found better prospects elsewhere, but the Indians from the South failed to materialize, leaving us to wonder if they ever intended to arrive.

As I gazed at my mother's grey head, my wife's dark hair, and our child's golden locks, a pang of doubt gnawed at my heart. Would they fall prey to the violence that threatened to engulf us? It was we who had enlisted these allies, and now they seemed to be a curse rather than a blessing. But I dared not attribute our troubles to Providence, lest I appear presumptuous. Perhaps our use of the Indians and mercenaries had brought about their own downfall, as the Continental army triumphed over the defeated Germans, and the brutal murder of Miss McCrea by a band of drunken Indians dealt a devastating blow to the Royal cause.

When the Indian threat receded, my mother's courage returned, and she began to worry about the danger I courted by staying in Virginia.
