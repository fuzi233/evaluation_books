As I walked through the market, the scent of roasting meats wafted through the air, mingling with the pungent aroma of freshly baked bread. The sun beat down upon my skin, warming the rough stone beneath my feet. I had been walking for hours, my stomach growling with hunger, when I stumbled upon the scene unfolding before me. A pair of figures stood pinioned in the marketplace, their faces pale and drawn. They were the night birds, the vampires who preyed on weary travelers in the darkness.

The crowd murmured and shifted, their faces filled with a mix of fear and curiosity. A woman, her eyes wide with a mixture of shock and grief, pushed forward, pointing a trembling finger at one of the accused. Her voice cracked as she spoke, but her words were lost in the din of the crowd. She collapsed, overcome by emotion, and the onlookers surged forward to aid her.

As she regained consciousness, she launched herself at the landlord, her eyes blazing with a fierce maternal love. "My child!" she wailed, snatching at the golden hair tied around his neck. She ripped it free, covering it with kisses, and then, as the truth dawned on her, she sank to the ground, overcome by grief. The crowd erupted into a savage roar, and the accused was torn from the scene, his life slipping away in the chaos.

Denys, the young noble, strode forward, his sword drawn, shouting a warning to the mob. "Back, comrades! Back, I say!" His words were met with a chorus of growls and snarls, but the archers stood firm, their steel glinting in the sunlight. The municipal officers joined forces with the archers, forming a barrier between the crowd and the accused.

As the commotion died down, Denys appealed to the crowd, his voice calm and rational. "What sort of vengeance is this? Would you rather rob the wheel and give the vermin an easy death?" The mob seethed, their eyes flashing with anger, but the archers held firm, their presence a reminder of the consequences of their actions.

The prisoners were led away, the crowd jeering and hurling insults. Denys watched them go, his expression grim, before turning towards the White Hart Inn, where he hoped to find his friend Gerard.
