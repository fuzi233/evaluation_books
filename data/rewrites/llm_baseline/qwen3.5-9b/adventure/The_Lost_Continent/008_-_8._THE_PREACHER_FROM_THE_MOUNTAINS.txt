I had not known true rest for some time, so when the day finally yielded to evening, I surrendered to a deep, undisturbed sleep while Nais kept vigil. By nightfall, we remained concealed within the sanctuary's sanctum, adhering to our plan: I would linger there until the camp settled, buying myself a window of opportunity to reach the coast unnoticed.

Night descended in a damp haze, a steady drizzle soaking the stones. Through the narrow slits in the temple walls, we observed the sprawling encampment below. Fires were tended with care, casting flickering light on figures huddled in skins and rags, steam rising as warmth battled the chill. Those who suffer in such discomfort are notoriously sharp-tongued and cruel in spirit. Nais watched the scene with a furrowed brow, displeased by the inclemency of the weather.

"A fine night," she murmured. "I would have sent my lord back to the city long ago, leaving no one the wiser. Yet in this biting cold, people sleep fitfully. We must wait until the hour drugs them into deeper slumber."

Thus we waited, seated together on a pavement untouched by worshipers for generations. Words were scarce, yet the silence between us was comfortable.

As the hours dragged on, the stillness fractured. From the distance, a horn or shell blew a harsh, raucous note that persisted. The sound grew louder, drawing nearer, while voices erupted from the fires, railing against the disturber. Soon, the noise coalesced into a stationary point. Rising, we peered through the wall-slits to see the camp waking from uneasy dreams, clustering around a figure standing atop the pedestal of a war engine.

We could not hear his words, nor did our curiosity burn brightly. As Nais whispered, everyone awake seemed determined to tire themselves out listening to this fellow. In such chaos, my escape should have been simple—a slip in the opposite direction.

But fate, that relentless busybody, intervened. A dozen pairs of feet splashed through the mud toward the temple, shouting demands that Nais join the assembly. Her tongue was evidently eloquent; they feared the speaker on the war engine would fracture their ranks unless countered by someone of skill.

Suddenly, I found myself cornered by circumstance. Yet Nais was resolute. "No fight," she insisted, her voice firm. "If my courage can turn this tide, we avoid bloodshed."

"Come out with me," she whispered urgently. "Stay far from the firelight."

"But how shall I explain my presence?"

"There is no need to explain anything," she replied, bitterness coloring her tone. "They will assume you are my lover. It is the custom here. Oh, why did the Gods not grant you a beard, curled like a man's? Then you could have vanished safely two hours ago."

"A smooth chin suits me better."

"So it does," she murmured, leaning her weight against the heavy stone doorframe and pushing it open. "Your chin."

The ragged mob outside—men and women alike—did not pause to scrutinize us. Coarse jokes flew through the air (jokes I might have met with steel), and they surged into the darkness, glancing back only to ensure we followed. Everywhere, black shadows pressed inward; the rain hissed violently on the campfires as we passed. Sleepers were gone; all were drawn to the preacher on the war engine. Had we attempted to deviate, we would have been spotted instantly.

We moved through the dark, soon falling within earshot.

At first, the preacher's words were indistinct. Then, a fervent question rang out: *"Who are your chiefs?"* Immediately, rational discourse drowned in uproar.

*"We have no chiefs!"* the crowd roared. *"We are done with chiefs! We are equals here. Remove your silly magic. You may kill us with it if you wish, but rule us you shall not. Not the other priests, not Phorenice, not anyone. We are done with rulers."*

Pressing closer, we saw the man clearly. He was old, white hair tumbling onto broad shoulders, a long, untrimmed white beard framing a gaunt face. Bare except for a scrap of rag at his waist, his body glistened wet under the torchlight.

In his hand, however, he held the mark of his caste. He wielded it to punctuate his sentences, sometimes swirling it to bathe his naked form in a halo of light. It was a wand tipped with unconsumable fire, glowing and blazing like a fallen star. It was the Symbol of Our Lord the Sun, a credential no mortal could forge, trusted implicitly by any civilized man.

Indeed, the frantic rabble questioned his priesthood not for a second. Though they screamed that he could kill them with magic, they stubbornly refused to return to their old allegiance. *"We have suffered too much in recent years,"* they cried. *"We are done with rulers forever."*

For my part, I viewed the old man with dread. This was Zaemon, father to Nais—the same man who had sat beside me at Phorenice's elbow just yesterday, yet today denounced me as Deucalion. These rebels had nearly sunk a navy to destroy me four days prior. If they knew I was nearby, even with Nais advocating for me, her logic would fall on deaf ears. The High Gods guard their secrets well, but I suspected mine was dangerously close to being severed.

The beautiful woman perceived my tension. She intertwined her fingers with mine, pressing them gently as a farewell. I squeezed back, sorrowful not to see her again. Yet, I couldn't suppress a grim satisfaction imagining the mound of corpses these unskilled rebels would create before they tore me down. Such was inevitable. Of all passions, the joy of strife burns hottest, and only an old warrior facing his final battle understands how deeply this lust is woven into the human soul.

For the moment, Zaemon was lost in his rant, unaware of my presence. Free to observe, I listened to the burning words spreading through the crowd.

His sermon was familiar. He claimed that since history's dawn, the Gods had designated one clan to rule as Priests. Until Phorenice's rise, they had ruled with justice, fighting invaders, battling beasts, and predicting earthquakes to spare the populace. They were selfless servants of Atlantis, guiding the kingdom favored by the High Gods. Under their guidance, humanity had reached its zenith.

*"But,"* the old man cried, waving the glowing Symbol until his wet body shimmered, *"the people grew fat and careless. They believed their fortune was earned by their own wit and strength, forgetting it was a divine gift. Neglect followed. Temples stood barren. Punishment ensued. The Gods decreed that a wife of a Governor—a man of great power—should find a woman abandoned by the roadside and adopt her. The Gods, in infinite wisdom, forged this child into a scourge for Atlantis. You who feel Phorenice's hand know the completeness of their design."*

*"Yet, as they build, they can destroy,"* Zaemon continued. *"Those who debase Phorenice are now appointed. The old rule returns, but only after you sinners are chastened enough to cry out for relief."* He brandished the Symbol. *"Behold the unspeakable Power it signifies. I am its mouthpiece. Decide now: Shall the Gods overthrow this woman who has scorned Them and trampled you? Or shall you be purged further of your pride before you are ripe for salvation?"*

He paused, his ragged beard sinking to his chest. A young man, clad in skin and armed, shoved through the crowd to the platform.

*"Hear me, brethren!"* he bellowed. *"We are done with tyrants. Death comes, and we fear it little. But we will not own rulers again—that is our final decree. My lord,"* he addressed the old man bravely, *"I know you can kill me with magic, but I have spoken, and I accept the cost."*

*"I can kill you, but I will not,"* Zaemon replied calmly. *"You have spoken your nonsense. Return to the ground."*

*"Yes, but you will,"* the old man snapped, his brows tightening. No blow was struck; the glowing Symbol did not lift in threat. Yet the young man tried to retort, seized by sudden dumbness, and stumbled back in fear. The crowd thrilled, expecting a miracle to sway them back to obedience.

The old priest glowered in fury. *"You dogs! Empty-witted creatures! Do you demand I degrade the Higher Mysteries into a mummer's show? Do you seek temptation? Come in humility, and I will declare your pardon. Remain stubborn, and the scourge Phorenice may annihilate you before she answers for her crimes. Choose!"*

Voices rose again in the wet night, weapons raised menacingly. The faction for independence outnumbered the weary, and terror kept the reluctant from surrendering. It was a bitter irony regarding their boasted freedom of speech. With sly malice, I whispered my observation to Nais. She clutched my hand, imploring silence. *"Be quiet, my lord, or they will tear you apart. They are mad with mischief."*

*"Yet a few hours ago, you sought to kill me,"* I reminded her.
