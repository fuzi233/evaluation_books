The silence of the storeroom, heavy with dust and the weight of exhaustion, shattered abruptly. I do not remember how many hours I had lain upon the floor, lost to consciousness, but the air grew thick with the stench of impending violence before I fully woke. A scream tore through the subterranean gloom, followed immediately by the thunderous crack of gunfire. The sound echoed endlessly through the stone corridors, a terrifying chorus of death that vibrated through the soles of my bare feet.

Before I could sit up, twelve figures materialized from the shadows of the far doorway. They were lesser therns, their faces twisted in a mixture of panic and malice, rifles trained in a deadly arc. Before them lay the broken forms of my companions. Only Thuvia and the Great Tarkas remained alive, having shared my fortune on the dusty floor and escaped the initial volley.

As I scrambled to my feet, the weapons of the therns dropped. Their countenances shifted from aggression to sheer terror.

"What madness is this?" I roared, my voice cutting through the haze of shock. "Will Sator Throg be slain by the hands of his own servants?"

"Hold, O Master!" cried one of the trembling fools, edging backward toward the exit. "Forgive us!"

"Tell me your purpose," Thuvia urged quietly, pressing close beside me. "Speak."

"Why are you here?" I demanded, stepping forward.

"The Father of Therns commanded us," the leader stammered, his gaze darting nervously toward the massive figure of Tars Tarkas. "Two fugitives from the outer world roam the dominions of the Therns. One was pale with black hair, the other a giant of green skin."

"And where is the pale one?" I asked sharply.

"This one stands before you," Thuvia said, gesturing to the silent, imposing Thark. "And if you examine the corpse near the door, you will find the other. These men failed in their duty; they could not capture both. Instead, they slaughtered us. For this failure, Sator Throg would surely have spared us, but your foolishness has left us all but three. You have nearly killed the mighty Sator himself."

The soldiers slunk back, their eyes wide with shame and fear.

"Now," Thuvia whispered, "do as I say. Carry these bodies to the plant-men and return to your quarters. Tell them what happened."

"Yes, O Mighty One," they murmured, lifting the dead. As they carried the body of Sator Throg, one paused, staring down at the face. He glanced quickly toward me, a flicker of suspicion crossing his features, before resuming his burden. As he exited, a cunning, triumphant smile played upon his lips—a silent acknowledgment that the truth was known, or at least suspected.

Alone again, the gravity of our situation settled heavily upon my heart. Our chances for freedom had evaporated with the lives of our comrades. Before the alarm could spread, before the entire fortress descended upon us, we needed to move.

"It cannot be good," Thuvia said, her voice urgent. "Even if this fool dares not accuse you openly, there are those higher up who will demand answers. And they will find them."

"I feel nothing but despair," I admitted, rubbing my bruised ribs. "My wounds burn, and there is no medicine here to heal them. I long for the salves of the Green Women, but they are gone."

"Look at me," Thuvia said softly, catching the stray lock of Mota's hair that drifted before my face. "If we act swiftly, before the general alarm sounds, we may still slip away. Can we not?"

"How much time do we have?"

"He will go straight to Matai Shang," Thuvia explained. "He is a Thorian, high in rank. Matai will not keep him waiting long. Once Matai believes the tale, search parties will sweep the galleries and gardens within an hour. We must leave now."

"There is no time for deliberation," I said, steeling myself. "What is the path?"

"Up the cliffs, through the gardens, across the temples to the outer court," Thuvia directed. "From there, the ramparts. But beware, Prince. Since the dawn of time, the Therns have built their defenses stone by stone. A million warriors await inside the temples. None can pass unseen."

"Tars Tarkas suggests we wait for nightfall," the great warrior offered. "Daylight offers no cover."

"Nights are colder, yes, but the guards remain vigilant," Thuvia countered. "However, fewer patrol the gardens by night. It is the lesser risk."

"We shall test the windows," I decided.

Following Thuvia through winding tunnels, we emerged into an opening overlooking the Valley Dor. To our right, the sun dipped below the western range of Otz, a vast red sphere vanishing into the horizon. Below, on his balcony, stood Matai Shang, the Holy Thern, his scarlet robes drawn tight against the biting chill that would soon descend. On Mars, the atmosphere is thin; it holds no heat, and when the sun sets, there is no twilight. The transition from blinding light to absolute, suffocating darkness is instantaneous, like a single lamp being snuffed out in a sealed room. Soon, the two moons would rise, casting their ghostly, silver glare across the crimson landscape.

Beneath us, the eastern banks of Korus glowed under the fading light. Herds of Plant-Men grazed among the trees, their tall, tail-swishing forms moving with jerky, unnatural grace. I realized then that the trimmed foliage I had seen earlier was not the work of civilization, but of these grotesque scavengers. Further down, a canoe laden with prisoners floated down the River Iss. As soon as the Herald spotted them, he let out a shrill, unearthly cry. The Plant-Men surged from the grove, a horde of ugly, leaping monstrosities, and devoured the helpless victims with gaping, sucking mouths.

"They feed on the flesh first," Thuvia observed grimly. "Then the White Apes take the rest."

Just as the great white monsters began their grisly harvest, darkness swallowed the valley whole. Thuvia seized my arm, and we retreated into the labyrinthine corridors, ascending toward the surface.

Our progress was hindered twice by Banths, the great, savage beasts that roamed the underground halls. Each time, however, Thuvia uttered a low, commanding word, and the snarling monsters slunk away, cowed by her authority. She had once fallen into a pit filled with these beasts, and instead of killing her, they had become her devoted guardians. Now, she walked with a host of fifty of them trailing in our wake.

"To use them as a diversion above ground?" I suggested, watching the lathered jaws of the beasts that eyed Tars Tarkas with hunger.

"Aye," Thuvia agreed, her voice dropping to a purring hum as she guided the pack. "It will draw the eyes away from us."

We moved through the dim, radium-lit passages, a strange procession: naked humans flanked by maned predators, led by the beautiful girl, carrying the crown of a Holy Thern. Finally, we reached a chamber filled with the horrors of the underworld—a chaotic heap of hybrids, deformed creatures born of prison and captivity, resembling corpses more than living things. It was a veritable Inferno, a graveyard of lost souls.

Passing through the chambers of the dying and the chained, we climbed the final steep ascent to the surface. Here, the air grew cool and smelled of soil and growth. Thuvia opened a door leading to the inner gardens, and the Banths, eager for the scent of fresh earth, surged ahead, breaking into the moonlit expanse.

Suddenly, a cry rang out from the rooftops of the temples—an alarm bell that echoed across the fortress, summoning the defenders. The great sword of Tars Tarkas flashed from its sheath, and Thuvia clung to my side, her heart pounding. We had bought a moment, but the gates of hell were swinging wide, and the hunt for us had begun anew.
