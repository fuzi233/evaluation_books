I collapsed onto the soft bed, my mind reeling over every detail of the tale the princess had just shared. She had slipped, unguarded for a single reckless second, and that one careless line had solved the riddle of how I’d found her half-dead in the forest: the leopardess had bounded clear of the bank, the princess had crumpled into the mud, and the running stream had melted her self-cast glamour away. Her own admission of what she’d come to this realm to hunt laid bare the terrible peril the Little Ones were in at that very moment. I had saved the life of their single most feared, ravenous enemy.
I had barely landed on that horrifying conclusion before sleep dragged me under. The heady, sweet wine she’d served me was far from harmless, it seemed.
I woke to nightfall, a softly glowing lamp strung from the ceiling gilding every edge of the chamber in hazy, warm light. A thick, delicious languor wrapped around my bones, like I was drifting far out to sea on the bosom of a twilight ocean. Just existing felt like a gift. No ache, no weariness. I thought I must be dying, peaceful as I was—until a searing, sickening stab lanced straight through my heart. Then another, sharper still, so hot it made my vision swim. I couldn’t lift a hand to press to my chest, some invisible weight pinning my limbs fast. The pain ebbed, but my whole body lay paralyzed, my will screaming to move and not a single muscle obeying. Some hateful, hungry thing was feeding on my vitality, inches away.
I stopped fighting, lay still, and then felt a soft palm press down on my face, shoving my head deep into the pillow. The weight vanished from my chest. I blinked my eyes open.
The princess was standing on the bed right over me, staring off into the empty room like she was trapped in a waking dream. Her great dark eyes were clear, calm, her mouth curved in a line of sated hunger. She wiped a thin streak of red from her lips. She caught me staring, bent fast, and swiped the linen handkerchief in her hand across my eyes—its edge dragged like a cold knife blade, and I went blind for a few long, breathless seconds.
I heard the dull soft thud of a massive padded paw landing after a small jump. My sight cleared just in time to see the long, spotted tail of the great leopardess flick out of sight through the half-open door. I bolted after her. The creature was gone without a trace. I tore down the stairs and into the alabaster hall, where the high moon bleached the pale stone until the whole space glowed like the hollow interior of a moon rock. The princess was not there.
I had to find her. Only when she was in my line of sight could I defend myself. Left unguarded, asleep, I was nothing but tame prey for her to feed on, a human fountain for her ravenous, unquenchable thirst. She’d been kind to me only to make me easier to devour. I didn’t fear her while my eyes were open, but sooner or later I would blink, I would fall asleep, and she would creep in. She was everywhere I could not see, waiting in the dark gaps of every room, every secret hollow of sleep.
Outside the alabaster hall, the dark was absolute. I groped my way forward with hands and feet, until my fingers brushed a heavy velvet curtain. I shoved it aside and stepped into the Black Hall. A silent, massive assembly milled inside. I had no way to tell how I could see them: walls, floor, and ceiling were swallowed in a blackness deeper than the darkest moonless, starless midnight, but I could vaguely make out dozens of shifting shapes weaving through the void. My eyes felt fogged, no matter how hard I rubbed and stared, nothing snapped into focus. Form tangled with shadow, silence hummed with undefined motion, and the whole wide space throbbed with a dim, confused dance of creatures I recognized from past encounters. Now a woman with blazing golden eyes peered out from a grinning skull, now a armored rider trotted past on a skeleton horse, now one of the hideous burrowing tunnel phantoms slithered past my boots. No order, no rhythm, just shifting eddies of half-solid shapes and free-floating shadows that moved of their own unmoored will.
I searched every face for the princess, but she was nowhere to be found. Not a single being in the crowd paid me any mind as I wandered through the horde. At last I gave up, groped my way along the dark wall until my fingers found the curtained doorway leading to the vestibule. Moonlight spilled dimly over the space, and the leopardess’s cage was the stage of a silent, thrashing brawl: two forms, half human half beast, tangled so tight their limbs merged, writhing and wrestling in a vicious locked embrace.
Blink, and the leopardess was already outside the cage, padding calmly toward the open front door. I threw a glance over my shoulder as I ran after her, and there she was, still crouched motionless in the cage, exactly as I’d first found her. The moon hung round and high in the sky, the bodiless shadow I’d spotted the night before gliding through the trees toward the outer gate, and the real spotted leopardess followed close behind, swinging her tail slow.
I trailed them a short distance back, silent as the dark, and neither of the two glanced over their shoulders once. We passed through the open gate and down into the sleeping city, every stone bathed in pale moonlight that hummed with quiet, waiting expectation. The shadow headed straight for the staircase where I’d woken the night before, climbed it without pausing, the leopardess at his heels. I sprinted after them, and a shriek of raw, unutterable horror split the stillness. A second later, a heavy soft weight thudded to the pavement right at my feet: the crushed, blackened, unrecognizable but familiar body of the woman who had first led me home, then locked me out in the dark.
I stood frozen, and the spotted leopardess came bounding down the stairs, a tiny baby clamped gentle but firm in her jaws. I lunged to grab her before she could twist away at the stair foot, but in that exact instant, a streak of glowing silver shot across the moonlit pavement—my old friend the white leopardess, slamming full force into the spotted beast’s throat. The baby slipped from the leopardess’s jaws, I caught it mid-fall, and stood back to watch the battle unfold.
It was a wild, breathtaking sight: first one cat on top, then the other, both too absorbed to make any noise louder than a low rumbling growl, a sharp snarl of hate, a whimper of pain. Claws scrabbled madly for purchase on the smooth stone as they bit and dragged and heaved, fighting for every inch of ground. The spotted leopardess was larger, heavier, and I tensed to step in to aid my white-furred friend—until I saw that for all her speed and strength, the spotted beast had nothing on the white leopardess’s iron endurance. Not once did she loosen her death grip on the other’s throat. At last a howl of agony tore out of the spotted beast, rising in a ragged, mounting crescendo that shifted from feline snarl to a woman’s raw, screaming peak of unendurable pain.
The white leopardess relaxed her jaws. The spotted one staggered back, and rose upright on her hind legs, right in the silver moonlight. The dark spots that covered her pelt peeled off like swarms of fleeing moths, swirling across her pale skin, rushing for refuge in her dark eyes until they merged and vanished. Only a handful of straggling spots got tangled in her streaming dark hair, leaving her glowing bright as the moon after a horde of small wind-tossed clouds have cleared off its disc—save for a thin thread of blood trickling down the pale column of her throat from the white leopardess’s terrible bite wounds.
She turned away, took a few staggering steps with the wild, unearthly gait of Hecate the huntress, then the spots burst back over her skin, and she shifted at once back into leopard form, galloping off into the dark at a full stretch. The white leopardess turned to me next, sprang up to pry my arms gently open to catch the baby I nearly dropped in my shock, and flew off with the little one clutched safe in her jaws, racing for the outer gate.
