The scent of fresh bread wafted through the air as I entered the dimly lit room, the faint glow of the reading lamp casting long shadows across the walls. My mistress sat hunched over an open cookbook, her eyes scanning the pages with an intensity that seemed almost desperate. I approached her quietly, not wanting to disturb the silence that filled the space.

"Officer," she said, without looking up, "is it crucial to the investigation that you know if anyone in this household plans to leave?"

Sergeant Cuff's deep voice replied, "Yes, my lady. It's essential."

I felt a flutter in my chest as my mistress's gaze shifted towards me, her eyes narrowing slightly. "Miss Verinder intends to visit her aunt, Mrs. Ablewhite, at Frizinghall. She's arranged to depart early tomorrow morning."

The sergeant's eyes met mine, and I felt a pang of unease. I took a step forward, then hesitated, my heart racing with a mix of emotions. "May I ask, my lady, when Miss Verinder informed you of her decision?" the sergeant inquired.

"A little over an hour ago," my mistress replied, her voice steady.

The sergeant's gaze lingered on me before he turned back to my mistress. I felt a surge of anxiety, my thoughts racing with possibilities. "I have no authority to dictate Miss Verinder's actions, my lady," he said. "However, I would be grateful if you could persuade her to delay her departure until later in the day. I need to visit Frizinghall myself tomorrow morning, and I'd like to speak with her before she leaves."

My mistress nodded, her eyes never leaving the book. "I'll instruct the coachman to delay the carriage until two o'clock." She paused, then looked up at the sergeant. "Is there anything else you'd like to add?"

The sergeant's expression was inscrutable. "Only one thing, my lady. If Miss Verinder seems surprised by the change in plans, please don't mention my involvement in postponing her journey."

My mistress's head jerked up, as if she'd been about to respond, but she caught herself and returned to her book, dismissing us with a wave of her hand. As we stepped out into the hallway, the sergeant murmured, "That's a remarkable woman. Her composure would have unraveled the mystery sooner, if she hadn't kept her wits about her."
