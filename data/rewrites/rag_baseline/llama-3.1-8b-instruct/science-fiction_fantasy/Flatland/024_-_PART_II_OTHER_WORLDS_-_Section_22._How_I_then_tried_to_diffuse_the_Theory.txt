In the hushed hours before dawn, when the mist-shrouded streets of Flatland lay shrouded in an eerie stillness, Axiom's thoughts turned to the weighty burden he bore. His eyes, weary from the endless struggle to convey the unfathomable truths he had witnessed, wandered to the sheaf of letters scattered across his desk. Each one bore the confident scrawl of Mr. Lang, a man whose words dripped with an unsettling blend of fascination and disdain. Axiom's fingers drummed a staccato rhythm on the polished wood as he pondered the futility of his efforts. His treatise, "Through Flatland to Thoughtland," remained unfinished, its diagrams and illustrations a frustrating jumble of lines and shapes, inaccessible to the minds of his fellow Flatlanders.

The memory of his Grandson's skepticism still rankled, a constant reminder of the chasm between the world he inhabited and the one he sought to describe. Eleven months had passed since his return from Spaceland, yet the experience continued to taunt him, a siren's call to the unattainable. He recalled the Cube, that impossible shape that defied the very fabric of his existence. His mind's eye strained to recreate the sensation, but the image remained elusive, a will-o'-the-wisp dancing just beyond his grasp.

Axiom's thoughts were interrupted by the faint sound of footsteps outside his door. His servant, a diligent but obtuse man named Thistlewaite, entered with a tray bearing a steaming teapot and a selection of delicate china cups. As he poured the tea, Axiom's gaze drifted to the window, where the first light of dawn was creeping over the rooftops. The sky was a deep, bruised purple, a color that seemed to hold within it the promise of secrets waiting to be unlocked.

The tea, however, was a poor substitute for the intellectual stimulation Axiom craved. His mind continued to wander, and his lips moved of their own accord, uttering phrases that would have been treasonous in any other company. "The eye that discerns the interiors of things," he murmured, his voice barely audible above a whisper. "The all-seeing land." Thistlewaite, oblivious to the subtle nuances of Axiom's words, nodded sympathetically and refilled his cup.
