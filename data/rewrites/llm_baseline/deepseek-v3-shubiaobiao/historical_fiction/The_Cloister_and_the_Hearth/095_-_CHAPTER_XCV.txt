The hermit’s eyes darted wildly between the sleeping child in his arms and Margaret, who knelt before him, her face alight with a radiance he had never seen before—maternal pride. His breath came in short gasps, his mind reeling.

*"Mine?"* he whispered hoarsely, his gaze flickering between the boy and her.

Margaret laughed softly, a sound like music in the dim cell. "What, does a father not know his own flesh? A man of God, too? Fie, Gerard, to feign ignorance! Look at him—see the gold in his hair, the mole upon his finger—just like yours. Ask your mother if you doubt me!"

Clement’s hands trembled. The child stirred but did not wake, nestled against his chest. Margaret reached out, her fingers brushing the boy’s tiny hand before lifting it to her lips, pressing a fervent kiss. "He found you for me," she murmured. "When I watched you feed the birds from your window, it was this little mark that told me—*you* were his father."

A choked sob broke from Clement’s throat. The weight of solitude, of relentless fasting, had left him frail. His knees buckled, and he swayed, nearly collapsing. Margaret caught him, her touch steadying. "Hush now," she soothed. "You are not the first man to return from wandering with more than he left with."

But the truth struck deeper. Here was his son—*his*—a living bond to the life he had forsaken. And Margaret, fierce and tender, holding him to the earth with her love.

---

The moon cast silver light over the path to Gouda manse, where a single candle burned in the window. Inside, Catherine knelt in prayer, her face lined with years of grief. A sharp rap at the glass—*"Mother!"*—and before she could rise, the door burst open.

Clement fell at her feet, arms outstretched.

*"My boy!"* she cried, clutching him to her breast.

No titles mattered now—no hermit, no priest. Only a son, returned.

Margaret watched from the threshold, her heart full. The long night had ended. Dawn would find Gerard Eliassoen in his rightful place—no longer a fugitive from the world, but its shepherd.

And the child, the living thread between past and future, slept on, unknowing, in his father’s arms.
