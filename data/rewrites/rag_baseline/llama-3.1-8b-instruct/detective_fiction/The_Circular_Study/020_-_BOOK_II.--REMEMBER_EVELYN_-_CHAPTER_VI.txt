In the dimly lit study, a faint scent of pipe smoke lingered, mingling with the whispers of the family gathered around the fireplace. The rain-soaked evening cast a melancholy veil over the room, much like the somber mood that had settled upon Lady Violet Lebas as she pondered the events of the day. The flickering flames danced across the walls, casting eerie shadows that seemed to weave a tale of their own. As she sat amidst the hushed tones and gentle murmurs, her mind wandered to the tragic events that had unfolded within these very walls.

A series of questions swirled in her thoughts, each one a puzzle piece waiting to be fitted into the larger tapestry of the mystery. Like the intricate patterns etched onto the ancient stones that adorned the estate, the threads of truth and deception began to reveal themselves, one by one. The faces of those present – Thomas Adams, his wife, the inspector, and the enigmatic Mr. Gryce – seemed to blur together as she tried to recall the specifics of the day's events.

As the clock struck nine, the room fell silent, the only sound the soft ticking of the grandfather clock in the corner. It was then that Lady Violet's thoughts turned to the mysterious message scrawled on the paper found near the lifeless body of Mr. Felix Adams. The words "I return you your daughter. Neither you nor she will ever see me again. Remember Evelyn!" seemed to sear themselves into her memory, a haunting reminder of the complexities that lay beneath the surface of this seemingly tranquil household.

A faint rustling sound broke the stillness as Thomas Adams shifted uncomfortably in his chair, his eyes locked onto his wife's face. The inspector's gaze, however, remained fixed on the paper, his brow furrowed in concentration. Mr. Gryce, too, appeared lost in thought, his hands clasped together as if in prayer.

As the night wore on, the questions continued to swirl in Lady Violet's mind. Why had Mr. Adams's face shown sorrow rather than hate in the moments leading up to his death? Why had he chosen to swallow the incriminating paper, only to die with it clenched between his teeth? The steel plate found between the doorframe seemed an enigma, a relic from a world beyond the one they knew. And what of old Poindexter's sudden appearance, his words a jarring note in the otherwise stately melody of the evening?
