As the moon ascended above the rooftops of Florence, its gentle light cast an eerie glow over the piazza in front of the Old Palace. The air was alive with tension, the usually bustling streets eerily quiet. Instead of the soft hum of night wanderers, the sounds of the city were dominated by the cacophony of shouts, imprecations, and clanging steel echoing off the stone walls. The pavement was lost beneath the sea of upturned faces, all straining to catch a glimpse of the unfolding drama.

Inside the palace, the Signoria had gathered in the council-chamber, their deliberations having raged on throughout the night. The debate had been intense, the arguments pro and con the Appeal hanging precariously in the balance. But in the end, it was Francesco Valori's impassioned plea that swayed the majority, his words dripping with conviction and warning. The four dissenting voices were silenced, their white beans cast aside as the nine black beans declared the fate of the five condemned men. The decision was met with a mixture of shock and resignation, the weight of their verdict settling heavy on the shoulders of the Florentine people.

As the Eight of the Night departed to prepare for the execution, the five prisoners were led barefoot and shackled through the council-chamber, their friends' desperate attempt to stir the hearts of the crowd falling flat. The sight of the dignified Bernardo del Nero and Niccolò Ridolfi, their faces etched with resignation, was meant to evoke sympathy, but it only seemed to steel the resolve of the onlookers. Meanwhile, the news of the decision had spread like wildfire, drawing in the crowds from all corners of the city.

Romola stood at the edge of the piazza, her eyes fixed on the entrance of the palace, her heart heavy with anticipation. She had managed to secure permission to visit her godfather in the evening, and now she waited with his confessor, Giovan Battista Ridolfi, for the moment when the guard would escort him to the Bargello. Her determination to stay by his side until the very end was unwavering, a testament to the bond between them. As she stood there, the sound of clashing steel and the wails of the crowd created a sense of chaos, a stark contrast to the serenity of the evening air. The moon above seemed to be watching, its gentle light a reminder of the fragility of life in the face of death.
