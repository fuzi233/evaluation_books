As the evening sun cast its golden glow upon the modest Dashwood estate, Mrs. Jennings descended upon the sisters' quarters with an air of genuine concern etched upon her countenance. Without awaiting an invitation, she swept into the room, her presence stirring the faint scent of exotic spices and fresh herbs wafting from her person. Her eyes, like two warm lanterns, shone with a deep empathy as she approached Marianne, who lay listlessly upon the bed, her gaze averted.

"How do you fare, my dear?" Mrs. Jennings asked, her voice a gentle breeze carrying the soothing undertones of a summer rain. Marianne's response was a silent turn of her head, a subtle rebuff that spoke volumes of her turmoil. Mrs. Jennings' eyes widened in dismay as she took in the ravaged beauty of her young friend, her heart swelling with compassion. "Ah, Miss Dashwood, you look quite undone. It's no wonder, really. I've just received word that Edward Ferrars is to wed soon – that insipid, good-for-nothing young man! I'm at a loss to understand how he could treat a lady of your standing so callously."

Her words spilled forth like a rich, fragrant sauce, each syllable a testament to her genuine distress. As she spoke, she poured forth a stream of anecdotes, each one a flavorful morsel designed to lift Marianne's spirits. The savory aroma of roasting meats wafted through the air, mingling with the scent of simmering herbs and spices, transporting the sisters to a realm of comfort and security. Yet, despite the warmth of her intentions, Mrs. Jennings' words only served to intensify Marianne's anguish, her pain a palpable thing that refused to be assuaged.

Elinor, attuned to her sister's sorrow, watched with a knowing glance as Mrs. Jennings lavished attention upon Marianne, her gestures a gentle dance of affection. Though her own heart ached with sympathy, she recognized the futility of attempting to alleviate Marianne's suffering through words alone. As the evening wore on, the sisters' meal was served, a sumptuous feast of roasted meats, steaming vegetables, and delicate pastries, each dish a testament to the culinary prowess of Mrs. Jennings. Marianne, though visibly distressed, surprised her sister by consuming a few bites, her eyes cast downward, her thoughts a million miles away.
