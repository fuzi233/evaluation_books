The sun beat down upon the cobblestones of Florence's Piazza della Signoria, casting long shadows across the crowd gathered to witness the trial by fire. It was a spectacle unlike any other, one that had drawn a multitude of onlookers, all eager to behold the miracle that was to unfold. As mid-day approached, the air grew thick with anticipation, and the crowd surged forward, seeking vantage points on the facades and rooftops of the surrounding buildings.

Men perched precariously on iron rods, grasping slim pillars, and balancing on the necks of ancient statuary, their faces set with determination. Some found temporary reprieve on narrow ledges, while others clung to rusty staples embedded in the stone walls, their knuckles white with tension. The atmosphere was electric, the very air charged with expectation.

The focal point of this gathering was the long wooden platform, its surface heaped with a pyre of dry oak, crackling thorns, and well-oiled rags. The fuel was arranged in a deliberate pattern, a testament to the careful planning and organization that had gone into preparing this spectacle. The platform stretched from the marble terrace in front of the Old Palace, where Marzocco, the stone lion, watched over the proceedings with a stern expression.

Three contingents of armed men stood guard, their presence a reminder of the gravity of the occasion. Five hundred hired soldiers of the Signoria stood before the palace, their armor glinting in the sunlight. Across the Piazza, five hundred Compagnacci under Dolfo Spini waited, their faces set with a mixture of curiosity and hostility. Meanwhile, three hundred armed citizens, loyal supporters of Savonarola, stood watch before Orgagna's Loggia, where the Franciscans and Dominicans would soon take their places alongside their champions.

As the crowd held its collective breath, the air vibrated with an almost palpable sense of anticipation. The trial by fire was about to begin, and the fate of the two monks hung precariously in the balance. If they emerged unscathed, it would be seen as a clear sign of divine approval for Fra Girolamo's prophetic claims. Conversely, if they failed to emerge unharmed, it would be taken as a sign of divine disapproval. The outcome was far from certain, and the crowd's collective heart pounded in unison with the drums beating out a rhythmic cadence in the distance.
