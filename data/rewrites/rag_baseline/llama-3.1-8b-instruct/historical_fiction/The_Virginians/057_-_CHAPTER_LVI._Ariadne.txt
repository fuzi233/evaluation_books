The morning light seeped through the windows of Kensington Square, casting an unforgiving glow on the faces of the Castlewood family. They gathered around the breakfast table, a ritualistic display of civility amidst the chaos of their lives. My lord lounged in his bedgown and nightcap, still recovering from the previous night's revelries at the Star and Garter. His sister Maria sat beside him, her eyes downcast, her expression a mask of sorrow.

Their nephew, Will, burst into the room, a bundle of energy and malice. "I've got news," he announced, his voice dripping with excitement. "Tom Claypool saw them both at Ranelagh, and Jack Morris heard it from Harry Warrington's own lips." The room fell silent, awaiting Will's revelation. "It seems our beloved Fortunate Youth, our Virginian, is not who we thought he was."

The room erupted into a cacophony of reactions. Maria's face paled, her eyes welling up with tears. "It's a shame, it's a shame!" she sobbed, hiding her face in her handkerchief. Their stepmother and sister exchanged a look, their expressions a mixture of confusion and concern.

My lord, ever the wit, couldn't resist a jest. "Ah, Maria, my dear, I didn't mean to hurt your feelings." But Maria was beyond consolation. "You've taken everything from him, all he had to lose," she accused, her voice trembling with anger. "And now you mock him in his misfortune!"

The room fell silent once more, the tension palpable. Will, ever the provocateur, reveled in the drama he had unleashed. "Tell us what happened, Will," my lord said finally, his voice a gentle prod to continue the conversation. Will obliged, spinning a tale of deceit and betrayal, of a man who had been living a lie. As he spoke, the room seemed to shrink, the walls closing in on the family's secrets and lies. The morning light outside grew brighter, illuminating the cracks in their facade, revealing the emptiness beneath.
