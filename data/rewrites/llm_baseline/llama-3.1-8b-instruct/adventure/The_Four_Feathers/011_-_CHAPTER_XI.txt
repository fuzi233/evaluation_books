The scent of freshly brewed coffee wafted through the air as Durrance stepped off the train and onto the bustling platform of London's Waterloo Station. A month had passed since his return from Egypt, and the city's familiar sounds and smells were a welcome respite from the arid desert landscapes he'd grown accustomed to. As he made his way to his club, a letter awaited him, tucked discreetly into his pocket. It was from Ethne, her handwriting elegant and precise, the black border on the envelope a stark reminder of the somber news it conveyed.

Durrance's thoughts turned somber as he arrived at Hill Street the following afternoon, the sound of the doorbell echoing through the hallway as he entered. Ethne greeted him warmly, her eyes red-rimmed from lack of sleep, but her composure unwavering. They sat together in the drawing room, the soft ticking of the grandfather clock providing a gentle counterpoint to their conversation.

"I didn't write to Wadi Halfa," she began, her voice barely above a whisper. "I thought you'd be on your way home before my letter could reach you. Father passed away in late May." The words hung in the air, heavy with unspoken emotion.

Durrance's expression softened, and he reached out to take her hand in his. "I'm so sorry, Ethne. I had feared as much." Her fingers intertwined with his, a subtle gesture of gratitude.

As they sat in silence for a moment, Ethne's gaze drifted toward the window, her eyes lost in thought. "It's strange, but just before he passed away, Father became... different. He regained some of his old spirit, his old fire." Her voice trailed off, and Durrance sensed a deep sadness beneath her words.

"He called for me, and I stayed with him for a while," she continued, her voice steady. "Then he asked me to leave, and he wanted me to keep the dog with me. He said the dog would tell me when to come back." Her words paused, and Durrance felt a shiver run down his spine as he pictured the scene. "I waited outside the door, listening for the dog's signal. It was a long, cold night, and the darkness seemed to stretch on forever."
