Since Mirdath, My Beautiful One, died and took the very terroir of my life with her, I have been a man subsisting on ash. In her sweet companionship, the world possessed flavor and resonance; our shared days were a masterclass in the delicate culinary art of living, spiced with a private, unspoken dialect of glances and shared meals. Without her, I have suffered an anguish so utterly devoid of sensory warmth that it stuns me. The world has become a utilitarian vessel, its sounds reduced to the sterile, commercialized din of a society that consumes without tasting, and speaks without meaning. 

Yet, I have taken up my pen again. Of late, a wondrous hope has fermented within me, slowly bubbling into consciousness. In my sleep, I have waked into the deep, interwoven future of this world, experiencing a sweeping, circular timeline where mythic occurrences fold seamlessly into the everyday domestic routines of a strange, dark age. I have visited the distant womb of Time where she and I shall reunite—breaking asunder in the bitter tang of pain, only to blend once more in a glad and mighty wonder.

To me, it was not as if I dreamed, but that I waked into the dark future of a dying world. The sun had long since cooled into a dead, untranslatable memory. To look back upon this, our Present Age, from that far vantage was to gaze upon a sensory feast my soul knew was real, yet which appeared to my newly-seeing eyes as a hallowed, golden myth. 

Always, upon waking into that Everlasting Night, I was first greeted by a blurred greyness, smelling faintly of crushed ozone and the subterranean damp of ancient, hydroponic soil. As the dusk cleared, I looked out upon a world of darkness. I woke not to ignorance, but with a sudden, innate fluency in the dominant lexicon of that Time, much as a man wakes in his own bed, instinctively knowing the scent of his own hearth and the subtle acoustic rhythms of his house.

I was a youth, seventeen years grown. In my earliest memory of that future self, I stood in one of the embrasures of the Last Redoubt—a great Pyramid of grey metal, housing the last millions of humanity. As I stood there, breaking my fast on a meticulously prepared wafer of spiced root-bread and honey-steeped lichen—a recipe preserved by the traditional agrarian elders who cultivated the one-thousandth plateau—I was entirely the youth of that age. The nuanced flavors of the sustaining wafer grounded me against the crushing existential weight of the Night Land outside. Yet, beneath the crunch of the bread and the comforting, resonant hum of the Pyramid, I carried the misty, half-remembered cravings of my Present self, and a visceral hunger for the one I knew as Mirdath. 

I looked outward to the North-West through a queer, heavy spy-glass, its cold brass resting against my cheek. I gazed upon a landscape I had studied under the meticulous tutelage of the Pyramid's historians, who sought to find profound meaning in our survival. Through the wide field of my glass, I saw the bright, sulfurous glare of the Red Pit shining upward against the vast chin of the North-West Watcher. "That which hath Watched from the Beginning, and until the opening of the Gateway of Eternity..." The words of Aesworpth, the Ancient Poet, drifted into my mind. Yet, as I recited his verse, I felt the tragic erosion of cultural memory. Aesworpth’s poetry was composed in a dying language; the very words for "sunlight" and "breeze" had become hollow, untranslatable phonetic shells in our defensive lexicon. In that moment, the vivid, golden sunlight of our Present Age flashed in my soul, and I was stunned by the absolute impossibility of translating that warmth to a people who knew only the chill of the Night Land.

I must make it clear how fully these two lives were interwoven. He, of that far date, was infused with the sensory memories of my present life, confounding the men of learning who viewed existence solely as utilitarian survival. Meanwhile, I, of this present time, was struck by the sudden, physical hunger for My Beautiful One—a craving so sharp it flew across the eons, turning a memory-dream into the agonizing reality of starvation. I realized how deeply I lacked her, and from that moment, I went about my days listening. I listened for the specific acoustic signature of her voice, a sonic environment essential to my psychological well-being.

Seeking solace from this temporal dissonance, I turned my attention back to the inconceivable mystery of the Night Land. The black monstrosity held at bay by our refuge was a constant, mythic presence, yet watching it had become an everyday routine for old and young alike. 

To the right of the Red Pit lay the Vale of Red Fire, reeking in my imagination of charred iron and boiling stone. Beyond it stretched the absolute blackness, cut only by the cold, sterile light of the Plain of Blue Fire. On the borders of the Unknown Lands sat the Black Hills, crowned by the Seven Lights. They neither twinkled nor faltered; they were an oppressive, silent constant. Down in the Great Library, our meticulous historians maintained the crumbling records of those who had ventured into that dark, documenting the tragic loss of both life and the native dialects of the explorers who never returned.

How shall I convey the sheer scale of this reality? Our histories dealt not in mere thousands of years, but millions. The lore of the old earth, of a time when the sun still warmed the skin, was dismissed by the dominant authorities as myth, an untranslatable subjective experience. Yet, I remembered my nurse of that future age swaying me as a child, crooning impossible lullabies about a golden orb in the sky. Her dialect was rough, thick with the agricultural slang of the lower-level terrace farmers, but her songs held the fading embers of our lost cultural memory.

To the North stood the House of Silence upon a low hill. In a world where the sonic environment was the critical determinant of our sanity, this House was accounted the greatest danger of all. It emitted no sound—not a whisper, not a reverberation that our most sensitive distance-microphones could capture. This predatory silence was a terrifying void, an acoustic death that threatened to swallow the comforting, life-giving thrum of our home. Winding past it was the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, a path said to be born of healthy, rhythmic human toil long ages past, though its true origins were lost beneath a thousand contrary texts and forgotten lexicons.

I stepped away from the embrasure and walked upon the central traveling-roadway of the one-thousandth plateau, a space spanning a mile across and sitting six miles above the dark plain. The quietness of the Sleeping-Time was a deeply soothing acoustic ecology, underscored by the continuous, harmonious vibration of the Earth-Current. Reaching the South-Eastern wall, I looked through The Great Embrasure toward the Three Silver-fire Holes shining before the Thing That Nods. Nearby rose the squat, lumbering bulk of the South-East Watcher, illuminated by Torches that cast long, flickering shadows.

From the lightless East came a sudden, dreadful sound—the Great Laughter. Deep as thunder, it was an industrial, mechanized cacophony that rolled across the dark. I felt it as a violent disruption, a blast of noise pollution that assaulted the subtle power dynamics of our inner harmony. We called its source "The Country Whence Comes The Great Laughter," and each time I heard it, the discordant roar made me keenly aware of our fragile insignificance.

When the Laughter finally faded, restoring the delicate acoustic balance, I turned my spy-glass upon the Giants' Pit, South of the Giants' Kilns. Here, a grotesque mockery of a hearth played out. The fitful, red light of the Kilns flared like the ovens of an infernal kitchen, casting wavering shadows over the raw, crawling forms of the giants as they tended their monstrous roast. The scent of that place, even imagined, was a foul corruption of sustenance. Behind the Pit stood The Headland From Which Strange Things Peer, a vast black brow where unnamable shapes crept forward into the Kilns' light, only to dart back into the shadows.

Scanning the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, I caught sight of a lone, cloaked figure. Shrouded and looking neither right nor left, the Silent One moved with a terrifyingly muted footfall. I tracked the road as it swept South-East, illuminated briefly by the Silver-fire Holes, past the Dark Palace, and finally Westward, behind the living hill of watchfulness known as The Watcher Of The South. 

This vast, hunched monster brooded over the pale radiance of the Glowing Dome. The Monstruwacans—our dedicated acoustic and vibrational ecologists—had recorded its arrival a million years gone. They were the ones who listened to the Great Forces, meticulously charting the tectonic grumbles and ultrasonic whispers of the Beasts. The Monstruwacans understood the subtle power embedded in their work; to name and record the movements of a monster was to wield a linguistic defense against it. They noted how the Watcher had crept forward until the Glowing Dome rose from the earth to block its path, proving that there were still profound, unknown forces of preservation at work in the sonic and physical terroir of the Night Lands.

Before I conclude this portion of my history, I must speak of the Days of the Darkening. The lore of that era, passed down like an ancient, nearly forgotten recipe, spoke of olden sciences that disturbed the Outward Powers, rupturing the barrier of life and allowing Ab-human creatures and grotesque Forces to materialize. As the world succumbed to lawlessness, degeneracy, and the death of its original languages, the remaining sound millions banded together. 

They built the Last Redoubt. Its one thousand three hundred and twenty floors rose nearly seven miles high, driven by the rhythmic, agricultural heartbeat of humanity. Here, we established a new domesticity, substituting the dead sun with the hum of the Earth-Current and the meticulously tended subterranean fields. Through the forgotten centuries, monsters laid siege to our metal mountain, glutting themselves on those who dared explore the dark, their untranslatable cries swallowed by the abyss. And as the Eternal Night lengthened, fresh monstrosities were drawn to our solitary peak of life—infernal sharks circling the last great hearth of mankind. Thus has it ever been, a sweeping cycle of terror and domestic endurance, vibrating through the dark.
