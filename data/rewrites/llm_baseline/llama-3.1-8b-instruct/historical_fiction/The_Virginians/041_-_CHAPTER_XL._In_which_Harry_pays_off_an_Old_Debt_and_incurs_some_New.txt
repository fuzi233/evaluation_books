As the autumn air whispered its arrival, our friends at Tunbridge began to feel the familiar pangs of restlessness, their spirits yearning for the vibrant city of Bath. Madame de Bernstein, with its promise of lively gatherings and stimulating company, beckoned like a siren. She would depart after paying a few visits to her countryside friends, and Harry, though less than enthusiastic, promised to accompany Lady Maria and the chaplain to Castlewood. The route took them past Oakhurst village, a place where memories lingered, and the warm hospitality of its residents still lingered in Harry's mind. Maria's effusive remarks about the village's young ladies and their admiring glances toward him only served to deepen his discontent. 

"I'll not trouble ourselves to call on them," Mr. Warrington declared, a hint of irritation creeping into his voice, as they rode past the hospitable house where he had once been so warmly received. Lady Maria's disapproval of Oakhurst's inhabitants weighed heavily on Harry's spirits that evening at the inn where they stopped, a few miles further along the road. Her attempts to engage him in conversation fell flat, her tears, summoned at will, failing to elicit even the faintest glimmer of sympathy from Mr. Warrington. His curt responses left her visibly crestfallen, and, eventually, she retired to her chamber, the chaplain, in an unspoken agreement, remaining behind to keep Harry company. The air was heavy with tension, the only sound the soft crackle of the fire.

As the night wore on, the inn's landlady and her maid attended to Lady Maria's needs, while Harry excused himself, slipping out into the moonlit night. The darkness seemed to swallow him whole, offering a respite from the stifling atmosphere within. It was then that the question of his obligations to Madame Bernstein began to weigh heavily on his mind, the memory of a long-forgotten promise stirring, like a forgotten melody, in the recesses of his consciousness. 

Back at the inn, the chaplain sat alone, puffing on his pipe, as Mrs. Betty entered with Lady Maria's bottle of smelling salts. "Where has Mr. Warrington gone?" she inquired, but Sampson merely shook his head, his eyes fixed on the flames dancing in the hearth.
