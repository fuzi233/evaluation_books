As Deronda carefully arranged the fine gray flannel shirts and the dressing-gown, reminiscent of a Franciscan's simple garb, his mind wandered to Mordecai's reactions. He imagined the way his friend's eyes would light up at the sight of these humble, yet dignified, articles, and how Mirah's joy would shine through in her gaze. Deronda's pleasure lay not only in pleasing Mordecai, but also in seeing the world through Mirah's eyes, and basking in the warmth of her fervent affection. Yet, as he worked, a nagging doubt crept in – was he not projecting his own sentiments onto Mordecai, overlooking the complexities of his character?

The Meyricks' influence weighed heavily on Deronda's mind, and he knew that any display of sentimentality towards Mordecai would only serve to fuel their skepticism. He recalled the Meyricks' dinner table conversations, where they often spoke of life's grand tapestry, and how the intricate threads of fate wove together the lives of individuals, imbuing them with a deeper meaning. Deronda wished he could share in that reverent perspective, but his own heart remained torn between the rational and the emotional.

That evening, as the fire crackled in the hearth, Mrs. Meyrick called the family to gather around, her eyes aglow with excitement. "Kate, put out the candles, and let us sit together," she said, her voice filled with a quiet urgency. "Hans, dear, stop teasing the girls with your poems, and join us. I have something remarkable to share."

As Kate snuffed out the candles, the room grew darker, and the firelight danced across the faces of the children. Hans, still chuckling to himself, pushed his chair closer to the fender, settling in for the tale. "I've known you were hiding something, Ma," he said, grinning mischievously.

Mrs. Meyrick smiled, her eyes sparkling. "Very well, Hans. If you're so perceptive, perhaps you already know that Mirah's brother has been found."

The room fell silent, except for the crackling of the fire. Hans's expression darkened, and he muttered under his breath. "Confound it!" The girls exchanged nervous glances, and Amy asked, "Is her mother alive?"

"No," Mrs. Meyrick replied gently. "She passed away."
