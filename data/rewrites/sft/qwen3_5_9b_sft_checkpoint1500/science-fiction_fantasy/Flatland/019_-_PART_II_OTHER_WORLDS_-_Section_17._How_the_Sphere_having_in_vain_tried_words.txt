**Section 17.** **How the Sphere, having in vain tried words,** **resorted to deeds**

All argument proved futile. I braced my sharpest corner against the intruder, driving it home with a ferocity capable of shattering any ordinary circle. Yet, beneath my assault, I sensed him not breaking, but dissolving—slowly, inexorably sliding out of my grasp, unmoored from right and left, slipping away from the fabric of existence itself until he vanished into a void. A sudden, absolute blankness filled the space where he stood. And yet, the voice persisted, echoing from nowhere and everywhere.

"SPEAKER OF THE PLANE," the voice boomed, resonating with a terrible authority. "Why do you stubbornly reject the light of reason? I approached you expecting a mind of logic, a scholar of mathematics worthy to carry forward the Gospel of the Third Dimension—a message I am permitted to speak but once in a millennium. Yet here I stand, baffled. Words fail. Behold, then, the power of action. Listen closely."

"I have already declared," the voice continued, its cadence shifting from mockery to demonstration, "that from my vantage point in true Space, I perceive the interior of every object you deem sealed. Look to the cabinet beside you. To your eyes, it is a box containing money and ledgers, hidden behind solid wood. But to me, it is transparent. I see the stacks of coin and the slates of account lying bare within. I saw you lock it an hour past, knowing you hold the key. Yet I step *through* the barrier of the doors without them opening. Now, I am within, grasping the ledger. Now, I ascend."

With terrifying speed, I lunged at the cabinet, flinging the door wide. My hands found air where the paper should lie. One of the tablets had been stolen. Just then, the Stranger materialized in the farthest corner of the chamber, the missing document materializing simultaneously onto the floorboards. I snatched it up. There could be no denial—it was the stolen page.

A cry of pure horror tore from my throat. Was I mad? Did I sleepwalk through a nightmare? The Stranger, however, spoke calmly. "Observe, and let logic take root. What you call solids possess no depth, merely surface. Your so-called Space is but a vast, flat sheet. I dwell in the realm above, gazing down upon the hollows of your world. You, too, might escape this plane if you possessed the will. A mere shift upward, a single breath of vertical motion, and you would see all that I see. As I rise, the world shrinks beneath me, revealing neighbors in distant homes, the inner sanctums of theaters, the private studies of circles—all laid bare."

"And for final proof," the voice cooed, descending rapidly, "permit me a gentle nudge in your midsection. It shall cause no lasting harm, though a fleeting sting remains. Consider it the price of enlightenment."

Before I could protest, a jolt of electric fire shot through my gut, followed immediately by a laugh that sounded demonic, issuing from within my own belly. Then, as swiftly as it arrived, the agony faded, leaving only a dull, throbbing ache. The Stranger reappeared, growing larger with every second. "See?" he said, his form filling the room. "Hurt you little enough? If this does not persuade you, nothing will. What is your verdict?"

My resolve hardened into iron. To live under the whimsical tyranny of a magician who could probe one's entrails was intolerable. I needed to trap him, to pin him against the wall until rescue arrived.

Again, I drove my corner into him, screaming for aid, alerting the entire household to the intrusion. At the moment of impact, I believed he had slipped beneath the plane, struggling to regain his footing. He froze. Hearing the approach of help, I pressed my attack with renewed vigor, my shouts ringing out in the empty hall.

Then, a convulsion racked the Sphere. *"He must not be allowed to free himself,"* I heard the internal thought of the intruder, *"unless he accepts the truth, I must employ the ultimate sanction of civilization."* His voice rose, urgent and commanding. "Listen! No outsider must witness this revelation. Send your wife back immediately, before she crosses the threshold! The Gospel of the Third Dimension must not be ruined by mortal interference. Not after a thousand years of waiting! Hear her steps. Retreat! Flee from me, or I shall drag you away—to a dimension unknown, where laws as you know them do not apply!"

"Fool! Lunatic! Anomaly!" I roared, "Never again shall I let thee loose! Thou shalt answer for thy deceptions!"

"Hark! Has it come to this?" the Stranger thundered. "Then accept your destiny. Depart from your plane. Once... twice... thrice! 'Tis done!"
