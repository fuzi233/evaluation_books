As Catherine rocked her nearly three-month-old son Gerard to sleep, a messenger burst into the cozy cottage, hot from the village of Tergou. Catherine's eyes narrowed as she listened to the man's words. "Tell them I'm busy," she said firmly, "and I won't be leaving just yet." The messenger hastily departed, and Catherine turned to Margaret, her daughter-in-law, with a look of resolve.

"Child, I must get back to work," she said, "those women are in an uproar, and I've been summoned three times already. If I don't return tomorrow, Eli will come himself and fetch me, grumbling about a flea in his ear." Catherine recounted her past experiences with infants and instructed Margaret on how to handle each potential crisis, pressing a few coins into her hands. Margaret politely declined, and Catherine's expression darkened.

"Come, out with it," Catherine said, her voice firm. "Can you really bear the thought of parting ways with me?" Margaret hesitated, and Catherine's gaze softened. "Oh, girl, I've seen it before – the ache of separation. When I was your age, we thought we'd live forever." Margaret looked down, and Catherine continued, "But now, I'll have to leave. Tell Cornelis to offer me a warm welcome, just like last time." Margaret's cheeks flushed, and Catherine's eyes sparkled mischievously.

As Margaret prepared to leave, Catherine handed her a small, fragrant pouch filled with herbs and spices. "For the soup, dear one. Add a pinch of thyme and rosemary, and it'll be just like my mother used to make." Margaret's eyes welled up, and the two women shared a tender moment before Catherine departed.

When Catherine left, Gerard began to cry, and Margaret searched his tiny body for the source of his distress. Her fingers brushed against a small, golden coin lodged between his clothes and skin. She carefully removed it, wrapping the angel in a piece of soft cloth and placing it at the bottom of her sewing box. "There, little one," she whispered, "granny may be stubborn, but sometimes she's also wise."
