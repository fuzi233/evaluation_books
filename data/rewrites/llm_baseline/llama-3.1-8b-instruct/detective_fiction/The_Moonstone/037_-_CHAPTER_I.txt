As I stood at the threshold of my desert camp, the scorching sun beating down upon my shoulders, I gazed out at the endless dunes stretching before me like a canvas of golden sand. It was spring of the year eighteen hundred and forty-nine, and I had altered my travel plans, much to the dismay of my lawyer and banker back in London. The change necessitated sending one of my servants to retrieve my letters and remittances from the English consul in a distant city, a place I had initially planned to visit but now intended to bypass.

The servant, tasked with this errand, had been delayed by unforeseen circumstances, and I had been forced to wait with my people for a week, camping on the unforgiving borders of the desert. The sun beat relentlessly down, baking the earth beneath our feet, as we anxiously awaited the return of my emissary. Finally, after seven long days, he appeared at the entrance of my tent, the letters and money in hand.

"I'm afraid I bring you bad news, sir," he said, his voice laced with a hint of sorrow, as he handed me a letter adorned with a mourning border. The handwriting on the envelope belonged to my trusted advisor, Mr. Bruff.

The letter I held in my trembling hands was like a dagger to my heart, piercing through the layers of dust and sand that had accumulated within me. It was a letter that spoke of loss, of a void left by the passing of a loved one – my father. His legacy had left behind a vast fortune, one that now rested in my hands, along with the weight of responsibility that came with it. Mr. Bruff implored me to hasten my return to England, to claim what was rightfully mine.

As the night fell, casting a dark veil over the desert landscape, I knew I had no choice but to heed his call. By dawn's early light, I set off towards the east, leaving behind the unforgiving expanse of sand and rock, bound for the land of my birth, where memories of Rachel lingered, like the sweet scent of cardamom carried on the breeze.

The image of myself, as painted by my old friend Betteredge, was, I confess, somewhat exaggerated. He had taken one of his mistress's wry remarks about my foreign education and woven it into a tapestry of reality, embellishing the French, German, and Italian influences that had shaped me, though in truth, they were mere figments
