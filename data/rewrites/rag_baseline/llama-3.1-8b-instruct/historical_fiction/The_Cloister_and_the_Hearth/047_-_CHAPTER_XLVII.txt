In the warm glow of the afternoon sun, Catherine's kitchen bustled with activity, the scent of freshly baked bread wafting through the air. Denys, her trusted companion, sat across from her, his eyes fixed intently on the task at hand – searching for Margaret, his friend and Catherine's daughter, who had vanished without a word. As they sipped their tea, the tension between them was palpable, the unspoken words hanging like the steam rising from the cups.

"It's not for me to speak ill of them," Denys said, his voice low and measured, "but Gerard views them as nothing more than acquaintances in this matter. As his comrade, it's a soldier's code not to reveal anything to the enemy – only lies." Catherine's sigh was almost imperceptible, but it spoke volumes of her frustration.

The tale of Margaret's departure had left them both reeling, their minds racing with possibilities. The thought of their friend, Gerard, returning soon, only to find Margaret gone, was a prospect too terrible to contemplate. Denys, however, remained resolute, convinced that he must search for her, no matter the cost. "If Gerard returns next week, and Margaret's nowhere to be found, what kind of figure will he cut?" he asked, his voice laced with a hint of desperation.

Catherine's expression softened, her feelings for Margaret suddenly shifting from annoyance to compassion. She was just as eager to find her daughter, to welcome her back before Gerard's return. Yet, she couldn't agree with Denys that searching the entire neighborhood was the best course of action. "She must have told someone where she was going," she reasoned. "They're not people to flee the country; they owe nothing in Sevenbergen, and dear Denys, you can't hunt all of Holland for her."

Denys's response was grim, his jaw set in determination. "Can I not?" he repeated. After a moment's contemplation, he proposed a plan – Catherine would stay behind, keeping watch and listening for any news, while he scoured every town in Holland for Margaret. "But she won't be far," he said confidently. "Three people don't travel as swiftly or far as one."
