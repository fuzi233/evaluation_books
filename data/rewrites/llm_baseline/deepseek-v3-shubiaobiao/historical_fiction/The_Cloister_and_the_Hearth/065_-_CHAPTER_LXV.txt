Two days after the events I have recounted, Gerard found himself wandering through one of Rome’s grimier streets when a sudden thunderstorm forced him to take shelter in a dim, low-ceilinged tavern. The rain drummed heavily against the roof as he called for wine, and with each cup, his thoughts grew muddled. Eventually, he slumped forward, his head resting on his hands, drifting in and out of a dull stupor.

As the storm raged outside, the tavern filled with rough voices and coarse laughter, rousing him slightly. Through the haze, he became aware of two men nearby, speaking in hushed tones. One was a pardoner, his robes marking him as a seller of indulgences. The other, dressed plainly but with a furtive air, kept his hat pulled low, obscuring his face—a man who clearly preferred shadows to scrutiny.

They were negotiating a transaction, not of goods, but of sin. The pardoner recited prices from a worn parchment, a grim ledger of transgressions and their corresponding fees. Crimes varied in cost, some surprisingly affordable, others steep—though murder, curiously, was priced lower than lesser offenses that might shock a moral man. The haggling concluded with exchanged coin and murmured assurances, the bargain struck before the sin itself had even been committed.

Gerard, still slumped at the table, caught only fragments of their exchange. He lacked the will to intervene, his mind clouded by grief and wine. When the two men parted ways, he lifted his head just enough to glare at the retreating figure of the buyer—a would-be assassin, now absolved in advance.

*If Margaret were alive,* he thought bitterly, *I’d throttle you where you stand.* But she was gone, and with her, his last tether to outrage. The world could rot for all he cared.

Later, as he staggered home, he noticed a figure in a slouched hat pacing briskly across the street. The same man from the tavern, perhaps? It hardly mattered. Gerard’s heart was too numb to kindle suspicion or fear. He passed by without a second glance, the weight of his sorrow drowning all else.
