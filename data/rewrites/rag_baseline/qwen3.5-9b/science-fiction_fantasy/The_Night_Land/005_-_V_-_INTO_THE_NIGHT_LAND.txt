The destruction of the Ten-Thousand had settled over the world like a final, suffocating ash. With the fresh, chilling confirmation of the Terror of the Night Land, the concept of succor evaporated. Those youths walking the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk were already too far gone; they existed in a realm beyond our reach.

We considered signaling them. We imagined using the Home-Call, that great Voice emanating from the Machine atop the sealed base of the Mighty Pyramid. But we could not. To activate it would be to announce to the Monsters of the Night Land that survivors were straggling from the Pyramid. We dared not gamble whether the Evil Forces possessed the knowledge or the ignorance of our presence. In truth, no one could truly know the scope of their cognition.

Still, a strange, quiet influence permeated the land. Our instruments recorded it, though they struggled to quantify its nature. We believed it originated from the House of Silence, situated on a low hill north of the Great Road. Some Monstruwacans feared this influence targeted the youths specifically, but certainty was impossible. We could only wait and watch.

At this juncture, the youths approached the section of the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk where it curved sharply northward. They were nearing the grim, horrid House.

Suddenly, the influence intensified. My senses confirmed its origin lay within the House, though proof remained elusive. I conveyed this conviction to the Master Monstruwacan. He trusted my judgment and shared my intuition that a secret power radiated from the House of Silence.

Whispers circulated of sending the Home-Call to warn the youths of our fear and urge them to return safely. This was an error, rejected firmly by the Master. It was unfit to endanger their souls unless we were certain they were doomed without intervention. The Home-Call was a voice that spanned the world; its activation would instantly reveal that others remained outside the Great Redoubt. In those ages, it served only when great flying-ships patrolled the skies.

Day and night passed. Multitudes peered into the Night Land, watching the youths approach their fate. Panic, rumor, and foolish kindness filled the air, yet no courage emerged to launch a rescue mission—a lack of action unsurprising given the circumstances.

The land itself seemed awake, unquiet, vibrating with a sense of impending doom and horrid vigilance. Low roars traversed the terrain. If I had omitted this earlier, it stands as a failure of my narration; the difficulty of my task is immense, requiring patience and courage to recount what I witnessed.

During this day and night, the youths slept not, ate only once according to observers through the Great Spy-Glass, and hastened relentlessly north along the Great Dismal Road. Their pace was woeful, unsustainable, suggesting they would soon collapse or kill themselves through sheer exertion.

This behavior confirmed our fears: they were under a spell from the distant House. A Monstruwacan reported a sudden, mighty influx of influence. Simultaneously, through the Spy-Glass, I saw the youths break from the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, sprinting with desperate speed toward the House of Silence.

The Master Monstruwacan hesitated not. He unleashed the Home-Call across the world, reaching even the doomed youths unaware of the terror driving them. Immediately, he transmitted a message via the natural eye: a warning that they were being drawn to destruction by a force from within the House. He urged them to summon the strength of their spirits to battle for their souls. If victory over the drawing force was impossible, he commanded them to slay themselves quickly rather than enter the House and face utter annihilation.

Silence fell upon the Pyramid, heavy with the bellowing of the Home-Call. Millions understood the Master's plea for the youths' souls. Unknowingly, a counter-force surged from the Mighty Pyramid, born of the prayers and wills of countless millions.

I heard this counter-force clearly, a beat that swelled the world's aether into a wave of supplication, stunning my spirit with its magnitude. It seemed a vast spiritual noise filled the night. Trembling, I looked through the Spy-Glass. The youths stopped their frantic run, gathering into a confused crowd, as if waking abruptly from sleep to find themselves in a strange place.

Then, a great roar erupted from the million eyes in the embrasures—from nearly five hundred thousand vantage points. The shouting rose like a triumphant wind. Yet, it was premature. The counter-force, forged from the intensity of so many wills, fractured. The Evil Force from the House reasserted dominance, drawing the youths onward. They ignored their salvation, turning once more to their flight.

Shaken silence gripped the Pyramid, quickly replaced by lamentation and horror. But a new wonder occurred. Before the youths arose billows of mist—pure white fire, shining with a chilling brilliance that offered no warmth.

This mist of cold fire blocked their path. We knew a Sweet Power of Goodness was fighting for their souls, striving to shield humanity from forces of evil and destruction. All millions witnessed it, some with clarity, many with doubt, yet all spiritually heightened beyond the norm of this age.

None possessed the Night-Hearing to detect a soul speaking across the globe, though some had been gifted such ability in former times, as I had been.

A Monstruwacan reported that the influence ceased affecting our instruments. We knew the force from the House of Silence was severed from us and the youths. A mighty power was undoubtedly fighting for their salvation.

The peoples fell silent, save for an undercurrent of wonder. Hope and fear mingled as everyone sensed the youths might yet survive.

While the youths wavered, visible through the Spy-Glass and felt in my soul, the Master Monstruwacan sounded the Home-Call again. He implored them, for the sake of their souls and their mothers' love, to return homeward while the protective power still shielded their sanity.

Some looked toward the Pyramid, reading the message. But moments later, they turned. They obeyed a leader they followed blindly: Aschoff, a great athlete of the Nine-Hundredth-City. Driven by boldness and bravery, Aschoff unwittingly destroyed their chances. He leaped into the billows of bright, shining fire—the barrier against destruction—and vanished into it.

Immediately, the fire sank, faded, and became nothingness. Aschoff resumed his run toward the House of Silence, and all who followed him did so faithfully, ceasing no longer.

They reached the low hill bearing the horrid House and ascended swiftly. There were two hundred and fifty of them: wholesome-hearted, innocent, save for a natural waywardness of spirit.

They entered the great open doorway—"open since the Beginning"—through which the cold, steadfast light and inscrutable silence of Evil had created a palpable, eternal quiet. Uncased windows emitted this silence and light, the utter desolation of unholy emptiness.

Aschoff ran through the great doorway of silence, followed by the rest. They never emerged, nor were they seen by any human again.

Mothers and fathers watched from the Night Land, witnessing the tragedy. The people fell silent. Some hoped the youths would return, but deep down, they knew the young men had gone to destruction. There was something in the night that spoke horror to every soul, accompanied by a sudden, total quietude.

To me, who possessed the Night-Hearing, came a great fear—not of sound, but of whispers entering my spirit from the night's quietness: the agony of those young men. Yet, no sound reached the hearing of the soul, then or in the years to come. Had they passed into a Silence the heart cannot conceive?

The strange quiet filling the land, brooding within the darkness, was more horrific than any roar that had preceded it. It was a relief to hear the far-echoing thunder of the Great Laughter, the whining from the Silver-fire Holes near the Thing that Nods, the bay of hounds, or the roar of giants. These dreadful sounds could not offend me as this silence did. One can judge the dread of this quiet by its capacity to overwhelm.

No one now believed anyone could succor the Peoples of the Lesser Redoubt. No one knew where it stood. Thus, it was plain they must suffer and die alone, unhelped. A sad, dreadful thought. Yet, those within the Great Pyramid had already endured sorrow and calamity from previous attempts to aid them. Only failure, the grief of mothers, and the loneliness of wives remained. Now, this dread horror extended to us regarding the lost youths.

The sure knowledge that we could offer no help to the Lesser Redoubt weighed heavily on my heart. Foolishly, I had harbored vague hopes of secretly expediting into the Night to discover that Lesser Pyramid and rescue the thousands. Above all, I dreamed of a sweet moment: stepping out of the night and mystery, putting forth my arms to Naani, declaring, "I am That One." Knowing that she, my love from a bygone eternity, would recognize me instantly, call out, and return to me in that age as she had in this.

To contemplate that this would never happen—that in that very moment of thought, she might be suffering horror under a foul monster—felt like madness. Nearly, I could seize the Diskos and rush unprepared into the evil and terror of the Night Land to find her or sacrifice my life in the attempt.
