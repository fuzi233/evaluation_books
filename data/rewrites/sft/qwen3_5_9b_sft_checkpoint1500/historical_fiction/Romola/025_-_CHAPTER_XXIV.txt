Within the Great Cathedral.

As Baldassarre forced his way back, his wrists bound tight and a heavy hemp cord looped around his neck and torso, pushing aside the heavy curtains, he froze. The air inside the Duomo struck him with the shock of a physical blow. He had expected the hollow, silent grandeur he knew so well—a vast nave stripped of life save for cold, stone idols and flickering tapers, where the faint murmur of a monotonous chant drifted from the distant choir. That was how churches appeared to a man who sought them only for shelter, not for worship.

Instead, he found a sea of humanity. Hundreds, perhaps thousands, filled the space from the nave to the choir. They were of every rank: robed magistrates and ladies of noble birth sat alongside rough-hewn artisans and peasants in coarse wool. All were turned upward, their faces illuminated by a breathless, hushed silence directed toward the pulpit set at the angle where the aisle met the choir.

Upon the raised platform stood a Dominican friar. He possessed features of stark intensity and hair the color of midnight. In his hand, he held a wooden crucifix, moving it with a rhythmic, commanding cadence as he preached.

For the first few minutes, Baldassarre took no note of the man's words. Though his entry had been silent, several pairs of eyes near the threshold had turned sharply toward him, fixed with suspicion. Yet, the rope around his neck marked him clearly as an escaped prisoner. By custom, the church was a sanctuary; his advanced age and the wild, ragged misery etched onto his face invited pity rather than fear. As he stood perfectly still, his gaze drifting aimlessly from the vast assembly to the worn stones beneath his feet, the observers lost interest, returning their attention to the sermon that commanded the room.

Among those eyes that had initially scanned him were Romola's. She had entered late through a side door, positioned so that she held a direct line of sight to the main entrance. She had watched Baldassarre with a long, piercing attention. Something about the grey hairs framing his temples, combined with the distinctive marks of suffering carved into his face and the constriction of the cord, struck a deep chord within her. Her entire life had cultivated a sensitivity toward the sorrows of the aged, and this man invoked it instantly. She fancied that their gazes had locked briefly in that initial moment of recognition, but Baldassarre did not truly see her. His awareness of her presence was merely a fleeting flash, a spark instantly consumed by the ferocious turmoil of emotions ignited by his recent encounter with Tito.

Images from a fractured past surged through him like delirious visions, twisted by a gnawing thirst and sheer anguish. No coherent thought for the future could take root amidst this internal storm. The closest approximation to reflection was a bitter realization of his own enfeeblement, coupled with a vague, sweeping resolve toward total distrust. Suddenly, his body began to vibrate, attuned to a sound that resonated like the thundering echo of his own inner rage. A voice, penetrating his very bones with an accent of triumphant certainty, proclaimed—"The day of vengeance is at hand!"

Baldassarre shuddered and looked up. Too distant to discern the preacher's features clearly, he saw only the silhouette of a man standing with his right arm outstretched, lifting the crucifix high. Yet, he gasped for those threatening words again, as if they were a promise of paradise. A pause followed, deliberate and heavy. Slowly, the preacher lowered his arm. He laid the crucifix gently upon the edge of the pulpit and folded his arms across his chest, gazing out at the multitude as if challenging every single soul to meet his gaze.

"All ye in Florence are my witnesses, for I spoke not in a corner," the friar declared, his voice ringing with authority. "Ye are my witnesses that four years ago, ere signs of war or tribulation, I preached the coming of the scourge. I lifted my voice as a trumpet to the prelates, princes, and people of Italy, declaring: 'The cup of your iniquity is full.' Behold, the thunder of the Lord gathers, destined to shatter the vessel. Your sins, which taste like sweet wine to you, shall be poured out upon you, becoming molten lead.

"And you, O priests, who mock and say, 'Ha, ha! There is no Presence in the sanctuary—the Shechinah is void, the Mercy-seat bare: we may sin behind the veil, and who shall punish us?' To you I said, 'The presence of God shall be revealed in your temple as a consuming fire. Your sacred vestments shall become winding-sheets of flame. For sweet music there shall be shrieks and hissing; for soft couches, thorns; and for the breath of wantons, pestilence. Trust not in your gold nor silver, trust not in your high fortresses. Though walls of iron and castles of adamant, the Most High shall instill terror into your hearts and weakness into your councils, until you are confounded and flee like frightened women. He shall break countless mighty men and raise others in their stead. For God can endure no longer the pollution of His sanctuary; He shall thoroughly purge His Church.'"

"And since Scripture declares that God reveals His purposes to His prophets alone, He hath chosen me, His unworthy servant, making His design manifest to my soul through the living Word and the works of Providence. By the ministry of angels, these truths have been revealed to me in vision. His word possesses me so completely that I am but a branch of the forest when the wind of Heaven blows through it; I cannot remain silent, even if I am to be mocked by the scornful. For four years, I have preached in obedience to the Divine Will. In defiance of ridicule, I have proclaimed three things entrusted to me by the Lord: first, that *in these times God will regenerate His Church*; second, that *before regeneration comes the scourge over all Italy*; and third, that *these things will come quickly*."

"But hypocrites, who cloak their hatred of truth with a veneer of affection, have told me, 'Come now, Frate, abandon your prophecies; it suffices to teach virtue.' To them I answer: 'Yes, you speak in your hearts that God dwells afar off, and His word is no more than parchment written by dead men, dealing not as in ancient days—rebuking nations, punishing oppressors, striking down unholy priests as He smote the sons of Eli. But I cry again in your ears: God is near, not distant. His judgments do not alter. He is the God of Armies; the warriors who march to battle are His ministers, just as storm, fire, and plague. He drives them by the breath of His angels, and they descend upon the chosen land which has forsaken the Covenant. And thou, O Italy, art the chosen land; hath not God placed His sanctuary within thee, and hast thou polluted it? Behold, the ministers of His wrath are upon thee—they stand at thy very doors!'"

Savonarola's voice had climbed to a peak of impassioned force before falling abruptly into silence. He dropped his hands, clasping them quietly before him. This silence, rather than signaling restlessness, acted as a potent spell over the crowd. Through the vast expanse of the cathedral, men and women sat like living statues, faces uplifted, until the voice returned in clear, low tones.

"Yet there is a pause—even as in the days when Jerusalem was destroyed, there was a pause that the children of God might flee. There is a stillness before the storm: lo, darkness hangs overhead, yet not a leaf trembles; the winds are stayed, that the Voice of God's warning may be heard. Hear it now, O Florence, the chosen city in the chosen land! Repent and forsake evil; do justice; love mercy; purge all uncleanness from among you, that the Spirit of Truth and Holiness may fill your souls and breathe through every street and dwelling. Then the pestilence shall not enter, nor the sword pass over you unhurt.

"For the sword hangs suspended from the sky; it quivers, ready to fall! *The Sword of God upon the earth, swift and sudden!* Did I not tell you years ago that I beheld the vision and heard the voice? And behold, it is fulfilled! Is there not a King with his army at your gates? Does not the earth quake beneath the tread of horses and the rolling wheels of swift cannon? Is there not a fierce multitude capable of laying bare the land as with a razor? I tell you, the French King with his host is the minister of God: He shall guide him as a hand guides a sharp sickle, and the joints of the wicked shall melt before him, mown down as stubble. He who flees shall not escape, he who escapes shall not be delivered. And the tyrants who built their thrones from the vices of the multitude, and the unbelieving priests who traffic in souls and fill the sanctuary with fornication, shall be hurled from their soft couches into burning Hell; and the pagans and those who sinned under the old covenant shall stand aloof, crying, 'Lo, these men have brought the stench of a new wickedness into the everlasting fire.'"
