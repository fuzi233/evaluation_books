The land opposite Europe and Africa was never meant for habitation, a fact confirmed by the superstitions of the common folk who claimed the evil dead stalked its daylight hours. Criminals fleeing justice preferred the torture of execution to the fabulous terrors of this zone, where the mountains boiled perpetually and the earth trembled daily. Unseen vapors stole breath, rivers ran poison without changing color, and great cracks opened to swallow water before spitting it back in explosive fury. Boiling mud and molten rock were constants.

Yet amidst this chaos existed sombre forests of unimaginable age, devoid of fire, where the air hung thick with warm steam. To survive here required strength and cunning beyond the ordinary, for these lands were the domain of lizards that grew to monstrous sizes. Some could be fought, but others possessed a hugeness that defied human force. I had witnessed one emerge from a lake, its weight shaking the wet land as it trod. It could have swallowed Phorenice's mammoth whole; even a charging beast would have been harmless against its horny plates and spine-laden ridges. These creatures were swift and sagacious, knowing when to retreat into deep lakes or muddy wallows during volcanic eruptions, immune to the heat that would destroy man.

My new life in this steaming hell was perilous from the outset. Only a fervent trust in the High Gods kept me from succumbing to pure fear. For months, I wore nothing but rotting rags until I was as naked as a European savage. Ague racked my limbs, and I subsisted on tree-grubs and tainted leaves, eating beasts I knew little of, which brought me pain and sickness. Adaptability is man's birthright, however. Eventually, I learned the limits of this existence. I found a great black tree with a hollow core near its roots and established a harbor there. A monstrous lizard once attempted to suck me out like a worm from a log, its sheer weight rocking the sapling. I escaped by divine will, though I knew my weapons could not harm its horny tongue. So, I carved a ladder of footholes up the trunk, resting ten man-heights above the ground, creating a floor and arrow-slits for defense, and hauling sand up to build a hearth.

As my ribs healed from the diet of river fish and lesser turtles, the lack of salad and gruel remained an insurmountable barrier. Worse were the longings that arose as the brute part of my nature settled. I carved a representation of Nais from the ivory of a river-horse's tooth. Though my art was clumsy and the result pitiful, the curve of her neck and the outline of her face made my old love glow anew within me as I sat and stared at it. It did little to satisfy the craving to be with her; instead, it sharpened the desire to know her fate.

Others of the Priests' Clan had mastered the Higher Mysteries here, returning to civilization with reputations for magic that fueled the populace's religious ardor. I could not follow their abstruse studies; my mind belonged to Nais, a lien that refused to be ousted. Her memory was my sole solace. But eventually, longing demanded release. Through an arrow-slit window, I watched a great mountain top whitened with snow, dyed red by the setting sun. Night after night, I remembered my duty to adore the Lord rising behind those same snows, and realized that from this crest, I could see across the continent to the Sacred Mount, where the city lay and where I had buried my love.

I set out on a perilous journey. Heavy mists blinded my path, forcing me to traverse three times the direct distance. Great lizards hunted the swamps, and I was a hundred times within an ace of being devoured, saved only because the High Gods blinded the beasts when I sought cover. Rivers of scalding water roared across my path twice, requiring me to build rafts from black timber to cross safely. The journey was tediously slow, yet time flew on winged feet, for I could bear the strain no longer. Let me but see the place where she lay, feast my eyes upon it, and then I could return to my tree-abode to await the Gods' will.

As I ascended above the tree-line onto higher ground, the air grew chilly. I fashioned buskins and a coat of woven grasses against the keen wind. In the eternal snowfields, frozen into solid banks and bergs of ice, progress was difficult, and the chill numbed my natural strength. Overhead, huge man-eating birds pursued me with forbidding swoops, threatening to pull me down if my instincts deemed me weak. Yet the lure ahead outweighed these difficulties. Though the mountain air induced sickness and panting, I pressed on toward the elusive summit. Time after time, I thought the next spur would reveal the view my soul yearned for, only to find another bank of snow to climb. Finally, I reached the crest and thanked the High Gods for their protection.

Far away, the Sacred Mountain glowed with a ring of pale fires. Although the splendid city at its foot was invisible, I knew its plan and its throne of granite in the square before the royal pyramid—the spot where I had buried Nais. Had Phorenice violated the tomb? I leaned on my spear, warming to the smoke of familiar mountains, consumed by a burning love for the woman. My whole being seemed to stretch out to meet her, leaving no room for anything else. A voice had dimly called me before, but I ignored it; I came for communion with Nais alone.

Then, the voice grew too loud to neglect. I started, gripping my weapon, expecting to see another human for the first time since Tob and his company. The reply came clearly from the snowy slopes. A man approached, old and feeble, his body bent, his hair and beard white as the ground. It was Zaemon. He moved with incredible speed for his years, carrying the glowing Symbol of our Lord the Sun, which lent him holy strength.

We exchanged the sign of the Seven, completing the gesture with due ceremony. Zaemon saluted me as a messenger appointed by the High Council of the Priests before the Ark of the Mysteries, and I bowed humbly.

"In all things I will obey the orders that you put before me," I said.

"Such is your duty, my brother. Return immediately to the Sacred Mountain. If human means can still prevail, you, as the most skilled general Atlantis owns, may yet save the country from final wreck. Phorenice persists in her infamies. The land groans under her heel. She has laid siege to our Sacred Mountain itself, swearing that not one soul shall remain alive in Atlantis who does not bend to her will."

"It is a command, and I obey. But let me ask of another matter intimate to both of us. What of Nais?"

"Nais rests where you left her, untouched. Phorenice knows, through her arts and stolen ancient knowledge, that you still live. She keeps Nais unharmed beneath the granite throne, hoping to use her as a weapon against you someday. Little does she know the sternness of our Priests' creed. Even I, her father, would sacrifice her blithely if her death served Atlantis."

"You go beyond me with your devotion."

The old man leaned forward, his brow glowering. "What!"

"Or has my old blind adherence to ancient dogma been sapped by events? You must buy my full obedience, Zaemon, if you want it. Promise me Nais—and your arts can snatch her—and I will be a true servant to the High Council, dying in the last ditch if needed to carry out order. But let me see Nais given over to the fury of that wanton woman, and I shall have no inward left except to take my vengeance and see Atlantis piled in ruins as her funeral stone."

Zaemon looked at me bitterly. "And you are the man the High Council thought to trust as they would trust one of themselves? Truly, we are in an age of weak men and faithless ones. But, my lord—nay, I must call you brother still—we cannot be too nice in our choosing today. You are the best there is, and we must have you. We little thought you would ask a price for your generalship, having once taken oath on the walls of the Ark of the Mysteries that you would be a servant of the High Council without fee or hope of advancement. But this is the age of broken vows, and you are going no more than trim with the fashion. Indeed, brother, perhaps I should thank you for being no more greedy in your demands."
