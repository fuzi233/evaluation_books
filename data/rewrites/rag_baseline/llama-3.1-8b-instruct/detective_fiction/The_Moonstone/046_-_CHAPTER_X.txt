As I stepped back into Frizinghall, the biting wind whipped my hair into a frenzy, carrying with it the scent of damp earth and decaying leaves. I hastened my pace, anxious to escape the chill and find solace in the warmth of Mr. Candy's house. My stomach growled in protest, reminding me that I had skipped lunch in my haste to prepare for my meeting with Ezra Jennings. The thought of a hot meal wafting from the kitchen drew me to the house like a siren's call.

Upon entering, I spotted Ezra Jennings waiting for me in a small, Spartan room adjacent to the surgery. The space was utilitarian, with walls adorned with faded diagrams of various maladies, their colors muted and uninviting. Shelves lined with dusty medical texts and a skull perched atop a bookcase added to the somber atmosphere. A worn, deal table dominated the center of the room, its surface scarred from years of use. Water splashed from a nearby sink, the sound jarring against the otherwise oppressive silence. I shuddered inwardly, my thoughts straying to the countless stories those instruments had witnessed.

Ezra Jennings rose from his chair, his eyes warm and inviting. "Please, Mr. Blake, do not apologize for the accommodations," he said, his voice low and soothing. "This is the only room in the house where we can ensure uninterrupted conversation." He gestured to the papers spread out before him. "Here are my notes, prepared specifically for our discussion. And these two books may prove useful in our exploration."

I took a seat, my fingers brushing against the cold wood of the chair. Ezra Jennings handed me a pair of large, yellowed sheets, the pages crackling as I unfolded them. One sheet bore scattered notes, while the other was filled with dense writing in red and black ink. My impatience got the better of me, and I pushed the second sheet aside, feeling a twinge of frustration.

"Please, Ezra, have mercy on me," I said, my words tumbling out in a rush. "Tell me what I'm to expect before I try to decipher this."

Ezra Jennings's eyes crinkled at the corners as he smiled, his expression both sad and kind. "Of course, Mr. Blake. If I may ask, do you mind if I pose a few more questions?"

"Not at all," I replied, my curiosity piqued.
