As I rose early, the tantalizing aroma of roasting meat wafted through the air, signaling that breakfast was underway. My attention, however, was riveted on the exquisite hide of the kangaroo, its supple texture and vibrant hues begging to be preserved intact. Once Fritz had readied our expedition gear, I summoned Ernest and Jack to impart some final instructions before our departure.

Alas, they had vanished soon after breakfast, leaving behind only whispers of speculation as to their whereabouts. Their mother surmised they might have ventured out to procure some potatoes, though I suspected otherwise. Upon discovering they'd taken Turk along, my concerns dissipated, albeit with some trepidation, as I entrusted my dear wife with the care of little Franz, buoying her spirits with the promise of our swift return bearing new treasures from the wreck.

Our steady progress across the Jackal River's bridge was interrupted by a sudden, startling apparition – Jack and Ernest bursting forth from a concealed vantage point, their mischievous grins radiating unbridled joy. It was clear they believed they'd secured permission to accompany us to the wreck.

I swiftly dispelled their notion, though I couldn't muster the sternness to chastise their impulsive escapade, especially considering my desire to convey a message to my wife. I instructed them to hasten back home, assuring their mother that our prolonged absence wouldn't leave her in suspense. As they'd already traversed considerable distance, I suggested they collect some salt, which would serve as a token of our appreciation for their efforts.

Before parting ways, I entrusted them with a message: my work on the wreck would necessitate an extended stay, and I requested their mother to endure our prolonged absence for the night. This was a decision I'd initially intended to communicate, but my courage faltered, knowing how fervently she'd object to the idea. With the boys' innocent interference, however, I seized the opportunity to send word, ensuring our night's lodgings would be spent on the wreck.

As I joined Fritz in the tub-boat, he called out to the boys, "Farewell, be cautious!" as we pushed off into the current. The gentle flow swept us out of the bay, and we expertly moored ourselves alongside the wreck. Scrambling up its weathered hull, we found ourselves standing on the remaining deck, whereupon we began laying out our plans.
