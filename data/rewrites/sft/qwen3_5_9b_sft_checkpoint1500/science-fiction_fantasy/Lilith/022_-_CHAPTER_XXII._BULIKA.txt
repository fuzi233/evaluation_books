The disorientation within me was absolute, a swirling void of helpless impatience that drove me forward like a leaf caught in a storm. Suddenly, the silence broke. I stepped directly into the path of a leopardess, yet not the beast itself, but the trail it left—a torrent of crimson blood pouring from its paw. The fluid rushed past my ankles with the force of a mountain stream, carrying a heavy, metallic weight. An instinct, raw and unformed, screamed at me to identify the owner of this gore, urging me to flee. Yet, I stayed close to the sound, tracing the stream upward along the bank, convinced it would lead me to Bulika.

But as I walked, reason cut through the panic. No leopardess, no elephant, no titan of the pre-human world could sustain such a deluge unless every vessel in its body were shattered and it drank endlessly from the earth itself. This could not be blood. I knelt and dipped a finger into the rush. It was cool, clear, and devoid of iron. A soft, murmuring rivulet of water spilled over the grass, unchanneled and wild. Sweet as its song, I dared not drink; I pressed on, eyes fixed on the horizon, listening for the familiar hum of the hot springs that lay beneath the surface. The mere touch of this water on my skin washed away the grime of my journey, restoring my limbs until the encroaching twilight faded the sky.

Then, against the pale, bruising aurora, I saw them: the wall-towers of a city. They stood ancient, seeming older than time itself. I squinted downward, searching for the brook. It was gone. For some time, I had noted its fading cadence, but finally, I had stopped listening. Looking back, I saw the grass where it had flowed bent in permanent curves, dotted with small, glistening pools. Beyond those bends, heading toward the city, there was no trace of it. Near where I stood, the fountain's flow must have paused abruptly.

Surrounding the fortress were gardens, teeming with vegetables I did not recognize. There were no flowers, no animals, no signs of life save for the green. These gardens hugged the massive walls, separated from them by great mounds of gravel and refuse dumped from the battlements.

I approached the nearest gate. It hung ajar, neither locked nor guarded, its hinges rusted in a state of perfect stasis. Through the gap, I peered down a long, ancient street. Utter silence reigned. Was this a graveyard of souls? I turned and fled back the way I came, trudging over dust-heaps and crossing intersecting roads, refusing to return until the city stirred.

*For what purpose am I here?* the question echoed in my mind. *What do I seek?*

*I must see her.* The woman I had breathed life into. I craved no company from her; she had awakened horrors in me, making friendship—and certainly love—an impossibility. Yet, her existence had altered me, forcing me to confront a strange, magnetic influence. I needed to understand the inscrutable nature of her being, to peer into marvels beyond imagination. It was a dangerous curiosity; a man should not court temptation for the sake of knowledge. Furthermore, I had resurrected an evil force teetering on the edge of extinction. I was responsible for whatever mischief she might unleash. I had learned she hated children; perhaps the "Little Ones" were now in peril. I had sought light regarding their fate, and I needed more. I needed to know how to shield them.

At length, a faint commotion rippled through the air. I slipped through the next gate, descending a narrow thoroughfare lined with tall, looming houses. I found a seat on the base of a pillar, topped by a grotesque, bat-like creature. Soon, citizens began to drift past. I addressed one, but he returned only a coarse glare and a harsh word before vanishing into the crowd.

Rising, I navigated street after street, the avenues slowly filling with idlers. As expected, I saw no children. Eventually, near a gateway, a cluster of young men cornered me. Their features reminded me of the bad giants I had known. They circled me, staring, then pushed and shoved, throwing debris at my clothes. I endured the assault, trying to avoid provoking further hostility. I called out to passersby who seemed kindlier, but no one stopped to hear me. My appearance marked me as an outsider; to these people, poverty was a crime, deformity a tax, and sickness a burden. Above all, Princess Bulika's laws, which enforced the subjugation of the poor to enrich the wealthy, were revered.

Desperate, I sprinted away. Only one clumsy follower, a man sitting by the gate devouring bread, pursued me. In his frantic stupidity, he hurled not a stone but a lump of bread. I snatched it, and he dared not follow to retrieve it; outside the walls, everyone was a coward. I ran a few hundred yards, collapsed onto the grass, and devoured the bread. Exhaustion claimed me, and I sank into a deep, restorative sleep under the blazing sun.

Night fell, and I awoke to the silver gaze of the moon. It felt like an old friend, having witnessed my first terrifying night in this strange realm. A cold wind drifted from the gate, carrying a stench of decay, yet it failed to chill me, for the day's warmth lingered in my bones. I crept back into the city, joining the few souls crouching in corners to escape the biting blast.

Walking slowly down a long, narrow street, I saw it: a huge, white shape bounding across the road, flashing briefly in the moonlight before disappearing. Instinct took hold. I turned into the next alley, driven to catch a glimpse again.

It was a lane so narrow it barely admitted a man, leading into a broader avenue. The moment I emerged, I spotted the creature on the opposite side, hidden in the gloom. Behind it, following with the devotion of a hound, was the shadow I had chased. Every few moments, the figure glanced back, ignoring the animal, offering it no command, driving it away not a step. Where the figure crossed a patch of moonlight, I realized with a jolt: he cast no shadow. He was not solid matter, but a flat, superficial illusion, existing in two dimensions. Yet, he was opaque; he blocked vision entirely, rendering anything behind him invisible.

In the shade, he was darker than the darkness itself. In the moonlight, he appeared as if he had drawn his shadow up around his form, leaving no trace beneath his feet. Beside him glided the leopardess, a flash of white against the gray. Her movement dragged a gliding shadow blacker than the night itself over the ground. When they passed from the shadow into the full moonlight, the Shadow deepened into absolute blackness, while the animal flared into blinding radiance.

I stood on the opposite side, my bare feet striking the flat stones. The leopardess never turned her head, never twitched an ear. The Shadow seemed to glance at me, though for a split second, I saw nothing but a sharp, vertical line where a face should have been. In that instant, the wind seized me, blowing straight through my body. I shuddered from head to toe, and my heart hammered against my ribs like a pebble rattling in a child's toy.
