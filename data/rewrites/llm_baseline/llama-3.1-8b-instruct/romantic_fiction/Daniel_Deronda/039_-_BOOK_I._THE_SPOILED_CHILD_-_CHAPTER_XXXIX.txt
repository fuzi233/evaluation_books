In the midst of their tranquil domestic routine, a sudden commotion shook the small house at Chelsea, echoing the momentous events that had transpired during Gwendolen's visit. Yet, for the family group, the pivotal sequence connected to Mirah's public name unfolded two days later. As the clock struck four, the sound of carriage wheels halted before the door, accompanied by the familiar chime that signaled the arrival of a visitor in this quiet neighborhood where the most thrilling announcements often came from the muffin-man's cart. All the girls were at home, and the adjacent rooms merged into one space, accommodating Kate's artistic endeavors and a majestic embroidery piece, crafted by skilled hands, which now stretched elegantly across the front room. Mrs. Meyrick worked diligently on one end, Mab sat in the center, and Amy attended to the other, while Mirah, whose needlework skills were still developing, read aloud to the group from a camp stool. She was bringing the captivating words of Elia's essay, "The Praise of Chimney-Sweeps," to life, and the girls were enchanted by its wit and charm, when the resounding knock and chime interrupted their reverie.

"Oh dear!" exclaimed Mrs. Meyrick, "Could it be Lady Mallinger? Is there a grand carriage outside?"

"No, only a hansom cab," replied Amy, her eyes fixed on the window.

"The Prime Minister, perhaps?" Kate quipped dryly, "Hans says even the greatest man in London can ride in a hansom cab."

"Oh, oh, oh!" Mab squealed excitedly, "What if it's Lord Russell!"

As the girls' bright faces turned towards the door, the maid, flustered, left the parlor entrance ajar, revealing a figure standing before Mrs. Meyrick – tall, imposing, and resplendent in his fine attire, with a commanding presence that seemed to fill the room. The card in his hand confirmed his identity as Julius Klesmer, and the girls' amusement turned to curiosity as they took in his striking features – his flamboyant hair, gold-rimmed spectacles, and an aura of grandeur that seemed to belong to a world beyond their quiet lives.
