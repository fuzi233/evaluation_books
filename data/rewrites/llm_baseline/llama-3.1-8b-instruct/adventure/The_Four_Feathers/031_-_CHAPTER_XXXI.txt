As the sun rose over the sleepy town of Ramelton, casting a warm glow over the thatched roofs and the winding streets, Harry Feversham rode into town on a sturdy steed, his face bronzed by the scorching suns of the Soudan. The intervening years, filled with memories of his time in Egypt and the Continent, had begun to fade like the morning mist that rose from the Lennon River. His thoughts were no longer consumed by the ghosts of what could have been, but by the simple pleasures of the present. He rode swiftly, lost in thought, as the larks sang sweet melodies above him and the landrail's harsh cry echoed through the meadows.

The scent of freshly baked bread wafted from the nearby bakery, enticing Harry's senses and drawing him back to the familiar comforts of home. He stopped his horse in front of the church, its stone walls standing sentinel above the road, and wondered if Ethne still lived in Ramelton. Had old Dermod Eustace survived the passing years, and what kind of welcome would he receive? The questions swirled in his mind like the gentle breeze rustling the leaves of the nearby trees.

Shaking off the nostalgia, Harry urged his horse forward, riding down the incline and up the ascent, surrounded by the phantoms of people and memories that lingered in the air. He passed through a thick grove of trees, a broken fence, and a gateway with no gate, his eyes scanning the landscape for any sign of life. The neglect and decay of the once-grand estate were a stark reminder of the transience of human endeavors. As he rode through the overgrown drive, the sound of his horse's hooves echoing through the silence, Harry felt a pang of melancholy wash over him.

Dismissing the emotions, he tied his horse to a tree and entered the ruined house, calling out into the emptiness. No response came, and he explored the deserted rooms, his footsteps echoing off the walls. In the garden, the uncut grass and tangled flowerbeds told a tale of abandonment and neglect. With a heavy heart, Harry remounted his horse and rode back to the town, his thoughts consumed by the memories of what once was.
