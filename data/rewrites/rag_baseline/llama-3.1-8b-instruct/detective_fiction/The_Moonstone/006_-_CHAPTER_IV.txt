As I sat in my favorite armchair, nestled in the sun-drenched backyard, I found myself lost in thought, the gentle hum of bees a soothing accompaniment to my reverie. My daughter Penelope had long since departed, leaving me to my quiet solitude, and I had grown accustomed to taking my meals in the comfort of my own sitting room. The clinking of dishes in the servants' hall signaled the start of dinner, and I rose from my chair, stretching my limbs in anticipation of the meal to come.

Just as I was settling back into my seat, a commotion erupted outside, as Nancy, our pert kitchen maid, burst into the room, her usually pleasant demeanor marred by a sullen expression. Her eyes flashed with annoyance as she attempted to brush past me, but I caught her by the ear, a gesture that served as both reprimand and affectionate reminder of my approval. "What's amiss, Nancy?" I asked, my voice gentle but firm. "Why have you abandoned your dinner?"

Nancy's response was curt, her words laced with resentment. "Rosanna's late for dinner again," she said, her tone dripping with disdain. "I'm to fetch her in, but I'll have none of it." I recognized the strain of frustration etched on her face and decided to intervene, sensing that a harsher tone might exacerbate the situation. Instead, I chose to walk alongside Nancy, my intention to fetch Rosanna myself, and offer her a gentle warning to be punctual in the future.

As we strolled through the garden, the scent of blooming flowers mingled with the salty sea air, carrying with it the whispers of secrets yet to be revealed. "Where is Rosanna?" I inquired, my voice carried away by the breeze. Nancy's response was a toss of her head, accompanied by a dismissive remark: "At the sands, of course!" Her words hung in the air like the faint tang of salt, leaving me to ponder the mysterious allure of the sea.
