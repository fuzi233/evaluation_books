The moorland stretched out before us, a seemingly endless expanse of dry, rustling plants and mosses that seemed to absorb the last vestiges of the fading sunlight. A gray cloud enveloped the sun, casting an early twilight over the landscape, and the wind whispered secrets from a realm beyond our own, where night reigned supreme. The air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and decaying leaves, a morbid perfume that filled my lungs and chilled my bones.

"Ah, we've arrived at last," croaked the raven, his voice like the crackle of a dying flame. "What a long journey it has been! I could have flown to the heavens and back in half the time, visiting my cousin, the one who never returned to Noah's Ark. Alas, it feels like winter itself has arrived."

"Winter?" I repeated, incredulous. "It seems but a day since we left home."

The raven cocked his head, his black eyes glinting with a knowing light. "That's because we've traveled at such a pace, my friend. You mortals can't defy gravity and let the world spin beneath your feet as we ravens do. But here we are, at my wife's humble abode, the sexton's cottage. She's kind enough to allow me to reside here, and I'm grateful for her generosity."

I scanned the desolate landscape, searching for signs of a church or cemetery, but saw nothing but the endless heath. The raven pointed his beak towards the horizon, and I followed its trajectory with my gaze. As I did, the wind swept across the moor, revealing a vast, unmarked graveyard. Every step, every gust, every whisper of the wind seemed to disturb the rest of the dead. The raven was indeed the sexton of this forsaken place, the lord of all that lay forgotten.

I felt as though I'd traversed the seasons themselves, leaving behind the warmth of spring, summer, and autumn, and arriving in a realm where winter reigned supreme. The day stretched on, containing multiple seasons within its span – winter slumbering through the night, spring awakening at dawn, summer blazing forth at noon, and autumn creeping in with the fading light. As we approached the cottage, the sun plunged below the western horizon, casting the world in an eerie twilight.
