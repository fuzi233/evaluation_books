Since the dawn of creation, when the Gods shaped man from the earth while watching from afar, certain corners of the world have proven unfit for his survival. None are more hostile than the section of Atlantis bordering the savage continents of Europe and Africa. Common folk shun this region, driven by a superstitious dread that the spirits of the wicked dead roam its daylight hours, slaughtering anyone the landscape's overt dangers fail to claim. Consequently, criminals fleeing justice often surrender willingly to these "tormentors" rather than face the area's legendary horrors. While scholars dismiss many tales as myths, a lingering unease makes life here a relentless struggle filled with peril.

Everywhere, the mountains churn; earthquakes are daily occurrences. Invisible, malevolent vapors creep with cunning speed, choking victims before escape is possible. Rivers rise with poison yet retain their color, while great fissures tear the ground open to reveal subterranean fires. Waters rush into these chasms only to erupt with devastating explosions, accompanied by frequent outpourings of boiling mud or molten rock. Despite this chaos, vast, somber forests thrive here, populated by trees of unimaginable antiquity where wildfires are rare. Beneath the canopy lies water, and the air hangs thick with warm, oppressive steam. Living in such constant, humid heat is mortifying to the human frame. Yet, strength and extraordinary cunning are required to survive, for these lands are the domain of lizards—the most enormous and terrifying beasts imaginable.

Countless species inhabit this realm. Some can be fought with a chance of victory, but others possess a hugeness that defies human force. I witnessed one emerge from a lake after feeding, shaking the wet earth with every step. It could swallow Phorenice's mammoth whole; even a charging beast would be powerless against it. Great horny plates armored its head and body, while spines along its back, tail, and limbs tore through the black trees as it moved.

Remarkably, they endure the region's greatest hazard—extreme heat—with ease. Thrown into this nightmare of neighbors, my new existence became perilous and harsh from the start. I barely understood fear until a fervent trust in the Highest Gods and a conviction that my life was under Their protective care held me back. For months, until I grasped the rhythms of this steaming hell, I existed in misery worse than anything Phorenice could imagine. My clothes rotted away in the perpetual damp, leaving me naked like a European savage. Ague racked my limbs, and lacking medicinal herbs, I suffered endlessly. For days, my diet consisted solely of tree-grubs and leaves. When I managed to hunt, ignorance of the beasts' properties often led me to eat poisonous game, bringing fresh sickness.

Yet, as humans adapt, so too did I. Learning the limits of this life, I gathered knowledge of its resources. I found a great black tree with a hollow core and a root-level opening. I took shelter within, until one night a monstrous lizard, its sheer weight rocking the tree like a sapling, attempted to suck me out like a bird picking a worm from a log. Escaping by divine will—I could not have harmed that horny tongue with my weapons—I vowed never to risk such proximity again. Instead, I carved a ladder of footholes up the trunk, ascending ten man-heights to create a sanctuary. I notched the wood for resting platforms and carved arrow-slits for defense, even hauling sand up to build a hearth so I could cook safely above the dangers of the forest floor.

Gradually, I mastered catching large-scaled river fish and smaller turtles, allowing my ribs to heal. However, the lack of varied greens and gruels remained a curse; the local vegetation tasted so strongly of tar that I could never force my palate to accept it. Beyond hunger loomed the threat of giant lizards and other native perils.

As months bled into years, satisfying my base needs, deeper longings arose. Using the ivory of a river-horse's tooth, I attempted to carve a likeness of Nais, as I remembered her. Though my heart poured love into the work and my intent was pure, my artistic skill was dull. The result was always clumsy and pitiful. Yet, seeing a curve here or an outline there reignited my memory of her, making my old love glow within me as I sat and stared at the crude figure. Still, it failed to quell my craving to be with her again, or at least to know her fate.

Other members of the Priests' Clan had settled in these Dangerous Lands, mortifying their flesh to gain intimacy with Higher Mysteries, achieving feats that surpassed mere human learning. Those who eventually returned to civilization carried reputations for magic and miracles, fueling the populace's zeal for the Gods. But my mind, clouded by obsession, could not achieve the clarity needed for such abstract studies. On my voyage home from Yucatan, I had communed with them with growing insight; now, however, my mind belonged to Nais. Her presence was my sole solace, refusing to be displaced.

Finally, my longing could no longer be denied. Through an arrow-slit window, I watched a distant mountain peak capped with eternal snow, dyed red by the setting sun nightly. Night after night, I strained my eyes toward that ruddy light, remembering my duty as a novice priest to adore the Lord rising behind those same snows. Always followed the thought: from that crest, one could see across the continent to the Sacred Mount, where the city lay and where I had buried my love.

Giving way to desperation, I set out on a perilous journey. Heavy mists obscured the lower grounds, rendering my path blind and treacherous. I easily traveled three times the direct distance. Throughout the swamps, giant lizards hunted, and unfamiliar with safe havens, I was a hundred times closer to being spotted and devoured than I cared to admit. Yet, the High Gods seemed to favor me; they blinded the great beasts just as I slipped into cover. Twice, rivers of scalding water boiled across my path, forcing delays while I collected black timber to build rafts for dry passage.

The journey was infinitely tedious, yet after years of waiting, time flew on winged feet. Separation from my love had become unbearable; I merely needed to glimpse the place where she lay, feast my eyes upon it briefly, and then return to my tree-dwelling patience. As I ascended above the tree line onto higher ground glaring down on the world, the air grew chilling. I fashioned boots and a coat of woven grasses to ward off the keen wind. Eventually, reaching the zone of eternal snow, where drifts froze into solid banks and icebergs, progress became a struggle against perilous mazes. The cold numbed me, weakening my natural strength.

Overhead, huge man-eating birds circled with forbidding swoops, occasionally closing in so closely I had to brandish my weapons. Yet, the lure ahead outweighed these dangers. Though the thin mountain air induced sickness and panting, I pressed on with all my speed toward the elusive summit. Time after time, I thought the next push would grant me the view my soul yearned for, only to find another bank of snow or ice blocking the way.

At last, I reached the crest and thanked the Highest Gods for their protection. Far away, the Sacred Mountain appeared, its ring of fires burning pale under the day. Though the splendid city nestled at its foot remained invisible from my vantage point, I knew its layout perfectly. My spirit soared toward the granite throne in the square before the royal pyramid—the spot where I had once buried my love.

Had Phorenice left the tomb intact? Leaning on my spear, I drank in the prospect, warming myself mentally on the smoke of familiar mountains. God! How fiercely my love burned for this woman. My entire being seemed to stretch out to meet her, leaving no room for anything else. A voice seemed to call dimly, but I ignored it. I had come to commune with Nais alone, needing no interruptions.

Then, the voice grew too loud to neglect. Startled from my reverie, I gripped my weapon and demanded, "Who calls?"

The reply echoed clearly from the slopes below. A man emerged over the snows, moving with incredible speed despite his apparent age and frailty. He was old and bent, his hair and beard white as the ground beneath him. I recognized him instantly: Zaemon. Carrying the glowing Symbol of our Lord the Sun, which imbued him with holy strength, he approached rapidly.

He drew near and made the Sign of the Seven. I returned the gesture with due ceremony. Then, saluting me as a messenger appointed by the High Council of the Priests before the Ark of the Mysteries, I bowed humbly.

"In all things I will obey the orders you put before me," I declared.
