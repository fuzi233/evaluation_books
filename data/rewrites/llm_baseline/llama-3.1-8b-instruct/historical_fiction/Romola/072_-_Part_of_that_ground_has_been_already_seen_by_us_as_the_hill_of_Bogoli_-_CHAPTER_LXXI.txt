As the last wisps of April's chill dissipated, Romola ushered Tessa and the children back into their Florence home, the city's anxiety palpable in the air. The publication of Fra Girolamo's trial, or rather, the confessions extracted from him by the sixteen Florentine citizens appointed to interrogate him, had set tongues wagging. The document, sanctioned by the Signoria, had sparked a maelstrom of public skepticism and discontent, prompting swift measures to recall it. Copies had slipped through the cracks, however, and a clandestine second edition soon found its way into eager hands.

Romola, growing increasingly desperate to speak with Fra Girolamo, poured over the evidence with an intensity that bordered on obsession. She sought to sift through the cacophony of assertions and counter-assertions, to discern a thread of truth amidst the chaos. In the fervent ranks of Savonarola's devotees, his wavering resolve under torture and retraction of prophetic claims had created an unshakeable unease, swiftly supplanted by the notion that any discordant statements attributed to him were, in fact, the product of Ser Ceccone's mendacious pen. That notorious notary had compiled the examination's digest, sowing seeds of doubt and mistrust.

Yet, glaring discrepancies in the document's composition cried out for attention. Hadn't the list of examiners been deliberately stacked with the Frate's most ardent detractors? And wasn't Dolfo Spini, a man notorious for his malice, among the new Eight hastily elected to stack the deck against the prophet? The whole proceeding reeked of calculated manipulation, a deliberate attempt to destroy a man who had become an inconvenient thorn in the sides of the Pope, the Italian states, and the city's corrupt elite. For in the Old Palace, a sinister game of politics was afoot, with the Signoria intent on striking a Faustian bargain with the Pope and the Duke of Milan. The Frate's fate had been sealed, and the only question remaining was whether the Republic would hand him over to the Pope alive or permit him to meet a gruesome end on the city's piazza.

Under such circumstances, who could possibly credit this confession? If the Frate had recanted his prophetic gift, was it not because the agony of torture had shattered his resolve?
