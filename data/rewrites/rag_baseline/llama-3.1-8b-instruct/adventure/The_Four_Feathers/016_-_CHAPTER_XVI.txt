As Ethne sat amidst the verdant enclosure, her senses were transported to a different realm, one where the weight of her memories was eclipsed by the radiant presence of Harry Feversham. It was as if the warm sunlight and vibrant blooms had conspired to erase the trials of the past five years, leaving her with only the faintest recollection of the struggles she had endured. Yet, like the delicate petals of a flower, her thoughts were fragile, and the slightest breeze – in this case, the sound of Colonel Durrance's voice at the entrance of the garden – could shatter the tranquility.

"Hush," she whispered to Captain Willoughby, her face pale and her eyes shutting tight for a moment as a spasm of pain washed over her. Though her emotions threatened to overwhelm her, Ethne steeled herself, knowing that she couldn't afford to indulge in sentiment. Her conversation with Captain Willoughby had been a fleeting respite, but now it was time to resume her responsibilities, ones that weighed heavily upon her shoulders like the dense foliage of the surrounding hedges.

With a mix of trepidation and resignation, Ethne surveyed her surroundings, searching for an escape route. The creek flowed serenely before her, and the high walls of the enclosure hemmed her in on all sides. Durrance stood at the entrance, his presence as imposing as the ancient trees that towered above. There was only one path leading out, and Durrance blocked it, his broad figure a formidable barrier.

"Stay silent," she whispered urgently, her voice barely audible over the rustling of leaves. "Do you know him?"

Captain Willoughby nodded, his eyes narrowing as he recalled their shared experiences at Suakin. "We were comrades for three years. I heard he'd lost his sight, but I see that's not true."

"Speak softly," Ethne cautioned, her voice a mere breath. "He is indeed blind."

Willoughby's brow furrowed in concern. "What can I say to him?"

"Nothing," Ethne replied firmly, her grip on his arm tightening.

Durrance's voice, low and measured, carried across the space between them. "Ethne?" The sound sent a shiver down her spine, and for an instant, she forgot the present, recalling the countless moments she had spent with Harry Feversham. Her gaze locked onto Durrance, her eyes drinking in every detail, as if searching for some hidden truth.
