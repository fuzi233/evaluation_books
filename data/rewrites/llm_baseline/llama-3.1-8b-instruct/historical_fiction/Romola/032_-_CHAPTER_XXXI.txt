The warm sunlight streaming through the windows of their humble home illuminated the tranquil scene unfolding before Tito as he sat hunched over his desk, quill poised in hand. It was the second morning since his impassioned speech in the Piazza del Duomo, and he had announced to Romola that he would be receiving distinguished guests, including the esteemed Milanese Count and the influential Seneschal de Beaucaire. Tito's eyes drifted toward Romola, who stood before him, her dark hair tucked beneath her hood, her features set in a gentle expression.

"My Romola," he began, "you need not trouble yourself with the formalities of receiving these visitors. They will be more interested in inspecting our antiquities and perusing the library. Perhaps it would be best if you were to excuse yourself today and visit your cousin Brigida instead." A hint of a wish was woven into his words, and Romola, attuned to the subtleties of her husband's emotions, quickly agreed.

As she turned to depart, Romola's hand rested upon Tito's head, a familiar gesture that usually brought a smile to his lips and a lifting of his gaze to meet hers. But today, something felt different. Tito's head remained bowed, his pen moving across the parchment with a mechanical ease, as if he were trying to maintain a façade of normalcy. Romola's touch, meant to be a soothing balm, felt instead like a leaden weight, and she withdrew her hand, sensing a change in her husband's demeanor.

"Farewell, my dear," Tito called out as she departed, his voice laced with a forced lightness. "Take Maso with you, and enjoy the day." Romola's footsteps echoed through the hallway, leaving Tito alone with his thoughts. For the first time, he felt a pang of awareness that their marriage had undergone a subtle shift. Was it his own doing, or was something external at play? The enigmatic circumstances surrounding the armor and the portrait hinted at a truth he dared not confront.

As soon as Romola vanished from sight, Tito laid down his pen and raised his gaze, his eyes scanning the room with a sense of relief.
