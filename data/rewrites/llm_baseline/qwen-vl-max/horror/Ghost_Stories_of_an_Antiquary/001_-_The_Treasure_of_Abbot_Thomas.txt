The Treasure of Abbot Thomas

Let it be known, for those who care to trace the roots of my tales, that St Bertrand de Comminges and Viborg are places of genuine existence—though their names serve here more as echoes than anchors. In “Oh, Whistle, and I’ll Come to You,” I had Felixstowe in mind, its quiet shores and cold winds shaping the mood of the story. As for the learned fragments that drift through these pages—those references to obscure texts and forgotten manuscripts—almost all are inventions of my own. The book I cite in this tale, "The Treasure of Abbot Thomas," never existed; no such volume was ever penned or preserved.

"Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book" was written in 1894 and published shortly thereafter in the *National Review*; "Lost Hearts" appeared in the *Pall Mall Magazine*. The remaining five stories, most of which were read aloud at Christmas gatherings among friends at King’s College, Cambridge, I remember only faintly—save that "Number 13" was composed in 1899, while "The Treasure of Abbot Thomas" took shape during the summer of 1904.

M. R. James
