The Finale of a Feast Well-Served.

As the last course of our narrative draws to a close, let us gather 'round the table once more and indulge in a delightful dessert – the sweet tales of those who have graced our pages. Let us start with the humble curates, those unsung heroes of our story. Malone, I see, rises to the occasion, his face aglow with modesty. He knows his own worth when he hears it.

But, alas, dear reader, we cannot linger on Peter Augustus's exploits. His life and times are a tale too complex, too multifaceted, for the likes of us. A discriminating public has its quirks, you see, and the unvarnished truth, like a fine vintage wine, requires careful handling. We dare not risk offending the tender sensibilities of our readers by serving up the raw, unadulterated facts. No, let us rather savor the essence of his story, filtered through the prism of imagination, where the nuances of his character shine like a perfectly roasted capon at a festive banquet.

Now, let us welcome the esteemed Mr. Sweeting, accompanied by his lovely wife, Mrs. Sweeting, née Dora Sykes. Together, they formed a match made in heaven, their union blessed by the warm sunshine of a comfortable living and a generous dowry. Their years together were filled with laughter, love, and the quiet contentment that comes from a life well-lived. They were the epitome of a happy couple, beloved by their parishioners and a circle of dear friends.

As we raise our glasses in a toast to their happiness, let us also acknowledge the excellent Mr. Donne, who has proven himself a true master of the art of living. His marriage to a sensible, gentlewoman was the key to unlocking his full potential, and he became a pillar of his community, a shining example of a devoted parish priest. His little church, school, and parsonage were testaments to his hard work and dedication, each one a masterpiece in its own right. And, oh, the skill with which he begged for the funds to support his endeavors! He had a gift for persuasion, a silver tongue that could charm even the most skeptical of hearts.
