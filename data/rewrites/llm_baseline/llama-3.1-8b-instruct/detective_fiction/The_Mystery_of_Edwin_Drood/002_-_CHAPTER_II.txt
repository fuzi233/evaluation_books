As the last wisps of sunlight faded from the sky, casting long shadows across the Close, the rooks began their evening ritual, winging their way homeward in a stately procession. Two birds, however, broke away from the flock, retracing their steps and pausing in mid-air, as if deliberating on some weighty matter. It was a habit often observed in these birds, and one that seemed to hold a certain mystique for those who watched them.

In the Cathedral, the service had concluded, and the choir had dispersed, leaving behind a scattering of venerable rooks, their faces stern and wise. Two of these elderly gentlemen, Mr. Crisparkle and Mr. Tope, stood outside, engaged in conversation. The Dean, a man of considerable dignity, emerged from the Cathedral, his footsteps echoing off the stone walls.

"Not a moment too soon," he said, eyeing the pair. "I see you've stayed late, Mr. Tope."

"Yes, Mr. Dean," replied Mr. Tope, his voice low and measured. "I waited for Mr. Jasper, who was feeling somewhat unwell."

"Ah, yes," said the Dean, his brow furrowed with concern. "I've heard rumors of his condition. Tell me, when did he fall ill, and what were the symptoms?"

Mr. Tope hesitated, choosing his words carefully. "He was...breathing rather heavily, sir, when he arrived. It seemed to distress him greatly to take notes, and afterwards, he experienced a sort of...lapse."

The Dean's eyes narrowed. "A lapse? You mean to say he suffered some sort of fit?"

Mr. Tope nodded, his eyes flicking towards Mr. Crisparkle. "Yes, sir. His memory became...dazed, and he felt a sense of giddiness wash over him. But a little rest and some water seemed to restore him, and he's now returned home, feeling quite himself again."

The Dean nodded thoughtfully, his gaze drifting towards the old stone gatehouse, where a warm fire glowed through the latticed window. "And how is he faring now?" he asked.

"He's settled in for the night, sir," replied Mr. Tope. "The fire is lit, and he's making himself comfortable. It's chilly after the rain, and the Cathedral feels damp and cold."
