The gentle lullaby of a newborn's presence still lingered in the air, a soothing melody that seemed to harmonize the discordant notes of the world outside. Little Miles had arrived, a sweet serenade to the grand symphony of life, and the Palace itself echoed with the joyous fanfare of his birth. As the Royal George made his debut, the very fabric of society seemed to vibrate with anticipation, and multitudes gathered to catch a glimpse of the young prince, swaddled and nestled in a gilded crib, surrounded by noble nurses who watched over him with devotion.

But amidst the grandeur, our little Miles found solace in the quiet companionship of his loyal Gumbo and kind Molly, who showered him with affection, their hearts overflowing with love and admiration. His parents, Theo and I, had chosen a name that spoke to the simplicity and beauty of our own humble beginnings – a name that had been passed down through generations of Warringtons, a testament to the enduring power of tradition and heritage.

As we shared the news of our child's arrival with our royal parents, we couldn't help but feel a sense of trepidation, knowing that the world beyond our little circle would soon be aware of our circumstances. We took a deep breath and sent a letter to the Daily Advertiser, announcing the birth of our child in the unassuming setting of Church Street, Lambeth. The response from Aunt Bernstein was characteristic of her wit and warmth, a gentle rebuke for revealing our humble abode to the world, accompanied by a beautiful silk coverlid, a lace-edged gift fit for a prince, which Theo and Molly adored.

Dr. Heberden visited frequently, ensuring that all was well with my wife, and introduced us to a skilled local surgeon who tended to her needs. As she recovered, our small precinct was once again graced by the presence of Lady Castlewood's chariot, bearing gifts and stories of the splendor at court. Her jelly, though perhaps not to everyone's taste, was a reminder of the grand feasts and celebrations surrounding the birth of the royal heir. Mr. Johnson's visit coincided with one of these occasions, and he was taken aback by the Countess's regal presence, his bows more respectful than elegant. She smiled warmly, addressing me as cousin, and helped transfer the jelly to our humble crockery, her kindness and generosity a balm to our spirits.
