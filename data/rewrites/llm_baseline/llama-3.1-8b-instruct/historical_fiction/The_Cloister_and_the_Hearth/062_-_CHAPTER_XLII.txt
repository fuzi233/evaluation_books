As the warm sunlight filtered through the windows, Gerard slowly regained consciousness, his parched lips parting to form a faint smile. His old landlady, watching over him with a mixture of concern and hope, gently stroked his forehead. For days, she had tended to him, praying for his recovery as fervently as she prepared the rich stews and hearty breads that filled their small kitchen with savory aromas.

Her thoughts turned to the fragrant herbs she had gathered from the garden outside – the fresh rosemary and thyme that would add depth to tonight's bouillabaisse – as she carefully poured a cup of cool water into a nearby jug. Gerard's fever had raged like a wild fire, consuming his young body and threatening to snuff out the flame of life within him.

The clock on the mantle ticked steadily, marking the passing hours as Gerard slipped in and out of delirium. His landlady whispered a silent prayer to the Virgin, asking for her intercession in Gerard's recovery. As the sun dipped below the horizon, Gerard's eyelids grew heavy, and he sank into a deep slumber.

The next morning, Gerard's landlady rejoiced at his peaceful rest, lighting a small candle to the Virgin and offering a quiet thanksgiving. But as the hours passed, her joy gave way to concern – Gerard had slept for twelve hours, and now he had been under the covers for twenty-four. She summoned the local healer, a wise woman known for her knowledge of herbs and remedies.

The healer examined Gerard with a discerning eye, her hands moving deftly as she checked his pulse and felt his forehead. "He's a young man," she said, "and his body will heal itself. Let him be, and let nature take its course." Gerard's landlady nodded, grateful for the reassurance.

As the hours ticked by, Gerard's room became a place of pilgrimage for the curious and concerned. Fra Colonna and his friend Fra Jerome, a towering figure with a calm demeanor, arrived to pay their respects. They sat in silence, watching over Gerard as the hours passed.

It was on the sixtieth hour of Gerard's sleep that he finally stirred, his eyes fluttering open like a flower unfolding its petals. He gazed around the room, taking in the familiar faces of his friends and the kindly features of his landlady.
