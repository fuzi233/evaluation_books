As the sun began its slow descent towards the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the rolling hills, Gerard and Denys found themselves at a crossroads. A quaint town beckoned from one direction, its chimneys puffing gentle wisps of smoke into the evening air, while a monastery stood sentinel in the distance, its stone façade weathered to a soft mossy gray. Denys's eyes gleamed with anticipation, his thoughts drifting to the promise of a warm bed and a hearty meal at the local inn. Gerard, however, felt an inexplicable pull towards the monastery, its tranquil atmosphere a siren's call to his weary soul.

As they stood there, a silent understanding passed between them, and Denys yielded to Gerard's wishes. The monk's chart, a worn leather-bound book, had led them thus far, and now it seemed to have reached its terminus. With a nod, they veered off the main road, leaving the bustle of the town behind. Gerard's curiosity got the better of him, and he asked Denys why he had suddenly developed an aversion to the monasteries that had so graciously provided for them in the past. Denys hesitated, his eyes clouding over, before the words spilled out in a torrent.

"It's not just this place," he said, his voice low and rough. "It's all the others like it. The cold stone floors, the endless bells tolling like a mournful knell, the rules and rituals that suffocate the very life from you. I've been trapped in those places for too long, forced to don the cloak of a servant rather than a man. The monks may speak of humility and devotion, but I've seen only a different kind of servitude – one where the individual is lost in the sea of faces, a mere cog in a machine that grinds out piety and obedience."

Gerard listened intently, his expression sympathetic, as Denys continued his indictment.
