As we entered the drawing room, the warm glow of the setting sun cast a golden light upon the familiar faces gathered around the table. My father, still sipping his tea, joined us, his eyes fixed upon Carmilla with a gentle concern. The afternoon had been filled with laughter and card games, and though Carmilla had abstained from the chocolate, her spirits seemed to have lifted. I watched as she smiled and chatted with Madame and Mademoiselle De Lafontaine, her presence weaving a subtle magic within the room.

Papa leaned forward, his brow furrowed with worry. "Carmilla, have you received any news from your mother?" he asked, his voice low and soothing.

Carmilla's gaze drifted away, her eyes clouding over like a summer storm. "No," she replied, her voice barely above a whisper.

He pressed on, his fingers drumming a gentle rhythm on the armrest. "Do you know where a letter might reach you?"

"I'm not sure," she admitted, her words laced with a hint of mystery. "But I've been thinking of leaving soon. I've caused you too much trouble, and I fear I've overstayed my welcome."

My father's expression turned firm, his voice taking on a note of resolve. "You mustn't even think of it, Carmilla. We couldn't bear to lose you, and I won't let you leave without your mother's permission. Besides, the news of the strange illness spreading through the neighborhood grows more disturbing by the day. I fear for your safety, and for the safety of those around you."

Carmilla's smile faltered, her eyes welling up with unshed tears. "Thank you, sir," she whispered, her voice trembling. "You've been too kind to me. I've never known such happiness as I've found here, surrounded by your warmth and care."

As the evening drew to a close, I walked Carmilla to her room, the silence between us heavy with unspoken thoughts. We stood before the mirror, her hands moving deftly as she prepared for bed.

"Do you think you'll ever trust me completely?" I asked, my voice barely above a whisper.

Her gaze met mine, a soft smile playing upon her lips. She made no answer, but her eyes spoke volumes of the secrets she kept hidden.

"You won't tell me?" I pressed, my heart aching with a mix of curiosity and concern.
