As the morning sun cast its gentle light through the narrow windows of the spunging-house, Harry Warrington arose from his bed with a sense of resolve etched on his face. His brief stay within these cold walls had not softened his heart towards the world outside, but rather hardened it further. The memories of his family and friends, once dear to him, now seemed tainted by their inability to help him in his time of need. His aunt, his friend March, and his cousin Castlewood had all failed him, leaving him to face the consequences of his actions alone. The thought of begging for their aid was unbearable, and so he had made up his mind to escape this unforgiving society and find solace in the wilds of Virginia or the battlefields of Germany.

As he dressed, Gumbo entered the room, bearing the morning post. "I brought some letters from Bond Street, sir," he said, his voice laced with a hint of concern. "But Mr. Ruff, the landlord, refused to let me bring any more of your belongings." Harry's eyes narrowed as he scanned the envelopes, his heart sinking as he saw they were all bills. A fourth letter, however, caught his attention – a notice from the landlord, informing him that unless he paid his debt, his belongings would be sold to settle the account. The thought of losing his precious possessions was a bitter pill to swallow.

Just then, Gumbo spoke up, his words dripping with a mixture of loyalty and unease. "I found refuge with friends at Lord Wrotham's house, sir. Colonel Lambert is a kind man, and he offered to help you if you need him." Harry's expression darkened as he recalled the Colonel's past transgressions, and he ground his teeth in frustration. He would never stoop to asking for help from someone who had wronged him so grievously. The memory of Hetty's supposed fainting spell, however, gave him pause. While he suspected Gumbo of exaggerating, the thought of her distress touched his heart.

As he pondered his next move, Harry requested his breakfast and dispatched Gumbo to summon Mr. Draper, a lawyer from the Temple. The message, penned in a haughty tone, was a reflection of Harry's growing disdain for those around him.
