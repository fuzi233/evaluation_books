As the last wisps of sunlight faded beyond the Cloisterham rooftops, Mr. Thomas Sapsea settled into his armchair, cradling a glass of fine port wine in his hand. The fire crackled and spat, casting a warm glow over the modest sitting-room, where the scent of old leather and polished wood mingled with the faint tang of decay. Mr. Sapsea's eyes, red-rimmed from a day spent surveying the town's properties, drifted to the wooden effigy above the doorway – a likeness of his father, resplendent in a curly wig and toga, selling something unseen. A faint smile played on Mr. Sapsea's lips as he recalled the admiration his father's craftsmanship had inspired.

As he sipped his wine, Mr. Sapsea's gaze wandered to the manuscript on his writing-desk, where a fragment of music caught his eye. He rose from his chair, his thumbs disappearing into the arm-holes of his waistcoat as he began to pace the room, repeating the notes to himself in a low, sonorous voice. The single word "Ethelinda" echoed through the space, a whispered promise of beauty yet to be realized.

Just then, his serving-maid entered with a quiet knock, announcing the arrival of Mr. Jasper. Mr. Sapsea's face brightened, and he waved her forward, drawing two glasses from the tray as she departed. As Mr. Jasper entered, Mr. Sapsea rose to greet him, his movements deliberate and measured, like the ticking of the eight-day clock on the mantle.

"Glad to see you, sir," Mr. Sapsea said, his voice booming through the room. "I congratulate myself on having the honour of receiving you here for the first time."

Mr. Jasper's smile was warm, but tempered by a hint of irony. "The honour is mine, and the self-congratulation is mine, I think."

Mr. Sapsea chuckled, a low rumble that seemed to vibrate through the air. "You are pleased to say so, sir. But I do assure you that it is a satisfaction to me to receive you in my humble home. And that is what I would not say to everybody." His eyes sparkled with a hint of condescension, as if daring Mr. Jasper to disagree.
