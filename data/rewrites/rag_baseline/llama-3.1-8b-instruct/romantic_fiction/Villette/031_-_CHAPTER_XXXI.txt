As I navigated the labyrinthine corridors of my mind, the ephemeral nature of my connection to the world outside became increasingly apparent. The advent of spring had brought with it a marked shift in temperature, rendering my usual reserve of energy woefully inadequate. Even the most minor exertions left me fatigued, and sleepless nights only exacerbated the problem, inducing languid days that seemed to stretch on interminably.

One Sunday afternoon, I found myself drawn to the Protestant church, a tranquil oasis in the midst of the bustling town. As I returned, weary and exhausted, I took refuge in my makeshift sanctuary, the first classe, and collapsed onto my desk, utilizing it as a makeshift pillow for my arms and head. The soothing hum of bees in the nearby berceau proved a gentle accompaniment to my reverie, as I watched, through the glass door and the delicate spring foliage, Madame Beck and her entourage strolling beneath the blossoming orchard boughs, their laughter and conversation carrying on the breeze like a gentle melody.

My gaze was drawn to a particular figure among the group, a young woman whose acquaintance I had previously made as a visitor at Madame Beck's residence. She was described to me as the "filleule," or god-daughter, of Professor Emanuel, and her connection to him was said to be rooted in a long-standing friendship between her mother or aunt and the learned scholar. As I observed her, I noted the ease with which she interacted with the professor, her mannerisms suggesting a deep affection and familiarity that bordered on the familial. In fact, I had witnessed her run up to him, placing her arm through his, and leaning upon him with a freedom that spoke volumes about their relationship. A fleeting, unsettling sensation had arisen within me upon observing this scene, a feeling that defied analysis, but I chose to set it aside, focusing instead on the young woman herself, whose name was Mademoiselle Sauveur.

As I continued to watch her, my attention was drawn to the vibrant hue of her silk robe, which seemed to shimmer and dance amidst the lush foliage and emerald leaves. My gaze followed the path of her movement, becoming increasingly entranced until, finally, my eyelids grew heavy, and I succumbed to a deep slumber.

Time passed, unnoticed by me, until I awoke to find the sun dipping below the horizon, casting a warm, golden light over the garden and the room. The bees had returned to their hives
