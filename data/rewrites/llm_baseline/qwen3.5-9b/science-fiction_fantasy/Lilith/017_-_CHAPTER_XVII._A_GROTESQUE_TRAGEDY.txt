I hadn't taken ten steps before I spotted something peculiar ahead and hurried closer to identify it. It was a rotting carriage of archaic design, ruined yet miraculously standing upright on its massive wheels. Flanking the pole, still in their original positions, lay the skeletons of two horses. From their two grim, bleached skulls, the shriveled reins ascended to the skeletal hand of the coachman, who sat atop his tattered hammer-cloth. Both doors had long since collapsed inward. Inside, two skeletons reclined, one in each corner.

As I watched, they suddenly jerked awake. With a rattling crash of bone against bone, each leaped from the door nearest it. One crashed down and remained prone; the other stood for a fleeting moment, its entire structure trembling dangerously, before struggling—its joints stiff and immobile—to creep along the rear of the carriage. Clinging desperately to the backboard, it moved to the opposite side, where its slender leg-bones seemed barely capable of supporting its weight. Kneeling beside its fallen partner, it attempted to lift the prostrate figure, nearly toppling itself in the effort.

Slowly, through a sudden exertion, the fallen skeleton rose to a sitting position. For a few moments, it turned its yellowed cranium this way and that. Then, ignoring its neighbor, it managed to stand by gripping the spokes of the hind wheel. Standing half-erect, back-to-back with its companion, it held onto one of its own knee-joints with both hands. With similar difficulty and numerous contortions, the kneeling skeleton followed suit and addressed its partner.

"Have you injured yourself, my lord?" it asked, its voice sounding distant and poorly articulated, as if blown aside by a spectral gale.

"Yes, I have," the other replied in a matching but rougher tone. "You offered no assistance, and this cursed knee is dislocated!"

"I did my very best, my lady."

"No doubt, my lord, for it was terrible! I feared I'd never find my footing again—but, bless my soul, madam! Are you also missing your bones?"

She glanced down at herself. "I have nothing else to be missing," she retorted. "--And *you*, at least, cannot complain! But what on earth is happening? Am I dreaming?"

"You *may* be dreaming, madam—I cannot say; but this knee of mine forbids me the comforting illusion. Ha! And I, too, perceive that I possess nothing to walk upon but bones! Not so unbecoming for a man, however! I trust to goodness they aren't *my* bones! Every ache is worse than the last, and this loose knee is the worst. The bed must have been damp—and I so drunk I didn't notice!"

"Probably, my lord of Cokayne!"

"What?! What?! That makes me think I'm dreaming too—all these aches included! How do *you* know the title my rowdy bullies give me? I don't recall you! Anyway, you have no right to act familiar! My name is... I am Lord... tut, tut! What do you call me when I'm... I mean, when you're sober? I can't remember! Why, what *is* my name? I must have been incredibly drunk when I went to bed! I often am!"

"You visit so rarely that I don't know, my lord; but I'll take your word for *that*!"

"I hope so!"

"--if for nothing else!"

"Hmph! I've never lied in my life!"

"You've never spoken a single truth to me, my lord!"

"On my honor! Why, I haven't seen the woman before!"

"You knew me well enough to deceive, my lord!"

"It seems I'm beginning to dream that we've met before, but upon my oath, I recognize nothing of you! Without your clothes, how can anyone tell who you truly are? One thing I *can* swear—that I've never seen you so undressed before! By heavens, I have no memory of you!"

"I'm relieved to hear that; my memories of you are the least unpleasant of all. Good morning, my lord!"

She turned away, hobbling and clacking a few paces before stopping again.

"You are just as cold-hearted as... as... any other woman, madam! Where in this hellish place shall I find my valet? What was the cursed name I used to call that fool?"

He twisted his bare skull this way and that on its creaking pivot, still clutching his knee with both hands.

"I will serve as your valet today, my lord," the lady said, turning back to him. "--What can I do for you? It's difficult to tell!"

"Tie my leg on, naturally, you fool! Can't you see it's practically detached? Alas, my dancing days are over!"

She scanned her surroundings with empty eye sockets, found a tuft of fibrous grass, and proceeded to bind the disconnected parts of his knee together. Once finished, he took a few careful, tentative steps.

"You used to stomp quite differently, my lord!" she remarked as she rose from her knees.

"Eh? What?! Now that I look at you again, it seems I used to hate you! Eh?"

"Naturally, my lord! You hated many people—your wife, certainly, among them!"

"Ah, I begin... I begin... But—I must have been away for ages! I truly forget! There! Your damned, miserable scrap of grass is breaking!"

"We used to manage quite well together, eh?"

"Not that I recall, my lord. The only happy moments I shared with you were scattered across the first week of our marriage."

"Is that how it was? Ha, ha! Well, it's over now, thank goodness!"

"I wish I could believe it! Why were we sitting in that carriage together? It brings dread!"

"I believe we were divorced, my lady!"

"Hardly sufficient proof; we remain bound!"

"A tragic truth, yet remediable: the forest appears vast!"

"I doubt it! I doubt it!"

"I apologize; I can't think of a compliment without lying. Judging by your figure and complexion, you've lived hard since I last saw you! I can't possibly be *quite* so naked as your ladyship! I beg your pardon, madam! Please accept this as jesting within a dream! Regardless, whether dreaming or waking, everything is the same—all mere appearance! You can't be sure of anything, which is as good as knowing there is nothing! Life teaches any fool that lesson!"

"It has taught me the fool I was for loving you!"

"You weren't the only fool to fall for me! Women had a habit of falling in love with me—I had simply forgotten you were one of them!"

"I did love you, my lord—a little—at one time!"

"There was your error, my lady! You should have loved me deeply, devotedly, savagely—eternally! Then I would have grown tired of you sooner and hated you less afterward! But let past grievances be buried! WHERE are we? Location is the question! To be or not to be is NOT the question!"

"We are in the afterlife, I presume!"

"Granted—but which realm, or what sort? This isn't Hell!"

"It must be! Marriage exists here! You and I are damned to each other."

"Then I'm unlike Othello, damned to a beautiful wife! Oh, I remember Shakespeare, madam!"

She picked up a broken branch fallen into a bush, steadied herself with it, and walked away, tossing her small skull.

"Give me that stick," cried her former husband. "I need it more than you."

She gave no reply.

"You intend to make me beg for it?"

"Not at all, my lord. I intend to keep it," she answered, continuing her slow retreat.

"Give it to me immediately! I demand it!"

"Unfortunately, I think I need it myself!" the lady replied, walking slightly faster, her joints cracking and bones clinking sharply.

He tried to follow but nearly toppled; the grass binding his leg had burst. Swearing under his breath, he stopped, seizing his leg again.

"Come and tie it properly!" he would have thundered, but instead, he merely whistled.

She turned to look at him.

"Come and tie it up instantly!" he repeated.

She took a step or two further away.

"I swear I won't touch you!" he cried.

"Swear away, my lord! No one is here to believe you. However, please don't lose your temper, or you'll shake yourself to pieces. As for finding enough string to bind all your erratic joints, I couldn't tell you."

She returned and knelt beside him again, though first she placed the disputed stick beyond his reach and within hers. The instant she finished re-binding the joint, he lunged, seemingly intending to seize her by the hair, but his hard fingers slipped off her smooth skull.

"Disgusting!" he muttered, grabbing her upper arm-bone instead.

"You'll break it!" she warned, looking up from her knees.

"I will, then!" he answered, straining against it.

"I won't tie your leg again the next time it loosens!" she threatened.
