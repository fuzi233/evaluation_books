Among the vibrant stalls, Gerard marveled at the array of fresh produce, his senses heightened by the vibrant colors and enticing aromas.

Upon rejoining Fra Colonna, the friar led him to a humble trattoria, where they sat down to a meal of steaming pasta and fragrant herbs. As they ate, the friar began to expound upon the nature of Rome, its grandeur and decay. "This city," he said, "is a tapestry woven from threads of past and present, with fragments of ancient grandeur hidden beneath the surface." He pointed out the remains of the Temple of Peace, its stones weathered by time, and the great sewer, a testament to the engineering prowess of the Romans.

Gerard listened intently, his mind whirling with the possibilities of this ancient city. "Would that we could see the Capitol in all its glory," he sighed, "with its twenty-five temples standing tall." The friar smiled, his eyes twinkling with amusement. "Ah, but that would be a feast for the imagination, my son. Suffice it to say that Rome's history is a rich broth, flavored with the spices of empires risen and fallen."
