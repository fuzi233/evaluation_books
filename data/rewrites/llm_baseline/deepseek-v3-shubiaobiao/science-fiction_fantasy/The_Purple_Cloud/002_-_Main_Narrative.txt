But I laughed. *“Me? I wouldn’t go if you begged me. I’m to be married.”*

*“You would,”* Clark countered, smirking. *“A man like you? Botany, navigation—you’d pick it up in no time.”*

*“You speak as if it’s a real possibility,”* I said, forcing another laugh. *“There’s Peters, after all—”*

I changed the subject, but the seed was planted. That night, as I prepared to visit Clodagh, the Voices returned—two warring whispers that had haunted me since childhood. One urged caution, the other reckless abandon. I had learned to heed them, for they were never wrong.

Clodagh was waiting, her sharp face half-lit by the fading sun. She turned from the window, a rose between her fingers. *“Clark spoke to you about the expedition?”*

*“A passing fancy,”* I said, too quickly.

*“The first man to stand at the Pole will be immortal,”* she murmured. *“Titled. Rich beyond measure.”*

*“And frozen,”* I countered.

Her lips curled. *“You wouldn’t go, even for me?”*

I hesitated. She knew my weakness.

Then came the news—Peters, ill. Poisoned. Atropine, they said. A tragic accident, the inquest ruled.

Three weeks later, I stood on the deck of the *Boreal*, London fading behind me. Clark’s dream had come true.

And Clodagh’s kiss still burned on my lips.
