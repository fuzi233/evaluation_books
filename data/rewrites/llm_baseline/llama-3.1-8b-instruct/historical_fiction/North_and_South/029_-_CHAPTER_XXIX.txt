As Margaret settled into her morning routine, a letter arrived from Edith, its words dancing across the page like fireflies on a summer evening. The affection that poured out of every sentence was a balm to Margaret's own heart, and she smiled as she read of Edith's precious boy, resplendent in his tiny cap, a miniature version of the man Margaret had once been. The memory of their shared laughter and whispered secrets rose up, like the scent of blooming jasmine on a warm breeze. Edith's words wove a tapestry of warmth and longing, a call to leave behind the chill of England and join her in the radiant south, where sunshine poured like honey and the air was alive with music.

"Come, Margaret," the letter begged, "let us dance together under the stars, surrounded by the gentle lapping of the waves against the shore. Our baby's smile is the brightest star in the sky, and I am consumed by its radiance. My husband, Cosmo, is a pillar of strength, a stalwart companion in the face of life's challenges. Though he may grow stout and gruff, his heart remains full of love and kindness. And as for the baby, he is a tiny king, reigning over our hearts with a fierce and joyful cry."

Edith's words tumbled out in a joyful jumble, a kaleidoscope of emotions that left Margaret breathless and wanting more. She felt the sting of envy as Edith described the picnic given by the officers of the Hazard, anchored in the bay below. The music, the laughter, the warm sun on skin – it all seemed to beckon Margaret to leave behind the shadows of her past and join Edith in this sun-kissed world.

But amidst the effusive praise, Edith's tone shifted, like the tides, and she grew more serious. "Margaret, I know you must come," she wrote. "It would be a balm to Aunt Hale's spirit, a chance for her to feel the warmth of youth and vitality once more. The doctor will agree, I'm sure – it's the smoke of Milton that's doing her harm, not the climate itself. Three months of sunshine and grapes as common as blackberries will work wonders, I'm certain of it."
