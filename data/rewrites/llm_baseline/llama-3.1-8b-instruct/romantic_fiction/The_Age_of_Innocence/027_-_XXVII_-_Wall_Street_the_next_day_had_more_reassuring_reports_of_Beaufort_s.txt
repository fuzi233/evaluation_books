The sun cast its warm rays upon the bustling streets of Wall Street, where the previous day's uncertainty had given way to a sense of cautious optimism. Rumors of Beaufort's situation had spread like ripples on a pond, each one contradicting the last, yet none providing a definitive answer. Still, the general consensus was that he had called upon influential connections to bail him out, and that evening, Mrs. Beaufort's radiant smile and sparkling emerald necklace at the Opera seemed to confirm this notion. Society exhaled a collective sigh of relief, grateful that the Beauforts' fate remained uncertain, rather than being sealed.

New York's stern judgment of business impropriety loomed large, a constant presence that brooked no exceptions. Yet, the prospect of Beaufort and his wife facing the consequences was a daunting one, particularly since their disappearance would leave a gaping void in the city's social circle. Those who were either oblivious or apathetic to the moral implications lamented the loss of the Beauforts, citing the decline of the best ballroom in town.

As Archer made his way through the crowded streets, he found himself increasingly resolute in his decision to travel to Washington. He waited only for the commencement of the lawsuit, which would coincide with his departure, but when he met with Mr. Letterblair on the following Tuesday, he learned that the case might be postponed for several weeks. Undeterred, Archer decided to leave the next evening, regardless of the outcome. He was eager to see Madame Olenska, and there were several matters he needed to discuss with her.

The next morning, as Archer arrived at his office, Mr. Letterblair greeted him with a look of distress. Beaufort's predicament had taken a turn for the worse, and despite spreading rumors of his success in salvaging the situation, he had only managed to temporarily placate his depositors. As a result, a run on the bank had begun, and its doors might close before the day was over. The whispers of Beaufort's cunning scheme grew louder, and his impending downfall seemed all but inevitable.

Mr. Letterblair's usually composed demeanor had given way to anxiety, and he confided in Archer about the gravity of the situation. "This is a disaster of unprecedented proportions," he exclaimed.
