Moonlight spilled in silver ribbons down the rough stone stair to pool on the pitted street far below, where a sharp, unwholesome wind howled through alleyways thick with the stench of long-forgotten rot. Not a single breath of that gale crept into my sheltered nook above the landing, and I was just settling in to rest when my eyes flew open of their own accord. There, peeking over the uppermost step, was the luminous ivory head of the creature I’d seen trailing the faceless Shadow days before.
The moment our eyes locked, she froze, then began to back away, tail first, cautious as a trespasser. I sprang to my feet, and with no room to turn her broad, spotted-free pelt in the narrow stairwell, she tumbled head over hindquarters in a flurry of white paws, scrambled upright, and vanished down the steps before I could call out. I chased her all the way to the street, scanning the length of the moonlit lanes, but she was gone without a trace. I trundled back to my hard stone couch, my mind racing. Two predatory things roamed Bulika’s dark streets now: the marked shadow-beast, and this unblemished white leopardess. I cared little for the fates of the cruel, closed-off citizens of this city, but a child’s life was well worth risking my own ragged, borrowed life for. I resolved to stand watch by the hidden door for the rest of the night.
Before long, the latch of that door moved, slow as a whisper, so faint I almost thought I’d imagined it. I looked up to see the door ajar, and slipped inside soft as a shadow. The figure waiting behind it was not the widowed woman I’d helped days prior, but the muffled, hooded stranger I’d crossed paths with out in the glass dunes of the desert. She said not a single word, just led me a few steps to an empty stone-floored chamber worn smooth by centuries of foot traffic, and pointed to a woven indigo rug tossed on the floor. I wrapped myself in its thick wool, and lay down. She shut the chamber door, and I heard the outer entrance of the house open and click shut behind her.
The only light in the room seeped from the moon through a cracked high window, slashing silver streaks across the dark stone. I lay wide awake, and soon caught the sound of a stifled, distant moan. It dragged on for minutes, rising, until a newborn’s wail cut through it, followed by a shriek that curdled my blood. I launched myself up and bolted into the passage, and there she was: the white leopardess, jaws firm but gentle around the scruff of a squalling new baby, carrying it as easily as she would her own cub. I threw my full weight at her, driving my shoulder into her ribs, and forced her to drop the infant. It fell to the stone slabs with a piteous, breathless wail.
At the sound, the hooded woman appeared. She stepped clean over both of us, the beast and I, locked in our struggle in the narrow hall, picked up the crying child, and carried it away. She returned moments later, lifted me bodily off the leopardess’s back, opened the front door, and pushed me gently out onto the street. The great white cat was right on my heels.
For a heartbeat I thought she had betrayed me, handed me over to the beast to tear apart at her leisure. But I was not about to go down without a fight. I sprinted down the stairs, waiting for her to pounce on my back, but the attack never came. When I spun around to grapple her, she bounded clean over my head, and when I turned to face her again, she was crouched docile at my feet.
I stooped, and ran my hand over her soft, glowing white pelt. She responded by licking my bare feet with her rough, dry, warm tongue, and suddenly a well of tenderness rose up in my chest so thick it felt like it would spill over. She might still be treacherous, I knew, but if I turned away from every small show of kindness for fear it was a trick, I would never find that real, unfeigned love that had to exist somewhere in this strange half-waking world. I stood up, and she rose to her feet beside me, matching my height almost exactly, her purr low and steady.
A heavy, wet squelch echoed out across the street a few yards from us. I ran to the noise, and found a pulpy, mangled mass, just shape enough left to make out the body of a woman, thrown down from some high overhead window. I looked up, and spotted the faceless Shadow walking along the opposite side of the street, the white leopardess trotting obediently at his heel.
I followed, gaining on them, pleading silently to the cat, begging her that I knew she was not a free agent, that she was being forced. But the second I drew close enough to reach for her, she spun and flew at me with a hideous, guttural snarl that made me flinch back. Instantly, she turned and resumed her place behind the Shadow. I tried again, and the same thing happened, her eyes blazing like raw emeralds lit from within, and this time she snapped and sank her teeth into my forearm. I yelped in pain, and she looked back at me with a glance so clear no words were needed: Why would you make me do that?
I turned away, angry at my own foolishness for wasting so much time. Night or no night, I decided, I would march straight for the palace I had seen from the central square, perched high above the city’s core, ringed with battlements so thick it looked more a fortress than a royal home. But when I reached it, I found its fortifications just as abandoned and crumbling as the city’s walls. It was clear no sentry had manned them for hundreds of years. Its great iron gates hung wide open, the rocky chasm before it that had once held a moat was long dry, choked with rubble. The whole palace was so still it felt like the entire structure was asleep, the bright moonlight seeming almost rude and awake against the heavy hush. I crept around its walls like a stray outcast dog, until I found a small recess holding a weathered stone bench, tucked myself in out of the biting wind, and fell fast asleep despite the cold.
I woke to something leaping onto my chest, licking my face with the rough, warm tongue of a feline. “It is the white leopardess,” I thought, resigned. “She has come to drink my blood. What do I care? Fighting to defend it would cost me far more than giving it up.” I lay still, waiting for the sharp burn of her fangs, but no pain came. Instead, a slow, pleasant warmth began seeping through my frozen clothes, thawing the ice from my bones. She was stretched full length against my back, pressed as close as she could get, the heat of her body wrapping around me like a blanket, her breath soft and sweet against my neck, with none of the feral stink of a wild beast. I knew, with absolute certainty, that she meant me no harm. I turned over like a sleepy child, slung my arm over her, and sank into deep, dreamless unconsciousness.
When I began to rouse, I half-believed I was back in my own soft bed at home. I could almost smell the roses from my old garden drifting through the open window. I rubbed my eyes, and blinked the fuzz away. I was lying on bare cold stone, in the heart of that hateful, half-dead city of Bulika. I jumped off the bench. Had the leopardess truly been my bedfellow, or had I dreamed the whole thing? She had only just left, for the warm imprint of her body still clung to my clothes, sunk deep into my skin. I stepped out of the recess with a new hope, bright and unshakable even though I could not name its shape. One thing alone was clear: I had to find the princess. Surely I had some power over her, if not over this strange world. Had I not saved her life, and had she not prolonged her own life at the cost of mine? The thought gave me all the courage I needed to face her, no matter what terrible form she might take.
