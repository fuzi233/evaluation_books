As I sat beside my aunt, watching the lawyer, Mr. Bruff, hastily finalize the Will, I couldn't help but feel a pang of unease. The air was thick with tension, and I sensed an opportunity slipping away. My mission to save her soul hung precariously in the balance, like a delicate pastry about to collapse under its own weight. I had carefully prepared for this moment, packing my bag with a selection of precious publications, each one a potent tool in the fight against the forces of darkness that threatened to consume us all.

The Will, once signed, was swiftly concluded, and Mr. Bruff handed it over to my aunt with a nod. As he departed, I seized the moment, producing from my bag a slim volume bound in worn leather. The cover creaked as I opened it, revealing the title page of a rare and wondrous work: "The Serpent at Home." Its author, the revered Miss Bellows, had penned a masterpiece of Christian literature, exposing the insidious ways in which Satan weaves his evil threads into the very fabric of our daily lives. I had chosen this particular edition – the twenty-fifth printing – specifically for its relevance to my aunt's situation. The chapters I had marked, "Satan in the Hair Brush," "Satan behind the Looking Glass," and "Satan under the Tea Table," would surely strike a chord with her, and lead her down the path of righteousness.

As I handed her the book, I emphasized the significance of this moment, speaking words of persuasion that flowed like honeyed nectar from my lips. "Give your attention, dear aunt, to this precious book, and you will give me all I ask." Her eyes scanned the pages, lingering on the title of the chapter: "Satan among the Sofa Cushions." A fleeting look of confusion crossed her face, and I sensed a glimmer of resistance. But I pressed on, undeterred, knowing that the fate of her soul hung in the balance.

Just then, my aunt mentioned the doctor's name, and I felt a familiar sense of foreboding. Those men in white coats, with their potions and their pills, always seemed to think themselves the sole guardians of our well-being. They would stop at nothing to silence the voices of warning, to stifle the whispers of the spirit. But I knew better.
