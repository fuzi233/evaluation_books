As the steamer chugged down the Nile, Calder gazed out at the desolate landscape, the scorching sun beating down relentlessly upon the barren rocks and sand. The air was thick with the acrid smell of the river's waters and the cries of the native passengers on the lower deck. Amidst the sea of faces, one figure caught his eye – a motionless form swathed in a black veil, laid out upon an angareb, a traditional matting bedstead, in the stern of the barge.

The angareb had been brought aboard at Korosko that morning, accompanied by two laughing Arabs, who seemed oblivious to the figure beneath the veil. Was it a man or a woman? Calder strained his eyes to discern, but the black fabric obscured all features except the outline of the nose, the hollows of the eyes, and the curve of the mouth. The slanting sunlight cast long shadows across the deck, but the figure remained still, a statue-like presence amidst the bustle of the passengers.

As the sun climbed higher, its rays danced across the angareb, casting an eerie glow on the recumbent form. The natives nearby moved into the shade, but the Arabs in the stern showed no concern for their charge. Calder felt an inexplicable fascination with the sight, and as the sun's rays crept higher, he called out to the Arabs, "Move the angareb into the shadow, please!" They grudgingly obliged, and Calder asked, "Is it a man or a woman?"

"It's a man," one of the Arabs replied. "We're taking him to the hospital in Assouan, but he won't make it. He fell from a palm tree three weeks ago." Calder's expression turned grave. "You haven't given him anything to eat or drink?" The Arab shook his head. "He's too ill."

This was a tale all too familiar to Calder – a story of neglect and suffering, where life and death were seen as predetermined. The man beneath the veil had likely begun with a minor injury, easily treatable with simple remedies. But he had been left to the mercy of the sun and the flies, his wounds festering, his thirst unquenched. Now, it seemed, it was too late. The Egyptian Mudir of Korosko had only recently discovered the accident, sending the man on the steamer to Assouan in a futile attempt to save him.
