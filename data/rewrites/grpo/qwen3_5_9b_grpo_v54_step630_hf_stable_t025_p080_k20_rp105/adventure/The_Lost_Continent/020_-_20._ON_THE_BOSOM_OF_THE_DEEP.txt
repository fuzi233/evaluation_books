The Ark was a vessel of silence and surrender. Stripped of rudders, oars, and engines, it drifted solely at the whim of the High Gods. Inside, darkness pressed against the hull, thick with the scent of antiquity and the low, rhythmic groans of the survivors. I could not locate the fire-box to reignite our lamps, so we were forced to navigate the void in blindness, accepting whatever fate the waves might deal. The sea tossed us in a merciless game, yet I clung tightly to Nais, keeping her anchored to the bed. Words were scarce between us, but in our shared grief and our heavy silence, there existed a profound companionship.

When Atlantis finally succumbed to the depths, she left behind great whirlpools and spouts of drowned fire—a fleeting testament to the Sea Gods. I believe, though the black belly of the Ark hid such sights from view, that a vast hurricane followed immediately after, ensuring no fragment of the desolation remained untouched. This dreadful turmoil raged for seven nights and seven days, as later confirmed by the hour-reckoner hanging within the Ark. Then, the howling wind ceased, leaving only the roll of a long, still swell. It was a regular, oily motion, distinct from the chaos before. Only then did I dare ascend the stairs to open the hatch in the roof.

Sweet air gushed down, washing away the foulness within. As the Ark rode dryly over the seas, I went below to bring Nais up, seeking refreshment from the curing rays of our Lord, the Sun. Together, we bowed in adoration, giving thanks for His mercy in granting another day of light. We lay down to doze, seeking the easy rest we so desperately needed.

Yet, exhaustion did not bring sleep. For a long time, I stared wearily at the oily, sunlit waters. No blur of land met my gaze; the ring of ocean was unbroken on every side, and the vault of heaven remained unchanged. The bosom of the deep was littered with the wreckage of Atlantis, serving as a grim reminder that this was fact, not a nightmare. Trees, squared timber, a smashed hide-boat, and here and there the rounded corpse of a man or beast shouldered over the swells, keeping convoy with our Ark as it drifted under the guidance of the Gods and the current.

Finally, sleep claimed me. I dropped into unconsciousness, holding Nais's hand, and when I awoke, she was open-eyed, watching me with tender concern. Both of us were finely rested; rest brings a strange complacency. We were now more ready to accept the station the High Gods had ordained for us without repining. We descended once more into the Ark's belly to eat and drink, fortifying ourselves for the new life awaiting us.

Now, able to examine it at leisure, the Ark revealed itself as a wonder. Though it was the first time in its centuries of existence that it swam freely upon the waters, it showed not a single leak or crack. Inside, even the floor was bone-dry. While the grain indicated it was built of wood, no suture or joint was visible. The living timbers had been placed and then grown together by an art we have since lost, known only to the Ancients with perfection. Subsequently, a treatment—another secret of those forgotten builders—had rendered the wood as hard as metal and impervious to the weather.

In the gloomy cave of its belly, provisions were stored. At one end, in great tanks flanking a central alley, lay a prodigious store of grain. Sweet water filled tanks at the opposite end. Elsewhere were drugs, samples, and essences of beast-life, all intended for use while the Ark roamed under divine guidance. Carved into every wall, partition, and tank in the rude art of bygone times were representations of every beast that lived in Atlantis. I studied them with a hunter's interest, noting creatures strange to me that had perished along with the men who had preserved them in stone. A good store of weapons and handicraft tools also lined the walls.

For many weeks, our life endured in this Ark as the Gods drove it across the face of the waters. We possessed no government to direct our course; we could not increase our speed by so much as a hair's breadth. The High Gods, who had chosen the two of us as the sole survivors of Atlantis, held sole control of our fate. Into Their hands, we cheerfully resigned our future.

Of the land we eventually reached, where we made our abiding place and where our children were born, I shall speak in its proper time. But since this chronicle must proceed in the exact order of events, I must first narrate how we came by the sheets upon which it is written.

In a great coffer at the center of the Ark's floor, the Mysteries learned over ages were set down in accurate writing. I read through portions of them during the passing days, and the awfulness of the Powers they described appalled me. I had witnessed some of these Powers unleashed in Atlantis, bearing witness to her destruction. But here were Powers far higher still; here was the Great Secret of Life and Death which Phorenice had discovered and for which she had been destroyed. There were other things of which I cannot even attempt to scribe.

The thought of being custodian to these writings became more than I could endure. The longer the matter rested in my mind, the more intolerable the burden grew. Finally, I took hot irons and seared the wax on the sheets until every letter of the old writings was obliterated. If I acted wrongly, the High Gods, in Their infinite justice, will punish me; if it is well that these great secrets should endure on earth, They, in Their infinite power, will dictate them afresh to some fitting scribes. Yet, I destroyed them there as the Ark swayed over the waves. Later, when we came to land, I rewrote upon the sheets only the matters leading to the great Atlantis being dragged to her death-throes.
