If our sharply angled Soldiers of the Third Class are formidable, it is readily inferred that our Women are forces of a terrifying magnitude. For whereas a Soldier is but a wedge, a Woman is a needle; she is, so to speak, pure point concentrated at both extremities. Combine this lethal geometry with the supernatural ability to render oneself practically invisible at will, and one perceives that a Female in Flatland is a creature to be reckoned with, not trifled with. Yet, perhaps my younger readers in Spaceland pause here, asking how a two-dimensional being can vanish. The answer lies in the very nature of our existence. Place a needle upon a table. View it from the side, and you behold its full, dangerous length; view it end-on, and it collapses into a mere speck, effectively erased from perception. Thus does a Woman operate. When her flank faces us, she appears as a straight line, a threat to be avoided; when her luminous vertex—our shared organ of sight and speech—meets our gaze, she becomes a blinding star, a point of intense radiance; but when she turns her back, presenting only her dull, sub-lustrous rear, she fades into the background, her hind extremity serving as a cap of invisibility.

The peril posed by such entities is obvious to the simplest mind. If the obtuse angle of a respectable Middle-Class Triangle can cause injury, if a collision with a Working Man guarantees a gash, if striking an Officer results in severe wounds, and if a mere brush with the vertex of a Private Soldier risks instant death, then to run headlong into a Woman is to invite absolute and immediate annihilation. How difficult, then, must it be for the most vigilant citizen to navigate a city populated by these shifting specters? Consequently, the various States of Flatland have enacted a labyrinthine code designed to mitigate this existential hazard. In the hotter southern latitudes, where gravity pulls harder and involuntary spasms are more frequent, these laws are draconian. Generally, however, three statutes suffice for the orderly realms: First, every dwelling possesses a single eastern entrance reserved exclusively for Females, ensuring they approach "in a becoming and respectful manner," distinct from the western portals used by Males. Second, no Woman may traverse a public space without maintaining a constant, audible "Peace-cry," punishable by death for silence. Third, any Female afflicted with conditions causing involuntary motion—St. Vitus's Dance, violent sneezing, or similar afflictions—is summarily destroyed. Some states go further, mandating that women shuffle their backs from right to left to signal presence, or requiring a male escort, or confining them entirely within domestic walls save for religious festivals. Yet, the wisest legislators have recognized that excessive restriction breeds resentment. When confined or hampered, the Female spirit turns inward, venting its fury upon kin; in extreme cases, a single village has been wiped out in moments by a collective maternal outbreak. Therefore, the state relies not solely on law, but on the self-preservation of the Women themselves. Though they can strike down a victim with a backward thrust, they risk shattering their own fragile forms if they cannot disengage quickly. Furthermore, the tyranny of Fashion acts as a silent guardian. The rhythmic, modulated undulation of the back, once a legal requirement in uncivilized lands, has evolved into a natural instinct among the bred classes. From the wife of the humble Equilateral, who mimics the pendulum-like swing of her betters, to the aspirant Isosceles copying the graceful waves of the Circular elite, this "back motion" permeates every household of status. Husbands and sons in these homes enjoy a shielded immunity, spared the invisible sting of the unseen.

Do not mistake this caution for a lack of affection. Unfortunately, in the Frail Sex, the passion of the moment overrides reason. Deprived of angles, they possess no brain-power, lacking reflection, judgment, foresight, or memory. In their rages, they forget all claims and distinctions. I have witnessed a mother exterminate her entire household, only to wake an hour later, calm and confused, asking where her husband and children had gone. Thus, a Woman must never be provoked while capable of turning; once cornered in an apartment designed to deny her rotation, she becomes harmless, unable to retaliate and too amnesiac to remember the threats she issued moments prior. Domestic life proceeds smoothly in the upper echelons, barring the chaotic lower Military Classes, where husbands' reliance on their own sharp angles rather than diplomatic finesse leads to disaster. These reckless males, ignoring the proper construction of women's quarters and refusing to retract their harsh words, invite massacre. Paradoxically, this destruction serves a providential function, culling the brutish Isosceles and curbing revolutionary fervor. Even in the most harmonious Circular families, however, the ideal falls short of Spaceland standards. We trade slaughter for stagnation. Safety comes at the cost of comfort. In every distinguished household, it is instinctual for mothers and daughters to keep their eyes and mouths permanently fixed upon their husbands and male guests. To turn one's back upon the Master of the house is a portent of lost Status. While the working classes enjoy brief intervals of silence amidst the hum of the Peace-cry, the homes of the elite are ceaseless theaters of noise. The voluble mouth and piercing eye are ever directed outward, creating a torrent of discourse that no amount of tactical evasion can stop. Cynics argue they would rather face the silent, deadly sting than endure the safe, sonorous barrage of a Woman's endless chatter. To the citizens of Spaceland, our condition seems tragic, and indeed it is. A Male of the lowest Isosceles caste may hope for evolutionary advancement, but no Woman can dream of rising above her station. "Once a Woman, always a Woman" is the immutable decree of Nature; evolution itself seems to suspend its progress in her disfavour. Yet, we find a grim comfort in this prearranged design: since they hold no hopes for the future, they require no memory to recall past humiliations and no forethought to anticipate future misery. Their existence is a closed loop of perpetual present, stripped of the burden of recollection.
