My attempt to diffuse the Theory of Three Dimensions through alternative channels yielded results far less encouraging than my initial optimism warranted. The failure of my efforts with my Grandson did not, however, plunge me into despair; rather, it crystallized a new strategy. I realized that reliance on the cryptic maxim—"Upward, not Northward"—was insufficient. True enlightenment required a comprehensive demonstration, a visual exposition of the entire subject matter. Consequently, I resolved to turn to the written word. For several months, sequestered in solitude, I composed a treatise on the mysteries of the Third Dimension. In a calculated effort to circumvent the Law, I deliberately avoided referencing a physical dimension. Instead, I described a realm of pure thought, a "Thoughtland," wherein a figure could gaze downward upon Flatland, perceiving the interiors of all objects simultaneously. Within this theoretical construct, I posited the existence of a figure enveloped by six squares and terminating in eight points. Yet, as I penned these lines, I encountered a formidable obstacle: the inability to draw the requisite diagrams. In Flatland, our medium is strictly linear; we possess no tablets but lines, and no geometric illustrations but single strokes distinguished solely by variations in magnitude and luminosity. Thus, when I completed my manuscript, titled *Through Flatland to Thoughtland*, I harbored grave doubts regarding its intelligibility.

Meanwhile, my existence descended into a somber haze. Every pleasure lost its savor; every sight became a tantalizing provocation to treason, for I could not suppress the urge to contrast the deceptive two-dimensional reality with the true three-dimensional essence, and I struggled to keep these comparisons silent. I neglected my clientele and my professional duties to devote myself to the contemplation of the mysteries I had witnessed yet could not convey. The task of reproducing these visions within my own mind grew increasingly arduous each day. One evening, roughly eleven months after my return from Spaceland, I attempted to visualize a cube with my eyes closed, but I failed. Though I eventually succeeded, I remained uncertain of the accuracy of my reconstruction—a doubt that has plagued me ever since. This failure deepened my melancholy and compelled me to take decisive action, though the specific path remained obscure. I felt a willingness to sacrifice my very life for the Cause, provided it could generate conviction. Yet, if I could not persuade my Grandson, how could I expect to sway the most advanced Circles of the land? Nevertheless, my spirit occasionally surged beyond restraint, leading to perilous outbursts. I was already branded heterodox, bordering on treason, acutely aware of the precariousness of my position. Still, I could not prevent myself from erupting into suspicious or semi-seditious declarations even among the highest Polygonal and Circular societies. When debates arose concerning the treatment of lunatics claiming to perceive the interiors of things, I quoted an ancient Circle stating that prophets are invariably deemed mad by the majority. I could not resist slipping phrases like "the eye that discerns the interiors" or "the all-seeing land" into conversation. On rare occasions, I even uttered the forbidden terms "the Third and Fourth Dimensions."

Finally, driven by a culmination of minor indiscretions, I committed a fatal error at a gathering of our Local Speculative Society convened at the Prefect's palace. An individual of questionable intellect had presented a paper arguing that Providence had intentionally limited dimensions to two and reserved omniscience for the Supreme Alone. Overcome by passion, I recounted the entirety of my voyage with the Sphere into Space, my journey to the Assembly Hall in our metropolis, and my subsequent return, detailing every sight and sound experienced in both fact and vision. Initially, I feigned that I was narrating the fictional experiences of a hypothetical persona, but my fervor quickly stripped away this disguise. In a climactic conclusion, I exhorted my audience to shed their prejudices and embrace belief in the Third Dimension. Naturally, I was immediately arrested and brought before the Council.

The following morning, standing in the very chamber where the Sphere had recently appeared beside me, I was granted permission to speak without interruption. From the outset, I perceived my impending doom. The President, observing a guard composed of high-quality policemen—figures of angularity barely below fifty-five degrees—ordered them replaced by an inferior class, possessing angles only two or three degrees greater. I understood the implication perfectly: I faced execution or imprisonment, and my narrative would be erased from the collective memory by the simultaneous silencing of the witnesses. The President sought to substitute cheap victims for costly ones. Upon concluding my defense, the President, perhaps sensing that some junior Circles had been swayed by my sincerity, posed two final questions: First, could I indicate the direction implied by "Upward, not Northward"? Second, could I describe the figure I termed a "Cube" using diagrams or descriptions other than enumerating imaginary sides and angles? I declared my inability to answer further, committing myself entirely to the Truth, trusting that its cause would ultimately prevail. The President agreed with my sentiment, declaring that I could do no better. I was sentenced to perpetual imprisonment. However, he added that if the Truth destined me to emerge and evangelize the world, the Truth itself would ensure that outcome. Until then, I would suffer no unnecessary discomfort, barring attempts at escape, and, absent misconduct, I would be periodically permitted to visit my brother, who had preceded me to this confinement.

Seven years have now passed, and I remain a prisoner, isolated from all companionship save that of my jailers and the occasional visitations of my brother. He is a Square of exceptional integrity, sensibility, cheerfulness, and genuine fraternal affection. Yet, I confess that our weekly meetings inflict upon me the deepest anguish. He was present when the Sphere manifested in the Council Chamber; he witnessed the shifting cross-sections and heard the explanations given to the Circles. Despite this, scarcely a week has gone by in these seven years without my recounting my role in that event, accompanied by detailed accounts of Spaceland's phenomena and analogical arguments for solid bodies. And yet—I blush to admit it—my brother has never grasped the nature of the Third Dimension. He openly avows his disbelief in the existence of a Sphere. Consequently, I stand utterly devoid of converts, and the millennial Revelation appears to have been bestowed upon me in vain. Prometheus ascended to Spaceland to bind fire for mortals, but I, the poor Flatland Prometheus, lie here in chains, delivering nothing to my compatriots.

Nonetheless, I cling to the hope that these memoirs, through some unknown mechanism, may reach the minds of humanity in some higher Dimension, inspiring a generation of rebels unwilling to accept the confines of limited dimensionality. This is the hope of my brightest moments. Alas, darkness often overtakes me. The burden of uncertainty weighs heavily; I cannot honestly claim confidence in the exact shape of the cube I once beheld. In my nightly visions, the enigmatic command "Upward, not Northward" haunts me like a soul-consuming Sphinx. It is part of the martyrdom I endure for the Truth that there are seasons of mental frailty when cubes and spheres dissolve into the background of improbable existence. During these times, the Land of Three Dimensions seems as illusory as the Land of One or None. Indeed, even this impenetrable wall separating me from freedom, the very tablets upon which I write, and the tangible realities of Flatland itself, seem no more than the progeny of a diseased imagination or the tenuous fabric of a dream.
