As the flotilla navigated the treacherous waters of the Caribbean, the seasoned mariners, Yeo and Drew, expertly guided their vessel south-west across the vast bay, leaving behind the island of Tortuga to their right and the meadow-islands of the Piritoos to their left. Their familiarity with the coastline, honed from previous trading expeditions with Hawkins, allowed them to chart a precise course. Ahead, the imposing Caracas mountains rose majestically from the sea, dwarfing the hills of their native England. For Frank, accustomed to the Alps, and Amyas, familiar with the Andes, the sheer scale of these South American peaks was awe-inspiring. Meanwhile, Cary's notions of height were still limited by the rugged terrain of Ireland's M'Gillicuddy's Reeks, and Brimblecombe's experience with Exmoor paled in comparison.

As the sea began to churn and the wind picked up, the crew encountered the notorious eastward current that often foiled the plans of mariners attempting to sail westward. Despite the challenging conditions, the breeze remained gentle, and the crew pressed on, their vessel buffeted by the waves. Under the light of the full moon, the towering wall of Cape Codera loomed above them to the left, its rugged silhouette a testament to the unforgiving power of the region's geology.

The next morning, a large ship was spotted on the horizon, sailing in the opposite direction. Yeo advocated for a change in course to intercept the stranger, citing the importance of completing their mission to La Guayra. However, Amyas and Frank were hesitant, prioritizing their original objective over the potential benefits of capturing the Spanish vessel. Frank argued that their primary goal was to rescue the individual believed to be held captive in La Guayra, and that diverting their attention would only serve to delay their arrival.

Amyas, torn between his duty to the queen and his personal vow, ultimately decided to follow Frank's counsel and continue their journey eastward. Although he secretly hoped that the Spanish ship might attack them, he chose to press on, aware that his decision would significantly alter the course of events. The crew spotted a small canoe approaching under a distinctive three-cornered sail, its presence marking the beginning of a series of events that would shape the fate of their expedition.
