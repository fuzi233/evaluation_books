The sun dipped low over the bay of Santa Marta, painting the waters in molten gold. The air hummed with the song of cicadas, and fireflies winked like scattered embers in the gathering dusk. High above, the snow-capped peak of Horqueta blushed rose-red against the darkening sky before fading to a somber gray. Night fell swiftly, and Venus, radiant as a second moon, cast a shimmering path across the tranquil sea.

In the opulent cabin of the galleon *City of the True Cross*, the Bishop of Cartagena lounged in languid comfort, flanked by his guests—Don Pedro, the garrison commander; Don Alverez, the intendant of customs; and Don Paul, the ship’s captain. Two friars sat in deferential silence at the table’s end, their eyes downcast.

The bishop sighed, swirling his goblet of Alicant wine. "Ah, my friends, we have suffered greatly—our city sacked, our treasures plundered by those English devils. Yet Scripture reminds us: *There shall be a remnant*—a remnant like the gleaning of grapes after the harvest." He gestured grandly toward the shore. "Look! Even now, the land whispers of gold. The very waves sing of it!"

Don Alverez inclined his head. "Your wisdom consoles us, Excellency."

The bishop preened. "A poet’s soul feels deeply. Yet I am but a humble servant of the Church." He snapped his fingers at the Indian girl, Tita. "Fill their glasses. Tell me, Don Alverez, does more treasure come from the mines?"

"Alas, no. The mountain is milked dry."

The bishop sighed again, peering at the distant Horqueta. "Fit only to cool our wine now. Ah, but such is life—fleeting as Solomon’s birds."

"Solomon’s birds?" queried Don Pedro.

The bishop chuckled. "Once, hoopoes wore crowns of gold—until men hunted them without mercy. They begged King Solomon to change their fate, and lo! Their golden crowns became feathers, and they lived in peace." He smirked. "Would that all gold turned so harmless."

Fray Gerundio, the quiet Dominican, muttered, "Then men would find new vanities to kill for."

The bishop shot him a withering glance. "Convert your Indians, friar, and leave statecraft to your betters."

The conversation turned to Drake’s recent raid.

"Perfidious dogs!" the bishop spat. "They drank my own wine—my finest Xeres!—then demanded ransom for the priory after sacking the town!"

Don Pedro grunted. "They outmaneuvered us. Who’d have thought they’d attack from the beach?"

"Devilry," the bishop declared. "No Christian fights so."

As the night wore on, wine loosened tongues. The intendant lamented Cartagena’s ruined defenses; the captain cursed the English pikes, longer than their own.

At last, the bishop yawned. "Enough. I am weary. Tita, my hammock."

The guests departed, and the bishop, muttering prayers, clutched a small casket of jewels. "Perhaps enough for a cardinal’s hat," he mused before sleep took him.

---

Midnight passed. The ship lay silent, sentinels dozing at their posts.

Tita crept to the cabin door, where the intendant waited.

"He sleeps," she whispered. "But the casket is beneath his head."

The intendant’s eyes gleamed. "Then take it."

Tita bared her teeth. "I will—if you swear to marry me."

He hesitated, then kissed her crucifix. "I swear."

Knife in hand, she approached the hammock—but the bishop awoke with a start.

Before he could cry out, figures surged from the shadows—Englishmen! A giant (Amyas Leigh) seized the intendant, gagging him. Others bound the bishop, while more swarmed the decks.

"Fire!" Cary shouted, driving panicked Spaniards into the open.

Within minutes, the galleon’s crew, half-naked and unarmed, were herded into boats and cast adrift.

Amyas grinned. "Now, lads—let’s see what this prize holds!"

The sails unfurled, the anchor cut, the *City of the True Cross* glided free—her treasures now bound for England.
