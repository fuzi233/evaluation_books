This chronicle unfolds in the wind-scoured, salt-kissed corners of Elizabethan Devon, where Atlantic gales carry the sharp tang of distant uncharted shores and every cottage hearth holds a sailor’s half-spun tale of gold, glory, and ghostly omens seen far out beyond the horizon. These are the true, vivid adventures that will unfurl across the pages ahead, stitched from quayside letters, tavern tales, and parish records of the bold men of Bideford and Clovelly who sailed west to claim their fortune under the flag of the Virgin Queen:
First, you shall learn how Plymouth mariner Mr. John Oxenham spied a strange white bird wheeling over empty ocean, the unshakable omen that would lead him to his tragic, unmarked fate on the Spanish Main. Next comes the homecoming of fiery, sea-bitten Amyas Leigh, stepping off the deck of a returning merchant man for the first time since boyhood, already hungry for the voyages he has dreamed of through every long, storm-tossed journey.
You will meet two quick-witted Welsh gentlemen who run with the hounds one hour and vanish like the hunted deer the next, and learn of the two very different ways a heart can be crossed in love: one soft and sorrowful enough to break a gentle man, one sharp enough to turn a pious young scholar astray for decades. We will step across the threshold of Clovelly Court in the days when it was a safe haven for privateers and sea-rovers, and wander the wild, untamed combes of Devon’s far western edge, where gorse blooms gold all summer and outlaws hide from the king’s men in hollowed-out cliff caves.
We shall recount the grim, tragical full history of John Oxenham, whose name would become legend across every Devon quayside, before joining the noble Brotherhood of the Rose: that secret, oath-bound band of mariners who swore to stand together against Spaniard, storm, and the cruel officers of the Inquisition. You will sit at the raucous Christmas feast Amyas throws for his ragtag crew, watch the Mayor of Bideford strike a devil’s bargain that he will come to bitterly regret, and follow zealous young Eustace Leigh as he crosses paths with the Pope’s own legate deep in a European seminary.
There is a feast for the ages to attend, when the entire company of Bideford Bridge dines at the great manor of Annery, before we all line Plymouth’s docks to cheer as Sir Francis Drake’s legendary *Golden Hind* sails back into harbour, heavy with New World plunder. We will hear the old wild tale of Salvation Yeo, the puritan mariner who slew the cursed king of the local outlaw band the Gubbings, before we learn how simple, honest Mr. John Brimblecombe learned exactly how sacred an oath sworn before God truly is.
We shall sail across the Atlantic ourselves aboard the good ship *Rose*, through her most chivalric, daring adventure of all, making landfall on Barbados only to find the island entirely empty of all human inhabitants. Next we raid the pearl fisheries of Margarita, outrun Spanish cannon fire at La Guayra, and witness the brutal clash between Spanish war bloodhounds and the great English mastiffs the mariners brought with them for the fight. We will kneel with the whole crew to take communion beneath a gnarled old higuerote tree on a foreign shore, and face the unspeakable horrors of the Spanish Inquisition running rampant across the Indies.
Our voyage will carry us up the green, snake-haunted banks of the River Meta, where Amyas will be tested by the most terrible temptation the devil can lay before him, before we fall upon the great Spanish gold train hauling plunder across the jungle, and capture the vast, treasure-laden great galleon that no Englishman had ever dared seize before. It is there that Salvation Yeo, who had searched for his stolen little daughter for decades, will find her at last, alive and well.
When Amyas makes his third and final homecoming to Devon, he finds the kingdom in turmoil: the great Virginia colonial fleet is held back by the Queen’s own urgent command, and old veteran Admiral John Hawkins speaks out fiercely against all the cowards and croakers who say England cannot stand against Spain’s coming invasion. The Great Armada, that fabled fleet of a hundred galleons, sails up the Channel, and in the heat of the final battle Amyas will cast his sword into the foaming waves, his long quest over, his penance done. At last, the apple he has waited twenty years to see fall will drop from the bough, and his storm-battered life will find its quiet rest.
*Westward Ho* — the cry that sent a generation of Englishmen sailing over the horizon, to write their names into the uncharted history of the New World.
