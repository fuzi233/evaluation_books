Suddenly, I jolted awake, my eyes straining through the flap of my tent onto the desolate expanse outside. My wristwatch, pinned haphazardly against the canvas, glowed under the pale light of the full moon. It was well past midnight—the hour where sleep surrenders to waking terror—and yet, I realized with a sinking stomach, I had barely closed my eyes for two hours. Beside me, the Swede lay in a fitful slumber, his breathing heavy and ragged like the gale that howled relentlessly around us. But the wind alone could not explain the chill crawling up my spine, nor the frantic hammering of my own heart. Something was wrong here, in this immediate circle of darkness, a disturbance that vibrated in the air like a plucked string.

I threw off the covers and scrambled to the edge of the tent, peering out into the chaos. The trees were thrashing, their branches whipping back and forth as the gusts battered them with brutal force. Yet, miraculously, our small patch of green canvas remained anchored, nestled safely in the hollow, unscathed by the fury above. The wind roared over it, offering no resistance strong enough to tear it away. Still, the unease gnawed at me, a cold knot in my gut that refused to loosen. I crawled out of the tent, moving with deliberate silence so as not to rouse my sleeping companion, driven by a strange, electric excitement that made my skin prickle.

Halfway out, dropping to my knees on the coarse sand, I froze. My gaze caught the tops of the bushes directly opposite, their leafy tracery dancing wildly against the black sky. I sat back on my heels, staring, unable to breathe. It was impossible, yet there, standing amidst the willows and slightly elevated above them, were shapes. As the branches swayed, these forms seemed to coalesce, arranging themselves into grotesque, shifting silhouettes that twisted rapidly beneath the lunar glare. They were close, perhaps fifty feet away, looming out of the gloom.

My first, reflexive urge was to wake the Swede, to share this vision, but a sudden, paralyzing hesitation stopped me. Some primal voice whispered that I would not welcome confirmation from anyone else—that the truth needed to remain mine alone. So, I crouched there, staring in a daze, my eyes stinging with the strain of seeing what I dared not fully believe. *Not a dream,* I told myself, the words echoing in the hollow space of my mind. *I am wide awake.*

Then, slowly, the impossible became clear. These were not mere shadows cast by the swaying boughs. Emerging from behind the brush, towering immense and colossal, were figures of a bronze, metallic hue. They moved with a jerky, fluid independence, entirely separate from the chaotic dance of the trees. As I watched, mesmerized, I noted with growing horror that they were far larger than any man, and their proportions defied nature. They were not human. They shifted and flowed, rising in a continuous, spiraling stream from the earth straight up into the starless sky, vanishing completely the moment they pierced the upper darkness. They were interlaced, a single, writhing column of flesh and spirit, their limbs and massive bodies melting into one another, twisting in a serpentine helix that mirrored the convulsions of the wind-lashed forest. They were naked, fluid entities pouring upward, weaving *within* the leaves themselves, ascending in a living pillar toward the heavens. I could never see their faces; they were smooth, featureless masks of terror.

They poured upward unceasingly, bending in great, sweeping curves, their skin a dull, sickly bronze. I stared, forcing every ounce of focus from my weary eyes, trying to find a trick, a shadow play. For a long time, I convinced myself they must dissolve instantly into the movement of the branches, proving nothing but an optical delusion. I searched desperately for evidence of reality, yet deep down, I knew the scale had shifted. The longer I looked, the more certain I became: these figures were alive, breathing, and undeniably present, even if they existed outside the laws of biology or physics.

Far from trembling, I was seized by a profound, terrifying awe, a wonder I had never known before. It felt as though I was witnessing the elemental spirits of this cursed, primordial land given form. Our intrusion had stirred the ancient slumber of the place into violent activity. We were the cause of this awakening, and my mind raced, filling to the brim with myths and legends of gods and demons worshipped by men in ages past. But before I could construct a rational explanation, an impulsion drove me forward. I crept further out on the sand, forcing myself to stand upright. The ground beneath my bare feet still hummed with a residual warmth; the wind lashed my hair and stung my face; and the roar of the river crashed against my ears with a sudden, deafening violence. These sensory inputs screamed that I was sane, that my senses were functioning correctly. And yet, there they stood—or rather, they floated—in a great spiral of terrible grace and strength, silent and majestic, overwhelming me with a deep, primal emotion of worship. I felt compelled to kneel, to bow absolutely before these beings.

Perhaps I would have fallen to my knees in reverence in another minute, had not a sudden, crushing gust of wind swept against me, knocking me sideways and nearly sending me sprawling. It shook the trance violently from me, if only for a second. It offered me a new angle, a fractured perspective. The figures still hovered there, ascending from the heart of the night, but my reason, stubborn and cruel, began to claw its way back to the surface. *It must be subjective,* I argued, fighting the paralysis. *Real, yes, but merely a projection of my own broken mind.* The moonlight and the swaying branches were working together to paint these horrors onto the mirror of my imagination, which I had foolishly projected outward. I knew I had to believe this. I gathered what courage I could and began to step forward across the open patches of sand. *By Jove,* I thought, the doubt creeping in like ice water, *is it all a hallucination? Is it merely a nightmare?* Did not my reason argue in the old, futile way, clinging to the tiny, fragile standards of the "known"?

All I know for certain is that that great column of bronze figures ascended darkly into the sky for what seemed an eternity, existing with a completeness of reality that most men take for granted. And then, without warning, they vanished. Gone. Instantly erased from the landscape.

And the moment they disappeared, the spell broke. The immediate, blinding wonder of their presence faded, and fear rushed down upon me like a freezing flood. The esoteric, bloody meaning of this lonely, haunted region flamed up within me, burning away my sanity. I began to tremble with a dread that shook my bones to the marrow. I cast a quick, frantic glance around—a look of pure horror bordering on panic—as I calculated vain, desperate ways to escape. Realizing how utterly helpless I was to effect anything meaningful, I turned and crept back silently into the tent. I lay down again upon my sandy mattress, pulling the door-curtain down low to blot out the sight of the willows in the moonlight, and buried my face as deep as possible beneath the thin blankets, praying to deaden the sound of the terrifying wind that continued to scream outside.
