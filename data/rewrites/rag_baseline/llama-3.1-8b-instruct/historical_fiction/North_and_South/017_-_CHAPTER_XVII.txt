The fog crept through the narrow streets like a living entity, shrouding the dilapidated tenements and the bustling thoroughfares of Manchester in a damp, grey mist. As Margaret emerged from the doorway of her lodgings, the chill air enveloped her like a damp shroud, weighing heavily upon her shoulders. Yet, with each step, her feet seemed to find a rhythm on the worn cobblestones, her heart gradually lifting from the morose thoughts that had beset her since her arrival in this unforgiving city. The air was alive with the hum of conversation and the clanging of hammers, the cacophony weaving itself into a tapestry of sound that was both jarring and invigorating.

Her eyes, accustomed to the stark contrasts of the southern landscape, wandered over the unfamiliar faces that crowded the sidewalks. Men with their hands thrust deep into their pockets strolled by, their laughter carrying on the wind, while groups of girls clustered together, their voices raised in a chorus of excitement and independence. Further along, the disreputable denizens of the city's underbelly loitered on the steps of the beerhouses and gin shops, their gazes roving over the passersby with a mixture of curiosity and disdain. Margaret's heart sank at the thought of the long walk ahead, the prospect of traversing this unforgiving terrain before reaching the open fields she had been longing to see.

As she hesitated, a gentle voice called out from within the small, smoke-filled cottage that stood before her. Bessy Higgins beckoned her inside, her eyes red-rimmed from fatigue, her words laced with a quiet desperation. "Come in, Margaret, come in. I've been waiting for you." The warmth of the fire and the scent of woodsmoke enveloped her as she entered, Nicholas Higgins rising from his chair to greet her. His eyes, a deep, piercing brown, sparkled with a fierce intensity as he gestured for her to sit.

As they conversed, the fire crackling softly between them, Margaret learned of the strike that had brought the city to a standstill. Bessy's words were laced with a weariness born of hardship and struggle, her spirit battered by the relentless demands of her daily existence. Nicholas, on the other hand, exuded a quiet confidence, his words dripping with a sense of defiance and determination. "We won't be cowed," he said, his voice low and even.
