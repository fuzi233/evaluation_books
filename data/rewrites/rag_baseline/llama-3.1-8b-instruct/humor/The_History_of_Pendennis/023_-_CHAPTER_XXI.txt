In the rarefied atmosphere of the University of Oxbridge, a curious dynamic unfolded between the enigmatic Major Pendennis and his nephew, Arthur. As a man of discerning taste, the Major was drawn to the young man's effervescent personality, marked by a blend of candor and refinement. Through a series of carefully orchestrated social interactions, the Major cultivated a paternal affection for Arthur, often going to great lengths to lavish praise upon his nephew's intellect and charm. During his visits to the university, the Major would regale the young men with witty anecdotes and astute observations, his mannerisms imbuing the atmosphere with an air of conviviality. He attended debates, cheering and applauding with reckless abandon, and was particularly taken with Arthur's oratory prowess, likening him to the illustrious William Pitt. In his letters, the Major would offer playful advice and share the latest gossip, further solidifying his bond with Arthur. As the Major navigated the rarefied world of high society, he couldn't help but tout his nephew's prospects, predicting that the younger generation would eclipse their forebears in grandeur. In his zeal to promote Arthur's interests, the Major extended invitations to esteemed gatherings, such as the grand fête at Gaunt House, where he delighted in watching his nephew dance with the sisters of the young nobility.
