Silence enveloped the small study, a heavy blanket that stifled sound and movement. Mr. Crisparkle sat at his desk, surrounded by stacks of dusty books and the faint scent of pipe smoke. His mother, Mrs. Crisparkle, knitted in the corner, her needles clicking out a steady rhythm as she worked. The tension between them was palpable, a strain that vibrated like the strings of a harp.

"Ma," Mr. Crisparkle began, his voice low and measured, "I've been thinking... perhaps we've been a bit unfair to Mr. Neville."

Mrs. Crisparkle's hands paused, her eyes narrowing as she regarded her son. "I don't see why you're bringing this up now, Sept," she said, her tone even but firm.

"I'm not trying to defend him, Ma," Mr. Crisparkle continued, "but I think we should consider the circumstances. He was provoked, and perhaps a bit...influenced by the wine."

Mrs. Crisparkle's gaze didn't waver. "I don't think that excuses his behavior, Sept. He disgraced this house and showed disrespect to our family."

Mr. Crisparkle nodded, his eyes cast downward. "I agree, Ma. He was wrong to act as he did. But perhaps we should also consider his regret and apology. He's made amends, and I think he's genuinely sorry for his actions."

Mrs. Crisparkle's expression remained unyielding, her jaw set in a determined line. "I'll not be swayed by empty apologies, Sept. Mr. Neville's actions speak louder than his words."

The silence between them grew thicker, a heavy fog that seemed to cling to every surface. Mr. Crisparkle sighed, his shoulders sagging beneath the weight of his mother's disapproval. In the end, it was clear that Mrs. Crisparkle's opinion would not be swayed, and the conversation hung in the air like a still life, frozen in time.
