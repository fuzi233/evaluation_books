In the vast expanse of time, where generations blend like the hues of a sunset, our tale begins anew, its threads weaving through the fabric of a bygone era. The memories of our journey to Sitanda's Kraal, nestled where the Lukanga and Kalukwe Rivers converge, remain etched in my mind like the intricate patterns on a tribal mask. From Durban, we set forth at the end of January, embarking on a thousand-mile odyssey that would test our resolve and push us to the limits of human endurance.

As we traversed the unforgiving terrain, the biting tsetse fly became our nemesis, forcing us to abandon our wagons and continue on foot. Our party had dwindled, leaving us with only twelve oxen from the original span of twenty that I had purchased in Durban. One succumbed to the deadly venom of a cobra, while others fell prey to the ravages of poverty and thirst. The tulip plant, with its deceptively sweet allure, proved a double-edged sword, claiming three of our oxen and sicken five more. Yet, thanks to the resourcefulness of our companions, we were able to concoct an antidote from the boiled leaves, saving those who had fallen ill.

With a heavy heart, we parted ways with our trusty wagon, leaving it in the capable hands of Goza and Tom, our loyal driver and leader, along with a wise Scotch missionary who watched over them from afar. As we ventured forth on foot, accompanied by Umbopa, Khiva, Ventvögel, and a handful of local bearers, a sense of foreboding settled over us. Each of us wondered if we would ever lay eyes on our beloved wagon again, and I confess that I had little hope of seeing it once more.

Umbopa, ever the optimist, broke the silence with a lilting Zulu chant, his voice carrying through the stillness of the savannah. He spoke of brave men who, disillusioned with the mundane world, embarked on a perilous journey into the unknown, only to discover a hidden paradise teeming with life and abundance. His words brought a smile to our faces, and we took it as a portent of good fortune. Umbopa's presence was a balm to our spirits, and we grew to cherish his company, despite the occasional darkness that clouded his gaze.
