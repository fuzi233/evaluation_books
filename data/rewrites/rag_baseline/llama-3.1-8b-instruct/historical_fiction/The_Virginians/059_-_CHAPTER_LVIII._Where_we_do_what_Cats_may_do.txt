The morning light seeped through the windows of Kensington Palace, casting an eerie glow on the polished floors. A hackney-coach, hired by Mr. Warrington and Mr. Lambert, rattled to a stop outside the grand entrance. They alighted, their footsteps echoing off the stone façade as they approached the palace gates. The sentries, familiar with the General's face, nodded in recognition and allowed them to pass.

As they walked beneath the portico, the sounds of the city gave way to the soft murmur of conversation within the palace. They entered the gallery, passing beneath a sweeping archway adorned with intricate carvings. The walls, lined with rich tapestries and paintings, seemed to whisper stories of a bygone era. The air was heavy with the scent of old wood and dust.

Their footsteps led them through a series of rooms, each one more opulent than the last. They paused before a magnificent black marble staircase, its surface glinting in the morning light. The staircase, a masterpiece of Mr. Kent's craftsmanship, seemed to stretch up to the heavens like a dark, majestic ribbon.

Beyond the staircase lay the great drawing-room, a space filled with some of the most exquisite works of art the world had to offer. The "Venus" by Titian gazed out upon them, her beauty almost otherworldly. Nearby, the gentle face of Saint Francis, painted by Sir Peter Paul Rubens, seemed to radiate a sense of peace. The room was alive with the quiet chatter of courtiers and ladies-in-waiting, all waiting for the monarch to emerge from his private chambers.

George Warrington, still adjusting to the grandeur surrounding him, felt a mix of awe and trepidation. He had never set foot in a palace before, and the sheer scale of the decorations and the people overwhelmed him. His eyes wandered, taking in the busts and portraits that adorned the walls, each one a testament to the storied history of the monarchy. The sound of rustling silk and the murmur of conversation created a soothing background hum, a reminder that even in the midst of such grandeur, there existed a sense of normalcy.
