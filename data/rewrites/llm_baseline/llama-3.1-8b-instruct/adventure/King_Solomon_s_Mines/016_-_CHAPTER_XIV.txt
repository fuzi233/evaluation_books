The sun beat down upon the dusty plain, casting a golden glow over the regiments assembled before us. In a silence born of anticipation, the troops destined for the flanking movements disappeared into the rising ground, their footsteps swallowed by the landscape as they moved to conceal their advance from the watchful eyes of Twala's scouts. Half an hour passed, a span of time measured by the steady tick of the wind and the occasional cry of a bird, before the Grey Regiment and its supporting unit, the Buffaloes, stirred into action.

Their stillness had given way to an expectant quietude, like the calm before a storm, as the two regiments prepared to face the battle ahead. Both were fresh and strong, the Greys having been held in reserve earlier, and the Buffaloes having played a minor role in the defense, their only casualties the result of a brief skirmish when I charged with them, leaving me stunned but alive. Infadoos, a seasoned general with a deep understanding of the importance of morale on the eve of a critical engagement, took this pause to address his troops, his words dripping with poetic flair as he extolled the honor bestowed upon them by being placed at the forefront of the battle, and by fighting alongside the great white warrior from the stars.

As I gazed down the lines of stern faces beneath their waving black plumes, my heart swelled with a mix of emotions – awe, admiration, and a hint of envy. These veterans, not a man among them under forty, were about to march into the jaws of death, their fate sealed by the wise and ruthless hand of their commander. They knew their role – to hold the line, to wear down Twala's army on the narrow strip of green below, until they were annihilated or until the flanking regiments found an opening to strike. Yet, as I watched, I saw no fear, no trepidation, only a resolute determination etched on every face.

Old Infadoos' words hung in the air as he pointed to Ignosi, his voice booming across the plain. "Behold your king! Fight and fall for him, as is the duty of brave men, and may shame be upon the coward who turns his back on death for his king." A moment's silence followed, broken by the soft tap of spear handles against shields, a gentle whisper that grew into a deafening roar as the regiments responded to their commander's call.
