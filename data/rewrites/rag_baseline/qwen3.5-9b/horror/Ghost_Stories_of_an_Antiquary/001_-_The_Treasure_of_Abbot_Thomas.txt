"St. Bertrand-de-Comminges and Viborg exist," I noted, committing these geographical anchors to record. "Felixstowe served as the model for 'Oh, Whistle, and I'll Come to You.' However, regarding the scholarly footnotes scattered throughout this volume—fragments of ostensible erudition—they are entirely fabricated. No such tome as the one cited in 'The Treasure of Abbot Thomas' ever existed. 'Canon Alberic's Scrap-book' was penned in 1894 and published shortly thereafter in the *National Review*; 'Lost Hearts' appeared in the *Pall Mall Gazette*. Of the subsequent five narratives, most recounted to colleagues during the Christmas season at King's College, Cambridge, I retain only the memory that 'Number 13' was drafted in 1899, whereas 'The Treasure of Abbot Thomas' was composed in the summer of 1904."

M. R. James
