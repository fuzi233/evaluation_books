As I emerged from slumber, the earth beneath me felt damp and spongy, and the path back to the village was slowly disappearing into the quagmire. The ancient river was swelling, its gentle current transformed into a churning torrent, threatening to sweep away the orchard valley below. Its branches would soon split, racing towards the precipice with separate destinations: one branch to reclaim the fertile land, the other to potentially engulf the dark horde, and a third to traverse the treacherous Evil Wood. I hastened my pace, determined to return to those who had sent me on this journey.

Upon reaching the precipice, I navigated through the tangled branches, drawn to the humble cottage of Mara, hoping to catch a glimpse of her once more before my departure. The route was now familiar, and I knew exactly how to cross the channels, even if the rising waters were to fill them. Yet, upon arriving at the cottage, I found the door still ajar, the bread and water untouched on the table. An unsettling silence enveloped the space, and I called out, but there was no response. I continued on my way.

As I walked further, I came across a gray-haired man sitting on the sandy beach, overcome with grief. "What troubles you, sir?" I asked gently. "Are you abandoned?"

"I weep," he replied, his voice heavy with sorrow. "For they refuse to let me pass into the afterlife. Despite my advanced age, Death's mistress has rejected me. Will you intercede on my behalf, sir, if you know her?"

"I'm afraid I cannot, sir," I replied. "She accepts only those whose time has come."

"How do you know this?" he pressed. "You're too young to have sought death yourself!"

"Perhaps your words reveal that, were you young again, you wouldn't desire it either," I suggested.

The old man looked at me with a mix of surprise and curiosity. "Indeed, I wouldn't! And I'm certain you wouldn't either, given your youth."

"I may not be old enough to long for death, but I'm young enough to cherish life," I said. "That's why I'll seek out Death herself to learn if she'll finally take me in. You, on the other hand, seem to wish for death, but not for the right reasons. She won't accept you because no one can truly desire to leave life behind without first embracing it fully."
