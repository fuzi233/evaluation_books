As the last wisps of autumn fog surrendered to the morning sun, the ancient stones of our Styrian castle, or schloss, began to stir. The wind, a mournful sigh, rustled through the trees that stood sentinel around the clearing, their branches creaking in a language only the forest understood. Our modest estate, nestled on a gentle rise within the woods, seemed a tranquil haven, its seclusion a blessing in a world where money's value was measured by its scarcity. My father's pension and patrimony had secured this sanctuary, a bargain in a land where wealth stretched far and wide.

The schloss, with its many-windowed facade and Gothic spires, rose above the treetops like a spectral presence. Below, the drawbridge, rarely raised, spanned the moat, where swans glided effortlessly, their feathers glistening like moonlight on dark water. Water lilies floated on the surface, their white blooms a fleeting beauty in the dappled shade. The old, narrow road wound its way before the gate, disappearing into the forest's depths.

Our isolation was almost palpable. Fifteen miles of forest stretched to the right, twelve to the left, and the nearest inhabited village lay seven miles to the left, a tiny, forgotten speck on the map. Yet, just beyond the treeline, the ruins of a village beckoned, its crumbling church a testament to the transience of human endeavor. The moldering tombs within its walls bore witness to the proud House of Karnstein, now extinguished, their legacy reduced to a haunting whisper on the wind.

We were a small, close-knit family, comprising my father, kind and aging, and myself, a mere nineteen winters old. Eight years had passed since then, a span of time that had witnessed the departure of our household's sole other woman, my governess, Madame Perrodon. A native of Berne, her presence had filled the void left by my mother's untimely passing, and though I could hardly recall her absence, her warm, round face remained etched in my memory. She had been our steadfast companion, a bulwark against the darkness that sometimes threatened to encroach upon our secluded world.
