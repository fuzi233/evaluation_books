As the last wisps of fog lifted from the Thames, casting a pale silver glow over the city, Deronda's thoughts lingered on Mordecai, his imagination weaving a tapestry of vivid colors and textures around the young man. He pictured Mordecai swathed in fine gray flannel shirts, the soft folds of the fabric a gentle counterpoint to the austere beauty of his features. A dressing-gown, reminiscent of a Franciscan's simple garb, draped elegantly around his shoulders, accentuating the lines of his neck and head. Deronda's pleasure in envisioning this scene was twofold: he delighted in the prospect of seeing Mirah's joy, and in the opportunity to indulge his own artistic inclinations. Yet, as he stood there, surrounded by the quiet dignity of his London townhouse, he couldn't shake the nagging feeling that he might be misjudging Mordecai, that the younger man's sense of his own identity might be more complex than Deronda's romanticized vision allowed. Still, the Meyricks' approval was essential, and Deronda knew he must tread carefully to avoid inflaming their prejudices against Mordecai's Jewish heritage.

That evening, as the clock struck nine, Deronda descended to the parlour, where the fire crackled and spat, casting a warm, golden light on the faces of Mrs. Meyrick and her daughters. The room was filled with the sweet scent of pipe tobacco and the soft murmur of conversation. Mrs. Meyrick, her eyes sparkling with a secret, invited the girls to gather 'round the fire, and Hans, still chuckling over a poem, reluctantly joined them, his eyes glinting with mischief. Kate, ever the wit, teased him good-naturedly about his fondness for verse, while the girls settled in, their feet tucked beneath the fender. As the fire burned high, Mrs. Meyrick cleared her throat, a hint of drama in her voice.

"Girls, I have news," she began, her words dripping with excitement. "Mirah's brother has been found."

The room fell silent, the only sound the soft crackle of the flames. Hans let out a low whistle, and Kate's eyes widened in surprise. "Confound it!" he exclaimed, his tone a mixture of disappointment and dismay. Mab spoke up, her voice full of concern, asking about their mother, and Mrs. Meyrick's expression turned somber.
