In the musty silence of the library, the scent of aged books and worn leather bound the air, a comforting shroud that enveloped Lady Violet Lebas as she sat at her mahogany desk, surrounded by the soft glow of candlelight. Her fingers drummed a staccato beat on the polished surface, a rhythm that echoed the turmoil brewing within her. The morning post had brought a flurry of letters, each one a reminder of the tangled web of secrets and lies that threatened to consume them all. Mr. Andrew Lang's sprawling script danced across the page, his words a tantalizing promise of answers to questions she dare not ask.

As she read, the rain outside intensified, casting a melancholy mood over the room. The sound of droplets pattering against the leaded glass was a constant heartbeat, a reminder of the city's indifference to the quiet struggles of its women. Lady Violet's thoughts were a jumble of anxiety and curiosity, her mind racing with the implications of the events unfolding before her. The meeting with Mr. Gryce had left her feeling unsettled, her usual composure shaken by the weight of the mystery.

She rose from her chair, her movements economical and precise, a testament to her years of training in the strict school of manners. Yet, as she paced across the room, her steps betrayed a subtle agitation, a hint of the unease that simmered beneath her polished surface. The memory of Mr. Gryce's words lingered, his suggestion that they await the result of their investigation a gentle prod that stirred her determination.

As she paused before the window, gazing out into the rain-soaked night, Lady Violet's thoughts turned to Amos's son, the young man whose actions had set this chain of events in motion. What drove him to behave as he did, his gestures a macabre pantomime of the crime? Was he a witness, a pawn in a larger game, or something more sinister? The questions swirled through her mind like the rain outside, each one a thread in the intricate tapestry of deceit and betrayal that threatened to engulf them all.

Just then, a faint knock at the door broke the spell, and Lady Violet's heart quickened in anticipation. She knew that Mr. Gryce had returned, his presence a harbinger of answers, or perhaps, more questions. With a quiet sense of resolve, she turned to face the door, her eyes locked on the old detective's serious face, his manner a perfect balance of reassurance
