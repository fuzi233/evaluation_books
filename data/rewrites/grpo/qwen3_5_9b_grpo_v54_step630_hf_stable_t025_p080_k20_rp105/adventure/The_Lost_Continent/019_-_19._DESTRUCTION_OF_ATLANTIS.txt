The old priest stumbled forward, his hand trembling as it settled on my shoulder. "Well?" I demanded, my voice sharp with a lack of patience for interruptions.

"News has reached the Three, my King, of the threat looming over us."

"Then they know I stand here now, brother, to witness the finest fight of my life," I replied, my heart pounding with the thrill of combat. "When it ends, I shall go to the Gods, standing behind the stars to welcome them when they arrive. I regret only that they are too old and feeble to die and witness such a glorious slaughter themselves."

"I bear commands from them, my King," the priest said, his tone grave. "I fear you will find them distasteful. You are forbidden to find your death in this fighting. They have a further use for you."

I turned on him, anger flaring. "I shall take no such order, my brother. I refuse to believe it was ever given. You must have misunderstood. If I am a man, a priest, a soldier, or a King, then honor dictates that no enemy passes this gate while I live. Go back and spit that message in their teeth."

The old man smiled, a look of envy crossing his face. He, too, had been a keen soldier in his youth. "I told them you would not easily believe such a message. I asked for a sign, and they bore with me. Here it is, my King." He produced a jewel.

"How did they come by that? It is a bracelet from the elbow of Nais."

"They must have stripped her of it. I did not know its origin. The word I was to bring says the owner waits inside the Ark of the Mysteries. The Three's further use for you concerns her."

Even then, I confess my obedience was sorely tested. The promise of battle was intoxicating, yet my love for Nais ultimately prevailed. I offered the necessary salutation, signaling my acceptance of the order.

Turning to the knot of priests left to defend the gate, I made my farewells. "You will have what I shall miss, my brothers," I said. "I envy you that fight. Though I am King of Atlantis, I am only one of the Seven, and thus a servant of the Three. They speak the will of the Highest Gods, and we must obey. You will stand behind the stars before I join you, and I ask that you commend me to those you meet there. It is not my own will that keeps me from appearing by your side."

They smiled, saluting me courteously with their weapons, and we parted. I did not see the fight, but I knew it was fierce; the time that passed before Phorenice's hordes breached the mountain crest spoke volumes. The last remnant of the lesser priests of Atlantis died hard.

With a sour feeling in my gut, I ascended to the head of the pass, moving through the groves and between the temples and colleges on the upper slopes of the Sacred Mountain until I reached the boundary. In milder days, it was death for any but the privileged few to cross this line. But conventions were dead, and my temper was hot. I would have strode on with little scruple had I not been interrupted.

Waiting in the temple marking the boundary was old Zaemon. With due solemnity and invoking ancient rituals, he sought dispensation from the High Gods for my trespass, refusing to yield until his ceremony was complete.

Below, Phorenice's tower and bridge were already in position. The clash and yelling of battle told that the handful of priests on the last gate's rampart were bartering their lives for the highest return in death. They were trained fighters, yet old and feeble; the odds were insurmountable. Soon, the place would be stormed, and the roof of the Sacred Mountain left to the invaders' mercy. If anything remained to be done, it must be done quickly while peace lingered.

Zaemon, however, was minded otherwise. He led me to the Ark of the Mysteries, chiding my impatience, and waited until I had kissed it reverently. Then he called aloud, and another old man emerged from the opening at the Ark's top, climbing painfully down the side battens. He was hoary, frail, and emaciated, and along with Zaemon, he was one of only two priests raised to the highest degree, possessing knowledge of the highest secrets.

"Look!" Zaemon cried in his shrill voice, sweeping a trembling finger over the shattered city and the vast expanse of sea and country below. I followed his gaze, and a chill crawled through me. Our Lord the Sun burned high in a cloudless blue sky, day shimmering in His heat. Below, all seemed peaceful and still, save for the smoking mountains, the spouting fires, and the sea, which boiled with strange disorder.

But it was the sea that troubled me most. Far out on the distant coast, it surged against the rocks in enormous rolls. Up the great estuary, where Atlantis stood, it gushed in successive waves of terrifying height that never returned. The lower lands were already blotted out by tumultuous waters; the harbor walls were drowned, and the flood crept into the lower wards of the great city.

"You have seen?" Zaemon asked.

"I have seen."

"You understand?"

"In part."

"Then let me tell you all. This is the beginning, and the end will follow swiftly. The Highest Gods, who sit behind the stars, have a limit to Their patience, and it has been passed. Atlantis, the great continent beyond, and all within it are doomed to unutterable destruction. Of old, it was foreseen that this wiping-out would come through the sins of men, and to this end, the Ark of the Mysteries was built under the Gods' direction. No mortal implement can scratch its surface; no wave or rock can wreck it. Inside are stored on sheets of ancient writing all the world's learning not shared by the common people, plus grain sufficient for two persons for four years, sweet water, seeds, weapons, and all other matters deemed fit."

He paused, his voice heavy with destiny. "Of all this vast country, the High Gods have decreed that two shall not perish. Two shall be chosen—a man and a woman—fit and proper to carry away the ancient learning and rear a new race to honor our Lord the Sun and the other Gods elsewhere. The woman is already within the Ark, seated in her appointed place. Though she is my daughter, the burden of her choice rests with you. As for the man, the choice has fallen upon yourself."

I was half-numb with shock. "I do not know that I care to be a survivor."

"You are not asked for your wishes," the old man said firmly. "You are given an order from the Highest Gods, who know you to be Their faithful servant."

Habit rode strong upon me. I made the required salutation and declared that I heard and would obey.

"Then it remains to raise you to the sublime degree of the Three. If your learning is so small that you cannot understand the keys to many of the Powers and the highest Mysteries when they are handed to you, that fault cannot be remedied now."

Time was short. The fight still raged at the gate, a wonder how the handful of priests held their ground. But the ocean rolled in with increasing fury, the mountains smoked and belched volleys of rock as the weight increased on their lower parts. Soon, the besiegers would see the fate threatening them and rush forward in mad fury, sweeping all obstruction before them.

Yet Zaemon and his fellow sage showed no haste. They were without temple, without sacrifice or incense, having but the sky for a roof and the Gods themselves for witnesses. Still, they performed the raising of me to their degree with all the grand, majestic form that had gathered dignity from the ages, curtailing not a single sentence. A burning mountain burst with a bellowing roar as incoming waters met its fires, but they continued gravely, reciting their sentences. Phorenice's troops broke the last resistance, pouring in a frenzied stream among the groves and temples, yet they never faltered in the ritual.

It was said that this ceremony was the grandest of our holy religion, and I found it so. Even the tremendous circumstances hemming them in could not make these frail old men forget the deference due to the highest order of the Clan.

For myself, I owned I was less rapt. Standing bareheaded in the heat, I tried to concentrate, yet was torn by a thousand foreign emotions. The awful destruction unfolding around me demanded attention; a continent was meeting its end. If Zaemon could give his mind wholly to parochial priesthood matters, I was not so stoical. Moreover, my thoughts were filled with anxieties concerning Nais. Yet I preserved a decent show of attention, making all required responses and striving to memorize the sentences that were keys to power and learning, not merely phrasings of grandeur.

It became clear that if the ceremony did not soon conclude, the further plan would fail, and I would meet my death by fighting to a finish, as I had feared. My flickering attention saw soldiers coming nearer in their frantic wanderings and the sea rolling deeper into the land.

The fires ringing half the mountain spurted to double their height, burning with an unceasing roar. Yet, despite these distractions, the two old priests continued their work as if in the quiet of an ancient temple.

At last, the ceremony ended. "Kneel," cried Zaemon, "and make obeisance to your mother the Earth, and swear by the Highest Gods that you will never make improper use of the powers over Her which you have been granted today."

When I had done, he bade me rise as a fully installed member of the Three. "You will have no opportunity to practice the workings of this degree with either of us, my brother," he said. "Presently, our other brother and I go to stand before the Gods to deliver an account of our trust. What items you remember may turn of use to you hereafter. Now we give you our farewells and promise to commend you highly to the Gods when we meet Them behind the stars. Climb into the Ark and be ready to shut the door guarding it if these raging people attempt to invade. Remember, my brother, it is the Gods' direct will that you and the woman Nais go from this place living and sound. You are expressly forbidden to accept challenge or provocation to fight on any pretext. But as long as it can be done safely, you may look out upon Atlantis in her death-throes. It is fitting that one of the only two sent hence alive should carry the full tale of what has befallen."

I climbed to the top of the Ark via the battens, then descended the internal stair to find Nais waiting in a small chamber.

"I was bidden stay here by Zaemon," she said, her voice trembling. "He forced me to this place by threats and promises that my lord would follow. He is very ungentle, that father of mine, but I think he has a kindness for us both. Is there no chance to save him from what is going to happen?"

"He will not come into this Ark, for I asked him," I replied. "It has been ordained since the Ark's construction that when its purpose comes, only one woman and one man shall be its tenants. They are here already. Zaemon's will cannot be twisted. He has a message to deliver to the Gods, and (if I know him at all), he grudges every minute lost."

I left her and climbed back up to the roof. On the mountain top, men still ran distractedly, but they were gradually converging on where the Ark rested. For the moment, I passed them lightly. The drowning of the great continent spread out below filled my vision. The ocean roared in with furious waves; the plains and level lands were foaming lakes. The great city of Atlantis had vanished eternally. Only the mountains kept their heads above the flood, spewing rocks, steam, and boiling stone, or bursting when waters reached them, creating whirlpools of surging sea and bubbling mud.

In a few breaths, every living creature in the lower grounds was smothered, save for a few huddled in oarless galleys driven inland over what had once been black forest. Even as I watched, these were swallowed by the horrid turmoil. None but those who found standing ground on the upper slopes of the Sacred Mountain survived, and their span was short as the great mass of the continent sank deeper beneath the boiling inrush of the seas.

Though the soldiery were dazed and maddened, some possessed a valiant strength of mind. Presently, a compact body of people emerged from the houses and temples, heading directly toward the Ark. Marching on the outside were Phorenice's personal guards, weapons newly blooded. They had been forced to fight their way through their own fellow soldiers. The poor demented creatures had thought it every man for himself, until these guards, by their mistress's order, proved that Phorenice still came first.

In the middle of them, borne in a litter of gold and ivory by grotesque European slaves, rode the Empress. She was still calm, still lovely, seemingly divided between contempt and amusement. Her two children lay at her feet. On her right marched Tatho, gorgeously apparelled with a beard curled and plaited into a thousand ringlets. On the other side, walking with unruffled defense, was Ylga, once again a fan-girl and second lady in this dwindling kingdom.

The party halted half a score of paces from the Ark by Phorenice's order. "Do not go nearer to those unclean old men. They carry a rank odour, and for the moment we are short of essences to sweeten the air of their neighborhood." She lifted her eyebrows and looked up at me. "Truly a quiet little gathering of old acquaintances. Why, there is Deucalion, that once I took the flavor of and threw aside when he cloyed me."

"I have Nais here," I said. "Presently, we two will be all that are left alive of this nation."
