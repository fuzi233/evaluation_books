The morning light filtering through the windows of Castlewood House cast an eerie glow on the worn oak floorboards. Henry Esmond, Esq., stood at the edge of the room, his eyes fixed on the vast expanse of the James River beyond the panes. His thoughts drifted to the past, to the wars of Queen Anne's reign, and the compromises he had made. A lifetime of service, and yet, it seemed, he had gained little more than a title and a sense of disconnection. His father's ancestral lands in Ireland, once a promise of security, now felt like a weight he could hardly bear. The Prince of Orange, that most pacable of sovereigns, had ascended to the throne, and the Colonel's friends had worked tirelessly to smooth over his past transgressions.

As he turned away from the window, his gaze fell upon the portrait of his wife, Madam Esmond. She had been a widow when he married her, her previous husband, Viscount Castlewood, a victim of Lord Mohun's blade. A duel, a death, and a marriage born of circumstance. Her eyes seemed to follow him still, a reminder of the life they had built together in Virginia. This new world, with its boundless lands and endless possibilities, had been a balm to his weary soul. The Virginians, with their English customs and their patriarchal ways, had welcomed him as one of their own. He had named his estate Castlewood, a testament to his heritage and his longing for a simpler time.

The household bustled around him, the sounds of cooking and conversation drifting through the halls. His daughter and grandsons played in the distance, their laughter carrying on the breeze. The air was thick with the scent of blooming flowers and the distant tang of the river. It was a life he had chosen, one of quiet comfort and stability. Yet, as he walked through the rooms, he couldn't shake the feeling of disquiet that lingered within him. A sense of restlessness, a feeling that he was living someone else's dream, not his own. The whip and the corn-bag, tools of a bygone era, hung on the wall, a reminder of the world beyond the tranquil surface of Castlewood.
