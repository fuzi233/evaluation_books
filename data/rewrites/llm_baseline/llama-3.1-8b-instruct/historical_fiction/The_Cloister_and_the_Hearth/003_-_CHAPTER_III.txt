As Gerard rose from the table, a dignified seneschal approached their table, his silver hair a stark contrast to his crisp, black attire. "The Princess Marie requests your presence, young sir," he announced, his voice measured and polite. Gerard nodded, and the seneschal led him away, leaving behind a room full of curious glances.

Margaret watched Gerard depart with a mixture of concern and envy etched on her face. Peter, still half-asleep, suddenly sat up, his eyes scanning the room as if searching for something. William Johnson, however, stood up, a look of feigned surprise on his face. "And to think that there was I at your backs, and you saw me not," he exclaimed, his voice laced with a hint of jest.

Margaret raised an eyebrow, her tone icy. "Nay, cousin Johnson, I saw you long since." Johnson's expression faltered, and he seemed to realize he had overstepped. "You saw me, and spoke not to me?" he asked, his voice tinged with a note of inquiry.

Margaret's response was measured. "Cousin, it was for you to welcome us to Rotterdam, as it is for us to welcome you at Sevenbergen. Your servant denied us a seat in your house." Johnson's face reddened, and he muttered an apology under his breath. Margaret's eyes sparkled with amusement. "And I had a mind to see whether it was 'like maid like master,' for there is sooth in bywords."

Johnson's flush deepened, and he hastily changed the subject. "Come, let us go home with me, and I'll show you whether you're welcome to Rotterdam or not." Margaret smiled graciously, but declined, citing her desire to hear the minstrels play again. Johnson returned with a proposal, suggesting that his secretary, Hans Cloterman, wait for Gerard and escort him to his lodgings. Margaret's hesitation was palpable, but Johnson reassured her that Hans was a responsible young man, sworn to duty and decorum.

As Johnson summoned Hans, a solemn figure clad in dark attire arose from his seat, his features set in a mask of gravity. Margaret's eyes sparkled with amusement, but also a hint of concern.
