As the theatre of Chatteris struggled to fill its seats, a peculiar phenomenon became apparent. The usually lethargic cast of Pizarro, led by the enigmatic Mr. Bingley in the title role, suddenly transformed into a dynamic ensemble, their performances elevated by the arrival of two new patrons. Mr. Dolphin, a renowned theatrical manager from London, and his trusted secretary, Mr. Minns, had taken their seats in box No. 3, and their presence seemed to imbue the production with an unexpected energy.

The usually reserved Pen, sitting alone in the boxes, was oblivious to the sudden change, his gaze fixed intently on the captivating Miss Fotheringay, who had recently taken up residence in the town. Her stunning appearance, adorned in a white raiment and leopard skin, was accentuated by a sunburst design on her chest and ornate tawdry bracelets adorning her elegant arms. However, her piercing eyes, capable of captivating even the most discerning audience member, seemed to be focused on someone else entirely, leaving Pen to ponder the nature of his own unrequited passion.

Meanwhile, the usually lackluster dialogue and lack of audience engagement had begun to take a toll on the cast. Bingley's usually measured delivery had given way to an impassioned outpouring, as he and his co-stars attempted to draw the attention of the esteemed Mr. Dolphin and his companion. The usually subdued Mrs. Bingley, playing the role of Elvira, had suddenly found her voice, booming through the theatre like a bull of Bashan. Even the normally apathetic Bingley had been galvanized, dashing about the stage and bellowing like a man possessed. The usually lackadaisical actors, Garbetts, Rowkins, and Miss Rouncy, were all vying for the attention of the two visitors, showcasing their talents in a bid to impress the high-ranking Mr. Dolphin.
