As the appointed hour drew near, Pen and Warrington strolled down Paternoster Row, their footsteps echoing off the venerable buildings that housed the city's esteemed publishers. They approached Mr. Bungay's residence, eschewing the bustling public entrance where booksellers' boys hurried to and fro, laden with armfuls of Bungay's volumes. Instead, they made their way to the private door, reserved for the esteemed Mrs. Bungay, who would emerge from its portals to ascend to her carriage, her countenance set in a look of haughty disdain directed squarely at Mrs. Bacon's adjacent windows.

Mrs. Bacon, still a chaiseless woman, would often fling open the sash of her drawing-room window, inviting her four children to gaze out at the passing carriages, and in particular, at Mrs. Bungay's chariot, as if to say, "Behold, dear sister-in-law, these four precious jewels, the very reason I am denied the luxury of a carriage of my own." Emma Bacon would shoot these barbed arrows at Flora Bungay as she sat ensconced within her vehicle, childless and envious.

As Pen and Warrington reached Bungay's door, a carriage and a cab drew up before Bacon's residence. Out stepped the portly figure of Dr. Slocum, whose weighty presence was matched only by the sonorous quality of his voice, a presence that commanded respect from the publishers in the Row. Two dashing young men, resplendent in gleaming white waistcoats, emerged from the cab, their eyes shining with a mixture of excitement and anticipation.

Warrington chuckled, observing the gathering at Bacon's door. "It seems our host has also assembled a dinner party. Behold, that is Dr. Slocum, the celebrated author of 'Memoirs of the Poisoners.' I daresay you would scarce recognize our friend Hoolan in that gallant attire. Doolan, one of Bungay's trusted associates, has joined him, and it appears they have arrived together in a cab, sharing the fare between them." Indeed, Messrs. Hoolan and Doolan had come from the Strand, and as fate would have it, Doolan emerged from the other side of the street, his dark attire accentuated by a pair of pristine white gloves that seemed to gleam in the fading light.
