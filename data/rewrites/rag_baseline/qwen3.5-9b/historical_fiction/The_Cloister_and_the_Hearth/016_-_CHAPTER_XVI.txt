Martin Wittenhaagen stood poised at the base of the staircase, a figure of taut tension, his bowstring pulled tight enough to snap the air, the arrowhead hovering inches from the ceiling, while a dagger rested heavy against his hip. He froze, struck dumb by the absurdity of the scene unfolding above: the men returned, yet Gerard was nowhere to be seen. With a jolt of bewildered realization, he lowered the weapon, his mouth agape, staring upward at the group who now regarded him with equal parts confusion and suspicion.

"Why, mates, was the old fellow making ready to shoot at us?" someone demanded, their voice laced with disbelief.

"Stuff!" Martin retorted, forcing his stolid composure back into place like armor settling over skin. "I was merely testing my new string. There! If you deem it necessary, I will unstring the bow myself."

"Humph!" Dierich grunted, his eyes narrowing with a predatory suspicion that cut deeper than any blade. "There is something more in you than I understand. Put a log on, and let us dry our hides a bit ere we go."

A roaring fire erupted quickly, casting dancing shadows against the rough timber walls. The men clustered around its warmth, the smoke rising from their wet garments and tangled, long hair mingling with the heat. It was amidst this crackling intimacy that the shrieks tore through Margaret's chamber—a sound so primal it shattered the moment. Every head snapped upward. One man snatched the candle, sprinting up the narrow steps toward the sleeping quarters. Martin surged forward, driven by a sudden, visceral dread of the man's intrusive curiosity, attempting to bar his path. But Dierich moved with the speed of a coiled spring, throwing his arms around Martin's waist from behind, shouting orders for the others to hold him fast.

The candle-bearer slipped past the blockade, and the remaining men descended upon Martin. What followed was a chaotic, violent tangle of limbs and fury. For a long, fierce struggle, they rolled across the floorboards, a writhing mass of desperation, with Martin pinned in the center, fighting with the strength of a cornered lion. Yet, they eventually subdued the old Samson, binding his hands and feet tightly with a rope intended for Gerard. Martin groaned aloud, a raw sound of defeat, realizing with horror that the intruder had already breached the sanctuary of Margaret's room while they fought. Now, he was powerless, trapped in the very space he sought to protect.

"Ay, grind your teeth, you old rogue," Dierich panted, his chest heaving from the exertion, his voice dripping with triumph. "You shan't use them."

"It is my belief, mates, that our lives were scarce safe while this old fellow's bones were free," another man observed, wiping sweat from his brow.

"He makes me think this Gerard is not far off," a third added, his tone shifting to one of anxious speculation.

"No such luck," Dierich replied sharply, cutting short the hope. "Hallo, mates. Jorian Ketel has been in that girl's bedroom a long time. Best go and see after him, some of us."

The crude laughter sparked by this remark had barely died away when the sound of hasty footsteps echoed overhead, racing along the gallery.

"Oh, here he comes, at last," Dierich announced, turning toward the stairwell. "Well, Jorian, what is to do now up there?"
