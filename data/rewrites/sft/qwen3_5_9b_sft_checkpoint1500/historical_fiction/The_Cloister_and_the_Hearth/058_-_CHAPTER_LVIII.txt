Gerard walked silently beside Teresa, his artist's mind speculating on her intentions regarding his future, though he dared not ask. Finally, she revealed her plan. A friend had informed her of a goldsmith's wife seeking a writer, noting her shop was nearby.

Soon, they arrived at the goldsmith's dwelling. Teresa introduced Gerard with fervent praise, claiming he had saved her child at sea, hoping to secure favor. The goldsmith's wife, however, showed little interest in a writer despite being captivated by Gerard's handsome face. She quickly dismissed the idea, stating her gossip, Claelia the sausage-maker, required someone. Teresa bowed politely and departed.

They journeyed to Claelia's distant home, only to find her absent. Teresa waited with composed dignity, causing Gerard to feel a peculiar unease as he sketched her secretly. Upon Claelia's return, Teresa repeated her plea, recounting Gerard's maritime rescue. Claelia, equally uninterested in hiring a writer, mentioned a friend named Appia Claudia, a tailor living in the Via Lepida, who needed assistance. Teresa retreated gracefully, leaving Gerard to express his concern about the tediousness of their quest.

"Patience is the price of everything," Teresa replied gently, promising justice if they knocked on every door in Rome. Gerard, however, grew anxious, pointing out a shadow following them. Teresa admitted coldly that it was her poor servant, Lodovico. As the figure approached reluctantly, Teresa introduced Gerard to him, revealing that he had saved Lodovico's wife and child. Lodovico's demeanor shifted instantly from artificial bowing to heartfelt gratitude, embracing Gerard warmly. Yet, Gerard remained unsettled by Lodovico's previous secretive behavior, though he accepted his presence. Their path led them onward, with Teresa adopting a somber air.

Their search eventually led to Lucretia, a cheerful keeper of a glove shop, who required a writer for her confessor, Father Colonna. Despite Lucretia's initial refusal due to vows of poverty, she relented upon hearing of Gerard's heroic act, assuring him that a Colonna would never lack funds. She spoke with amusement of Father Colonna's eccentricities, describing how he once dismissed a confession of murder with a perfunctory penance, seemingly lost in a realm above mundane concerns. Lucretia warned Gerard that while the friar welcomed all, he despised incompetence, citing an incident where he angrily rejected a poorly carved crucifix. Teresa, confident in Gerard's ability, promised to answer for him, but Gerard pleaded for delay, fearing the friar's exacting standards. Reluctantly, Teresa agreed to postpone the visit until the next day.

Back at Gerard's lodging, near a shop selling vellum, he found himself drawn to a beautiful white skin in the window. Though unable to afford it immediately, he resolved to purchase it later. Parting with Teresa at his doorstep, he rushed upstairs to examine a bond from Sevenbergen. Overcoming his aversion to deciphering difficult script, he read the document aloud: it detailed a financial arrangement between Ghysbrecht Van Swieten and Floris Brandt regarding a piece of land. Realizing the importance of the evidence, Gerard decided against destroying it, vowing instead to fully understand its contents at leisure. Determined to buy the vellum, he descended the stairs.

There, he encountered the landlady and Teresa. To his astonishment, she produced the very sheet of vellum he desired, explaining that she could not bear to see him want it. Overwhelmed by gratitude, Gerard struggled to formulate a payment, but Teresa merely wished him prosperity before departing.

The following afternoon, Gerard visited Lucretia and was guided to Father Colonna's lodgings. He presented his work, trembling with anticipation. The friar's chamber was a chaotic treasure trove of antiquities and manuscripts, reflecting a mind vast and distracted. After examining Gerard's handwriting with a critical eye, the friar delivered his verdict. Instead of rejection, Gerard received a Dominican embrace—a rare honor indeed.

Meanwhile, across the sea in Holland, a bizarre procession approached Eli's cottage. A grotesque figure, a composite of man and horse, demanded the delivery of their dwarf, Giles. Eli and his wife Catherine protested fiercely, citing the dangers of sending their child to the Duke's court, where innocent lives could be sacrificed. The messenger, representing absolute authority, threatened dire consequences unless compliance was immediate. Just as the argument reached its climax, Giles emerged from the house. Defying his parents' pleas and the risks involved, he declared his willingness to join the monarch's service, dismissing the dangers as trivial compared to the opportunity. Refusing the small mule intended for him, he insisted on riding the highest horse, demonstrating his agility by scaling the animal's flank and leaping from a window onto its back. With a final, tearful farewell from his grieving parents, Giles rode away, leaving behind a household plunged into sorrow, while he ascended toward a destiny fraught with peril and intrigue.
