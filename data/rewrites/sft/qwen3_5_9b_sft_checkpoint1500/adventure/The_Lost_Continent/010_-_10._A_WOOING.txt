A murmur erupted around me, swelling rapidly into shouts. "Kneel!" a whisper hissed, growing urgent. "Kneel, sir, or you will be seen." Another voice roared, "Kneel, you without beard! Pay obeisance to the only Goddess, or by the old Gods I will make myself her priest and butcher you!" The cries rose into a deafening roar, the air thick with fanaticism.

Yet, amidst the storm, a single name began to ripple through the crowd like a stone dropped in still water: "Deucalion." The fervor instantly curdled into caution. While the populace knew little of the man who had fled Atlantis twenty years prior to rule Yucatan, they remembered the Deucalion who had ridden beside the Empress in the golden castle, beneath the canopy of serpents. As the weight of his potential vengeance settled upon them, the shouts died away, leaving the enthusiasts limp and silent.

When silence finally returned, Phorenice turned, her gaze sweeping over the sea of prostrate worshippers until it rested on me standing alone. Stepping out from the throng, I moved between two colossal stones and crossed the circle to where she stood beside the altar. I did not bow. At the prescribed distance, I offered the salutation she had commanded when I became her chief minister, greeting her with formal decorum as Empress.

"Deucalion, man of ice," she retorted, her voice sharp.

"I still adhere to the old Gods!"

"I was not referring to that," she said, a sideways smile playing on her lips.

Just then, Ylga approached, her face pale, her hand trembling as she interceded for my life. "If he will not abandon the old Gods yet," she pleaded, "surely you will pardon him? He is a strong man, and conversion is not swift. You may change him later. Think, Phorenice—he is Deucalion. If you slay him here for this solitary sin, there is no other soul within the marches of Atlantis worthy to serve..."

The Empress seized the words from her lips. "You slut!" she shrieked. "I keep you near me to manage my wardrobe and carry my fan, and do you dare meddle in my policies? Back to your post, outside this circle, or I'll have you whipped. Ay, and I'll do more. I will serve you as Zaemon served my captain, Tarca. Shall I point a finger at you and smite your skin with leprosy?"

The girl bowed, her shoulders shrinking, and retreated cowed. Phorenice then turned to me. "My lord," she said, "I am like a young bird in a nest that has suddenly found its wings. Wings have so many uses, and I am curious to try them all."

"May each new flight they take be for the good of Atlantis."

"Oh," she replied, her eyes flashing, "I know what occupies your thoughts most. But we shall return to the pyramid and discuss this at leisure. Pray, conduct me back to my beast."

Thus, I was pardoned for withholding worship, casting public doubt on her divinity, and Ylga's interference was dismissed as untimely. Though I could not fully discern the logic behind these rulings, I accepted them, knowing they advanced the scheme Zaemon had entrusted to me.

As the Empress extended her fingers—warm, delicate, yet skilled in grasping the weapons of war—I took them gravely and led her from the great circle, which she had, in her turn, profaned with her tricks. I had awaited the wrath of the Lord Sun upon this sacrilege, expecting divine intervention in the act itself. None came. I reasoned that the deity would choose his own moment for retribution, selecting his preferred instrument without my need to raise a puny arm to guard His honor.

We reached the huge red mammoth, standing tamely in waiting, the scent of sacrifice trailing us. She ascended the stairs to the golden cage atop the beast's back and bid me mount beside her. The spot for the fan-girl remained empty, and as we rode back through the streets, our conversation was unheeded by the crowds.

She pressed to know what had befallen me following the gate assault. I recounted the tale, emphasizing Nais's worthiness and suggesting that, with care, she might be reclaimed for allegiance. I withheld the specific commands Zaemon had given me in the sacred tongue, for it is unlawful to repeat such matters save before the High Council sitting before the Ark of the Mysteries.

"You possess an unusual kindness for this rebel, Nais," Phorenice observed.

"She proved to me more clever and thoughtful than the common herd."

"Ay," she sighed, perhaps genuinely, "an Empress loses much that meaner women gain as their common due."

"In what particular?"

"She misses the honest wooing of her equals."

"If you set yourself up as a Goddess—" I began.

"Pah!" She waved a dismissive hand. "I wish to be no Goddess to you, Deucalion. That guise was for the common folk; it grants me power and aids my schemes. All you Seven Higher Priests know the trick of calling down fire; it pleased me to filch it. Can you not be generous and admit a woman may be as adept at uncovering natural laws as your musty elders?"

"It remains that you are Empress."

"And nor am I. Consider this, Deucalion: a woman sits beside you on this cushion. Look upon her and say the first words that come to your lips. Cease with ceremonies, cease with statecraft. Are you to wait as you are until all your manhood withers? It is wise not to hasten unduly, yet who watches a fruit ripen and leaves it past its prime?"

I gazed upon her glorious beauty, yet it left me cold. Remembering Zaemon's command, I forced a smile. "I may have been fastidious, but I do not regret waiting thus long."

"Nor I. I have played my life as a maid in time enough. I am a woman, ripe and full-blooded; the day has come when I must be more than I have been."

I clenched my hand over hers. "Take me to husband, and I will be a good man to you. But I speak to Phorenice the woman, not the Empress, and I offer fair warning: I will be no puppet."

She regarded me sidelong. "I have been master so long that I suspect it will be an enjoyment to be mastered occasionally. No, Deucalion, I promise—you shall be no puppet. Indeed, it would take a lusty lung to pipe if you danced against your will."

"Then, as man and wife, we shall dwell together in the royal pyramid and rule this country with all the wisdom the High Gods bestowed. These miserable differences shall vanish; the rebels shall return to their homes to hunt beasts, the Priests' Clan shall be pacified. Phorenice, you and I shall pour our brains and souls into governance, making Atlantis rise as a nation surpassing all the world in peace and prosperity."

Petulantly, she withdrew her hand. "Oh, your conditions and your Atlantis! You carry a crudeness in these colonial manners, Deucalion, that palls after the first blunt flavor wears away. Must I do all the wooing? Is there no thrill of love beneath all your ice?"

"In truth, I know not what love may be. I have had little speech with women in these busy years."

"We were a pair when you landed, though I have heard sighs and protestations from every bearded man in Atlantis. Some tickled my fancy, but none moved me deeper. Nor have I learned love from my own feelings. But, sir, I think you will teach me soon if you persist in your coldness."

"From what I have seen, love is for the poor, the weak, and those of flighty emotions."

"Then I would rather another woman were Empress, and I some ill-dressed creature of the gutter whom a strong man could seize by force and carry home for sheer passion. Ah! How I could revel in it! How I could respond if he caught my whim!" She laughed. "But I should lead him a sad life if my liking were not so strong as his."

"We are as we are made; we cannot change our inward movers."

Her glance turned sullen. "If I do not change yours, my Deucalion, more trouble shall brew for this poor Atlantis. There will be ill-doings in this household if my love grows for you, and yours remains unborn."

I believed she would have compelled me to fondle her there, in the golden castle atop the beast's shabby back, before the city streets teeming with onlookers. She possessed little appetite for privacy. Yet, I could not comply. The Gods knew I was earnest in my task, and they knew how it repelled me. I was a true priest that day, having cast aside all personal inclination to fulfill the Council's commands. Had I known how to navigate this, I might have yielded to her mood, but lacking the requisite artifice, I stood frozen.

"There was no help," I realized, "but to leave unsaid all save what I truly felt." Diplomacy was my training, my tongue glib on most matters, but paltering with women was a lightness I had neglected.
