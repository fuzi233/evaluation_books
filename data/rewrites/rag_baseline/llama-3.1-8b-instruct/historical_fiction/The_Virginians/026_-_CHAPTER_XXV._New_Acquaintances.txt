The afternoon sunlight cast a pale glow through the windows of Madame de Bernstein's drawing room, illuminating the delicate dance of dust motes suspended in the air. Cousin Maria stood amidst this gentle chaos, arranging bouquets of flowers in various vases scattered throughout the room. Her fingers moved with a quiet precision, each petal carefully placed as she worked. The scent of roses and sweet-williams wafted through the air, mingling with the faint tang of beeswax polish on the furniture.

As she worked, Maria stole glances at young cousin Harry, her cheeks flushing with a soft pink hue. Her eyes sparkled with a mixture of shyness and curiosity, her gaze darting back to the task at hand whenever she felt the weight of her aunt's attention. Madame de Bernstein, seated in her armchair, her tortoiseshell stick resting in her lap, watched her niece with a discerning eye. Her voice, low and commanding, cut through the gentle atmosphere, rating the servants for their perceived transgressions – a snagged stocking, an over-abundance of pomade in the hair.

In the midst of this domestic tableau, the sounds of the Pantiles fair drifted in through the open window, a distant hum of laughter and music carrying on the breeze. The Baroness's drawing room, once a hub of activity, now seemed a tranquil oasis, a refuge from the vibrant colors and lively rhythms of the outside world. As Maria finished her arrangement, she cast a hesitant glance at her aunt, wondering if perhaps she might be allowed to stay and join the assembly. But Madame de Bernstein's expression remained resolute, her voice firm as she directed the servants to prepare for the evening's festivities. "Harry, my dear, you should dine at the White Horse Inn. We shall hear the bell ring in a few minutes. Go, enjoy yourself."
