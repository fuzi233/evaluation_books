Her relief at leaving her father's household was palpable as we distanced ourselves from the estate. As we traveled, Madame de Bernstein began to open up, sharing thoughts and feelings she had previously kept hidden. Though she never revealed the details of her tumultuous conversation with her niece, the tension between them seemed to dissipate with each passing mile.

He spoke of his nephew Eugene in a lighthearted manner, predicting a future where he would be dominated by his wife. The old lady also commended the American gentleman, Mr. Van den Bosch, for his practical nature and potential as a steward. She tactfully omitted any mention of her sister's past, choosing instead to focus on the present and the promise of a brighter future for her niece.

In contrast, her nephew, Mr. Warrington, remained hopeful that Madame de Bernstein would develop a close bond with her niece, Lady Maria. However, the old lady remained unyielding in her refusal to discuss Lady Maria. Instead, she launched into a series of anecdotes about her own niece, choosing to share tales of her life that, she implied, served as a cautionary example. Though I chose not to record these stories in full, I can attest to the old lady's kind nature and her willingness to offer guidance.
