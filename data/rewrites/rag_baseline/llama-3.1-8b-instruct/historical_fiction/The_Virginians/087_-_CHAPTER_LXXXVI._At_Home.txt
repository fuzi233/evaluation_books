At Manor House, the morning sun cast a faint glow through the windows, illuminating the faces of the family gathered in the parlor. The atmosphere was heavy with the weight of recent loss. General Fanshawe's eyes, red-rimmed from sleepless nights, wandered around the room as he struggled to find solace in the familiar surroundings. His daughter, Hetty, sat beside him, her slender fingers interwoven with his as they shared a moment of silence.

The general's thoughts drifted back to his late wife, whose passing had left an unfillable void. He remembered the way she would often speak of their children, of the joy they brought her, and how she longed to see them settled and happy. The ache in his chest deepened as he recalled the final words she whispered before departing this life: "She sees us, though we do not see her; and she thanks you, George, for having been good to her children."

As the morning wore on, the conversation turned to the subject of Hetty's marriage prospects. Mrs. Lambert, the general's sister, had recently visited and expressed her concerns about Hetty's decision to remain unmarried. "My dear Sir George," she had said, "I've told our dear Theo that I wouldn't have a pretty sister in my house to make tea for Jack when I was upstairs... And so I have in Jemima, but they don't come together in my house when I'm upstairs—that I promise you!"

The general's eyes met those of his wife, who smiled gently in an attempt to reassure him. "It seems, dear sister, that you have a certain... appreciation for the value of domestic order." Mrs. Lambert's face fell, and the general quickly intervened, steering the conversation towards more pleasant topics.

As the day unfolded, the family's attempts to find comfort in each other's company became increasingly strained. Hetty's resolve to remain unmarried remained unwavering, and the general's heart, though heavy with grief, began to understand the depth of her convictions. In the stillness of the evening, as the fire crackled in the hearth, he realized that his daughter's silence was not a rejection of the world, but rather a testament to the profound impact her mother's passing had left on her young heart.
