The silence in the library was oppressive. Archer sat in his favorite armchair, surrounded by the familiar scent of old books and dust. He stared blankly at the shelf in front of him, where the leather-bound volumes seemed to loom like sentinels guarding secrets. His mind wandered to the previous evening, when he had promised May he would join her at her grandmother's, only to forget in the haze of his own reverie. He felt a twinge of guilt, but it was quickly replaced by annoyance – why should one trivial mistake weigh so heavily on him? As he pondered this, he heard the soft rustle of fabric behind him and turned to see May enter the room.

She looked pale and drawn, her eyes lacking their usual sparkle. Her hair was styled in the same intricate coiffure he had grown accustomed to, but her face seemed to sag beneath the tight lacing of her dress. For a moment, Archer felt a pang of concern, wondering if the monotony of their life together had finally begun to tell on her. He pushed the thought aside, attributing her fatigue to the stress of her grandmother's illness.

"What kept you?" he asked, trying to sound nonchalant.

May's response was hesitant. "I was waiting at Granny's, and Ellen came alone, saying you'd had to leave abruptly for business."

Archer's mind replayed the events of the previous day, and he realized he had indeed forgotten about the letters he needed to send. "Just some correspondence I'd overlooked," he explained.

May's gaze lingered on him, and for an instant, Archer thought he detected a flicker of disappointment. But it was quickly suppressed, and she turned to adjust a stray lock of hair in the mirror above the fireplace. As she did so, Archer caught a glimpse of something languid and defeated in her posture, and he felt a sense of unease settle in his chest. Had their life together become as stifling for her as it had for him? He tried to shake off the thought, telling himself it was just his imagination playing tricks on him.

The conversation at dinner was stilted, with the Beauforts' decision to remain in New York serving as a reluctant conversation starter.
