One of them, the Inquisitor, stood motionless, his eyes fixed on the slumbering form of Frank, the Englishman who had narrowly escaped the brutal hands of the native warriors. The air was heavy with the weight of unspoken intentions, as Eustace hesitated, torn between his duty and the anguish that gnawed at his conscience.

The Inquisitor, sensing his presence, turned to him, his gaze piercing through the shadows. "Ah, Eustace, I see you've arrived. We must proceed with caution. The prisoner's recantation is crucial, and his heart is still tender from the ordeal." Eustace nodded, his eyes never leaving Frank's peaceful face, where a faint smile played on his lips, as if he whispered secrets to his mother and a higher power.

The Inquisitor's words hung in the air, a stark reminder of the task at hand. Eustace's thoughts were a jumble of conflicting emotions – loyalty to his faith, loyalty to his cousin, and a growing sense of unease at the Inquisition's methods. He felt a pang of guilt, knowing that his presence here was a betrayal of sorts, a betrayal of trust and friendship.

As he gazed at Frank, Eustace wondered if words alone could pierce the armor of conviction that had been forged within him. He recalled the countless times they had conversed about the intricacies of faith and reason, and how Frank's passion for knowledge had only deepened his convictions. Could he really expect to sway him now, after the torments he had endured?
