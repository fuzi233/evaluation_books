# CHAPTER LXXVI. Exeunt Omnes (that’s fancy old stage-speak for every last character, extra, and random stagehand shuffles off the set, no encores allowed)
My Dear Doctor,

Let me paint you the scene from thirteen whole months back: this rambling, silly story I’d been churning out page by page looked dead certain to wrap up permanently, and I was flat on my sickbed half-convinced I’d be checking out before I even got to write the final chapter for Pendennis. Then a good mate of ours turned up dragging you through my bedroom door, and I swear on all my half-empty inkwells and dog-eared notebook stacks, I’d never have dragged myself upright and back to my desk if it weren’t for your nonstop, hawk-like watchfulness and your clever, brilliant medical tricks.

I still love thinking back to all that kindness back then — not just yours, but all the little random, over-the-top acts of friendship and sympathy from folks I’d never have expected to care half that much, turning up with jellies and bad jokes at the exact time I was so wobbly and miserable that even the tiniest bit of warmth felt like a fresh blanket straight from the fire.

And the best, most ridiculous part? You refused every actual coin I tried to hand over as payment, said the only fee you’d accept was a plain old thank-you. Fair enough! I’m stamping that thank-you right here in ink, for me, for all my chaotic household, and even for Pendennis himself (who you basically gifted his happy ending, let’s be real).

Yours most sincerely, and more grateful than you could ever fit inside your entire doctor’s leather satchel,
