The sun cast a pale glow over the deserted beach of Roville-sur-Mer, where the morning crowd had long since dispersed. Sally sat with her back against a hillock of golden sand, watching the gentle lapping of the waves against the shore. Her eyelids grew heavy, weighed down by the warmth and tranquility of the scene. As she struggled to stay awake, the soft murmur of the waves lulled her into a state of half-consciousness, a sensation she'd grown accustomed to during her idyllic vacation. 

It had been a month of drifting lazily from one charming French town to another, each place blending seamlessly into the next. Roville, with its blue sky, its Casino, and its snow-white hotels along the Promenade, had captured her heart. Yet, the call of America beckoned, and she knew she couldn't stay indefinitely. Gerald's letter, brief and businesslike, had made it clear that she needed to return to witness the premiere of "The Primrose Way" in Detroit before its New York run. The thought of leaving this tranquil haven was bittersweet, but duty called.

As Sally's thoughts wandered, a rustling sound caught her attention. Two men had settled onto the sand nearby, their conversation carried on the breeze. She turned her gaze toward them, her eyes lingering on the first man, whose tight-lipped expression and angular features seemed to radiate an aura of severity. He was the type who shaved twice a day, and she could almost hear the faint whisper of suppressed irritation when the razor nicked his skin.

"Unpleasant," she thought, dismissing him with a mental label. "A lawyer or a man of similar temperament."

Her attention shifted to the second man, whose bright red hair and freckled complexion had immediately drawn her in. His eyes, a deep brown that sparkled with warmth, held hers with an intensity that made her feel seen. "Rather endearing," she decided, her gaze lingering on his face.
