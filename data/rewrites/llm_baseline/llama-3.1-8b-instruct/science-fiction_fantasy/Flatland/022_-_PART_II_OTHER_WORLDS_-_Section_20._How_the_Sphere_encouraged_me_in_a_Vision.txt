As I lay abed, my mind reeling from the events of the day, I felt an inexplicable urge to conceal my experiences from my wife. Not that I feared her judgment, but I knew that to her, my tale would be nothing short of fantastical. I concocted a fabrication, a story of a fall through the cellar's trapdoor, and hoped she would accept it without question. Her good sense, however, far surpassed that of most women in our land, and she saw right through my ruse. Though she didn't press the issue, her knowing gaze made me feel uneasy, and I was grateful for the excuse to retire to my chamber, where I could collect my thoughts.

As I sat in the stillness, a drowsy haze began to envelop me, but I fought it off, determined to grasp the elusive concept of the Third Dimension. My mind wandered back to the cube, and how it was born from the movement of a square. Though my understanding was still shrouded in mist, I clung to the phrase that had echoed in my mind: "Upward, yet not Northward." Repeating it like a mantra, I drifted into a deep sleep.

My slumber was interrupted by a vivid dream, one that transported me back to the presence of the enigmatic Sphere. His luminous aura had transformed, and his demeanor was now serene, as if he had laid aside his earlier ire. Together, we floated towards a pinpoint of light, to which he drew my attention. As we drew near, a faint hum arose, akin to the gentle buzzing of a Spaceland bluebottle, though softer still. Even in the vacuum-like stillness, the sound reached us only when we halted at a distance of mere spans from the point.

"Observe," the Sphere said, his voice low and measured, "in Flatland, you have lived; in Lineland, you've glimpsed a fragment of another realm; in Spaceland, you've soared to unimaginable heights. Now, I shall guide you to the nadir of existence, to the realm of Pointland, the Abyss of No dimensions."

He pointed to a wretched creature, a being confined to a non-dimensional void. This Point was a being like ourselves, yet bound to its own world, its own universe, with no comprehension of Length, Breadth, or Height. It knew not the number Two, nor the concept of Plurality.
