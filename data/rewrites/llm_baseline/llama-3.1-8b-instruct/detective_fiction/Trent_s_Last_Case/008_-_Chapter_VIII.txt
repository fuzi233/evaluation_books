The inquest convened in a grand, unfurnished hall attached to the hotel, its purpose still nascent – a makeshift ballroom or concert venue awaiting its inaugural event. Before the proceedings began, a phalanx of reporters stationed themselves at the front, their pencils poised over notebooks, ready to transcribe the unfolding drama. Behind the table, the coroner, a robust man with a flair for the theatrical, surveyed the assembly with a self-assured air, his copious vocabulary at the ready to ensnare the jury's sympathies. Flanking him on either side were the jurors, their coiffed hair and affected nonchalance a testament to their studied demeanor.

As the courtroom fell silent, the coroner's words hung in the air like a challenge, awaiting the first witness. Mrs. Manderson, her face shrouded by a thick veil, stepped forward, her pallor and composure striking an arresting note. Though her grief was palpable, it was tempered by an aura of quiet strength, a testament to the resilience of her character. As she began to speak, her voice, low and measured, wove a spell of fascination over the assembly.

The coroner's questions probed gently, drawing out the details of her husband's final hours. He had retired to his dressing room, a small, Spartan space connected to her own bedchamber by a door that remained open throughout the night. The dressing room and bedroom shared a common entrance with the passage, a layout that allowed for easy access and exit. Mrs. Manderson's testimony painted a picture of a husband who prized simplicity and seclusion, preferring the intimacy of a small room to the grandeur of a larger space.

As she recounted the events of that fateful night, Mrs. Manderson's words stumbled upon a threshold of recollection, only to falter and hesitate. The coroner's gentle prompting coaxed her forward, and she revealed that her husband had indeed spoken to her, though her own words, spoken in a state of half-sleep, were lost to her memory. She recalled, however, that he had mentioned a change of plans, a decision to forgo his moonlit drive in favor of a more subdued evening. When pressed for details, Mrs. Manderson's eyes clouded, her voice tinged with a hint of defiance as she alluded to her husband's reticence on matters of business.

"Did he elaborate?" the coroner asked, his tone a gentle prod.
