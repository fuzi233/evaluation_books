Once inside the palace, I guided Sola to the dining hall. After she exchanged the traditional greetings of the green Martians with her father, she began the tale of Dejah Thoris’ doomed pilgrimage.

"Seven nights past," Sola began, her voice low, "after her audience with Zat Arras, the Princess sought to flee the palace under cover of darkness. Though I did not know what had transpired between them, the anguish in her eyes spoke plainly enough. When I found her slipping through the shadows, I needed no words to understand her intent."

"Swiftly, I gathered twelve of her most loyal guards. They did not hesitate—each vowed to follow her, even to the cursed Valley Dor. We overtook her beyond the palace walls. Woola was at her side, but no others. She feigned fury, commanding us to return, but for the first time, we defied her. When she saw our resolve, her pride crumbled. She wept, embracing us, and together we turned southward into the unknown."

"For four days, we rode hard upon a herd of wild thoats, eating little, resting less. Then, at dawn on the fifth day, we saw them—a black swarm of warships blotting the horizon. Before we could hide, they descended like carrion birds. The guards fought to the last, but they were butchered without mercy. Only Dejah Thoris and I were spared."

"The Princess tried to fall upon her dagger, but one of the blacks wrenched it from her grasp. They bound us, rendering our hands useless. The fleet pressed northward—twenty battleships, flanked by swift scouts. That evening, one of their cruisers returned with another captive: a red-skinned woman snatched from the hills under the noses of three Heliumite warships."

"From their talk, we gathered they hunted fugitives who had escaped them days before. Their commander questioned her at length, then cast her into our cell. She was Thuvia of Ptarth, a princess who had taken the voluntary pilgrimage years ago. When she learned Dejah Thoris’ identity, she fell to her knees, kissing her fettered hands."

"Thuvia spoke of you, John Carter—how she had stood beside you and Carthoris that very morning. She told of your battles in the First Born’s domain, of your loyalty to Dejah Thoris. And when she confessed her love for you, the Princess wept—not in jealousy, but in grief for the cruel fate that had torn her from Helium so near your return."

"‘I do not blame you,’ Dejah Thoris told her. ‘Your heart is true.’"

"The fleet veered south when they realized you had eluded them. That night, a guard dragged me to the deck. ‘The Land of the First Born has no place for a green one,’ he sneered, then hurled me into the void."

Sola’s voice grew taut. "Yet fate spared me. The ship skimmed so low that I struck a thicket barely twenty feet below. By dawn, I saw the truth—I had been cast onto the peaks of a mountain range. The fleet had grazed the crest in the dark, never knowing their throw failed. I made my way to a Heliumite waterway, secured a thoat, and rode until I found you."

Silence gripped the room. Dejah Thoris—in the claws of Issus. My hands clenched. Then, like a surge of fire, certainty flooded me. I stood, blade raised.

"By my steel, I swear this: I will find her. I will free her. Or I will die."

A hundred swords flashed in answer, a hundred voices roared their allegiance. Carthoris stayed to rally them while I withdrew with Kantos Kan, Tars Tarkas, Xodar, and Hor Vastus. We plotted late into the night.

Xodar assured us Issus would choose both women as handmaidens, buying us time. Kantos Kan and Xodar would refit our fleet with the secret propellers to navigate Omean’s waters. Tars Tarkas would sound the Tharks, rallying their hordes. Hor Vastus would gather an army sworn to my banner.

As we dispersed, a shadow moved on the balcony. We gave chase, but the spy—likely Zat Arras’ creature—vanished like smoke.

"Let him report whispers of a new Jeddak," I said grimly. "But if he heard our plans for Dejah Thoris, then let war come. I will not be stopped."

One by one, they swore upon their blades to stand with me, even against the throne itself.

Exhausted, I retired—only to wake in the grip of masked men. A gag stifled my curses as they hauled me through the palace’s hidden veins, down to the pits beneath. A coded knock, a door swung wide—and there, smirking amid torchlight, sat Zat Arras.
