I sat back in my chair, puffing on my pipe as I gazed out the window at the rolling hills beyond the estate. The soft rustle of leaves in the gentle breeze was a soothing accompaniment to our conversation, one that I had been waiting to share with Sir Henry and Captain Good for a long time.

"What was it that you heard about my brother's journey at Bamangwato?" I asked, turning my attention back to my companions.

Captain Good leaned forward, his eyes sparkling with interest. "I heard he was starting for Solomon's Mines," he replied, his voice barely above a whisper.

The words sent a shiver down my spine, and I felt a thrill of excitement coursing through my veins. Solomon's Mines – a place of legend, a place of wonder. I had always been drawn to tales of adventure and discovery, and this one was the most tantalizing of them all.

"Where are they?" Sir Henry asked, his brow furrowed in curiosity.

I shook my head, a wry smile spreading across my face. "I don't know," I admitted, "but I know where they're said to be. I once caught a glimpse of the peaks of the surrounding mountains, but there were a hundred and thirty miles of desert between me and them. I'm not aware that any white man has ever made it across, save one."

The three of us sat in silence for a moment, lost in our own thoughts. Then, I spoke up, my voice low and measured. "I'll tell you the legend of Solomon's Mines, if you promise not to repeat a word of it without my permission."

Sir Henry nodded, his eyes locked on mine, while Captain Good murmured his agreement.
