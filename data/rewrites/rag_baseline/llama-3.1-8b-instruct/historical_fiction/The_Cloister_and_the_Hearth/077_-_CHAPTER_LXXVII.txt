As the last wisps of sunlight faded beyond the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the humble hearth of Tergou, the Peterson household gathered around the dinner table. The air was thick with the savory aroma of roasting meats and freshly baked bread, and the soft clinking of silverware against plates filled the evening air. Luke Peterson burst in, his dusty boots scuffling against the stone floor, and announced, "Good people, Mistress Catherine is wanted instantly at Rotterdam."

Kate, the family matriarch, looked up from her meal, her eyes narrowing as she took in the news. "My name is Catherine, young man. Kate, it will be Margaret," she corrected him, her voice firm but gentle. Luke, undeterred, continued, "Ay, dame, she said to me, 'Good Luke, hie thee to Tergou, and ask for Eli the hosier, and pray his wife Catherine to come to me, for God His love.'"

The room fell silent, the family exchanging worried glances. "Holy saints!" Kate exclaimed, her voice trembling. "He has come home, Kate. Nay, she would sure have said so. What on earth can it be?" She began to heap conjecture upon conjecture, her words tumbling out in a rapid stream.

"Mayhap the young man can tell us," ventured Kate's daughter, Margaret, timidly.

"That I can," said Luke, "Why, her babe is a-dying, and she was so wrapped up in it!"

Catherine's face paled, and she sprang to her feet, her eyes fixed intently on Luke. "What is his trouble?"

"Nay, I know not," Luke replied. "But it has been peaking and pining worse and worse this while."

A fleeting glance of satisfaction passed between Cornelis and Sybrandt, but Catherine's gaze was focused solely on her husband, Eli. "Now, Eli," she cried, her voice low and urgent, "if you say a word against it, you and I shall quarrel, after all these years."

Eli's expression remained calm, but his eyes betrayed a hint of unease. "Who gainsays thee, foolish woman? Quarrel with your own shadow, while I go borrow Peter's mule for ye."
