As the sun beat down upon Epsom Downs, the cream of society gathered to witness the grand spectacle of the Derby. Amidst the throngs of fashionable folk, Mrs. Bungay, resplendent in attire fit for Solomon, sat comfortably beside her companion, the demure Mrs. Shandon. Bungay, having indulged in a substantial luncheon, was now engaged in a spirited game of chance, his bald pate glistening with perspiration as he laughed and joked with the other gentlemen. Meanwhile, Shandon wandered among the assembled crowd, exchanging pleasantries with acquaintances and making polite conversation with the various gentry.

Finucane, ever the attentive gentleman, remained steadfast by the ladies' side, facilitating introductions and ensuring that their social obligations were met. As the afternoon wore on, a distinguished guest arrived to pay his respects: the esteemed Mr. Archer. With a gallant bow, he greeted Mrs. Bungay and inquired as to her whereabouts on the course. Pointing out the Prime Minister and several prominent dukes, he regaled her with tales of his connections with the highest echelons of society. "Behold, in the Grand Stand," he said, "sits the Chinese Ambassador, accompanied by his mandarins. Fou-choo-foo, the Governor-General of India's emissary, brought me letters of introduction from his Excellency, and I had the pleasure of hosting him at my table on several occasions. However, he insisted on bringing his own chef, who, in a most distressing incident, served my wife's beloved Blenheim spaniel, Fido, in the second course, stuffed with forcemeat."

Mrs. Bungay gasped in horror at the tale. "Good heavens!" she exclaimed. Archer continued, "My wife was beside herself with grief, and the cook's revelation that Fido's skin had been discovered in the area, mere moments after we had partaken of the dish, only added to her distress. The Lord Mayor, who had the pleasure of dining with us, found the dish quite delightful, especially when paired with green peas – it bears a curious resemblance to duck."
