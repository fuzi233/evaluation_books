The morning sunlight streaming through the windows of Margaret's house cast a warm glow over the kitchen, illuminating the utensils and cookware that hung from the rafters. Catherine, ever the busy homemaker, busied herself rearranging the furniture, her hands moving with a purpose that bordered on the relentless. As she worked, the air seemed to come alive with a gentle hum, like the soft thrumming of a harp string, and the scent of freshly baked bread wafted from the oven, enticing the senses.

But Catherine's zeal was not solely focused on the domestic arts. Her eyes sparkled with a mischievous glint as she summoned Denys and Martin, her loyal accomplices, to join her in her quest for order. With a flick of her wrist, she dispatched them on a errand to the nearby market, hinting that their absence might extend well into the afternoon. The two men, ever obedient, departed with alacrity, their footsteps echoing through the empty streets.

As the morning wore on, Margaret descended to the kitchen, her eyes scanning the room with a mixture of curiosity and concern. She spotted Catherine, her hands moving with a speed that belied her tranquil demeanor, and inquired after Denys. A fleeting blush rose to Catherine's cheeks as she replied that her friend had taken his leave, bound for the rolling hills of Burgundy, where he had not set foot in three years. Margaret's eyes welled up with tears, and Catherine's conscience pricked her, like a needle pricking the surface of a fine linen cloth.

Undeterred, Catherine offered a careless smile, attributing Denys's departure to Margaret's slumbering state, and Margaret's heart swelled with sorrow. Catherine's gaze drifted toward Martin, who sat on his stool, his eyes fixed on some distant point beyond the horizon. Her expression darkened, and she turned away, leaving Margaret to ponder the sudden chill in the air.

As the minutes ticked by, Catherine's mood shifted, like the tides, and she bestowed her attentions on Martin, lavishing him with warmth and kindness. But the old man, his spirit as dry as the autumn leaves, rebuffed her advances, his words as tart as a winter's night. Catherine's face contorted in a scowl, and she flounced back into the house, leaving Martin to his stoic silence.
