My journey across the vast continent of Atlantis had been fraught with peril, but necessity drove me forward. Only once did I pause for more than a brief respite, and that was out of sheer exhaustion. I had brought down a stag after a grueling chase across the open savannah, and as I prepared the meat, I failed to notice the danger lurking beneath the tall grass—a venomous legged worm, its bite swift and deadly.

I felt the sting on my heel before I saw the creature. With a quick slash of my knife, I severed its head, watching its grotesque body writhe in its death throes. I cleaned my blade and pressed it deep around the wound, letting the blood flow freely to purge the venom. Yet despite my efforts, my leg soon swelled with a throbbing ache, the skin stretched taut. Fever gripped me, and by nightfall, I could barely stand. I dragged myself into the branches of a tree, where I spent the night in fitful agony, listening to the growls of predators below.

At dawn, I forced myself onward, leaning heavily on my spear. The moss on the northern sides of trees guided my path, but delirium soon blurred my vision. The world spun, and my last memory before darkness took me was of a stranger emerging from the forest shadows.

When I awoke, I lay on a bed of leaves in a rocky crevice, the air thick with the scent of herbs. A woman crouched by a small fire, stirring a pot of wood grubs and honey.

“You brought me here?” I asked hoarsely.

She nodded. “Aye. I am a nymph of these woods—a healer, a teller of fortunes. Those who can pay, do. Those who cannot…” She grinned, revealing blackened teeth. “Well, they learn the price of refusal.”

“You have news of the city?”

“Phorenice still reigns,” she spat. “Now as a mother of twins, wed to Tatho. Yucatan is gone, devoured by little hairy men from the west. They ate everything—cities, people, even Tatho’s wife. And now they eye Atlantis.”

I clenched my fists. Yucatan, my once-great colony, reduced to nothing.

“And the plague?” I pressed.

“Born from her unburied dead. She kills without end, and the corpses pile higher than the carrion birds can feast.”

I stayed with her six days, enduring her poultices and bitter tonics until my leg healed. In repayment, I hunted for her, bringing down a cloven-hoofed horse before resuming my journey.

The land grew more desolate as I neared the city. Villages stood abandoned, their walls crumbling, their hearths cold. The people who remained were hollow-eyed and wary, some fleeing at the sight of me, others sizing me up for robbery. I avoided conflict where I could, pitying their wretchedness.

At last, I reached the outskirts of Atlantis. The Sacred Mountain loomed ahead, encircled by Phorenice’s army. The city’s once-white pyramids now gleamed with gold leaf, a gaudy testament to her vanity.

Hidden in the woods, I plotted my next move. Then, a man approached—Ro, a disgraced priest turned informant.

“Zaemon sent me,” he said, rubbing a withered hand. “He… persuaded me to find you.”

“What’s the message?”

“Light a fire on the plain. Tomorrow night, go to the cliffside—they’ll lower a rope.”

I did as instructed. The flames roared high, and under cover of darkness, I scaled the sheer rock face, hauled up by unseen hands.

At the summit, I was met by ancient priests, their faces lined with exhaustion. They led me to Zaemon, who greeted me with solemn gravity.

“Hail, Deucalion,” he said. “King of Atlantis, chosen by the High Council.”

“Then Phorenice is condemned?”

“She has defied the Gods for the last time. She claims to have discovered the Secret of Life—immortality for herself and her favorites. The balance must be restored.”

I clenched my jaw. “I’ll need Nais by my side before I face her.”

Zaemon nodded. “She will be brought to you.”

With that, I turned to the defenses. The Sacred Mountain’s fires burned eternal, a ring of light against the coming storm.

The siege had begun.
