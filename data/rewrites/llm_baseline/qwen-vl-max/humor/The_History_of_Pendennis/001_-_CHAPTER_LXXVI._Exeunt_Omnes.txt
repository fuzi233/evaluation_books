My Dear Doctor,

Thirteen months ago—give or take a few weeks, depending on how one counts the days of near-death and mild regret—I found myself in a state of such profound repose that I began to suspect the story had reached its final chapter. A kind soul (who shall remain nameless, though I suspect he was the same fellow who once tried to sell me a goat for my birthday) brought you to my bedside, where I lay like a particularly unimpressive statue in a museum of illness.

Now, while I may not have been at death’s door exactly—more like leaning against it with a cup of tea and a mildly apologetic expression—your constant vigilance, your soothing manner, and your uncanny ability to diagnose things without actually looking at me were nothing short of miraculous. You didn’t just save my life; you saved my dignity, which, let’s be honest, was hanging by a thread thinner than a surgeon’s needle.

And so, in lieu of payment—which you refused with the grace of a man who knows his worth but also understands the value of a good joke—I offer this humble tribute: thanks. Not just for me, but for my family, who endured my dramatics with remarkable patience (and occasional eye-rolling).

You’ve earned your place in the annals of medical heroism—and possibly in the next volume of *The History of Pendennis*, if I ever get around to writing it.

Yours most sincerely and gratefully,

P.S. If you ever need a patient who’s willing to complain about everything but still pay in praise, I’m available. Just don’t expect me to wear a nightcap again.
