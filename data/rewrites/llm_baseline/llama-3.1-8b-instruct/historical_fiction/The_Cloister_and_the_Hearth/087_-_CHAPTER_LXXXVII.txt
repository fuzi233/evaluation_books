As Jorian buttoned his doublet and jerkin, preparing to visit Peter's tomb, his thoughts wandered to the tales of the past. They said Peter was once known as a magician in the distant land of Sevenbergen, and that he had shared some of his secrets with the locals before departing. According to rumor, he had foreseen the arrival of Margaret's son, clad in fine attire and bearing scars from foreign battles, but lacking limbs. However, the prophecy proved false, and the family continued to mourn Peter's passing.

Jorian placed both hands on the weathered stone, ready to rise, when the friar's frail hand grasped his shoulder, and a hushed voice whispered—

"How long has it been since Peter Brandt passed on?"

"Two months, Father. Why do you ask?"

"He was buried by his daughter, wasn't he?" The friar's eyes searched Jorian's face intently.

"No, I laid him to rest, but Margaret covered his grave with a stone."

"Then, I must assume...she's alive?" The friar's voice trembled.

"I'm certain of it," Jorian replied, confusion etched on his face. "She saved my life just six weeks ago. If she were truly gone, I wouldn't be here today. My wife, Joan, feared the worst, but Margaret appeared, opened the windows, and nursed me back to health with an herb from my own garden – sweet feverfew. It's a humble weed, some might say, but it worked wonders. Since then, I've made it a point to acknowledge its power whenever I pass by."

Clement's eyes widened as the news sank in. "You knew one who loved her dearly, didn't you?" he asked, his voice barely above a whisper.

Jorian's gaze turned inquisitive. "Did you know someone who cherished Margaret Brandt? Where did you meet them?"

Clement hesitated, his mind racing with memories. "I met him in Italy," he finally said, choosing his words carefully.

"If you knew him, surely you can recall his name?" Jorian pressed gently.

"It was Gerard Eliassoen," the friar replied, his voice heavy with emotion.

"This is indeed a strange tale," Jorian mused. "Tell me, what drove you to believe Margaret was dead?"
