The afternoon sun cast long shadows across the worn wooden floorboards of the Three Castles Inn. Harry Warrington sat on a bench outside, nursing a cup of lukewarm ale. The air was heavy with the smell of damp earth and the distant tang of smoke from the blacksmith's forge. He gazed out at the old house of his ancestors, its windows like empty eyes staring back. The recollection of his dearest lost brother washed over him, and for a moment, he felt the weight of his sorrow anew.

As he sat there, lost in thought, a pair of riders thundered across the bridge, their horses' hooves pounding out a staccato rhythm on the wooden planks. One of the riders cursed under his breath, shouting at Harry to move out of the way. The other, perhaps concerned he had jostled the young man, urged his horse on, calling out to his companion to follow. Before Harry could react, they had vanished up the hill, leaving behind only the echoes of their laughter and the clatter of their horses' hooves.

Harry's gaze lingered on the spot where the riders had disappeared, his mind replaying the encounter. He couldn't shake the feeling that they had been in a hurry, that they had been running from something – or someone. The sound of the blacksmith's hammering and the calls of the birds in the nearby trees created a sense of unease, like a discordant note in an otherwise tranquil melody.

As the sun began to set, casting the landscape in a warm orange glow, Harry rose from his seat, his movements slow and deliberate. He had been in England for only a day, and already, he felt a sense of disconnection from the people and the place. The house of his fathers, once a symbol of pride and heritage, now seemed cold and unforgiving. He wondered if anyone there truly knew him, or if he was just a stranger in a strange land. With a heavy heart, he turned away from the house and continued on his journey, the silence of the evening air a constant reminder of his solitude.
