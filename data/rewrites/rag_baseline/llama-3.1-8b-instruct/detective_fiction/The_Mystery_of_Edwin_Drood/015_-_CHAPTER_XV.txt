The mist hung low over the heath, obscuring the hedgerows that bordered the narrow track. Neville Landless walked, the rhythmic crunch of gravel beneath his boots a steady accompaniment to his thoughts. The Tilted Wagon, a ramshackle tavern perched atop a hill, receded into memory, its worn facade and faded sign a reminder of the quiet desperation that lay beneath the surface of even the most seemingly ordinary lives. His stomach growled, protesting the meager crust of bread he'd consumed that morning, but he pressed on, driven by a restless energy that had become all too familiar.

As he walked, the silence began to feel oppressive, punctuated only by the distant call of a bird or the creak of a tree branch in the wind. Neville's senses grew attuned to the subtle sounds, his footsteps quiet on the heather-covered slope. He became aware of others behind him, their footsteps light, almost stealthy. Four figures emerged from the mist, their faces obscured by the gloom. They moved with a purpose, their eyes fixed intently on Neville.

He slowed, his heart beating with a growing sense of unease. The four ahead continued on, their gazes flicking back over their shoulders, as if checking on some invisible signal. The four behind drew closer, their movements deliberate and menacing. Neville's instincts screamed at him to flee, but his legs seemed rooted to the spot. The air was heavy with an unspoken threat, the silence between them a palpable thing.

"Why do you follow me?" Neville demanded, his voice barely above a whisper. "What do you want?"

The figure nearest him spoke up, its voice low and menacing. "We're just here to keep you company."

Neville's anger flared, but he bit back the words that threatened to spill out. Instead, he shouldered his stick and quickened his pace, intent on passing the four ahead. The largest of the group shifted to block his path, its eyes glinting in the dim light. Neville's heart pounded in his chest as he raised his stick, ready to defend himself. But the figure merely smiled, its expression twisted and cruel, and Neville felt a shiver run down his spine.
