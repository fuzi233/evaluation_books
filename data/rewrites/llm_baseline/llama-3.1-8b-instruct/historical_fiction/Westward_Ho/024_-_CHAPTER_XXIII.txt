The flickering flames of their campfire danced upon the tranquil surface of the Meta, casting an eerie glow upon the weathered faces of the remaining forty-four men. It had been nearly three years since that fateful eve beneath the towering Guayra tree, where they had first set forth in search of the fabled Golden City. The intervening years had been a blur of uncharted lands, forsaken rivers, and unfulfilled expectations. They had traversed the verdant banks of the Orinoco, braved the maelstrom of Maypures, and ventured into the mist-shrouded depths of the Amazon. In their relentless pursuit, they had traversed the cinchona groves of Loxa, oblivious to the healing properties hidden within. The snow-capped peaks of Chimborazo and Cotopaxi loomed above, their fury a testament to the unforgiving power of nature. Undeterred, they had doubled back eastward, plunging from the craggy Andean cliffs into the emerald vastness of the Montana.

As they navigated the labyrinthine streams that flowed from the majestic Suma Paz, the group had begun to realize that their quest might be a Sisyphean task. The whispers of Manoa's existence continued to tantalize them, only to vanish into the ether, leaving naught but the ache of disappointment. The losses had been staggering – Drew, their stalwart leader, lay entombed on the banks of the Rio Negro, alongside five comrades who had fallen to the poisoned arrows of the Indians. Two others had succumbed to the merciless hail that swept down from the condor's aerie, while four had perished in the treacherous rapids of the Orinoco. A handful of wounded men remained behind, tended by kindly Indians, their fate hanging precariously in the balance. Fever, serpents, jaguars, caimans, and electric eels had taken their toll, leaving behind only the haunting specter of their abandoned march.

Yet, amidst the desolation, the survivors sat, their eyes aglow with an unyielding resolve, their countenances etched with the quiet fortitude of men tempered by hardship. Their beards flowed like silken streams down their chests, their tresses bound with the threads of the Guazu-puti deer's skin, a testament to their adaptability and resourcefulness.
