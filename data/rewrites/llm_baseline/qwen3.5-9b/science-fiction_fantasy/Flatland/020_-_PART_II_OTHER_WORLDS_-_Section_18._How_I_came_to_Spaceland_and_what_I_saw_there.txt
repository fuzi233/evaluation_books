An unspeakable horror seized me. Darkness swallowed the light, replaced by a dizzy, sickening sensation of sight that defied all known laws. I perceived a Line that was no longer a Line; I beheld Space that was not Space. In that terrifying void, I existed as myself and yet ceased to be myself. When I finally regained the power of speech, I shrieked aloud in agony, "Either this is madness, or it is Hell!"

"It is neither," a calm voice replied—that of the Sphere. "It is Knowledge; it is Three Dimensions. Open your eye once again and try to look steadily."

I obeyed. Behold, a new world unfolded before me! Standing visibly incarnate before me was all that I had previously only inferred, conjectured, and dreamed of: perfect Circular Beauty. What appeared to be the center of the Stranger's form lay open to my gaze, yet within I saw no heart, no lungs, no arteries—only a beautiful, harmonious Something for which my vocabulary possessed no name. To you, my readers dwelling in Spaceland, this would simply be called the *surface* of the Sphere.

Prostrating myself mentally before my Guide, I cried out, "How is it, O divine ideal of consummate loveliness and wisdom, that I see thy inside, yet cannot discern thy heart, thy lungs, thy arteries, thy liver?"

"What you think you see, you do not truly see," he answered gently. "It is not granted to you, nor to any other being of Flatland, to behold my internal parts. I am of a different order of existence from those who inhabit your plane. If I were merely a Circle, you might perceive my intestines; but I am a being composed, as I told you, of many Circles—the Many united in the One, known here as a Sphere. Just as the exterior of a Cube appears as a Square, so too does the exterior of a Sphere present itself to your two-dimensional eyes as a Circle."

Though bewildered by my Teacher's enigmatic words, I ceased to struggle against them and instead offered him silent worship. His tone grew milder. "Do not distress yourself if you cannot immediately grasp the deeper mysteries of Spaceland. They shall dawn upon you by degrees. Let us begin by casting a glance backward at the region whence you came. Follow me briefly across the plains of Flatland, and I will show you that which you have often reasoned about and thought upon, but never witnessed with the organ of sight—a visible Angle."

"Impossible!" I exclaimed. Yet, led by the Sphere, I followed as if in a trance until his voice halted me once more: "Look yonder, and behold your own Pentagonal house, and all its inhabitants."

I looked down and saw with my physical eye every detail of domestic individuality that I had hitherto merely deduced intellectually. How poor and shadowy that inference appeared compared to the vibrant reality now revealed! I saw my four sons sleeping peacefully in the northwestern rooms; my two orphan grandsons resting to the south; the servants, the butler, and my daughter, each in their respective chambers. Only my affectionate wife, alarmed by my prolonged absence, had left her room and was pacing anxiously up and down the hall, awaiting my return. Even the page, roused by my earlier cries, had emerged from his chamber, feigning concern over my potential fainting fit while peering into the cabinet of my study.

All this I could now *see*, rather than infer. As we drew nearer, I discerned the contents of the cabinet itself, including the two chests of gold and the tablets mentioned by the Sphere.

Touched by my wife's evident distress, I attempted to spring downward to comfort her, but found myself utterly incapable of motion.

"Do not trouble yourself regarding your wife," said my Guide. "She will not remain in anxiety for long. Meanwhile, let us survey Flatland."

Once more, I felt myself rising through the ether. As the Sphere promised, the further we receded from the focal point, the wider the field of vision expanded. My native city, with the interior of every house and every creature within, lay exposed to my view in miniature. We ascended higher, and suddenly the secrets of the earth were bared: the depths of mines and the innermost caverns of the hills were laid bare before my unworthy eye.

Awestruck by the unveiling of terrestrial mysteries, I turned to my companion and declared, "Behold, I am become as a God. For the sages of our country teach that to see all things—or, as they phrase it, *Omnividence*—is the sole attribute of Deity."

There was a hint of scorn in the Teacher's voice as he responded. "Is that truly so? Then the pickpockets and murderers of my country must be worshipped by your sages as gods, for none of them sees less than you do now. But trust me, your wise men are mistaken."

"I. Then is Omnividence the attribute of beings other than Gods?"

"SPHERE. I do not know. However, if a pickpocket or a cutthroat of your nation can see everything contained within ours, surely that offers no justification for accepting them as deities. Does this 'Omnividence,' a term rare in Spaceland, render you more just, more merciful, less selfish, or more loving? Not in the least. So, how does it make you more divine?"

"I. More merciful, more loving!"

"But these are the qualities of women! And we know that a Circle is a higher being than a Straight Line, insofar as knowledge and wisdom are valued above mere affection."

"SPHERE. It is not for me to classify human faculties according to merit. Yet, many of the finest and wisest souls in Spaceland value the affections more highly than the understanding, and hold your despised Straight Lines in greater esteem than your celebrated Circles. But enough of this. Look yonder. Do you recognize that structure?"

I gazed afar and saw an immense polygonal edifice, which I identified as the General Assembly Hall of the States of Flatland, surrounded by dense rows of pentagonal buildings standing at right angles to one another—I knew these to be streets. I realized I was approaching the great Metropolis.

"Here we descend," announced my Guide.

It was now morning, the first hour of the first day of the two-thousandth year of our era. Acting in strict accordance with precedent, the highest Circles of the realm convened in solemn conclave, just as they had gathered on the first hour of the first day of the year 1000, and likewise on the first hour of the first day of the year 0. The minutes of these prior meetings were read aloud by one I instantly recognized as my brother, a perfectly symmetrical Square and the Chief Clerk of the High Council.

The record stated unequivocally: *"Whereas the States have been troubled by various ill-intentioned individuals claiming to receive revelations from another World, and professing to demonstrate proofs thereby inciting frenzy among themselves and others, it has been unanimously resolved by the Grand Council that on the first day of each millennium, special injunctions be issued to the Prefects of the several districts of Flatland. These injunctions mandate a strict search for such misguided persons, and, without the formality of mathematical examination, to destroy all who are Isosceles of any degree, to scourge and imprison any regular Triangle, to send any Square or Pentagon to the District Asylum, and to arrest anyone of higher rank, transporting them immediately to the Capital to be examined and judged by the Council."*

"You hear your fate," whispered the Sphere to me as the Council passed the formal resolution for the third time. "Death or imprisonment awaits the Apostle of the Gospel of Three Dimensions."

"Not so," I replied. "The matter is now crystal clear to me; the nature of true space is so palpable that I believe I could explain it to a child. Permit me to descend at this very moment and enlighten them."

"Not yet," said my Guide. "The time will come for that. Meantime, I must fulfill my mission. Remain where you are."

With great dexterity, he leaped into the vast expanse of Flatland, plunging directly into the ring of counsellors.

"I come," he proclaimed loudly, "to declare that there exists a Land of Three Dimensions!"
