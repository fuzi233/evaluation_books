In the flickering candlelight, the General's eyes, once a warm and welcoming shade of blue, now seemed to burn with a fierce, unyielding intensity, like embers smoldering beneath the surface of a winter's night. It had been nearly a year since we'd last seen him, and in that time, a weight had settled upon his shoulders, casting a shadow over his features. His rugged face, once creased by the lines of a thousand smiles, now bore the map of a man haunted by unseen demons.

As we drove through the rolling hills, the General's words poured forth like a torrent, a litany of anguish and accusation that shook the air around us. He spoke of his beloved niece, lost to the cruel hand of fate, and the "hellish arts" that had claimed her as their own. His voice rose, a crescendo of outrage and despair, as he wondered aloud how heaven could permit such evil to flourish on earth.

My father, ever the observer, watched the General with a mixture of concern and curiosity, sensing that something profound had shifted within him. He asked the General to recount the events that had driven him to such extremity, and the old man hesitated, as if weighing the risks of sharing his tale.

"I should tell you all, with pleasure," he began, "but I fear you would not believe me."

My father's response was immediate and reassuring. "Why should I not?" he asked, his voice steady and calm. "I'm not a man bound by dogma or prejudice. Try me, I say. I'll listen with an open heart and mind."

The General's expression softened, ever so slightly, as he regarded my father. For a moment, I thought I saw a glimmer of the man he once was, before the weight of his grief and his conviction had taken hold. He nodded, his eyes clouding over once more, as he launched into his tale of the preternatural forces that had ensnared his niece.

"I've been forced to confront the impossible," he said, his voice low and measured. "To believe in things I never thought I'd encounter in this life. I've been deceived, yes, but I've also seen the truth, and it's a thing that cannot be ignored."
