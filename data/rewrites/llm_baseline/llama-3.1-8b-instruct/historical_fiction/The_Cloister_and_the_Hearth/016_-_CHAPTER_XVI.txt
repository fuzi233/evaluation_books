As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow across the cloister's courtyard, Martin Wittenhaagen stood poised at the foot of the stairs, his fingers instinctively tightening around the string of his bow. His eyes, however, betrayed a moment's distraction as he beheld his companions emerging without Gerard. The tension in his body relaxed, and he lowered his bow, a sheepish grin spreading across his face. His men, too, seemed perplexed by the sight of him, their faces a picture of puzzlement.

"What's gotten into you, old fellow?" one of them asked, chuckling. "Were you practicing archery?"

Martin chuckled, shaking his head. "Nonsense! I was simply testing my new string. Watch this." He carefully released the tension, the string slapping against the bow's surface as he demonstrated its suppleness. The men exchanged amused glances, their faces relaxing into smiles.

As they settled in around the crackling fire, the warmth and light banishing the chill of the evening air, the sounds of the kitchen began to waft through the cloister – the sizzle of onions in hot oil, the aroma of roasting meat, and the soft clinking of pots. Margaret's room, however, was a different story altogether. A series of high-pitched cries pierced the air, sending the men leaping to their feet. One of them seized a nearby candle, its flame casting eerie shadows on the walls as he hastened up the stairs.

Martin, caught off guard by the sudden commotion, sprang to his feet, his mind racing with worst-case scenarios. He attempted to intercept the man, but Dierich, ever the pragmatist, quickly intervened, enfolding Martin in a firm grip and calling out to the others to restrain him. In the ensuing chaos, the man with the candle managed to slip away, and the group converged on Martin, pinning him to the ground in a flurry of limbs. It took several moments, and a great deal of exertion, for them to subdue the old man, binding him securely with a length of rope they had brought for Gerard.

As Martin lay helpless, his eyes met those of the man who had escaped to Margaret's room, and he felt a pang of dismay. What could be happening in there? Dierich's laughter, tinged with a hint of malice, only added to Martin's unease.
