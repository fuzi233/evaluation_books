When Arthur Pendennis, swathed in his finest dressing-gown (a relic of better financial days), strolled into Warrington’s chambers the next morning, he found only Mrs. Flanagan, the laundress, holding court over a kingdom of dust and abandoned inkwells. George himself had vanished—slipped away like a phantom, leaving behind nothing but a carpet-bag’s worth of absence and a note that read more like a telegram from a man fleeing creditors than a farewell from a best friend.

*"Dear Pen—Halfway home by breakfast. Christmas in Norfolk. My presence clearly unnecessary. Vale. G.W. P.S. Give my regards to your cousin."*

Pen stared at the note. Such brevity! Such efficiency! Such a *complete* avoidance of emotional labor! George might as well have signed it *"Yours in abruptness."*

Mrs. Flanagan, ever the dramatist, supplied the missing details. *"Found him at his desk, the poor dear, candle burned to a nub, bed untouched! Writing like a man possessed!"* Pen pictured it: George, hunched over his papers, the ink-stained martyr of Grub Street, while the dawn crept in like an uninvited guest. No doubt his latest article was a masterpiece—scathing, brilliant, and possibly dipped in just enough personal bitterness to make it *art*.

Pen sighed. *Why must the best of us always flee?* He glanced around the room—the open drawers, the chewed sealing wax (a nervous habit George would never admit to), the gap on the shelf where Helen’s Bible had been. *Ah. Of course.* The man had taken his heart with him.

Meanwhile, downstairs, Pen discovered another note in his letter-box—this one from Foker, of all people. The new Lord of Logwood and Brewery Fortune had been spotted emerging from a jeweler’s, clad in the kind of mourning attire that suggested grief was best endured with expensive accessories.

*"Foker!"* Pen hailed him.

*"Ah! Yes! Thank you! Very busy! Goodbye!"* Foker blurted, leaping into his black brougham like a man avoiding a subpoena.

Pen watched him go, bemused. *Ah, the mantle of wealth sits uneasily on young shoulders.* One day, you’re a carefree youth; the next, you’re a solemn heir, buying diamond trinkets (for yourself? For a chorus girl? For the ghost of your father’s disapproval?).

At Lady Rockminster’s, the conversation took a turn for the scandalous.

*"That foolish Ann Rosherville!"* the dowager huffed. *"Thrown away fifteen thousand a year to marry her father’s chaplain—a red-haired widower with two children!"*

Pen blinked. *"That’s… impressively self-destructive."*

Laura, ever the romantic, interjected with a sigh. *"Love conquers all."*

*"Love conquers common sense, you mean,"* Lady Rockminster sniffed.

Then came the letters—oh, the letters! Blanche’s effusive invitations, Laura’s determined replies, the *"dearests"* piling up like unpaid bills. And then—the bombshell.

*"What is this letter from Arthur?!"* Blanche’s latest demanded. *"What mystery is this?!"*

Ah. *That* letter.

But that, dear reader, is a revelation for another time. For now, let us leave Pen in suspense—and perhaps a little smug that he’s managed to stir the pot without even trying.
