In the fading light of day, Thomas Sapsea sat amidst the dusty relics of a bygone era. His auctioneer's chair, adorned with the faint scent of old leather, creaked softly as he shifted his weight. The air was heavy with the smell of stale port wine and the faint tang of decay, a reminder that even the most seemingly robust structures can crumble with time. His gaze drifted towards the small, ornate clock on the mantle, its ticking a metronome for the passing hours, a counterpoint to the silence that shrouded the High-street.

As he sat, a faint rustle outside signaled the arrival of his guest, Jasper. Sapsea's eyes narrowed slightly, his fingers drumming a staccato rhythm on the armrests of his chair. He was a man accustomed to being seen, to being heard, and to being regarded with a certain measure of awe. His voice, a low, sonorous drone, was a tool he wielded with precision, a instrument honed to convey the weight of his own importance.

With a flourish, he beckoned Jasper inside, his movements deliberate and measured, like a conductor leading an orchestra through a familiar symphony. As Jasper entered, Sapsea rose from his chair, his stomach rolling out like a gentle swell, and extended a hand. The gesture was one of welcome, but also of condescension, a reminder that he, Thomas Sapsea, was a man of substance and refinement.

"Glad to see you, sir," he said, his voice dripping with honeyed sincerity. "I congratulate myself on having the honour of receiving you here for the first time." The words hung in the air, a challenge, a gauntlet thrown down in the name of his own importance.

Jasper's response was a polite murmur, a gentle acknowledgement of Sapsea's grandeur. The auctioneer's eyes sparkled with pleasure, for in this moment, he was the master of his domain, the center of attention. And as he poured the wine, his hands moving with a studied elegance, he felt the weight of his own significance settle upon him like a cloak.

The glasses clinked together, a tiny, metallic music, and Sapsea smiled, his face a mask of benevolence. For in this quiet, intimate setting, he was the maestro, conducting a symphony of his own devising, a melody of self-importance that echoed through the empty streets of Cloisterham
