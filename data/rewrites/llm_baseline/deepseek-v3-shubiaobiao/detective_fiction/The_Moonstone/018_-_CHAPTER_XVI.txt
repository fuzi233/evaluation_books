The lamplight in my lady’s sitting room was dim, the shade drawn low over her face. She did not lift her eyes when we entered, her gaze fixed stubbornly on the open book before her.

“Officer,” she said, her voice cool, “does your investigation require advance notice if someone in this house intends to depart?”

Sergeant Cuff inclined his head. “It would be most useful, my lady.”

“Then you should know that Miss Verinder has arranged to leave for her aunt’s house in Frizinghall tomorrow morning.”

The Sergeant’s eyes flicked to me. My pulse hammered—whether from dread or anger, I could not say. I opened my mouth, then closed it, my courage failing.

“Might I ask when Miss Verinder informed you of this?” the Sergeant pressed.

“An hour ago,” my lady replied.

Again, the Sergeant’s gaze found mine. My heart pounded as if I were a young man again, though age should have steadied me.

“I’ve no right to hinder Miss Verinder’s movements,” said Cuff, “but I ask that her departure be delayed until afternoon. I must go to Frizinghall myself tomorrow and will return by two o’clock. If she remains until then, I wish to speak with her—unexpectedly—before she goes.”

At my lady’s command, I instructed the coachman to delay the carriage until two.

“Is there more?” she asked, her fingers tightening on the edge of her book.

“Only this: should Miss Verinder question the change, do not mention me as the cause.”

My mistress’s head lifted sharply, as if she meant to speak, but she checked herself. With a dismissive wave, she returned to her reading.

Once in the hall, Cuff exhaled. “A remarkable woman. Had she less restraint, the truth you seek would have been plain tonight.”

Then, like a bolt, understanding struck me. I seized the Sergeant by his collar, slamming him against the wall.

“Damn you!” I snarled. “You’ve known something foul about Miss Rachel all along!”

Cuff didn’t struggle. He merely met my fury with that weary, unshaken stare.

“Ah,” he said. “You’ve caught up at last.”

My grip slackened. Shame flooded me. For fifty years I had served this family. Miss Rachel had been the child who clambered onto my knees, who tugged my whiskers with laughing hands. Faults she might have, but to me, she was still the bright-eyed girl I’d loved as my own.

I muttered an apology, my voice thick.

Cuff waved it off. “In my profession, thin skin is a luxury. If it eases you, grip my collar again—though you’ve no talent for it.” His mouth twitched, as if at some private jest.

I led him to my sitting room and shut the door.

“Out with it, Sergeant. What do you *know*?”

“Not suspect—*know*,” he said. “Miss Verinder has had the Moonstone from the start. She confided in Rosanna Spearman, counting on our suspicion falling on the maid instead. There’s your case, neat as a pin.”

My hands clenched. “Your proof?”

“You’ll have it tomorrow. If Miss Verinder refuses to delay her trip—and she will—I’ll lay the matter before your mistress. Be present. Hear it yourself.” He rose. “Enough for tonight. Supper awaits, and grace must be said.”

I had no appetite. I saw him served, then retreated to the terrace, the night air heavy with coming storm. The sky churned; the dogs whined restlessly. My lady’s message recalled the Indians, soon to be released. Cuff, ever sharp, seized on it—he would seek the traveler mentioned in Seegrave’s report.

Penelope found me, her face drawn. Miss Rachel had raged at the delay, declaring the house intolerable with a policeman under its roof.

“Nothing’s right, Father,” Penelope whispered. “It’s as if doom hangs over us.”

Before I could answer, Rosanna rushed past, her hand pressed to her heart. When I called after her, she twisted away like a creature wounded.

Franklin, emerging, confessed he might have spurred her distress. He’d been at billiards when she approached, trembling, as if on the verge of confession.

“I handled it poorly,” he admitted. “Tell her I meant no cruelty.”

But Rosanna had locked herself away. The night deepened. I found Cuff later, asleep across three chairs near Miss Rachel’s corridor—a sentinel waiting for secrets to unfold.

“The Diamond’s to blame,” he said when I roused him. “Not me.”

I stared into the dark. “I wish to God it had never come here.”

The Sergeant sighed, shifting on his hard bed. “So do I.”
