The Cloister and the Hearth - Chapter LXXX

As I reflect on the pivotal moments that shaped Brother Clement's journey, I find myself drawn to the day he stood before the venerable walls of the cloister in Basle, ready to embark on a new chapter with Brother Jerome. The air was alive with anticipation, and Jerome's triumphant gaze hinted at the grand adventure that lay ahead. "Give the glory to God, Brother Clement," Jerome declared, his voice carrying the weight of expectation. "Thou canst now wend to England with me."

Brother Clement, a man of unwavering dedication, nodded in assent, his eyes aglow with excitement. "I am ready, Brother Jerome; and in the intervals of teaching and devotion, I have studied the English tongue somewhat closely." Jerome smiled, pleased with his companion's preparedness, and revealed that he had delayed their departure until he could obtain extraordinary powers from the Pope to collect money for the Church's use in England and to hear confession in all the secular monasteries.

With the formalities settled, the two friars set forth on their journey, their destination the distant shores of England. As they traveled down the Rhine, they would often preach together in the larger towns, their words weaving a tapestry of faith and conviction. In the smaller villages, they would divide, taking different sides of the river, and reunite at appointed spots, their spirits buoyed by the shared purpose that bound them.

Their oratory styles, though different, complemented each other beautifully. Jerome's voice was like a noble melody, soaring on the wings of eloquence, but occasionally veering into monotony when discussing the intricacies of faith. Clement's words, on the other hand, flowed like a gentle brook, meandering through the landscape of reason and the human heart. His delivery was varied, colloquial, and possessed a quality that stirred the soul.

In his more impassioned moments, Clement's oratory rivaled the greatest prophets and orators of old – "Vehemens ut procella, excitatus ut torrens, incensus ut fulmen, tonabat, fulgurabat, et rapidis eloquentiae fiuctibus cuncta proruebat et perturbabat." I recall the way his words danced on the wind, leaving an indelible mark on the hearts of those who listened.
