The evening sun cast its golden glow over the cobblestone streets of Florence, imbuing the festive atmosphere with a sense of anticipation. At six o'clock, the grand procession of the new Charlemagne finally began to wind its way along the Pisan road, accompanied by the thunderous beat of drums and the blaring fanfare of trumpets. The townspeople, stationed on the rooftops, gazed out at the spectacle with a mix of excitement and trepidation. Though the clouds obscured the sunlight, casting a veil of mystery over the proceedings, the vibrant colors of the banners, spears, and plumes still shone brightly, their intricate designs woven with symbolic meanings that told stories of chivalry and faith.

As the procession approached, the air grew thick with the scent of roasting meats and freshly baked bread wafting from the street vendors. Amidst the chaos, a sense of unease settled over the Signoria, who had gathered at the city gate to welcome the mighty guest. Their orator, Messer Luca Corsini, stood poised to deliver a well-rehearsed welcome, but the sudden downpour drenched the proceedings, sending the dignitaries scrambling to find shelter beneath the nearby awning.

In the midst of the confusion, a chance arose for Tito, a young secretary, to prove himself. As one of the officials stationed behind the Signoria, he found himself thrust forward by the pressing crowd. With a deep bow, he stepped forward and, with the poise of a seasoned diplomat, spoke a few hasty words in French on behalf of the Signoria. His presence of mind, honed by years of navigating the complexities of Florentine politics, allowed him to seize the opportunity with ease. Though he deflected the praise for his quick thinking, attributing it to his colleague Gaddi, Tito's natural aptitude for service had once again proven itself in the heat of the moment.

As the procession continued, the Florentine clergy and laity found themselves forced to take a detour through the narrow backstreets, their planned route to the Cathedral abandoned in favor of a more circuitous path. The young monarch, seated atop his charger beneath the majestic canopy, seemed oblivious to the minor setbacks, his confidence and charm radiating like a beacon through the crowd. Despite the initial missteps, the evening's festivities showed every promise of becoming a grand celebration, one that would be etched in the memories of the Florentine people for years to come
