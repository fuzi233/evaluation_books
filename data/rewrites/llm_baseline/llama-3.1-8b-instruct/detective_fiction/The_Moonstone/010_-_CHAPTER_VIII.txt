As I sit here, quill in hand, I find myself drawn to the events that transpired after Mr. Franklin Blake's arrival and Miss Rachel's birthday. While the days ticked by, filled with nothing of note, I've made a few hasty notes to jog my memory. Penelope's journal proved invaluable in helping me recall the particulars of those quiet weeks.

It was on the morning of the twenty-sixth that I presented Mr. Franklin with the mysterious bottle of sweet-smelling ink I'd discovered on the gravel walk the previous night. He examined it with great interest, his eyes narrowing as he considered the possibilities. "The Indians, no doubt, were lurking about, searching for the Diamond," he said, his voice low and measured. "And they believed in their own magic – the art of conjuring signs on a boy's head, pouring ink into his hand, and expecting him to see beyond the veil of reality." He went on to explain that such practices were not unique to the East, but could be found in our own country as well, where people claimed to possess a heightened sense of sight, which they called 'clairvoyance.' "The Indians, I suspect, assumed we would keep the Diamond here, and they brought their gifted boy to guide them to its location, had they managed to gain entry into the house that night."

I turned to Mr. Franklin with a question. "Do you think they'll try again, sir?" His response was thoughtful. "It depends on the boy's abilities. If he can indeed see the Diamond through the iron safe at Frizinghall, we can rest easy, knowing they won't trouble us again anytime soon. But if he fails, we may yet catch them off guard in the shrubbery before long."

As I waited for the inevitable confrontation, fate had other plans. Whether the jugglers learned of Mr. Franklin's visit to the bank, or the boy truly possessed the gift of clairvoyance, or if it was simply a matter of chance, the truth remains that not a single Indian set foot in our grounds for the weeks leading up to Miss Rachel's birthday. The town was abuzz with their antics, while Mr. Franklin and I watched and waited, resolute in our determination not to alert them to our suspicions.

On the twenty-ninth of the month, Miss Rachel and Mr. Franklin concocted a plan to occupy themselves during the remaining days. This particular endeavor holds significance, and I shall elaborate on it later.
