The soft glow of candlelight danced across the walls of the Abbey as the snowflakes continued their gentle descent outside, casting a serene silence over the winter wonderland. It was the twenty-ninth of December, and Deronda knew the Grandcourts had finally arrived. He had caught glimpses of their arrival, but hadn't had a chance to speak with them yet, too caught up in the joy of snowball fights and snow-building with the Mallinger girls. They had dubbed him "cousin," and he reveled in their affectionate company.

After a spirited bout of billiards, Deronda retired to his room, his mind still reeling from the exertion. As he changed for dinner, his thoughts began to wander to Gwendolen, whose marriage to Grandcourt had left him pondering. He wondered how she would be affected by their union, whether he would notice any changes in her demeanor, just as he had after their previous meetings. Her behavior at Diplow had been a far cry from the carefree girl he'd met at Leubronn, and he suspected that her marriage would only exacerbate this trend.

As he gazed into the mirror, Deronda mused about the nature of personal growth. Some people seemed to evolve at an alarming rate, while others stagnated. He was convinced that Gwendolen fell into the latter category, her strong impressions lingering long after the initial stimulus had faded. The incident with the necklace still lingered in his mind, a testament to her susceptibility to external influences. But this same trait also made her vulnerable to despair as easily as to hope.

Grandcourt, on the other hand, seemed a study in contrasts – dull and unyielding, yet somehow captivating. Deronda couldn't help but feel a twinge of pity for the woman who had chosen him, driven by a desire to escape poverty rather than any genuine affection. He recalled how she had initially avoided Grandcourt, only to be drawn back by the very circumstances that had led her to marry him. It was a sad fate, indeed, for someone so full of life and promise, so prone to making rash decisions without fully considering the consequences.

As he finished dressing, Deronda couldn't shake the feeling that Gwendolen's marriage was a union of convenience rather than love. He wondered if anyone truly pitied Grandcourt, or if his friends and acquaintances merely saw him as a means to an end.
