I had always felt like an outsider among my relatives, a perpetual observer of their antics and intrigues. But today, I was the one embarking on a journey, a journey that felt both necessary and inevitable.

As I packed my bags, a sense of restlessness grew inside me. I had always been drawn to the unknown, the unexplored corners of life where the familiar rules didn't apply. And now, I was finally taking a step towards claiming my place in the world. The thought of Sally, with her bright eyes and mischievous smile, haunted me still. Our last encounter at Monk's Crofton had left me reeling, and I couldn't shake the feeling that I had unfinished business with her.

I opened it to find Uncle Donald standing in the hallway, his massive frame squeezed into a chair that seemed to groan beneath his weight. His walrus moustache quivered as he spoke, his voice a slow, labored drawl that grated on my nerves.

"What's this? What's this?" he asked, his eyes narrowing as he took in the sight of my luggage.

"Yes," I replied curtly, trying to hide my impatience. For the first time in my life, I felt a twinge of guilt, a sense that I was doing something wrong.

"You going away?"

"Yes."

"Where you going?"

"America."

"When you going?"

"To-morrow morning."
