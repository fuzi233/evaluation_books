Her days were filled with devotion, charity, and a stern sense of duty, which she reinforced through her daily prayers and her rigid adherence to the social norms of her time.

Her children, the princes of the house, looked up to her with a mixture of awe and trepidation, aware that to cross their mother was to invite trouble. The local gentry, too, treated her with deference, seeking her counsel and approval on matters ranging from agriculture to etiquette. In the absence of his guiding presence, she found herself at loose ends, struggling to fill the void left by his passing. Her husband, the quiet and unassuming Colonel Esmond, had long since been eclipsed by her own ambition and drive, and she had grown accustomed to referring to herself as Madam Esmond, a name that commanded respect and authority. Yet, despite her outward confidence, a faint tremor ran through her whenever she thought of her father's words of wisdom, which had once tempered her fiery spirit. Now, with no one to rein her in, she found herself lurching from one decision to the next, driven by a mix of pride and a desperate need for validation.
