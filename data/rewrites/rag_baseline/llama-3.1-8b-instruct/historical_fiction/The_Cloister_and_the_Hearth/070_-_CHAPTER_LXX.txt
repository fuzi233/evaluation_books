As I recall the early days of my time within the cloister's stone walls, I am reminded of the novice Gerard's peculiar penance. His self-imposed task was to receive Lodovico kindly, though he saw the man as a slimy serpent. This act of contrition, though initially born of pride and self-satisfaction, soon blossomed into something greater. The more he extended kindness to the man he had saved, the more his heart opened, allowing the sweetness of compassion to seep in. One good deed begets another, and Gerard found himself averse to taking life, then overcome with remorse, and eventually, a genuine sense of penitence.

Teresa, the novice's companion, played a crucial role in this transformation. Her threats to leave him unless he changed his ways served as a catalyst, and the good fathers of the convent lent their support. Together, they dispatched Gerard and Teresa to Leghorn, a port city where Teresa had friends and connections. There, Gerard settled into a humble life as a porter, finding the dull routine of his new existence preferable to the fleeting thrill of being a hired killer. His exile had saved him from a gruesome fate, and the princess, thwarted in her pursuit of revenge, retreated into a profound melancholy.

As the princess brooded, she confided in a new confessor, Fra Jerome, a man renowned for his unwavering commitment to duty. He listened intently as she recounted her grievances, his expression unyielding as the stone walls of the cloister. With a few, well-chosen words, he stripped away her illusions, revealing the true depths of her guilt. "Thou art the devil," he thundered, "that with thy wealth hath tempted one man to commit murder, and then, blinded by self-love, hast sought more blood, but not from those as guilty as thyself."

The princess resisted at first, accustomed to being coddled by her confessors. But Fra Jerome's words struck deep, and he threatened her with the Church's curse, both in this life and the next. His words cut through her defenses, leaving her trembling with remorse and terror at the feet of the imposing Fra Jerome.
