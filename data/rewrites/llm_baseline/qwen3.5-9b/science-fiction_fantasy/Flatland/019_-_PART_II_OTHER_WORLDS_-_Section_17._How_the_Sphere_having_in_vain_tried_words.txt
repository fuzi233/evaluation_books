It proved entirely futile. I braved my sharpest right angle into a violent collision with the Stranger, driving him with a force capable of obliterating any ordinary Circle. Yet, I felt him slip slowly and inexorably from my grasp—not sliding right or left, but somehow exiting the world itself, fading into nothingness. Soon, there was only a void. Still, the Intruder's voice echoed in the silence.

"SPHERE," he called out. "Why do you refuse to listen to reason? I hoped to find in you—given your status as a sensible man and an accomplished mathematician—a worthy apostle for the Gospel of the Third Dimension, which I am permitted to preach but once every millennium. Yet now, I know not how to convince you. Hold fast; I have a new idea. Let deeds, rather than words, proclaim the truth. Listen, my friend.

I have told you that from my vantage point in Space, I perceive the interiors of all things you deem closed. Consider the cupboard near where you stand: I see several of your so-called boxes (which, like everything in Flatland, lack tops and bottoms) filled with coin; I also see two ledgers of account. I am about to descend into that cupboard to retrieve one of these tablets. I observed you locking it half an hour ago, and I know you hold the key. However, since I descend from Space, the doors remain perfectly still. Behold—I am now inside the cupboard, grasping the tablet. Now I ascend with it.

I rushed to the closet and flung the door wide open. One of the tablets was gone. With a mocking laugh, the Stranger materialized in the far corner of the room, and simultaneously, the tablet appeared upon the floor. I picked it up. There could be no doubt—it was the missing ledger. I groaned in horror, questioning my sanity, yet the Stranger persisted:

'Surely you must now see that my explanation alone fits the phenomena. What you call solid objects are merely surfaces; what you call space is nothing but a vast plane. I exist in Space, looking down upon the insides of the very things whose exteriors you alone perceive. You too could leave this plane, were you but summoned with the requisite will. A slight motion upward or downward would grant you sight equal to mine.'

'The higher I mount,' he continued, 'and the further I recede from your plane, the more I can see, though naturally on a diminished scale. Observe: I am ascending. Now I see your neighbor, the Hexagon, and his family within their respective apartments; now I glimpse the interior of the Theatre, ten doors distant, as the audience departs; and on the opposite side, a Circle seated in his study amidst his books. Now I return to you. And as a final proof, what say you to receiving a mere touch, the slightest touch, upon your stomach? It will cause you no serious injury, and the fleeting pain pales in comparison to the mental enlightenment you shall gain.'

Before I could utter a protest, a searing pain shot through my interior, and a demonic laughter seemed to erupt from within me. A moment later, the sharp agony subsided, leaving only a dull ache, as the Stranger began to reappear, growing larger with each passing second.

'There, have I not harmed you greatly?' he asked. 'If you are not convinced now, I know not what will persuade you. What say you?'

My resolve crumbled. It became unbearable to endure existence under the arbitrary whims of a magician who could manipulate one's very innards. If only I could pin him against the wall until help arrived! Once again, I drove my hardest angle into him, simultaneously raising a clamor for aid that alarmed the entire household. I believed that at the moment of my assault, the Stranger had sunk beneath our plane and struggled to rise. Regardless, he stood motionless while I, hearing what I thought were footsteps of approaching rescuers, pressed against him with renewed vigor and shouted for assistance.

A convulsive shudder racked the Sphere. 'This must not be,' I seemed to hear him mutter. 'Either he must submit to reason, or I must resort to the ultimate resource of civilization.'

Then, addressing me in a booming tone, he urgently cried: 'Listen: No outsider must witness what you have seen. Send your wife back immediately before she enters this apartment. The Gospel of the Third Dimension must not be thus thwarted. Not here must the fruits of a thousand years' waiting be cast aside. I hear her approach. Back! Back! Depart from me, or you shall go with me—wherever you know not—into the Land of Three Dimensions!'

'Fool! Madman! Irregular!' I screamed. 'Never shall I release thee; thou shalt pay the penalty of thy deceptions!'

'Ha! Is it come to this?' thundered the Stranger. 'Then meet your fate: Out of your plane you go. Once, twice, thrice! 'Tis done!'
