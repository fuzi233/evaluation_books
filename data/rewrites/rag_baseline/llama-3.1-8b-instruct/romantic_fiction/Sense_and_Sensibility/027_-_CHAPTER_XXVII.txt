As the warm sunlight danced across the breakfast table, Mrs. Jennings smiled brightly, her words spilling forth like the sweet fragrance of freshly baked pastries. "If this mild weather persists, Sir John will be loath to leave Barton next week," she said, her voice a gentle accompaniment to the clinking of cups and saucers. "A shame, really, for sportsmen to miss a day's pleasure."

Marianne's eyes sparkled with delight as she rose from her seat, her gaze drawn to the window where the soft breeze rustled the leaves of the nearby trees. "I hadn't considered that," she exclaimed, her voice full of mirth. "This weather will keep many sportsmen in the country." Her words were like a breath of fresh air, restoring her spirits and imbuing her with a sense of joy.

As she took her seat once more, a warm smile spread across her face. "It's perfect weather for them, isn't it?" she said, her eyes shining with enthusiasm. "How much they must be enjoying it!" Though her thoughts momentarily wavered, a flicker of anxiety dancing across her face, she pushed on, her words a gentle stream. "But, of course, it can't last forever. At this time of year, and after such a prolonged spell of rain, we'll soon see the onset of frost. Perhaps as early as tomorrow, or the day after – though I daresay it's unlikely to last beyond another day or two."

Elinor intervened, her voice a soothing balm to her sister's concerns. "At any rate, I'm sure we'll have Sir John and Lady Middleton in town by the end of next week," she said, her words a gentle reminder of the impending visit. Mrs. Jennings nodded in agreement, her expression a testament to her unwavering confidence in Lady Middleton's ability to command attention.

As the morning unfolded, the sisters set out to leave cards at the homes of Mrs. Jennings's acquaintances, a ritualistic gesture of courtesy and respect. Marianne, meanwhile, found herself drawn to the changing skies, her senses heightened as she watched the wind direction and the shifting patterns of the clouds. "Don't you feel the chill more acutely today, Elinor?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper. "I swear it's grown decidedly colder. Even my muff can't keep my hands warm."
