As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the city, Pietro Vanucci fidgeted with growing irritation. He and Andrea had been waiting for Gerard for what felt like an eternity, and still, there was no sign of his friend. It was nearly nine o'clock, and the kitchen was filled with the enticing aromas of simmering sauces and roasting meats, but Pietro's stomach churned with unease. He dispatched Andrea to Gerard's room, hoping against hope that his friend had slipped in unnoticed.

Andrea returned empty-handed, but with a crumpled piece of paper clutched in his fist. His eyes scanned the scribbled message, his brow furrowed in concentration. Though he couldn't decipher the written words, he had learned to recognize the elegant script of Pietro's writing. With a flourish, he handed the note to Pietro, who seized it with a mixture of trepidation and amusement.

As Pietro read the message aloud, his voice dripped with sarcasm. "'Dear Pietro, dear Andrea, life is too great a burden.' Ah, indeed it is, my boy, but that's no excuse for missing supper. Supper is not a burden, it's a feast!" Andrea's eyes widened as Pietro continued to read, his voice growing more serious. "'Wear my habits!' Ha! Said the poplar to the juniper bush." The words trailed off, and Pietro's expression turned somber.

The message was a jarring mix of melancholy and desperation. Gerard's words spoke of a troubled heart, a heart heavy with sorrow and longing. Pietro's eyes locked onto Andrea's, and he knew they had to act swiftly. The kitchen fell silent as Andrea's anguished cries echoed through the halls, summoning the household staff to gather around him.

"Oh, woe is me! Oh, calamity of calamities! Gerard, my friend, my patron! Help, help! He's taking his own life! Good people, please, help me save him!" The commotion drew a small crowd of onlookers, and soon, a handful of brave souls agreed to accompany Andrea on a perilous quest to find Gerard.
