As the last remnants of sunlight faded beyond the windows of the White Horse Tavern, the three gentlemen decided to retire to the adjacent bowling-green, where they could sip a cool bottle and enjoy the tranquil atmosphere. Mr. Morris suggested the excursion, and my lord quipped that Jack's true intention was to seek another excuse for a fresh drink. Jack replied that each gentleman had his own preference for indulgence, and his weakness lay in a good bottle.

Lord Chesterfield chuckled and said that my lord March's favorite pastime was a trifecta of cards, dice, and high-stakes wagers. "His lordship cannot resist the allure of the game," he said with a grin. My lord March shot back, "I have a few vices, to be sure – I love the thrill of competition, the rush of horse racing, the art of negotiation, and the satisfaction of acquiring new possessions." He added, "And you, Mr. Warrington, have fallen into my company, where fortune may smile upon you, or perhaps not."

Mr. Warrington smiled good-naturedly and replied, "With great pleasure, my lord, I'm always eager to test my luck." Lord March's eyes sparkled with amusement as he proposed a wager on Mr. Warrington's estate in Virginia, should his mother, Lady Warrington, precede him in death. "I'll stake a sum now against four times the amount to be paid at her passing," he said, his voice dripping with confidence.

Mr. Warrington hesitated, citing that his mother's longevity was uncertain, and he couldn't guarantee the future value of his estate. Lord March laughed and said, "I'll take that chance, my dear fellow. Your family's history is storied, and I've heard tales of your grandfather's exploits from my own Duchess. He was a man of letters, acquainted with Addison, Steele, and even the great poets of the day." He leaned in, his voice taking on a conspiratorial tone, "It's a shame he chose to leave England for the colonies, leaving behind a legacy that's been somewhat... exaggerated among the gentry."

Mr. Warrington revealed that he'd recently visited Castlewood, his family's plantation in Virginia, and had engaged in a few games of cards with his cousin, playing for modest stakes.
