After the devastation that befell the Ten-Thousand, and the chilling realization of what the Night Land truly harbored, there could be no thought left for rescue missions. Those youths venturing onto the Path Where the Silent Ones Walk were now too far beyond our reach to aid.

Yet, one might ask why we did not signal them using the Home-Call—the tremendous resonance emanating from the Machine atop the sealed base of the Mighty Pyramid. We dared not. To activate that Voice would be to broadcast to the Monsters of the Night Land that survivors were fleeing the Pyramid. While we hoped the Evil Powers remained ignorant of their whereabouts, the truth was that no mortal could fathom the depth of knowledge—or ignorance—those forces possessed.

However, we knew a subtle, strange influence lingered in the land, one that the Instruments barely registered. Our conviction was strong that this emanated from the House of Silence, a grim edifice situated on a low hill north of the Great Road. Many of the Monstruwacans suspected it targeted the youths, though we lacked certainty. All we could do was wait and watch.

Soon, the youths approached the northern bend of the Path, drawing dangerously close to that terrifying house. Suddenly, the influence in the land swelled with power. My intuition, though unproven, screamed that it originated from the House. I shared this unease with the Master Monstruwacan, who trusted my judgment and shared his own suspicion of a secret malignancy radiating from the House.

Whispers circulated of sending the Home-Call to warn the youths and urge a swift return, but the Master forbade it. It would not be right to endanger their souls based on mere fear. The Home-Call, a relic of the ancient flying-ships, carried a noise so immense it would instantly reveal the escapees' location to every monster in the region. Only when absolute certainty of doom was reached should we stir.

A day and a night passed. Multitudes peered into the Night Land, watching the youths approach their fate. Rumors flew, mixed with foolish speech and kind intentions, yet no courage emerged to launch another rescue attempt. The atmosphere shifted; the land woke, unquiet, filled with a sense of impending doom and horrid vigilance. Low roars echoed across the terrain. If I had not recorded this earlier, it is a failing on my part, for the difficulty of recounting these terrors is immense. I implore patience and understanding.

During this time, the youths had slept not, nor eaten, save for a single meal witnessed by the watchers behind the Great Spy-Glass. They hurried north along the Great Dismal Road at a woeful pace, destined to collapse or kill themselves with exhaustion soon. This relentless march confirmed our fears: they were under a spell from the House of Silence.

Suddenly, a Monstruwacan reported a massive influx of influence. Simultaneously, through the Spy-Glass, I saw the youths abruptly abandon the Path, breaking into a frantic run toward the House of Silence. The Master hesitated not. He unleashed the Home-Call across the world, directing it specifically at the doomed runners. Immediately, he transmitted a coded message to the natural eyes of the populace, warning them that the youths were being dragged toward destruction by a force issuing from within the House. He urged them to summon the strength of their spirits to battle for the souls of the boys, and if victory over the pull proved impossible, to slay themselves before crossing the threshold into utter annihilation.

A profound silence fell over the Pyramid, born of the Home-Call's terrifying portents. Millions understood the Master's plea for their lives. Unknowingly, a counter-force surged from the Pyramid, fueled by the prayers and collective wills of hundreds of thousands. I heard this spiritual surge beat through the aether, a wave of supplication so powerful it stunned my spirit. It seemed a vast, spiritual noise filled the night. Trembling, I watched the Spy-Glass as the youths stopped their sprint. They clustered together, confused, as if waking from a nightmare to find themselves in a strange place.

Then, a roar erupted from the embrasures—five hundred thousand voices rising like a triumphant gale. But it was premature. The unified will of the multitude broke, overwhelmed by the sheer intensity of the Evil Force pouring from the House. The youths, heedless of salvation, turned once more to their flight.

Silence returned to the Pyramid, shattered instantly by lamentation and horror. Then, a fresh miracle occurred. Before the fleeing youths, billows of mist materialized—not ordinary fog, but something akin to pure white fire, shining with a chilling brilliance that offered no warmth. These waves of "cold fire" halted their progress. We knew a Sweet Power of Goodness was fighting for their souls, striving to ward off the destructive forces of evil. The millions beheld this vision; some with crystal clarity, others with doubt, yet all elevated beyond normal perception.

None possessed the Night-Hearing required to catch a soul's speech across worlds, though some, like I, had been gifted with it in former ages. A Monstruwacan reported that the instruments no longer detected the influence. The force from the House was severed from the youths, and a mighty Power was actively saving them. Hope and fear rippled through the silent crowds.

While the youths wavered, the Master Monstruwacan sounded the Home-Call again. He implored them, for the sake of their souls and the love of their mothers, to return home while the protective shield held. Some appeared to turn toward the Pyramid, reading his message. But then, they spun about. They obeyed unseen commands, following a leader named Aschoff—a great athlete of the Ninth Hundred City. Unwittingly, Aschoff acted to destroy them all. He stepped forward and leaped into the barrier of bright, shining fire. Instantly, the mist collapsed, sinking into nothingness. Aschoff resumed his run toward the House, followed faithfully by the rest.

They ascended the low hill bearing the horrid House, two hundred and fifty wholesome, innocent souls, save for a natural waywardness. They entered the great open doorway—"open since the Beginning"—where cold, steadfast light and inscrutable silence had forged an eternal, felt silence. Uncased windows poured out the utter desolation of unholy stillness. Aschoff ran inside, and they followed. None ever emerged, nor were they seen by any human eye again.

Mothers and fathers stared into the Night Land, witnessing the tragedy. The people fell silent, though rumors arose that the youths might return. Deep down, everyone knew they had gone to destruction. There was a horror in the night that spoke to the souls of all, accompanied by a sudden, total quiet.

To me, who possessed the Night-Hearing, a terrible fear whispered into my spirit—the agony of those young men. Yet, no sound reached my ears, then or in the years to come. They had passed into a Silence the heart cannot conceive.

This strange, brooding quiet that filled the land was far more horrific than any roaring that had preceded it. It offered a grim respite compared to the distant thunder of the Great Laughter, the whining from the Silver-fire Holes near the Thing That Nods, the bay of hounds, or the roar of giants. This silence, holding so much horror, was unbearable.

Consequently, no thought remained of succoring the peoples of the Lesser Redoubt, whose location remained unknown. They were condemned to suffer and perish alone, a sad and dreadful reality. The Great Pyramid had already endured sorrow enough from previous failed attempts. Now, the shadow of the lost youths hung heavy over us.

This knowledge—that we could offer no succor—took root deep in my heart. I had foolishly clung to vague hopes of a secret expedition into the Night to discover the Lesser Pyramid and rescue those thousands. Above all, I dreamed of a sweet moment stepping out of the mystery, putting forth my arms to Naani, declaring, "I am That One." In my soul, I knew she would recognize me instantly, calling out, and returning to me just as she had in eternity.

To realize this would never happen, that she might be suffering horror at the claws of some foul monster, felt like madness. Nearly, I seized the Diskos to rush unprepared into the evil and terror of the Night, seeking her or casting off my life. Often, I called to Naani, sending the Master-Word beating through the night to assure her it was I speaking, not a foul thing weaving lies. I instructed her never to leave the shelter of the Redoubt, to await only the Master-Word, and to know that no friend would ever entice her into the dark.

Thus, I spoke to Naani, sending my thoughts via brain-elements, yet it was doleful and wearisome to speak into the void without hearing the answering beat of the Master-Word. Occasionally, the aether thrilled around me, and the Master-Word beat faintly, offering a sliver of comfort that her love-maid spirit still lived. Constantly, I strained to listen, my health failing under the effort. I chided myself for lacking wisdom, fighting to remain sane.

Day by day, my heart grew weary and restless. Life seemed trivial against the loss my heart felt. Voices spoke plainly from the night, purporting to be Naani's, uttered with anguish. Always, I answered with the Master-Word, silencing them. I did not scorn them, letting them bide their time, shutting them from my memory to focus on sweet and holy truths, particularly Naani.
