Silence enveloped the Nuns' House like a shroud, punctuated only by the faint hum of the wind outside. Rosa's memories swirled in her mind like autumn leaves on a forgotten stream. She recalled the summer day her mother drowned, her image frozen in time: a pretty, delicate thing, young and carefree, with petals of ruined flowers tangled in her long hair. The memory of her father's grief, his wild despair, and subsequent broken heart lingered, a heavy weight that had never fully dissipated.

Years passed, and Rosa's life became a tapestry of kindness and attention. People took turns consoling and coddling her, each trying to win her favor. The rivalry for her affection created subtle tensions within the Nuns' House, where veiled resentments simmered beneath the surface. Rosa, however, remained an amiable and affectionate soul, her heart overflowing with love for those around her. Yet, her dependence on the kindness of others had never truly tested her own depths.

The news of a quarrel between Mr. Neville and Edwin Drood arrived at the Nuns' House like a whispered rumor, carried on the wind or perhaps embedded in the very fabric of the morning air. By the time Miss Twinkleton emerged from her chambers, the gossip had already seeped into every corner of the old building. According to the whispers, Miss Landless's brother had hurled a bottle, a knife, and then a fork at Edwin Drood. The stories merged and evolved, like a folk tale passed down through generations, each iteration adding a new layer of detail to the narrative.

As Rosa went about her day, the events of the previous night began to take shape in her mind. She felt a flutter in her chest, a mixture of curiosity and concern for the young men involved. The Nuns' House, once a sanctuary of peace and tranquility, now seemed to vibrate with the unspoken tensions between its residents. Rosa's usually bright and carefree spirit began to falter, as the prospect of change and uncertainty loomed on the horizon.
