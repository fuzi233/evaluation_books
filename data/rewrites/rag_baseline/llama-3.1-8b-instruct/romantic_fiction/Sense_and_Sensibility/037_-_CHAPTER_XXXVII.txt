The kitchen of the Dashwood family's modest townhouse in Berkeley Street was filled with the warm, comforting aromas of simmering soup and freshly baked bread. It had been a fortnight since Mrs. Palmer's health had taken a turn for the better, and her mother, Mrs. Palmer's aunt, had begun to resume her daily routine, visiting her cousin in the mornings and spending her afternoons with her own family.

As she entered the drawing-room, where Elinor sat sewing by the window, Mrs. Jennings's voice carried the unmistakable hint of excitement. "Lord, Miss Dashwood! Have you heard the news?" Elinor looked up, her hands stilled, and Mrs. Jennings continued without pause, "Something so extraordinary! I've just come from Mrs. Palmer's, and I found her in quite a state over the child. It was crying, fretting, and covered in pimples. So I took a look, and I'm convinced it's just a case of the red gum. Nurse agreed, and Mr. Donovan concurred, saying it was nothing more than the red gum."

Elinor's curiosity was piqued, and she leaned forward, asking, "What else did he say?" Mrs. Jennings's expression turned sly, and she glanced around the room before leaning in closer. "Well, I asked him if there were any other news, and he seemed to know something he wasn't telling us. He smirked and simpered, but eventually revealed that there was no cause for concern about Mrs. Dashwood's health. However, I gathered from the way he spoke that there might be some... unpleasantness surrounding it."

Elinor's eyes widened, and she asked, "Is Fanny ill?" Mrs. Jennings nodded, her voice barely above a whisper. "Yes, it appears so. But that's not the worst of it, my dear. It seems Mr. Edward Ferrars, the young man we used to tease about, has been secretly engaged to my cousin Lucy for over a year. Can you imagine such a thing? It's a wonder nobody suspected anything, especially given how fond they all are of Lucy. I suppose it's not surprising, though, considering their families have been acquainted for years."
