For an hour I carried the Maid down through the depths of the Gorge, as I had said. By then, my strength was nearly spent—my legs trembled with fatigue, and each step felt like a battle against gravity itself. My feet no longer obeyed me with certainty; they dragged, unsteady, as if the very will to walk had begun to slip from me. I knew I could not go much farther without collapsing—either myself or both of us would fall, and that would be the end.

I forced myself to focus on the cliffs around us, though for much of that hour I had walked in a kind of trance. Only now, as I regained awareness, did I truly see where we were. The Gorge seemed new to me, as though I had awakened mid-step into a dream. And yet—memory stirred. I recalled the journey outward, every twist and turn, every landmark etched into my mind. And then I saw it: ahead, glowing faintly red, was the fire-hole—a familiar sight. Nearby, on the cliff face, I remembered seeing a cluster of small caves when I passed this way before. At the time, I had longed for such a place—to bring her here, safe and sheltered, away from the horrors that pursued us.

Now, that longing was about to be fulfilled. It was almost too good to be true. But I did not let joy blind me. There was still danger. Even here, deep within the Gorge, some monstrous thing might descend from above, drawn by our presence, and strike while we slept. I wished we had gone deeper still, but my body refused to carry on. The topmost cave—seven in all, carved into the left wall—would have to do. If we reached it, few creatures could climb up without warning. And even if they did, they would have to face the Diskos—and that, I knew, would be no easy task.

I gently roused the Maid. She lay in my arms, breathing softly, and when I kissed her forehead, she answered with a sleepy press of her lips. Her eyes fluttered open, half-lidded, dazed. I loved her so completely that my heart ached with it. I kissed her again, shook her lightly, and whispered that we had found a place to rest. She stood slowly, blinking, her limbs stiff with sleep, and scolded herself quietly for falling asleep while I bore her weight.

But I only smiled and kissed her once more. She looked at me, still half-asleep, her face soft and radiant, and said, “I love you—forever,” in a voice barely above a whisper. It was pure, unguarded truth.

Then I climbed to the highest cave, calling to her to walk a little while to wake fully. I wanted her alert, strong, ready to climb. When I reached the opening, I gasped. The cave was dry, warm, and sweet-smelling. A low heat seeped from somewhere deep in the rock, and the flickering light from the fire-hole below cast dancing shadows across the walls. It was quiet, secluded—perfect for rest.

I returned to her, calling out that the cave was safe. She followed, clinging to me as we ascended. We made it inside, breathless but whole, and for a moment, the world felt right. We were protected.

Before sleep, I freed the scrip and pouch, took the straps, and descended again into the Gorge. I found a heavy boulder—just manageable—and secured it to my back. With great care, I carried it back up and placed it at the mouth of the cave, balanced just so that a slight touch would send it tumbling down. Any creature attempting to enter would likely trigger it, and the noise would wake us. That was the purpose—not to kill, but to warn.

We settled in. She spread the cloak over the stone floor, a soft bed for both of us. I didn’t need it—my armor was too thick to feel comfort—but she insisted. It wasn’t for warmth, but for closeness. She arranged it with gentle hands, careful not to lose her modesty, yet unable to resist being near me. It was natural, tender, and deeply intimate. I loved her more than words could say.

She knelt beside me, kissed my lips softly, then lay down beside me. Within moments, we both slipped into sleep.

Twelve hours later, I woke to the hiss of water. The Maid was already awake, preparing our meal. She laughed when she saw me stirring—bright, sweet, full of affection. She came to me, kissed me, and knelt beside me, her gaze full of quiet devotion. I knew, without speaking, that she had watched me while I slept—like a mother guarding her child. How I knew, I couldn’t say. Perhaps it was in her eyes, or in the way her thoughts brushed mine.

I needed that love. All of us do. I reached for her, and she came into my arms, snuggling close with a joy so pure it made my chest ache. The armor between us was hard, unyielding—but she didn’t seem to mind. Or perhaps, in her own way, she found comfort in its strength. Still, I feared hurting her, and so held her gently.

After a while, she pulled away, and I let her go. I always honored her freedom, never forcing closeness. Yet I guarded it carefully—not out of fear, but because she deserved nothing less than reverence.

She went to the water and tablets, bringing them to me. She wouldn’t let me rise. Instead, she cradled my head in her lap, kissed my lips once, and fed me a tablet—then touched another to my lips, saving one for herself. We ate slowly, contentedly, like children sharing a secret. Then we drank.

When we finished, she asked me to stand. I hesitated—then rose, and nearly groaned. Every muscle screamed. I was stiff, bruised, worse than before. I hadn’t realized how badly I’d been hurt by the Four-Armed Man. But she had known. She had prepared the ointment, waiting.

She helped me remove the armor, then my clothes. The bruises were deep, visible beneath my skin. She looked at them with such tenderness that I felt a warmth spread through me. She laid me down, wrapped me in the cloak, and began to rub the ointment into my flesh with skilled, gentle hands. For an hour, she worked, soothing my pain until I felt renewed.

As she cared for me, I listened to the distant mutter of the fire-pits below—the sound of a world alive, yet peaceful here. I felt like a child cradled in safety, loved and guided. When she finished, she handed me my spare vest, but paused—her eyes wide with admiration.

She studied my arms, the muscles hard and defined from years of training. I had been raised in the traditions of the Mighty Pyramid, trained in strength and discipline. My body was shaped by both nature and nurture—by the woman who bore me, and by the life I lived. And now, she looked at me as if I were something beyond ordinary.

Her gaze softened. She dropped the vest and reached for me. I took her into my arms, trembling with emotion. She rested there quietly, then pressed her face to my chest—kissing the ridges of my muscles with a sweetness that made my heart swell. Then, suddenly, she stepped back and helped me dress.

When I stood again, she looked at me—half shy, half playful. She reached up, touched my shoulders, lowered her lashes, and stole glances upward. Then, without warning, she fell to her knees before me and wept.

I knelt beside her instantly.

I didn’t ask why. I understood. She was overwhelmed—by love, by awe, by the depth of what she felt. She saw me not just as her lover, but as a hero. And in that moment, I felt both proud and humbled. I was worthy of her love, yes—but also unworthy. I resolved not to pretend otherwise, nor to diminish myself in her eyes. I would earn her respect, not demand it. And I would accept her worship—not as arrogance, but as a gift.

I know I am not perfect. I have faults, as any man does. But I also know that love can make us greater. Think back to your own youth—how your beloved made you feel strong, noble, capable. That is what she did for me. And it is human. It is real.

Eventually, she calmed. She wiped her eyes, nestled close, and whispered her needs—quiet, trusting, utterly herself. I held her, feeling both father and lover in one. I slipped off her shoes, something I should have done earlier. I chided myself—then stopped. She was hurt by my self-reproach. I ceased, but the guilt lingered.

After tending to her feet, we gathered our gear and descended from the cave, careful, cautious. We moved down the Gorge, slower now, mindful of the path ahead. In six hours, we stopped to eat. I bathed her feet in warm water from a rock basin, rubbed them thoroughly with ointment, and eased her pain.

We continued onward, but more deliberately. I recalled the journey outward—now I knew we were nearing the Slugs’ territory. That stretch would take twelve hours or more, with no rest. We needed strength. We needed sleep.

So we searched for a place to rest. And there it was—the same cave where I had slept after escaping the Slugs. The spring, the fire-hole, everything was unchanged. We both exclaimed in wonder. She was thrilled to stand where I had once lain. It felt meaningful—almost sacred.

I checked the cave first, ensuring no creeping things lurked. Then we went to the hot spring. I inspected the waters—no snakes, no rat-things. She sat on the edge, and I removed her shoes, bathed her feet, and rubbed them with care, watching the Gorge all the while.

When done, we returned to the cave and gathered boulders. Together, we built a rough wall across the entrance, high enough to block most threats. I sealed the lower gaps tightly, so no small creature could sneak through. Then she set the scrip and pouch beside me as a pillow—her torn garments folded neatly beside her.

She placed the Diskos within reach, her fingers trembling slightly. The weapon terrified her—but it also comforted her. Then she covered me with the cloak and kissed my lips once, solemnly. She curled beside me, happy, secure.

She fell asleep quickly. I stayed awake for a while, watching her breathe. Then, slowly, she turned toward me, nestling close like a child. I longed to hold her, to kiss her—but I did not move. I respected her trust, even in sleep.

Eventually, I drifted into slumber. Eight hours passed before I woke to the hissing of water—she had risen early, as planned, preparing our food.

When I stirred, she smiled and rushed to me, wrapping her arms around my neck. She kissed me three times—soft, loving, lingering. In that moment, I remembered—I had dreamed of her kissing me. But now I knew: she had taken advantage of my sleep, kissing me while I was unconscious. And she said nothing. Neither did I. But I decided—next time, I would wake just in time to catch her in the act.

It sounds foolish. But it was true.

I rose, dismantled part of the wall, and looked out. The Gorge was still. Only one rat-thing lay asleep near the fire-hole, bloated and motionless.

We were safe—for now.
