The rain clouds gathered above Florence like a dark veil, casting a melancholy shadow over the city's cobblestone streets. As Romola, accompanied by Maso and the soft glow of their lantern, made her way back from the hospital of San Matteo, she spotted her husband, Tito, emerging from the monastery of San Marco. The flickering light of the lantern danced across the stone façade as Tito approached her, his eyes cast downward, lost in thought. With a courteous bow, he invited himself to escort her home, dismissing Maso, whose rapid pace had grown tiresome to him. This was a gesture of obligation rather than affection, a duty he performed with ease, as was their custom.

As they strolled through the quiet streets, Tito suggested a supper in the old library, now transformed into a cozy banquet hall. Romola agreed, her hands moving deftly to ensure every detail was attended to. Tito, in turn, respected her autonomy, leaving her to manage her daily routines, while he accepted her assistance with his work, transcribing documents and making digests. Their evenings together were a delicate balance of give-and-take, each respecting the other's boundaries, yet never truly bridging the chasm between them.

In the early days of her resolve to reconcile their marriage, Romola had made tentative overtures, hoping to rekindle their connection. However, she soon realized that such a reunion could only be achieved through open dialogue, a mutual understanding of their differences. Tito, on the other hand, preferred to maintain a safe distance, avoiding confrontation, and instead, sought to rekindle her affection, as if the past could be erased. He was content to accept her submission and gentleness, but genuine tenderness was a quality he seemed unwilling to reciprocate.

Their conversation flowed easily, discussing public and private matters, yet always within the bounds of their predetermined roles. Romola couldn't shake off the feeling that her husband was hiding secrets, particularly concerning his involvement with the monastery of San Marco. Her intuition whispered warnings, urging her to be cautious, yet she couldn't resist the temptation to observe, to watch for any sign that might confirm her suspicions.
