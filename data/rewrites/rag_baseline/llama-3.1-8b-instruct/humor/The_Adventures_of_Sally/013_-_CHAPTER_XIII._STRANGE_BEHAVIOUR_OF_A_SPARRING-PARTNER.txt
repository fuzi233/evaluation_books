Sally stood in front of the mirror, smoothing out wrinkles in her dress. Her eyes, still adjusting to the morning light, seemed to hold a lingering fatigue, a testament to the weariness that lingered within. It was a feeling she'd grown accustomed to over the past few months, a sensation that ebbed and flowed like the ebb and flow of a melancholic melody. Yet, today was different. Today, she felt a spark within her, a flame that had been flickering in the darkness, waiting to be fanned into a blaze.

She'd spent the morning unpacking and tidying up, the mundane tasks a gentle reminder that she was indeed back in New York, surrounded by the familiar rhythms of city life. As she gazed at her reflection, a sudden urge washed over her – she needed to see Ginger. His steady presence, a constant in her life, was the one thing that could soothe her frayed edges. She recalled the dock, the echoing customs sheds that had left her feeling lost and alone, and a pang of regret stirred within her. If only she'd sent him a cable, they could've met at the dock, and she wouldn't have felt so adrift.

Sally checked her watch, surprised to find it was still early. She could catch him at the office and convince him to take her out to lunch. A plan formed in her mind, and she headed out into the bright morning light. The streets were alive with the restless energy of the city, but Sally's footsteps were deliberate, her heart beating to a different rhythm.

As she entered the office building, a sense of disorientation washed over her. The outer office was different, the young clerk who'd replaced her acquaintance from her previous visit now standing behind the counter. His face, a map of pimply adolescence, seemed chiseled from granite, his manner a mask of stern suspicion. "I want to see Mr. Kemp," Sally said, her voice clear and steady.
