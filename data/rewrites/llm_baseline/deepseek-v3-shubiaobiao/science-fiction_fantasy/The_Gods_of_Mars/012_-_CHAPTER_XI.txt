The next dawn found Xodar and me deep in strategy. With the sharp edge of the gem I had taken from Sator Throg and a buckle from my harness, he etched a crude but serviceable map of the southern polar wastes onto the stone floor. I traced the path to Helium, calculating distances from the hidden passage to Omean. Next came a layout of Omean itself—Shador’s position, the dome’s exit—each line burned into my memory.

Xodar described the guards’ routines: a single sentinel during sleep hours, tracing a sluggish circuit around the prison. Ten minutes per round. Five minutes of vulnerability on each side.

*"This is all well for *after* escape,"* Xodar muttered. *"But how do we breach these walls?"*

I grinned. *"Watch."*

In one fluid motion, I leaped to the window’s bars, scaling them like a cat. Beyond, a dozen ships—some small, two massive warships—lay anchored near Shador’s shore.

*"Tonight,"* I began, but the cell door groaned open. A guard stood framed in the entrance, his back to me.

Five feet above me ran the partition wall to the next cell. My only hope. If he turned, I was doomed; dropping would land me atop him. I braced against the grating, vaulted silently to the wall’s edge, and slipped over just as the guard snarled, *"Where’s the white worm? Issus demands him!"*

Xodar played his part. *"He was here moments ago. Must you lose prisoners so easily?"*

As the guard stormed out to search adjoining cells, I dropped back beside Xodar.

*"Still doubt our escape?"* I whispered.

*"You bounce like a *sith* in mating season,"* he grumbled. *"But these bones won’t follow."*

The guard returned, eyes bulging at my reappearance. *"By the bones of Issus! Where were you?"*

*"Where you left me,"* I scoffed. *"Perhaps your eyes serve you as poorly as your wits."*

Snarling, he hauled me out, leaving Xodar behind. The red Martian youth from the neighboring cell joined us, his face hauntingly familiar—the set of his jaw, the way he moved—yet I couldn’t place him. Guards kept us apart as we marched through the gardens of Issus, past walls crawling with plant-men and white apes, toward a colossal amphitheater.

The arena was a slaughterhouse dressed in splendor. Issus perched on her throne, a grotesque idol flanked by jeweled slaves. Below, cages disgorged prisoners. The youth and I were herded into a pen with others, some chained, all resigned.

*"What’s this?"* I asked him.

*"The *Rites of Issus*,"* he said bitterly. *"Blacks wash their sins in our blood. If they die, it proves disloyalty. If they live, they’re absolved. We fight—against numbers, beasts, champions. Victory buys only more battles. Death is our only freedom."*

*"Often. A hundred blacks have fallen to me."* Pride flickered in his eyes. *"My father’s shadow is long."*

Before I could ask more, guards drove us toward the arena’s bowels. Cages lined the pit’s edge. Issus’ throne loomed opposite, surrounded by her elite, their slaves trembling like leaves in a storm.

Then the horror began.

Ten thern women—once proud, now broken—were paraded before Issus. A hulking black inspected them like cattle, selecting the plumpest. *"Dinner for the goddess,"* a prisoner spat.

Next, three white apes burst into the arena. The women huddled, screams piercing the air. One knelt, begging Issus, who leaned forward, saliva glistening on her chin.

Rage blinded me.

The guard at my cage gaped as I struck him down, snatched his sword, and leaped into the pit. The crowd roared as my blade sheared through an ape’s neck. The remaining beasts turned on me.

Behind me, the youth’s voice rang out: *"Men of the outer world! Die with swords in hand! Turn this tribute into *their* nightmare!"*

Pandemonium erupted. Prisoners stormed the racks, arming themselves. The youth fought like a demon, his speed uncanny—Martian, yet mirroring my own Earth-forged agility. Together, we carved through guards, forming a ring around the terrified women.

*"Fifty of you—guard them!"* I ordered. Then, raising my bloodied sword: *"To the throne! Topple Issus!"*

The cry spread like wildfire. *"To the throne!"*

We became a tidal wave of vengeance. Nobles rushed to defend their goddess, but we cut them down. The stands erupted as slave women turned on their masters, daggers flashing, teeth bared. *"Rise, slaves!"* became their war chant.

Issus’ face twisted in fear. Her guards fell until only a thin line stood between us. Phaidor, daughter of Matai Shang, appeared suddenly, clutching my would-be killer’s arm. *"Fly!"* she begged. *"You’re the last! Escape to the Golden Cliffs—tell my father!"*

But retreat wasn’t in me. Not while Issus still drew breath.

The youth and I lunged forward, blades singing, hell itself howling at our backs.
