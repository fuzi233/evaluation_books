From our vantage just below the Sacred Mountain’s crest, the city sprawled beneath us like a broken map. The harbor’s distant glimmer, the fuming hills, the stark stone circle of the Sun—all lay etched in the eerie glow of dawn. But my gaze fixed on the royal pyramid, its gilded sides ablaze with stolen light, and on the granite throne before it. There, for nine long years, my love had slept, sealed away by my own hands.

Zaemon followed my stare. “Aye,” he sighed, “I see where your heart lingers. The Deucalion who landed from Yucatan was a man of stone. The king we’ve chosen now has his weaknesses.”

“Then let Nais be my strength. Give her back to me, living and warm, and I’ll be more than that cold viceroy ever was.”

“You’ll have her,” he said grimly. “But the cost may crack this new kingdom of yours. And Phorenice—she’s no fool. She’ll sniff out our game before the final move.”

“I’ve seen her toy with the earth’s bones. What’s your plan?”

“Patience. Tonight, you descend into the city. You’ll take Nais when she’s freed and bring her here. And for that, you’ll need every scrap of strength left in you.” His eyes raked over me. “No one will recognize the polished Deucalion in this wild-haired savage. Just pray no rich fool mistakes you for a slave.”

I bared my teeth. “He’ll regret it.”

Zaemon’s rare laugh was short-lived. “Get your rest. I must prepare in the Ark of Mysteries. The powers we wield tonight aren’t gentle. If we don’t meet again—” He clasped my arm. “Farewell, brother.”

***

I slept like a soldier—deep and dreamless—and woke to darkness thick enough to drown in. The moon had fled; the air reeked of impending doom. The rope lowered me into the abyss, and I landed among tumbled rocks, the cliff a shadow at my back. Phorenice’s sentries huddled by their fires, restless as hunted beasts. I slipped past them, a shadow among shadows, until the tunnel mouth swallowed me whole.

The passage stank of rot and ancient secrets. The air itself seemed to writhe, thick with the promise of upheaval. Zaemon had already begun his work. But the city had changed—new masonry choked old exits, forcing me to emerge near the harbor walls, far from my goal. The ground shuddered underfoot like a living thing.

I ran.

Through alleys where panicked figures stumbled, through streets that groaned and spat dust, I ran. The earth bucked beneath me, and buildings groaned like dying giants. The royal pyramid loomed ahead, its stones grinding like teeth. And before it, the granite throne—its capstone shifting, sliding, inch by torturous inch.

Nais.

The thought of her lying there, exposed to the rabble’s groping hands, near drove me mad. I clawed my way through the crowd, a snarling specter, driving them back with fists and fury. They shrieked, calling me devil, demon—but I cared nothing. The throne’s stone grated, tilted—

Then fell.

The impact shook the square. Dust billowed, choking, blinding. Lightning split the sky, and in its glare I saw Phorenice herself, standing calm amid the chaos, red hair dusted but unshaken. Her fan-girl, Ylga, retched at her feet.

I scrambled onto the throne’s wreckage, fingers scrabbling at the clamps. The metal groaned, gave way—and there she was.

Nais.

Pale as moonlit marble, her hair a dark river, her body light as a husk. I lifted her, cradled her, kissed her cold lips—but she did not stir.

The city still heaved. Time was short.

I slung her over my shoulder and fled, through ruined gates, over broken walls, past the wretches who wailed that Atlantis was accursed. None stopped me. The rope dangled from the cliff like a lifeline.

Up we went.

In the dim light of the mountain’s refuge, I saw her clearly at last. Her beauty was pared to bone, her nails twisted like talons, her skin translucent as parchment. But she was mine.

I bathed her in tinctured warmth, the steam curling like a prayer.

And waited.

For life. For warmth.

For Nais.
