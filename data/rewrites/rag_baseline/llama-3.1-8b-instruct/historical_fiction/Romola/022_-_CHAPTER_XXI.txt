Florence vibrated with anticipation as the 17th of November 1494 dawned, casting a warm, golden light upon the city's terracotta rooftops. It had been eighteen months since Tito and Romola's joyous Easter celebration, marked by the festive shower of comfits that symbolized the blessings of the heavens. Since then, the fortunes of Florence had undergone a profound transformation, much like the tree that blooms with a myriad of blossoms, each dependent on the sap that courses through its veins.

As the people of Florence prepared to receive a most esteemed guest, the great palace in the Via Larga shone resplendent with an array of vibrant drapery, banners, and hangings that spilled out of the windows like a colorful tapestry. The air was alive with the sweet scent of incense and the soft murmur of hushed conversations. The city's residents were abuzz with excitement, each one eager to catch a glimpse of the illustrious visitor who was soon to arrive.

Messer Luca Corsini, a learned doctor of law, stood nervously outside the palace, his heart racing with anticipation as he rehearsed the Latin oration he was to deliver in honor of their guest. Every prominent elder in Florence was busy preparing for the procession, donning their finest attire, adorned with intricate embroidery and silk lucco. The young nobles, meanwhile, were busy adjusting their rich, French-inspired tunics, eager to make a favorable impression on the stranger.

As the morning wore on, the clergy gathered in the cathedral square, consulting among themselves on how best to arrange their procession, carefully balancing the sacred relics, banners, and consecrated jewels they were to bear. Their goal was to create a dignified display, one that would befit the grandeur of their guest's arrival, scheduled for three o'clock in the afternoon.

This unexampled visitor, it was whispered, brought with him an army unlike any Italy had ever seen – thousands of battle-hardened Swiss mercenaries, proud knights, and an unprecedented infantry, each equipped with an arquebus. His entourage also included cannons of bronze, capable of firing iron balls, drawn by powerful horses. Some likened him to Charlemagne, the legendary ruler who had rebuilt Florence and brought order to the Church.
