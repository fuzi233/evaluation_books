As Lona and I strolled along the winding path, the air was filled with the excited chatter of the Little Ones. They had finally managed to climb to the top of a towering tree, and from its lofty branches, they spotted something remarkable on the distant mountain – a solitary peak that seemed to touch the sky. According to their accounts, the mountain pierced the heavens, forcing the clouds to gather around its summit and veil its top.

Intrigued, I joined them at the base of the tree, and together we set off towards the mountain. The children led the way, their laughter and shouts growing louder with each step. The river beside us transformed into a breathtaking spectacle – its waters, a mesmerizing dance of crystal clarity, seemed almost otherworldly. It was as if nothing in our world could compare to its beauty.

As we drew closer to the mountain, the city within its misty veil began to take shape. A majestic tower, shrouded in clouds, stood sentinel above the city, its peak hidden from view. Dark, swirling clouds swirled around it, like a maelstrom of grays and purples. Suddenly, a blinding flash illuminated the scene, and for an instant, I thought I saw a figure standing before us. The Little Ones, however, were ecstatic, their voices filled with wonder.

"What did you see?" one of them asked.

"I saw," another replied.

"I saw a beautiful man!" Lona exclaimed.

The smallest of the group, Luva, piped up, "He said, 'My little ones, come along!'"

I had caught a glimpse of the flash, but heard nothing. Lona, however, had seen and heard everything. Another burst of light revealed a multitude of faces, ethereal and serene, only to vanish in an instant. The next flash was different – human forms, vivid and lifelike, appeared and disappeared as quickly.

I felt a pang of recognition, and my heart swelled with emotion. "I see my mother!" I cried.

Luva chimed in, "I see lots of mothers!"

The clouds flashed once more, revealing a kaleidoscope of creatures – horses, elephants, lions, dogs, and birds of every color and shape. I saw my childhood pony, and my heart ached with a mix of sadness and nostalgia. But then I realized that I could have reunited with it if I had only waited.
