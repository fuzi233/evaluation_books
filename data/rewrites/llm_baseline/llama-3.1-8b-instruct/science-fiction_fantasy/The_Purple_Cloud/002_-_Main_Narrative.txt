Three weeks prior to that fateful Sunday evening conversation, I found myself beset by a peculiar sense of unease, one that I couldn't quite place. It was around that time that Clark, the leader of an impending expedition, paid me a friendly visit. I had been established in my Harley Street practice for approximately a year, and, despite being under twenty-five, I possessed a respectable reputation among the medical community in Europe. My clientele was, however, limited, and I often felt the sting of financial insecurity. It was precisely during this period that my book, "Applications of Science to the Arts," achieved modest success, saving me from a potentially awkward situation.

As we conversed that afternoon, Clark mentioned in his characteristic carefree manner, "Adam Jeffson, I had the most intriguing dream about you last night. You were with us on the expedition." I sensed a flicker of surprise on my part, as I had experienced a similar dream the previous night, but I chose not to disclose this to Clark. My response was tinged with hesitation: "You? On the expedition? I wouldn't go, even if I were asked." Clark seemed unconcerned by my reticence, replying, "Oh, you would." I insisted, "No, Clark, you're jesting. I'm on the cusp of marriage, and besides, I'm hardly an expert in astronomy or magnetic phenomena."

Clark chuckled and continued, "But what about your knowledge of botany? That's exactly what we require from you. As for nautical astronomy, a man with your scientific inclinations would quickly grasp it." I replied, "You treat this as a feasible prospect, Clark, but such an idea never crosses my mind. Firstly, there's my fiancée, and—" Clark interrupted, "Ah, yes, the esteemed Countess. She, I assume, would be the first to urge you to join the expedition. The chance to set foot on the North Pole doesn't arise often, my friend."

I grew increasingly agitated, attempting to steer the conversation towards safer topics. "Do speak of something else, Clark! There's Peters to consider." Clark persisted, "Of course, there's Peters, but I assure you, the dream was vivid and unmistakable." I laughed loudly, feigning amusement, but my inner self knew, even then, that a pivotal moment was unfolding in my life.
