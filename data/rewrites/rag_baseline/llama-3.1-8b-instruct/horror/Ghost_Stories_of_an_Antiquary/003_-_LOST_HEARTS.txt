Lost Hearts

It was a crisp autumn evening in September 1811 when a post-chaise rattled to a stop before the imposing façade of Aswarby Hall, situated deep within the rolling hills of Lincolnshire. As the vehicle came to a halt, the sole passenger, a curious twelve-year-old boy named Stephen Elliott, alighted onto the driveway, his bright blue eyes scanning the surroundings with an air of quiet wonder. The Hall's architectural blend of 17th-century brickwork and 18th-century neoclassicism was a sight to behold – the stone-pillared porch, the tall, narrow windows with their delicate white woodwork, and the pediment-crowned façade all seemed to whisper tales of a bygone era.

As the clock in the nearby church tower struck six, its melodic chimes carried on the autumn breeze, a gentle reminder of the passing time. The evening light cast a warm, golden glow across the Hall's windows, imbuing the scene with an air of serenity. Beyond the manicured lawns, a sea of oaks and firs stretched towards the horizon, their branches etched against the fading sky like a delicate pen-and-ink drawing.

Stephen's thoughts, however, were elsewhere. Only six months prior, he had found himself alone in the world, an orphan left to navigate the complexities of adulthood. His arrival at Aswarby Hall was a result of a most unexpected turn of events – the generous offer of his elderly cousin, Mr. Abney. Little was known about the Professor of Greek at Cambridge, except that his interests lay in the esoteric realms of ancient pagan rituals and mysticism. His library was a treasure trove of knowledge, housing rare texts on the Mysteries, Orphic poems, and the worship of Mithras. A magnificent marble group of Mithras slaying a bull, imported from the Levant at great expense, stood proudly in the Hall's marble-paved entranceway.

As the Hall's front door swung open, Stephen caught a glimpse of his cousin, Mr. Abney, emerging from his study, his tall, gaunt frame and austere demeanor a testament to his reclusive nature. Yet, as their eyes met, a hint of warmth flickered across his face, and he hastened towards the young boy.

"How are you, my boy?" Mr. Abney asked, his voice warm and solicitous. "How old are you?"
