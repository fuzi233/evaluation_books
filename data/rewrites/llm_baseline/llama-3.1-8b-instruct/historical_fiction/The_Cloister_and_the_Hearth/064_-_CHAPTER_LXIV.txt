In the warmly lit kitchen of the Tuscan villa, the scent of fresh rosemary wafted through the air as Donna Alessia stood at the stone counter, her hands kneading dough with a fervent intensity. Her usually composed features were twisted in a mixture of distress and determination, her skin flushed and pale by turns. A young maid, her dark hair tied back in a neat knot, hovered near the doorway, awaiting her mistress's instruction.

The maid's voice was barely above a whisper as she announced, "Signora, the man has arrived."

Donna Alessia's grip on the dough tightened, and she turned to face the maid with a swift motion. She hastily donned a delicate lace mask, its intricate patterns a stark contrast to her porcelain-like complexion. As she did so, her agitation seemed to dissipate, replaced by an air of quiet calculation.

A man entered the kitchen, his bow low and deferential. He was a man of unassuming appearance – plain clothes, a round face, and an unruly shock of brown hair. Yet, it was his eyes that held one's attention – restless, like a bird straining to break free from its cage.

After a series of polite bows, he came to stand before Donna Alessia, awaiting her pleasure. "Those who spoke to you," she asked, her voice measured, "did they agree on the terms?"

"Yes, Signora," he replied, his eyes darting between her face and the mask. "The price is paid in full, and I've been instructed to... facilitate the lesser vendetta."

Donna Alessia's gaze narrowed. "I'm not familiar with the term."

The man hesitated, choosing his words carefully. "It means, Signora, that we'll take the man's life, but spare his soul. A swift, clean death, without pain or suffering."

Donna Alessia's expression hardened. "I want him silenced, not merely killed. His treachery will stop at nothing."

The man nodded, his anxiety growing. "I understand, Signora. But to carry out this task, I need more information about the man. His name, his address... Who spoke to me didn't know him, and I'll need your help to find him."
