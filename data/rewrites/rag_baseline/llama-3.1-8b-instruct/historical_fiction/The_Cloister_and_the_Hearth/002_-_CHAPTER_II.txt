The kitchen was alive with the scent of simmering vegetables and the soft clinking of pots against the hearthstone. Gerard's eyes sparkled as he announced, "The soup is hot!" But his words were met with a despondent inquiry from the senior, "How are we to get it to our mouths?" Margaret's lips curled into a sly smile as she whispered, "The young man has brought us straws."

The old man's gruff demeanor softened momentarily, and he exclaimed, "Ay, ay! But my poor bones are stiff, and indeed the fire is too hot for a body to kneel over with these short straws. St. John the Baptist, but the young man is adroit!" As he spoke, Gerard deftly removed his hat, revealing a hidden talent for improvisation. He swiftly tied two stones into the corners of his hat, wrapped his hand in the tail of his jerkin, and plucked the steaming flask from the fire. With a merry grin, he placed the hat beneath the old man's nose, and the senior inserted the makeshift straw with trembling hands.

As the first sip touched his lips, the old man's drawn face began to glow with renewed vitality. A low murmur of satisfaction rumbled from his throat, and he declared, "Hippocrates and Galen! 'Tis a soupe au vin—the restorative of restoratives. Blessed be the nation that invented it, and the woman who made it, and the young man who brings it to fainting folk." He beckoned Margaret to partake, saying, "Have a suck, my girl, while I regale our young host with the history and virtues of this wondrous concoction." As he began to speak, his words tumbled forth in a torrent of praise for the forgotten remedy, a testament to the ingenuity of the past.

Margaret's eyes sparkled with amusement, and she whispered, "Alas, he's off again." The old man's tale unfolded like a rich tapestry, weaving together the threads of ancient lore and modern neglect. He spoke of Hector's rejection of wine in the Iliad, and how a soupe au vin would have given the legendary warrior the strength and vigor to conquer even the greatest of foes. His words painted vivid pictures of battles won and lost, of generals and soldiers, and the eternal quest for sustenance and victory.
