A GROTESQUE TRAGEDY

As I strolled through the desolate cemetery, the crunch of gravel beneath my feet served as a morbid reminder of the transience of life. I chanced upon a decrepit, centuries-old carriage, its wooden slats weathered to a soft silver patina, and its wheels shrouded in a tangled web of vines. Two skeletal horses, their bony frames eerily preserved, stood guard beside the carriage, their shriveled reins still tied to the bony hand of the skeleton coachman, who perched atop the tattered hammercloth. The carriage doors hung limply from their hinges, revealing two empty seats within.

As I approached, the skeletons began to stir, their joints creaking in protest. One skeleton toppled to the ground, while the other struggled to rise, its bony legs trembling beneath its weight. It managed to hoist itself onto its feet, using the carriage wheel for support, and shuffled over to its fallen companion. The second skeleton, its yellowish skull tilted to one side, regarded its companion with a mixture of concern and annoyance.

"Have you injured yourself, milord?" it asked, its voice a mournful whisper, like the sigh of a dying ember.

"Aye, I have," replied the other, its tone rougher, as if blown by a chill wind. "You did nothing to assist me, and this wretched knee is aflame with pain!"

"I did my best, milord," the first skeleton replied, its voice tinged with a hint of defensiveness.

"No doubt, milady," the second skeleton retorted, "but 'twas a poor effort indeed! I thought I'd never regain my footing again—But, by the saints, madam! Are you out of your bones?"

The skeleton lady cast a sidelong glance at her own bony frame. "I have nothing else to be out of," she replied dryly. "And you, at least, cannot complain! But what in the name of the saints does it mean? Am I dreaming?"

"You may be dreaming, milady," the second skeleton countered, "but I fear I am far from it. This knee of mine protests loudly against such illusions. Ha! I too perceive that I have nothing to walk upon but bones! Not so unbecoming to a gentleman, perhaps, but every joint aches worse than the last, and this loose knee is the worst of all!
