I collapsed onto the bed, my mind churning through the princess’s tale. She had slipped—revealed more than she intended. That single careless word unraveled one mystery: the leopardess had *bounded* across the stream, while the princess lay helpless on the bank. The water had broken her spell. Her own confession laid bare the peril facing the Little Ones—and I, fool that I was, had saved their greatest enemy. The realization coiled in my gut as sleep dragged me under.

The wine, no doubt, had played its part.

When I woke, the chamber was bathed in the soft glow of a hanging lamp. A languid sweetness clung to me, as if I floated on a twilight sea, untethered from pain or fear. Dying, perhaps.

Then—*agony*. A searing sting through my heart. Again. And again. My body refused to move, pinned beneath an invisible weight. Something vile pressed down on me, smothering my will. I lay paralyzed, drowning in silent terror.

A hand brushed my face, gentle yet firm, pushing my head into the pillow. The weight lifted from my chest. I gasped, eyes fluttering open.

The princess stood over me on the bed, her gaze distant, dreaming. Her eyes were calm, her mouth smeared with red. She caught me staring and struck—a swipe of her handkerchief like a blade across my vision. Darkness.

A thud. The whisper of something heavy landing.

By the time my sight returned, only the flick of a tail vanished through the door. I lunged after it.

The hall of alabaster lay moonlit, a pale ghost of itself. No princess. She could be anywhere. Every shadow now held teeth.

Beyond, the black hall seethed with shapes half-seen. Phantoms wove through the dark, a chaotic dance of skull-faced women, skeletal riders, and burrowing horrors. No order, only hunger and motion. The princess was nowhere.

I fled to the vestibule.

Moonlight silvered the leopardess’s cage—where two forms writhed, locked in a soundless battle. Human and beast, tangled, furious. A blink, and the spotted leopardess slipped free, padding toward the open door. Behind her, the cage stood undisturbed, its occupant once more still.

The moon watched, expectant.

The shadow from the night before glided through the trees, the leopardess trailing. I followed, silent as they.

The city slept beneath the moon.

At the stair, the shadow ascended. The leopardess followed. Then—a cry. Something heavy thudded down, landing at my feet.

The woman who had led me home lay broken, her body blackened, twisted.

Before I could move, the spotted leopardess bounded down, a baby clamped in her jaws.

I lunged—

A streak of white lightning. The pale leopardess struck, teeth sinking into the other’s throat.

The battle was swift, brutal. Claws scrabbled on stone; growls tore the air. The spotted one was larger, but the white held on, relentless.

A howl—rising, twisting into a woman’s scream.

The white leopardess released her.

The spotted beast reared, melting into the princess, her skin swallowing the last of the leopard’s spots. For a heartbeat, she stood radiant, moonlight glinting off the blood streaking her throat.

Then—collapse. The spots surged back. She fled, a beast once more.

The white leopardess turned to me. In one motion, she wrenched the baby from my arms and vanished into the night.
