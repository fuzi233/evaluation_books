To navigate the labyrinthine corridors of peril, one must first surrender to the disorienting weight of unconsciousness. I do not recall the duration of my slumber upon the dusty floor of the storeroom, only that it stretched into a profound, dreamless void lasting many hours. Awakening was violent, a jarring intrusion of reality marked by screams of terror that shattered the silence. My eyes fluttered open, blurring the subterranean gloom, my mind reeling as I struggled to anchor myself in the present moment. Before I could fully orient my consciousness, the air erupted into a cacophony—a fusillade of rifle shots that reverberated through the cavernous tunnels, bouncing off the stone walls in a deafening symphony of violence.

Instinct took hold before thought could intervene. I surged to my feet, muscles coiled with adrenaline. Through the haze of confusion, I discerned the grim tableau: a dozen lesser therns stood blocking the massive doorway at the far end of the room, their wicked rifles leveled downward. Scattered across the floor lay the lifeless forms of my companions, save for Thuvia and Tars Tarkas. Like myself, they had succumbed to exhaustion on the ground, spared the initial volley of death. As I rose, the therns' faces twisted in a grotesque mixture of chagrin, consternation, and sheer panic; they lowered their weapons, realizing the gravity of their mistake.

"I rise to the occasion," I declared, my voice cutting through the tension with a tone of fierce indignation. "What manner of treachery is this? Is Sator Throg to be butchered by his own vassals?"

"Have mercy, O Master of the Tenth Cycle!" one of the trembling figures cried out, while the others instinctively edged backward toward the exit, desperate to flee the wrath of the mighty one.

"Speak plainly," Thuvia whispered urgently at my side, her eyes scanning the scene. "What is your mission here?"

"Why are you here, fellows?" I demanded, stepping forward.

"Two fugitives from the outer world roam free within the dominion of the therns," the leader stammered, casting a nervous glance toward Tars Tarkas. "We hunted them by the command of the Father of Therns. One was pale-skinned with black hair; the other, a colossal green warrior."

"Behold," Thuvia interjected sharply, gesturing to the Thark, "here stands one of them. And if you examine the corpse lying near the door, you may identify the other. It was left to Sator Throg and his pitiful slaves to achieve what the lesser therns of the guard failed to do. We slew one and captured the other; for this act, Sator Throg granted us our liberty. Now, in your foolishness, you have slaughtered everyone but myself, and nearly taken the life of the mighty Sator Throg himself."

The men hung their heads, their expressions sheepish and terrified under the weight of their failure.

"Shall we not dispose of these bodies to the plant men and retreat to our quarters immediately, O Mighty One?" Thuvia asked, turning to me.

"Do as Thuvia commands," I replied.

As the therns bent to lift the corpses, I observed the one tasked with carrying Sator Throg's body. Upon lifting the dead man, his gaze fixed upon the upturned face, a flicker of recognition sparked in his eyes. He stole a furtive, calculating glance in my direction from the corner of his eye. That he suspected the truth was evident; that he dared not voice it was proven by his silence. Again, as he dragged the body from the chamber, he shot a quick, searching look my way before returning his focus to the bald, shiny dome of the deceased in his arms. The final glimpse of his profile as he vanished from sight revealed a cunning smile of triumph curling his lips.

Only Tars Tarkas, Thuvia, and I remained. The lethal marksmanship of the therns had severed our last thread of connection to the perilous freedom awaiting beyond the world.

The moment the gruesome procession departed, the girl urged us to resume our flight. She, too, had noted the interrogative posture of the thern bearing Sator Throg's remains.

"It portends ill for us, O Prince," she warned softly. "Though this fool dared not accuse you in error, there are powers above capable of demanding a rigorous inquiry. Such scrutiny, Prince, would prove fatal."

I shrugged, a gesture born of weary resignation. In any event, our plight seemed destined for death. Sleep had restored some energy, yet I remained weak, drained by blood loss. Pain pulsed through my wounds, sharp and unyielding. No medicine seemed accessible. I yearned desperately for the miraculous healing properties of the strange salves and lotions used by the green Martian women; in an hour, they would have renewed me completely. Instead, discouragement settled over me like a heavy cloak. Never before had such utter hopelessness gripped me in the face of danger. Then, a stray draft caught the long, flowing yellow locks of the Holy Thern, sending them swirling around my face.

Could they still unlock the path to freedom? If we acted swiftly, might we escape before the general alarm was raised? We had nothing to lose but our lives; we must try.

"What is the first move of this fellow, Thuvia?" I asked. "How long before they return for us?"

"He will proceed directly to the Father of Therns, old Matai Shang," she replied. "He may await an audience, but being a thorian among the lesser therns, his status ensures Matai Shang will not keep him waiting long. Should the Father believe his tale, another hour will see the galleries, chambers, courts, and gardens swarming with searchers."

"Therefore, our actions must occur within the hour. What is the optimal route, Thuvia? The shortest path out of this celestial Hades?"

"Directly to the summit of the cliffs, Prince," she instructed. "From there, through the gardens to the inner courts. Our path lies across the temples of the therns to the outer court, and finally, the ramparts. O Prince, it is despair itself. Ten thousand warriors could not carve a path to liberty from this dreadful place. Since time immemorial, stone by stone, the therns have fortified their stronghold. An unbroken line of impregnable defenses circles the outer slopes of the Mountains of Otz. Within the temples behind the ramparts, a million fighters stand ready. The courts and gardens teem with slaves, women, and children. None can move a stone's throw without detection."

"If no alternative exists, Thuvia, why dwell on the impossibility? We must confront it."

"Cannot we attempt this after dark?" Tars Tarkas suggested. "Daylight offers no chance."

"Night affords a slight advantage, yet the ramparts remain heavily guarded, perhaps even more so than by day. Fewer patrols wander the courts and gardens, however," Thuvia conceded.

"What hour is it?" I asked.

"It was midnight when you freed me from my chains," Thuvia stated. "Two hours later we reached the storeroom. You slept for fourteen hours. It must now be nearing sunset. Come; let us inspect a nearby window overlooking the cliff."

Leading the way through winding passageways, she guided us until a sudden turn revealed an opening facing the Valley Dor. At our right, the sun descended, a massive red orb dipping below the western range of Otz. Below us, the Holy Thern stood sentinel on his balcony. His scarlet robe of office was drawn tight against the chill that arrives abruptly with darkness on Mars. The atmosphere here is so rarefied that it retains little solar heat; daytime brings scorching intensity, while night plunges everything into biting cold. Furthermore, the thin air fails to refract or diffuse sunlight as it does on Earth. There is no twilight on Mars. When the great orb vanishes beneath the horizon, the transition is instantaneous, akin to extinguishing a single lamp in a sealed chamber. Brilliant light gives way without warning to absolute darkness. Then, the moons appear—the mysterious, magical moons of Mars, hurtling like monstrous meteors low across the planetary face.

The fading sunlight illuminated the eastern banks of Korus, the crimson grass, and the resplendent forest. Beneath the trees, numerous herds of plant men grazed. Adults stood erect on their toes, their mighty tails balancing them as their talons pruned every available leaf and twig. It was then I comprehended the meticulous trimming of the trees, explaining my earlier misconception that the grove served as a playground for a civilized populace.

Our gaze drifted to the rolling Iss, issuing from the base of the cliffs. Soon, a canoe emerged from the mountain, laden with lost souls from the outer world—a dozen individuals of the highly civilized, cultured red race that dominates Mars. The eyes of the herald on the balcony below locked onto the doomed group simultaneously with ours. He raised his head, leaning precariously over the low railing of his dizzying perch, and unleashed the shrill, eerie wail that summoned the demons of this hellish realm to attack.

For an instant, the brutes stood rigid, ears erect, before pouring from the grove toward the riverbank, covering the distance with clumsy, bounding leaps. The party had landed on the sward as the horrific horde appeared.
