Martin Wittenhaagen stood at the base of the staircase, his bowstring pulled taut against his cheek and his dagger gripped firmly in his other hand. His heart hammered against his ribs until he saw the men return empty-handed, lacking Gerard. Confusion washed over his face; he slowly lowered the weapon, staring at them with wide eyes. The intruders, however, were no less baffled by his defensive posture.

"Why, mates, was the old fellow preparing to loose an arrow at us?" one asked.

"Rubbish!" Martin retorted, forcing his rough demeanor back into place. "I was merely testing my new string. There! If you doubt me so greatly, I will unstring the bow right before your eyes."

"Hmph!" Dierich grunted, his suspicion undimmed. "There is more to you than meets the eye. Put a log on the fire, and let us warm our frozen limbs before we descend further."

A roaring blaze was quickly kindled. The men clustered around the heat, hanging their wet garments and long, matted hair over the flames until the smoke curled upward. It was then that the shrill cries erupted from Margaret's chamber above. Every man leaped to his feet. One snatched a candle and bolted up the narrow stairway toward the sleeping quarters. Martin surged forward instinctively, driven by panic and a fear that the stranger's curiosity would lead to disaster. As he lunged to block the path, Dierich wrapped his arms around Martin's waist from behind, shouting for the others to hold him fast.

The candle-bearer slipped past the grapple, and instantly the group descended upon Martin. A brutal struggle ensued, sending them tumbling across the floorboards. Martin found himself pinned beneath the weight of four men, fighting desperately until they finally subdued the giant. Binding him hand and foot with a rope intended for Gerard, they left him gasping on the ground.

"Ay, gnash your teeth, you ancient rascal," Dierich panted, still catching his breath. "You shall not use them again."

"It seems our very lives hung by a thread while this old brute remained free," one of the men observed grimly.

"He makes me believe Gerard is not far behind," another added hopefully.

"No such fortune," Dierich snapped. "Listen! Jorian Ketel has been lingering in that girl's bedroom far too long. Some of us should go check on him immediately."

The crude laughter that followed this command had barely faded when the sound of hurried footsteps echoed loudly overhead.

"Oh, here he comes at last!" someone cried out. "Well, Jorian, what business have you up there now?"
