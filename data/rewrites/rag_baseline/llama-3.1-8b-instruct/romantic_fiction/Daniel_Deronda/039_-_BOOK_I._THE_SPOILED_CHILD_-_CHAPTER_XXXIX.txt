In the fading light of late summer, the tranquil facade of the Meyrick household at Chelsea concealed a maelstrom of emotions, each member of the family navigating their own inner turmoil like wisps of smoke carried on the wind. Mirah, perched on a camp-stool, read aloud from Walter Pater's "The Praise of Chimney-Sweeps," her voice weaving a spell of enchantment over the gathered listeners. Her sisters, Kate and Amy, sat entranced, their fingers pausing mid-stitch as they listened to the words dance across the pages. Meanwhile, Mrs. Meyrick worked on the pièce de résistance of their needlework projects – a majestic embroidery that stretched across the front room like a banner, its intricate patterns a testament to the skill of the talented fingers that had created it.

As Mirah's voice rose and fell, the sound of a hansom cab rattling down the street outside interrupted the peaceful atmosphere. The girls exchanged excited whispers, speculating on the identity of the visitor. "It must be a gentleman," Amy said, her eyes sparkling with curiosity. Kate, ever the wit, dryly observed, "The greatest man in London may get into a hansom cab, after all." Mab, the youngest, squealed with delight, "Oh, oh, oh! Suppose it's Lord Russell!"

Their musings were cut short as the maid-servant returned, card in hand, and announced the arrival of a visitor. The family turned to face the door, their faces alight with anticipation. And then, in strode Julius Klesmer, his imposing figure commanding attention as he bowed low over Mrs. Meyrick's hand. His flamboyant hair and gold-rimmed spectacles gave him an air of the Continental, while his massive face and broad shoulders seemed to embody the very essence of music itself.

As he entered, the room seemed to shrink, the familiar contours of the cottage piano and the embroidered masterpiece now seeming fragile and insignificant beside his grand presence. Mirah, however, felt a thrill of excitement run through her veins. Could it be that this magnificent creature had come seeking her out, rather than the other way around? She sensed that he was a man accustomed to grand stages and vast audiences, his consciousness constantly straining towards the infinite possibilities of the world beyond the confines of this humble home.
