For a fleeting heartbeat, I stood frozen as the horde surged forward, but their initial momentum threw me stumbling backward. My foot searched for purchase on the stone floor but found only void; I had backed straight into the chasm that had swallowed Issus. For a split second, I teetered on the precipice, clutching the boy tightly against my chest, before gravity claimed us both. We plummeted into the black abyss, striking a smooth, polished chute just as the grate above slammed shut with magical precision. We shot downward, unscathed, crashing into a dimly lit chamber deep beneath the arena.

As I scrambled to my feet, the first thing that greeted me was the malevolent visage of Issus, staring through the heavy iron bars of a grated door lining the cell wall.

"Rash mortal!" she shrieked, her voice echoing like grinding stones. "You shall pay the terrible price for your blasphemy! You will rot here alone in eternal darkness, beside the decaying corpse of your accomplice, until madness and hunger drive you to feast upon the maggots that once were human!"

With that, she vanished. The faint light that had illuminated the cell dissolved instantly into Cimmerian night.

"Pleasant old lady," a voice murmured from the shadows beside me.

"Who speaks?" I asked, startled.

"'Tis I, your companion," the voice replied, warm with relief. "I have had the honor today of fighting shoulder-to-shoulder with the greatest warrior Barsoom has ever seen clad in steel."

"I thank the Gods you are alive," I breathed, glancing at the gash on his forehead. "I feared for that nasty cut."

"It merely stunned me," he chuckled softly. "A trivial scratch."

"Perhaps it would have been kinder if it had ended it," I muttered grimly. "Our situation seems dire; starvation and thirst await unless we find a way out."

"Where are we?"

"Beneath the arena," I replied. "We fell down the very shaft that consumed Issus moments ago."

He let out a low, delighted laugh, then reached out through the inky darkness to pull my ear close to his lips. "Nothing could be better," he whispered conspiratorially. "There are secrets hidden within the secrets of Issus that she herself does not suspect."

"What do you mean?"

"A year ago, I labored alongside other slaves remodeling these subterranean galleries. During that work, we discovered an ancient network of corridors and chambers sealed away for eons. The overseers allowed a few of us to explore them. I know this labyrinth perfectly. Miles of tunnels honeycomb the earth beneath the gardens and the temple itself. One specific passage descends to connect with the lower regions, opening onto the water shaft that feeds Omean."

"If we can reach the submarine undetected," he continued, his voice trembling with hope, "we might yet make the sea. There are countless islands where the blacks never venture. We could survive there for a time, and perhaps fate will provide a means of ultimate escape."

He spoke in a hushed tone, clearly fearing unseen spies even in this isolated cell. Matching his volume, I whispered back, "Lead us back to Shador, my friend. Xodar, the boy, is there. We planned to escape together; I cannot abandon him."

"No," the boy insisted firmly. "One does not desert a comrade. It is better to be recaptured together than apart."

He began to grope across the floor of the dark chamber, searching for the mechanism of the trapdoor. Finally, he signaled me with a low, urgent hiss. I crept toward the sound, finding him kneeling at the edge of an opening in the floor.

"There is a drop of roughly ten feet," he whispered. "If you hang by your hands, you will land safely on a level bed of soft sand."

Quietly, I lowered myself from the cell above into the abyss below. The darkness was absolute; I could not see my own hands inches from my nose. Never before had I experienced such total deprivation of light as in the pits of Issus. Hanging suspended in mid-air, a primal panic seized me—the terrifying sensation of feet touching nothing, of plunging into unknown depths. Though the boy assured me it was only ten feet, my heart hammered as if I dangled over a bottomless void. With a final release, I dropped four feet onto a cushion of sand. The boy followed suit.

"Raise me to your shoulders," he instructed, "and I will reset the trap."

Once done, he took my hand, guiding me slowly through the pitch-black chamber, pausing frequently to ensure we hadn't wandered into a dead end. Soon, we began descending a steep incline.

"We shall see light soon," he promised. "At the lower levels, we encounter the same layer of phosphorescent rock that illuminates Omean."

I shall never forget that journey through the bowels of Issus. Devoid of immediate danger, it was charged with a strange, electric thrill of adventure, rooted in the unknowable antiquity of these forgotten passages. The Stygian darkness concealed wonders from my eyes, yet my imagination conjured vivid scenes of the ancient peoples of this dying world, reviving their labors, intrigues, mysteries, and cruelties as they fought their last stand against the swarming hordes of the Dead Sea Bottoms, retreating step-by-step to the highest peaks of the world, barricaded behind walls of superstition.

Beyond the Green Men, three principal races had once populated Barsoom: the Blacks, the Whites, and the Yellow Men. As the planet's waters receded and resources dwindled, existence became a relentless struggle for survival. The three superior races had long dominated the Green Savages of the flooded lands, but as the seas withdrew, forcing them into nomadic life and fragmented communities, they eventually fell prey to the ferocity of the Greens. The result was a partial amalgamation of Black, White, and Yellow bloodlines, giving rise to the magnificent Red Man of today.

I had always believed all traces of the original races had vanished from Mars. Yet, in just four days, I had encountered vast numbers of Whites and Blacks. Was it possible that somewhere in this vast, dying sphere, a remnant of the ancient Yellow Men still survived?

My reverie was shattered by the boy's excited cry, "At last, the lighted way!" Looking ahead, I saw a dim radiance growing brighter with every step. Soon, we emerged into well-lit passageways. Our pace quickened until we reached the terminus of a corridor dropping directly onto the ledge surrounding the submarine pool.

The vessel lay at her moorings, hatch wide open. The boy raised a finger to his lips, tapped his sword in a silent signal, and crept noiselessly toward the ship. I trailed close behind. Dropping silently onto the deserted deck, we crawled on hands and knees toward the hatch. A cautious look below confirmed no guards were watching. With the agility of cats, we dropped into the main cabin. Still, there was no sign of life. We quickly covered and secured the hatch.

The boy moved to the pilot house, pressed a button, and the boat sank smoothly into the swirling waters, heading for the bottom of the shaft. Miraculously, there was no scurrying of feet or shouting as we anticipated. While the boy remained at the controls, I slipped from cabin to cabin, frantically searching for any crew member. The craft was completely deserted. Such luck seemed almost impossible.

Returning to the pilot house to share the news, the boy handed me a piece of paper. "This explains the absence of the crew," he said.

It was a radio-aerial message addressed to the submarine's commander: *"The slaves have risen. Come with all available men and those you can gather en route. Too late to request aid from Omean. They are massacring everyone inside the amphitheater. Issus is under threat. Haste. — ZITHAD"*

"Zithad is the Dator of the Guards for Issus," the boy explained. "We gave them a frightful scare—they won't forget it soon."

"Let us hope this marks the beginning of the end for Issus," I said.

"Only our First Ancestor knows," he replied cryptically.

We arrived at the submarine pool in Omean without further incident. We debated whether to sink the craft before departing, but concluded it offered no advantage. If captured, the numerous Blacks on Omean would thwart us regardless of reinforcements arriving from Issus's temples. Now faced with a new dilemma: how to bypass the patrols guarding the island around the pool?

Finally, I devised a plan. "What is the rank or name of the officer commanding these guards?" I asked the boy.

"A fellow named Torith was on duty this morning," he answered.

"Excellent. And who commands the submarine?"

"Yersted."
