It was the evening of August 30, and the sleepy town of Donegal was abuzz with the latest news: Harry Feversham's disappearance had become the talk of the town. The townsfolk gathered at the local market, their conversations spiced with speculation and rumor, as they sipped sweet tea and savored the scent of freshly baked scones. Gossip spread like wildfire, with some claiming to have seen Harry hurrying along the causeway by Lennon Bridge on the morning after the ball at Lennon House. Others speculated that he had been spirited away by dark forces, and the suggestion of a drag-net was tossed about with reckless abandon. Mr. Dennis Rafferty, a local merchant, refused to eat salmon, declaring that he was no cannibal, and the notion stuck, becoming a local catchphrase.

Meanwhile, in a nearby cottage, Ethne and Dermod O'Brien went about their daily routine, seemingly oblivious to the commotion surrounding them. Ethne's smile was a fraction softer, Dermod's temper a shade more short-fused, but their lives remained unchanged. As the evening drew to a close, the townspeople retired to their homes, leaving the gossipmongers to spin their tales.

In a distant land, in the bustling city of London, Lieutenant Sutch stood amidst the crowds gathered outside the War Office, his injured leg aching beneath him. The air was thick with excitement as people watched the flickering lights of the War Office, their faces pale and expectant. Rumors of a great battle fought in the Egyptian desert had sent shockwaves through the city, and the crowd's collective breath hung in the air like the sweet scent of roasting meats wafting from a nearby food cart. Sutch's eyes scanned the crowd, his gaze landing on a figure that made his heart skip a beat – Harry Feversham, his face deathly pale, his eyes burning with an inner fire.

"Ah, Sutch, my friend," Harry exclaimed, his voice barely above a whisper. "I might have been out there tonight, in the midst of that chaos, fighting for our country. Think of it! I might have been lying by a campfire, sharing stories of the battle with Jack Durrance, or...or something worse." Harry's words trailed off, lost in the din of the crowd.
