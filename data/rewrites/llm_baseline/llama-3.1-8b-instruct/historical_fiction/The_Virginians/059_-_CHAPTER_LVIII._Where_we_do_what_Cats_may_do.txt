As the sun cast its gentle rays over the tranquil streets of London, Madam Esmond pondered the wisdom of her sons' decision to adopt Jacobite sympathies. Though their youthful idealism was endearing, she couldn't help but feel a sense of pride knowing that the banished Prince had recognized her family's noble heritage. The memory of her sister, Madame de Bernstein, and the Old Chevalier's tumultuous past was easily set aside, for in the grand tapestry of history, even the most scandalous threads were woven alongside the majestic narratives of the Stuarts and Hanoverians alike. After all, the king de facto held the throne, and his Majesty de jure, though defeated, remained a potent symbol of a bygone era.

Madam Esmond's opinion was clear: her sons owed it to themselves and the sovereign to attend court, where the monarch's benevolence might bestow favors upon them. A lucrative post or a prestigious title would be a fitting reward for their loyalty, and she had no doubt that the King was aware of the Virginian Esmonds' noble lineage. With this in mind, she instructed her sons to present themselves at court, and though she might have been displeased if she knew of George's decision to don Harry's laced coat on the day of their audience, she trusted that the King would see their devotion.

A century ago, the King's drawing-room was a bustling hub of activity, where nobility and gentry gathered to pay their respects to the monarch. Loyalty was a virtue that could be expressed multiple times a month, and the King's absence from Hanover, due to the war and the threat of enemy ships, only served to underscore the gravity of the situation. Yet, the Countess of Walmoden, a Hanoverian lady whose virtues had won the prince's heart, sought to console him for his exile. Through her words, the King heard the echoes of his homeland, and in her presence, he found a measure of comfort.

It was to this beloved Sovereign that Mr. Warrington requested his uncle, a devoted courtier, to present him. As Mr. Lambert also needed to attend court to express his gratitude for his promotion, the two gentlemen decided to travel together, hiring a hackney-coach to convey them to Kensington Palace.
