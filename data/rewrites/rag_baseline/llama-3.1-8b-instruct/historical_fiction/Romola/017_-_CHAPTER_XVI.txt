As Tito strolled back to his lodgings in the Piazza di San Giovanni, the warm autumn sunlight cast a golden glow across the cobblestone streets of Florence. The air was alive with the sweet scent of ripening fruit and the gentle hum of morning chatter. It was a festive atmosphere, as if the city itself was embracing the celebration of the Virgin's Nativity. The narrow thoroughfares were abuzz with activity, stalls overflowing with vibrant produce and flowers, and mules quietly munching on hay beneath their nosebags.

Tito navigated the bustling streets with a mix of trepidation and resignation. His encounter with the Genoese stranger had left him feeling uneasy, and the thought of parting with his onyx ring still lingered. Yet, he couldn't shake off the feeling that fortune had abandoned him, and that his actions were beginning to bear consequences beyond his control. The weight of his deeds, like uninvited guests, had taken up residence in his mind, refusing to be silenced.

As he approached the Piazza di San Giovanni, Tito deliberately avoided the Mercato Vecchio, knowing that running into Tessa might further unsettle him. Instead, he opted for the quieter route, allowing himself to absorb the tranquil atmosphere of the square. The Duomo's imposing presence loomed above, its marble steps a gathering place for the city's inhabitants. There, a young mother coaxed her infant to test its tiny feet on the warm stone, while nearby, a pair of shaggy-haired boys watched with rapt attention as a crippled child carved intricate patterns onto a cherry pit.

Tito's destination was Nello's barbershop, where the skilled craftsman plied his trade beneath a colorful awning. It was the perfect spot for gossip and socializing, and Tito knew he couldn't avoid being drawn into the lively conversations that flowed like a river through the square. He took a deep breath, steeling himself for the inevitable encounters, the polite smiles, and the witty repartee that came with being a respected member of Florentine society. After all, as Nello would say, "to be shaved was a fashion of Florentine respectability," and Tito was determined to maintain his dignified façade, no matter the cost.
