The entire theological and philosophical framework of the Circles can be distilled into a single, absolute maxim: *"Attend to your Configuration."* Whether applied to politics, religion, or morality, every teaching serves one ultimate goal: the refinement of individual and collective shape. Specifically, this applies to the Circles themselves, whose perfection renders all other pursuits secondary.

It is the supreme achievement of the Circles that they have eradicated the ancient heresy suggesting that character is shaped by willpower, effort, training, or encouragement. They have convinced humanity that *Configuration makes the man*. If you are born an Isosceles with unequal sides, you are destined for error unless corrected at the Isosceles Hospital. Similarly, Triangles, Squares, and Polygons born with irregularities must seek cure at the Regular Hospitals; failure to do so results in imprisonment or execution by the Angle of the State Executioner. Under the philosophy of Pantocyclus—the great Circle who quelled the Colour Revolt—no fault, from minor misconduct to heinous crime, exists outside of physical deviation from perfect Regularity. These deviations may stem from birth, collisions in crowds, improper exercise, or sudden temperature changes causing the body to expand or contract. Consequently, Pantocyclus argued that praising or blaming conduct is illogical. Why praise a Square's integrity when one should merely admire the geometric precision of his angles? Why condemn a thiefly Isosceles when the true tragedy is the incurable inequality of his sides?

While theoretically flawless, this doctrine creates severe practical dilemmas. When an Isosceles pleads poverty of spirit due to his unevenness, the Magistrate replies that such a nuisance deserves consumption. However, in domestic disputes where death is not an option, the theory falters. I often find myself torn when a Hexagonal grandson excuses his disobedience by citing a recent chill that distorted his perimeter. Logically, I cannot accept that his shape dictates his behavior; yet, I lack the courage to reject his plea outright. Thus, I resort to a compromise: I assume that a stern scolding possesses a latent power to strengthen his configuration, despite having no scientific basis for this belief.

I am not alone in this pragmatic evasion. Even high-ranking Circles acting as judges utilize praise and blame, speaking of "right" and "wrong" with passionate conviction, as if these concepts possessed tangible existence. Furthermore, our societal commandments have been inverted relative to Spaceland. Where Spaceland teaches children to honor their parents, we teach men to honor their grandsons—or sons—with a reverence that borders on worship. By "honor," we do not mean indulgence, but a devoted commitment to the higher interests of posterity, subordinating parental needs to ensure the state's future stability.

However, the greatest weakness in the Circle system lies in its treatment of Women. To prevent irregular births, society strictly forbids marriage between individuals with irregular ancestry. While male irregularity is measurable, women appear visually Regular (being straight lines); thus, their "invisible" genetic flaws must be deduced through meticulously maintained state-supervised pedigrees. Without certification, marriage is impossible.

Ironically, the stricter the scrutiny regarding a woman's lineage, the less rigorous it becomes as one ascends the social ladder. An ambitious Isosceles seeking an Equilateral son will scrutinize a bride's ancestry down to the root. A Square or Pentagon checks back only five hundred generations. A Hexagon or Dodecagon is even more lax. Yet, a Circle—a being of supreme status—has been known to marry a woman with an irregular Great-Grandfather solely for her superior luster or the melodious charm of her voice, qualities we prize immensely in females. Such unions frequently result in sterility or the reduction of sides. While the loss of a side in a complex polygon is often unnoticed or repaired via Neo-Therapeutic operations, the Circles tend to view infertility as a natural law of advanced evolution. If unchecked, this trend threatens to erode the Circle class entirely, potentially collapsing the Flatland constitution before a new Chief Circle can emerge.

A second, equally grave threat arises from our official policy toward female education. Three centuries ago, the Chief Circle decreed that, lacking reason and overflowing with emotion, women should cease to be treated as rational beings. They were barred from reading and denied arithmetic skills necessary to count the angles of their husbands and children. The result has been a generational decline in female intellectual capacity, a policy of enforced quietism that persists today.

My deepest fear is that this suppression has inadvertently damaged the Male sex. We now live a dual existence: a "bi-mental" life. With women, we employ a vocabulary of love, duty, pity, and hope—emotional fictions designed to manage feminine excess. Among ourselves, however, we revert to a cold lexicon of necessity, fitness, and anticipated benefit. We perform a ritualistic display of deference to women, convincing them that we adore them as fervently as the Chief Circle, while privately viewing them as "mindless organisms."

Our theology differs radically depending on whether we address a woman or another man. This double standard places an unbearable burden on the youth. At age three, boys are forcibly removed from maternal care to "unlearn" the emotional language of their mothers and replace it with the rigid dialect of science. I already perceive a weakening in the grasp of mathematical truth among the current generation compared to the robust intellects of our ancestors three hundred years ago.

Beyond the erosion of male intellect, there looms the terrifying prospect of a woman secretly acquiring literacy. Should she read even a single popular volume, or should an infant boy accidentally overhear the secrets of logic, the entire foundation of our society could crumble. Based solely on the enfeeblement of the male mind, I humbly urge the Highest Authorities to reconsider the regulations governing female education.
