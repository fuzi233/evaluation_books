In the sterile atmosphere of the Detroit Hotel Statler, Sally poured out her second cup of coffee, the bitterness a poor substitute for the warmth she craved. Her eyes drifted toward the lobby, where a figure had caught her attention – a young man who bore an uncanny resemblance to her brother Fillmore. As she watched, he stood peering about, his brow furrowed in concentration. The faint scent of stale air and worn leather wafted in from the lobby, mingling with the acrid aroma of freshly brewed coffee.

Sally's gaze snapped back to the young man as he finally spotted her and made his way over. His nervous demeanor was palpable, and she wondered if he was hiding something. "Why, Sally?" he asked, his voice laced with a mix of anxiety and embarrassment. She sensed a guilty conscience behind his words, and her mind began to spin with possibilities. Was he here to confess some secret, or perhaps share news that would change everything?

As they exchanged pleasantries, Sally's thoughts turned to her own recent absence and her reasons for returning. She had spent the past week nursing Mr. Faucitt, her heart heavy with worry and fatigue. The prospect of seeing Gerald, her friend, had lifted her spirits, but his listless greeting over the phone had left her feeling deflated. She pushed the thought aside, focusing on Fillmore's visit. What could he possibly be doing here, so far from New York? Her brother's usual haunts were the city's bustling streets, not the quiet corridors of a Detroit hotel.

"I got back a week ago," she said, trying to sound casual, "but I've been caring for Mr. Faucitt since then. I'm here to see Mr. Foster's play, 'The Primrose Way.' Have you heard anything about it?"

Fillmore's face contorted in a mixture of discomfort and annoyance. "It hasn't opened yet," he said, his voice tight with tension. Sally's eyes narrowed, sensing a discrepancy. "Don't be silly, Fill," she pressed on. "It opened last Monday." Fillmore's expression faltered, and he coughed nervously. "No, it didn't. They've closed all the theatres due to the Spanish influenza. You must have seen it in the papers."
