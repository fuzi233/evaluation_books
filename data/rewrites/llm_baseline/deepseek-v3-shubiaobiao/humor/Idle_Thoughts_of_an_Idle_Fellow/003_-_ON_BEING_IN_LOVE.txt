Ah, love! You’ve been in love, haven’t you? If not, don’t worry—it’s coming for you. Love is like the measles: everyone gets it once, and only once. After that, you’re immune. You can stroll through moonlit gardens, linger in cozy parlors, and waltz in dimly lit ballrooms without a care. You can even attend weddings without flinching. Once love has scorched you, you’re safe—free to admire without burning, to flirt without falling.

Love is a firework—a brilliant, fleeting burst that lights up the sky before sputtering into darkness. The heart, like Prometheus, dares to steal divine fire, but it can’t keep it. What’s left afterward? Warm embers—affection, comfort, companionship. A quieter, steadier glow, better suited to the long winters of life.

But oh, those first flames! Edwin and Angelina, you think your love will blaze forever. You don’t realize how quickly the fuel burns away. One day, Edwin notices Angelina no longer races to greet him; Angelina sighs when Edwin forgets her handkerchief. The grand passion fades, leaving behind something quieter—if you’re lucky. If not? A cold hearth and two people wondering where the magic went.

Youth believes love eternal. At twenty, you can’t fathom that by forty, your heart won’t still race at a glance. But time dulls the edges of all emotions—love, grief, even ambition. The grand opera of youth becomes a modest chamber piece.

And yet—oh, the madness of those early days! The breathless declarations, the jealous agonies, the way her slightest frown could shatter you! You worshipped her, not as a mere mortal, but as a goddess. You memorized the curve of her smile, the sound of her laugh. You treasured her discarded gloves, her forgotten notes. You’d have fought dragons for her, or at least braved a rainstorm to fetch her shawl.

But youth’s love, for all its fervor, is fragile. The world creeps in. Money, pride, petty grievances—they smother the flame. And what’s left? Regret? Nostalgia? Or, if you’re wise, a quieter, deeper bond—one built on shared jokes and mutual eyerolls instead of grand gestures.

So cherish love’s madness while it lasts. Let it make you brave, foolish, kind. And when it fades, don’t mourn its passing. Build something steadier in its place—a fire to warm your hands by, not a blaze to scorch your soul.

Because love, like measles, only comes once. After that, you’re on your own.
