As the weeks passed since the tumultuous departure of the family's chaplain, a sense of fragile peace settled over Castlewood. George's mother, Madam Esmond, and her son appeared to have put aside their differences, though the weight of their quarrel still lingered, like a gentle fog that clings to the landscape. George's mind, however, seemed to be haunted by the memory of their dispute, for he soon fell ill with a fever that left him tossing restlessly in his bed. His cries of "Broken! Broken! It can never be mended!" echoed through the stillness of the night, striking fear into the heart of his mother, who sat vigil beside him.

The widow's remedies and potions failed to alleviate her son's suffering, and she was forced to seek the help of Mr. Dempster, the local physician. On a snowy morning, she rode to his humble abode, her pony's hooves crunching through the frost. Mr. Dempster, though initially reluctant, agreed to tend to George, putting aside his own feelings of hurt and resentment towards the widow. As he worked tirelessly to nurse George back to health, the fever slowly began to recede, and the boy's strength began to return.

The suggestion of a change of air, perhaps a voyage to England, was floated, but the widow's estrangement from her children's relatives made it unfeasible. Instead, a journey to the north and east was planned, with Mr. Dempster accompanying the two young gentlemen as their tutor, along with a pair of servants to attend to their needs. They set sail for New York, then made their way up the majestic Hudson River to Albany, where they were warmly welcomed by the local gentry. From there, they traveled into the French provinces, where they were lavishly entertained by the French nobility.

While Harry immersed himself in the wilderness, hunting furs and bears with the Indians, George found himself drawn to the refined society of the French court. He quickly mastered the French language, much to the admiration of the ladies, who were accustomed to few English gentlemen speaking it so fluently. George's musical talents also blossomed, and he became enamored with the violin, playing the latest French catches and songs with elegance and precision. Though his voice was still developing, he dreamed of singing alongside his instrument, but for now, he was content to accompany himself with the sweet sounds of the violin.
