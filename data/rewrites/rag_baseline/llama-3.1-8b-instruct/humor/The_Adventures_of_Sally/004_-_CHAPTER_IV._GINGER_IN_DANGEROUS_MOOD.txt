The dim gas lamps of the Piccadilly streets cast long shadows on the pavement as Bruce Carmyle strolled along, his eyes scanning the crowded thoroughfare with a mixture of disinterest and purpose. It was a day like any other in the city, the hum of conversation and the rattle of horse-drawn carriages a constant accompaniment to his thoughts. He had just parted ways with his cousin, Ginger, and the memory of their brief encounter lingered, a faint discordant note in the otherwise harmonious melody of his day.

As he turned a corner, the sound of a piano drifted from a nearby music hall, the melancholy tune weaving in and out of the din of the street. Bruce Carmyle paused, his footsteps slowing as he listened. He had always found solace in music, a respite from the complexities of his own emotions. But today, the notes seemed to fall flat, unable to lift the weight of his thoughts.

He continued walking, his destination a small club in a quiet alleyway off the main street. Inside, the air was thick with the smell of cigar smoke and old leather. Bruce Carmyle made his way to the bar, where he ordered a whiskey and began to peruse a newspaper. The headlines seemed to drone on, a monotone litany of events that held no interest for him.

As he read, a figure caught his eye – a woman with piercing green eyes and raven-black hair, her features set in a determined expression. She was sitting alone at a table near the fire, her gaze fixed intently on a book in her hands. Bruce Carmyle's interest was piqued, and he found himself drawn to her, his feet moving of their own accord towards the table.

The woman looked up, her eyes meeting his, and for a moment, they simply regarded each other. Then, with a faint smile, she nodded in greeting. Bruce Carmyle felt a spark of connection, a sense of possibility that he couldn't quite explain. He took a seat across from her, ordering a coffee as he introduced himself.

Her name was Sally, and she was a writer, working on a novel that seemed to fascinate her completely. As they talked, Bruce Carmyle found himself becoming more and more absorbed in her words, the world outside receding into the background. It was a rare sensation, one he hadn't experienced in a long time, and he savored every moment of it.
