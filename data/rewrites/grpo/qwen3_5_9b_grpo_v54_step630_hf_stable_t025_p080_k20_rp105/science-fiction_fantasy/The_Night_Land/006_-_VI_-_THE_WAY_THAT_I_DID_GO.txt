I moved forward, deliberately keeping my gaze fixed ahead, refusing to look back. To turn now would be to invite despair; what lay before demanded every ounce of my courage and spirit. My sole purpose was to reach that distant Maiden in the World's darkness, or to face my inevitable death with dignity.

I pressed on, steady and resolute, acutely aware of the sorrow of leaving my mighty Home. I felt the weight of the tenderness and wisdom that had guided our curbed existence, a warmth that lingered in my heart even as I departed. My gratitude was profound toward Master Monstruwacan for descending in armor to join the Full Watch, standing as a pillar of strength to bolster my spirit at this critical juncture.

As I walked, the night air swirled around me, charged with the chaotic thoughts and desperate wishes of the millions I had just abandoned. Soon, the coldness of the night became palpable, biting at my lungs and sharpening my palate with a keen, metallic tang. It tasted denser, heavier than the air of the Thousandth City, suggesting a different composition entirely. This variation was expected; the atmosphere shifted depending on altitude, and many peoples had migrated to levels that offered better health, all under strict regulation. Yet, nature was complex; some thrived where others perished.

Deeper in the fields, the air was a tapestry of wonders, capable of enchanting one soul while saddening another, provided one wandered with reason as a guide.

I continued, filled with a mix of new thoughts, ancient memories, and breaking wonders, though doubt and fear still tugged at my mind. It was uncanny to finally step onto the outward earth of the Night Land—a place my imagination had visited a thousand times, yet never before touched with my feet. To those of the present age, this thought would seem quaint, perhaps impossible.

Eventually, I reached the Circle surrounding the Redoubt. To my surprise, it was not the massive structure I had reasoned it should be. Instead, it was a small, clear tube, barely two inches thick, yet it emitted a brilliant, intense light that seemed to expand when viewed from a distance. This tiny beacon had witnessed countless things; I remembered seeing beast-monsters peering over it, their faces emerging from the night. As a child, I had kept watch through the embrasures, hoping to be the first to spot a monster gazing inward at the Mighty Pyramid. We took pride in identifying the ugliest horrors, treating it as a game until a more fearsome brute appeared. Now, looking at that small tube, a half-familiar shudder ran through me, mingling with a childish joy in the safety that made such dangers seem manageable.

These memories surged forth, causing me to hesitate instinctively, fearing what might emerge from the Dark beyond. But soon, my heart cleared, and I gained the courage to bid farewell. I turned to gaze upon the wondrous Home of the Last Millions. The sight was breathtaking; a mountain of life rising measurelessly into the night, spreading across the earth to hold the world in its grasp.

On the nearer slopes stood three hundred thousand great embrasures, totaling a million and twenty thousand across the four sides of the Redoubt, as recorded in the schoolbooks and the quaintly named Atlas volumes. Half a mile upward began the lowest tier of these window-places, followed by countless others stretching into the distance. A great light poured from them, illuminating the darkness for miles. I looked up, separating each embrasure from its neighbor, until they faded into a constant, glimmering fire that shaped a shining peak against the black heavens. This was the Everlasting Monument.

I realized that the tiny shapes clustered against the embrasures were the countless millions of the Redoubt. In the lower tiers, they were visible like insects against the inner light, watching me through spy-glasses. I knew they looked out, and I returned my gaze upward along the grey metal slope, following it to the little star crowning the Wonder of the World—the Tower of Observation where I had spent my life. I knew Master Monstruwacan bent his Great Spy-Glass upon me, the lens through which I had so often spied. I raised the Diskos in salute and farewell, though I saw him not.

My heart swelled, yet my soul grew stronger. Then, a murmur rose from the night, dim and distant. I saw the little shapes in the lower embrasures moving constantly. Instantly, I understood that the Multitudes were taking my salute, crying out and waving farewells, or perhaps pleas to return.

Alone before that great mountain of metal and life, I knew the danger of lingering. I raised the Diskos, reversed it, offering a salute from one young man to all the Millions. I looked swiftly upward through eight great miles of night toward the Final Light, ensuring my friend knew I thought of him in this last moment. Perhaps the invisible millions high in the Upper Cities interpreted this too as a farewell; a faint, vague wind-like sound drifted down from the monstrous heights.

Lowering the Diskos, I turned away. I stepped across the Circle into the lonesomeness of the Night Land, breasting the Air Clog. I looked back no more; the sight of my Home weakened my heart, and I resolved not to glance behind for a great while.

Yet, as I walked, the world's aether surged around me, carrying the continual prayers, wishes, and watches of my people and kin. This gave me a sense of companionship, but it also brought a chilling realization: this disturbance might signal an Evil Force that I was abroad in the Land. How could I stop it? If I had explained the danger to the multitudes, their collective thought alone would have created a disturbance too great to contain.

At first, I walked blindly, intent only on putting distance between myself and the ache in my heart. But soon, I slowed, applying reason to my path. I feared the over-watchfulness of the area trodden by the Youths, so I altered my course. Instead of heading directly North, I turned North-West, intending to circle around the back of the North-West Watcher, then proceed to the North of the Plain of Blue Fire, eventually finding a straight path North. This detour would take me clear of the House of Silence, which terrified me more than any other horror in the Land.

This made the journey longer, but survival demanded caution. Haste led to destruction; slowness offered a chance of success. My assurance in heading North came from an inward knowing and from studies of olden books within the Pyramid.

One such book, a strange, ancient metal volume forgotten in the Great Library for perhaps a million years, had recently caught my attention. While much of its content was dismissed as fairy tales by my contemporaries, I sensed ancient truths beneath the shell. It recounted a catastrophic event from a time long past: the world braked upward in a vast earthquake, rending the globe for a thousand miles. A mighty chasm opened, swallowing an ocean, shaking all cities, and blanketing the earth in mist and rain.

While the people of the Mighty Pyramid treated this as mere entertainment for nurseries, this specific book quoted witnesses with a gravity that demanded belief. Its final sections described a later age, perhaps a million years hence, when the Sun was dying. In that time, the Mighty Chasm had settled into a deep, wondrous Valley holding seas, forests, and lands of varying climates—some healthy, others filled with fire, steam, and poisonous sulfur clouds.

Here, in the warmth of the valley floor, Great Beasts bred anew, born from primal forces. Humanity, having grown soft through ages of ease, faced a cold, unfriendly world as the Sun waned. A hardy race emerged, fighting for survival. They discovered the Valley was a place of warmth and life and began the arduous climb down the wondrous height. Generations died climbing "The Road," unaware of the destination, until finally, they reached the bottom.

They fought monsters, built cities, and extended the Road along the Valley. But at the Great Corner where the Sun's light failed, they encountered a constant shadow. Driven by the horror of the Valley of Shadow and its evil things, the Road Makers retreated to the Red Light of the Westward Valley, living there for a hundred thousand years. There, in ignorance despite their wisdom, they experimented with harmful forces, opening gates to disaster.

When the twilight of the world deepened, madness spread among the city dwellers. Cities were attacked by monsters from the West, sparking a Pandemonium. An Age of Sorrows followed, breeding a determined generation and a Leader who united the sound Millions. He waged a mighty battle against foulness, scattering enemies up and down the Valley.

He warned his people of the growing Darkness and the dread powers abroad, urging them to build a Mighty Refuge. They acclaimed the idea, and a Great House was begun, but it proved insufficient. The Leader led the Peoples to wander, eventually reaching the Bight where the Great and Mighty Pyramid was finally constructed.

This was the sense of the book, discussed briefly with Master Monstruwacan before my sudden resolve to leave took hold. We agreed that the Great Road of the Silent Ones must be the same path made by those hardy peoples. If the Lesser Redoubt existed, it must lie within the mighty Valley. Whether the Road leading West to the Ab-humans or the Northward path would lead me there remained uncertain. I might wander a thousand miles astray, or fall into dreadful trouble before finding it.

There was little hope the Lesser Pyramid lay West or North beyond the House of Silence. Yet, I felt a strong certainty it lay to the North. The Valley was the only logical refuge; the upper world, a hundred miles up in the night, was too bleak and deadly for life, lacking the sweet, needful air found in the deep places of the earth.

Strange to think of the wondrous cliffs girding us, hidden in the dark, unknown except through the book's telling. We had always believed we lived in the bed of an ancient sea, sloping gradually away, not abruptly up into savage heights. The general populace held no deep conviction of such hidden worlds; they accepted the strange fires, the ever-abiding night, and the monsters as the natural order. Some fancied possibilities, but the majority were content with what they saw.

With this certainty, I made speed towards the North. I knew there were only two paths: the cold, dead lonesomeness of the hidden upper world, or the Valley below. The Valley held the answer. Though I had taken a circuitous route for sound reasons, I pressed on, driven by the hope that the Road Makers' legacy would guide me to safety.
