The words spoken by Tatho that night served as no sedative for my slumber. Instead, they stirred a restless conscience within me. I began to question my decision to bury myself entirely within the administration of Yucatan, severing ties with the currents of events rippling through Atlantis. For decades, I had observed that the seafarers who traversed the oceans spoke with caution, their tongues bound by the Empress's discretion. News flowed from her realm only when she deemed it fit to leak. Yet, I had been so consumed by the duties of my colonial post that I lacked the curiosity—or perhaps the moral fortitude—to tear aside a veil deliberately lowered by my superiors. Furthermore, it ran counter to my principles to subject men to interrogation simply because they had received orders of silence from those above them, solely to satisfy my private inquiries.

Nevertheless, the rigid discipline of our Priestly Clan offered me no alternative path. By custom, I had been stripped of my office with abrupt finality. From that moment, all authority and archives passed to my successor. Though courtesy might permit me to linger in the pyramid that had recently housed me, witnessing another sunrise, it was strictly enjoined that I must vacate the territory immediately and hasten back to Atlantis.

To his credit, Tatho was eager to advance my interests to the utmost limit of his power. Before dawn, he stood by my side, placing all his resources at my disposal.

My needs were minimal. "A ship to take me home," I said, "and I shall be your debtor."

His reaction surprised me. "Certainly, you may have one if you wish. However, my vessels are fouled by the length of their passages and require careening. If you take them, your return to Atlantis will be a slow, arduous voyage. Why do you not command your own navy? The ships are currently in harbor—I saw them when we arrived. They are brave vessels, too."

"But they are not mine. That fleet belongs to Yucatan."

"Very well, Deucalion. You are Yucatan, or rather, you *were* Yucatan until yesterday, and perhaps for the last twenty years."

I grasped his implication, and the notion disquieted me. I replied stiffly that the ships were either owned by private merchants or the State, and I could not claim even a single ten-slave galley.

Tatho shrugged. "I suppose you know your policies best, though to me it seems ill-advised for a man of your stature not to maintain a personal navy. One never knows when a recall order might arrive. Without such precautions, a lifetime's earnings can vanish in a dozen hours."

"I have no fear for mine," I answered coolly.

"Naturally not, because you know me to be your friend. But had another vice-royal been appointed to this post, you might have been severely stripped, Deucalion. Few men can withstand a cozy hoard sitting ready in the very coffers they have filled."

"My Lord Tatho," I said, "it is evident to me that our tastes have diverged. Every penny I have amassed in this colony, few men would envy. Here I stand in these poor garments you see, clutching a box of medicinal herbs that have proved useful for the stomach. I possess three slaves—two scribes and a sturdy European savage who cooks and bathes me. For twenty years of service, I have drawn only a soldier's ration from the State. If any soul in Yucatan has extorted so much as an ounce of bronze from a citizen in my name, I beg you, as a final favor, to hang them as liars and thieves."

Tatho regarded me with a mixture of awe and pity. "I am torn between admiring and pitying you. Astonishment or contempt—I cannot decide. We heard tales of your integrity across the sea, of your stern justice, yet I swear by the Old Gods that no soul imagined you would carry your austerity so far. Money, man, is power. With wealth and what it buys, nothing stands in your way; without it, a single slight setback can trample you irrevocably."

"The Gods will choose my fate."

"Possibly. But for myself, I prefer to cultivate it. Let me be frank: I have not come here to mimic the model you built for a vice-royalty. I intend to govern Yucatan wisely and well, to the best of my ability, but also for the benefit of Tatho the Viceroy. I brought eight ships and a personal guard, plus my wife, her women, and her slaves. All must be provided for. Why should it not be? If a people is to be ruled, it is their privilege to pay handsomely for their prince."

"We shall not agree on this point. You hold the power now and may wield it as you see fit. If I thought it beneficial, I would humbly supplicate you to exercise lenience when taxing these subjects. They have grown quite dear to me."

"You have disgusted me, and I am grieved. Yet, even to retain your good regard, Deucalion—which I value more than any other man's living—I cannot act as you did. It would be impossible, even if I wished it. Do not measure all men by your singular, lofty standard: a Tatho is no colossus compared to a Deucalion. Moreover, I have a wife and children who must be supported, regardless of my own self-denial."

"Aha!" I exclaimed. "There, it appears I hold the advantage. I have no wife to encumber me."

He seized upon my remark instantly. "It seems to me you possess nothing that makes life worthwhile. No wife, no children, no riches, no cooks, no retinue, no finery. Please forgive me, old comrade, but you are woefully ignorant of certain matters. Take dining, for instance. After days of weary voyages subsisting on meager sea provisions, I promised myself that your welcome feast would redeem the abstinence. Oh, I tell you, that banquet was one of the most vivid spectacles I have ever witnessed. Then, when we reached the reality, what was it? A country farmer daily sits down to more delicate fare. You described the preparation. Yes, your European savage may be robust and perhaps faithful, but he is a devil-possessed cook. Gods! I have lived better on a military campaign."

"I know," I interjected, "this is a colony lacking the home refinements. But if, in future days, the deer of the forest, the fish of the streams, and the local resources are not utilized better than they have been, I shall deem it my duty as ruler to fry some kitchen staff in grease to encourage better cooking. Gods! Deucalion, have you forgotten the nature of a palate? Have you no esteem for your own dignity? Man, look at your clothes. You are garbed like a herdsman, devoid of jewels to brighten your form."

"I eat," I said coldly, "when hunger dictates, and I wear this single robe until it falls apart and requires replacement. The grossness of excessive feasting and the effeminacy of multiple garments are achievements that never appealed to me. However, I believe we have discoursed long enough, and there seems little prospect of agreement. You have changed, Tatho, with the passing of years, and perhaps I have altered as well. These shifts creep imperceptibly into one's being as time advances. Let us part now, and, forgetting these current discordances, remember only our friendship of twenty years ago. That bond, for me at least, always retains a pleasant flavor when recalled from memory."

Tatho bowed his head. "So be it."

"And I would still entrust myself to your generosity regarding that ship. Dawn cannot be far off, and it is undignified for a man who ruled here so long to walk through the streets in daylight on the morning following his dismissal."

"So be it," said Tatho. "You shall have my humble navy. I could have wished you had asked for something grander."

"Not the navy, Tatho; one small ship. Believe me, more is squandered."

"Now, there," said Tatho, "I shall play the tyrant. I am Viceroy here now, and I shall have my way. You may depart stripped of all possessions; I cannot prevent that. But you shall not leave for Atlantis unattended."

Thus, in conclusion, as the choice became absolute, it fell upon the "Bear," Tatho's own private vessel, accompanied by the entirety of his navy sailing in escort, that I finally made my transit.

Yet the departure was not immediate. The vessels lay moored against the stone quays of the inner harbor, hollowed of their stores, with crews utterly exhausted. To force them out then and there to face the seas again would have been suicidal.

Consequently, the courtesies were performed as the craft bearing me was hauled out into the entrance basin and anchored in the swells of the fairway. Immediately, she and her companions took in wood and water, cured meats and fish from shore, and underwent necessary refitting with all possible speed.

For myself, there arrived, for the first time in twenty busy years, a respite from labor. I had severed all official connection with the land of my toil; indeed, I had already departed it.
