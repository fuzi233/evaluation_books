The following tight, steadily more prickly exchange of notes passed between Franklin Blake and Miss Drusilla Clack, the pious, fiercely opinionated Verinder relative contributing her first-hand testimony to the growing collection of accounts being compiled to unpack the Moonstone’s baffling disappearance:

1.  Miss Clack presents her compliments to Mr. Franklin Blake. Enclosed with this note is the fifth chapter of her humble personal narrative. She writes that she finds herself entirely unfit to properly expand on the harrowing, unexpected event of Lady Verinder’s death as the gravity of the circumstances demand, and has for that reason attached a large bundle of extracts from precious, carefully preserved religious tracts in her possession, every page relevant to the terrible subject of sudden, unforeseen passing. She fervently prays those extracts will ring out like a trumpet blast to rouse her respected kinsman, Mr. Franklin Blake, to long-overdue serious reflection.

2.  Mr. Franklin Blake presents his compliments to Miss Clack, and thanks her for the fifth chapter of her narrative. He returns the full set of extracts sent along with it, and will refrain from noting any personal distaste he might hold for that particular category of devotional literature. He merely states that the proposed additions to her manuscript are entirely unnecessary for the strictly fact-finding purpose of the narrative he is assembling to solve the Moonstone’s mystery.

3.  Miss Clack sends her thanks for the return of her Extracts. She affectionately reminds Mr. Franklin Blake that she is a devout Christian, and therefore it is literally impossible for him to give her offence. She persists in holding the deepest, most earnest interest in his spiritual wellbeing, and pledges that the first time sickness lays him low, she will press the full use of her carefully curated Extracts on him a second time. In the meanwhile, before she begins writing the final chapters of her narrative, she wishes to know if she has permission to make her humble contribution to the case record complete by drawing on the new, recent discoveries that have come to light around the Moonstone’s baffling theft.

4.  Mr. Franklin Blake regrets having to disappoint Miss Clack. He can only restate the exact instructions he gave her the day she began drafting her narrative: she is strictly requested to limit her writing only to her own personal, first-hand experience of people and events, exactly as they are recorded in her private diary. All later discoveries relating to the mystery are to be left to the pens of the actual, on-the-scene witnesses who saw those events unfold, as per the ground rules of the compilation.

5.  Miss Clack writes again, apologising for troubling Mr. Franklin Blake with yet another letter. Her Extracts have been sent back to her, and her carefully considered, mature views on the subject of the Moonstone have been forbidden from inclusion. She is painfully aware that, by worldly standards, many would say she has been firmly put in her place. But she learned perseverance in the school of adversity, and she will not be dissuaded. Her reason for writing is simple: does Mr. Franklin Blake, who has prohibited every other proposed addition to her manuscript, also prohibit this full sequence of their correspondence from appearing in her published narrative? Some explanation for the position his interference has placed her in, as a serious authoress, is only a matter of basic justice, and she is most eager that their letters be allowed to speak for themselves without editorial meddling.

6.  Mr. Franklin Blake agrees to Miss Clack’s proposal, on the strict condition that she treats this note as his final word on the matter, and that this consent closes all further correspondence between the two of them on the subject of her narrative.

7.  Miss Clack writes one last time, stating that it is an act of Christian duty, before their correspondence ceases, to inform Mr. Franklin Blake that his last letter — clearly written with the deliberate intent to offend her — failed utterly to achieve that uncharitable goal. She affectionately requests Mr. Franklin Blake to retire to the privacy of his own room, and reflect carefully on whether the devout training that can elevate a poor, weak woman like herself entirely out of the reach of insult is not far more worthy of his admiration than he is currently disposed to admit. The moment he sends her a note acknowledging that admiration, she solemnly pledges to mail the full complete set of her precious Extracts straight back to him, no further discussion required.

To this final note, no reply of any kind was ever received. No further comment is needed.
(Signed) Drusilla Clack
