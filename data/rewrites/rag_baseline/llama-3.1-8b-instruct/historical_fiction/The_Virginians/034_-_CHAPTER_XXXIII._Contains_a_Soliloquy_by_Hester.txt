Hester's thoughts swirled like autumn leaves on a forgotten riverbank. Her father's words, once sharp as a knife, now hung heavy as the morning mist. A gentle breeze rustled the pages of the old Bible, worn smooth by generations of fingers. She stood at the threshold, a leaf caught between seasons, torn between the comfort of her childhood and the uncertain tides of adulthood.

The Colonel's words, a mixture of frustration and resignation, still lingered in the air. His anger, like a winter storm, had passed, leaving behind a chill that seeped into the bones. Her mother's calm, a still pond reflecting the sky above, was a refuge from the turmoil. Yet, even her words held a hint of sadness, a whispered promise that the world would soon take her daughter from her arms.

Hester's gaze wandered to the window, where the sun cast a faint glow on the dust motes dancing in the air. Outside, the world moved with its relentless pace, unaware of the fragile balance within these walls. Her parents' love, like the gentle lapping of waves on a summer shore, had been a constant presence in her life. But now, the undertow of adulthood threatened to pull her away, into the unknown depths.

As she stood there, the sound of her mother's voice carried on the wind, a melody that echoed through the rooms of her memory. "It will be a sad thing, parting with her." The words hung in the air, a poignant reminder of the choices that lay ahead. The Colonel's laughter, a distant rumble of thunder, signaled his acceptance of the inevitable. He would go out, to order raisins and currants for the wedding cake, a ritualistic gesture that masked the ache within.

Hester's thoughts turned to the journey ahead, a vast expanse of miles and seas that stretched before her like an unfurled map. Her father's words, a mix of jest and gravity, echoed in her mind: "A hundred and seventy-three thousand three hundred and ninety-one and three-quarters, my dear, by the near way." The numbers blurred together, a meaningless jumble of digits that seemed to mock her uncertainty. She longed for the comfort of her mother's hand, the familiarity of the familiar route, and the security of the past. But like the autumn leaves, she was caught in the undertow of change, swept away by the currents of fate.
