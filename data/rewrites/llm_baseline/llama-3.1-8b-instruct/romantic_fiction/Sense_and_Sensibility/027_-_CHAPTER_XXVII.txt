As the warm sunlight streamed through the windows of Mrs. Jennings's townhouse, illuminating the delicate china and fine linens of the breakfast table, she turned to Elinor and Marianne with a concerned expression. "If this pleasant weather continues," she said, "Sir John will be most disappointed when he leaves Barton next week. Sportsmen such as he take great joy in the hunt, and a lost day's pleasure can be a significant blow to their spirits."

Marianne, who had been quietly observing the day's forecast from the window, nodded thoughtfully in agreement. Her eyes sparkled with excitement as she exclaimed, "Yes, indeed, it's a perfect day for them! They'll be able to enjoy the countryside without a care in the world." As she sat down to join her sisters at the table, her face radiated a newfound happiness.

However, her enthusiasm was tempered by a flicker of worry. "Still, it can't last forever," she mused, her brow furrowed in concern. "With the season changing and the recent rain, we can expect the weather to turn cold soon. Perhaps even tonight." Elinor, ever the pragmatist, tried to reassure her sister, suggesting that they would likely have Sir John and Lady Middleton in town by the end of the week.

Mrs. Jennings smiled knowingly, saying, "Ay, my dear, I'm sure you're right. Mary always gets her way." As the conversation flowed, Elinor couldn't help but wonder if her sister's friend had already written to her brother-in-law at Combe, sharing news of their arrival in town. Though she kept a close eye on Marianne, she couldn't quite discern the truth behind her sister's thoughts.

Meanwhile, Marianne's gaze drifted back to the window, where she continued to observe the wind patterns and sky conditions. Her mind whirled with possibilities, as she speculated about the approaching frost and its potential impact on the local cuisine. She envisioned hearty stews and roasts, simmering over open fires, filling the air with savory aromas that would soon give way to the crisp, biting chill of winter.
