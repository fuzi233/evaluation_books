As the fog lifted from the Thames, Colonel Altamont descended the gangway of the Soho, its passengers disembarking onto the bustling docks of Antwerp. Amidst the throng, he stood tall, resplendent in his finest travelling attire, his body-servant attending him with the deference due a gentleman of his stature. The Colonel's robust constitution allowed him to indulge in copious amounts of brandy-and-water during the voyage, yet he emerged unscathed, a testament to his remarkable resilience. His reputation as a man of discerning taste preceded him, and his presence drew whispers of admiration from the assembled crowd.

The evening prior, he had shared a sumptuous repast with the Chevalier and a select few at Blackwall, toasting his health with a generosity that bordered on extravagance. As the night wore on, the conversation turned to the topic of pecuniary matters, and Altamont's erstwhile acquaintance, the Chevalier, extended an offer of financial assistance, citing their long-standing bond as justification. However, the Captain declined, assuring his friend that he was presently well-provided for, though unable to repay the previous loan in full. Their parting was marked by a cordiality that belied the undercurrents of their relationship.

As Strong pondered the Colonel's transformation, he found himself questioning whether true change had occurred, or merely an illusion born of newfound wealth. Was Altamont genuinely improved, or was it simply the allure of prosperity that had captivated Strong's perception? He weighed the scales of their friendship against that of his acquaintance, Sir Francis Clavering, Bart., and found himself drawn to the latter's rougher, more rugged character. Altamont's flaws, though numerous, seemed somehow more forgivable, his capacity for loyalty and courage undeniable. Strong's introspection revealed a certain regret over the fib he had told the Colonel, a falsehood born of a desire to maintain a veneer of honesty. Yet, he knew himself too well to expect the same level of integrity from Clavering, whose empty promises hung in the air like a challenge.

In the silence of his chambers, Strong found himself missing the Colonel's presence, his conversations, and the camaraderie they had shared.
