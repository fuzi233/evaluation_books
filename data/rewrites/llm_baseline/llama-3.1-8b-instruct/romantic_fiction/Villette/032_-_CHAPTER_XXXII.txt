As I strolled along the tranquil boulevard, basking in the warmth of a gentle April afternoon, my thoughts wandered to Paulina Mary, whose whereabouts had been a mystery for some time. My recent correspondence with the esteemed Hôtel Crécy had been sporadic, owing to the extended travels of M. and Miss de Bassompierre, who had divided their time between the provinces and the French capital. News of their return reached me shortly thereafter.

As I walked, lost in contemplation, a group of riders came into view, halting momentarily as they exchanged greetings on the broad, tree-lined path. A middle-aged gentleman, a young lady, and a handsome young man comprised the trio, their faces familiar to me. Drawing closer, I recognized the Count Home de Bassompierre, his daughter, and Dr. Graham Bretton. Dr. Graham's countenance shone with an animation that bespoke a deep-seated joy, though one tempered by a certain reserve. His admiration for Paulina was not merely a matter of personal taste; it was also a reflection of the societal expectations that governed his actions. For Dr. John, it was not enough to appreciate beauty or charm in isolation; they had to be sanctioned by the broader social consensus, validated by the approval of high society and the world at large. The presence of Paulina, resplendent in her refined attire and accompanied by her father, fulfilled all these conditions, rendering her irresistible to him.

As they parted ways, Dr. Graham rode off with a sense of purpose, his eyes fixed intently ahead. The Countess called out to me, her voice melodious and friendly. "Lucy, dear Lucy, do come here!" she exclaimed. I hastened to join her, and she dismounted to bestow a warm kiss upon me.

"I was planning to visit you tomorrow," she said, "but now I propose that you come to see me instead."

We agreed on a time, and the next evening found me in her company, ensconced in her private room. It had been some time since our last meeting, when her claims had been weighed against those of Ginevra Fanshawe, and I had emerged victorious. Now, she regaled me with tales of her travels, her words flowing effortlessly in our tête-à-tête. Her descriptions were vivid and engaging, delivered in a style that was both artless and captivating.
