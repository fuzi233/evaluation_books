As the first light of dawn crept into the room, Romola was roused by a soft knock at the door. Maso stood before her, his eyes widening slightly as he took in the figure cloaked in the somber gray mantle and dark cowl. "It is well, Maso," she said calmly, attempting to reassure him as she handed over the traveling wallet. "You will proceed quietly, and I will not be far behind. When you pass through the gates, you may slow your pace, as I might join you before reaching Trespiano."

With the door closed, Romola's hand instinctively sought the small key that had lain hidden in the embroidered pouch attached to Tito's discarded tunic. It was the original key to the delicate tabernacle, one that Tito had inadvertently left behind when he abandoned her. She had discovered it years ago, yet never found the courage to use it, keeping it instead as a poignant reminder of her past. Now, as the cold morning light cast an unforgiving glow, she felt an overwhelming urge to confront the memories it represented.

The tabernacle, relocated to the side of the room near a window, stood bathed in a pale, eerie light. The intricate paintings that adorned its surface – the triumphant Bacchus, the loveshowering roses, the rippled sea – seemed a cruel mockery, a bittersweet reminder of a life left behind. Romola's gaze lingered on the image of Ariadne, her hyacinthine curls entwined among the vines, as if holding secrets she dare not reveal. A pang of sadness struck her, and she wondered how this radiant figure would appear amidst the vines and roses now.

Romola's eyes drifted to the mirror, and she gazed at her reflection, her face shrouded in the darkness of her cowl. The morning light only served to heighten the sense of unease, and she hastily laid the mirror down, as if it was a weight she could no longer bear. The thought of embracing the fanaticism she had always despised filled her with trepidation, and she hastened to unlock the tabernacle, her fingers trembling as she hung the crucifix around her neck.
