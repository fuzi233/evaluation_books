As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the tranquil landscape, Harry Warrington settled into the evening routine at Nether Hall. His daily games of whist or quadrille with the ladies had become a staple of his afternoons, but it was the clandestine card games, held deep into the night, that truly allowed him to showcase his skills. The stakes were high, and the company was select – his cousin William, the pious chaplain, and even the esteemed Lord Castlewood had all been known to join in, their conversations flowing as freely as the claret and punch that lubricated the proceedings.

Harry's reputation as a shrewd player had preceded him, and his cousins had been eager to test their mettle against his. But Harry was a master of discretion, and he had carefully guarded his true nature, revealing only glimpses of his rough-around-the-edges upbringing in the American colonies. His descriptions of life on the plantation had been laced with a mix of truth and embellishment, a trait that was both endearing and disconcerting to those who knew him.

In the New World, the aristocracy had little need for the trappings of refinement, and the evenings were often spent in the pursuit of more rustic pleasures – hunting, drinking, and gaming. The hospitality was warm and generous, with every man's door open to his neighbor, and the idle gentry would often ride from one grand estate to another, seeking adventure and camaraderie. Harry had grown accustomed to the rough-and-tumble life, sleeping on straw mattresses and waking to the sound of hounds baying in the distance. But despite his tough exterior, he had a certain vulnerability, a sensitivity that he reserved for those closest to him.

As the night wore on, the fire crackled and spat, casting flickering shadows on the walls of Nether Hall. Harry's cousins and their companions were well-matched opponents, but he held his own, his mind sharpened by the quiet rhythms of the night. It was a world away from the stifling propriety of English society, where manners were polished and tongues were tempered. But Harry was not one to shy away from the rough edges of life – he had a certain fondness for the unvarnished truth, and a taste for the untamed beauty of the world around him.
