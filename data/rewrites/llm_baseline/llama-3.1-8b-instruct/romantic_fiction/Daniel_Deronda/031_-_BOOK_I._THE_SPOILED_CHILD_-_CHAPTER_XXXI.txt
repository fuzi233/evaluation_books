The morning dawned bright and crisp on the day of Gwendolen Harleth's union with Grandcourt, the air carrying the faint scent of frost that lingered on the leaves. As the sun climbed higher, its rays danced across the pathway leading to the church, where the bridal party had gathered. The rector, acting as father to the happy couple, officiated the ceremony with an air of quiet dignity, accompanied by an old friend who had traveled from afar to perform the rites. The villagers of Pennicote lined the path, their faces aglow with curiosity and good wishes for the young pair.

However, amidst the joy and celebration, two faces betrayed a hint of sorrow. Mrs. Davilow's delicate features were red-rimmed, as if she had spent the entire night weeping. Her parting from her beloved daughter, the radiant Gwendolen, weighed heavily on her heart. It was only natural, given the magnitude of the match, that she should feel a pang of loss. Yet, Anna's distress seemed more puzzling, her usual lighthearted demeanor tempered by a somber expression. Her bridesmaid's gown, typically a highlight of the day, failed to lift her spirits, and her eyes seemed clouded by a private sorrow.

As the bride emerged from the church, a collective gasp of admiration swept through the crowd. Gwendolen's figure, carriage, and bearing all seemed to radiate an aura of elegance, as if she had been born to be a lady of title. Her face, though perhaps lacking the rosy hue some might associate with nobility, shone with a captivating intensity, drawing all eyes to her. Grandcourt, too, was admired for his dark complexion, which seemed to complement Gwendolen's beauty perfectly. The villagers whispered among themselves about the match, speculating on the happiness that lay ahead for the young couple.

Nearby, a group of women engaged in hushed conversation, their words carrying on the wind. "Men are men, gentle or simple, they're much of a muchness," she warned her daughter, a wide-eyed girl of fourteen.
