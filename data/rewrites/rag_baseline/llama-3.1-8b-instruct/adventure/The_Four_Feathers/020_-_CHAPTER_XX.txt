As Durrance entered his home at Guessens, the darkness seemed to swallow him whole. He navigated the familiar contours of the space, guided by the tactile memory of countless evenings spent within these walls. His body-servant awaited him, ready to extinguish the lights and retire for the night. "You may leave now," Durrance said, his voice a gentle command. With a quiet nod, the servant vanished into the darkness, leaving Durrance alone to succumb to the shadows.

His study, a sanctuary of sorts, was a room that defied categorization. It was a gun-room, a trophy room, and a repository of memories, all woven together like the intricate patterns on a Persian rug. The air was heavy with the scent of aged leather and polished metal, and the soft creak of wood beneath his feet served as a reminder of the countless hours he'd spent within these walls. Durrance wandered the room, his fingers trailing over the surfaces of the cups and goblets that adorned the shelves. Each one held a story, a tale of triumph and adventure etched into its curves and lines.

He picked up a delicate goblet, its stem slender as a reed, and turned it over in his hands. This was the trophy he'd won at Colchester, the memory of the steeple-chase still vivid in his mind – the grey sky, the ploughed fields, and the determined faces of his competitors. Next, he caressed the worn surface of a pewter pen holder, its history dating back to his freshman year at Oxford. The weight of it in his hand transported him to a different era, one of late-night studies and camaraderie with fellow students. A silver-mounted horse's hoof, a testament to his prowess in the field, hung proudly on the mantle, its presence a reminder of the triumphs that had defined his life.

As he touched each trophy, the memories flooded back – the thrill of the hunt, the rush of adrenaline, and the satisfaction of a job well done. His guns, suspended from the walls like sentinels, stood silent witnesses to his exploits. He reached out to claim a Remington, feeling the cool metal beneath his fingers. The weight of it in his hands transported him to the scorching hills west of Berenice, where he'd stalked a lion across the open terrain, and brought it down with precision at three hundred yards.
