As the dawn broke over the rolling hills, Major Arthur Pendennis stepped down from the creaking mail-coach, his weary eyes scanning the familiar landscape of Fairoaks. The previous night's journey had been a trial, with a boisterous fellow-traveller dominating the conversation, a widow lady's potent spirits wafting through the air, and the incessant clanging of the coach's bell at every turnpike gate. The Major's gouty knees ached and his joints protested the prolonged confinement, but he had endured such hardships before, and the nostalgia for a bygone era brought a wistful smile to his lips. For in the old days, people had borne such discomforts with equanimity, and even managed to find merriment in the midst of it all.

As he stretched his cramped limbs, the Major's gaze fell upon a pair of schoolboys, their faces flushed with excitement, who had disembarked at the previous stop. They were heading home for the summer holidays, and their eagerness to indulge in a hearty meal at the local inn at Bagshot was infectious. The Major watched with amusement as they stocked up on an impressive array of victuals – ham, eggs, pie, pickles, tea, coffee, and boiled beef – which left him feeling somewhat envious, as he sipped a meagre cup of tea, his thoughts wandering to the lavish dinner party that Lord Steyne was hosting at the same moment.

The Major's attention was soon diverted by the presence of a young lord, a scion of the nobility, who had travelled with him in the coach. The boy's father was a acquaintance of the Major's, and he felt a sense of connection to the young man. As he observed the boy sleeping soundly, despite the chaos around him, the Major couldn't help but feel a pang of admiration for the youth's resilience.

Meanwhile, Helen had been watching from the window, anticipating the Major's arrival. However, she chose not to greet him immediately, knowing that he preferred a certain degree of ceremony before making an appearance. This was partly due to a childhood prank, in which the young Pen had pilfered the Major's morocco box, containing his false teeth, leaving him feeling embarrassed and self-conscious. The Major's wig, too, was a sensitive subject, with Morgan, his man, taking great pains to style it in private, before presenting it to the Major with a flourish.
