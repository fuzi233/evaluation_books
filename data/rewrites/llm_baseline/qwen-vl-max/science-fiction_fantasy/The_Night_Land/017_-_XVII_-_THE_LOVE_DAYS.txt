When I returned to life, I found myself ascending in the Lift, lying upon that same bed where I had once believed I would never need rest again—nor ever rise from the depths of the Country of Silence. My awareness was faint, drifting like smoke on a still night. From the endless dark beneath the world, I heard the deep thunder of the Underground Organs—a distant, mournful music that seemed to echo beyond death itself. A rolling chant rose and fell, as though countless voices sang across far mountains; sometimes it was the whisper of wind through caverns, other times it swelled into the ancient melody of the Song of Honour. I sensed, half-dreaming, that the millions below were rejoicing, celebrating this miracle of joy that had come to us.

But all was dim and veiled. My eyes could not open fully. I floated on currents of unreality, caught between waking and dreaming. Yet there were scents—sweet and real—that drifted to me: the perfume of blossoms from the great Fields, blooming endlessly along the paths of the Lifts. The machine carried me upward through miles of silence, yet life stirred around me.

I may have moved slightly. Then came the soft voice of the Master Doctor, gentle as moonlight: “Rest. All is well with the Maid.”

And so I slipped into a haze, drifting through days that blurred between sleep and wakefulness. I no longer knew if I lived or died. Then came a time when I lay still, thoughtless, while the Master Doctor bent over me often, his gaze sharp and searching.

At last, another figure leaned close—Mine Own. Her face, dear and radiant, looked down at me. Her eyes spoke love directly into my soul, calm and quiet, yet full of strength. And then, slowly, I began to live again. I fumbled weakly with my hands—she took them in hers. Life flowed from her to me. She said nothing, only held me, tender and still. Contentment grew within me, and soon I sank into a peaceful slumber.

A day came when I was allowed to sit up. Those who cared for me carried me to one of the Quiet Gardens of the Pyramid and left me there, alone.

Then, around a bush, she appeared—shy, yet glowing. It was Mine Own Maid. But I had never seen Naani dressed so beautifully, so delicately. She looked more exquisite than I had ever imagined. I tried to rise toward her—but she darted forward, stopping me with a laugh, gentle and loving. She sat beside me, cradling my head against her breast, offering her lips without hesitation. In that moment, she was both maiden and mother, fierce and tender.

Afterward, she insisted I remain still. We sat together in perfect silence, wrapped in a happiness too deep for words, until the attendants returned. Among them was the Master of the Doctors, and I saw satisfaction in his face.

From that day on, I saw her every day. I recovered swiftly—not from medicine, but from Love. Soon, I was permitted to descend into the Fields, though by hidden paths, for the Multitudes might have followed me, drawn by curiosity and reverence. I needed peace.

She was with me always. The Master Monstruwacan and the Master of the Doctors had agreed—so an Officer of Marriage wed us. Our wedding was simple, quiet. I was still too weak for the grand Public Marriage, which would come later. But when it did, the Millions formed a Guard of Honour eight miles high, stretching from the summit to the base of the Mighty Pyramid—a ceremonial tribute, for they could not deny me their honor.

Yet even then, she remained by my side. She was now my wife, my strength, my healing. My health returned completely, and we entered the Love Days—the most beautiful time of all, when Love is true.

We wandered freely through the vast Fields, walking the Love Paths near the scattered villages. I kept our name hidden, lest others seek us out in kindness or curiosity. We needed only each other, and quiet.

We chose our resting places where flowers bloomed in wild splendor, carrying food with us, but also eating in the villages—small towns spread across lands as large as nations. She fulfilled her promise a hundredfold, preparing hearty meals and teasing me playfully as I ate, kissing me before I could defend myself. She was everything my heart and spirit longed for—companion, lover, soul.

Once, we descended into the Country of Silence, but stayed only briefly, for my memories were returning. Later, we returned often—walking through that sacred land with memory, holiness, and love holding us together.

As we left, I told her how, when she had been suspended in death by the Horrid Force of the House, I had suffered a terrible pain—the knowledge that I had slept through her final kiss. She blushed deeply, lovely and ashamed, for she had never known I was aware of her secret tenderness. She confessed that she had felt only agony during her stillness, unaware of the world until the Earth-Force revived her.

The Doctors explained that her spirit had been stunned, frozen—her life suspended. Only the immense life-force of the Earth-Current had awakened her, restoring her body, her blood, her breath. They had searched ancient records and found one similar case—yet none like this in ages.

As we walked and rested in the Fields, I shared my thoughts with her. I learned she had studied many things while I was unconscious—though not much, for she too had been near death. When I lay still, she had risen from her own bed, driven by the Master Doctor’s command—he feared I might die unless my spirit was stirred. And she had held my hands, brave and gentle, though she could barely stand.

I whispered a silent prayer of gratitude for her courage.

And so I come to the end of my tale—save for one final thing.

Some time after our second marriage—the Public Ceremony—one day, my Wife, Mine Own, led me with sweet cunning to the Hall of Honour.

When I entered, I saw the People gathered in silence, standing still as if waiting for something greater than words. She guided me to the center—and then I understood why.

In the Place of Honour stood a statue: a man in shattered armor, holding a maid forever in his arms.

I stood dumbfounded.

You who live in this age may not know what such an honour means. It is given only to the Great Dead—those whose deeds echo through eternity. I was young, unknown, far from greatness. Yet here I stood, honoured not for power or conquest, but for love—for having loved with every part of my being.

And you will understand, as I did, that Love makes the heart brave, the soul sweet. I felt wonder, humility, and a quiet pride that no title could match. That anyone should think to honour me in such a way—was a gift beyond measure.

Mine Own wept beside me—joy and pride shining in her eyes. The entire hall was hushed, filled with a profound, loving silence. No one spoke. No one interrupted. They simply let us go, together, in peace.

And I walked with her—loving, thoughtful, whole. She was close, always near.

I had gained honour. But I had learned that honour is but ash without love.

I had love.

And to have love is to have all.

For true love gives birth to honour and faithfulness—and together, the three build the House of Joy.
