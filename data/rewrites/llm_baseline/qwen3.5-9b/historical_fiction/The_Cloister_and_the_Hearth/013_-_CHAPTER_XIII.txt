Gerard and Margaret departed for Sevenbergen buoyant with the fresh exhilaration of regained freedom and a recent triumph. Yet, this initial elation quickly curdled into somber reflection. Gerard remained a fugitive, perpetually vulnerable to recapture and punishment; consequently, separation became inevitable. Furthermore, he harbored a deep-seated animosity toward his homeland. While Margaret urged him to depart the country temporarily, the mere prospect of him traveling to Italy caused her heart to falter. Conversely, Gerard accepted the necessity of parting from her solely because it allowed him to pursue his dream of visiting Italy. He was convinced that there he could secure wealth and renown, thereby removing every barrier to their union. He recounted to her the warnings of the Demoiselle Van Eyck, reminding her that the gold pieces bestowed upon him were specifically intended for this Italian journey. Clearly, the path ahead served his interests alone. As a craftsman and artist adrift in this uncouth region, he believed Italy would finally recognize his worth. Moved by this selfless logic, the gentle girl granted her consent, though her agreement was accompanied by torrential tears. Hand in hand until midnight, they alternated between lamenting their cruel destiny and painting vivid, hopeful visions of a shared future. In these moments, Margaret's sorrow would overwhelm her, causing even Gerard's eloquent comfort to dissolve into a sigh. By dawn, resignation had taken hold, yet neither possessed the courage to name the hour of departure—a hesitation that might well have delayed their parting indefinitely.

Then, around three in the afternoon, Giles arrived. Having ridden great distances to evade suspicion, he galloped directly to the cottage door. Both lovers rushed forth, driven by urgent curiosity. "Brother Gerard!" Giles cried in his booming voice, "Kate commands you to flee for your life! They accuse you of theft; you have handed them proof. Do not attempt explanation; seek no justice in Tergou. The parchments you seized were merely a ruse. She has seen death in the men's gazes; a bounty hangs over your head. Fly! For Margaret's sake and for all who cherish you, do not waste another moment!"

It was a thunderclap that left two pale faces staring in stunned silence at the harrowing messenger. Then, breaking from his rehearsed message, Giles added words of his own. "Every constable was at our dwelling searching for you, and so was Dirk Brower. Kate is prudent, Gerard. Listen to her counsel and run!"

"Oh, yes, Gerard!" Margaret shrieked wildly. "Flee immediately! Alas, those parchments! My instincts warned me; why did I permit you to take them?"

"Margaret, they are only a distraction," Giles insisted. "Regardless, that old villain shall never see them again. I will not leave until I have concealed his treasure where he can never find it."

After offering Giles a fervent thanks, Gerard bid him farewell, instructing him to return and tell Kate that he was gone. "For I shall be gone before you reach home," he declared. He then summoned Martin, explaining the crisis and begging him to patrol the road toward Tergou. "If you spot Dirk Brower or any of his men," Gerard instructed, "shoot an arrow into the oak tree in our garden. Upon seeing it, I will dash into the nearby forest and meet you at the Weird Hunter's Spring. There, you will guide me through the woods."

With this contingency arranged, Gerard drew a breath he scarcely knew he had been holding. Accompanied by Margaret, he stood beneath the oak tree, watching it tremble anxiously, fearing every rustle might signal an incoming shaft. Meanwhile, Gerard began digging a deep pit to bury the stolen documents. One by one, he cast them into the earth. Most were charters and records of the burgh, but one document appeared to be a private deed between Floris Brandt, father of Peter, and Ghysbrecht.

"Why, this belongs just as much to him as to you," Gerard observed. "Let me read it."

"Oh, not now, Gerard, not now!" Margaret implored. "Every second you delay fills me with dread. Look! Large drops of rain begin to fall, and the clouds darken ominously."

Yielding to her plea, Gerard slipped the deed into his breast pocket, covered the remaining parchments with soil, and trampled the earth down firmly. Just then, a bolt of lightning split the sky, followed by a roll of distant thunder, and the heavens opened in a deluge. Margaret and Gerard scrambled into the house, swiftly joined by Martin.

"The road is clear," Martin reported, "but a violent storm approaches."

His prediction proved accurate. Thunder grew louder, crashing overhead like a giant's hammer, while flashes of lightning strobed rapidly, resembling the lashes of a whip. Rain poured in torrents. Margaret buried her face, refusing to witness the terrifying display. Seizing the moment, Gerard raised the rough shutter and lit a candle. The lovers conferred quietly, and Gerard thanked the tempest for granting them a few precious hours together. The sun vanished unnoticed, swallowed by the gloom. Thunder continued its relentless pealing, lightning danced across the darkness, and rain washed the world clean.

Supper was laid out, yet neither Gerard nor Margaret could bring themselves to eat; the knowledge that this might be their final meal together choked them. As the storm subsided slightly, Peter retired to bed. However, Gerard was to depart at first light, and neither lover could afford to squander an hour in slumber. Martin lingered briefly, carefully restringing his bow, a task he performed with meticulous care. Beside him, the lovers whispered their griefs and their undying love.

Suddenly, the old man raised a hand, signaling for absolute silence. They stilled themselves and listened, hearing nothing but the wind. Then, abruptly, a footstep cracked softly upon the autumn leaves scattered across the garden behind the cottage. To those without fear, such a sound would be insignificant; to those hunted, it was a death knell. It was a foot attempting to remain unheard. Martin nocked an arrow and snuffed out the candle.

In that same instant, to their sheer horror, they heard additional footsteps approaching the front door—not quite as silent as the first, but moving with deliberate stealth—followed by a deadly pause. Their blood turned to ice.

"Oh, Kate, oh, Kate! You said to fly instantly!" Margaret wailed, wringing her hands in agony, terror, and wild remorse for having detained Gerard.

"Hush, girl!" Martin commanded in a harsh whisper.

A heavy knock resonated against the door, striking not just the timber, but the very hearts within.
