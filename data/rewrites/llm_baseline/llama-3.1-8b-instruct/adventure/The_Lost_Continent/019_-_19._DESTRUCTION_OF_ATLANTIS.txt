As I stood at the precipice of the impending battle, a frail, elderly priest approached me, his eyes clouded with age and wisdom. His touch on my shoulder was like a whispered warning, a gentle reminder that even amidst the chaos, there were those who still held sway.

"What news?" I asked curtly, my patience wearing thin, yet my mind racing with the prospect of the fight ahead.

"The Three have sent word," he replied, his voice barely above a whisper. "News of a threat, one that requires your attention."

I snorted in derision, my thoughts consumed by the thrill of combat. "Tell them I'm here, brother, ready to face whatever fate has in store for me. When this battle is done, I'll join the stars, and the gods will finally have a warrior worthy of their presence."

The old man's expression turned wistful, a hint of envy flickering across his face. He, too, had once known the rush of battle, the high of victory and the agony of defeat. "I've been instructed to convey a message from the Three," he continued, his words measured. "One that you won't take kindly to, I fear."

My anger flared, but I forced myself to listen, intrigued despite myself. "What is it?" I demanded, my hand on the hilt of my sword.

The old man produced a small, delicate jewel, its facets glinting in the fading light. "This was taken from Nais," he said, his voice heavy with significance. "The Three have a use for you, one that concerns her as well. They require you to refrain from seeking your death on the battlefield."

For a moment, I wavered, torn between the promise of the fight and my loyalty to the Three. The thought of Nais, of her beauty and her music, stayed my hand. I knew that I could never abandon her, not even for the sake of glory.

With a sense of reluctance, I nodded my acceptance, and the old man's face softened in understanding. "Go back and tell the Three that I've received their message," I said, my voice heavy with resignation.

As I turned to leave, the knot of priests defending the last gate caught my eye. They stood resolute, their faces set against the coming storm. I envied them their bravery, their willingness to sacrifice themselves for the greater good. With a heavy heart, I bid them farewell, knowing that I would soon join them
