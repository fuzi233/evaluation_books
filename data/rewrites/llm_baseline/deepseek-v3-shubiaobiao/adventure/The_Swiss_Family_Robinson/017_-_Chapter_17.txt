My wife and Franz were overjoyed at our safe return, though their relief was tinged with concern at Fritz’s absence. As we recounted our battle with the lions and Jack’s narrow escape, my wife grew pale, her hands trembling. Franz, his eyes brimming with tears, asked if we had brought back Juno’s remains. The next day, we buried her near the house, and Ernest, ever the scholar, carved an epitaph upon a stone:

The wild boar’s meat and truffles were handed to my wife, who promised savory feasts—though I had to deny her the head, as it was destined for Ernest’s museum. The lions’ hides, along with the boar’s trophy, were taken to Whale Island for tanning.

Five days passed with no sign of Fritz. Anxiety gnawed at me, and I resolved to set sail in search of him. To my surprise, my wife agreed to join us, and soon we were aboard the pinnace, skimming toward Cape Minster.

The sea was calm, the breeze steady—until a dark shape loomed beneath the waves. I wrenched the helm, bracing for impact—only for the "rock" to vanish. Before we could recover, Jack shouted, *"Another! To starboard!"* The mass surged upward, a jet of water erupting from its blowhole.

The beast, enraged by our near-collision, thrashed toward us. Jack and I fired in unison. The shots struck true; the whale retreated, only to reappear in the shallows, staining the water crimson. A second volley silenced its struggles forever.

Just as we cheered, Franz gasped. *"A canoe!"* A dark figure—a savage, we thought—paddled toward us in what looked like Fritz’s kayak. Terror seized me—had Fritz been captured?

Ernest, ever composed, raised the speaking trumpet. *"Fritz, ahoy!"*

The figure hesitated—then waved. In moments, Fritz was aboard, his face smeared with charcoal, grinning at our stunned faces. *"I thought you were pirates!"* he laughed, explaining his disguise. After sharing our whale tale, he offered to guide us to an island with *"something worth seeing."*

Now certain of his secret, I revealed Fritz’s mission to my wife. Her hands flew to her mouth. *"You mean—another person? A woman?"*

The boys, baffled by our hushed excitement, followed Fritz ashore. Through the trees, we spotted a hut, smoke curling from its fire. Fritz vanished inside—then emerged with a slender figure in naval attire.

*"Meet Edward Montrose,"* he announced.

We embraced the "young officer" warmly—though my wife and I alone knew the truth. The boys, oblivious, were enchanted by their lively new companion, who regaled them with tales over supper. Only when Fritz began his story—of the albatross, the tiger, and the castaway’s signal fire—did the truth dawn.

*"Miss Montrose?!"* Jack exploded.

Laughter erupted as Fritz confessed. The boys cheered, toasting their new sister with mead. Then, with the fire crackling, Fritz resumed his tale—of Jenny Montrose, the colonel’s daughter, shipwrecked and surviving alone for years with nothing but wit and a trained cormorant for company.

As the night deepened, we marveled at the twist of fate that had brought us together—and the new chapter beginning for us all.
