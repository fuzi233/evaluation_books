The effect of the shot was instantaneous and bewildering, leaving the marksman himself momentarily stupefied. Before he could fully recover, he called upon his comrades to press the attack, sprinting upward along the slope. Yet, scarcely halfway up, the silhouette of Martin Wittenhaagen emerged against the sky, drawing a heavy, bent bow. Seeing this, Eric Wouverman, whose prowess with the crossbow was legendary and who still bore the memory of the fallen hound, instantly dove behind a thick trunk, shrinking into the shadows as best he could.

Peering anxiously around the rough bark, Eric saw the tip of an arrow still leveled directly at his hiding place, gleaming in the sun. Paralyzed by fear, he dared not shift even an inch. Minutes passed in tense silence before his allies arrived in full force. With a derisive chuckle, Martin vanished, soon heard riding away on his mule, leaving the hunters baffled.

A surge of men rushed forward to the high ground, which overlooked a narrow, seemingly endless glade. From afar, they spotted Gerard and Margaret fleeing at a frantic pace, appearing no larger than gnats. Hot on their heels, Martin spurred his mount, racing *ventre à terre*. The pursuers found themselves both outsmarted and outpaced. To understand Martin's strategy, one must look closer at the circumstances. Though coincidences often reveal cause, they can sometimes be deceptive illusions. Much like how a hare might tumble over a thicket precisely when a gunshot rings out, raising hopes only to dash them by escaping unharmed, so too did fate intervene here. At the very moment the harmless bolt whistled overhead, the mule stepped into a rabbit hole, stumbled, and threw both rider and passenger. Gerard managed to catch Margaret, but her weight and momentum dragged him down with her. In an instant, the stage was littered with dramatic figures.

The docile beast rose immediately, standing trembling amidst the dust. Martin was next to stir, scanning the scene until he realized only a single pursuer remained. Seizing the opportunity, he urged the young lovers to flee on foot, checking his own enemy as previously recorded. He then galloped after them once more. After a grueling chase, when he finally caught up, he swiftly hoisted Gerard and Margaret onto the mule. Running alongside them until his lungs burned, he then swapped places, taking the reins while the others rested. By rotating duties, he ensured a runner was always fresh. Long before their exhausted footsteps slowed, every trace of them was hopelessly lost to Dierich and his men, who returned crestfallen to attend to their leader and their winged bloodhound.
