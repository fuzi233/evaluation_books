Though she had initially recoiled at his arrival, Elinor couldn't deny the pang of regret that now accompanied his departure. The memory of his captivating smile, his affable nature, and the ardent love he professed for Marianne – all these conspired to create a sense of commiseration within her, a feeling that only intensified as she considered the isolation and hardships that lay ahead for him.

For a time, Elinor remained suspended in this state of emotional turmoil, her mind racing with thoughts of Willoughby's future, Marianne's safety, and their mother's impending arrival. It was as if the very fabric of her being had been rent asunder, leaving her vulnerable to the whims of fate. Yet, even as the weight of her worries threatened to consume her, she felt an inexplicable surge of vitality, a resilience that seemed to stem from the unyielding bond she shared with her sister.

As she eventually descended to the hallway, Elinor's senses were greeted by the soft glow of candles and the faint scent of woodsmoke wafting from the kitchen. The anticipation of their mother's return was palpable, and Elinor hastened to answer the door, her heart swelling with a mix of trepidation and hope. When Mrs. Dashwood finally stepped into the house, her eyes scanning the room with an almost frantic intensity, Elinor rushed forward to catch her as she stumbled, her mother's fragile form swaying precariously in her arms.

In that moment, all thoughts of Willoughby's influence and Marianne's well-being were subsumed by the overwhelming joy that radiated from their reunion. As they collapsed onto the sofa, embracing each other with a ferocity that bordered on desperation, Elinor felt a sense of peace settle over her, a sense that she had been waiting for what felt like an eternity to experience.
