Night passed without disturbance, leaving the weary bird refreshed by dawn. At ten o'clock, Mr. Crisparkle burst into the room, arriving from Cloisterham as if propelled by a single, urgent leap from the river. Beside him stood Mr. Grewgious.

"Miss Twinkleton was in a state of profound unease, Miss Rosa," Mr. Crisparkle explained, addressing the young woman. "She received your note and, overwhelmed by wonder, urged me to act immediately. To calm her spirit, I seized the first train this morning. I confess, at the time, I wished you to join me, but reflection suggests it was far better that you remained with your guardian."

"I did consider you," Rosa replied gently. "But Minor Canon Corner was so near..."

"Naturally so," Mr. Crisparkle acknowledged.

"He has heard everything you shared last night," Mr. Grewgious interjected smoothly. "Though I intended to write to him instantly, his arrival proved most timely. Indeed, it was particularly kind of him to return so soon after departing."

Rosa turned to them both, her brow furrowed. "Have you decided what course to take regarding Helena and her brother?"

"Why, truly," Mr. Crisparkle stammered, "I am at a loss. Even Mr. Grewgious, whose intellect stretches far ahead of mine and who has spent a full night in contemplation before me, remains undecided. If he is baffled, what hope do I possess?"

Just then, the servant known as The Unlimited peered around the doorframe, having first knocked and received permission to enter. She announced that a gentleman sought audience with Mr. Crisparkle, though she offered humble apologies if such a person was not present.

"The gentleman is here," Mr. Crisparkle confirmed, "but he is currently engaged."

"Is he a man of dark complexion?" Rosa asked, stepping back slightly toward Mr. Grewgious.

"No, Miss. He appears to be of a brown hue."

"And not of black hair?" Rosa ventured, gathering courage.

"Absolutely certain, Miss. Brown hair and blue eyes."

Mr. Grewgious leaned forward, his expression cautious yet encouraging. "Pray, let us receive him, Reverend Sir. In times of difficulty, one never knows whence salvation may emerge. My business principle is simple: never shut a door, however unlikely, but observe every path that offers a glimpse of light. I could recount a relevant anecdote, but perhaps it is premature."

"If Miss Rosa consents," Mr. Crisparkle said, "then the gentleman may enter."

The stranger entered, offering a frank yet modest apology for interrupting what he assumed was private conversation. Turning to Mr. Crisparkle, he smiled enigmatically and posed a question that hung heavily in the air: "Who am I?"

"You are the gentleman I observed smoking beneath the trees at Staple Inn moments ago."

"Precisely. There I beheld you. But who else might I be?"

Mr. Crisparkle fixed his gaze upon a handsome, sun-browned face. For a fleeting second, the shadow of a lost childhood boy materialized in the room, vague and indistinct. The visitor noticed the flicker of memory in the Minor Canon's countenance and smiled again. "Now, what shall we consume for breakfast? You appear to lack jam."

"Wait!" cried Mr. Crisparkle, holding up his hand. "One brief moment! Tartar!"

Two men embraced with a warmth that bordered on the miraculous for Englishmen. They laid hands upon each other's shoulders, gazing deeply into one another's eyes with a joy that transcended mere greeting.

"My old fag!" gasped Mr. Crisparkle.

"My old master!" responded Mr. Tartar.

"You saved me from drowning!" Mr. Crisparkle declared.

"After which you took to swimming, you know!" Tartar quipped.

"God bless my soul!"

"Amen!"

They resumed their vigorous handshake. "Imagine," Mr. Crisparkle exclaimed, his eyes shining with emotion, "Miss Rosa Bud and Mr. Grewgious, picture Mr. Tartar when he was but a small junior, diving for me, seizing me—a large, heavy senior—by the hair, and striking out for the shore like a water-giant!"

"I conjure the image of never allowing him to sink, for I was his fag!" Tartar added. "Yet the truth is, he was my finest protector and friend, doing me more good than all my tutors combined. An irrational impulse seized me to retrieve him, or perish with him."

"Hem! Pray permit me," Mr. Grewgious stepped forward, extending his hand, "to pay my respects. I hold this honor in high regard. I trust you escaped the chill. Did the excessive intake of water inconvenience you? How have affairs progressed since then?"

It was evident that Mr. Grewgious spoke with feigned familiarity, though his intent was clearly one of deep, albeit awkward, appreciation.

"If Heaven," Rosa thought silently, "had bestowed such courage and skill upon my poor mother's aid! And look at him—he was so slight and young back then!"

"I seek no compliments, I assure you," Mr. Grewgious interrupted himself, pacing the room with jerky, unpredictable strides that made everyone stare, unsure if he choked or suffered a cramp. "I *believe* I recall Mr. Tartar residing in the upper suite of the house adjacent to the corner lot?"

"Yes, sir," Tartar confirmed. "You are correct thus far."

"I am correct thus far," Grewgious repeated, ticking the point off mentally with his thumb against his palm. "Do you happen to know the name of your neighbor in the upper suite across the partition wall?" He approached closely, ensuring not to miss a feature of Tartar's face due to his short stature.

"Landless."

"Ticked off," Grewgious muttered, resuming his erratic trotting. "Any personal acquaintance, sir?"

"Slight, yet existent."

"Ticked off," Grewgious noted, circling back. "Of what nature?"

"I suspected him to be a youth in straitened circumstances and requested permission to share my flowers, merely a day or two past—that is, to extend my floral domain to his windows."

"Will you kindly take seats?" Mr. Grewgious commanded. "For I *do* have an idea!"

They obeyed; Tartar complied all the more willingly given his disorientation. Seated centrally, hands resting on his knees, Mr. Grewgious delivered his theory with the cadence of a memorized sermon.

"I remain undecided whether it is prudent to maintain open communication, under current circumstances, between the fair member of this company and either Mr. Neville or Miss Helena. I have cause to believe a local associate of ours—one for whom I reserve a passing yet fervent curse, with the blessing of my reverend companion—intrudes constantly, darting to and fro. When not acting personally, he likely employs an informant lurking nearby, perhaps a watchman, porter, or similar fixture of Staple life. Conversely, Miss Rosa naturally desires to behold her friend Miss Helena, and it seems vital that Miss Helena, if not her brother as well, learns privately from Miss Rosa's lips what has transpired and what threats loom. Am I aligned with the general consensus?"

"I wholly concur," Mr. Crisparkle noted, who had listened with intense focus.

"As I am certain I ought," Tartar added with a smile, "were I to comprehend these words."

"Fair and soft, sir," Grewgious cautioned gently. "We shall place our full confidence in you forthwith, provided you grant us leave. Now, should our local friend harbor an informant on the premises, it becomes reasonably clear that said agent can only be tasked with monitoring the chambers occupied by Mr. Neville. Reporting to our local friend, who moves freely about, this agent would deduce the identities of the parties based on prior knowledge. No individual can surveil all of Staple, nor concern themselves with visitors to other suites of chambers: unless, indeed, it is mine."

"I begin to discern your aim," Mr. Crisparkle admitted, "and I highly endorse your prudence."

"It needs repetition that I know not the 'why' or 'wherefore'," Tartar stated calmly. "Yet I grasp your objective. Therefore, let me declare openly that my chambers stand freely at your disposal."

"There!" Mr. Grewgious cried, smoothing his hair in triumph. "Now the vision is complete. Do you perceive it, my dear?"

"I believe I do," Rosa murmured, a flush rising as Tartar glanced quickly in her direction.

"You see," Mr. Grewgious continued, outlining the plan, "you proceed to Staple accompanied by Mr. Crisparkle and Mr. Tartar. I shall move in and out, as per my custom, alone. You ascend with these gentlemen to Mr. Tartar's quarters; you inspect his flower-garden; you await Miss Helena's appearance there, or signal her proximity; and you communicate with her freely, undetected by any spy."

"I am dreadfully apprehensive," Rosa hesitated.

"Frightened?" Grewgious asked gently.
