Every now and then, I caught a glimpse of a gaunt circle of stones on a distant hilltop, their presence a reminder of the strange, unspoken power that lurked beneath the surface of this forsaken place. The villagers seemed to sense my unease, their hushed conversations and furtive glances betraying a deep-seated fear that had taken hold of their collective psyche.

I had come to Dunwich with a singular purpose: to unravel the mystery of the Frye tragedy. The annihilation of the Elmer Frye house and family was a tale of unspeakable horror, one that had left the residents shaken to their core. As I rode through the desolate landscape, I noticed the lingering signs of the event: the tarry stickiness on the ground, the blasphemous tracks in the Frye yard, and the wounded Seth Bishop cattle that wandered aimlessly, their eyes vacant and unseeing.

My companions, Rice and Morgan, seemed equally unnerved by the eerie atmosphere. We rode in silence, each lost in our own thoughts as the shadows grew longer and darker. We knew that the State Police had arrived earlier that day, but their absence was a mystery that weighed heavily on us. It wasn't until we stumbled upon the empty car near the Frye ruins that we realized the full extent of the horror that had transpired.

The natives, too, seemed perplexed by the disappearance of the police. Old Sam Hutchins, a grizzled local, spoke of the eerie occurrences that had preceded the tragedy: the unexplained tracks, the strange smell, and the haunting cries of the whippoorwills that echoed through the night.
