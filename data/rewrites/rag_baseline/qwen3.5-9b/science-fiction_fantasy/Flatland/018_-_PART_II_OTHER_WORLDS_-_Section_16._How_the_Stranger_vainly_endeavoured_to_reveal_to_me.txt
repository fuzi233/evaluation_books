The final echo of my Wife's Peace-cry faded into the dry, crisp air, leaving a silence that felt heavier than usual. Seizing the moment, I stepped forward, intending to bid the Stranger sit and examine him more closely. Yet, as I approached, my legs froze, and my tongue turned to stone. Astonishment paralyzed me. He possessed none of the jagged edges that define existence in our world; instead, he shifted constantly, fluctuating in size and radiance with a fluidity impossible for any figure within my limited comprehension. A terrifying suspicion seized me: was this a burglar, a cutthroat, some monstrous Irregular Isosceles who had masqueraded as a Circle to gain entry, now poised to strike me with his lethal acute angle?

Inside the sitting-room, the absence of fog—and the season's remarkable clarity—rendered visual recognition treacherous, especially at such close range. Overcome by primal fear, I lunged forward, breaking protocol with a frantic, "You must permit me, Sir—" before pressing my palm against his form. My Wife had been right. There was not a trace of an angle, no roughness, no inequality. Never in my life had I encountered a more perfect Circle. He stood motionless while I circled him, starting from his eye and returning to it. He was circular throughout, a flawless geometric entity; doubt was impossible.

Then, the dialogue began, which I record here as faithfully as memory allows, omitting only my profuse apologies—for I was consumed by shame and humiliation that a Square, of all shapes, should dare touch a Circle. The Stranger started with palpable impatience regarding the length of my introduction.

**STRANGER:** Have you felt me sufficiently by now? Are you not yet acquainted with me?

**I:** Most illustrious Sir, pardon my clumsiness. It stems not from ignorance of social etiquette, but from a sudden, nervous surprise at this unexpected visit. I beseech you to keep this indiscretion secret, particularly from my Wife. But before we proceed, would your Lordship deign to satisfy the curiosity of one eager to know whence this visitor hails?

**STRANGER:** From Space, from Space, Sir: whence else?

**I:** Pardon me, my Lord, but are you not already *in* Space, along with your humble servant, even at this very moment?

**STRANGER:** Pooh! What do you know of Space? Define it.

**I:** Space, my Lord, is height and breadth indefinitely prolonged.

**STRANGER:** Precisely. You see, you do not even know what Space is. You believe it consists of only Two Dimensions; but I have come to announce a Third—to you, height, breadth, and length.

**I:** Your Lordship jests. We too speak of length and height, or breadth and thickness, using four terms to denote Two Dimensions.

**STRANGER:** But I mean not merely three names, but Three actual Dimensions.

**I:** Would your Lordship indicate or explain to me the direction of this Third Dimension, unknown to us?

**STRANGER:** I came from it. It lies up above and down below.

**I:** My Lord seems to imply it is Northward and Southward.

**STRANGER:** I mean nothing of the kind. I refer to a direction in which you cannot look, because you possess no eye on your side.

**I:** Pardon me, my Lord; a brief inspection will convince you that I have a perfect luminary at the junction of two of my sides.

**STRANGER:** Yes: but to perceive Space, you require an eye not on your Perimeter, but on your side—that is, on what you would likely call your interior, whereas we in Spaceland term it your side.

**I:** An eye in my interior! An eye in my stomach! Your Lordship jokes.

**STRANGER:** I am in no joking mood. I tell you I come from Space—or, since you refuse to grasp the concept, from the Land of Three Dimensions, from where I but recently surveyed your Plane, which you foolishly call Space. From that vantage point, I discerned everything you claim is SOLID (meaning "enclosed on four sides"): your houses, your churches, your very chests and safes, yes, even your insides and stomachs—all lying open and exposed to my gaze.

**I:** Such assertions are easily made, my Lord.

**STRANGER:** But not easily proven, you mean. Yet I intend to prove mine.

When I descended, I observed your four Sons, the Pentagons, each in his chamber, and your two Grandsons, the Hexagons. I watched your youngest Hexagon linger briefly with you before retiring to his room, leaving you and your Wife alone. I saw your Isosceles servants, three in number, dining in the kitchen, and the little Page in the scullery. Then I arrived here. How do you suppose I entered?

**I:** Through the roof, I assume.

**STRANGER:** Incorrect. Your roof, as you know, was recently repaired and contains no aperture through which even a Woman could pass. I tell you I come from Space. Are you not convinced by my account of your children and household?

**I:** Your Lordship must realize that such details concerning the property of your humble servant could be readily verified by anyone in the neighborhood possessing your Lordship's extensive resources for inquiry.

*(Turning inward)* **STRANGER:** What must I do? Wait; one final argument occurs to me. When you behold a Straight Line—take your Wife, for instance—how many Dimensions do you attribute to her?

**I:** Your Lordship treats me as if I were among the vulgar, those ignorant of Mathematics who suppose a Woman is truly a Straight Line, possessing only One Dimension. No, no, my Lord; we Squares are wiser. We know as well as you that a Woman, though colloquially termed a Straight Line, is scientifically a very thin Parallelogram, possessing Two Dimensions like the rest of us: length and breadth (or thickness).

**STRANGER:** But the very visibility of a Line implies it possesses yet another Dimension.

**I:** My Lord, I have just conceded that a Woman is both long and broad. We observe her length; we infer her breadth, which, though minute, is measurable.

**STRANGER:** You misunderstand. I mean that when you see a Woman, you ought—not only to infer her breadth—but to see her length, and to *SEE* what we call her HEIGHT, although that dimension is infinitesimal in your country. If a Line were mere length devoid of "height," it would cease to occupy Space and vanish. Must you not recognize this?

**I:** I must confess I utterly fail to comprehend your Lordship. When we in Flatland see a Line, we perceive length and BRIGHTNESS. If the brightness fades, the Line extinguishes and, as you say, ceases to occupy Space. Am I to suppose you grant the title of a Dimension to brightness, equating what we call "bright" with what you call "high"?

**STRANGER:** No, indeed. By "height" I mean a Dimension akin to your length; only, for you, "height" is imperceptible, being exceedingly small.

**I:** My Lord, your claim is easily tested. You assert I possess a Third Dimension, which you term "height." Since Dimension implies direction and measurement, simply measure my "height," or indicate the direction in which it extends, and I shall become your convert. Failing that, your Lordship's own logic excuses me.

**STRANGER:** *(Muttering)* I can do neither. How shall I convince him? Surely a plain statement of facts followed by ocular demonstration should suffice. —Now, Sir; listen.

You dwell on a Plane. What you style Flatland is the vast, level surface of a fluid, upon whose top you and your compatriots move, unable to rise above it or fall below it.

I am not a plane Figure, but a Solid. You call me a Circle; in truth, I am not a Circle, but an infinite stack of circles, ranging in size from a Point to a circle thirteen inches in diameter, layered one atop another. As I cut through your plane as I do now, I create a cross-section that you correctly identify as a Circle. Even a Sphere—which is my proper designation in my homeland—if it manifests itself to a Flatlander, must appear as a Circle.

Do you not recall—for I, who see all, detected last night the phantom vision of Lineland etched upon your mind—how, when you entered the realm of Lineland, you were forced to manifest not as a Square, but as a Line? That Linear Realm lacked the dimensions to contain your entirety, displaying only a slice or section of you. In precisely the same manner, your Two-Dimensional country is too narrow to represent me, a being of Three; it can only exhibit a slice or section of me, which is what you call a Circle.

Your eye dims slightly, signaling disbelief. Prepare, then, for irrefutable proof. You cannot see more than one of my sections, or Circles, simultaneously, for you lack the power to lift your eye out of the Flatland plane. However, you can witness that as I ascend in Space, my sections diminish. Watch: I shall rise. The effect upon your sight will be that my Circle shrinks until it dwindles to a point and finally vanishes.
