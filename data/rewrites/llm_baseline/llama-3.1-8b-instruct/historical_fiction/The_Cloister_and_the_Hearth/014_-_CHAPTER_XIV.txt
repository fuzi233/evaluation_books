The warm glow of the embers crackled in the hearth as Martin Wittenhaagen carefully stirred the porridge simmering in the cauldron, the aroma of sweet spices wafting through the cold winter air. His wife, Margaret, hovered nearby, busily kneading a batch of dough for the day's bread. The sudden thud against the back door made both of them freeze, the spoon hovering above the pot. Margaret's eyes locked onto Martin's, and for an instant, they shared a knowing glance.

As if on cue, the knocking grew louder, insistent. "Who dares disturb us at this hour?" Martin called out, his deep voice firm but wary. A gruff reply came from the other side of the door: "Open, Martin Wittenhaagen, or face the consequences!" The voice sent a shiver down Margaret's spine, and she quickly ushered Martin toward the stairs leading to her father's chambers.

"Dirk Brower, what brings you here?" Martin asked, his hand on the bolt, as the door creaked open to admit Dierich and his companions. The leader's stern expression was a stark contrast to the warmth of the kitchen, where the scent of baking bread and simmering stew filled the air.

"Where is Gerard Eliassoen?" Dierich demanded, his eyes scanning the room. Martin shrugged, his movements economical as he lit a candle and sat down by the fire. "Gerard was here, but now he's gone," he replied, his tone matter-of-fact. "His brother Giles came by, and Gerard departed soon after." Martin began to whittle a piece of wood, his hands moving deftly as he worked.

Dierich's countenance fell, his eyes narrowing as he processed the information. "We've been duped," he muttered. "We should have searched Sevenbergen first, not wasted our time searching Tergou for those cursed parchments." Martin's grin was wry, his eyes glinting with amusement.

The group decided to search the house, and Dierich led the way upstairs, followed by three of his companions. Martin remained below, his mind racing with possibilities. He knew Gerard's hiding spots, and the young man was likely to choose one of them – either Peter's room or Margaret's, both accessible from the ground.
