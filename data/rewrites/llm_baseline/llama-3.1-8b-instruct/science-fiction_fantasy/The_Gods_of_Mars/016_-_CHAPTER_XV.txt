As I regained consciousness, the warm glow of a torch illuminated the cold, damp corridor ahead, casting eerie shadows on the walls. The eyes that had stared back at me from the darkness were nowhere to be seen. I sat up, rubbing my forehead where it had struck the stone flagging as I fell. A faint bruise throbbed beneath my fingers, but otherwise, I seemed unscathed.

Without hesitation, I scrambled to my feet, my senses heightened as I scanned the corridor for any sign of danger. The torch, held by one of a party of four green warriors, cast flickering shadows on the walls as they rushed down the passageway towards me. I didn't hesitate; I slipped into the nearest intersecting corridor, careful not to stray too far from the main passageway this time. My heart pounded in my chest as I pressed myself against the wall, holding my breath as the party approached.

As they passed by, I exhaled a silent sigh of relief. It was the same group I had followed into the pits – Tars Tarkas and his three guards. I fell in behind them, my eyes fixed on their retreating backs as they made their way towards the cell where the great Thark was being held. My mind whirled with a plan, one that would allow Tars Tarkas and me to escape together, back to the safety of my little camp in the hills.

The torch had been left burning in a socket beside the cell door, casting a warm, golden light over the scene. As the two warriors disappeared around the bend in the corridor, I crept closer to the entrance, my longsword at the ready. My stomach twisted with unease at the thought of what I was about to do, but I knew it was necessary if we were to escape undetected.

Keeping close to the wall, I inched forward, my sword poised above my head, ready to strike. The sound of footsteps echoed through the corridor, growing louder with each passing moment. The jailer was approaching, unaware of the danger lurking in the shadows. I steeled myself for what was to come, my heart racing with anticipation.

In a flash, it was over. The jailer lay motionless on the ground, and Tars Tarkas, now clad in the metal of a Warhoon chief, strode confidently down the corridor, the Warhoon's torch casting eerie shadows on the walls. I followed closely behind, my footsteps quiet on the stone
