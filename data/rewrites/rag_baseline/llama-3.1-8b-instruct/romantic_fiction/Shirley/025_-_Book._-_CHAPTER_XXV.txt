The West Wind Blows.

In those fleeting moments when the tempests of life raged within me, I couldn't help but recall the countless nights spent beside my Master, pleading for mercy with a voiceless cry that pierced the darkness. The fervent supplications of my own soul still lingered, like the scent of damp earth and smoke that clung to the crumbling walls of our island refuge. I implored the heavens to spare my beloved, to heal the life force that ebbed and flowed within me like the tides. And yet, even as I cried out for deliverance, I knew that the divine wind could be as unforgiving as the rocky shores that encircled our prison.

As I stood there, suspended between the abyss and the void, a gentle breeze rustled the leaves of the withered trees, a soft whisper that seemed to carry the promise of renewal. But I knew better than to trust in such ephemeral respite. For in the stillness that followed, I heard the sound of footsteps, heavy and deliberate, as if the very earth itself was shifting beneath my feet. My Master's presence loomed large, a specter of a man who had lived a lifetime of hardship and toil, his features etched with the lines of a thousand midnights.

I retreated into the shadows, my heart pounding in time with the beat of the wind. But even as I fled, I felt the weight of his gaze upon me, a cold and calculating assessment that reduced me to nothing more than a fragile reed in the tempest. And yet, in that moment, I knew I was not alone. For in the distance, a gentle voice, a melody of reassurance, wafted on the breeze, a lullaby that spoke of comfort and solace.

It was the voice of Mrs. Pryor, a gentle soul who dwelled in the nearby village of Briarfield. Her words, a soothing balm to the parched earth, carried on the wind, a promise of hope in a world torn asunder by the fierce gusts of fate. And as I listened, entranced, I felt the first stirrings of a new dawn, a morning that would bring with it the sweet promise of renewal.
