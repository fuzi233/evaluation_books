As we traversed the majestic thoroughfare, which unwound steadily to the northwest, Infadoos and Scragga walked alongside us, their footsteps echoing in unison with our own. However, their entourage marched ahead, a procession of faces set in determined lines, their eyes fixed on some unseen horizon. I asked Infadoos, "Who laid the foundation for this remarkable road?"

He paused, his eyes drifting toward the snow-capped peaks that encircled the land like a sentinel, before responding, "It was crafted, my lord, in ages past, its origins lost to the sands of time, even to the wise woman Gagool, who has witnessed generations rise and fall. We, the Kukuana people, do not possess the knowledge to replicate such feats of engineering. Yet, the king ensures that no blade of grass dares to sprout upon its surface."

As we journeyed on, I turned my attention to the intricate carvings etched into the walls of the caves through which we had passed. "What stories do these hieroglyphics tell?" I inquired, my fingers tracing the curves of an ancient symbol.

Infadoos' gaze followed mine, his expression a mixture of reverence and mystery. "The hands that fashioned the road also crafted these writings, my lord. Their secrets remain locked within the hearts of the ancients."

I pondered the weight of his words, the echoes of history resonating through the stillness of the landscape. "When did the Kukuana people first set foot in this land?" I asked, my voice barely above a whisper.

Infadoos' eyes seemed to cloud, as if the passage of time itself was conjuring images from the depths of his soul. "Our ancestors arrived like a tempest, ten thousand thousand moons ago, from the distant lands beyond," he replied, his hand sweeping toward the north, where the snow-capped peaks shimmered like shards of splintered moonlight. "The high mountains that surround our land forced them to pause their journey, and they settled here, their numbers growing like the shifting sands of the desert. Today, our people are as boundless as the ocean, and when Twala, our king, summons his warriors, their plumes stretch across the plain, a sea of color that stretches beyond mortal sight."
