The winter that followed was no less unsettling than Wilbur Whateley’s first venture beyond Dunwich. Letters to libraries—Harvard’s Widener, the Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris, the British Museum, the University of Buenos Aires—had all been met with refusal. The book he sought was too dangerous, too forbidden. Desperate, he turned to the last option: Miskatonic University in Arkham, where a single copy of the accursed *Necronomicon* lay chained in the depths of its library.

Wilbur arrived in Arkham like a nightmare given flesh. Towering nearly eight feet tall, his frame was gaunt yet powerful, his face a grotesque fusion of man and something far older. His beard was unkempt, his clothes reeked of damp earth and something fouler. In one hand, he clutched a cheap valise from Osborn’s store; inside, wrapped in stained cloth, was his grandfather’s incomplete English translation of the same dread text.

The city did not stir him. His eyes, dark and gleaming with alien intelligence, fixed only on the university gates. The library’s watchdog, a massive brute with teeth like splintered bone, howled at his approach, straining its chain as if it sensed the wrongness in him. Wilbur ignored it.

Inside, he was met by Dr. Henry Armitage—librarian, scholar, and one of the few men alive who knew the true horror lurking in Dunwich’s shadowed hills. Wilbur wasted no time. He needed the Latin edition, the *Necronomicon* in its most complete form, to cross-reference a passage missing from his own.

The name slithered through his mind like a serpent. The key, the gate, the guardian. The thing that was all things.

Armitage, though wary, allowed him access. As Wilbur pored over the ancient pages, the librarian’s gaze flickered to the open text. The words there were not mere script but a warning, a prophecy carved in the language of the damned:

*"Nor is it to be thought that man is either the oldest or the last of earth’s masters… The Old Ones were, the Old Ones are, and the Old Ones shall be… Yog-Sothoth knows the gate. Yog-Sothoth is the gate. Yog-Sothoth is the key and guardian of the gate…"*

A chill crept into Armitage’s bones. The air thickened, tainted with a stench like opened graves. He knew then, with dreadful certainty, that the thing before him was no man.

Wilbur’s voice, when he spoke, was wrong—too deep, too resonant, as if something else shaped the sounds. *"I need this book,"* he rasped. *"There are rites I must perform—conditions that can’t be met here."*

Armitage refused.

Wilbur’s face twisted, something inhuman flickering behind his eyes. For an instant, the librarian glimpsed the abyss that yawned behind the creature’s guise. Then, with a forced chuckle, Wilbur relented. *"Fine. Maybe Harvard won’t be so stingy."*

He left, his loping stride unnatural, the watchdog’s frenzied barks chasing him into the night.

Armitage locked the *Necronomicon* away, but the stench lingered. The same stench from the Whateley farm. The same stench that clung to Wilbur.

The words haunted him. What had sired Wilbur? What walked the hills of Dunwich, waiting in the dark?

That summer, Armitage began his own research, digging into old records, consulting scholars of the occult. The more he uncovered, the deeper his dread grew.

Something was coming.

And Wilbur Whateley was only the beginning.
