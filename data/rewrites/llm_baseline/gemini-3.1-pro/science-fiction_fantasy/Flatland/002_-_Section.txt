Here, in the quiet damp of my pentagonal kitchen, I crush the luminescent, mineral-rich tubers harvested by the traditional agrarian elders of the southern strata, inhaling the sharp, earthy aroma of fermented fog-root. It is a slow, cyclical process of culinary alchemy, much like the sweeping, multi-generational saga of our people that I am now compelled to record. The rhythmic, wooden *thump-thump* of my pestle creates a soothing acoustic resonance, a localized harmony that stands in stark defiance of the grating, industrial clatter that now pollutes the sonic landscape of our modern geometric cities. 

I simmer this history over a low flame, treating our collective past as a complex, ancestral broth. To truly digest my tale, one must first consume Section 1, *Of the Nature of Flatland*, and savor the terroir detailed in Section 2, *Of the Climate and Houses in Flatland*. I must meticulously document Section 3, *Concerning the Inhabitants of Flatland*, and Section 4, *Concerning the Women*—voices with subtle, straight-lined timbres that have been tragically drowned out by the harsh noise of our era. 

The aroma of the steeping herbs deepens, reminding me of Section 5, *Of our Methods of Recognizing one another*. Once, our society relied upon a profound acoustic ecology, identifying the nuanced vocal signatures of our kin through the damp air. Yet, as cultural memory eroded, the elite forced upon us Section 6, *Of Recognition by Sight*. They replaced the intimate, auditory language of our ancestors with a sterile visual lexicon. 

This loss of our native sensory dialects tastes distinctly bitter, a flavor that permeates Section 7, *Concerning Irregular Figures*. It brings to mind Section 8, *Of the Ancient Practice of Painting*, an era when our world was spiced with vibrant hues, our ancestors blending pigments like master chefs balancing complex curries. But the dominant powers, fearing the untamed richness of this physical sensation, enacted Section 9, *Of the Universal Colour Bill*, resulting in the tragic and violent events of Section 10, *Of the Suppression of the Chromatic Sedition*. They wiped our plates clean, leaving a bland, tasteless visual existence mandated in Section 11, *Concerning our Priests*, and the hollow, echoing mandates found in Section 12, *Of the Doctrine of our Priests*, which prioritize utilitarian fuel over the passionate sustenance of the soul.

Yet, the true meat of my narrative—the mythic occurrence that forever disrupted my quiet, domestic routine—lies in the untranslatable beyond of Part II: *Other Worlds*. 

I will recount Section 13, *How I had a Vision of Lineland*, a realm of deeply impoverished dialect. I will record Section 14, *How I vainly tried to explain the nature of Flatland* to its monarch, tasting the bitter frustration of linguistic death. The saga will then swell with Section 15, *Concerning a Stranger from Spaceland*. The Sphere brought a conceptual flavor so alien, so structurally complex, it shattered my understanding of existence. I will document Section 16, *How the Stranger vainly endeavoured to reveal to me in words the mysteries of Spaceland*, hindered by the subtle power dynamics embedded in my own flat, two-dimensional lexicon. It is a testament to the inherent untranslatability of subjective experience that Section 17 occurred: *How the Sphere, having in vain tried words, resorted to deeds*, forcing me to consume the truth directly through physical intervention.

The transcendent taste of that upward ascension remains on my palate as I recall Section 18, *How I came to Spaceland, and what I saw there*, and Section 19, *How, though the Sphere shewed me other mysteries of Spaceland, I still desired more; and what came of it*. I will recount Section 20, *How the Sphere encouraged me in a Vision*. 

Upon returning to my hearth, the inability to share this new spatial vocabulary haunted me. I will describe Section 21, *How I tried to teach the Theory of Three Dimensions to my Grandson, and with what success*, watching the rich, volumetric language of the heights die on his lips, inevitably yielding to the dominant flat tongue of his peers. Finally, I will relate Section 22, *How I then tried to diffuse the Theory of Three Dimensions by other means, and of the result*.

The fog-broth is ready. The sonic hum of the boiling pot settles into a steady, comforting rhythm against the oppressive silence of my confinement. I set down my ladle, pick up my pen, and turn my mind back to the foundational ingredients of Part I: *THIS WORLD*. 

I take a deep breath of the spiced vapor, whispering to myself the ancient, agrarian wisdom passed down through generations:

"Be patient, for the world is broad and wide."
