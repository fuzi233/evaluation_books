Hollow's Cottage

Morning sunlight crept through the grimy windows of the mill, casting an eerie glow on the dusty floorboards. Moore's optimism still lingered, buoyed by the promise of a new day. He and Joe Scott had spent the previous night in the cramped recesses of the counting-house, their makeshift beds a testament to the harsh realities of their existence. As the master began his morning routine, his voice echoed through the deserted halls, a melodic French song that seemed out of place among the industrial din.

"Rise and shine, Joe," the master called out, his words laced with a hint of irony. "Not a single coin has escaped my notice, mon garçon."

Joe emerged from the shadows, his eyes squinting against the bright light. "Not a stiver, maister," he replied, rubbing the sleep from his eyes.

The master's enthusiasm was infectious, and he beckoned Joe to join him on a tour of the mill. As they walked, he outlined his ambitious plans for the future, determined to bring the machinery online despite the setbacks. "You know Bruce, the Scottish engineer?" he asked, his eyes sparkling with excitement.

"Aye, and the spider, too," Joe replied, his dialect thick with northern accents. "I've read the history of Scotland, and I reckon I know as much as you do. You're planning to persevere, aren't you?"

"I am," the master said, his jaw set in determination.

As they walked, Joe inquired about the master's background, his curiosity piqued by the man's mysterious past. "Is there many of your kind in your homeland?" he asked, folding up his makeshift bed and stowing it away.

The master's expression turned wry. "My homeland? Which is my homeland?" he asked, a hint of frustration creeping into his voice.

"Holland, then?" Joe ventured, his brow furrowed in thought.

The master shook his head. "No, not Holland. You're confusing Antwerp with Amsterdam. I'm an Anversois, born and bred. My mother was from Anvers, though her family hailed from France. That's why I speak French, you see."

Joe's eyes widened in understanding. "But your father was from Yorkshire, which makes you a bit of a Yorkshireman, too. Anyone can see you've got a knack for making money and getting ahead."
