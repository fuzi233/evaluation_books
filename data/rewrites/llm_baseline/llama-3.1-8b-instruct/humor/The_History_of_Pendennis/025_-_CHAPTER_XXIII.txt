As the sleepy residents of Fairoaks drifted through their daily routines, a distant murmur began to stir within the imposing walls of Clavering Park. The venerable estate, nestled across the River Brawl, had long slumbered under the tenure of two successive generations of Claverings, its grandeur slowly fading like a watercolor painting left exposed to the elements. However, the arrival of a sensational announcement in the provincial journals sent ripples of excitement throughout the county, echoing in every hamlet, village, town hall, and manor house within a wide radius of Clavering Park.

In Clavering Market, where the local squire's carriage encountered the vicar's humble conveyance, the occupants paused to engage in lively conversation. At Cackleby Fair, as the sun shone brightly overhead, the news spread like wildfire among the gathered crowd. In Tinkleton, the tolling church bell signaled the start of Sunday worship, and the procession of villagers, resplendent in their white smocks and scarlet cloaks, carried the whispers of the news with them. Everywhere, the same refrain echoed: Clavering Park was to be reoccupied.

Five years prior, the county gazettes had chronicled the union of Francis Clavering, scion of the esteemed Sir Francis Clavering, Baronet of Clavering Park, with Jemima Augusta, daughter of Samuel Snell, Esq. of Calcutta, and widow of the late J. Amory, Esq. At the time, whispers circulated that Clavering, a man beset by financial woes, had secured a wealthy bride from India. Some of the county's more adventurous souls had caught glimpses of the newlyweds in Florence, where they resided at the British Legation. Clavering's father, a man of dubious repute, had long been known for his reckless behavior, and the marriage to a wealthy widow was seen as a calculated move to revive his fortunes.

Rumors of the Claverings' whereabouts followed them as they traveled through Italy, where they occupied the Poggi Palace in Florence, hosted lavish parties, and lived a life of comfort. Young Peregrine, a wanderer from Cackleby, chanced upon the couple at Schloss Schinkenstein on the Mummel See during his Grand Tour.
