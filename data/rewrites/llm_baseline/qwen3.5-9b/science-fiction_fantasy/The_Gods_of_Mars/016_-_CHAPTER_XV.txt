My consciousness returned in a flash, lasting perhaps only a heartbeat before fading entirely. When awareness surged back, the darkness surrounding me was pierced by a blinding radiance. The eyes of my captors were gone, replaced by a sudden, overwhelming light. I checked myself; save for a minor bruise on my forehead where I had struck the stone flagstones, I was unhurt. Instinct took over. I leaped to my feet, scanning the corridor for the source of the illumination.

A torch flared in the grip of one of four green warriors, casting long, dancing shadows as they advanced rapidly down the passage. They had not yet spotted me. Seizing the moment, I darted into the nearest intersecting corridor, pressing myself flat against the wall. Unlike my previous attempt to evade detection—which had cost me Tars Tarkas and his guard—I kept my distance measured and precise. As the party thundered past the mouth of the alcove where I hid, I exhaled a breath I didn't realize I'd been holding. I was safe. More importantly, the group was familiar: Tars Tarkas and his three loyal guards.

I trailed them silently until we reached the cell where the great Thark had previously been shackled. Two warriors remained outside to stand watch while the key-bearer entered with the captive to reapply his iron bonds. Once the prisoner was secured, the two sentries began a leisurely patrol along the spiral ramp leading upward, vanishing around a bend before I could lose track of them. The torch had been shoved into a sconce beside the door, bathing both the corridor and the cell in steady light.

Seeing the guards disappear, I moved toward the cell's entrance. My mind had already formed a grim plan. Though the thought sickened me, there was no other way for Tars Tarkas and myself to return safely to our camp in the hills. I pressed close to the wall, positioning myself just inside the threshold. With my longsword raised high, gripped in both hands, I prepared to strike the skull of the jailer the moment he stepped forward.

I hesitated, unwilling to dwell on the violence of the act, but the sound of approaching footsteps silenced my doubts. Within minutes, Tars Tarkas—clad in the armor of a Warhoon chief—hurried down the corridor toward the ramp, carrying the Thark's torch. Twelve paces behind him walked John Carter, Prince of Helium. Meanwhile, the two companions of the man now lying slumped against the cell door had begun ascending the ramp.

"Why so long, Tan Gama?" one of the men called out.

"I encountered difficulty with the lock," Tars Tarkas replied. "Now I find I have left my short-sword in the Thark's cell. You proceed; I shall return to retrieve it."

"As you wish, Tan Gama," the other man responded. "We await you above."

"Yes," Tars Tarkas acknowledged, feigning intent to retrace his steps. Instead, he waited until the pair vanished onto the upper floor. Only then did I join him. We snuffed out the torch and crept toward the spiral incline leading to the upper levels.

At the first floor, the hallway terminated abruptly, forcing us to cross a rear chamber teeming with green-skinned inhabitants before we could reach the inner courtyard. Our only option was to ascend to the second floor and traverse the long gallery I had used earlier. Moving cautiously, we climbed the stairs. Faint voices drifted from the room above, but the hall remained dark and empty as we reached the summit. We threaded the corridor and arrived at the balcony overlooking the courtyard unseen.

To our right lay the window into the room where Tan Gama and his warriors had departed earlier that evening. His companions had returned, and we overheard a fragment of their conversation.

"What delays Tan Gama?" one asked.

"He certainly cannot require this long merely to fetch his short-sword from the Thark's cell," another countered.

"His short-sword?" a woman queried.

"What do you mean?"

"Tan Gama abandoned his short-sword in the Thark's cell," the first speaker explained. "He left us at the ramp to retrieve it."

"Tan Gama wore no short-sword tonight," the woman stated. "It broke during today's battle with the Thark. He gave it to me for repair. Look," she said, drawing the blade from beneath her sleeping silks and furs.

The warriors sprang to their feet instantly.

"There is foul play afoot!" one cried.

"It is exactly what I suspected when Tan Gama left us," another agreed. "His voice sounded strange then."

"Haste to the pits!"

We listened no further. Slinging my harness into a single strap, I lowered Tars Tarkas to the courtyard below, dropping to his side immediately after. Since felling Tan Gama at the cell door, we had exchanged fewer than a dozen words. Yet, in the flickering torchlight, I had seen the utter bewilderment etched upon the great Thark's face.

"By this time," he had muttered, "I should have ceased wondering at anything John Carter accomplishes."

That was all he needed to say. He understood the depth of my loyalty, the risk I had taken to rescue him. This fierce green warrior, who had once charged me with leveled spear and murderous hate on the dead sea bottom beyond Korad, was now my dearest friend among the inhabitants of two worlds. Among all the beings I had encountered, none surpassed Tars Tarkas, Jeddak of the Tharks.

Reaching the courtyard, we paused in the shadows beneath the balcony to finalize our strategy.

"There are five of us now, Tars Tarkas," I said. "Thuvia, Xodar, Carthoris, and ourselves. We shall require five thoats."

"Carthoris!" he exclaimed. "Your son?"

"Yes. I found him imprisoned in Shador's dungeons on the Sea of Omean, in the land of the First Born."

"I know not these places, John Carter. Are they upon Barsoom?"

"Both above and below, my friend. Wait until we have escaped, and I shall recount the strangest tale any Barsoomian of the outer world has ever heard. Now, we must steal our thoats and flee northward before these fools discover our deception."

In safety, we reached the great gates at the far end of the courtyard, the only exit for our mounts to the avenue beyond. Handling five such magnificent, savage beasts was no small feat. By nature, thoats are as wild and ferocious as their masters, kept in check only by cruelty and brute force. As we approached, they sensed our alien scents, squealing in rage and circling wildly. Their massive necks arched, raising gaping maws high above our heads. These creatures, standing nearly ten feet at the shoulder, possess sleek, hairless hides of dark slate that fade to vivid yellow at their eight powerful, nailless feet, while their bellies burn pure white. A broad, flat tail, thicker at the tip than the root, completes the picture of this terrifying Martian war-steed. Guided solely by telepathy, they require no reins or bridles. Our objective was clear: find two willing to obey our silent commands.

Charging about us, we managed to break their unity, preventing a coordinated assault. The cacophony of their screams threatened to draw investigators, but patience paid off. I successfully positioned myself atop one great brute before it could react. Moments later, Tars Tarkas had caught and mounted another. Between us, we herded three or four more toward the great gates. Tars Tarkas rode ahead, throwing open the barriers, while I held the stragglers back. Together, we spurred our stolen mounts through the gates and fled toward the southern boundary of the city, leaving the doors wide open.

Our escape had been miraculous, and fortune continued to smile upon us. We passed the outskirts of the dead city and reached our camp without a whisper of pursuit. A low whistle—the prearranged signal—announced my arrival. We were greeted by the remaining members of our party with boundless enthusiasm. Little time was wasted on reciting our exploits. Tars Tarkas and Carthoris exchanged the formal, dignified greetings customary on Barsoom, though I sensed intuitively that the Thark adored my boy and that Carthoris returned the sentiment. Xodar and the green Jeddak were introduced with proper ceremony. Then Thuvia was lifted onto the least fractious thoat, Xodar and Carthoris mounted theirs, and we set out at a rapid pace toward the east.

At the city's far extremity, we circled north. Under the glorious dual moons, we sped silently across the ochre wastes of the dead sea, escaping the Warhoons and the First Born, unaware of what new perils awaited.

By noon the following day, we halted to rest our mounts and ourselves. The beasts were hobbled to graze on the ochre moss-like vegetation that served as both food and water. Thuvia volunteered to keep watch while the others slept for an hour.

It seemed I had barely closed my eyes when her hand touched my shoulder and her soft voice warned of fresh danger.

"Arise, O Prince," she whispered. "Something approaches from behind. It resembles a large body of pursuers."

She pointed toward the horizon from whence we had come. Rising, I confirmed her suspicion; a thin, dark line stretched across the far distance. I woke the others.
