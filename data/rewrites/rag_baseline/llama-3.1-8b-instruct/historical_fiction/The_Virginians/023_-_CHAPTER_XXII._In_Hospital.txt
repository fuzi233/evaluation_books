As the summer sun streamed through the hospital windows, casting a warm glow over the room, Warrington slowly regained consciousness. The pain in his shoulder throbbed in time with his heartbeat, a dull, rhythmic ache that ebbed and flowed like the tides. He lay still, trying to gather his thoughts, but they were as scattered as the threads of a torn tapestry. Fragments of memories danced before his eyes: a young woman with red cheeks and black hair, her smile as fleeting as a summer breeze; an elderly woman with a stern face and a voice that sent shivers down his spine; the sound of music, sweet and melancholy, echoing through the halls of his mind.

Mrs. Lambert's gentle voice broke the silence, her words a soothing balm to his parched throat. "What is it, my dear child?" she asked, her concern etched on her face like the lines on a well-worn book. Warrington tried to sit up, wincing as the movement jarred his wounded arm. "Nothing, madam," he lied, his voice barely above a whisper. "Just a twinge in my shoulder."

Mrs. Lambert's expression softened, and she reached out to smooth his hair, her touch as gentle as a summer zephyr. "We are old friends, Mr. Warrington," she said, her eyes sparkling with warmth. "My husband, Colonel Lambert, knew your father, and I and your mother were schoolgirls together at Kensington. You were no stranger to us when your aunt and cousin told us who you were."

Warrington's gaze drifted to the window, where a vase of fresh flowers sat on the sill, their petals a vibrant splash of color against the dull grey of the hospital room. "Are they here?" he asked, his voice a little stronger now. Mrs. Lambert's smile faltered, and she exchanged a glance with her husband. "They must have lain at Tunbridge Wells last night," she said. "They sent a horseman from Reigate yesterday for news of you."

Warrington's thoughts turned to his family, his mind racing with memories of his aunt and cousin. He remembered the sound of their laughter, the way they had teased him mercilessly when he was a child.
