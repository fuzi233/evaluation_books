The evening sky was a deep, foreboding bruise, the starlight and moonlight suffocated by a shroud of gray rain-clouds that would have been indistinguishable from the dull canvas of the day sky. Malone navigated the road with a practiced indifference to the world around him, the changes in nature slipping past him like the gentle lapping of waves against the shore. He could walk for miles on a spring day, the sun's warm caress on the hillsides and the delicate dance of earth and heaven lost on him, just as the rhythmic beat of the rain and the mournful sigh of the wind through the trees failed to stir him tonight. He didn't trouble himself to contrast this muffled, streaming vault with the clear, star-studded sky of a frosty night, nor did he wonder where the constellations had fled, leaving behind only the faint, eerie glow of the Stilbro' ironworks on the horizon.

As he walked, the causeway beneath his feet gave way to the muddy ruts of the road, the flags replaced by a soft, sucking morass that threatened to claim his boots. Still, he pressed on, his eyes fixed on the landmarks that guided him: the spire of Briarfield Church, the lights of Redhouse Inn, where the warmth and camaraderie of a fire and a glass of whisky-and-water beckoned like a siren's song. But Malone's resolve held firm, and he continued on his way, the rain seeping into his bones as he sought out the comforts of Hollow's Mill.

A shortcut across the fields promised to shave precious minutes off his journey, and Malone took it, leaping over hedgerows and walls with a practiced ease. The fields themselves were a sea of monotonous green, stretching out before him like an endless expanse of emerald. A single building broke the horizon, its gabled roof and chimney stacks rising like sentinels from the flat landscape. The windows were dark, the only sound the soft drip of water from the eaves and the mournful cry of the wind through the trees.

As the field descended into a vale, the sound of running water grew louder, a steady pulse that drew Malone closer to his destination. A single light flickered in the distance, a beacon guiding him through the darkness. He quickened his pace, his heart beating in time with the rhythm of the stream.
