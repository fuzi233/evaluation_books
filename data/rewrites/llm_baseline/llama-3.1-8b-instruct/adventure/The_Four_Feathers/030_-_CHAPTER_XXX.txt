The desert sun beat down relentlessly, its fierce heat tempered only by the cool breeze blowing in from the north. The camels, refreshed by the gentle gusts, trotted out at a swift pace, their soft padded feet barely making a sound on the sandy dunes.

"Let's move quickly," Trench urged, his voice low and urgent. "Idris may already be gathering men to pursue us."

Feversham nodded in agreement, though his eyes kept drifting back towards the fading light of Omdurman. He could no longer hear the distant beat of the drums, a small comfort, but one that gave him hope. As they rode deeper into the desert, the silence grew thicker, a palpable presence that wrapped itself around them like a shroud. No sound of galloping horses echoed through the stillness, no warning cry to alert them to the danger closing in behind.

Feversham's gaze scanned the darkness, half-expecting to catch a glimpse of a white turban bobbing up from the horizon. But Trench rode on, his face set, his eyes fixed straight ahead. His fear was no less real than Feversham's, but he refused to look back, dared not, lest he see the city's walls looming behind him, the fires burning brightly in the streets, and the men of the House of Stone racing to recapture their prisoners.

A sudden question from Feversham broke the spell, and Trench's response came back to him, detached, his mind elsewhere. "What is it?"

"I can no longer see the fires of Omdurman," Feversham replied, his voice barely above a whisper.

Trench's response was abstracted, his voice carrying on the wind. "The golden blot, very low down?" Feversham didn't press him for an explanation, nor could Trench have given one. The phrase had come to him unbidden, a fleeting image from a memory long buried. They rode on in silence, the only sound the soft padding of the camels' feet on the sand.

As they crested a dune, two figures emerged from the darkness ahead, their faces pale in the moonlight. Abou Fatma's voice called out softly, "Instanna!" The camels halted, their knees folding beneath them as they settled to the ground.
