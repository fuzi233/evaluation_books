As I strolled along the East India Dock Road, the scent of damp earth and saltwater filled my nostrils, transporting me back to a simpler time. The road, once a bustling thoroughfare, now lay empty and still, its pavement worn smooth by the footsteps of countless travelers. The dock walls loomed on one side, their stone façades reflecting the fading light of day like a canvas of grey and blue hues. On the other, the occasional small house stood like a sentinel amidst the market gardens, drying grounds, and rubbish heaps that seemed to stretch on forever.

My thoughts drifted back to a visit I had made to Plaistow just that afternoon, to see the kindly family dentist. His out-of-the-way location only added to the charm of the visit, and my mother's words echoed in my mind: "Have the half-crown in your hand, but if he won't take it, bring it home again." I remembered the velvet chair of state, where I'd sit and watch as he unrolled his case of instruments, extolling the virtues of each one with a twinkle in his eye.

But it was the tea that followed that I cherished most – the sweet, fragrant brew infused with the flavors of the garden fruits that his housekeeper transformed into jams and jellies. Her creations were a symphony of flavors and colors, a testament to the abundance of the garden. Our conversation often turned to my mother, and the tales of her youth that the dentist would regale me with. I recall the stories of her prankish, wilful nature, which my mother would later dismiss as mere fantasy, laughing and telling me to find more interesting topics to discuss.

Yet, despite her denials, I sensed a hint of truth behind the tales, and I found myself drawn to the idea of my mother as a free spirit, unencumbered by the responsibilities of adulthood. As the tea things were cleared away, the dentist would produce his trusty microscope, and I'd gaze through its lens into a world of wonder, where tiny creatures danced and swirled, their secrets revealed in the gentle glow of the instrument. In those moments, I saw him as a benevolent sorcerer, conjuring worlds from the tiny.
