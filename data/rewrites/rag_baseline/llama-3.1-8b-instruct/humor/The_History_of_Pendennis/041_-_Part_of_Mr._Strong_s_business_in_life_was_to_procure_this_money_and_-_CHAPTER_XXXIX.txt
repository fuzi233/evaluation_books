On the appointed day, Major Pendennis and his nephew, Arthur, arrived at the Clavering estate, accompanied by the enigmatic Captain Strong, whose reputation as a shrewd businessman and tactician preceded him. The Major's demeanor, however, betrayed a certain unease, stemming from his perception of Captain Strong as little more than the house-steward of the Clavering household, a classification that the Major deemed beneath his own stature.

As they entered the drawing room, they were greeted by Sir Francis and his wife, as well as Captain Strong, who was warmly welcomed by Arthur. The arrival of Mr. Welbore Welbore, a respected country gentleman and fellow parliamentarian, momentarily alleviated the tension, as the elder Pendennis felt at ease in the company of someone of his standing. The assembly was soon joined by other notable figures, including the Dowager Lady Rockminster, who had her own reasons for maintaining a close relationship with the Clavering family, and Lady Agnes Foker, accompanied by her son, Mr. Harry.

As the evening progressed, Miss Blanche Amory made her entrance, resplendent in a stunning white silk gown that accentuated her porcelain complexion. Her charm and affability immediately captivated the attention of the assembled company, particularly Mr. Foker, who was struck by her amiability and found himself surprisingly articulate in her presence. Despite being separated from her during their descent to the dining hall, Mr. Foker managed to secure a seat beside her at the table, where he proceeded to regale her with his thoughts on the current topics of interest, including his enthusiasm for the ballet and its legendary performer, Taglioni.
