The clock struck midnight, marking the final hour of the 1999th year before the Long Vacation began. Exhausted from hours of geometric contemplation, I lay down with a lingering puzzle unsolved in my mind. Before sleep claimed me, however, the fabric of reality tore open, revealing a realm so alien that my senses struggled to comprehend it.

Before me stretched an endless, straight corridor—a universe devoid of width, depth, or curvature. Within this line moved a chaotic throng of entities. Some were tiny, shimmering dots, darting back and forth with frantic energy; others were elongated segments, resembling thin strips of light, weaving through the dots with a mechanical regularity. As they moved, they emitted a cacophony of chirps and screeches, a dissonant symphony that erupted whenever they shifted position, falling into a suffocating silence the moment they halted.

Driven by curiosity, I approached one of the larger segments, expecting a greeting. Instead, I met a wall of silence. Repeated attempts to engage yielded nothing but empty air. Frustrated by what felt like a profound rudeness, I stepped directly into the path of her motion, forcing her to stop and blocking her view with my own form. I shouted, "Stranger, why does this entire existence consist of a single, unvarying line? What purpose serves this ceaseless, monotonous oscillation?"

"I am not a stranger," the segment replied, its voice resonating within the confines of the line itself. "I am the Sovereign of this realm, Lineland. And you... who dare trespass into my domain?"

Apologizing profusely for startling the monarch, I introduced myself as a traveler from beyond these bounds and begged for an account of his kingdom. Yet, explaining the nature of his world proved nearly impossible. His Majesty, convinced that familiarity bred knowledge, repeatedly dismissed my questions as playful ignorance. Through persistent inquiry, however, I slowly pieced together the terrifying truth of his existence.

To this monarch, the straight line *was* everything. It was his world, his space, and the totality of existence. He possessed no concept of anything outside it because, to him, the exterior simply did not exist—it was a void, a non-entity where nothing could possibly reside. Though I had spoken to him, the sounds reached him as a jarring vibration inside his stomach, indistinguishable from internal noise until I physically obstructed his path. Before that moment, he had never truly seen me; he merely registered the pressure of my presence against his boundary. To a Linelander, the world outside the line was not a dark space, but absolute nothingness.

Every inhabitant of Lineland, from the smallest dot to the grandest ruler, was bound to this single dimension. Their vision was restricted to a mere point, meaning they could see nothing but the tip of an object approaching them. A man, a woman, or a child all appeared as identical points to the eye. The only distinction between sexes or ages was the timbre of the voice. Furthermore, because everyone occupied the entirety of the narrow path, no one could ever pass another. They were locked into eternal adjacency; neighbors became lifelong companions, separated only by death, much like spouses in my own dimension.

The life here seemed to me a prison of infinite boredom, yet I was baffled by the monarch's apparent vitality. Puzzled about how such a restrictive existence could accommodate love and family, I finally pressed him on the subject of his household. "My wives and children," he declared cheerfully, "are thriving."

Confounded, for I had observed only male figures near the throne, I stammered, "Sire, how can you perceive or reach your families when dozens stand between you? Can proximity be irrelevant to marriage and procreation in Lineland?"

"My dear friend," the King laughed, "your question betrays a lack of understanding. If love depended on physical closeness, the universe would surely be barren. Proximity is unnecessary for the union of souls. Let me teach you, as if you were a newborn infant."

He explained that every male Linelander possessed two mouths—one emitting a deep bass tone, the other a high tenor—and similarly, females produced soprano and contralto notes. "Nature decrees that every man must wed two women," the King proclaimed. "A perfect harmony requires four voices united: the Bass and Tenor of the husband, matched by the Soprano and Contralto of the pair."

"But what if a man prefers one wife?" I asked.

"It is impossible!" the King cried, his voice rising in indignation. "As inconceivable as two plus one making five, or an eye seeing a straight line."

He then described the Great Weekly Chorus, a ritual mandated by natural law. For roughly a hundred beats, the inhabitants vibrated violently up and down the line. At the fiftieth beat, all movement ceased, and every soul released their purest, sweetest tone simultaneously. This was the sacred moment of creation. Despite distances stretching thousands of leagues, lovers recognized each other instantly through the resonance of their specific frequencies. Love transcended the barriers of the line, binding the triad—the man and his twin brides—in a vocal embrace that resulted in triple offspring.

"Always three?" I asked. "Does every wife bear twins?"

"Yes!" the King roared, his face flushing with righteous anger. "Would you deny the Alphabet of Nature? For every boy, two girls must be born to maintain the cosmic balance."

After a brief, heated silence, the monarch calmed and continued his tale. Not every bachelor found his destiny immediately during the Grand Chorus. Most required years of courtship. A suitor might align his voice with one potential bride but clash with the other, or fail to harmonize with either initially. In these cases, the weekly choruses served as a tuning mechanism. Each attempt revealed a slight dissonance, prompting the imperfect lover to adjust their pitch, inch by invisible inch, toward the ideal frequency. Over countless cycles, the trio gradually drifted closer, their voices weaving tighter together. Finally, on a destined day, the three distant souls would sing in perfect unison, locking into a permanent, vocal duality before they even realized the change, and Nature would rejoice in another addition to the lineage of Lineland.
