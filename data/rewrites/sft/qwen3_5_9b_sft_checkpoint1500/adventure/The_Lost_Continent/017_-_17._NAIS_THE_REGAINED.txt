From the ridge where we stood, overlooking the sacred mountain, the city sprawled beneath us like a sprawling map etched in stone and shadow. Below, the harbor and the vast estuary shimmered, while the distant hills smoked with the usual haze of industry. Dominating the center of the urban sprawl stood the Great Stone Circle of Our Lord the Sun, grim and stark against the twilight. Closer to us, rising majestically, was the Royal Pyramid, its golden facets drinking in the fading light of the sun. Before it, in the great plaza, sat the throne of granite—a mound no larger than a molehill from this height—beneath which my love had slept for nine long, agonizing years.

Old Zaemon followed my gaze, his expression heavy. "Ay," he sighed, "I know exactly where your heart lies. When Deucalion first arrived from Yucatan, he was a man of iron. But the King we have chosen—and he is the best among us—carries a fatal weakness."

"It can be forged into strength," I replied, my voice steady despite the turmoil within. "Give me Nais, living and warm, a worthy foe to fight for, and I shall surpass that cold viceroy you speak of a thousandfold."

"I have already pledged her to you," Old Zaemon said, his tone somber. "But the price will be high for your new kingdom. Perhaps we will also rid you of Phorenice and her brood, though I doubt it. She is too cunning. Once we start our game, she will likely deduce our intentions and slip away before harm reaches her. The High Gods, who send these trials to refine us, have granted her a dangerous knowledge: how to set these earth tremors in motion."

"I have seen her manipulate them," I admitted. "May I hear your plan?"

"The scheme will reveal itself in time. For now, I bid you rest. Tonight, you must descend into the city, retrieve your woman—the daughter I spoke of—as soon as she is freed, and bring her back here. You will need every ounce of a warrior's strength for this." He stepped back, studying me from head to toe. "They would never mistake you for the tidy, clean-chinned Deucalion anymore, my brother. Your appearance will serve as armor tonight. You look wild enough, hairy enough, naked enough, and filthy enough to pass as a savage newly landed from Europe. Be careful, however, that no refined citizen mistakes your form for servitude and tries to enslave you."

"I pity the fool in whose household he lands, if that happens."

Old Zaemon laughed, a rare sound, before his gravity returned. "Come to think of it, so do I. Well, go to bed, my King. Leave me to enter the Ark of Mysteries and prepare with another of the Three for what must be done. Handling these tremendous powers requires focus, and there is danger for both of us. If we do not meet again standing behind the stars to answer to the Gods, farewell, Deucalion."

I slept as a soldier sleeps, allowing no thought to disturb the depth of my rest. When the darkness fully enveloped the world, I roused and gathered those to tend the rope. The moon offered no light at this phase of the cycle; instead, the air was choked with a profound, suffocating blackness. Lowered silently, I vanished from sight.

Reaching the tumbled rocks at the cliff's base, I moved with extreme caution. Phorenice's soldiers squatted around their campfires, their posture suggesting foreboding of the coming disaster. I had no intention of stirring their nerves further. Creeping swiftly through the deepest shadows, I arrived at the end of the passage I had previously used to breach the city walls.

A lamp burned in place, guiding my path through the windings. The air felt more noxious than before, heavy with vapors, a testament to Zaemon's preparations to convulse the city. Yet, I faced a new obstacle: finding an exit. The masonry had shifted entirely. In former days, the Priest's Clan dictated architecture, ensuring no clashes with secret passages. Now, the priests held sway over nothing, and architects, often through sheer blunder, had sealed countless outlets.

Forced to navigate the network of tunnels wherever I could, rather than where I intended, I emerged at the far side of the city, nearly where the outer wall meets the harbor. I slipped out unseen, scrupulously closing all traces of my exit. Beneath my feet, the earth sprang like the deck of a ship in calm waters. Though no movement yet disturbed the sleeping populace, I felt the solid deadness of the ground vanish; any moment it might erupt into devastating waves.

*Gods!* Was I too late? Would she be exposed to the public gaze as the terrified swarmed into the open squares? I pictured their crude, cruel hands thrusting upon her helpless form, and my heart curdled at the thought.

Driven by a madness of urgency, I ran down the narrow, winding streets, fixated solely on reaching the square before the royal pyramid. With exquisite cruelty, I had buried her alive beneath the granite throne, and if my strength and speed served me, I would reclaim her with the same ferocity.

Few hindered my furious rush. Here and there, a wretch in the gutter cried, "A thief!" but if any pursued, I outran them. Soon, as the earth's swing grew keener and the stonework of the buildings grated and groaned, sending showers of dust into the air, people fled their homes in screams. Yet, none stopped the ragged, shaggy savage who ran past, flinging mud from his naked feet.

Thus, I reached the great square, arriving just in time. It was filling with a fleeing crowd, a chaotic sea of darkness, noise, and dust. Beneath us, the ground rippled like a troubled sea, the undulations growing terrifyingly intense. Houses crashed down unseen, adding to the tumult.

Yet, the great pyramid, built by ancient masters to withstand rude shocks, stood firm. Its stones were dovetailed with marvellous cleverness, clamped by ponderous tongues of metal. It was boasted that half its foundation could be dug away, and it would still stand four-square under heaven.

Flickering torches revealed its great stone doors wide open. Frightened inmates scurried out only to vanish into the gloom. But I cared little for the pyramid's fate, nor for Phorenice, whether trapped or escaped. I crouched by the granite throne in the center of the square, hearing its stones grate together like broken bones as it rocked on the earth-waves.

In that night of dust and darkness, seeing one's own hand was impossible. Yet, I think the Gods, in requital for the love that had ached so long within me, granted me special vision. Watching closely, I saw the great carved rock forming the capstone shift slightly, then again, then again. Each jerk corresponded to an earth-pulse, but the stone consistently moved to one side.

There was method in Zaemon's desperate work, a detail I had missed in my blind panic. I climbed the steps on the side where the capstone moved and clung there, aflame with expectation.

The earth-swings grew more violent, the din of falling houses and human screams drowning the night. Dust thickened the air until coughing became strangled, and the black clouds obscured the stars. Clinging to an angle of the granite throne, I could barely maintain my footing against the violence.

But the huge capstone, carved with the snake and the outstretched hand, still held my love locked in her living tomb. I bit the cold granite in frustration at the barrier between us. The crowds thronging the square were mad with terror, praying to forgotten gods, begging for aid. "Phorenice, Goddess, aid us now!" they cried, and when silence answered, they fell to reciting old confessions, turning to deities they had spurned for decades.

The crash of falling masonry grew louder. From the royal pyramid itself, sounds of grinding stones and cracking metal signaled that even its majestic strength was breaking strain. Zaeomon's threat echoed in my mind: *"You shall see... this royal pyramid torn tier from tier, scattered as feathers spread before a wind!"*

The earth surged heavier. The pavement gaped and upheaved, crushing feet under grinding blocks. The pyramid itself began to split, gaping, and topple. Roaring thunders joined the devil's orchestra, and vivid lightning splashes lit the flying dust-clouds.

Then, a shock. A flare of lightning revealed Phorenice safe in the square, standing not far from me. She stood on a great flagstone, swaying her supple body to the shocks. Her face was calm, her loveliness untouched by the years. She brushed away settling dust from her short, curling red hair. No fear marred her countenance, only weariness, contempt, and a tinge of amusement. More than the crumbling of her royal pyramid impressed Phorenice with the powers of her enemies.

*Gods!* How the sight of her cool indifference maddened me. I could have strangled her if she were within reach. But she stood swaying easily, a sailor on a ship's deck.
