The cold water stung my skin as I blinked it from my eyes. Dawn’s pale glow shimmered across the raven’s obsidian feathers where he perched on the rim of the great stone basin. Below him, I floated on my back, half-submerged in the fountain’s pool—the same one my father had built, its steel-bright pillar spearing skyward before bursting into foam.

*"You told me nothing,"* I spat, nettled by his calm.

The raven tilted his head. *"I told you to refuse any request from those you distrust."*

"An easy thing to forget in the moment," I muttered.

"And yet, you will not forget the consequences," he replied, extending a hand. I took it, and in an instant, I stood dripping on the lawn beside him.

"You’ll need dry clothes," he observed. "Where you come from, a little water means nothing. Here, it carries weight."

With that, he was a raven again, striding toward the open door of the house, his gait oddly regal.

"I haven’t much to change," I admitted, thinking of the robe I’d discarded.

The raven cast a glance over his wing. *"A luxury I lack. It’s been an age since I shed a feather."*

Inside, the house lay silent, untouched by stirring life. I found a dressing-gown in my room and made for the library. The librarian emerged from the shadows of the shelves as I entered, and I sank onto a couch. The raven—now a man again—settled into a chair beside me.

For a long moment, neither of us spoke.

"What does any of this mean?" I finally asked.

"A fine question," he mused. "No one knows *what* things are—only what they *mean*. And that depends on what you do with them."

"I’ve done nothing with any of it."

"Not yet. But you recognize that, which is more than most manage in a lifetime. At least you *wanted* to be of use."

"I did. For the children—the Little Ones."

"And yet you began all wrong."

"I didn’t know the right way."

"No. But that’s no excuse for not seeking it."

I exhaled. "Then tell me plainly. What should I have done?"

"Stayed. Acted where you stood. You left to find your purpose, only to circle back empty-handed."

"But how could I have helped them? They were already beyond me."

"Beyond your *knowledge*, perhaps. Not beyond your care. They weren’t growing—not truly. You saw that. They feared the very idea of it."

"And I was supposed to force growth upon them?"

"No. Only to clear the obstacles in their path."

"What obstacles?"

He leaned forward. "The lack of water, for one. They had none to weep with."

"I’d have spared them tears if I could."

"Of course you would. Like every well-meaning fool. But without weeping, your world would never have been worth saving."

I flinched. "I thought—perhaps water *was* what they needed. But I never considered digging a well."

"Not even when you heard the rivers beneath the earth?"

"It crossed my mind once. But the giants—"

"Ah, yes. The giants." His voice sharpened. "You let fear rule you. You bowed to brutes while the Little Ones looked to you as their protector. You showed them cowardice disguised as caution."

Heat rose in my chest. "I served the giants to shield the children!"

"Did you? Or did you delay their strength by coddling their fears? You could have taught them to stand. Given them water. Let them learn their own power. Instead, you let the giants keep their chokehold."

The truth of it settled like a stone in my gut.

"And now?" I asked quietly.

"Now," said the raven, "you carry the weight of silence. The chance is gone."

The words hung between us, heavier than any chains.
