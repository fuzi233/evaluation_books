I moved cautiously toward the North and West, wary of the Great Watcher that loomed in that quarter. With each step, I measured my pace, knowing the journey ahead was vast and uncertain. To endure, I resolved to keep order in my travels—eating and resting at set intervals, lest my strength fail when I needed it most.

Every sixth hour, I would eat; every eighteenth, sleep until the twenty-fourth. Thrice I would nourish myself, and six hours would be given to slumber. A disciplined rhythm, fit for the terrors of the Night Land. Yet even then, I knew the Land would not always permit such regularity. Perils lurked in every shadow, demanding wakefulness when weariness pressed heaviest.

And so, in foolish defiance of my own rule, I walked for one-and-twenty hours without pause, creeping low where the Watcher’s gaze might fall. Hunger gnawed, but I ignored it, promising myself sustenance later. Only when my legs trembled and my vision blurred did I relent, searching for a place to rest.

Not far off, a fire-hole glowed—a small pit of bubbling flame, its sulfurous breath curling into the gloom. I settled near its warmth, the Diskos resting at my side, and turned my gaze back toward the Mighty Pyramid. Though I had journeyed far, its immensity made the distance seem trivial, and for a moment, my spirit faltered at the enormity of the task before me.

Yet comfort lingered in its nearness. Countless eyes watched from its heights, though I made no signal. Farewells were not for those who meant to return. Still, I smiled at the thought of the Master Monstruwacan peering through the Great Spy-Glass, perhaps even now observing my rest.

With effort, I forced myself to eat—three dense tablets, chewed slow, and water conjured from dust in a cup. A meager feast, but enough to sustain life in this desolation. Then I checked my belongings: the Diskos, the scrip of food, and the pouch at my hip. Within it lay the compass, crafted by the Master’s own hands, its needle meant to guide me should I lose sight of home.

I set it upon the ground, but it spun aimlessly, unsettled by the churning Earth-Currents beneath me. I wondered if the Master watched this test, though the Night Land’s shifting lights and fumes often obscured even the Great Spy-Glass’s sight.

Satisfied, I wrapped myself in my cloak and lay beside the fire-hole, the Diskos close, its hum a lullaby. As sleep took me, I felt the distant pulse of millions—their thoughts brushing the aether, willing me safety. And in dreams, I walked with *her*, the one I sought, bathed in a light that banished the Dark.

---

A roar shattered my rest—the Home-Call, blaring across the Night. I surged awake, the Diskos already in my grip. Danger neared.

I scanned the darkness, then looked up toward the Pyramid. Green fire flashed in the Tower of Observation, spelling warning in the Set-Speech: *A Grey Man scents you. It comes from the bushes behind.*

I lunged into the moss-bushes to my left, heart hammering. Moments later, a hulking shape emerged—grey, brutish, sniffing along the ground. It hesitated at the fire-hole’s glare, then retreated, only to reappear elsewhere, testing the light from new angles. Each time, it pressed low, jaws working, eyes searching.

Then—silence. It vanished into the brush.

A whisper touched my mind—the Master’s voice, warning through the Night-Hearing: *It circles. It means to take you from behind.*

I barely had time to leap aside before a grey hand parted the moss where I had hidden. The Beast-Man’s head followed, snuffling into the empty space.

I struck.

The Diskos flared, shearing through the creature’s neck. Its body convulsed, legs twitching, then stilled. I stepped back, the weapon humming, alive with victory.

I raised it high, letting its fire announce my survival to those who watched. No further word came from the Master; to speak more risked drawing worse things from the Dark.

But the aether trembled with the relief of millions.

---

I had slept ten hours—too long. I chastised myself, vowing to keep better discipline. After gathering my things, I spared one last look at the Pyramid, then turned away.

The Diskos felt like a companion in my grip, its energy thrumming in tune with my own. None but its wielder could command it fully—a truth known for ages. Even in death, a warrior’s Diskos was laid to rest with them, returning its power to the Earth-Current. A tradition of wisdom, and of love.

Now, as I pressed forward, every shadow seemed alive. My nerves frayed at imagined movements, but nothing attacked. Still, fatigue crept in, and I paused to eat, washing the meal down with conjured water.

For six more hours I walked, cautious, weaving through moss-bush and sulfur vents. The Watcher loomed ever nearer, a monstrous sentinel against the black horizon. Foolishly, I edged closer, drawn by some reckless urge to see it clearly.

A dangerous curiosity. If it saw me, it would summon forces beyond my power to resist.

Yet I stepped nearer still.

The heart is a strange thing—swinging between fear and recklessness. Or perhaps something *pulled* me.

Who can say?
