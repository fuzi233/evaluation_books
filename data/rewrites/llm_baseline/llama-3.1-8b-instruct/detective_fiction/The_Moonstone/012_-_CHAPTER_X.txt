As the last of the Ablewhites joined the gathering, the total count of the company reached twenty-four. The Rector of Frizinghall, with a rich, melodious voice, rose from his seat to offer a blessing over the dinner table. The warm glow of the candles cast a golden light on the faces of the assembled guests, each one eager to begin the feast.

I'll spare you the details of the guests, save for two notable exceptions. Those two individuals sat on either side of Miss Rachel, who shone like a radiant star at the center of the gathering. Her stunning birthday gift, the Moonstone, sparkled unadorned against her white dress, drawing admiring glances from everyone present. However, only two people ventured to comment on its exceptional size and beauty.

To Miss Rachel's right sat Dr. Candy, the gentle and affable physician of Frizinghall. Though he had a talent for storytelling and a penchant for making others laugh, his hasty nature often led him to blurt out unsolicited remarks, causing unintended offense. Despite this, his medical expertise earned him the respect of his patients and colleagues alike. As he turned to Miss Rachel, his eyes twinkling with mirth, he proposed an outlandish solution to safeguard the precious gemstone. "My dear Miss Rachel," he said with a grin, "if you're willing to lend me the Moonstone, I'd be happy to conduct a little experiment. We could heat it to a precise temperature, expose it to a gentle breeze, and – voilà! – we'd render the stone inert, freeing you from the burden of its safekeeping." His words were meant in jest, but his lady companion looked on with a hint of concern, as if she wished Dr. Candy's proposal were genuine.

On Miss Rachel's left sat the renowned explorer, Mr. Murthwaite. A tall, lean figure with sun-kissed skin and an air of quiet intensity, he exuded an aura of weariness, as if the comforts of home had grown dull after years of traversing the unforgiving landscapes of the East. Though he spoke sparingly throughout the meal, his eyes lingered on the Moonstone, its reputation having reached even the farthest corners of his travels.
