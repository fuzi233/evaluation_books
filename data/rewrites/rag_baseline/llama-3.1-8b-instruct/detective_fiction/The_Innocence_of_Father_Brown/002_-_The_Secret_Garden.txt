The Secret Garden


Aristide Valentin, Chief of the Paris Police, was running late for his dinner, and the tension was palpable among his guests as they gathered in the grand entrance hall. Ivan, the chief's loyal and seasoned servant, stood watchfully at his post, his weathered face a testament to years of dedicated service. The entrance hall itself was a space both imposing and ornate, lined with the traditional symbols of the Valentin family's storied past: ancient swords, battle-axes, and the like. Tonight, however, it was not the martial heritage that held sway, but the promise of an exquisite dinner party.

As the guests chatted and laughed, Ivan discreetly guided them towards the dining room, where the pièce de résistance awaited. Meanwhile, the master of the house was making his way through the labyrinthine corridors, his footsteps echoing off the stone floor. His dinner jacket was immaculate, his red rosette a subtle nod to his position as a respected figure in the city's culinary scene. The dinner guests had been carefully selected for their refined tastes and discerning palates, and tonight's menu promised to be a true tour-de-force.

Valentin's own passion for haute cuisine was legendary, and his chef had outdone himself in preparing a series of dishes that would delight even the most jaded gourmands. As the Chief made his way through the house, he couldn't help but feel a sense of pride in the evening's offerings. There was the foie gras en croute, the bouillabaisse provençale, and, of course, the pièce de résistance: a traditional French tarte tatin, its caramelized apples and buttery pastry a true masterpiece.

As he entered the dining room, Valentin's eyes scanned the gathering, taking in the familiar faces of his guests. There was Lord Galloway, the English Ambassador, whose robust features and florid complexion spoke of a love of good living. Beside him sat his wife, Lady Galloway, a statuesque woman with a silver bob and an air of quiet sophistication. Their daughter, Lady Margaret Graham, looked pale and delicate, but her copper-colored hair shone like a beacon in the soft candlelight. The Duchess of Mont St. Michel and her daughters were also in attendance, their dark eyes flashing with a hint of mischief.
