As the morning sun struggled to penetrate the grey haze shrouding the Barton estate, Elinor observed her friend's low spirits with growing unease. Marianne's usual exuberance had given way to a somber quietude, and Elinor's visit from Edward the previous evening had offered little respite. Though he had joined them in the breakfast-room the following morning, his reserved manner only added to her concern. Before Marianne had even reached the top of the stairs, Elinor noticed Edward slipping out of the parlour, citing a need to inspect his horses in the nearby village.

"I'll be back shortly," he called out, disappearing into the chill of the morning air.

When Edward returned, his eyes sparkled with renewed enthusiasm as he recounted the vistas he had beheld during his brief excursion. The village, situated higher up the valley, offered a panoramic view of the rolling hills and verdant meadows below. Marianne, ever eager to nurture their happiness, seized upon this opportunity to engage Edward in conversation about the local landscape. As they strolled across the grounds, she asked him to elaborate on the aspects of the scenery that had captivated him.

Edward, however, was hesitant to indulge in overly poetic descriptions, instead opting for a more straightforward assessment. "I think it's a lovely place," he said, "with steep hills, beautiful timber, and comfortable farmhouses scattered about. The fields are lush and the air is crisp – it's just the sort of place I imagine as being both beautiful and practical." Marianne looked at him quizzically, sensing that beneath his words lay a deeper truth. "Why do you speak of it so matter-of-factly?" she asked, her voice tinged with a hint of amusement.

Elinor intervened, sensing a nuance to Edward's reticence. "Perhaps, dear Marianne, Edward's reluctance to wax poetic stems from his distaste for those who pretend to admire nature without truly feeling it. He wants to avoid appearing affected, so he chooses to be more subdued in his praise." Marianne nodded thoughtfully, recognizing the validity of Elinor's insight. As they continued their stroll, the trio engaged in a discussion about the aesthetics of landscape, with Marianne confessing her disdain for the pretentious jargon often employed by those who claimed to appreciate the picturesque. Edward listened attentively, his eyes never leaving hers, as if searching for a connection that went beyond words.
