In the warm sunlight that streamed through the open window, casting a gentle glow on the river below, Mirah descended the stairs, her dark hair curling softly around her face, a testament to a restful night's sleep. Her eyes, though still bearing the faint blue semicircles of fatigue, sparkled with a hint of hope as she donned Mab's black dress, its simple elegance accentuated by the delicate feel of the tiny felt slippers that Mab had hastily purchased to fit her smaller feet. The borrowed dress ended just above her ankles, revealing the worn, yet endearing, clothing that clung to her feet like the tender shoots of a bud. The farthing buckles, polished to a subtle shine, added a touch of whimsy to the overall ensemble.

As she entered the parlor, Mab couldn't resist exclaiming over the slippers, her eyes shining with excitement. "Oh, if you please, Mama, look at them! How perfectly they fit! I declare, Mirah, you resemble the Queen Budoor – two delicate feet, the handiwork of a benevolent creator, supporting her, and I wonder how they can bear the weight above." Mirah's gaze dropped to her own feet, her expression childlike and innocent, before she smiled warmly at Mrs. Meyrick, who watched her with a mixture of curiosity and wariness.

"I fear those feet have borne a heavy burden of late," Mrs. Meyrick said, her voice gentle, "but today, they will rest, and I shall have your company." Mab, however, felt a pang of disappointment, knowing she would miss out on the conversation, which she sensed would be a poignant and intimate one. Kate had already departed for her sketchbook, and Amy was out on errands, leaving the room quiet and still.

As Mrs. Meyrick settled into her chair, surrounded by the serene atmosphere of the parlor, she felt a sense of calm wash over her. The walls, adorned with an assortment of engravings, seemed to whisper secrets of the past, their subjects a diverse tapestry of human experience – the Virgin soaring amidst her angelic retinue, the prophets and sibyls, the tragic muse, and the children lost in thought or play.
