The sunlight filtering through the leaded panes of the estate's conservatory cast dappled shadows across the polished surface of the breakfast table, a tranquil tableau that belied the tension simmering beneath. Mr. Henleigh Mallinger Grandcourt lounged in his chair, his legs crossed, a cigar clenched between his teeth as he gazed out at the park beyond the open windows. His companion, Mr. Lush, sat opposite, sipping his coffee with an air of studied nonchalance. The air was heavy with the scent of blooming flowers and the distant hum of bees, a soothing melody that only served to heighten the discordant note that had been growing between the two men.

Their canine entourage, a motley assortment of breeds, wandered in and out of the room, their tails wagging lazily as they vied for attention. Fetch, a beautiful liver-colored water-spaniel, stood steadfastly by Grandcourt's side, her eyes fixed adoringly on her master as she watched him stroke the tiny Maltese dog in his lap. The smaller dog, its silver collar glinting in the sunlight, seemed to be the center of Grandcourt's universe, and Fetch's piteous whimpers as she gazed up at her master only served to underscore the depth of her devotion.

But Grandcourt's attention was elsewhere, his thoughts consumed by the events of the previous day. The memory of his encounter with the young woman at the archery meeting still lingered, a tantalizing thread that he couldn't quite shake. He had meant to visit Quetcham the following day, but now, with Lush's words hanging in the air, he found himself hesitating. The question of whether to ride or drive to Quetcham had become a mere pretext, a distraction from the turmoil brewing within him.

"I am not going to Quetcham," he said finally, his voice low and even.

Lush raised an eyebrow, his expression a mask of polite curiosity. "You did not go yesterday," he ventured, his words dripping with a subtle rebuke.

Grandcourt's response was to fall silent, his gaze drifting back out the window as he puffed on his cigar. The air was heavy with unspoken words, the tension between the two men palpable. And then, without warning, Lush spoke up again, his tone measured and detached.

"I suppose you sent your card and inquiries."
