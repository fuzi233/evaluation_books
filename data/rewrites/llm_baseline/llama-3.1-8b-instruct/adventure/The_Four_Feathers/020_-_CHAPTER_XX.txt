As the last rays of sunlight faded beyond the rolling hills, Durrance stepped into the cool evening air, his feet carrying him towards the familiar contours of his home, "Guessens." His body-servant, anticipating his arrival, had already lit the candles in the hallway, casting a warm glow that seemed to beckon him deeper into the house. "You may retire, I'll take care of myself," Durrance said, his voice low and soothing, as he walked into his study, a room that was more akin to a trophy room than a traditional study.

The air was heavy with the scent of old leather and polished metal, a testament to the countless hours Durrance had spent within these walls. He navigated the room with ease, his fingers trailing over the intricate carvings on the wooden paneling, his fingertips tracing the shapes of the various cups and goblets that adorned the shelves. Each one told a story, each one evoking memories of far-off lands and distant battles. He picked up a delicate, thin-stemmed goblet, its crystal glinting in the candlelight, and remembered the steeple-chase at Colchester, the grey skies and ploughed fields that had framed the event like a canvas. The pewter pen holder, perched on his writing desk, was a relic from his freshman year at Oxford, a reminder of late-night study sessions and the camaraderie of his college "fours."

As he wandered the room, his fingers danced across the surface of his trophies, each one a tangible connection to a bygone era. He recalled the time he'd hunted a lion in the hills west of Berenice, the thrill of stalking prey across the open terrain, and the satisfying crunch of the rifle's trigger beneath his finger. The memory of his first ibex shot in the Khor Baraka flooded his senses, the taste of the African air, the sound of the wind rustling through the acacia trees. Even the quiet moments, like the ones spent in his small boat on the Salcombe estuary, were etched into his mind – the feel of the Greener gun beneath his shoulder, the soft lapping of the water against the hull.
