Briarmains.

The late afternoon sun cast a golden glow over the estate, a stark contrast to the chill that seeped into the bones of its inhabitants. Messrs. Helstone and Sykes, still basking in the warmth of their earlier camaraderie, now found themselves relegated to the periphery as Mr. Moore's demeanor darkened. His countenance, a canvas of stoic reserve, betrayed no hint of the turmoil brewing beneath. The rector, ever perceptive, chose to tactfully disengage, remarking to Sykes, "Come, sir; our roads converge for a short stretch. Shall we stroll together, leaving Mr. Moore to his contemplations?"

Sykes, oblivious to the undercurrents, replied, "And where is Sugden?" Moore's gaze rose, his eyes narrowing as if searching for something lost. "Ah, yes," Helstone chimed in, "I've been keeping busy. I thought it prudent to expedite matters. While you were engaged with Mr. Farren, I slipped out to the stables and summoned Murgatroyd to fetch your gig, Mr. Sykes. I then discreetly escorted Sugden and his brother through the back window, with your permission, of course." With a flourish, Helstone concluded, "Sugden now takes the reins, driving with the fervor of Jehu. In another quarter of an hour, Barraclough will be safely ensconced in Stilbro' jail."

Moore's response was measured, his voice a monotone. "Thank you. Good-day, gentlemen." He ushered them toward the door, ensuring they departed his premises. For the remainder of the day, he remained taciturn, his interactions with Joe Scott reduced to mere necessities.
