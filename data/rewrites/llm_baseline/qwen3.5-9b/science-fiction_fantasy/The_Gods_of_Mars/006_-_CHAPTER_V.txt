I cannot recall how long I remained unconscious upon the cold stone floor of the storeroom, only that the duration must have stretched into many hours. My awakening was violent—a sharp startle induced by distant cries of terror. Before my eyes could fully focus, before my mind could coalesce enough to orient itself within this labyrinthine nightmare, a hail of gunfire shattered the silence. The bullets rang out in a deafening fusillade, their echoes bouncing wildly through the subterranean tunnels.

Instantly, I was on my feet.

From a massive doorway at the far end of the room, a dozen lesser Therns materialized, blocking our exit. Scattered around me lay the lifeless forms of my companions. Save for Thuvia and Tars Tarkas, who, like myself, had been sleeping on the floor and thus evaded the initial volley, everyone else was dead. As I rose, the Therns lowered their wicked rifles, their faces twisted in a mixture of chagrin, panic, and sheer alarm.

Seizing the moment, I stepped forward, my voice ringing with fierce indignation. "What madness is this?" I demanded. "Is Sator Throg to be assassinated by his own vassals?"

"Have mercy, O Master of the Tenth Cycle!" shrieked one of the soldiers, while the rest edged backward toward the doorway, desperate to flee the gaze of the mighty one.

Thuvia leaned close to my ear, whispering urgently, "Ask them their purpose here."

"What business do you have in this place, you fools?" I roared.

One of the men stammered, "We were commanded by the Father of Therns to hunt two fugitives from the Outer World. One was a white man with black hair; the other, a colossal green warrior." He cast a suspicious, sidelong glance toward Tars Tarkas.

"Behold," Thuvia interjected, gesturing to the Thark, "one of them stands before you. And if you examine the corpse lying by the door, you may identify the other. It was left to Sator Throg and his pitiful slaves to finish what the guard failed to do. We slew one and captured the other; had Sator Throg granted us our freedom, this would have been our reward. Instead, in your foolishness, you have slaughtered all but myself and nearly killed the great Sator Throg himself."

The men hung their heads, looking sheepish and terrified.

"Should they not dispose of these bodies to the Plant Men and retreat to their quarters immediately, O Mighty One?" Thuvia asked me.

"Yes," I commanded, "do exactly as Thuvia instructs."

As the soldiers gathered the corpses, I observed the one who lifted the body of the late Sator Throg. Upon seeing the upturned face, the man flinched. He then stole a furtive, calculating glance in my direction from the corner of his eye. I knew instantly that he suspected the truth—that the dead man was not who he claimed to be—but the fear held him silent. As he carried the body out, he shot another quick, searching look at me before returning his gaze to the bald, shiny dome of the corpse in his arms. The final glimpse of his profile as he vanished from the chamber revealed a cunning smile of triumph curling his lips.

Only Tars Tarkas, Thuvia, and I remained.

The deadly aim of the Therns had stripped our group of everything but ourselves, severing our last slim chance of escaping to the free world outside. Once the grim procession departed, Thuvia urged us to flee again. She, too, had detected the wary attitude of the soldier carrying Sator Throg's body.

"It portends ill for us, O Prince," she warned. "Even though this fool dared not accuse you openly, there are superiors above with the power to demand a thorough investigation. Such scrutiny would surely prove fatal."

I shrugged, a sense of resignation weighing heavily on me. Death seemed inevitable regardless of our actions. Though refreshed from my sleep, I was still weak from blood loss, my wounds throbbing painfully. Medicinal aid was impossible here. I longed desperately for the miraculous healing salves of the Green Women; in an hour, they would have restored me to full strength. Instead, despair settled over me like a heavy cloak. Never before had I felt such utter hopelessness in the face of danger.

Suddenly, the long, flowing yellow locks of the Holy Thern caught in a draft, blowing across my face. Could they still lead us to freedom? If we acted swiftly, might we slip away before the general alarm was raised? We had nothing to lose but our lives.

"What will the fellow do first, Thuvia?" I asked. "How long before they return for us?"

"He will proceed directly to the Father of Therns, Old Matai Shang," she replied. "Though he may wait for an audience, being a Thorian among the lesser Therns grants him precedence. Matai Shang will not keep him waiting long. If the Father believes his tale, another hour will see the galleries, courts, and gardens swarming with searchers."

"Our options must be decided within an hour," I said grimly. "What is the quickest route out of this celestial hell?"

"Directly to the summit of the cliffs, Prince," she answered. "Then through the gardens to the inner courts. Our path lies across the Thern temples to the outer court, and finally to the ramparts. 'O Prince,' she sighed, 'it is hopeless. Ten thousand warriors could not carve a path to liberty from this dreadful place.' Since time immemorial, the Therns have slowly added stone to stone, building an unbreakable circle of fortification around the slopes of the Mountains of Otz. Within the temples behind the ramparts, a million fighters stand ready. The courts and gardens are teeming with slaves, women, and children. None can move a stone's throw without detection."

"If there is no other way, Thuvia, why dwell on the impossibility? We must face it."

"Could we not attempt it after dark?" Tars Tarkas suggested. "There seems no chance by day."

"There is a slight advantage at night," Thuvia conceded. "Fewer patrols roam the courts and gardens, though the ramparts remain heavily guarded."

"What hour is it?" I asked.

"It was midnight when you freed me from my chains," Thuvia recalled. "Two hours later we reached this storeroom. You slept for fourteen hours. It must now be nearing sunset."

She led us through winding corridors to a window overlooking the Valley Dor. To our right, the sun was setting—a vast, burning red orb dipping below the western range of Otz. Below us, the Holy Thern stood sentinel on his balcony, his scarlet robes drawn tight against the approaching chill. On Mars, the atmosphere is so rarefied that it retains little solar heat during the day, making nights intensely cold. Furthermore, the thin air offers no refraction or diffusion of light. When the great red sun vanishes, there is no twilight; the transition is abrupt, plunging the world from blinding brilliance into absolute darkness, much like extinguishing a single lamp in a sealed room.

As the sun dipped, the twin moons appeared, hurling across the sky like monstrous meteors. Their pale light illuminated the eastern banks of Korus, the crimson grass, and the magnificent forests. Beneath the trees, herds of Plant Men grazed. Adults stood erect on their toes, their mighty tails sweeping the ground, their talons pruning every available leaf and twig. This observation clarified my earlier mistake regarding the trimmed trees; they were not a playground for a civilized people, but a managed resource for these alien herbivores.

Our gaze drifted to the rolling Iss River issuing from the mountain base. Emerging from the gloom was a canoe laden with captives from the Outer World—twelve souls of the highly cultured Red Race. The Herald on the balcony below spotted them instantly. Leaning far over the railing, he emitted a shrill, eerie wail that summoned the demons of this hellish realm.

For a heartbeat, the brutes paused, ears stiffened, then surged from the grove toward the riverbank, bounding across the distance with clumsy, powerful leaps. The party had landed and stood on the grass as the terrifying horde arrived. A brief, futile struggle ensued before silence fell as the huge, repulsive shapes covered their victims. Dozens of sucking mouths clamped onto the flesh of the prey.

I turned away in revulsion.

"Their work ends quickly," Thuvia noted. "The great White Apes claim the meat once the Plant Men have drained the arteries. Look; they are coming."

Indeed, a dozen of the great white monsters raced across the valley toward the riverbank. Just then, the sun vanished completely, and a darkness so profound it seemed tangible swallowed us whole.

Thuvia wasted no time, leading us toward the corridor winding upward through the cliffs, thousands of feet above our current level. Twice, great Banths, roaming loose through the galleries, blocked our path. Each time, Thuvia uttered a low command, and the snarling beasts slunk away sullenly.

"If you can dissolve obstacles as effortlessly as you command these fierce brutes, I see no difficulty ahead," I told the girl, offering a strained smile.

"How do you achieve this?" I asked.
