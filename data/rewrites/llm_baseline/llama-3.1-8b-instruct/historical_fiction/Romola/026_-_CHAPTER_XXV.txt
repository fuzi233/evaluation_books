As the fervor of Savonarola's sermon subsided, a second figure slipped unnoticed into the grand space within the Duomo. Piero di Cosimo, a painter of discerning taste, had arrived unobtrusively, his eyes fixed intently on the figure of Baldassarre, still swaying under the weight of his recent liberation. While the congregation remained entranced, Piero moved quietly forward, his hand extended to touch the shoulder of the erstwhile prisoner. Baldassarre's gaze, still wet with tears, slowly cleared as he regarded the stranger. With a deep sigh, he shook off the lingering emotions, and Piero, sensing the moment was ripe, spoke in a low, measured tone.

"May I assist you, signore? I've heard of your... unfortunate circumstances."

Baldassarre's response was hesitant, his eyes narrowing warily as he searched the stranger's face. Yet, as Piero produced a knife and deftly cut the cords binding his wrists, a hint of gratitude crept into his expression. "Grazie," he murmured, flexing his fingers in relief.

"You should take care of yourself," Piero suggested, his words laced with a gentle concern. "Perhaps a place to rest, a warm meal, would do you good?"

Baldassarre's suspicion ebbed, replaced by a glimmer of curiosity. "I... thank you," he said, his voice a little stronger now.

As they stepped out into the morning light, Piero gestured toward the distant Porta Santa Croce. "The hospital for travelers is just beyond. You're welcome to seek refuge there."

Their path took them through the winding Via dell'Oriuolo, lined with vendors and artisans, their wares on display. The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, mingling with the fragrance of herbs and spices. As they walked, Piero posed a question, his eyes sparkling with interest. "Would you sit for me, signore? I'm a painter, and I believe your story would make a compelling subject."

Baldassarre's expression darkened, his eyes clouding over once more. "No," he said firmly, his voice tinged with a hint of warning.

Piero's smile faltered, but only for an instant. "Very well," he said, his tone neutral. "Then I'll leave you to your journey. The hospital is just ahead."
