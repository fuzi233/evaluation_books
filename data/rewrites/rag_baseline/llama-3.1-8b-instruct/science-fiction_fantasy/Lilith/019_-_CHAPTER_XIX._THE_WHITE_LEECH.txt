The White Leech

I awoke one morning from a deep slumber, my hand throbbing with a dull ache. A small, triangular wound marred the back of my palm, its edges slightly swollen. As the day wore on, the discomfort dissipated, and by evening, the injury had all but healed. I combed through the cave, scrutinizing every crevice and nook, but found nothing that could have inflicted the wound. The memory of the previous night's events remained hazy, but I recalled the sensation of being bitten, the sudden searing pain that had left me gasping.

Days passed, and my companion remained motionless, her body unresponsive. Yet, I detected subtle changes – her features seemed softer, the sharp angles of her bones rounding, her skin regaining its natural hue. It was as if the tide of life, which had ebbed to the brink of oblivion, was beginning to swell once more. Twenty times a day, I checked for signs of revival, and twenty times a day, doubt assailed me. However, the image of her as I first found her, fragile and lifeless, still lingered in my mind, fueling my hope.

As the weeks went by, I grew accustomed to the rhythm of the cave, the gentle gurgle of the nearby stream, and the scent of damp earth. One evening, I arose from my vigil, thinking to step outside and breathe the cooler night air. The moon cast an ethereal glow, illuminating the cave's dark recesses. I paused, entranced by the sight of my companion, her face bathed in an otherworldly light. Her eyes, dark as the void, seemed to shimmer with an inner radiance, as if they had been carved from the very essence of night itself. I drew closer, my heart pounding in anticipation, and bent over her. Disappointment washed over me as I realized her eyelids remained closed, my imagination having played a cruel trick on me. I collapsed onto the cave floor, overcome with despair.

But a spark of hope remained. What if, for a fleeting moment, her eyes had opened, and then closed again? What if she was alive, merely resting? The thought gave me solace, and I drifted into a troubled sleep.
