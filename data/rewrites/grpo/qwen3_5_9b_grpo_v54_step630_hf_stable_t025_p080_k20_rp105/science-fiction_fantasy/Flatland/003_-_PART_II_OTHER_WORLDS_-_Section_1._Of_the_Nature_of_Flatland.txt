I designate our realm Flatland, not by our own nomenclature, but to render its essence intelligible to you, fortunate dwellers of the Third Dimension.

Envision an infinite plane where geometric forms—lines, triangles, squares, pentagons, hexagons, and countless others—drift freely across the surface, utterly incapable of ascending above or descending below it. They exist much like shadows cast upon a wall, yet possessing substance and glowing, defined edges. This is the accurate image of my nation and its citizens. Once, I might have termed this entire existence "my universe," but my consciousness has since been expanded to comprehend higher realities.

In such a dimension, it is logically impossible for any object of "solid" nature to exist. You might reasonably assume, however, that we could at least visually distinguish between the various shapes as they moved about. This is a grave error. We perceive nothing of the sort; we cannot differentiate one figure from another through sight alone. To us, the only visible reality consists of straight lines. I shall now demonstrate this necessity.

Place a coin upon the center of a table in your world and gaze down upon it from above. To your eyes, it appears a perfect circle. Now, retreat to the table's edge and slowly lower your head until your eye aligns with the surface. As you descend, the coin transforms from a circle into an increasingly elongated oval. Finally, when your eye rests exactly upon the table's plane, mirroring the condition of a Flatlander, the oval vanishes entirely. The coin ceases to be a shape and becomes, in your vision, a mere straight line.

This phenomenon applies equally to any polygon cut from pasteboard. Whether it be a triangle, a square, or any other form, once your viewpoint reaches the level of the surface, the figure dissolves into a line. Consider, for instance, an equilateral triangle, which among us signifies a tradesman of reputable standing. Figure 1 depicts how you would see him if you hovered above; Figures 2 and 3 illustrate his appearance as your eye nears the table's level; and if your eye were perfectly flush with the surface—as it is for us in Flatland—you would behold nothing but a straight line.

When I resided in Spaceland, I learned that your sailors encounter a parallel experience when traversing your oceans. A distant island or coastline may possess intricate bays, promontories, and angular recesses, yet from afar, you perceive none of these details unless the sun casts sharp shadows that reveal depth through light and dark. Instead, you see only a grey, unbroken line stretching across the water.

Precisely so is it with us. When a triangular acquaintance or any other citizen approaches, we see no complexity. Lacking a sun and the play of shadows that aids your vision, we possess no such optical crutches. As a friend draws nearer, his line grows larger; as he departs, it shrinks. Yet, regardless of whether he is a triangle, a square, a pentagon, a hexagon, or a circle, to our eyes he remains nothing more than a straight line.

You may wonder how we manage to distinguish one friend from another under such visual constraints. This inquiry, though natural, finds its proper answer only when I describe the inhabitants of Flatland in detail. For now, I shall defer that discussion to briefly address the climate and architecture of our world.
