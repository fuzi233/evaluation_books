Errina sat, the spindle spinning in her thick-coiled raven hair, her deepest agate eyes fixed on the surging visions of a destiny that promised only insect-labor byssus. Around her, gods and men forged histories sung by poets, while she spun threads of silence. Yet, in the grand theater of fate, the spotlight shifted abruptly from the mythic to the painfully immediate.

In the antechamber of the *Italia*, the air crackled with a tension that mirrored Deronda's own premature agitations. Two servants watched him with marked surprise; the doctor their lady had summoned was not a frail elder, but a striking young gentleman whose very presence seemed to demand the evening dress he wore as a crown of adornment. But the true shock awaited him beyond the threshold.

At the far end of the vast room, a figure stood waiting, draped in black lace that cascaded from the summit of her whitening hair to the floor, trailing behind her tall, imposing silhouette. Her arms, bare to the elbow save for rich bracelets, were folded tightly before her chest. Her head held a fine, statuesque poise that rendered her even more formidable than her weary features suggested. Deronda moved forward without hesitation, closing the distance until he stood before her. She extended a hand, and he took it, lifting it reverently to his lips.

She retained her grip, studying him with an examining intensity, while his own consciousness recoiled from the sheer volatility of her gaze. Her eyes pierced him; her face was a landscape of shifting moods, capable of transforming into a stranger in the span of a heartbeat. Even as she scrutinized him, a silent dialogue played out across her brow and nostrils—a tacit language of judgment and inquiry. Deronda froze, paralyzed by the uncertainty of what emotional performance she required. He felt his cheeks flush with a heat akin to a girl's, yet he marveled at his own emotional numbness. After fifteen years of idealized imaginings, this reality felt more distant than fiction. He couldn't guess the tongue she would use; he feared it wouldn't be English.

Suddenly, she released his hand, placing both palms firmly on his shoulders. In that instant, the weariness vanished from her face, replaced by a flash of raw admiration that smoothed every line, restoring a youthful radiance.

"You are a beautiful creature!" she whispered, her voice low and melodious, carrying the faint, agreeable outline of a foreign accent. "I knew you would be."

She kissed him on each cheek, and he returned the gesture, but the intimacy felt less like a reunion and more like a formal greeting between sovereigns.

A pause settled between them, the lines of age creeping back into her expression. Then, her tone hardened into ice. "I am your mother. But you can have no love for me."

"I have thought of you more than of any other being in the world," Deronda replied, his voice trembling with nervous energy.

"I am not like what you thought I was," she stated decisively, pulling her hands away to fold them once more, inviting him to dissect her anew. He had often conjured her image in his mind as a mirror of his own soul; now, he saw fragments of that likeness buried beneath a striking, alien strangeness. She was a remarkable entity, possessing a worn beauty that felt detached from humanity—as if she were a Melusina, bound to a realm independent of theirs.

"I used to think you might be suffering," Deronda said, driven by an anxiety to avoid wounding her. "I wished to be your comfort."

"I *am* suffering. But with a suffering you cannot comfort," the Princess replied, her voice sharpening as she moved to a sofa cushioned with careful precision. "Sit down." She indicated a seat nearby. Noticing the distress etched on his face, her demeanor softened. "I am not suffering at this moment. I am at ease. I am able to talk."

Deronda sat, waiting, feeling less like a son returning home and more like a witness summoned before a mysterious Fate. He observed her with wonder, separated from her by a chasm of spiritual distance.

"No," she began, cutting through his reverie. "I did not summon you to comfort me. I could not foresee what you would feel toward me. I possess no foolish notion that you can love me simply because I am your mother, especially when we have shared no life together. But I believed I chose something better for you than mere proximity. I did not think I deprived you of anything valuable."

"You cannot wish me to believe your affection would have been worthless," Deronda countered, sensing her pause as an invitation to respond.

"I do not intend to speak ill of myself," the Princess declared with proud impetuosity. "But I possessed little affection to offer. I desired none. I had been stifled by it. I sought to live out the life inherent in me, unhampered by the weight of others' lives. You wonder what I was? I was no princess then."

She rose with a sudden, fluid motion, standing as she had before. Deronda followed suit instantly, feeling breathless.

"No princess in this tame existence I lead now. I was a great singer, and an actor as well. All others were poor beside me. Men followed me from nation to nation. I lived a myriad lives in one. I did not want a child."

There was a passionate defensiveness in her tone, casting aside all social precedent. She sought justification only in the most intense words available to describe her experience, flinging them outward as shields against potential reproach. Deronda stood clutching his coat collar, feeling as though he were clinging to a lifeline in turbulent waters, his blood rushing in a commotion reminiscent of witnessing a sacred rite that sanctified crime.

What else could she reveal? She continued with the same ferocity, a pale illumination brightening her face.

"I did not wish to marry. I was forced into wedlock with your father—not by my choice, but by my father's commands. Paradoxically, marriage was my best path to freedom. I could rule my husband, but I could never rule my father. I had a right to liberty. I had a right to escape a bondage I loathed."

She reseated herself, her eyes and closed lips performing a subtle dance of suppressed speech. Deronda remained standing. Moments later, she looked up, her defiance softening into a pleading plea.

"And the bondage I hated for myself, I sought to spare you. What greater gift could a loving mother bestow? I liberated you from the bondage of being born a Jew."

"Then I *am* a Jew?" Deronda exploded, his deep-voiced energy causing his mother to recoil slightly against her cushions. "My father was a Jew, and you are a Jewess?"

"Yes, your father was my cousin," she answered, her gaze shifting, revealing a flicker of fear.

"I am glad of it," Deronda blurted out, his voice veiled in passion. He hadn't anticipated the force of these words, nor that they would emerge in such impulsive opposition to his mother. A mixed anger shook him—one that reason struggled to quell—against a mother who seemed to have borne him unwillingly, made herself a stranger, and now revealed herself reluctantly. This final suspicion illuminated her previous speech.

But the mother was shaken by a different, equally volatile anger, one her frame could barely contain. Her physical tremors were visible, her eyes appearing larger and wilder against her pallor.

"Why do you say you are glad?" she cried violently. "You are an English gentleman. I secured that status for you."

"You did not know what you secured for me. How could you choose my birthright for me?" Deronda retorted, sinking sideways into his chair, leaning his arm over the backrest as he averted his gaze.

An intolerant fire burned within him, foreign yet undeniable. Struggling to master himself, he feared uttering words that would mark an irreversible epoch. Silence stretched between them until his mother's voice returned, firmer, its finely varied tones resisting the storm.

"I chose for you what I would have chosen for myself. How could I know you would inherit my father's spirit? How could I know you would love what I detested—if you truly loved to be a Jew?" The bitterness in her last words suggested a hatred that any listener might assume had festered between mother and son.

Yet Deronda reclaimed his composure, recalling the reality of her life: the years gone, the signs of suffering etched into her frame, and the desperate effort to recount a past that belonged to both of them. His habitual shame at accepting events as solely his burden aided him here. As he stared at her in silence, his face regained a penetrating calm, yet it exerted a strangely agitating influence. Her eyes fixed upon him with fascination, devoid of maternal delight.

"Forgive me if I speak hastily," he said with diffident gravity. "Why resolve now to disclose what you ensured I remain ignorant of? And why, since you appear angered by my gladness..."

"Oh—the reasons of our actions!" the Princess interrupted, her voice ringing with a note of sarcastic scorn. "When you reach my age, it will not seem so simple a question: 'Why did you do this?' People reduce motives to cut-and-dried formulas. Every woman is presumed to share a standard set of motivations, or else she is a monster. I am not a monster, but I have not felt exactly as other women feel—or say they feel, terrified of being deemed unlike them.
