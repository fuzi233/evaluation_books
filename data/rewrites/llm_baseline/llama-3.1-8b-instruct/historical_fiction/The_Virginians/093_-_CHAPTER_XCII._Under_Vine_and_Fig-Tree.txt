As the warm sunlight filtered through the leafy branches above, casting dappled shadows across the family gathering, our home was filled with an unmistakable sense of joy and contentment. My mother's face glowed with happiness, her eyes shining like the stars on a clear summer night as she welcomed her children back under her loving care. Her heart swelled with pride, and her hands instinctively rose to her lips, as if to kiss each one in turn.

Aunt Hetty, however, remained resolute in her decision to leave us. Though her motherhood had grown increasingly dear to her, she felt an inexplicable pang at relinquishing her role as guardian to her elder siblings. The weight of her responsibility, coupled with the knowledge that her mother's return would necessitate a shift in their dynamic, proved too great to bear. She chose instead to relocate to a charming cottage in Bury St. Edmunds, just a short distance from our home, where she could devote herself to spoiling her beloved nephew and niece in private.

It was during this period that I learned, to my surprise, of Mr. B's enduring affection for Aunt Hetty. Though she had rejected his proposal, his constancy to her was evident in the substantial inheritance he left her upon his passing. His legacy would soon prove a bittersweet reminder of the sacrifices she had made for those she loved.

Meanwhile, Monsieur Miles continued to indulge in his extravagant lifestyle, fueled by his commission, uniforms, and various indulgences in the city. I often found myself pondering the true cost of his pursuits, and the notion that even a gentleman such as himself could not sustain them on his modest annual allowance of five hundred pounds. Aunt Hetty, ever the pragmatist, had long since resigned herself to a life of spinsterhood, vowing to wait for a partner worthy of her father's stature. "I will not settle for anything less," she declared, her voice firm and unwavering.

Her words echoed in my mind as I reflected on the passing of our dear General, a man whose life had been marked by wisdom, kindness, and a deep love for his family. My wife, ever the sentimental soul, wept openly at his departure, confessing that she had not loved him enough. Yet, Aunt Hetty, though visibly moved, remained stoic, recalling his merry sayings and gentle ways with a warmth that belied her reserved exterior.
