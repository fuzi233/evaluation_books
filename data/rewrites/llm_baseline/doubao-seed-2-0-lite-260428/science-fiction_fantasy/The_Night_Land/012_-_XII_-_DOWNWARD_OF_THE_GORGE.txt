I had carried Naani a full hour down the winding, shadow-choked gorge, as I have told, when exhaustion stole so deep into my bones that I staggered with every step, my feet fumbling over the sharp volcanic rock as if they belonged to a stranger. I grew so heavy with drowsiness that I near nodded off mid-stride, and knew I would topple headlong with my precious maid in my arms if I did not find a place to rest, and quickly.

Shaking the fog from my wits, I dragged my attention to the gorge walls, and realized I had walked that entire hour as a sleepwalker, half-dreaming, no clear memory of the path beneath me. Now that I forced my will to perceive my surroundings, the whole narrow, fire-glowing cut felt new to my eyes, until the sharp, well-trained map of my outward journey rose unbidden in my mind. A great ruddy fire-vent burned not far ahead, I recalled, and set into the left cliff near its glow were seven small caves I had marked when I passed that way climbing upward, my heart then swollen with the desperate wish that I might bring my Own Safe to shelter in one of them on our journey back. That quiet, old longing now felt like a gift come true, and my tired heart lifted so light I near forgot the ache in my legs.

A dozen paces further, and I found them exactly as I remembered: seven small hollows cut high in the cliff face, the topmost of them dry, sheltered, and almost impossible for any scaled thing or abomination from the dark upper lands to climb without making noise enough to wake me. I dared not assume we had outrun the evil things that hunted us in the Redoubt’s dark country, and I knew if we nested in that high cave, any monster that tried to reach us would have to climb straight up against the edge of my Diskos before they could lay a claw on us.

I kissed Naani where she lay limp in my arms to wake her, and she kissed back soft and drowsy without opening her eyes, so deeply asleep she did not rouse. My love for her swelled so big it near hurt my chest, and I kissed her again, shook her gentle by the shoulder until her lashes fluttered, and told her we had reached a place safe enough for us to sleep. She blinked at me, still fogged with rest, and immediately chided herself for sleeping while I bore her weight all that way, her cheeks pink with shame. I kissed her again where she stood swaying on her feet, her eyes still soft with the haze of sleep, and she leaned into me, half-gone still, and whispered that she loved me, utterly and forever, for all the days left to us.

I bade her walk slow back and forth along a flat stretch of rock to wake herself fully, then climbed the cliff face up to the top cave first to scout it out. It was sweeter than I dared hope: warm from the underground fires that burned deep beneath the rock, dry as dust, the red glow of the nearby fire-vent leaking in soft and dim, no damp crevices for creeping things to hide. I called down to Naani that it was fit for our rest, then climbed back down to help her make the short, steep climb up, until we both stumbled over the threshold and stood inside, breathless, and giddy with the quiet certainty that for a little while, we were safe.

Before we could lay down, I slung our scrip and pouch over one shoulder, climbed back down to the gorge floor, and hauled up a boulder as heavy as I could carry, strapping it to my back to bring it up. Naani waited for me at the cave mouth, her face tight with anxiety that I might slip and dash myself against the rocks below, and when I climbed back in, I set the boulder right on the lip of the cave entrance, balanced so light that the faintest nudge from something climbing upward from below would send it thundering down the hundred foot drop. It would not kill a large monster, but the roar of its fall would rouse me from even the deepest, most exhausted sleep.

Naani had already spread our thick wool cloak across the smooth cave floor for us to lie on. The cave was so warm we had no need of it to cover us, and I could not even feel its softness through the thick hard plate of my armour, but she had laid it out as a shared couch, natural and sweet, no thought of impropriety in her head, only the quiet, unspoken wish to lie as close to me as she could. She patted the spot on the cloak beside her, and when I lay down on my side, she knelt to kiss me soft and solemn on the lips, then curled up beside me. We were both asleep in less than a minute.

I woke twelve hours later to the faint fizz of water heating over our small thermal chemical pack, and found Naani already up, kneeling by our stores, preparing our rations. She laughed when she saw me blink awake, bright and tender, and came to kneel beside me to kiss me, so full of soft, motherly fondness I knew at once she had been awake for an hour or more, watching me sleep. I held my arms out to her, and she snuggled against my chest happily, unresisting, though the cold hard plate of my armour dug a little into her soft cheek. I did not squeeze too tight, for fear of hurting her, but she pressed closer anyway, as if the hard, unyielding metal of my protection felt like safety to her.

She fussed at me to lie back when I moved to get up and check the boulder at the cave mouth, chiding that she meant to spoil me for once. She had checked the lip a dozen times since waking, she said, the boulder was still balanced perfectly, and there was no sign of any creature moving along the gorge floor below. I obeyed, happy to let her tend to me, and she lifted my head to rest on her knees, brought me a cup of warm water, and kissed each of our ration tablets before she passed them to me to eat. We took our second ration, drank deep of the warm water, and laughed quiet, soft, giddy as children, our hearts light for the first time since we had fled the Redoubt.

When I stood up a few minutes later, I near cried out with the sharp, burning ache of bruises I had not even felt while I was running and fighting. The four-armed yellow beast man I had slain back in the dark lands had done far more damage to me than I had realized, and my whole body was stiff and sore as if I had been beaten with clubs. Naani had seen it coming, it turned out, and already had the jar of healing ointment unstoppered at the ready. She helped me unlatch my armour, peel off my tunic, and rubbed every bruise and sore muscle slow and gentle for a full hour, until the stiffness melted out of me, leaving me loose and pain free. Her eyes were soft and tender as she tended to me, and I felt so wrapped in her care I might have been a small child, held safe in the arms of someone who loved me more than anything in the world.

When she helped me to my feet again, I moved my limbs easy, no sharp pain left, and my courage surged back. I had been terrified I would be too sore to protect her when danger came. She brought me my spare clean tunic from the pouch, and then paused, holding it to her chest, her cheeks pink and soft as she stared at my arms, thick and corded with muscle from the daily strength exercises all folk of the Great Pyramid had practiced from childhood. Before I could tease her, she set the tunic aside, and held her arms out to me, that I might hold her.

I lifted her to my chest, my heart swelling so big I felt humbled, unworthy to hold something so soft and good. She rested her face against my breast for a long quiet minute, and I felt her press a secret, sly little kiss to the hard muscle of my chest where her face was hidden, before she pulled away, blushing bright, and helped me back into my tunic, then laced my armour shut for me.

When she was done, she stood back, half shy, half mischievous, then stepped forward, her eyes downcast, and sank to her knees in front of me, tears spilling over her cheeks. I did not ask what ailed her, for I knew: she was overflowing with love, and awe, and soft, strange worship, all tangled up in her heart, because to her I was the hero who had crossed the Night Land to save her. My own heart felt both proud and unutterably humble, and I knelt down to gather her up, and did not pretend to brush away her fond adoration. She was mine, and I would spend every rest of my days proving I was worthy of the way she looked at me.

She wiped her eyes after a minute, and nestled against my chest, quiet, until I slipped her little shoes off her feet one by one, chiding myself for not thinking of it earlier, for her soles were blistered and sore from all the miles we had walked. I tended to her feet, rubbed ointment into the blisters, tied her shoes back on, and then we climbed carefully down the steep cliff face from our high cave, setting back off down the gorge.

We traveled for six more careful, uneventful hours, the red glow of the fire vents lighting our path, the air warm, sharp with faint whiffs of sulfur, the distant low rumble of underground fires thrumming through the rock under our feet. We stopped to rest and eat, and I bathed her feet in a warm rock pool fed by the thermal springs, rubbing the ointment in deep to soothe the soreness. I knew we were no more than six or seven hours away from the long dark stretch of the gorge where the great slugs laired, and we would need to make that twelve hour stretch without pausing, no rest, no shelter, until we came out the other side. It would be wiser to rest and sleep now, fully refreshed, before we faced that horror.

I did not tell Naani about the slugs yet. She was happy now, charmed by the warm fire glow and the soft steam vents that hissed up through the rock, and felt quiet in her soul, sure that no evil thing would hunt us here. I would not spoil that peace until we had to.

Six more hours of easy walking, and we stumbled straight upon the very cave I had sheltered in on my outward journey, right beside the hot spring I had washed my wounds in, a stone’s throw from a small bubbling fire vent. Naani lit up with delight, eager to rest in the very spot where I had once hidden, half-dead and exhausted, after escaping the slugs. It was not set high up in the cliff like our last shelter, but it was dry and empty, no creeping things hiding inside, and we could block the mouth with boulders to make a barrier that would alert us if anything tried to break in while we slept.

We gathered all the heavy small boulders we could carry, and stacked them one by one across the cave mouth after we stepped inside, packing the gaps tight at the bottom so no small rat-thing or slug spawn could crawl through. It left us dim in the dark, but we felt utterly safe. Naani tucked our scrip and pouch under my head for a pillow, set the Diskos within arm’s reach beside me, even though the sharp, whirring edge of it made her shiver, then tucked the cloak around my shoulders and kissed me soft and solemn on the lips before she curled up on her own side of the small bed. She was asleep in minutes, breathing soft and steady.

I lay awake for a little while, watching her, until she snuggled over against me in her sleep, as if it was her right to press close to my side. I did not move to wrap my arms around her, or kiss her, for she trusted me utterly, and I would not take advantage of that soft, sleeping trust. Sleep claimed me soon after, and I did not stir for eight full hours.

I woke to the familiar fizz of Naani heating water, and saw her moving in the dim glow leaking through the gaps in our boulder wall. She noticed me wake, and gave a small, quiet cry of joy, hurrying over to throw her arms around my neck and kiss me three times quick on the lips. I realized then, half through the fuzz of waking, that I had felt a soft kiss on my lips in my dreams, moments before. The little mischief had snuck a kiss while I was still deep asleep, and said nothing of it. I said nothing either, only smiled, resolving that next time I would wake fast enough to catch her at it.

I climbed to my feet, pulled half the boulder wall down to let in the light, and peered out over the gorge floor. No monsters lurked there, no abominations crept through the red glow. The only living thing in sight was one of the fat, pale rat-things, gorged full of carrion, dozing peacefully on the edge of the small fire vent.
