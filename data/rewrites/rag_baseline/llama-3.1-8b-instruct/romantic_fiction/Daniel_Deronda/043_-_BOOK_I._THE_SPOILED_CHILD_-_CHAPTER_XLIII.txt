In the flickering candlelight, Mordecai's eyes locked onto Deronda's, a deep satisfaction spreading across his features like a gentle warmth. The air in the small study was heavy with the scent of old books and the faint tang of smoke, the silence between them a palpable thing. As Deronda rose to move closer, his chair scraping softly against the floor, Mordecai felt a sense of comfort, a sense of ease that was both familiar and fleeting. His words began to flow, a low murmur that seemed to come from someplace deeper than his lips.

"In the ancient wisdom of the Cabbala," he said, "souls are reborn, again and again, until they are perfected, freed from the weight of their mortal shells. A soul, once liberated, can join with another, and together they will complete their work, leaving the world behind. But it is the imperfections of those already born that hinder the arrival of new souls, and delay the coming of the Messiah." His words trailed off, lost in thought, and for a moment, Deronda felt the weight of unspoken meaning settle upon him.

"I'll do everything I can to make your life meaningful," he said finally, his voice barely above a whisper. Mordecai's response was immediate, a quiet conviction that seemed to dismiss the need for further reassurance. "I know you will," he said, his eyes never leaving Deronda's face. "You see it all, you're with me on the mountain of visions, and you see the paths of fulfillment that others refuse to acknowledge."

The silence that followed was a palpable thing, a living entity that wrapped itself around them both. And then, without warning, Mordecai's voice took on a meditative quality, his words tumbling out in a slow, measured cadence.
