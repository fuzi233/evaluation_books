The scents of my youth still linger, a bittersweet bouquet that wafts on the wind, transporting me to a realm where laughter and camaraderie reigned supreme. I recall the halcyon days spent with Tubby, whose golden locks now seem to have lost their luster, and Langley, whose dented nose had become an endearing trademark. There was Shamus, the stalwart soul with a heart of gold, and Neal, the slender one, whose presence always seemed to brighten the atmosphere. And then, of course, there was Dicky, "Dicky Dick," the jovial giant, and Ballett, whose tears seemed to well up at the mere mention of his name. Each of these dear friends has vanished into the ether, leaving behind only memories and the ache of longing.

As I wander through the labyrinthine corridors of my mind, I find myself drawn back to a particular afternoon, when Tubby and I ventured into the Regent's Park canal, seeking adventure and the thrill of the unknown. We huddled together, sharing a frugal meal of toasted "elephant's tongue," its uninitiated appearance belied by its rich, gamey flavor. The sounds of the jungle – the distant roars of lions and tigers, the mournful growls of polar bears – served as a poignant reminder of our place within the grand tapestry of the natural world. It was a moment frozen in time, a snapshot of innocence and joy that I dare not disturb.

Time, that great sculptor, continues to shape the world around us, covering the scars of the past with the gentle touch of the seasons. Even now, as I reflect on those halcyon days, I am struck by the transience of life. It was only a short while ago that I saw Tubby hurrying down Throgmorton Street, his side whiskers and greying locks a testament to the passage of time. I should have stopped him, but I was bound for Liverpool Street, where I had to catch a train and shave on the fly. I wonder if he recognized me, if our eyes met in a fleeting moment of recognition. Gallant, kind-hearted Shamus, now an income tax assessor, may Providence guide him to more favorable climes than mine.
