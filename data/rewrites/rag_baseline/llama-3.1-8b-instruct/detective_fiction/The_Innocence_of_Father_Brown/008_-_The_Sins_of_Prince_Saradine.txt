The scent of damp earth and blooming wildflowers wafted across the tranquil Norfolk waters as Flambeau guided the small sailing-boat through the narrow channels of the Broads. It was a serene morning, the sky a deep, rich blue that seemed to stretch on forever, and the air was alive with the songs of birds and the gentle lapping of the water against the hull. The two men had moored their vessel the previous evening under a bank shrouded in tall grasses and stunted trees, and the stillness of the night had given way to a peaceful dawn.

As the first light crept over the horizon, Flambeau and Father Brown emerged from their slumber, their movements quiet and deliberate. The large lemon moon, now a ghostly presence in the sky, cast an ethereal glow over the landscape, imbuing the scene with an otherworldly quality. The tall grasses and wildflowers seemed to stretch up towards them like sentinels, their delicate petals swaying gently in the morning breeze. It was as if they had stumbled into a world from a child's nursery rhyme, a place where the boundaries between reality and fantasy blurred.

The riverbed dropped away beneath them, creating a sense of depth and perspective that drew their gazes upwards, towards the grasses and flowers that seemed to stretch up to the sky. Flambeau's eyes sparkled with delight as he exclaimed, "By Jove, it's like being in fairyland!" Father Brown, too, felt a sense of wonder wash over him, his eyes drinking in the beauty of the scene before them.

Their thoughts, however, were not solely consumed by the enchantment of their surroundings. Flambeau's mind was drawn back to the mysterious invitation that had sparked his decision to visit Prince Saradine. The visiting-card, with its enigmatic message and elegant script, had lingered in his memory for years, a reminder of a life left behind. He had always meant to investigate further, to learn more about the prince and his past, but the allure of adventure and the promise of the unknown had kept him from pursuing the matter.
