"What is it?" I asked the girl.

She pointed upward, her finger tracing a line through the violet haze.

I looked, and suspended above the temple, the courtyard, and the blooming gardens, shadowy forms danced silently. Suddenly, beams of blinding light fractured the dark, followed by the thunderous crackle of musketry. Flashes erupted from the temple walls, returning fire with frantic intensity.

"The Black Pirates of Barsoom, O Prince!" cried Thuvia.

The marauder vessels spiraled downward in widening circles, descending upon the defenders of the Therns. Wave after wave of gunfire vomited from the temple battlements, seeking the elusive fliers, while the pirates returned with equal ferocity. As the black crafts neared the earth, soldiers of the Therns flooded from the sanctuaries into the open grounds. This exposure drew scores of enemy flyers darting in from every angle. The Therns fired through shield-plates attached to their rifles, but relentlessly onward came the grim, ebony machines. Most were small, twin-seater skimmers, while a few larger motherships hovered high, raining bombs from keel-mounted batteries onto the holy precincts.

Finally, responding to a unseen command, the pirates surrounding us launched a reckless charge directly into the heart of the Thern lines. Before their engines could settle, the crews leaped from their craft, striking the ground warriors with the manic fury of demons. The combat that ensued was a spectacle of unparalleled violence. Under the silver gaze of Mars' dual moons, the scene burned with vivid clarity: golden-haired, pale-skinned Therns battling with desperate resolve against their ebony enemies.

Here, a cluster of stragglers trampled a bed of magnificent pimalia flowers; there, a black blade sought the heart of a defender beside a statue carved from living ruby; yonder, a dozen Therns pinned a lone pirate against a bench of emerald, inlaid with diamond patterns.

Beside the chaos stood Thuvia, the daughter of the Tharks, and myself. The tidal wave of battle spared us, yet the skirmishers swung close enough for intimate observation. The Black Pirates fascinated me. Rumors of them had haunted my memories since my first captivity on Mars, tales of a people dwelling on the lesser moon who descended only to unleash horror. They were said to carry away firearms, ammunition, and young girls as trophies, sacrificing the latter to a vile deity in orgies of cannibalism.

Now, standing amidst the carnage, I examined them closely. They were giants, towering over six feet, with features sharp and handsome. Their eyes were large and well-set, yet narrowed by a cunning slant; the irises were pitch-black, contrasting with unnaturally clear, white sclerae. Physically, they mirrored the Therns, the Red Men, and even myself, save for their skin—a polished, jet-black hue that, despite being alien, possessed a terrifying, majestic beauty.

Yet, if their bodies were divine, their souls were infernal. No malice for bloodshed equaled the madness with which these aerial demons tore into the Thern ranks. Around us lay the sinister craft, untouched by the defenders, a fact that puzzled me. Now and then, a black warrior would burst from the fray carrying a young woman in his arms, sprinting straight for his machine while comrades covered his retreat. Meanwhile, Therns rushed to rescue the captive, only to be swallowed instantly into a vortex of screaming fiends, hacking and hewing like incarnate spirits. Yet, invariably, the pirates prevailed, lifting their prizes unharmed into the outer darkness aboard their swift flyers.

Similar clashes echoed throughout the valley, a simultaneous assault along the ribbon-like domain of the Therns circling the Valley Dor. As the immediate fighting paused, Thuvia turned to me.

"Do you comprehend now, O Prince, why a million warriors guard the domains of the Holy Therns day and night?"

"This scene repeats itself, played out a score of times during my fifteen years of imprisonment," I replied. "From time immemorial, the Black Pirates have preyed upon the Therns. Yet, they never push their raids to the point of total extermination, as logic suggests they could. It seems they view the Therns merely as playthings, satisfying a ferocious bloodlust and collecting 'taxes' in weapons, ammo, and captives."

"Why do they not simply destroy the flying machines?" I asked, gesturing to the grounded fleet. "Eliminating them would end the attacks, or at least curb their audacity. Look how carelessly they leave their craft exposed, as if parked safely in a hangar."

"The Therns dare not," she answered solemnly. "Decades ago, they attempted to strike back. For a single moon thereafter, a thousand massive black battleships circled the Mountains of Otz, saturating the temples, gardens, and courts with projectiles. Every Thern who survived fled into the subterranean galleries. They know they live only by the pirates' sufferance. Having tasted near-extinction, they will not risk it again."

As she spoke, a new variable entered the battlefield, unforeseen by either foe. The great banths we had freed in the garden had initially held their ground, awed by the din of war and the thunder of rifles. But soon, enraged by the relentless noise and intoxicated by the scent of fresh blood, the leader of the pack erupted from the shrubbery, crashing into the melee. A primal scream tore from his throat as his talons found warm flesh.

His cry became a rallying cry for the rest. The entire herd lunged forward. Panic seized the field. Thern and pirate alike turned against the common enemy, for the beasts showed no favor. The massive brutes crushed hundreds under the sheer weight of their frames, mowing down warriors with powerful paws before turning to rend them with frightful fangs.

The scene was horrific, yet a spark of hope ignited within me. While the armies were distracted by the beasts, escape might finally be possible. I scanned the horizon for a breach in the lines. If we could reach the ramparts, perhaps the pirates had thinned the guard, leaving a path to freedom. My gaze fell upon the hundreds of airships scattered unguarded around us. It was obvious now—the simplest route to liberty lay not through the gates, but through the sky.

I was intimately familiar with every aircraft mechanic on Barsoom. For nine years, I had served in the Helium Navy, commanding the largest dreadnoughts and piloting agile scouts through the thin, deadly atmosphere. To think was to act. Seizing Thuvia by the arm, I signaled to Tars Tarkas to follow. We slipped toward a solitary, small flier parked at the edge of the clearing. In a heartbeat, we were crouched on its deck. My hand closed around the starting lever; my thumb pressed the button for the ray of repulsion, that Martian miracle allowing navigation in such slender air.

The craft shivered but remained stationary. Then, a cry of warning shattered the silence. Twirling around, I saw a dozen black pirates charging from the melee. Discovery. With shrieks of rage, they leaped for us. Frantically, I jammed the repulsion button, but the vessel refused to bud. The truth dawned: it was a two-man craft. The repulsion tanks held barely enough energy to lift two average men. Tars Tarkas, a mountain of muscle, anchored us to our doom.

The blacks were upon us. There was no room for hesitation. I slammed the button deep and locked it, cranked the lever to maximum speed, and as the assassins closed in, I leaped from the deck, drawing my longsword to meet them.

Simultaneously, a girl's shriek pierced the air behind me. An instant later, as the blades fell upon me, I caught Thuvia's faint, terrified voice echoing from above: "My Prince... I would rather remain and die with—" The words dissolved into the roar of combat.

I knew my ruse had bought them time. Thuvia and Tars Tarkas were safe, the key to escape secured. For a fleeting moment, the odds seemed insurmountable. A sea of black faces pressed in, but again, as countless times before, I found my earthly strength transcended theirs. The disparity was an illusion. My seething blade wove a web of death. They pressed close, their shorter swords flashing, but soon they recoiled, respect dawning on their furious countenances.

Yet, I knew it was a matter of minutes before their superior numbers overwhelmed me, bypassing my guard entirely. I would fall, surely, to certain death in this hellish garden, far from the love of Dejah Thoris, slain by nameless savages. A chill ran through me at the thought.

Then, the blood of my Virginian ancestors surged hot through my veins. The ancient spirit of the fighter reasserted itself. The lust for battle, the joy of the clash, washed over me. A smile of defiance touched my lips. Death faded from my mind, replaced by a fury that those who survived would remember forever.

I anticipated reinforcements, so even as I fought, my mind raced for an exit. It appeared from the shadows behind me. Just having disarmed a hulking brute, I had forced the pirates to pause, breathing heavily. They eyed me with malignant hatred, yet a flicker of awe lingered.
