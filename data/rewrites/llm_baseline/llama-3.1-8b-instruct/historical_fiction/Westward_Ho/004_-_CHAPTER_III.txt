It was no surprise, given the tumultuous events of the previous day, coupled with the enigmatic presence of John Oxenham that had left such an indelible mark on his psyche.

Throughout the evening, Amyas' mind had wrestled with the memories of the elusive adventurer, reenacting each glance and gesture in his mind's eye, searching for some hidden meaning or clue. And now, even in sleep, his subconscious continued to pursue the quest, transporting him to a fantastical realm where he sailed westward, relentlessly chasing the receding sun. Before him, a small sail billowed on the horizon, its identity unmistakable – John Oxenham's vessel. Yet, despite his urgent need to catch up, the ship remained tantalizingly out of reach, ensnared by the sargasso weed that choked her hull with its snaking tendrils. Amyas' frustration grew as the sun dipped below the waves, plunging the world into an inky darkness. The moon ascended, casting an eerie glow over the scene, and in that instant, John Oxenham's ship loomed before him, its sails tattered and its timbers worn, the very fabric of its being seeming to unravel. A macabre spectacle unfolded as Amyas beheld a row of lifeless forms dangling from the mainmast, their faces deathly pale. And then, the unblinking gaze of John Oxenham's corpse fixed upon him, its eyes burning with an otherworldly intensity as it beckoned and pointed – not towards the horizon, but backward, towards the route they had traveled. The direction sent a shiver coursing through Amyas' veins as he turned to behold the snow-capped peaks of the Andes rising in the distance, a poignant reminder that they were farther from home than ever, with the vast expanse of America stretching between them. The corpse continued to gesture, its lips contorted in a silent scream, as if urging Amyas to uncover some long-buried truth.
