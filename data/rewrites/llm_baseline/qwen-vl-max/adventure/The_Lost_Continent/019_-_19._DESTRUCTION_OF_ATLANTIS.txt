A trembling old priest approached me, placing a hand on my shoulder. I turned sharply—no time for interruptions. “Well?” I snapped.

“News has reached the Three,” he said, voice low but steady. “Of what is coming.”

I met his gaze with fire in mine. “Then they know I stand here now, ready to fight the greatest battle of my life. When it ends, I shall join the Gods, standing behind the stars to greet them when they too come. I regret only that they are too old and frail to die with honor—to witness such a fight themselves.”

He smiled faintly, a ghost of the warrior he once was. “They have given me orders, my King—ones I fear you will not like. You must not die in this battle. They still have need of you.”

I turned on him, anger flaring. “I will not obey such a command. It cannot be true. You’ve misheard. If I am man, priest, soldier, king—then my duty is clear: no enemy passes this gate while I live. Tell them their message can rot in the dust.”

The old man’s smile deepened. “I told them you would refuse. So I asked for a sign. And they gave one. This jewel—I was to give it to you.”

I stared at the gem in his hand—a bracelet once worn by Nais. “How did they get this?”

“They took it from her,” he said quietly. “And they told me she waits inside the Ark of the Mysteries. The Three’s purpose for you concerns her directly.”

Even then, I struggled. My pride screamed to stay and fight. But love for Nais won. I bowed my head, offering the formal salute—the mark of obedience.

To the remaining priests guarding the pass, I said farewell. “You will have what I shall miss,” I told them. “I envy you this final stand. Yet though I am King of Atlantis, I am also one of the Seven—and thus bound to serve the Three. Their word is the will of the High Gods. You will reach the stars before I do. Please… speak for me when you meet Them. It is not my choice to fall behind.”

They saluted me with their weapons, solemn and respectful. Then we parted.

I did not see the battle—but I knew it was fierce. Time stretched before Phorenice’s horde broke through onto the mountain crest. The last defenders fought hard—old men, yes, but trained warriors. They bought time with their lives, trading breath for blood.

With a heavy heart, I climbed toward the summit, winding past temples, colleges, and homes clinging to the slopes of the Sacred Mountain. I passed beyond the ancient boundary—once forbidden ground. But now, conventions meant nothing. My temper burned hot. I might have stormed forward without hesitation, had I not been stopped.

At the temple marking the edge, old Zaemon waited. With grave ritual, he sought divine permission for my trespass. He refused to move until the ceremony was complete.

Below, Phorenice’s tower and bridge were in place. The clash of arms echoed—the last few priests held the gate, fighting like lions against an avalanche. They were seasoned fighters, but outnumbered and outmatched. Soon, the mountain would be breached.

The sea surged violently. Waves crashed against distant cliffs. Fires erupted from the mountains. And the water—unrelenting—rose up the great estuary, flooding the land, swallowing harbors, creeping into the city itself.

Zaemon gestured, his voice thin but sharp. “Look!”

I followed his finger. A chill ran through me. The sun blazed overhead. The sky was blue. But beneath us, chaos reigned. The sea roared, never retreating. The lower lands vanished under churning waters. The city of Atlantis—gone.

“You see?” Zaemon asked.

“I see.”

“You understand?”

“In part.”

“Then let me tell you all. This is the beginning. The end follows fast. The High Gods, who dwell beyond the stars, have exhausted Their patience. Atlantis, the continent beyond, all its people—doomed. Annihilation is upon us.”

He spoke of prophecy—an ancient decree. Long ago, the Gods foresaw this doom. To preserve knowledge, the Ark of the Mysteries was built. Impenetrable. Immovable. Inside: scrolls of wisdom, grain, water, seeds, weapons—all for two survivors.

One woman already waits within. She is my daughter. But the choice of the man rests with you.”

I froze. “I don’t wish to survive.”

“You are not asked,” Zaemon said. “You are commanded. The High Gods know you as Their servant. You will obey.”

Habit overruled doubt. I bowed, repeating the sacred words. “I hear. I obey.”

Then came the rite—the raising to the rank of the Three. No temple. No incense. Only sky and silence. But the elders performed every step with reverence, unshaken by the world collapsing around them.

Mountains exploded. Fires roared. Phorenice’s army broke through the gate, streaming across the mountain in frenzy. Yet Zaemon and his companion continued—words precise, movements deliberate.

I stood bareheaded, sweat on my brow, mind torn between ritual and reality. A continent drowned. My father died in silence. Nais waited. And I—was becoming something greater than a king.

But time was running out.

Finally, Zaemon said, “Kneel. Bow to your mother, the Earth. Swear by the High Gods you will never misuse the power now granted.”

I knelt. I swore.

Then he raised me, fully initiated. “You will not train with us,” he said. “We go to meet the Gods. Our account is due. What you remember may save you. We bid you farewell. We will commend you to the divine court.”

He pointed to the Ark. “Enter. Shut the door if they try to break in. You and Nais are the only ones chosen to live. Do not fight. Not even in defense. But look upon Atlantis as she dies. Bear witness.”

I climbed the battens, entered the Ark, descended the inner stair.

Nais sat waiting. Her eyes shone with fear and hope.

“Zaemon forced me here,” she whispered. “Threats. Promises. He said you would come. He’s harsh—but I think he loves us both.”

“Can we save him?” I asked.

“He will not come. The Ark was built for one man and one woman. That is law. He has a duty—to deliver our story to the Gods. And he’ll not waste a moment.”

I left her, stepped back onto the roof.

Men ran wild on the mountain top. The sea swallowed everything. Cities, plains, forests—vanished. Only peaks remained, erupting in fury. Trees twisted in whirlpools. Mud bubbled. Life ended.

Then—movement.

A small group emerged from the ruins. Phorenice, carried in a golden litter, surrounded by guards. Her slaves bore her aloft. Tatho marched beside her, proud and gaudy. Ylga walked close—calm, composed.

They halted twenty paces from the Ark.

“Do not approach those foul old men,” Phorenice said. “They reek of death. We lack perfume to mask it.”

She looked up at me. “Truly, a quiet gathering of old friends. Deucalion—how odd to see you again. Once I tasted you… and discarded you when you grew stale.”

“I have Nais,” I said. “Soon, we will be the last alive.”

“Nais is welcome,” she laughed. “I’ll watch your country cooings from the stars. You’re so rustic, Deucalion. They say the old men crowned you king. Did it swell your ego? Or did you remember there’s still an Empress—so long as Atlantis exists?”

“Save your jests,” I said. “I won’t answer a woman who will soon die.”

“Bah! You’ll live in the past. Don’t they know I hold the Great Secret? I am a Goddess now. I can make life eternal.”

“The sea will test that,” I replied.

But before more could be said, Zaemon stepped forward, holding the Symbol of the Sun.

He cursed Phorenice—his voice raw with wrath. The roar of the flood rose, but he spoke clearly.

Phorenice ordered her guards to kill him. Even tried to rise and strike herself.

But Zaemon held the Symbol high. Its light flared. The power of the Gods overwhelmed her magic.

She fell back, humbled. Defeated.

Only Ylga moved. She stepped forward, leaned against the Ark, and called to me through the storm.

“I wish I could save you,” I said.

She smiled. “I wouldn’t come, even if you begged. I demand all or nothing. But I’ll face death knowing you’ll carry me in your memory. I know men, Deucalion—even men like you. You’ll never forget how close you came to loving me.”

I think she said more—but the waves drowned her voice.

A thick mist rose from the burning mountains. Flames lit the clouds like gold.

I saw soldiers rush up the slope—only to be swept back by surging water.

Then—Zaemon waved the Symbol. A silent command.

I obeyed. I went down the stairs. I shut the door.

Bolts locked. Silence.

Nais and I—alone.

Outside, blows rang against the Ark. Men climbed the sides, wrenched at the door.

The end drew near.

Then—surge. The Ark lifted, trembled, dropped.

Blows ceased below. But above—frenzied pounding.

Screams echoed—dull, muffled.

Another surge—thunderous—lifted the Ark like a leaf. Spun it. Then—no land. No rock.

We floated on endless, furious seas.

The lamp fell. Darkness swallowed us.

And in that dark, I clung to Nais—knowing we were the only survivors of a lost world.

No more Atlantis. Only the ocean. The sky. And the weight of what we had seen.
