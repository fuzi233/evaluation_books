The air was alive with the lilting strains of a lively tune, one that stirred the blood and set the heart a-thumping. Miss Keeldar's eyes sparkled with excitement as she listened, her gaze darting from one face to the next, as if daring anyone to deny the thrill of the music. Old Helstone, catching her eye, couldn't help but chuckle, and she laughed in response, her voice like a warm breeze on a summer's day.

"Not a battle cry, nor a call to arms," he said with a grin, "just a pleasant stroll through the countryside. Our country's at peace, and there's no need for us to take up arms. Let's keep our spirits high, but not our passions ablaze." He winked at her, and she playfully rolled her eyes.

Caroline leaned over to whisper, "I think she's trying to stir up trouble, Miss Keeldar. She's got a look in her eye that says she'd rather be anywhere else." Shirley just smiled mischievously and murmured back, "I'll borrow from my imagination what reality won't provide. We're Lowlanders on a mission to Palestine, or perhaps Covenanters bound for a secret meeting in the hills. Whatever the truth, the music's got me feeling alive, and I'm ready to defend my faith, my land, or even a lover's heart."

Just then, Caroline pointed ahead, her finger tracing the path of a small red speck on the horizon. "Look, Shirley! What's that above Stilbro' Brow?" Miss Keeldar raised her gaze and followed the line of her friend's finger. "It's a cluster of riders, cavalry, and they're heading straight for us. But wait, they've veered off to the right, avoiding our procession. Where do you think they're bound?"

Mr. Helstone intervened, his voice calm and measured. "We'll take the shortcut through Royd Lane to reach Nunnely Common. It's a narrow path, but it'll save us time." And with that, the procession filed into the lane, their footsteps echoing off the stone walls.

As they made their way down the lane, the tension grew thicker than the air itself. Boultby's spectacles quivered, and Helstone's Rehoboam seemed to vibrate in his hands. The curates exchanged nervous glances, their faces pale. Mr. Hall turned to the ladies and smiled reassuringly.
