Since Mirdath the Beautiful left this world and cast me into solitude, I have known a torment beyond words—an endless, gnawing hunger for her presence. She was my joy, my world, and without her, existence became a bleak and hollow thing. Yet now, a strange hope stirs within me. In dreams, I have awakened into the far future of this Earth, where wonders and horrors beyond imagining unfold beneath an eternal night. There, in the womb of Time, she and I shall meet again, part, and reunite across unfathomable ages.

This is the tale of what I have seen—visions so vivid they are more memory than dream. I set them down now, both to ease my own heart and perhaps to offer solace to others who suffer as I have. Some will call it madness; others, a fable. To them, I say only: *Read.* And in reading, glimpse with me the edge of eternity.

When I stepped into that future, it was not as a dreamer, but as one who wakes to a new reality. The sun had died long ago, and the Earth lay swathed in everlasting darkness. To my awakened mind, *this* present age—our world of light—seemed but a distant, half-remembered dream.

At first, a grey haze clouded my vision, then dissolved like mist, revealing the Night Land in all its terrible splendor. I stood within an embrasure of the Last Redoubt—a monstrous pyramid of metal, the final fortress of mankind against the horrors outside. I was no longer the grieving man of this age, but a youth of seventeen, steeped in the knowledge of that far time.

And yet, beneath that knowledge lay another: the faint, aching memory of *this* life, and of Mirdath.

Through a viewing-glass, I gazed upon the nightmare landscape beyond the pyramid’s walls:

- To the **North-West**, the **Red Pit** burned, its glare illuminating the chin of the **North-West Watcher**—a colossus that had stood sentinel since the dawn of time.
- To the **East**, a sound echoed now and then—deep, thunderous laughter from the **Country Whence Comes the Great Laughter**, a place no living soul had seen.
- The **Giants’ Pit** seethed with shadowy figures, crawling beneath the flickering light of the **Giants’ Kilns**.
- The **Headland From Which Strange Things Peer** loomed black and ominous, its edges alive with unseen watchers.
- The **Road Where the Silent Ones Walk** stretched into the darkness, empty save for a single, hooded figure drifting soundlessly along its path.

And in the **South**, the greatest terror of all: the **Watcher of the South**, a living mountain of flesh that had crept closer over millennia, only to be halted by the rise of the **Glowing Dome**—a pale, mysterious barrier.

The **Great Redoubt** was a city unto itself, its 1,320 floors housing the last millions of humanity. Its walls were sealed, its lower depths forbidden, and its power drawn from the dwindling **Earth-Current**. For ages beyond counting, mankind had endured, besieged by forces both monstrous and unseen.

Some whispered that not all powers in that darkness were evil—that unseen allies worked against the horrors. The **Glowing Dome**, the **Silent Ones**, even the halting of the South Watcher hinted at forces beyond human understanding.

How can I convey the magnitude of that age? Our histories stretch back mere thousands of years; theirs spanned *millions*. The oldest records spoke of the **Days of Darkening**, when mankind’s own arrogance had torn the veil between worlds, allowing abominations to enter. The Redoubt was built as a final stronghold, and though wars had raged, humanity endured—watching, waiting, knowing that one day the Earth-Current would fail.

And so they lived, generation upon generation, gazing into the eternal night, while beyond the walls, things older than reason hungered.

In that moment, I was both the youth of the future and the man of the present. I knew the Redoubt as my home, yet my heart ached for Mirdath, whose memory lingered like a ghost in the depths of my soul.

And so I listened—as I listen still—for the echo of her voice across the ages.
