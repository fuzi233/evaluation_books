Twelve years had elapsed since I interred the remains of my great-uncle, Captain John Carter of Virginia, within the silent, sun-drenched mausoleum of the old Richmond cemetery. In those quiet intervals, I had frequently dissected the peculiar testamentary instructions he left behind regarding his final resting place: specifically, the mandate that he lie exposed in an open casket, and the intricate mechanical contrivance designed so that the massive bolts sealing the vault's colossal door could be manipulated solely from the interior—a paradoxical safeguard suggesting a consciousness that refused total oblivion.

Twelve years had also passed since I committed to paper the extraordinary manuscript of this enigmatic figure, a man whose biography defied linear chronology. He possessed no recollection of infancy, offering only a vague estimation of his age, yet he carried the vitality of youth while having once cradled my grandfather's great-grandfather upon his knee. This warrior had spent a decade traversing the alien landscapes of Mars, engaging in a dualistic struggle against both the verdant Martians and the crimson-skinned Tharks. Through these conflicts, he had secured the hand of Dejah Thoris, Princess of Helium, and for nearly a decade had reigned as a prince within the House of Tardos Mors, the Jeddak of Helium itself.

Now, twelve years after his body was discovered upon the bluff overlooking the Hudson, a shadow of uncertainty lingered in my mind. Had John Carter truly perished, or did he continue to wander the desiccated seabeds of that dying world? Had he returned to Barsoom only to discover that he arrived too late, finding the frowning portals of the atmospheric plant already sealed, condemning countless millions to suffocation on that catastrophic day that hurled him ruthlessly through forty-eight million miles of space back to Earth? Perhaps he sought his dark-haired Princess and the slender son he had dreamt awaited him in the royal gardens of Tardos Mors, only to find a living death upon a dead world. Was he truly gone forever, severed from both Mother Earth and his beloved Mars?

Thus lost in these futile speculations on a sultry August evening, old Ben, my body servant, approached with a telegram. My fingers trembled as I tore it open. The message was stark: *'Meet me tomorrow, Hotel Raleigh, Richmond.'* Signed simply, *JOHN CARTER*.

Early the following dawn, I boarded the first train for Richmond. Within two hours, I was ushered into the room occupied by the man I thought dead. As I crossed the threshold, he rose to greet me, his face illuminated by an old-time cordiality that seemed to banish the shadows of mortality. He appeared untouched by the passage of time, retaining the straight, clean-limbed physique of a fighter in his prime. His keen grey eyes burned with undimmed intensity; the only markings upon his visage were the etched lines of iron character and unyielding determination that had defined him since I first knew him nearly thirty-five years prior.

"Well, nephew," he greeted me, his voice resonating with familiar warmth, "do you feel as though you are witnessing a specter, or merely suffering the lingering effects of too many of Uncle Ben's juleps?"

"Juleps, I reckon," I replied, steadying my breathing, "for I certainly feel mighty good; but perhaps it is merely the sight of you again that stirs such emotion. Have you returned to Mars? Tell me. And Dejah Thoris? Did you find her well, awaiting your embrace?"

"Yes, I have returned to Barsoom again," he answered, a somber weight settling in his tone. "But it is a tale too vast to recount within the fleeting moments I possess before I must depart. I have uncovered the secret, nephew, and I may now traverse the trackless void at my will, moving freely between the countless planets as I choose. Yet, my heart remains anchored in Barsoom. While it resides there, in the keeping of my Martian Princess, I doubt I shall ever again abandon the dying world that constitutes my very life."

"I have come now because my affection for you compelled me to see you once more before you pass forever into that other life which I shall never know," he continued, his gaze piercing. "And though I have died thrice and shall die again tonight—as you know death better than any mortal—I remain as unable to fathom its mechanics as you are. Even the wise and mysterious Therns of Barsoom, that ancient cult credited with guarding the secrets of life and death within their impregnable fastnesses upon the hither slopes of the Mountains of Otz, are as ignorant as we. I have proven this truth, though I nearly forfeited my life in the process. You shall read everything in the notes I have compiled during the last three months of my return to Earth."

He gestured to a swelling portfolio lying on the table beside him. "I know you are intrigued and convinced, and I know the world shares this interest, though they will not believe for many years—perhaps many ages, for they lack the capacity to comprehend. Men of Earth have not yet evolved to a point where they can grasp the truths written in those notes."

"Give them what you deem fit, what you believe will not harm them," he instructed gently. "But do not feel aggrieved if they mock you."

That night, I walked down to the cemetery with him. At the threshold of his vault, he paused and pressed my hand firmly. "Good-bye, nephew," he said softly. "I may never see you again, for I doubt I can bear to leave my wife and boy while they live. Furthermore, the span of life upon Barsoom often exceeds a thousand years."

He stepped inside. The great door swung slowly shut, the ponderous bolts grating into place with a sound like grinding bones, until the lock clicked with finality. I have never seen Captain John Carter of Virginia since.

Yet, here is the account of his return to Mars on that subsequent occasion, gleaned from the immense mass of notes he left upon the table of his room in the Hotel Raleigh. Much has been omitted; much I have not dared to reveal. However, you will find the story of his second quest for Dejah Thoris, Princess of Helium, even more remarkable than the first manuscript I presented to an unbelieving world some time ago, through which we followed the fighting Virginian across the dead sea bottoms beneath the moons of Mars.
