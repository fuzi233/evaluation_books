As twilight descended upon the grand estate, casting long shadows across the manicured lawns, Gwendolyn Deronda stood poised at the threshold of a momentous decision. A day had passed since her mother's enigmatic message, summoning him to a second audience, and now, on the third morning, he had received a curt instruction to attend her at once. He entered the same dimly lit chamber as before, but the atmosphere was heavy with an air of resignation, the blinds and curtains drawn to shield the Princess from the world beyond.

Gwendolyn emerged from the shadows, her dark orange silk wrap a striking contrast to the somber hues of the room. Her eyes, larger and more vibrant in the subdued light, seemed to hold a deep sadness, as if the weight of her years bore down upon her like an unyielding burden. Her arms, bare beneath her flowing sleeves, stretched out to claim her son, and she pressed her lips to his cheeks with a tender urgency. As she settled into her cushions, her bearing was that of a woman resolved, her eyes flashing with a quiet determination.

Deronda took his seat beside her, his voice barely above a whisper as he asked, "Are you quite recovered, Mother?" The Princess's response was measured, her words chosen with the deliberation of a queen, rather than the fervor of a mother. "I am at peace," she said, her voice a soothing balm to the turmoil that churned within him.

The conversation turned to the past, and Deronda's inquiry about the house in Genoa where his grandfather had lived drew a dismissive wave of her hand. "It is gone, destroyed, leaving no trace behind." Yet, she offered a glimmer of hope, pointing him towards the chest filled with family papers, where he might uncover the secrets of their lineage. "You will find all that I am, all that I have been," she said, her words dripping with a sense of detachment.

Deronda struggled to conceal the pain that threatened to overwhelm him, his voice barely steady as he asked, "Is there anything else you wish to share with me?" The Princess's response was laced with a cold calculation, as if she had carefully scripted her words to spare herself the agony of reliving the past. "I have told you all that is necessary," she said, her eyes clouded with a mixture of regret and resolve.
