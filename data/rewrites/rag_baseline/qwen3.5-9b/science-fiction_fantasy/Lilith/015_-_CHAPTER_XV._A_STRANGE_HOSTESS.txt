The moon hung full and oppressive overhead, a celestial body I had never witnessed in my previous existence. Tonight, as it descended toward the western rim, I perceived a distortion in its surface—a faint, mocking smile etched onto its cratered visage. Just as its lower edge dipped beneath the horizon, a vision superimposed itself upon the lunar disk: a solitary cottage, glowing with an internal luminescence that seemed to emanate from within its open door and window. The image brought a sudden, visceral certainty—that I was summoned there.

Instantly, the moon vanished, swallowed by the encroaching darkness. The cottage dissolved with it. I found myself stranded in a landscape defined by a relentless succession of shallow ravines. With no path visible and the night thickening into absolute obscurity, I decided to halt. I settled into a sandy depression, consumed the dried fruits the children had gifted me before our separation, and succumbed to exhaustion.

I awoke abruptly, staring upward at constellations alien to my former world. After a moment of stunned observation, I registered the presence of a figure seated on the ground, positioned slightly above and beyond me. Startled, I realized I was no longer alone. The silhouette loomed between me and the starfield, appearing unnaturally large due to my low vantage point.

It shifted its posture, and only then did I discern that its back was turned toward me.

"Will you not come with me?" a voice intoned—sweet, mellow, and unmistakably female.

Desiring to gauge the nature of this hostess, I responded calmly. "I thank you, but I am not uncomfortable here. Where would you have me go? I prefer the open air."

"There is no harm in the air," she replied, "but the creatures that roam these wastes at night are not companions a man would wish to encounter while sleeping."

"I have not been disturbed," I stated.

"No," she confirmed. "I have been sitting by you since you lay down."

"That is remarkably considerate. How did you locate me? Why extend such favor?"

"I saw you," she answered, maintaining her position with her back exposed. "In the fading moonlight, just as she set. My vision is impaired during daylight, but perfect at night. The shadow of my dwelling would have concealed you, yet both doors stood ajar. I was out on the waste when I spotted you entering this hollow. You were already asleep before I could approach, and I chose not to disturb you. People panic if I appear suddenly. They call me the Cat-Woman. It is not my name."

My memory flashed back to the children's warnings: *she is ugly, and she scratches.* Yet, her voice possessed a gentle cadence, tinged with apology. She could not be a malevolent giantess.

"You shall not hear it from me," I assured her. "Please, tell me what I may address you as!"

"When you truly know me, call me by the name that fits best," she replied. "That choice reveals your own nature. Few grant me the correct title; it is fortunate when they do."

"I suppose, Madam, you reside in the cottage I glimpsed in the heart of the moon?"

"Yes. I dwell there alone, save for visitors. It is a humble place, but I do my utmost for my guests. Sometimes, their sleep becomes sweet."

Her voice resonated within me, inducing a profound sense of stillness. "I will accompany you, Madam," I said, rising.

She stood instantly, leading the way without a backward glance. I could barely distinguish her form, yet I managed to follow. She towered over me, though not as impossibly high as I had anticipated. Her refusal to turn her face toward me sparked curiosity rather than dread; her voice rang with such authenticity that apprehension remained absent. However, attempting to walk alongside her proved futile. When I quickened my stride, she accelerated effortlessly, maintaining a commanding lead.

A creeping unease began to take root. Why such vigilance regarding visibility? Extraordinary deformity might explain it—a fear of terrifying me. A horror of inconceivable monstrosity assailed my imagination. Was I traversing the darkness toward an unheard-of hideousness? Regret washed over me for accepting her hospitality.

Silence stretched between us, becoming intolerable. I *must* break it.

"I seek a destination I have heard of, but whose name remains unknown to me. Perhaps you can identify it?"

"Describe it, and I shall direct you. The Stupid Bags know nothing, and the Careless Little Lovers forget almost everything."

"Where do those dwell?"

"You have just arrived from them!"

"I never encountered those designations before!"

"You would not have heard them. No one knows their own name!"

"Remarkable!"

"Perhaps so! Yet hardly anyone anywhere knows their true name! Imagine the reaction of a distinguished gentleman hearing himself addressed by his actual designation!"

I fell silent, contemplating the possibility.

"What do you currently imagine your name to be?" she continued, seemingly anticipating my thoughts. "Pardon me, it is trivial."

I opened my mouth to respond, only to discover that my name had evaporated from my mind entirely. I could not even recall its initial letter! This marked the second instance of being queried on my identity and failing to provide an answer.

"Never mind," she dismissed lightly. "It is unnecessary. Your true name is inscribed upon your forehead, but presently it swirls chaotically, unreadable to all. I shall assist in steadying it. Soon it will slow, and hopefully, settle permanently."

Startled, I fell quiet.

We departed the channels, walking for a considerable duration without sighting the cottage.

"The Little Ones spoke to me," I said finally, "of a smooth, verdant country, pleasant to the feet!"

"Yes?"

"They also mentioned a girl-giantess who rules somewhere. Is that her domain?"

"There exists a city within that grassy expanse where a woman holds the title of Princess. The city is named Bulika. However, the Princess is certainly not a girl. She predates this world and originated from yours, carrying a terrible history that remains unresolved. She is an evil entity who wields significant influence over the Prince of the Power of the Air. Originally, the people of Bulika were simple agrarians, tilling soil and grazing sheep. She arrived among them, met with hospitality, and instructed them to excavate diamonds and opals for trade, abandoning agriculture and pastoralism to construct a city. On one occasion, they slew a colossal serpent, an act that enraged her so profoundly that she proclaimed herself their Princess and became a terror to them. At that time, the nation was known as THE LAND OF WATERS; the dry channels I have traversed were then flooded with raging torrents, and the valley now occupied by the Bags and Lovers was a vast lake receiving their currents. But the wicked Princess gathered the available water into her lap, sealing it within an egg and fleeing. Her lap could contain only half the volume; the moment she departed, the remainder fled underground, leaving the country arid and dusty, mirroring her own heart. Without the subterranean aquifers, all life would have perished millennia ago, for without water, there is no rain, and without rain, no springs. Since then, the Princess has resided in Bulika, keeping her subjects in perpetual terror while stifling their population growth. Yet, they boast of prosperity, convinced of their own satisfaction—they excel in commerce, both honest and deceitful; united by shared interests yet treacherous when they diverge; proud of their sovereign and her power, despising those they outmaneuver; never doubting their inherent honor, each believing themselves superior to all others. The depth of their worthlessness and the height of their arrogance defy comprehension unless one has visited, unless one has learned to recognize these miserable, misgoverned, self-deluded creatures."

"I thank you, Madam. Pray, enlighten me concerning the Little Ones—the Lovers. I yearn to serve them. Who are they? What is their nature? How did they come to be here? These children represent the greatest marvel I have encountered in this world of wonders."

"In Bulika, you may find illumination. There is an ancient poem in the palace library, reportedly unreadable by any there, yet clearly stating that after enduring great tribulations and learning their true names, the Lovers will fill the land and enslave the giants."

"By that time, they will have matured somewhat, will they not?" I asked.

"Yes, they will have grown; yet I suspect they will not have grown. It is possible to grow without growing, to diminish while expanding, simultaneously—even to grow through the mechanism of stagnation!"

"Your words are peculiar, Madam!" I countered. "Yet I have heard that some terms, because they signify more, paradoxically appear to signify less!"

"That is accurate, and such words *must* be understood. It would benefit the Princess of Bulika greatly if she heard what the very silence of her land shouts in her ears daily! But she is far too cunning to comprehend anything."

"Then I presume, when the Little Lovers mature, their land will regain its waters?"

"Not precisely. When they thirst sufficiently, they will possess water; and when they possess water, they will grow. Growth requires hydration. Beneath, the flow continues."
