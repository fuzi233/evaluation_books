The sound of the prison cart wheels rattled against the cobblestones, a jagged rhythm that shattered the silence of the Council Chamber. Beside me, my brother was dragged away, his form reduced to a trembling line of ink in my vision, stripped of the dignity of his existence. A surge of desperate volition seized me; I lunged forward, my limbs straining to intercept the guards, to offer a plea or at least a final word of comfort. But my body refused to obey. I was paralyzed, a statue of stone within a living frame. My Guide stood impassive, his voice dripping with a cold, gloomy finality. "Heed not thy brother," he commanded, his tone devoid of empathy. "Haply thou shalt have ample time hereafter to condole with him. Follow me."

With a heaviness that pressed against my soul, I was compelled to rise. We ascended once more into the vast, boundless expanse of Space. Until this moment, my education had been confined to the flat, deceptive simplicity of Plane Figures and their interiors. Now, the Sphere prepared to dismantle my limited perception. He gestured to a chaotic array of movable square cards scattered before us. "Behold," he instructed, his voice echoing with the authority of absolute truth. "See how I place one card upon another—not Northward, as you are accustomed to navigate Flatland, but *upon* it. Layer by layer, I build a Solid. Observe how these squares, parallel to one another, stack until the figure becomes as high as it is long and broad. Thus is the Cube born."

"Pardon me, my Lord," I stammered, my eyes struggling to reconcile the visual data with my ingrained logic. "But to my eye, this appears nothing but an irregular Plane Figure, its interior laid bare for inspection. I see no Solid, only a monstrous, gaping wound—a shape so grotesque it causes physical pain to my sight."

"True," the Sphere conceded gently. "It appears to you as a Plane simply because you lack the faculty of Light, Shade, and Perspective. Just as a Hexagon appears to you as a mere Straight Line unless you possess the Art of Sight Recognition, so too does the Solid deceive the uninitiated eye. Yet, in reality, it is a Solid, a truth you shall verify through the sense of Feeling."

He guided my hand to touch the construct. Instantly, the deception vanished. The sensation confirmed what the eye could not grasp: I was touching a being composed of six distinct planes and eight terminal points, the solid angles where the faces met. I recalled the Sphere's earlier lesson—that a Square moving parallel to itself in Space generates exactly such a creature—and a profound joy surged through me. Even a humble Square like myself could be the progenitor of such an illustrious offspring. Yet, the concepts of light, shade, and perspective remained elusive abstractions. I dared not hide my confusion.

Were I to recount the Sphere's subsequent explanation in full detail, it would seem tedious to any native of Space who already possesses this knowledge. Suffice it to say that through his lucid demonstrations, shifting the positions of objects and sources of illumination, and allowing me to physically feel both the external surfaces and his own sacred person, he finally bridged the gap. I learned to distinguish clearly between a Circle and a Sphere, between a Plane Figure and a Solid. This revelation marked the climax, the paradise, of my strange and eventful history.

From this point onward, my narrative shifts from ascent to a miserable fall—a fall most unjust, yet inevitable. Why should the thirst for knowledge be ignited only to be extinguished by punishment? My volition recoils from the painful task of reliving my humiliation, yet, driven by a spirit akin to Prometheus, I shall endure this torment if it serves to awaken a rebellious consciousness within the interiors of Planar and Solid humanity. Let us discard all personal considerations. I shall proceed to the end of my tale, following the plain path of dispassionate history, recording the exact facts and the precise words burned into my memory, allowing my readers to judge between me and Destiny.

The Sphere, eager to continue his indoctrination into the conformations of all regular solids—cylinders, cones, pyramids, pentahedrons, hexahedrons, dodecahedrons, and spheres—ventured to pause. I interrupted him, not from weariness, but from an insatiable hunger for deeper truths. "Pardon me," I said, lowering my head in reverence despite my audacity, "O Thou Whom I must no longer address as the Perfection of all Beauty. Let me beg thee to vouchsafe thy servant a sight of thine interior."

"My what?" the Sphere demanded, startled.

"Thine interior: thy stomach, thy intestines."

"Whence this ill-timed impertinent request?" he snapped, his composure cracking slightly. "And what mean you by saying that I am no longer the Perfection of all Beauty?"

"My Lord," I pleaded, rising to the challenge, "your own wisdom has taught me to aspire to One even greater, more beautiful, and more perfectly approximating to Perfection than yourself. As you, superior to all Flatland forms, combine many Circles into One, so doubtless there exists One above you who combines many Spheres into a Supreme Existence, surpassing even the Solids of Spaceland. Just as we, now in Space, look down upon Flatland and perceive the insides of all things, so of a certainty there lies a higher, purer region above us. Whither thou dost surely intend to lead me—O Thou whom I shall always call my Priest, Philosopher, and Friend—is some yet more spacious Space, a dimensionality beyond the reach of current comprehension. From that vantage point, we shall look down together upon the revealed insides of Solid things, where thine own intestines, and those of thy kin, will lie exposed to the view of the poor wandering exile from Flatland, to whom so much has already been granted."

"Pooh! Stuff!" the Sphere scoffed, regaining his stern demeanor. "Enough of this trifling! Time is short, and much remains to be done before you are fit to proclaim the Gospel of Three Dimensions to your blind, benighted countrymen in Flatland."

"Nay, gracious Teacher, deny me not what I know is in thy power to perform," I insisted, desperation coloring my voice. "Grant me but one glimpse of thine interior, and I am satisfied forever, remaining henceforth thy docile pupil, thy unemancipable slave, ready to receive all thy teachings and to feed upon the words that fall from thy lips."

"Well, then, to content and silence you," the Sphere relented, "let me say at once: I would show you what you wish if I could; but I cannot. Would you have me turn my stomach inside out to oblige you?"

"But my Lord has shown me the intestines of all my countrymen in the Land of Two Dimensions by taking me with him into the Land of Three!" I argued, seizing the precedent. "What is more difficult now than to take his servant on a second journey into the blessed region of the Fourth Dimension? There, I shall look down with him upon this land of Three Dimensions, seeing the inside of every three-dimensional house, the secrets of the solid earth, the treasures of the mines in Spaceland, and the intestines of every solid living creature, even of the noble and adorable Spheres."

"But where is this land of Four Dimensions?" the Sphere asked, genuinely puzzled.

"I know not," I admitted humbly, "but doubtless my Teacher knows."

"Not I," the Sphere declared firmly. "There is no such land. The very idea of it is utterly inconceivable."

"Not inconceivable, my Lord, to me, and therefore still less inconceivable to my Master," I countered, my conviction hardening. "Nay, I despair not that, even here, in this region of Three Dimensions, your Lordship's art may render the Fourth Dimension visible to me. Recall the past: Was I not taught below that when I saw a Line and inferred a Plane, I in reality perceived a Third, unrecognized Dimension, not identical to brightness, but called 'height'? Does it not now follow that, in this region, when I see a Plane and infer a Solid, I truly perceive a Fourth, unrecognized Dimension, not the same as color, but existent, though infinitesimal and incapable of direct measurement?"

"And besides," I pressed, "there is the Argument from Analogy of Figures."

"Analogy! Nonsense: what analogy?" the Sphere retorted impatiently.

"Your Lordship tempts his servant to test whether he remembers the revelations imparted to him," I said, my voice gaining strength. "Trifle not with me, my Lord; I crave, I thirst, for more knowledge. Doubtless we cannot *see* that other higher Spaceland now, because we have no eye in our stomachs. But, just as there *was* the realm of Flatland, though that poor, puny Lineland Monarch could neither turn left nor right to discern it, and just as there *was*, close at hand and touching my frame, the land of Three Dimensions, though I, blind senseless wretch, had no power to touch it, no eye in my interior to discern it... so of a surety there is a Fourth Dimension, which my Lord perceives with the inner eye of thought. And that it must exist, my Lord himself has taught me. Or can he have forgotten what he himself imparted to his servant?"
