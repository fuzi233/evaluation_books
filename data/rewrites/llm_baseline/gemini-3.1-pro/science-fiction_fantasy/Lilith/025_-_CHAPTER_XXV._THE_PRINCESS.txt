Making a circuit of the castle, I allowed the shifting acoustic ecology of the grounds to guide me. The wind through the towering, poplar-like trees in the paved court provided a rhythmic, organic susurration that stood in stark contrast to the oppressive, heavy silence of the ravine-like moat I had just crossed. Between the immense trunks, I caught glimpses of the palace. Its architecture—long, low, crowned with lofty towers and a central dome—spoke of an ancient Indian origin, a multi-generational edifice whose original builders’ language had long since faded into the earth. 

The main entrance, a low semi-elliptical arch, stood open. Unchallenged, I stepped into a vast hall. Here, the natural reverberations of the outside world were abruptly severed by the harsh, discordant clink of industrial steel. Toward one side couched a huge leopardess, white with dark oval spots. She was chained by a heavy steel collar, her mouth muzzled and her paws muffled—a brutal, manufactured silencing of a creature meant to move with the silent grace of the wild. Her canoe-shaped pupils and great green irids stared at me, though her body remained as rigid as an iron bar. 

From this vestibule branched a labyrinth of irregular passages. Squeezing through a narrow corridor, a page collided with me. He recoiled in terror, but soon gathered an unearned impudence, puffing his chest to demand my business in a shrill, grating tone that entirely lacked the resonant depth of native courtesy. 

“To see the princess,” I answered. 

“A likely thing!” he scoffed. “I have not seen her highness this morning myself!”

I caught him by the back of the neck. “Take me to her at once, or I will drag you with me till I find her. She shall know how her servants receive her visitors.”

He yielded, pulling me like a blind man’s dog toward the palace kitchens. I had expected the warm, rhythmic percussion of a functioning agrarian hearth—the grounding sounds of mortar, pestle, and chopping blade—but I found only a lethargic assembly of servants, feebly engaged in disjointed, sterile routines. The erosion of their culinary memory was palpable; the air smelled of cold ash rather than toasted spices or blooming yeast. Yet, they did not attack me. Instead, their eyes widened in ghastly terror at something behind me. I turned to find the white leopardess at my heel.

As one servant began to recover from the shock, I released the page. “Take me to the princess.”

“She has not yet left her room, your lordship.” 

“Let her know that I am here, waiting audience of her.”

“Will your lordship please to give me your name?”

“Tell her that one who knows the white leech desires to see her.”

“She will kill me if I take such a message: I dare not.”

“You refuse?”

He cast a glance at the white beast and hurried away. The remaining kitchen staff stood paralyzed, thoroughly alienated from their daily sustenance by the intrusion of this mythic presence. I stooped and patted the graceful creature, feeling the warm splendor of her coat against the gloomy chill of the room. She rose, placed her paws on my shoulders, and I embraced her. Then, pricking her ears, she broke away and vanished, taking with her the only authentic vitality in the room.

The servant returned, his voice trembling. “Please to come this way, my lord.”

I followed him into a room so expansive and dark that its boundaries defied the acoustic mapping of my footsteps. A single spot of light illuminated the black marble floor, which absorbed all reflection and sound. I looked up to an oval aperture in the roof, noting the reddish lines that seemed to vanish when examined—a visual untranslatability that mirrored the disorientation of the space. 

Suddenly, a radiant form stepped into the center of the darkness. Over a robe of soft white, her hair flowed in a cascade as black as the marble. Her arms and feet were like warm ivory, and she greeted me with the innocent smile of a young woman just stepping over the threshold of adulthood. 

Could this be the woman I had rescued—the one who had struck and scorned me? She stood gazing into the dark, searching, until her luminous black eyes locked onto mine. 

“You have found me at last!” she said, stepping forward to lay a hand on my shoulder. “I knew you would!”

My frame quivered with conflicting consciousnesses, an untranslatable subjective experience I lacked the vocabulary to analyze. I was simultaneously attracted and repelled. 

“You shiver!” she noted, her voice an imposing, dominant lexicon attempting to define my state. “This place is cold for you! Come.”

Struck dumb by her beauty and the sweetness of her tone, I allowed her to lead me to the light. “You have grown brown since last I saw you,” she said. 

“This is almost the first roof I have been under since you left me,” I replied. 

“Whose was the other?”

“I do not know the woman’s name.”

“I would gladly learn it! The instinct of hospitality is not strong in my people!” 

She guided me up a white stair to a beautiful chamber. “How you must miss the hot flowing river!” she said, weaving her narrative over my own memories. “But there is a bath in the corner with no white leeches in it! At the foot of your couch you will find a garment. When you come down, I shall be in the room to your left.”

Left alone, I questioned this marvelous change. Was this genuine hospitality, or a curated performance? I approached the bath, sunk in the marble floor. The crystal-clear water sent a sparkling flash that invited my weary body. I plunged in, and immediately, my palate and olfactory senses were overwhelmed by a landscape as strange as it was delicate. It was a complex aroma—notes of mineral-rich earth overlaid with a subtle, botanical sweetness that I did not altogether trust. Had she medicated it? 

I remembered the crushed paw of the leopardess and sprang from the bath. Yet, I reasoned, was it not the same profound sustenance the pitcher-plant secreted from its heart for the weary traveler? Water was a celestial ingredient; its essence remained pure regardless of the earthly vessel that gathered it. 

Drying myself, I donned the white wool robe, meticulously embroidered at the neck and hem, and descended to the room she had indicated. It was a circular chamber of alabaster, lacking windows but suffused with a pearly, luminescent shimmer. The acoustics here were soft, muffling the sharp edges of the world outside. The princess awaited me in a silver mail of argentine rings and lozenges, falling unbroken from her neck but leaving her arms bare.

Before us sat an ivory table, an altar of culinary history. It bore cakes and fruit, an ivory jug of milk, a crystal jug of pale rose-colored wine, and a white loaf. 

“Here we do not kill to eat,” she said, “but I think you will like what I can give you.”

I took my seat on the couch beside her, my senses deeply engaged by the profound meaning of the sustenance offered. The crust of the white loaf spoke of wild, multi-generational fermentation, an ancient domestic routine preserved in starch and yeast. She poured a bowl of milk, rich and herbaceous, and filled two silver goblets of grotesquely graceful workmanship with the pale rose wine. 

I drank, and my palate was flooded with botanical history. Every extinct blossom of Hybla and Hymettus must have surrendered its ghost to swell the terroir of that vintage. It was an evocative, gustatory-rich nectar that communicated a forgotten era of the earth.

“And now that you will be able to listen,” she said, her voice attempting to assert a power dynamic through her dominant lexicon, “I must do what I can to make myself intelligible to you. Old age is to you a horror; to me it is a dear desire. I am not yet ripe, and have lived thousands of your years. When you found me, I found a man! I put you to the test; your love was genuine! But it was far from ideal. Pity has, but is not love. To satisfy the hunger of my love, you must follow me, looking for nothing! I yield the contest.”

She hid her face in her hands, but I had caught the superficial, commercialized sparkle behind her tenderness. I did not believe her. 

“Beautiful princess,” I said, guarding my native identity against her semantic colonization, “let me understand how you came to be found in such evil plight.”

“I had set out to visit a part of my dominions occupied by a savage dwarf-people,” she explained, casually dismissing an entire culture as “opposed to every kind of progress”—the classic justification of an imperial tongue erasing a marginalized dialect. “I did not know that upon the hot stream, a certain woman had cast a spell. In the very act of bounding across it, an indescribable cold invaded me. I dragged myself to the wood, until I saw you asleep, and the horrible worm at your neck. I dragged the monster from you, and laid my lips to the wound.”

She rose, eyes flashing, throwing her arms high. “What you have made me is yours!” she cried, offering herself like a transactional commodity. “My power, my beauty, my love are your own: take them.”

She dropped to her knees, laying her arms across mine. It was then I noted the large, clumsy glove on her left hand. Beneath the fabric, the untranslatable truth of hidden hair and claws betrayed her words. I turned my eyes aside, refusing to consume the lie.

She started to her feet, waving her arms in a mystic fashion. Suddenly, a frightful roar shattered the room’s carefully curated acoustic ecology. It was a pure, uncommercialized vibration that rebounded against the walls of my chest, shaking the alabaster into trembling shivers. 

The princess shuddered. “My wine was too strong for you!” she quavered, attempting once more to rewrite my sensory reality. “Go and sleep now. I do not wonder that roar startled you. For a moment I feared she had escaped.”

But I knew the truth held in that sound. The roar did not belong to the princess's captive beast; it came from the white leopardess, an ancestral, earth-shaking resonance meant entirely for me, speaking a language far older and truer than the walls of the palace itself.
