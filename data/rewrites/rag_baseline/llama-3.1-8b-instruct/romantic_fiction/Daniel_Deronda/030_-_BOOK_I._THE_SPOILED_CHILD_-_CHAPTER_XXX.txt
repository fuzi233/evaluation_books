The last wisps of autumn's warmth lingered in the air, heavy with the scent of damp earth and decaying leaves, as Lady Violet Lebas stepped out onto the crumbling flagstones of Gadsmere's overgrown garden. The once-manicured lawns now gave way to a tangle of wildflowers and weeds, a testament to the estate's neglect. A rusty weathercock atop the ancient round tower creaked in the gentle breeze, its faded colors blending with the muted tones of the stone walls. The surrounding trees, their branches etched against the gray sky like bony fingers, seemed to lean in, as if sharing secrets whispered on the wind.

Inside, the warm glow of candles cast flickering shadows on the walls as Mrs. Glasher sat by the fire, her four children gathered around her. The flames danced and spat, casting a hypnotic rhythm that seemed to match the beat of the children's laughter. It was a scene both ordinary and extraordinary, a moment of peace in a world that seemed determined to shroud itself in darkness. Outside, the coal-mining district sprawled, a bleak expanse of blackened earth and skeletal trees, a reminder of the unforgiving forces that shaped this corner of England.

It had been ten years since the scandal of the elopement, when the beautiful Mrs. Glasher had abandoned her husband and child to run away with the dashing young Grandcourt. The memory of that event still lingered, a whispered rumor that drew curious glances and knowing smiles. Most had assumed that Grandcourt would soon tire of his mistress and seek a more suitable match, one that would bring him the social standing he craved. Yet, as the seasons passed, the rumors of his attachment to Mrs. Glasher only grew stronger.

In truth, Grandcourt's feelings for her had never wavered. Though his passion had cooled, a lingering attachment remained, a dull ache that echoed through his days. It was a sentiment he dare not acknowledge, not even to himself. Society would never understand, and his family would be scandalized. So he kept his thoughts hidden, locked away behind a mask of indifference. The death of her husband had only strengthened his resolve to marry her, to legitimize their relationship and secure his own reputation. Yet, as the months dragged on, he found himself unable to take the final step, trapped by his own doubts and fears.
