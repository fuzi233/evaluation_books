The silence of our victory was shattered before it could truly begin. Standing amidst the grotesque carnage surrounding the great boulder, Tars Tarkas and I found no respite to exchange the stories of our survival. From every vantage point along the broad valley floor, a torrential flood of terrifying forms surged forward, answering the unearthly, shrill summons of the strange figure perched high above us.

"Come!" Tars Tarkas roared, his voice cutting through the rising din. "We must flee to the cliffs. There lies our only sliver of hope—a fleeting chance for escape. Perhaps there, wedged in a cave or upon a narrow ledge, two souls may hold the line forever against this motley, armed horde."

Driven by the primal instinct of survival, we launched ourselves across the scarlet grass. I timed my strides with military precision, pushing my legs to ensure I did not leave my slower, heavier companion behind. Between the site of our recent triumph and the looming cliffs lay perhaps three hundred yards of open ground, a gauntlet we had to clear before finding a sanctuary capable of withstanding the assault.

They were gaining on us with terrifying speed. Sensing the narrowing margin, Tars Tarkas commanded me to surge ahead, to scout the potential refuge while he held the rear. It was a prudent stratagem; every minute saved was a heartbeat stolen from the jaws of death. Abandoning caution, I poured every ounce of my terrestrial strength into the earth, launching myself in great, bounding leaps. In moments, I had cleared the final distance and planted my boots at the base of the cliffs.

The escarpment rose vertically from the valley floor, a sheer wall of rock devoid of the usual debris-strewn slopes that soften the approach of natural formations. Scattered boulders, remnants of ancient disintegration, lay partially buried in the turf—the only testament to the slow erosion of this massive pile. My initial scan of the cliff face filled me with dread. Nowhere could I discern a foothold, save for the distant silhouette of the weird herald still shrieking his summons atop the heights.

To my right, the cliff's base vanished into the dense, verdant canopy of the forest, which reared its lush foliage a thousand feet against the stern, forbidding stone. To my left, the rock continued unbroken across the valley's head, disappearing into the hazy outlines of a mountain range that hemmed the valley in on all sides. Some five thousand feet above me, the river seemed to break directly from the cliff's foundation. With no hope of escape downstream, I turned my gaze back to the forest.

The cliffs towered with majestic indifference. The sun had not yet kissed their faces, leaving them a dull, shadowed yellow, streaked with patches of dusky red, emerald green, and flashes of white quartz. They were undeniably beautiful, a masterpiece of geological artistry. Yet, in my desperation, I viewed them not with awe, but with hatred—as a prisoner might loathe the iron bars of his dungeon.

Just as my despair began to crystallize, Tars Tarkas approached, and behind him, the awful horde thundered closer. It seemed the choice was stark: the forest or oblivion. I was on the verge of signaling him to follow when the sun crossed the cliff's zenith. Suddenly, the dull surface erupted into a blinding symphony of light. Millions of scintillating sparks ignited—burnished gold, flaming red, soft greens, and gleaming whites. It was a spectacle of such gorge that the human eye struggles to comprehend.

Upon closer inspection, the entire face of the cliff revealed itself to be shot through with veins and patches of solid gold, appearing as a living wall of the precious metal, interrupted only by outcroppings of ruby, emerald, and diamond boulders. This was a faint, alluring whisper of the vast, unguessable riches buried deep within.

Yet, what seized my attention with electric intensity were several black spots that emerged clearly against the shimmering backdrop, high near the forest's crown and extending behind the branches. Instantly, I recognized them: the dark maws of caves piercing the solid rock. Possible avenues of salvation, provided we could reach them.

There was but one path, leading through the mighty trees on our right. I knew I could scale them; Tars Tarkas, with his immense bulk and weight, faced a trial likely beyond his prowess. On the entire surface of Mars, hills rarely exceeded four thousand feet, and their gradual ascents offered few challenges to the climbing instincts of Martians, who preferred circuitous routes around eminences to the arduous climb. Nevertheless, there was no alternative.

The Thark grasped the gravity of the situation instantly. With our relentless pursuers closing in, it seemed impossible for the Jeddak of Thark to reach the forest before they did. Yet, he moved. The green men of Barsoom do not relish flight, nor have I ever witnessed one flee from death unless compelled by a force greater than honor. Tars Tarkas was the bravest of the brave, having faced countless mortal combats. But behind his flight lay something deeper than courage; it was a spark of life kindled in a race that typically seeks only the grave. In my case, that spark was fueled by love—for the divine Dejah Thoris. The source of the Thark's sudden reverence for life remained unfathomable to me, for these strange, cruel, loveless people often court death more eagerly than life.

Finally, we reached the shadows of the forest. Behind us, the swiftest of our pursuers—a giant plant-man with claws extended to tear at our throats—sprang forward. He was a hundred yards ahead of his kin. I signaled to Tars Tarkas to ascend a great tree brushing the cliff's face, while I engaged the plant-man, buying the less agile Thark time to reach the higher branches before the horde cut off every avenue of escape.

I had miscalculated. I underestimated the cunning of my immediate antagonist and the terrifying velocity with which his fellows closed the gap. As I raised my long-sword to deliver a fatal thrust, the creature halted mid-charge. My blade sliced harmlessly through empty air. In that split second, the great tail of the beast swept across the sward with the force of a grizzly bear's paw, knocking me flat to the ground.

Before I could rise, the brute was upon me. Its hideous mouths lunged for my breast and throat, but I reacted with the reflexes of a man who knows no mercy. Grasping a writhing tentacle in each hand, I locked my fingers around the thick, muscular limb. The plant-man was heavy, powerful, and well-muscled, but my earthly sinews and superior agility, combined with a death-grip strangulation, promised eventual victory—if only we had the time to debate our strengths.

Struggling and thrashing about the tree where Tars Tarkas climbed with infinite difficulty, I glanced over my adversary's shoulder. What I saw froze my blood. A great swarm of pursuers descended upon me. These were the most dreaded of Martian creatures—the Great White Apes of Barsoom.

My previous encounters on Mars had acquainted me thoroughly with their methods. Of all the fearsome, weird, and grotesque inhabitants of that strange world, none came closer to instilling pure terror than the White Apes. Their resemblance to Earth men, coupled with their monstrous size, created an uncanny horror. They stood fifteen feet tall, walking erect on hind legs, possessing an intermediate set of arms midway between their upper and lower limbs. Their eyes were close-set but not protruding; their ears high and lateral; their snouts and teeth reminiscent of the African gorilla. Crowning their heads was an enormous shock of bristly hair.

Into the eyes of such horrors did I gaze, and then, in a mighty wave of snarling, snapping, screaming, and purring rage, they swept over me. The most hideous sound assailing my ears as I fell beneath them was the horrid, vibrating purr of the plant-men.

Instantly, a dozen cruel fangs and keen talons sank into my flesh. Cold, sucking lips fastened themselves upon my arteries. I struggled to free myself, and despite being weighed down by these immense bodies, I managed to rise to my feet. Clutching my long-sword, I shortened my grip, using it as a dagger, and wrought such havoc among them that for a brief, glorious instant, I stood free.

What took minutes to recount occurred in mere seconds. During that time, Tars Tarkas, seeing my plight, dropped from the lower branches he had reached with such toil. As I flung the last of my immediate antagonists aside, the great Thark leaped to my side. Once again, we fought back-to-back, as we had a hundred times before.

Time and again, the ferocious apes sprang to close with us, and time and again, we beat them back with our gleaming blades. The great tails of the plant-men lashed with tremendous power about us as they charged from various directions or sprang with the agility of greyhounds above our heads. Every attack met a sword-hand renowned for twenty years as the finest in the martial history of Mars; for Tars Tarkas and John Carter were names spoken with reverence by the fighting men of the warrior worlds.
