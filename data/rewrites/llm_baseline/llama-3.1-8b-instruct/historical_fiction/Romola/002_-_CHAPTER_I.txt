In the early morning of the 9th of April 1492, beneath the ancient Loggia de' Cerchi, nestled in the heart of Florence's winding streets, two men locked gazes. One stood tall, his rugged features a testament to a life shaped by the rough-hewn hills and valleys of Tuscany. His eyes, worn from years of squinting into the sun, seemed to hold a world of stories, though his voice was smooth as honey as he spoke. Before him lay a sturdy pedlar's basket, its wicker woven with the care of generations, filled with threads of crimson and gold, and sparkling shards of glass, treasures gathered from far-flung corners of the land.

The second figure, sprawled on the stone floor, slowly came awake, his movements stiff from a night spent in the open air. As he rose to his feet, his gaze fell upon the ring adorning his finger, and he hesitated, as if recalling a promise or a secret. The standing man, Bratti Ferravecchi, pointed to the ring, his gnarled fingers crooked with age, and his voice rumbled like thunder on a summer's day.

"Young man," he said, "when your beard has grown strong enough to rival the oak trees of the Mugello, you'll learn to choose your resting places more wisely. This city is not a place for a stranger to sleep with the wind as his blanket, especially not when you wear a ring such as that. By the saints, I'm not one to steal, and neither am I one to take advantage of the unwary. A San Giovanni's blessing is a rare thing, but I've earned mine through years of paying my respects to the Festa. Perhaps you've heard the tale of the blind beggar who came to me three years past? I gave him a proper burial and said a mass for his soul. Now, tell me, young traveler, how does a young man such as yourself, with a face as noble as Messer San Michele, end up sleeping on a stone bench?"

As Bratti spoke, the stranger's eyes darted to the ring, then back to the pedlar's basket, his fingers absently smoothing the fabric of his doublet. For an instant, a flicker of unease danced across his features, but he quickly regained his composure, pushing back the rich brown locks that had fallen across his forehead.
