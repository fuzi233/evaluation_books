Martin Wittenhaagen stood at the foot of the stairs, his bowstring taut, arrow drawn nearly to the head, a knife gripped behind his back. His sharp eyes widened as the men returned—without Gerard. Slowly, he lowered his weapon, his face slackening in confusion.

The men, too, stared at him, their brows furrowed. One of them, Dierich, narrowed his eyes. "What’s this, then? Were you fixing to loose an arrow at us, old man?"

Martin exhaled, forcing his features into their usual stolid calm. "Just testing the pull of a new string," he grunted. "See?" He unstrung the bow with deliberate slowness. "Satisfied?"

Dierich snorted. "There’s more to you than meets the eye. But no matter—let’s get a fire going. Dry off before we head back."

A blaze was soon crackling in the hearth, its light flickering across their faces as steam curled from their damp clothes. Then—piercing the quiet—a woman’s shrieks tore through the house.

Every man jolted upright. One snatched the candle and bolted for the stairs.

Martin moved to block him, his pulse hammering. *Margaret.*

Dierich lunged, locking his arms around Martin from behind. "Hold him!"

The candle-bearer vanished into the upper darkness while the others piled onto Martin. A brutal scuffle erupted—bodies crashing, fists flying, the floorboards shuddering under the weight of grappling men. Martin fought like a wolf, but numbers overwhelmed him. Rope bit into his wrists and ankles as they trussed him tight.

He groaned, teeth grinding. That man was in Margaret’s room now, and he was helpless to stop it.

Dierich wiped sweat from his brow, panting. "Aye, gnash your teeth, old devil. You’ll do no more damage with ’em."

One of the others spat. "Reckon our necks wouldn’t be safe with this one loose."

"Got me thinking," another muttered, "maybe Gerard’s closer than we thought."

Dierich barked a laugh. "Wishful thinking. But where’s Jorian? Taking his sweet time with that wench upstairs."

Coarse chuckles rippled through the group—until the sound of frantic footsteps thundered overhead.

"About time," Dierich called, grinning. "Well, Jorian? What’s the fuss up there?"
