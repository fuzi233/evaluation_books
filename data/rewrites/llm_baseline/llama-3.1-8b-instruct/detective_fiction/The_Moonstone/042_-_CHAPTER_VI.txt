As I stepped out into the crisp evening air, Gabriel Betteredge fell into step beside me, his usual volubility noticeably absent. I fingered the letter in my pocket and the carefully packed nightgown in its small bag, mentally preparing myself for the scrutiny Mr. Bruff would apply to them come nightfall. We traversed the quiet grounds, the only sound the soft crunch of gravel beneath our feet.

Breaking the silence, I spoke as we cleared the lodge gates. "Before I depart for London, I have two questions for you, Betteredge. They concern my past, and I suspect they will surprise you."

"If they'll dispel the image of Rachel's letter from my mind, Mr. Franklin, I'm willing to hear them," he replied, his eyes squinting slightly against the fading light.

"My first query, Betteredge, pertains to my state on Rachel's Birthday. Was I in my cups that night?" I asked, watching him intently.

"Good heavens, Mr. Franklin! You're always a moderate drinker, sticking to your wine at dinner and never touching a drop thereafter," Betteredge exclaimed. "But on that particular evening, I recall you did stray from your routine. You appeared quite unwell, and I suggested a brandy and water to steady your nerves."

I recalled the events of that night, and my doubts resurfaced. "I'm not accustomed to brandy and water. It's conceivable I could have become—"

Betteredge chuckled knowingly. "Ah, I remember now. I poured you a generous measure of our finest Cognac, tempered with nearly a full glass of cold water. Even a child would have found it hard to get tipsy from that concoction, let alone a grown man."

His words brought a wave of relief. I trusted his recollection implicitly in matters like this. With that settled, I moved on to my next question. "As a youth, you spent considerable time with me. Do you recall anything peculiar about my nocturnal habits? Did you ever observe me wandering about in my sleep?"

Betteredge's gait faltered, and he turned to face me, his eyes narrowing thoughtfully. "Now I understand where you're headed, Mr. Franklin. You're attempting to explain away the paint on your nightgown without recalling it yourself. It won't work, sir. You've never been a sleepwalker in your life!"
