In the cloistered halls of the Dominican order, where once the fervent voices of the preaching friars had echoed through the land, a new era dawned. Their rival Franciscans, now ascendant, had eclipsed their influence across Europe, even in the devout realm of England, where the Black Friars had once held sway. As the sagacious leaders of the order gazed out upon this shifting landscape, they sought to reclaim their former glory by nurturing a new generation of zealous sons, eager to reclaim the world for their order.

Among these hopeful novices was Clement, whose remarkable facility with languages – he spoke Latin, Italian, French, and the rustic dialects of the Low Countries – had already begun to make waves within the order. His superiors envisioned a future in which Clement would travel to England, preaching and corresponding with the Roman center, but Jerome, a trusted advisor to the prior, counseled caution.

"Clement," Jerome said, "remains untested, his heart still bearing the tender impressions of his former life. Let us not be swayed by his zeal and humility. We must temper his fervor, lest we find ourselves leaning on a reed for a staff."

The prior concurred, and Jerome, following the ancient wisdom of their order, decided to put Clement to the test. He led Clement to a sun-drenched field, where the young men of the order often gathered to play and laugh together. Jerome knew that this was a haunt of Clement's old friends, and indeed, soon enough, Pietro Vanucci and Andrea appeared, casting a carefree glance upon the two friars. Clement's eyes widened, and for a moment, he seemed to falter, but then he bowed his head and recited a quiet prayer.

"Would you not speak with them, brother?" Jerome asked, probing Clement's resolve.

Clement hesitated, his gaze lingering on the faces of his former companions. "It was good for me to see them," he said finally. "They remind me of the sins I can never fully atone for."

Jerome nodded, making a discreet note in Clement's favor. Next, he led Clement to the bedside of the dying, to the darkest dungeons, where the air reeked of despair and the inmates seemed like wild beasts.
