In the labyrinthine streets of Paternoster Row, where the city's literary elite converged, a most intriguing dinner party was unfolding at the esteemed residence of Mr. Bungay. On the appointed day, our protagonists, Pen and Warrington, made their arrival at the private door of the house, eschewing the public entrance where booksellers' boys hawked their wares and aspiring authors waited with their manuscripts in hand. Instead, they alighted at the discreet entrance reserved for the household's inner circle, where the resplendent Mrs. Bungay would emerge to take her customary drive, casting defiant glances at the neighboring abode of Mrs. Bacon.

As the latter gazed out of her drawing-room window, her four children gathered around her, a familiar ritual played out. Mrs. Bacon would fling open the sash, scrutinizing the carriages of her sister-in-law's acquaintances as they arrived, her eyes flashing with a mix of envy and indignation. Today, however, was different. Dr. Slocum's imposing carriage, accompanied by a cab, drew up before her door, its gaunt horses a poignant reminder of the social disparities that had long plagued the community. Mrs. Bacon's ire was piqued, and as she gazed out at the gathering, her thoughts were consumed by the perceived slights of her sister-in-law's opulence.

Meanwhile, the esteemed Dr. Slocum descended from his carriage, his presence commanding attention. A couple of dashing young men, resplendent in white waistcoats, emerged from the cab, their arrival a harbinger of the evening's festivities. Warrington chuckled, observing the scene unfold, and pointed out the unlikely transformation of their acquaintance, Hoolan, now clad in finery. As the evening progressed, the atmosphere grew more convivial, with the house porter and liveried attendants receiving the guests' hats and coats, their names bellowed up the stairs.

The gathering was a veritable Who's Who of literary London, with the likes of Mr. Bole, the true editor of the influential magazine, Mr. Trotter, a poet turned reader for Mr. Bungay, and Captain Sumph, a charismatic raconteur with a storied past and a penchant for anecdotes about Lord Byron.
