In the hushed hours before dawn, the silence in our small house on Church Street was oppressive. I stirred, my body aching with the memory of yesterday's events. Theo's gentle snores provided a somber counterpoint to the stillness. Outside, the city began to stir, its rhythms a muffled accompaniment to our morning routine. I rose, careful not to disturb the cradle where our son, Miles, slept, his small chest rising and falling with each breath.

The past few days had been a blur of hospital visits, anxious conversations with the doctor, and hasty decisions. Our little one's arrival had brought a mix of joy and trepidation. Theo and I had chosen a name that held a special place in our family's history – a name that reminded us of simpler times and the kindness of strangers. Miles's birth had been a surprise, but it had also brought a sense of purpose to our lives. We'd written to our parents, announcing the news, and included a brief mention in the Daily Advertiser, careful not to draw too much attention to ourselves.

As I made my way downstairs, the aroma of stale bread and yesterday's tea filled the air. Our landlady, Mrs. Jenkins, had left a note, reminding us that the rent was due soon. I smiled, thinking of the little silk coverlid with lace that Aunt Bernstein had sent – a gift that had brought a touch of elegance to our humble abode. It wasn't much, but it was a reminder that we weren't entirely forgotten.

The door creaked open, and Dr. Heberden entered, his face creased with concern. He'd visited us often since Miles's birth, ensuring that everything was in order. His presence was a comforting reminder that we weren't alone in this new chapter of our lives. I offered him a cup of tea, and he accepted, his eyes scanning the room as he waited for it to brew. The good doctor had a way of making us feel seen, of reminding us that, even in the midst of uncertainty, we were not forgotten.

As we sipped our tea, the sound of carriage wheels echoed outside. Lady Castlewood's chariot had arrived, bearing a gift – a pot of jelly, no doubt served at one of her lavish gatherings.
