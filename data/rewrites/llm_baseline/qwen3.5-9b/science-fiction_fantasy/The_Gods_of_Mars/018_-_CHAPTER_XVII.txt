Just moments before dawn on the appointed morning, a formidable detachment of Zat Arras's officers arrived at our quarters to escort us to the Great Hall of the Temple. We entered the chamber in pairs, marching down the Broad Aisle of Hope toward the central platform. Flanking us were armed guards, while three solid lines of Zodangan soldiers lined the aisle from the entrance to the rostrum, creating a corridor of steel.

Upon reaching the raised enclosure, I caught sight of the judges. By Barsoomian custom, there were thirty-one of them, ostensibly chosen by lot from the noble class, for nobles were those on trial. Yet, to my utter astonishment, not a single familiar face greeted me. Practically all were Zodangans—the very nation that owed its defeat to the Green Hordes and its subsequent vassalage to Helium to my actions. Little justice could be expected here for John Carter, his son, or the great Thark who had led the savage tribesmen that had overrun Zodanga's broad avenues, looting, burning, and murdering.

Surrounding us, the vast circular coliseum was packed to absolute capacity. Every class, age, and sex was represented. As we entered, the hum of subdued conversation died instantly. When we halted upon the platform—or the Throne of Righteousness—the silence of death enveloped the ten thousand spectators. The judges sat in a great circle around the periphery of the platform. We were assigned seats with our backs to a small platform in the exact center of the larger one, placing us facing the judges and the audience. On the smaller platform, each would stand during the hearing of their specific case.

Zat Arras himself took his place in the golden chair of the presiding magistrate. As we settled and our guards retreated to the foot of the stairway, he rose and called my name.

"John Carter!" he bellowed. "Take your place upon the Pedestal of Truth to be judged impartially according to your acts, and here to learn the reward you have earned!"

Turning to scan the crowd, he began his narration of the charges determining my fate.

"Know ye, O judges and people of Helium," he declared, "that John Carter, former Prince of Helium, has returned by his own admission from the Valley Dor and even from the Temple of Issus itself. Know ye that in the presence of many men of Helium, he has blasphemed against the Sacred Iss, the Valley Dor, the Lost Sea of Korus, and the Holy Therns themselves—and even against Issus, Goddess of Death and Life Eternal. Furthermore, witness with your own eyes that he has indeed returned from these sacred precincts, defying our ancient customs and violating the sanctity of our religion. 'He who is once dead may not live again.' He who attempts it must be made dead forever. Judges, your duty is plain: there can be no testimony in contravention of truth. What reward shall be meted to John Carter for his crimes?"

"Death!" shouted a judge immediately.

Then, a man sprang to his feet in the audience, raising his hand high. "Justice! Justice! Justice!"

It was Kantos Kan. As all eyes turned to him, he leaped past the Zodangan soldiery and bounded onto the platform.

"What manner of justice is this?" he cried at Zat Arras. "The defendant has not been heard, nor has he had the opportunity to call witnesses in his behalf. In the name of the people of Helium, I demand fair and impartial treatment for the Prince of Helium!"

A great roar erupted from the crowd: "Justice! Justice! Justice!" Zat Arras dared not deny them.

"Speak, then," he snarled, turning to me, "but blaspheme not against the things that are sacred upon Barsoom."

"Men of Helium," I cried, addressing the spectators over the heads of my judges, "how can John Carter expect justice from the men of Zodanga? He cannot, nor does he ask it. It is to the men of Helium that I state my case; nor do I appeal for mercy to any. It is not in my own cause that I speak now—it is in yours. For your wives and daughters, and for wives and daughters yet unborn. It is to save them from the unthinkably atrocious indignities I have witnessed heaped upon the fair women of Barsoom in the place men call the Temple of Issus. To save them from the sucking embrace of the Plant Men, from the fangs of the Great White Apes of Dor, from the cruel lust of the Holy Therns, from all that the cold, dead Iss carries them to—from homes of love, life, and happiness."

"Sits there no man here who does not know the history of John Carter?" I continued. "How he came among you from another world, rose from a prisoner among the Green Men through torture and persecution to a place high among the highest of Barsoom. Nor ever did you know John Carter to lie in his own behalf, or to say aught that might harm the people of Barsoom, or to speak lightly of the strange religion which he respected without fully understanding."

"There is no man here, or anywhere else upon Barsoom today, who does not owe his life directly to a single act of mine, in which I sacrificed myself and the happiness of my Princess that you might live. So, men of Helium, I believe I have the right to demand that I be heard, that I be believed, and that you let me serve you and save you from the false hereafter of Dor and Issus, just as I saved you from the real death that other day."

"It is to you of Helium that I speak now. When I am done, let the men of Zodanga have their will with me. Zat Arras has taken my sword from me, so the men of Zodanga no longer fear me. Will you listen?"

"Speak, John Carter, Prince of Helium!" cried a great noble from the audience, and the multitude echoed his permission until the building seemed to rock with the noise of their demonstration. Zat Arras knew better than to interfere with such a sentiment expressed in the Temple of Reward. Thus, for two hours, I spoke with the people of Helium.

When I finished, Zat Arras rose, turned to the judges, and said in a low tone: "My nobles, you have heard John Carter's plea; every opportunity has been given him to prove his innocence if he be not guilty; but instead, he has utilized the time in further blasphemy. What, gentlemen, is your verdict?"

"Death to the blasphemer!" cried one, springing to his feet. Instantly, the entire thirty-one judges rose with upraised swords, signaling the unanimity of their verdict. If the people did not hear Zat Arras' charge, they certainly heard the tribunal's decision. A sullen murmur rose from the packed coliseum, growing louder with every passing second.

Kantos Kan, who had remained on the platform beside me since first taking his stand, raised his hand for silence. When he could be heard, he spoke to the people in a cool, level voice.

"You have heard the fate the men of Zodanga would mete to Helium's noblest hero. It may be the duty of the men of Helium to accept the verdict as final. Let each man act according to his own heart. Here is the answer of Kantos Kan, Head of the Navy of Helium, to Zat Arras and his judges."

With that, he unbuckled his scabbard and threw his sword at my feet. In an instant, soldiers and citizens, officers and nobles crowded past the Zodangan soldiers, forcing their way to the Throne of Righteousness. A hundred men surged onto the platform, and a hundred blades rattled and clanked to the floor at my feet. Zat Arras and his officers were furious but utterly helpless. One by one, I raised the swords to my lips and buckled them back onto their owners.

"Come," said Kantos Kan. "We will escort John Carter and his party to his own palace."

They formed about us and started toward the stairs leading to the Aisle of Hope.

"Stop!" cried Zat Arras. "Soldiers of Helium, let no prisoner leave the Throne of Righteousness."

The soldiery from Zodanga were the only organized body of Heliumetic troops within the temple, so Zat Arras was confident his orders would be obeyed. However, he did not look for the opposition raised the moment his soldiers advanced toward the throne. From every quarter of the coliseum, swords flashed and men rushed threateningly upon the Zodangans. Someone raised a cry: "Tardos Mors is dead—a thousand years to John Carter, Jeddak of Helium!"

Hearing this and seeing the ugly attitude of the men of Helium toward Zat Arras's soldiers, I knew that only a miracle could avert a clash ending in civil war.

"Hold!" I cried, leaping to the Pedestal of Truth once more. "Let no man move till I am done. A single sword thrust here today may plunge Helium into a bitter and bloody war the results of which none can foresee. It will turn brother against brother and father against son. No man's life is worth that sacrifice. Rather would I submit to the biased judgment of Zat Arras than be the cause of civil strife in Helium."
