The misty veil of morning lifted over the sleepy streets of London, casting a melancholy haze over the city's inhabitants. In a small, cluttered flat above a bakery on a winding street, a young writer sat hunched over his desk, staring blankly at the faint scratch of his pen on the page. His fingers drummed a staccato rhythm on the armchair beside him, a nervous habit he'd cultivated over years of struggling to eke out a living. He had intended to write something profound, something that would shake the foundations of the literary world, but all that came to mind was the nagging ache of poverty.

He thought back to the countless afternoons spent rummaging through second-hand shops for scraps of fabric to patch together his worn coat, or the evenings spent pacing the streets, too proud to admit to himself that he couldn't afford a warm meal. It was a peculiar thing, this feeling of being hard up, but it seemed to seep into every pore of his being, making him question the value of every penny he spent. He recalled the provincial actor he'd once been, eking out a meager existence on the margins of the stage, and the "gentleman connected with the press" he'd become, living on the fringes of respectability. The memory of those lean times still lingered, a constant reminder of the delicate balance between comfort and desperation.

As he sat there, the rain lashing against the windowpane like a chorus of forgotten worries, he found himself pondering the peculiar rituals of his family. His sisters and aunts would scold him for sitting with his hands in his pockets, deeming it ungentlemanly. But what did they know of the solace it brought him? The jingle of coins in his pocket, the weight of keys and a few coppers, was a small comfort in the grand tapestry of life. He thought of the young men he'd met, those who'd never known the sting of poverty, and how they'd recoil at the very idea of eating a hearty meal at a common pub. He remembered the look of disgust on their faces, and the mad urge to take them by the hand and lead them to a place where the air reeked of roasting meat and the sound of laughter was genuine.
