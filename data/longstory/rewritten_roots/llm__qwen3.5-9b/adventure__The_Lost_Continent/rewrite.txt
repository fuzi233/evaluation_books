Together with Zaemon, he was one of only two remaining priests elevated to the highest degree of our clan, possessing exclusive knowledge of the deepest secrets and powers.

"Look!" Zaemon cried, his shrill voice cutting through the tension. He swept a trembling finger across the shattered landscape visible below.

I followed his gaze, and a chill crawled up my spine. Everything was laid bare. Our Lord the Sun burned high in a cloudless blue sky, casting a shimmering heat over the scene. From this distance, the world below appeared peaceful and still, save for the smoking mountains, the erupting fires, and the sea, which churned with unnatural disorder.

It was the behavior of the ocean that disturbed me most. Far out on the distant coast, it surged against the rocks in monstrous rollers. Up the great estuary, where the city of Atlantis stood, it rushed in successive waves of terrifying height that never receded. The lower lands on either side were already obliterated beneath tumultuous waters; the harbor walls were submerged, and the flood crept upward into the lower wards of the great city.

"You have seen?" Zaemon asked.

"I have seen."

"Do you understand?"

"In part."

"Then let me explain. This is the beginning, and the end will follow swiftly. The Most High Gods, who dwell behind the stars, have limits to their sublime patience, and that limit has been crossed. The city of Atlantis, the great continent beyond, and all within it are doomed to unutterable destruction. Long ago, it was foreseen that this great erasure would occur due to the sins of mankind. Thus, the Ark of the Mysteries was constructed under the direction of the Gods. No mortal tool can scratch its surface; no wave or rock can wreck it."

Zaemon paused, his voice heavy with prophecy. "Inside are stored on sheets of ancient writing all the knowledge of the world not shared with the common people. There is grain sufficient for two persons for four years, fresh water in tanks for the same duration, along with seeds, weapons, and other necessities deemed essential."

"From this vast country, the High Gods have decreed that two shall not perish. Two shall be chosen—a man and a woman—fit and proper vessels to carry away the ancient learning, to dispose of it as they see fit, and eventually to raise a new race that will build another kingdom and honor our Lord the Sun and the other Gods elsewhere."

"The woman is already within the Ark, seated in her appointed place. Though she is my daughter, the burden of her selection rests with you. As for the man, the choice falls upon yourself."

I was half-numb with shock. "I do not know that I wish to be a survivor."

"You are not asked for your wishes," the old man stated firmly. "You are given an order from the High Gods, who know you to be their faithful servant."

Habit rode strong upon me. I offered the required salute and declared my obedience.

"It remains to raise you to the sublime degree of the Three," Zaemon announced. "If your learning is insufficient to grasp the keys to many of the Powers and the highest Mysteries when they are revealed, that fault cannot be remedied now."

Time was running critically short. The fight still raged at the gate, a marvel that the handful of priests had held their ground so long. Yet the ocean rolled inland with increasing fury, and the mountains smoked and belched volleys of rock as the crushing weight mounted. Soon, the besiegers would realize their impending doom and charge blindly, sweeping everything before them.

There was no time to hurry Zaemon and his colleague. They lacked a temple, sacrifices, or incense; they had only the sky for a roof and the Gods themselves as witnesses. Nevertheless, they proceeded with the grand and majestic formality accumulated over ages, refusing to curtail a single sentence of the ritual.

A burning mountain erupted with a bellowing roar as the incoming waters met its fires, but the priests continued gravely, reciting their sentences in turn. Phorenice's troops broke the last lines of resistance, pouring in a frenzied stream among the groves and temples, yet the priests' voices never faltered. It was said that this ceremony was the grandest of all in our holy religion, and I found it to be true. Even the catastrophic circumstances surrounding them failed to diminish the deference owed to the highest order of the clan.

For my part, I admitted I was less rapt. Standing bareheaded in the heat, I struggled to concentrate, torn by a thousand conflicting emotions. The apocalyptic event unfolding around me demanded attention, and my thoughts constantly drifted to Nais. Still, I maintained a decent facade, responding to the required cues and striving to memorize the sentences containing the keys to power and learning, rather than mere phrases of grandeur.

The Ark was a vessel without a rudder, without oars, and devoid of any machinery; it could move only where the High Gods willed. Within, darkness reigned, thick with the scent of antiquity and filled with the low, mournful groans of a dying world. I could not locate the fire-box to reignite our fallen lamps, forcing us to navigate the coming terrors in absolute blindness. Outside, the waves tossed us like toys in a game of cruel indifference. I clung desperately to the side of the Ark, holding Nais close against the heaving deck. Words were scarce between us, yet in our shared grief and our heavy silence, there existed a profound companionship.

When Atlantis finally succumbed to the depths to become a new ocean floor, she left behind great whirlpools and spurting vents from her extinguished fires—a fleeting testament to the Sea Gods. Then, I believe, though hidden from view within the Ark's black belly, a vast hurricane of wind descended to ensure no fragment of the desolation remained untouched. This dreadful turmoil raged for seven nights and seven days, as recorded later by the hour-reckoner hanging within the Ark. Finally, the howling ceased, leaving only the rhythmic roll of a long, still swell.

It was regular, oily, distinct from the chaotic churning before. Only then did I dare ascend the stairs to open the hatch in the roof. Sweet air gushed down, washing away the foul stagnation inside. As the Ark rode high and dry above the seas, I went below to bring Nais up, seeking refreshment from the healing rays of our Lord, the Sun. Together, we bowed in adoration, giving thanks for His mercy in granting another dawn. Exhausted beyond measure, we lay down to seek the easy rest we so desperately needed.

Yet, despite my weariness, sleep refused to claim me. I stared listlessly at the oily, sun-drenched waters. No blur of land met my gaze; the ring of the ocean was unbroken on every horizon, and the vault of heaven remained unchanged. The bosom of the deep was littered with the tragic wreckage of Atlantis, serving as grim proof that this was reality, not a nightmare. Trees, squared timber, a smashed hide-boat turned upside down, and here and there the rounded corpses of men or beasts bobbed over the swells, keeping a somber convoy with our Ark as it drifted under the command of the Gods and the currents.

Eventually, sleep claimed me. I drifted into unconsciousness while still clutching Nais's hand. When I awoke, she was already awake, watching me with tender eyes. Both of us were fully restored; rest brings a natural complacency. We were now more willing to accept the station the High Gods had ordained for us without complaint. Descending once more into the Ark's belly, we ate and drank, fortifying ourselves for the new life awaiting us.

Now, viewed with leisure and intimacy, the Ark revealed itself as a marvel. Though it was the first time in its centuries of existence that it floated freely upon the waters, it showed neither leak nor crack. Even the interior floor was bone-dry. While one could discern the grain of the wood, there were no visible sutures or joints. The living timbers had been placed and then caused to grow together by an art we have since lost, known perfectly only to the Ancients. Furthermore, a secret treatment applied by those forgotten builders had rendered the wood as hard as metal and impervious to the elements.

In the gloomy cavern of its hold were stored countless treasures. At one end, in great tanks flanking a central aisle, lay a prodigious supply of grain. At the opposite end, tanks held sweet water. Elsewhere were stored drugs, biological samples, and the very essences of animal life—all provisions for the Ark's journey under divine guidance. Carved along every wall, partition, and tank in the rough style of a bygone era were representations of every beast that once roamed Atlantis. I examined them with a hunter's fascination; some were creatures entirely unknown to me, having perished alongside the men who had immortalized them in stone. There was also a substantial stockpile of weapons and tools for various crafts.

For many weeks, our existence continued aboard this Ark as the Gods steered it across the face of the waters. We possessed no authority over its direction; we could not increase its speed by even a hair's breadth. The High Gods, who had chosen Nais and me as the sole survivors of all Atlantis, held absolute control over our destiny. Into Their hands, we cheerfully surrendered our future course.

Of the land we eventually reached, where we established our home and raised our children, I shall write in its proper place. However, since this chronicle follows the strict order of events, it is necessary first to explain how we acquired the sheets upon which this story is written. In a great coffer centered on the Ark's floor, the entirety of the Mysteries accumulated over ages of study were inscribed in precise writing. During the days that followed, I read portions of them, and the terrifying magnitude of the powers described therein appalled me. I had witnessed some of these forces unleashed during the destruction of Atlantis, but here were powers far greater—here was the Great Secret of Life and Death that Phorenice had discovered, leading to her own demise, and other truths too terrible for my pen to record.

The burden of being the custodian of such knowledge became unbearable. The longer the thoughts lingered in my mind, the more intolerable the weight grew. Finally, taking hot irons, I seared the wax on the sheets until every letter of the ancient writings was obliterated. If I acted wrongly, the High Gods, in Their infinite justice, will mete out punishment; if it is right that these secrets remain buried, They, in Their infinite power, will dictate them anew to worthy scribes. Thus, I destroyed them while the Ark swayed over the waves. Later, upon reaching land, I rewrote onto the sheets only the accounts of the events that led to Atlantis dragging herself toward her final throes.
