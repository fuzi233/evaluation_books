As time went on, the island of my existence became increasingly isolated, like a forgotten spice jar on a dusty shelf. Few visitors dared to brave the treacherous stairs that led to my doorstep, and even the postman's visits grew fewer and farther between. His letters, which once arrived with such regularity, now became a rare delight, and I cherished each one like a precious bouquet of flowers. Publishers wrote to me, inquiring about the manuscript of my new book, but I delayed responding, and eventually, they stopped writing altogether.

The weather transformed, and the privet bushes that lined the notice-boards burst forth with vibrant blooms. The streets, where I once walked with such purpose, now teemed with flower-women, their baskets overflowing with colorful delights. I succumbed to the temptation of their wares, and my room became a sanctuary of flowers, each bloom a testament to my desire for beauty and tranquility. But as the days passed, the thought of venturing out to select these treasures began to weigh heavily on me, and I found solace in the quietude of my home.

My senses, once attuned to the world beyond my doors, began to adjust to a new scale of perception. Fear crept in, taking the form of agoraphobia, a terror of the unknown that lurked in every unguarded moment. I started to dread the open spaces, the unencumbered air, and the shadows that danced upon the walls. My hands trembled as I contemplated the thought of venturing out, and I found myself retreating further and further into the safety of my rooms.

I hit upon a solution – I arranged for my food and flowers to be delivered daily at my doorstep. Ah, bliss! Now I could choose whether to venture out or remain ensconced within my walls, free from the horrors that lurked beyond. And so, I chose to stay, content to exist in a world of my own creation.

But my happiness was not without its price. Discontent seeped in, like a slow-moving fog, and I found myself yearning for something more. I fretted and sighed, sometimes on the verge of tears, yet simultaneously aware that I would not trade my sorrow for the cacophony of the world outside. Noise, once a symphony of sounds that filled my days, now grated on my nerves, a constant reminder of the terror that lurked within me.
