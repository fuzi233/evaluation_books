As the morning sun cast its warm rays through the narrow windows of the cloister, Ghysbrecht Van Swieten carefully retrieved the key to Gerard's prison from his worn leather pouch. He smiled to himself, thinking that a bit of hunger might just be the thing to break the young man's spirit. At exactly ten o'clock, he made his way up the creaking stairs, a freshly baked loaf and a jug of ale in hand, accompanied by his trusted servant, Dierich.

Ghysbrecht paused outside Gerard's cell, listening intently for any sign of movement within. The silence was oppressive, and a sly grin spread across his face. "Albert Koestein was broken by now," he thought to himself, expecting Gerard to be similarly defeated. He pushed open the door, but instead of finding Gerard, he was met with an eerie stillness.

Ghysbrecht's body seemed to freeze, his usual composure deserting him. His servant, sensing something was amiss, crept closer and peeked over his master's shoulder. The sight of the empty cell, the rope hanging limp, and the chest lying open sent a shiver down Dierich's spine. He let out a low whistle of surprise, and his master's reaction only added to the chaos.

Ghysbrecht dropped to his knees beside the chest, frantically feeling its contents as if searching for some hidden truth. His servant watched in confusion, unsure how to react. "Master, what's wrong?" Dierich asked, trying to make sense of the scene unfolding before him.

Ghysbrecht's lips moved, but no words emerged. His hands fell to his sides, and he stared blankly into the chest. The servant's question hung in the air, unanswered. Dierich's gaze followed his master's, and he spotted the empty space where the iron bar had once held Gerard captive. "Ah, see here! The clever rascal took out the bar, and—"

Ghysbrecht's eyes snapped back to life, his voice rising in a crescendo. "GONE! GONE! GONE!" Dierich's eyes widened in alarm as his master's expression transformed from shock to fury. "Stop thief!" Ghysbrecht bellowed, grabbing Dierich and shaking him. "Run! Find it! A hundred crowns to whoever retrieves it for me! No, no! It's all lost, all lost!"
