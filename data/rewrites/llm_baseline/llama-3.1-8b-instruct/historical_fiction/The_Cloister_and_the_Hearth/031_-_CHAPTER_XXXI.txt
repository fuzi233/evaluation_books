As the sun began to set, casting a warm orange glow over the rolling hills, Gerard and Denys found themselves standing at a crossroads, facing a quaint little town and a monastery nestled off the beaten path. Denys, ever the romantic, was drawn to the prospect of a cozy night at the local inn, but Gerard's eyes lingered on the monastery's weathered stone façade. He suggested they take a detour, and to his surprise, Denys agreed, albeit reluctantly, on the condition that they would always opt for the inns of Burgundy once they arrived.

As they made their way towards the monastery, Gerard couldn't help but ask why his friend had suddenly developed a distaste for the humble abodes that had provided them with free lodging and sustenance so many times before. Denys hesitated, then revealed a long-held secret: his aversion to monasteries was rooted in a past experience that still rankled. "I recall the powdered drink they served me," he confessed, his voice tinged with bitterness. "It was a subtle poison, one that made me feel like a fish out of water among those stern-faced monks."

Gerard listened intently as Denys recounted his tale of woe, his words painting a vivid picture of life within the monastery's cold, grey walls. "The dortour was a prison, the great refectory a place of forced silence, where one's every word was policed and judged," Denys said, his voice rising in indignation. "And then, of course, there was the bell – that incessant, maddening tinkle that seemed to pierce the very soul. It was a sound that struck at the heart of all joy and freedom, leaving one feeling like a slave to its whims."

Gerard nodded sympathetically, understanding the depth of his friend's animosity. "I see," he said gently. "You feel trapped and stifled by the rules and rituals of the monastery." Denys nodded vigorously, his eyes flashing with emotion. "Aye, and the worst part is, they have no concept of warmth or hospitality. A simple gesture, like wiping one's knife on the cloth, is met with scolding and rebuke. It's as if they've forgotten the value of good fellowship and the simple pleasures of life."
