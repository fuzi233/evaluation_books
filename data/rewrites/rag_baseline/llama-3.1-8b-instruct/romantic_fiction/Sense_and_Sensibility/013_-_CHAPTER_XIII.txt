The excursion to Whitwell, intended to be a leisurely morning out among the rolling hills and verdant pastures, began with unfulfilled expectations. Elinor had anticipated a day spent getting wet, tired, and perhaps a bit anxious, but the reality was far more disheartening: they wouldn't be going at all. By ten o'clock, the entire party had gathered at the park, where they were scheduled to break their fast. Although the morning's weather had shown signs of improvement, with the clouds dispersing and the sun occasionally peeking through, the remnants of the previous night's rain still lingered.

As they sat down to eat, the letters were brought in. Amidst the pile, one caught Colonel Brandon's eye – he took it, examined the direction, and hastily excused himself from the room. "What's wrong with Brandon?" Sir John asked, his brow furrowed with concern.

No one seemed to know. "I hope it's not bad news," Lady Middleton ventured, her voice tinged with worry. "It must be something extraordinary to make Colonel Brandon leave my breakfast table so abruptly."

A few minutes passed before he returned. "No bad news, I assure you," Mrs. Jennings said, as soon as he entered the room. "Was it from Avignon? I hope it's not to say that your sister is worse."

"No, ma'am, it came from town, and it's merely a letter of business," Colonel Brandon replied, his expression guarded. But his manner suggested that this was far from the truth.

Mrs. Jennings pressed him for more information. "But how did the hand discompose you so much, if it was only a letter of business? Come, come, Colonel, let's hear the truth of it."

Lady Middleton cautioned her mother to be mindful of her words, but Mrs. Jennings was undeterred. "Perhaps it's to tell you that your cousin Fanny is married?" she suggested, her eyes sparkling with curiosity.

"No, indeed, it is not," Colonel Brandon said, his color rising slightly. Mrs. Jennings' next question, however, was more perceptive. "Well, then, I know who it is from, Colonel. And I hope she is well."

"Whom do you mean, ma'am?" he asked, his tone polite but guarded.

"Oh, you know who I mean," she replied, her voice dripping with implication.
