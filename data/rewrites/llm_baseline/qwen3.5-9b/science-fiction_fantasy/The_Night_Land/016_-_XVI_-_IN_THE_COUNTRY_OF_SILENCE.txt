I approached the Mighty Pyramid, looming closer with every step, while my home ascended vast into the everlasting night like a mountain of sweet life and safety. Its sheer magnitude should have filled me with awe anew, yet despair and exhaustion gripped my heart so tightly that I cared for nothing but bringing my own Maid safely within the refuge's walls. She remained far ahead, a distant beacon.

I pressed forward across the desolate land with a determined stride. As I passed a hollow where a fire-hole burned, something emerged from the darkness. It crawled upward, revealing itself as a great, hairy man. He looked at me, then advanced eagerly, extending his hands. In the flickering firelight, I saw them clearly: monstrous, armed with brutish, horrid claws capable of tearing flesh like a wild beast.

My own hands flew to the ground; I cared not for life anymore. This creature was delaying me, fueling my fierce despair that anything could halt my progress. Suddenly, I leaped forward with cold fury at the giant. I struck at the monster, but he sidestepped instantly, escaping the blow. From the half-darkness cast by the dancing fire, he flung his massive arm out, snatching my helmet from my head with such brute strength that he threw me nearly twelve feet onto my back.

I was unharmed internally, though shaken and bruised. Rising immediately, I charged the giant again, swinging the Diskos—a weapon that roared and blazed in my grip. I brought it down upon his midsection. The Diskos glutted itself, piercing the giant as if he were nothing, despite his immense size and strength. His upper body collapsed horrifically to the earth, leaving his legs and torso standing stark in the firelight, while blood erupted upward like a fountain against the night sky.

I made no pause. Leaping toward my Maid, I gathered her into my arms and surged onward past the fallen corpse, which crashed down with a terrible sound. The night seemed filled with astonishment and upliftment for the Millions; their spiritual cries echoed around me, telling me they had witnessed this feat and poured their love and delight upon me, stirring a vast excitement within them.

Scarcely had I traveled another mile when two vague shapes emerged from a dark rocky area. I struck them down with the Diskos and continued, unaware of their true nature. After that, it seemed I was fighting forever. Strange things crept from bushes and rocks repeatedly, making the entire land crawl with foul, monstrous life. I moved as in a dream, smiting relentlessly, driven by increasingly fierce despair. Surely, the end of our lives had arrived, and I lacked the power to save my Maid.

The land groaned with grim, monstrous roars and eerie, deadly low sounds. Once, I heard the noise of giants running. All night, evil reigned. How I avoided being slain by some dread force remains unknown, unless I had been burned free of all weaknesses that an Evil Power could exploit, thanks to the bitter training I endured for so long.

Then, the deep, dreadful baying of the Night-Hounds returned from the Southeast, growing nearer. I realized no strength of mine could protect my own. Suddenly, from the upper reaches of the night where the Last Light hung, a strange blue flash descended, striking the land toward the Southeast. Again, and perhaps a dozen times thereafter, flashes fell from the heights, accompanied by a peculiar crackling sound—less thunderous than the storms of this age, yet louder than any other sound imaginable. I knew then that the Humans were beginning to fight for me, striving to bring my Maid safely home.

It felt as though all previous wakefulness in the land was merely sleep compared to the awakening now occurring. The night rocked with the roarings of monsters and the stirring of great forces. Overhead, the yowling of that strange, dreadful laughter drifted from the hidden country in the lost East. Constantly, the hoarse, terrifying bays of the hounds signaled a mighty pack on the hunt. They appeared no more than a mile away to the Southeast. I stood alone, holding only the dying Maid in my arms. I looked vainly and desperately for the Hundred Thousand prepared to aid me, who had supposedly descended to my rescue. Truly, there was nothing to see but strange lights and shadows, and the movement of monstrous life everywhere. The hounds closed in with every passing moment; death was imminent.

Yet, I ceased not my stride. I began to run, for the Pyramid was not far in the night. The shine of the Circle surrounding it was plainly visible, save where obscured by strange shadows. A despairing hope fueled me: perhaps I could still reach the safety of the Circle with my Maid.

The baying grew ever nearer. It was doubly hideous and bitter to lose my beloved one so close to home, watching the great mountain of my sanctuary rise before me into the night, appearing so near that I felt almost there, yet perhaps still two great miles away. Behold, I cried out in vain despair, wondering why no aid came in this extremity. The hounds bayed now but half a mile to my left, surely having scented me by their dreadful cries.

Truly, the Millions felt an anguish of sympathy for me. The spiritual noise of their emotion was plain to my spirit. They had seen and interpreted my desperate looks and calls. In a moment, a great and sweet spiritual force surrounded me, born of their shared understanding and love. They perceived I was nearing the end of hope, and the hounds were almost upon me.

In this critical moment, the shaking beat of the Earth-Current reached my ears. I knew the Humans were taking desperate measures to save us. To my left, a vast pack of hounds rushed forward at a great pace, their heads low, towering as large as horses. Seen plainly, then vanishing into shadow, then reappearing—all in the same breath—as they closed in.

In truth, I knew we would both be dead within a minute if the Humans did not act swiftly. I stopped where I stood; there was no point in running further. Looking from the hounds to the Mighty Pyramid, and back again, my hope faded. The hounds were scarcely two hundred fathoms away, numbering in the hundreds. Just as I took that final glance at the Pyramid, a monstrous bursting flame erupted from the sealed lower section of the structure. The flame rushed downward, striking the land where the hounds ran. All the night vanished from my sight in the brightness and strangeness of that mighty flame. I saw no more of the Pyramid or anything else, only the shining, dreadful glory of the fire.

The flame created a blast in the night and a heat that seemed to wither me even at a distance. I perceived that the Humans had truly unleashed the Earth-Current upon the hounds to ensure my survival. A constant great thundering rolled over the land as the Earth-Force tore and split the air, ripping up the earth. The roaring of the monsters was drowned out by this mighty sound. I saw no trace of the hounds, only flames and broken landscapes where the Earth-Force struck. Great rocks were hurled everywhere with a vast noise. Truly, it was a mercy I was not slain a hundred times by the failings and burstings of these great rocks and boulders.

In a moment, the Humans cut off the Earth-Force, regaining control. Now, a great silence settled over the land, an utter darkness, save for the lingering flames and noise from where the Current had struck. Quickly recovering from the daze, I resumed my run. In truth, it seemed I might yet win safety with my Maid.

My eyes adjusted to their usual function. I scanned all directions, fearing some new threat might claim us even then. For a while, I saw nothing, nor heard the waking sounds of the land, except for the grim, horrid laughter drifting from the dead East.

Often, as I ran, I stared hungrily upward at the Mighty Pyramid. It always seemed less bright than before. Initially, I attributed this to my eyes being dazzled by the great Flame of the Earth-Force. Soon, however, I realized it was otherwise: there was truly a diminished brightness emanating from the Mighty Redoubt. I conceived this loss owed to the great expenditure of the Earth-Current's power used to save us. This thought chilled my heart deeply. If the Force of the Current dropped too low, there would be danger for all the Humans living, including the great Millions of the Mighty Refuge. This was surely known by the Masters; they would have no more power to aid me with the Current until it flowed strong again, lest they destroy all the peoples of the Earth. All this became clear to me in a moment as I ran, making me even more desperate to reach instant safety with the Maid.

Surely, I expected the Hundred Thousand to arrive, but they did not come. Around me, the land began to emit the noises of the monsters again, sending forth new and peculiar sounds, indicating more entities were awake than I had ever heard before. Presently, I saw living things creeping between me and the light of the Circle. I knew I still faced a bitter fight if I wished to bring the Maid safe. I swung the Diskos free and ran on.

Suddenly, my spirit warned me of a new peril. I looked upward into the night, hoping the Master Monstruwacan might signal the danger via the Set Speech. But truly, there came no quick flashings of the Set Speech; only an upward stillness and a dimming of the Pyramid's lights.
