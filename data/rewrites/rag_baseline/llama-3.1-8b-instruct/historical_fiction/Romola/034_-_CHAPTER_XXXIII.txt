Baldassarre trudged up the hill of San Giorgio, the moonlit slope unfolding like a patchwork quilt beneath his feet. The city of Florence lay before him, its rooftops a tangled tapestry of terracotta and terra cotta, the Arno River a silver serpent winding through the heart of the metropolis. His stomach growled with hunger, and the prospect of finding a humble outhouse for the night seemed a distant dream. Bratti had told him that Tito's dwelling was in the Via de' Bardi, and he hoped to find a refuge nearby.

As he climbed, the air thickened with the scent of blooming jasmine and the distant tang of woodsmoke. The hill was sparsely inhabited, its gardens a verdant oasis amidst the stone and brick of the city. A rough patch of ground, scarred by ancient landslides, stretched out before him like a barren wasteland. Amidst this desolation, a peculiar little building rose, its truncated tower roofed in fluted tiles, a testament to the city's ancient history.

Nearby, a small outhouse leaned against a ruined stone wall, its door ajar as if inviting him in. Under a half-dead mulberry tree, its branches etched against the moon like skeletal fingers, an elderly woman tended to a goat and its kids. Her weathered hands moved with a practiced ease, and Baldassarre sensed that she was a woman who had lived a hard life. As he approached, she looked up, her blue eyes narrowing as she took in his foreign features and rough-hewn clothing.

Baldassarre tried to communicate with her, but his words were lost in the night air until he drew close and spoke directly into her ear. She shook her head, her expression a mask of deafness, but he persisted, explaining that he was a poor traveler from afar, seeking only a simple shelter for the night. She regarded him warily, her gaze flicking to the abandoned outhouse and back to him, before a glint of calculation appeared in her eyes.

She produced a hatchet from the house and gestured to a chunk of wood lying in the corner, partially buried in litter.
