Gerald's departure allowed Sally to focus on the many positive developments that had taken place. Fillmore's theatrical enterprise continued to thrive, with "The Primrose Way" captivating audiences and critics alike in Chicago. The city, it seemed, had become obsessed with the play, its residents enthralled by the magic of the stage. As Fillmore recounted, the production had brought a sense of unity to the city, a shared experience that transcended petty squabbles and local concerns.

For Sally, however, the most significant achievement was the solution to Ginger's future. With his appointment as a member of Fillmore's staff, Ginger had finally found stability and purpose. Though his role remained somewhat undefined, Ginger was content to serve as a guardian of sorts, protecting Fillmore from the constant influx of visitors and admirers. His gratitude towards Sally was palpable, and he often expressed it through his actions, as he did during their frequent visits to Sally's new apartment.
