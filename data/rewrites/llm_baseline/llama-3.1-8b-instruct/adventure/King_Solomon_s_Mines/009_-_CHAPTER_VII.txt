As we emerged from the cave, the harsh sunlight stung our eyes, making them water in the biting wind. We stood there, hesitant and uncertain, unsure of what to do next.

"I'm going back," Sir Henry said, his voice firm.

"Why?" Good asked, his brow furrowed in concern.

"It's just a thought," Sir Henry replied, "but what we saw...it might be my brother."

His words sent a shiver down my spine as we turned to re-enter the cave. The darkness within was almost palpable, a heavy shroud that wrapped around us like a damp cloak. Our eyes, still reeling from the glare outside, struggled to adjust to the dim light within. Slowly, however, our vision began to clear, and we made our way back to the dead man.

Sir Henry knelt beside him, his eyes scanning the face with a mixture of hope and trepidation. "Thank God," he breathed, relief washing over him. "It's not my brother."

I approached cautiously, my gaze falling upon the corpse. The body was that of a man in middle life, with a strong jawline and a long, grizzled beard. His skin was a deep, mottled yellow, stretched taut over the skeletal frame. A yellow ivory crucifix hung around his neck, a stark contrast to the stark, frozen landscape surrounding us. His clothing, save for a pair of worn woolen hose, had been stripped away, leaving him exposed to the elements.

"Who could it be?" I whispered, my voice barely audible over the howling wind.

Good's eyes sparkled with a hint of mischief. "Can't you guess?"

I shook my head, my mind racing with possibilities.

"Ah, come now," Good said with a grin. "It's the old Dom, José da Silvestra, of course. Who else?"

I felt a jolt of incredulity. "Impossible," I gasped. "He's been dead for three centuries."

Good's expression turned thoughtful. "What's to say he won't last for three millennia in this cold? If the temperature stays low enough, flesh and blood will preserve like the finest game meat. And look," he said, stooping to pick up a peculiarly shaped bone, "here's the 'cleft bone' he used to draw the map with."
