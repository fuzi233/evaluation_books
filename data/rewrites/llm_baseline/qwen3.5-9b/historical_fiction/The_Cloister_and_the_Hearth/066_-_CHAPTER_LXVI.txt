Two nights following this event, Pietro Vanucci and Andrea waited expectantly for Gerard's arrival at supper. As the clock struck past nine, Pietro's patience began to fray, his demeanor growing increasingly irritable. Finally, driven by desperation, he dispatched Andrea to Gerard's chamber, hoping against hope that the scholar had slipped inside unnoticed. Andrea merely shrugged, accepting the mission, and departed.

He returned empty-handed regarding Gerard's presence but clutching a folded scrap of paper. In that era, scholars possessed the literacy that modern charity schools still strive to instill; yet Andrea, though illiterate, possessed a sharp eye and had long since memorized the distinctive handwriting of Pietro Vanucci. "That is for you, I believe," Andrea declared, puffing slightly with pride at his deduction.

Pietro snatched the note and read aloud, his tone laced with biting satire. "'Dearest Pietro, dearest Andrea, life is a burden too heavy to bear.'"

"Indeed it is, my boy," Pietro retorted dryly, "but that offers no excuse for wandering the streets during supper hour. Supper itself is no burden."

"'Wear my religious habits!'"

"Said the poplar tree to the humble juniper bush," Pietro mocked.

"And you, Andrea, take my amethyst ring; and keep me in both your hearts for a month or two."

"Why, Andrea?" Pietro pressed, sensing a shift in the wind.

"For my body, before you finish reading this, it shall lie within the Tiber," Andrea recited, his voice trembling. "Do not trouble yourself to search for it; it is not worth the effort. Alas, what an unfortunate day it was when I was born! Oh, blessed night that finally frees me from its grasp!"

"'Farewell! Farewell!'"

"The broken-hearted Gerard."

"This is a lamentable jest from a peevish fool," Pietro muttered aloud, attempting to mask his own pallor and the violent tremors shaking his teeth. Yet, his composure crumbled under the weight of the news.

Andrea, overcome with panic, filled the household with shrill cries. "Oh, wretched day! Oh, catastrophe upon catastrophe! Gerard, my friend, my beloved patron! Help! Help! He is destroying himself! Oh, good souls, aid me in saving him!"

His alarm spread rapidly through the house, but once outside, his pleas fell on deaf ears. A crowd gathered, yet Andrea's frantic energy failed to stir their lethargy. "Shall we descend to the river?" someone suggested cautiously. "Nay," another replied, "that is none of our concern." "But which part of the river?" "It is folly to venture near the water after sunset; too many ghosts haunt those banks throughout the night."

However, a servant who had lingered opposite the house volunteered to accompany Andrea. His involvement drew three or four other youths, forming a small, eager band. They hurried toward the riverbank. The servant questioned Andrea, who, weeping bitterly, recounted the letter and Gerard's recent melancholy. Unbeknownst to them, this servant was a spy for Princess Claelia. Their Italian conversation flowed swiftly until they approached the Tiber.

Yet, the mere scent of the cool spring breeze and the mist rising from the stream silenced them instantly. The moon hung high and clear in a cloudless sky, casting a stark light upon the scene. Their footsteps echoed loudly and ominously on the stones, while their voices died away completely.

Suddenly, rounding a corner, they encountered a solitary figure. The man paused, hesitating at their sudden appearance. He was bareheaded, his hair dripping wet and gleaming in the moonlight. Upon closer inspection, his garments were soaked through.

"He is here!" cried one of the youths, who did not recognize Gerard's face.

The stranger spun around and bolted. The group pursued him with renewed vigor, Andrea leading the charge, followed closely by the princess's agent. Andrea closed the distance momentarily, but the fugitive twisted sharply into a narrow alleyway. Unable to stop his momentum, Andrea shot past, leaving the others behind. Soon, the pursuers found themselves lost in a labyrinthine maze, chasing a swift-footed escapee who knew every shadowed inch of the path, audible only by the sound of his fleeing breath.

They returned to their companions, who remained frozen at the spot where the man had vanished, utterly bewildered. "Are you certain?" they asked. "He had just emerged from the river. By the saints' names, why did he flee at our approach?"

Then spoke Vanucci: "Friends, I perceive this has nothing to do with the man we seek. What shall we do, Andrea?"

At this point, the spy interjected. "Let us follow the trail to the water's edge to confirm. Observe; he came dripping all the way."

This counsel was accepted, and with ease, they retraced the man's steps. However, a new puzzle presented itself almost immediately. Having traveled barely fifty yards, the trail of moisture veered away from the river, directing them instead toward the gate of a large, gloomy structure—a monastery. They halted before the dark edifice, staring into its shadows, feeling as though it concealed some terrible secret.

Suddenly, Andrea let out a triumphant shout. "Behold! The drops return!" he cried. "And this path leads straight to the river."

Resuming their pursuit, it quickly became evident that the wet footprints were guiding them homeward. The trail grew progressively deeper and wetter until they reached the very brink of the Tiber. There, on the muddy bank, lay a puddle clearly formed by a bucketful of water dumped directly from the stream.

Initially, they shouted in triumph, believing they had solved the mystery. But reflection soon set in; the evidence meant little. Undoubtedly, a man had entered the water and emerged safely, yet that individual was not Gerard. One suggested knowing a fisherman nearby equipped with nets and drags. They located the fisher and paid him generously to cast nets downstream and haul them upstream and downstream, promising a golden reward if he discovered the body.

Thus, they wandered uselessly along the riverbanks, which flowed with a strange, voiceless calm, guarding a thousand such mysteries.

Suddenly, Andrea burst back toward the house, his face alight with a cry of false hope. He returned in less than thirty minutes.

"No," he groaned, wringing his hands in despair. "What hour is it?" the spy asked.

"It is four hours past midnight," Andrea whispered.

"My pretty lad," the spy said solemnly, "pray say a mass for your friend's soul; for he is no longer among the living."

Morning broke over a silent city. Exhausted by fatigue, Andrea and Pietro trudged home, their hearts heavy with sickness. Days rolled on, as mute and indifferent as the Tiber, offering no answer to the fate of Gerard.
