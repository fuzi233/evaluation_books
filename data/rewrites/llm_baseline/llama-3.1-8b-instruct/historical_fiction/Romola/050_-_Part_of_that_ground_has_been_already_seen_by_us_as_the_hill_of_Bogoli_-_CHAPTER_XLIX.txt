As the chill of winter clung to Florence, the days blended together in a monotony of sameness, like the white ships that glided silently along the Arno, each bearing within them the seeds of transformation. Romola's life had become a careful balance of duty and trepidation, her thoughts consumed by the tumultuous world beyond her doorstep. Tito's words had instilled a sense of foreboding, and she found solace in the familiar rhythms of her daily routine.

The German Emperor's departure had brought a measure of relief, but the city's troubles lingered, and Romola's responsibilities remained unchanged. Her mind, ever a sanctuary from doubt, found comfort in the tangible tasks that filled her days. Yet, with the Frate's party now triumphant, Romola couldn't help but feel a growing unease. Francesco Valori, the fiery leader of the Piagnoni, had taken the reins as Gonfaloniere, and his zeal for reform had ignited a fire that threatened to consume everything in its path.

As Carnaval drew near, Romola's apprehensions intensified. The air was electric with tension, and the Mediceans were stirring, determined to undermine the Piagnoni's grip on power. By the last day of the festival, the city was abuzz with anticipation. Romola agreed to meet her cousin Brigida at the Corso degli Albizzi, planning to join the crowds in the Piazza della Signoria for a spectacle unlike any other.

The Piazza was a tapestry of color and sound, a kaleidoscope of laughter and music. Amidst the revelry, a towering pyramid stood tall, its sixty-foot height a marvel of engineering. The structure, adorned with shelves and platforms, was a riot of color, its base stretching eighty yards wide. Young figures, dressed in white, moved with purpose, their olive wreaths a symbol of the season's themes. Artists and monks mingled, their faces intent on the task at hand, while a small group, including the renowned Piero di Cosimo, stood apart, their expressions a mix of disdain and disappointment.

Romola approached the pyramid, her footsteps lost amidst the throngs of people. As she drew closer, she took in the sheer magnitude of the structure, its tiers a testament to the creativity and skill of the artisans who had built it.
