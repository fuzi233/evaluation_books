As the last echoes of the Cathedral service faded into the warm summer air, Rosa stood alone in the tranquil haven of the Nuns' House, her heart heavy with the weight of memories. The morning's festivities, marked by Miss Twinkleton's parting words and the sweet scent of pound cake, now seemed a distant recollection. Helena Landless had departed to tend to her brother's affairs, leaving Rosa to face the solitude that had grown increasingly oppressive.

Outside, the Cathedral and the ancient monastery ruin stood bathed in a soft, golden light, their stone facades glowing with an ethereal radiance that seemed to emanate from within. The nearby gardens, ablaze with ripening fruit, seemed to whisper secrets to the passing breeze, their vibrant colors a stark contrast to the dusty, travel-worn individuals who wandered the streets, seeking refuge from the scorching heat. The city's pumps, usually bustling with activity, now stood quiet, their metal surfaces reflecting the stillness of the afternoon.

It was on this tranquil afternoon, when the High Street cast long shadows across the pavement, that a servant arrived to inform Rosa that Mr. Jasper desired to see her. The news sent a shiver down her spine, and for a moment, she felt trapped, her mind racing with desperate thoughts. Had he chosen this moment to find her alone? If so, it was a calculated move, one that struck fear into her heart.

"What shall I do?" Rosa's hands fluttered to her lips, as if searching for answers. The maid's words echoed in her mind – Mr. Jasper had not asked if she was at home, but had simply stated that he knew she was, and requested to see her. A sense of desperation took hold, and in a burst of resolve, Rosa decided to meet him in the garden, where she could still maintain some semblance of freedom.

She donned her garden hat, its familiar weight a comfort against the uncertainty that lay ahead. As she stepped into the garden, the warm sunlight danced across her face, but her eyes were drawn to the figure standing beside the sun-dial. Mr. Jasper, his features etched in somber lines, leaned against the stone, his dark attire a testament to the depths of his sorrow.
