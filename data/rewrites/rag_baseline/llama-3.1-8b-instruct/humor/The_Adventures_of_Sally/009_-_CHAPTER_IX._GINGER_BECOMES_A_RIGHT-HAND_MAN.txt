As the afternoon sun filtered through the dusty windows of the boarding house, Ginger stirred from a restless slumber, his cheek pressed against the cool floorboards. The smell of stale air and yesterday's cooking wafted up, a familiar scent that brought little comfort. He rubbed his gritty eyes, the grittiness a reminder of his nocturnal escapades. 

Sally's room was small, cluttered with the detritus of a life lived in transit. Ginger's gaze drifted to the bed, its rumpled sheets a testament to the previous occupant's hasty departure. His own presence, hidden beneath it, was an afterthought, a secret kept from prying eyes. 

He sat up, his joints creaking in protest, and scrubbed his face with a grimy hand. The gesture was habitual, a ritual performed to ward off the unease that lingered long after the initial fright had passed. 

Sally's laughter pierced the air, a sound that sent a shiver down his spine. He turned to face her, her eyes sparkling with mirth, and felt a flush rise to his cheeks. "I say, you know," he ventured, trying to sound nonchalant, "I think I might have overstayed my welcome."

Sally's grin faltered, replaced by a look of genuine concern. "Oh, I'm so glad to see you," she said, her voice warm. "I was worried sick." Her gaze roamed over him, taking in the smudge on his nose and the rumpled state of his hair. "You're a bit of a mess, aren't you?"

Ginger's eyes darted to the mirror, a faint smudge indeed marred his otherwise smooth complexion. "Ah, yes," he muttered, snatching a handkerchief from his pocket. "I seem to have acquired a few...characteristics."

Sally's eyes crinkled at the corners as she smiled. "You should really try to avoid hiding under beds, dear. It's not very becoming." Her tone was gentle, but Ginger detected a hint of teasing beneath the surface.

He hesitated, unsure how to respond. The truth – that he had been fleeing Mrs. Meecher's wrath – seemed too complicated to articulate. Instead, he opted for a half-truth. "I was just...resting. Yes, that's it. Resting."
