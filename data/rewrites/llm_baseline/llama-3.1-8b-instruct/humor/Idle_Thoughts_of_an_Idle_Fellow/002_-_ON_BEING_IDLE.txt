The art of being idle had long been my forte, a gift that I possessed in abundance, though I never sought to claim it as my own. My grandmother, that wise and witty matriarch, had often observed that I was destined to leave undone much that I ought to do, but that I would indeed accomplish all that I should not. And so it has proven, for despite my propensity for idleness, I have managed to do my fair share of things that I ought not to have done.

Yet, in one respect, her words have held true – I have consistently neglected much that I should not have neglected. It is a peculiar talent, this ability to be idle, and one that few possess. The genuine idler is not one who lounges about with a slovenly air, but rather one who is always engaged, even if it is merely in the pursuit of nothingness.

Idling, I've found, requires a certain amount of work to truly be enjoyed. Without it, time becomes a burden, an exhausting occupation that drains one's energy. Like a whispered secret, idleness must be savored, stolen moments snatched from the busyness of life. Many years ago, when I was a young man, I learned this lesson the hard way, forced to spend a month in Buxton under doctor's orders to do nothing, to rest and recover from an illness that had beset me.

The prospect was tantalizing – a chance to indulge in the sweet pleasure of doing nothing, with the added allure of a touch of illness to lend it a certain poignancy. I envisioned myself lounging in a hammock, surrounded by the soothing sounds of nature, lost in the pages of a sentimental novel, its melancholy ending a perfect accompaniment to my languid state. Or, when the weather permitted, I would sit propped up in a window seat, a vision of wistful elegance, as the world went by outside, oblivious to my idyllic existence.

Twice a day, I would be wheeled to the Colonnade to drink the waters, a ritual that promised both rejuvenation and sophistication. I had heard tell of these waters, how they were a fashion among the aristocracy, a nod to a bygone era. But, alas, their reality was far from the romantic notion I had conjured.
