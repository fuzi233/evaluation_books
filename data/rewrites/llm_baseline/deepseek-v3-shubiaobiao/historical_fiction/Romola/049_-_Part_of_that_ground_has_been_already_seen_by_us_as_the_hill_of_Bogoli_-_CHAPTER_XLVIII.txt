The fading light of evening cast long shadows across Romola’s chamber as she bent over her work, the delicate strokes of her pen slowing as dusk encroached. She had nearly set the page aside when the door opened, and Tito stepped inside. There was an unfamiliar rigidity in his posture, a coldness in his gaze that made her fingers still. He did not remove his cloak or cap but stood before her, one elbow resting on the cabinet, his presence deliberate, his silence weighted.

Romola had spent the day wrestling with remorse. The Frate was safe—her fears had been unfounded—and the suspicion she had voiced so publicly now seemed hasty, unjust. She had resolved to speak softly, to bridge the chasm between them. But the hardness in Tito’s eyes chilled her.

"You took a step this morning," he said at last, his voice smooth and deliberate, "one that was not only futile but reckless. It exposed us both—me to political peril, you to ridicule."

She met his gaze, her own expression contrite. "I acted rashly. I regret any harm I may have caused you."

His lips thinned. "Regret is not enough. I need you to understand that such impulses cannot be repeated. Our positions demand discretion—yours as my wife, mine as a man whose influence hangs by a thread."

The rebuke stung. "And what of your discretion?" she countered, her voice tightening despite herself. "If you trusted me, if you were open with me—"

"Open?" His laugh was brittle. "So you might dissect every move I make? You, who confessed last night you have no faith in me?"

She flinched but pressed on. "I want to believe you. But you shut me out. You treat me as though I am incapable of reason."

He studied her, unmoved. "And if I were to share every secret, what then? Would you rush to the Signoria again, crying conspiracy? Do you know what you risk? Not just my disgrace—but the ruin of men like Bernardo del Nero. Is that what you want?"

The blood drained from her face. The accusation struck like a blade. "I would never—"

"Yet you nearly did." His voice was merciless. "Florence is not a stage for your righteous dramas. Misjudge again, and the blood will be on your hands."

She swallowed, her defiance crumbling. "I will be more cautious."

"See that you are." His gaze did not waver. "Or the next time you act on impulse, you may find you have lit a fire that consumes us all."

A silence stretched between them, heavy with unsaid things. Then Romola straightened, her pride rallying. "Is this how it will always be? Walls instead of words?"

Tito’s expression did not soften. "Some walls are built by necessity. You chose to doubt me long ago. That doubt has shaped us both."

She turned away, despair settling deep. There was no reaching him. No matter how she pleaded, the distance between them would remain—unyielding as stone.

And as Tito left the room, his mind was already turning to other calculations. Florence was becoming precarious. Romola, once a jewel in his life, was now a liability.

That night, before venturing out again, he fastened his coat of chain-armour beneath his cloak. The world was not safe. And neither, he knew, was his own house.
