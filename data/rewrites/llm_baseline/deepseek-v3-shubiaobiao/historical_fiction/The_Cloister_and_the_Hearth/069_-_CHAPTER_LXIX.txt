In the dimly lit guest chamber of a Dominican convent, a lone figure lay upon a narrow bed, his body wracked with exhaustion. The violent fits of nausea that had tormented him had finally subsided, leaving him as weak as Margaret had been that fateful night in Holland. A fire crackled on the hearth, its orange glow flickering over the walls, and beside it hung the stranger’s damp clothes, slowly drying in the warmth.

A massive friar sat at his bedside, the leather-bound breviary in his hands whispering with each turn of a page. The patient’s eyes flickered open occasionally, drawn by the murmured prayers, before fluttering shut again with a groan. The monk knelt, pressing his forehead to the floor in fervent supplication before rising with a sigh.

“Day breaks,” he said softly. “I must prepare for matins.”

The stranger stirred. “Father Jerome—before you go—how did I come to be here?”

The friar’s voice was solemn. “By the hand of Heaven. You cast away God’s gift, and He returned it to you. Think on that. You have tasted the bitterness of the world. Now let the Church offer you peace.”

With a final blessing, the friar departed, leaving the man alone with his thoughts. He drifted in and out of consciousness, the events of the past hours slipping through his fingers like water.

When he next awoke, a new figure sat beside him—a layman with sharp, restless eyes, a stark contrast to the tranquil presence of Friar Jerome. The man leaned forward, his voice low.

“How do you feel?”

“Weak… terribly weak.” The stranger frowned. “Where have I seen you before?”

The man hesitated, then gave a grim smile. “None the worse for my gauntlet, I hope? I struck you—else we’d both be at the bottom of the Tiber.”

Recognition flickered in the stranger’s eyes. “You—you saved me?”

“Aye. I was by the river on… business, when you came to me, begging for death. But before I could oblige, I knew you—the man who once saved my wife and child upon the sea.”

A shudder passed through the stranger. “Teresa’s husband. And yet—an assassin?”

“At your service,” the man replied dryly. “You flung yourself into the Tiber, commanded me to stand aside. Had it been anyone else, I’d have obeyed. But you—you cried out a name as you sank. *Margaret.*”

The stranger’s breath hitched.

“And my heart answered with *Teresa*,” the assassin continued. “How could I face her if I let you die? So I went in after you. You fought me—but I dragged you to shore.”

The stranger closed his eyes, the weight of his own folly pressing down on him. He had cursed Heaven, defied it—and yet here he was, pulled from death by the hand of a killer. Was this divine mercy or mockery?

A distant organ swelled, its notes rising through the convent walls, followed by the pure voices of the choir. Among them, one stood out—a boy’s voice, clear and angelic, lifting toward the heavens. The sound wrapped around him, stirring memories of his own youth, of simpler days when faith had been unshaken.

A tear slipped down his cheek. “The Church is peace,” he whispered. “And I have strayed so far.”

Then, as if in answer to his grief, a familiar face appeared—Father Anselm, the man who had once tended his wounded body in Juliers. The sight of him was like a balm.

“Father,” he choked out, “you healed my flesh before. Can you now heal my soul?”

Anselm’s expression softened. He sat beside him, placing a gentle hand upon his brow. “Confess, my son. Unburden yourself.”

And so, with trembling voice, the stranger poured out his sins—his rage, his despair, his defiance of God. Anselm listened, his eyes filled with sorrow and understanding.

“Your sins are great,” he said at last. “But so too was your temptation. Rest now. I will speak with the prior.”

As Anselm left, the stranger clasped his hands together, his lips moving in silent prayer. For the first time in months, he felt the crushing weight begin to lift.

Penitence had come at last. And with it, the longing to remain within these walls—to turn his back on the world that had brought him only pain.

Beyond the convent, whispers spread of his death. The Princess Claelia, upon hearing the news, stood frozen, her face a mask of horror.

“So soon?” she breathed. Then her grief twisted into fury. “Then let his murderer follow him.”

And in the shadows of her palace, steel glinted as men waited, poised to strike when the traitor came to claim his reward.
