Martin Wittenhaagen stood frozen at the base of the stairwell, his bowstring pulled taut against his cheek, the arrow nocked and trembling near the fletching. Behind him, the heavy blade of his knife rested against his thigh. A profound shock seized him as the returning men shuffled past without Gerard. Confusion rippled through his features; he lowered the weapon, his mouth agape, staring at them as if they were ghosts. The men, in turn, halted, their faces etched with bewilderment at the sight of the old giant poised like a hunter about to strike.

"Why, mates," one of them chuckled nervously, "was the old fellow preparing to loose an arrow at us?"

"Silence!" Martin barked, forcing his stony demeanor back into place. "I was merely testing the tension of this new string. Behold!" He reached for the bowstring to loosen it, offering a display of feigned innocence.

"Hmph," grunted Dierich, his eyes narrowing with suspicion. "There is more to you than meets the eye, old man. Put a log upon the fire and let us warm our frozen hides before we ascend further."

A crackling blaze soon roared to life, casting dancing shadows against the stone walls. The men huddled around the heat, stripping off their cloaks and letting the flames lick at their long, matted hair until the scent of singed wool filled the air. It was then that the shrill, piercing cries erupted from Margaret's chamber above. Every head snapped upward in alarm. One of the men snatched a candle from the hearth and bolted up the narrow staircase.

Martin surged to his feet, his heart hammering against his ribs. The sudden screams, mingled with the intruder's frantic movements, ignited a surge of protective fury. Driven by a desperate need to shield the woman within, he lunged forward to intercept the man with the light.

Dierich moved with surprising speed, wrapping his arms around Martin's broad chest from behind and shouting for the others to hold him fast. The man with the candle slipped past their grasp, but the remaining men swarmed Martin. A brutal contest ensued, a chaotic tumble of limbs and curses where the three assailants rolled across the floor, pinning the massive Samson beneath them. With ropes meant for Gerard already in hand, they bound Martin's wrists and ankles, rendering the great man utterly helpless.

Martin groaned, a low sound of anguish tearing from his throat. He watched helplessly as the intruder, having navigated the struggle, slipped into Margaret's room. Now, he was a prisoner, his strength useless against the cords.

"Aye, grind your teeth, you old rascal," Dierich panted, wiping sweat from his brow. "You shall not use them again."

"It is my conviction, mates," another man muttered, "that our very lives hung by a thread while this old brute remained unbound."

"He makes me suspect Gerard is not far behind," someone else offered.

"No such fortune," Dierich retorted sharply. "Hark! Jorian Ketel has been lingering in that girl's chamber far too long. Some of us must ascend and see to him."

The rough laughter that followed this command had barely faded when the sound of hurried footsteps echoed loudly overhead, signaling the arrival of the intruder himself.

"Oh, here he comes at last," Dierich called out, his voice dripping with mockery. "Well, Jorian, pray tell us what mischief awaits us up there?"
