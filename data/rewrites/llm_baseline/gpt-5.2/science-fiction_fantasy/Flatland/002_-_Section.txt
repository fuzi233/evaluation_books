Part I: THIS WORLD

“Be patient, for the world is broad and wide.”

I set these notes down as one sets out a table—first the board laid bare, then the vessels, then the slow and careful naming of each thing that will be eaten and remembered. For in Flatland, as in any well-ordered household, the manner of division matters. A feast without courses is only noise in the mouth; a history without sections is only clamor in the mind.

So let this Chapter be a bill of fare for what follows—twelve plates of our own world, and ten of worlds beyond it. Read the headings as you would read the labels on jars in an old pantry: each name holds a season, each season a scent, and each scent a people.

Thus is the work portioned. The titles may seem at first like dry grains held in the palm, but wait: they will be milled, soaked, warmed, and made to give up their hidden savor. “Be patient,” says the old proverb, as any agrarian elder would murmur while listening for the right hush in the wind before planting, or for the right simmering whisper in a pot before salting. “For the world is broad and wide.” And broadness—whether of land or language or the dimly-imagined spaces beyond—cannot be swallowed whole. It must be cut into sections, tasted in order, and heard with care, lest the loudest voices drown the subtler notes and the memory of what we truly are be lost under the pounding, industrial din of careless speech.
