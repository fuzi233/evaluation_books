The sun beat down on the open plain, casting a golden glow over the advancing columns of warriors. Three distinct groups, each with their own unique rhythm, moved steadily forward, their footsteps echoing through the stillness. As they drew closer, the central column slowed to a stop at the base of a tongue of grassy slope that led up into the heart of the hill. They paused, awaiting the signal to converge and unleash a coordinated assault.

Sir Henry's voice cut through the air, his words laced with a hint of frustration. "A gatling gun would make short work of them, wouldn't it, Good?" he mused. Good's response was a low groan, a testament to the longing for a weapon that seemed as elusive as the wind.

Quatermain, ever the marksman, stood poised, rifle at the ready. His eyes locked onto the tall figure at the forefront of the enemy ranks, the one who seemed to be directing the charge. A wager was proposed, a challenge issued: if Quatermain could hit the commander within five yards, Sir Henry would pay him a sovereign – an offer that piqued the hunter's interest.

Loading the express rifle with precision, Quatermain waited for the perfect moment to strike. His quarry, accompanied by a single orderly, stepped forward, providing a clear target. Taking aim, Quatermain calculated the trajectory, allowing for the drop in distance. The shot rang out, a puff of smoke billowing into the air. But instead of striking true, the bullet whizzed past, hitting the orderly with deadly precision. The soldier crumpled to the ground, while the commander remained untouched, his alarm growing with each passing moment.

Good's words of encouragement only fueled Quatermain's ire. He took aim once more, his anger and pride driving him to fire again. This time, his shot found its mark, striking the commander with fatal force. The regiments watching this display of marksmanship erupted into cheers, seeing it as a sign of their own impending victory. The opposing force faltered, their commander fallen, and began to retreat in disarray.

As the dust settled, Sir Henry and Good joined Quatermain in the firing line, their rifles cracking out shots as the enemy lines advanced. Ignosi contributed his own firepower, taking down several warriors with precision shots.
