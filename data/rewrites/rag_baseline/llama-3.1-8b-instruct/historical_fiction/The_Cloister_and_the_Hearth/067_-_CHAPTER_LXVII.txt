In the fading light of dusk, Gerard stole down to the river, his footsteps echoing off the water's edge. He had made his decision with a resolute determination, one that echoed the desperation of those who, driven by a singular purpose, often surrender to their own destruction. With a quiet resolve, he stuffed his pockets with every coin he possessed – the silver and copper that clinked together like a cold melody – ensuring a heavier burden for the water to claim. The darkness seemed to swallow him whole as he hurried to a secluded stretch of the stream, one he had observed was rarely frequented.

Gerard glanced around cautiously, his eyes straining to pierce the shadows. Some souls, especially those of women, might pause to ensure they weren't alone, but Gerard's heart was set on solitude. Yet, as he scanned the area, a figure materialized from the corner of an alley, leaning against the wall with an air of nonchalance. Gerard pretended to stroll carelessly away, but his curiosity drew him back. The figure reappeared, this time emerging from a side street and lingering nearby.

A shiver danced down Gerard's spine as he wondered if he was being watched. "Impossible," he whispered to himself. He quickened his pace, walking along a few streets, making a detour, and returning to the river's edge. The man had vanished, but Gerard's unease lingered. On closer inspection, he spotted the figure a few yards behind, fiddling with his shoe.

A cold dread seeped into Gerard's bones as he realized he was being stalked. Under the moon's silvery glow, a glint of metal caught his eye – a steel gauntlet in the sentinel's hand. Panic clawed at his throat, and for a moment, it dawned on him that his life might be the target. But the thought was fleeting, and he attributed it to mere paranoia.

With a burst of courage, Gerard approached the bravo, his voice trembling slightly. "My friend," he said, "sell me your arm! A single stroke! See, here is all I have," and he forced his coins into the bravo's hands. "Oh, prithee! Pray, do one good deed and free me from this wretched existence!" Even as he spoke, he unfastened his doublet and bared his chest.
