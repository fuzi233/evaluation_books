As Margaret Hale descended the stairs, the scent of freshly brewed tea wafted from the kitchen, mingling with the faint tang of iron on fabric. Her father, Mr. Hale, stood in the little drawing-room, his eyes roving over the familiar furnishings as if searching for something to distract him. Margaret's keen eye picked up on the gesture, recognizing the nervous habit that often preceded a difficult conversation.

"Father, what's troubling you?" she asked, setting down the basket of linens she'd been carrying.

Mr. Hale turned to her, a mixture of trepidation and resolve etched on his face. "I've invited Mr. Thornton to join us for tea tonight."

Margaret's mother, reclining in her armchair, opened one eye lazily before shutting it again, a look of quiet suffering on her features. "Mr. Thornton? Tonight? Whatever for?" she drawled, her voice tinged with annoyance.

Mr. Hale explained, "He's come to discuss some business matters, my dear. I'm sure he'll be interested in our humble abode." As if to illustrate his point, the east wind howled outside, causing the windows to rattle and the smoke to dance in the chimney.

Margaret's mother shifted uncomfortably in her chair, pulling her shawl tighter around her shoulders. "I do hope the east wind doesn't bring the rain. Dixon is already struggling with the muslins and laces. We can ill afford to have them spoiled."

Margaret smiled wryly, remembering the countless times her father had spoken about Mr. Thornton's tenacity. "He's not one to let a little rain dampen his spirits, Mother. Besides, I'm sure he'd be more than happy to battle the elements, just as he battles the challenges of his work."

Her mother shot her a skeptical glance, but Margaret continued, "I'll go help Dixon with the ironing. Perhaps we can even make some cocoa-nut cakes for our guest. It's the least we can do for someone Papa considers a friend."

As the afternoon wore on, Margaret found herself growing increasingly restless, her mind wandering to the letter she wanted to write to Edith, the book she longed to finish reading, and the visit she'd promised herself to the Higginses.
