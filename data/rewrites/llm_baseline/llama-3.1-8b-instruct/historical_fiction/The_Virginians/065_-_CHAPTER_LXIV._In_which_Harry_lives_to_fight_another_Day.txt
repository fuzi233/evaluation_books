As the days passed since Mr. Harry's enlistment, Gumbo struggled to come to terms with his beloved master's departure. His sorrowful demeanor was evident to all who knew him, and he sought solace in the comforting presence of the Lambert household's female staff. The maids, with their gentle hearts and kind words, were a balm to his troubled soul. They treated him not merely as Mr. Harry's loyal servant but as a brother, offering him a shoulder to cry on and a listening ear. Hetty, in particular, took a special interest in the melancholy Gumbo, her laughter and teasing now replaced by a soft, compassionate tone.

As she busied herself with her daily tasks, Hetty couldn't help but think of her father, who had been away at the Ordnance Office for what felt like an eternity. She worried about his safety, wondering if he was well, if he had received any news, or if he had been injured in the line of duty. Her mind often wandered to the stories of soldiers who had returned from battle, their bodies maimed and their spirits broken. She would have given anything to have her father back home, safe and sound, without a scratch.

Gumbo, too, had his own concerns, though they were of a different nature. He had been left behind, and the thought of his master facing danger without him was almost too much to bear. As he helped himself to generous portions of beer and cold meat in the kitchen, he couldn't shake the feeling of abandonment. Yet, despite his flaws – his love of drink, his laziness, and his tendency to spin tales – Hetty saw beyond his rough exterior and treated him with kindness and understanding.

As the family sat down to dinner, Hetty's usual vivacity was noticeably absent, replaced by a quiet, subdued demeanor. She moved about the room with a newfound humility, her tongue no longer quick with witty remarks. Even when her father ordered her to perform small tasks, she obeyed without hesitation, her eyes cast downward in submission. It was as if she had undergone a transformation, one that spoke to her deep-seated fears and anxieties about the war. Her thoughts were consumed by the possibility of losing those she loved, and the weight of that fear hung heavy in the air.
