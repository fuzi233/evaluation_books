The stillness was a palpable thing, weighing upon us like a physical presence. It was as if the very earth itself had swallowed all sound, leaving only an oppressive silence that clung to our skin like a damp shroud. Above us, the wind whispered secrets to the snow, its gentle caress a cruel reminder of the world beyond our prison. Yet, even that distant murmur was muffled, a faint echo of a sound that refused to reach us. We were entombed, buried beneath the mountain's icy heart, a living sepulcher where the dead made no noise and the living were reduced to mere whispers.

In this eerie quiet, the weight of our situation bore down upon me like a physical force. Treasures beyond measure lay scattered around us – riches that could have changed the course of empires, paid off debts, or built a fleet of mighty ships. And yet, we would have traded them all for the faintest glimmer of hope, the merest whisper of a chance to escape. Later, we would have surrendered even those fleeting promises for a crust of bread or a sip of cool water. In the end, it would be the simple act of breathing that became the ultimate treasure, the price of which we would pay dearly.

As the night wore on, Sir Henry's voice cut through the stillness, a low rumble that seemed to shake the very foundations of our confinement. "How many matches do you have left?" he asked, his words a stark reminder of our dwindling resources.

"Eight, Curtis," I replied, my voice barely above a whisper.

"Strike one, then," he instructed, "and let us see the time."

The flame that erupted from the match was a tiny, fierce star that blazed in the darkness, casting flickering shadows on the walls. My watch glowed with a soft, ethereal light, marking the passing of time. Five o'clock. The dawn was rising, its pink hues bleeding across the snow-crowned peaks, beckoning us toward a new day.

"We should eat something," I suggested, trying to find a reason to cling to life. "We need to keep our strength up."

"What's the point?" Good countered, his voice laced with despair. "The sooner we're done with this, the better."

Sir Henry's words were a gentle rebuke. "While there's life, there's hope," he said, his voice a steady heartbeat in the darkness.
