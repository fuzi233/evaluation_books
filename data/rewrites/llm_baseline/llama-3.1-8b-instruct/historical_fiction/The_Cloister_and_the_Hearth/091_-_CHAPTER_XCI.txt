The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air as Margaret Brandt stood in the kitchen of her late mother's house, her eyes scanning the familiar space now filled with an unbearable sense of emptiness. It had been only a few days since the news of Margaret Van Eyck's passing had reached them, leaving behind a vast inheritance, including a beautiful, intricately furnished house and a priceless collection of art pieces. Among these treasures, one painting stood out to Margaret – "A Betrothal," a masterpiece that held a deep emotional significance for her.

As she walked through the rooms, her thoughts turned to Gerard, who had been absent for so long. She remembered the day he had left, donning the habit of a monk, and how she had felt as though a part of her had been torn away. A faint knock at the door broke the silence, and Margaret's heart skipped a beat as she saw Luke Peterson standing before her. His face lit up with excitement as he shared the news that he had come to visit her, and his words tumbled forth in a rush.

"Good mistress, I heard that Gerard is a monk now," he exclaimed. "If he cannot marry you, I can!" Margaret's response was laced with a mix of amusement and annoyance. "You? Why, I have seen him," she said, her voice tinged with a hint of sadness. Luke looked puzzled, and Margaret continued, "He was my husband, and the father of our child, long before he became a friar."

Luke's face fell, and he muttered something about seeking advice from his cousin, a lawyer, to clarify Margaret's marital status. But Margaret's patience was wearing thin. "I shall ask my own heart, not a lawyer," she said firmly. "So that is your regard for me; to go making me the talk of the town?" Her words stung, and Luke looked crestfallen.

As the days passed, Margaret's anxiety grew. Gerard's absence weighed heavily on her mind, and she found herself wondering if he had indeed abandoned her. The convent, once a place of solace and comfort, now seemed cold and unforgiving. A shiver ran down her spine as she thought of the monks who had taken Gerard in, their faces stern and unyielding. Could he have truly loved her, she wondered, and yet still chosen to leave without a word?
