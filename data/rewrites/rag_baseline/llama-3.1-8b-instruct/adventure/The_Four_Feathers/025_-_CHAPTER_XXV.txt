As I rode along the winding white road of Hampshire, the scent of heather and gorse wafted through the air, mingling with the faint tang of damp earth. The sun cast long shadows across the common, and I felt the weight of my thoughts settling heavy upon me. I had been troubled by the disappearance of Harry Feversham, and my mind was a jumble of worst-case scenarios. "I foresaw there would be trouble," I muttered to myself, the words echoing through the stillness like a mantra. The rhythm of my horse's hooves on the gravel road was the only sound that broke the silence, a steady beat that seemed to underscore the turmoil brewing within me.

The landscape unfolded before me like a canvas of rolling hills and darkening forests, the trees growing taller and closer as I crested a rise. A sudden dip in the road revealed a glint of steel in the distance – the railway tracks that sliced through the heart of the forest, a lifeline that connected the world beyond our isolated corner of England. My heart quickened as I spotted the red-tiled roof of the station building, a beacon of warmth and life in the fading light of day. I reined in my horse, my eyes fixed on the tiny settlement, and a pang of unease twisted in my chest.

I had promised Harry to keep his secret, to remain silent about the truth that I knew. The words echoed through my mind like a litany, a reminder of my duty and my loyalty. "I promised faithfully in the Criterion grill-room," I whispered to myself, the phrase a talisman against the doubts that assailed me. The sound of a distant engine pierced the air, its whistle a shrill cry that shattered the stillness. I watched as a plume of smoke appeared on the horizon, a ribbon of white that grew larger and more defined with each passing moment.

As the train drew near, I wondered what had brought it here, what secrets it might carry. My anxiety spiked, and I urged my horse forward, the animal responding eagerly to the pressure of my heels. We descended the hill, the station looming ahead, its wooden buildings and rusty tracks a testament to the industrial heartbeat of the modern world. Two figures emerged from the train, their faces illuminated by the fading light – Durrance and his servant, their features etched in sharp relief against the dull background of the station.
