1. **Miss Clack to Mr. Franklin Blake**
*"Miss Clack presents her compliments to Mr. Franklin Blake and, in submitting the fifth chapter of her modest narrative, regrets that she cannot adequately expand upon the tragic event of Lady Verinder’s passing under such distressing circumstances. She has, therefore, appended extensive passages from esteemed publications in her possession, all pertinent to this grievous matter. May these extracts (Miss Clack earnestly prays) resound as a clarion call to her esteemed kinsman, Mr. Franklin Blake."*

2. **Mr. Franklin Blake to Miss Clack**
*"Mr. Franklin Blake acknowledges receipt of Miss Clack’s fifth chapter and thanks her for the submission. While returning the accompanying extracts, he refrains from voicing personal objections to this literary form, merely observing that such additions are unnecessary for the fulfillment of his intended purpose."*

3. **Miss Clack to Mr. Franklin Blake**
*"Miss Clack acknowledges the return of her extracts. She tenderly reminds Mr. Blake that, as a Christian, she is impervious to offense. She remains deeply invested in his well-being and vows, should illness befall him, to once more offer the solace of her extracts. Meanwhile, she seeks his permission, before concluding her narrative, to incorporate insights from later discoveries regarding the Moonstone mystery."*

4. **Mr. Franklin Blake to Miss Clack**
*"Mr. Blake regrets disappointing Miss Clack but reiterates his initial directive: her narrative must confine itself to her personal experiences, as recorded in her diary. Subsequent revelations are best left to those who witnessed them firsthand."*

5. **Miss Clack to Mr. Franklin Blake**
*"Miss Clack apologizes for troubling Mr. Blake again. Her extracts have been returned, and her reflections on the Moonstone forbidden. Though worldly judgment might deem her humbled, adversity has taught her perseverance. She inquires whether Mr. Blake, having restricted her narrative, will at least permit the inclusion of their correspondence, as fairness demands some explanation of her constrained position as an author."*

7. **Miss Clack to Mr. Franklin Blake**
*"In Christian duty, Miss Clack informs Mr. Blake that his last letter—clearly designed to provoke—has failed to wound her. She urges him to retire to his chambers and reflect on whether the fortitude that elevates a humble woman above insult does not merit greater respect. Should he reconsider, she solemnly pledges to return the full collection of extracts to him."*
