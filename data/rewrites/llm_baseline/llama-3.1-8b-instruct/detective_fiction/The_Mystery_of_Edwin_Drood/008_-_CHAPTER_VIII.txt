As Edwin Drood and Neville Landless walked away from the Nuns' House, the moonlit courtyard seemed to cast a faintly mocking glow over their exchange. The sound of their footsteps echoed through the deserted street, punctuated only by the occasional hoot of an owl in the distance.

"Do you plan to stay in Cloisterham for long, Mr. Drood?" Neville asked, his tone measured but laced with a hint of curiosity.

"Not this time," Edwin replied, his words barely above a murmur. "I'll be leaving for London tomorrow. But I'll return, sporadically, until Midsummer. Then, I'll bid farewell to Cloisterham and England, for a while."

"Are you traveling abroad?" Neville pressed, his eyes narrowing slightly.

"I'm heading to Egypt," Edwin said with a dismissive wave of his hand. "To stir the dust, a bit."

Neville's gaze lingered on Edwin's face, as if searching for something beneath the surface. "I've heard you're quite accomplished in engineering," he ventured.

Edwin's response was abrupt, his tone tinged with a hint of disdain. "Reading? No, I'm engaged in work, overseeing the operations of the Firm I'm associated with. My father, a former partner, left me a modest inheritance, which I'll inherit when I come of age. Until then, Mr. Crisparkle serves as my guardian and trustee."

Neville's comment about Edwin's betrothal hung in the air like a challenge. Edwin's response was laced with a mixture of irritation and annoyance. "Everybody in Cloisterham seems to be talking about it. I wonder why no one's set up a public house with my portrait as the sign – 'The Betrothed's Head.' Though, perhaps, it should be 'Pussy's Portrait' instead."

Neville's expression turned guarded, and he chose his words carefully. "I mentioned it to you, Mr. Drood, because I assumed you'd be proud of the prospect of marrying Miss Helena."

Edwin's response was laced with a hint of condescension. "I live a busy life, Mr. Neville, and I speak under correction from those who think they know more than I do. Perhaps what people are most proud of is precisely what they least wish others to discuss."
