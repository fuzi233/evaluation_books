The Inquest


As the coroner, a man of imposing stature and jovial demeanor, took his seat at the makeshift tribunal, he was acutely aware of the scrutiny upon him. His reputation as a masterful jurist, tempered by a flair for the dramatic, had been piqued by the enigmatic circumstances surrounding Manderson's untimely demise within his jurisdiction. The coroner's courtroom, a drab, unfurnished space appended to the hotel, was abuzz with reporters and spectators, all eager to unravel the tangled threads of this mystery.

The jury, a seemingly composed ensemble of men with slicked-back hair, sat to one side of the coroner, their eyes fixed intently upon the proceedings. Across from them, a contingent of witnesses, including Manderson's widow, Mrs. Manderson, awaited their turn to testify. As she stepped forward, her veil lifted to reveal an ashen complexion and an aura of unbroken composure, the coroner's tone shifted to one of sympathetic inquiry. Mrs. Manderson's voice, though low and measured, betrayed the weight of her emotional burden, as she recounted the events of that fateful Sunday evening.

Her husband, she explained, had retired to their shared bedroom around his customary hour, entering through the connecting door from his dressing room. Though she had been half-asleep, she recalled speaking to him, her words hazy in the fog of slumber. Manderson had mentioned a moonlit drive in his automobile, and she had inquired about the time, as she felt certain that she had only just dozed off. He had replied that it was half past eleven, and then revealed that he had changed his mind about taking the drive. When pressed by the coroner to elaborate on her husband's motivations, Mrs. Manderson hesitated, her expression veiled in a mixture of confusion and determination.

The coroner's gentle prodding coaxed forth the explanation: Manderson had chosen not to share the details of his business dealings with his wife, citing a long-standing policy of reticence. It was this very secrecy that had piqued her interest, and she had pressed him for more information. Specifically, he had mentioned sending Mr. Marlowe to Southampton, a detail that now assumed greater significance in the context of the investigation.
