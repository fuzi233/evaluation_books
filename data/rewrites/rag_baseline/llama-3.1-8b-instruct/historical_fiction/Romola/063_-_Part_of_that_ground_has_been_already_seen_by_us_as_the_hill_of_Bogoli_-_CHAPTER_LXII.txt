The Benediction.

February's twenty-seventh dawned over Florence, casting a pale glow across the cobblestone streets of San Marco. As the Carnival reached its final day, the city's inhabitants began to congregate in the Piazza, drawn by the promise of the second Bonfire of Vanities outside the Old Palace. Yet, amidst the throngs, a palpable sense of anticipation focused on the Dominican Brethren of San Marco, assembled around a semicircular barrier in front of their church. Within this enclosure, the Dominicans stood like sentinels, their dark robes a unifying presence amidst the swirling crowds.

The wooden pulpit, erected above the church-door, stood empty, awaiting the arrival of Girolamo Savonarola, the excommunicated preacher. His words, laced with defiance and conviction, had stirred the people to flock to his sermons, despite the Pope's threats of excommunication and economic reprisals. The Frate's unyielding stance against the corrupt papacy had created a schism within the community, with many questioning whether the Divine lightnings truly resided in this humble monk or the official successor of Saint Peter.

As the crowd pressed forward, their faces turned toward the pulpit, a collective murmur grew, a whispered expectation of the miracle that would validate Savonarola's mission. The air was heavy with the weight of uncertainty, the people torn between the promise of salvation and the fear of consequences. Would the Frate's words be enough to convince them of his divine calling, or would the need for a tangible sign become too great to ignore?

Within the ranks of the Dominicans, a sense of trepidation hung like a specter, for they knew that Savonarola's fate was inextricably linked to the people's faith. His unwavering speech, a testament to his unshakeable conviction, masked a deepening unease – a nagging doubt that, one day, the weight of his words might prove too great to bear. Yet, for now, the people's hunger for reassurance had become a palpable force, driving them toward the empty pulpit, awaiting the moment when Savonarola would step forth to meet their expectations.
