He hesitated for a moment before sharing the news he had been carrying with him for days. "I've decided to tell you the rest, mother, and daughter," he said, choosing his words carefully. "It's not that I don't trust Cornelis and Sybrandt, but Gerard has asked me not to involve them in this matter, and I'm sworn to follow his wishes." 

Catherine's sigh echoed through the room, but she said nothing. Denys continued, recounting the tale of his adventure and the pain of parting with his friend. His voice cracked as he spoke of Gerard's final words, but he pressed on, determined to find Margaret. As he finished, the kitchen fell silent, the only sound the ticking of the clock on the wall. Catherine's eyes welled up with tears, and Denys knew she was thinking of her own desire to find Margaret and welcome her back home before Gerard's return.

"We must be practical, though," Denys said, trying to sound calm. "If Gerard comes back next week and Margaret is still missing, what will people think?" Catherine nodded in agreement, but she couldn't help feeling that searching for Margaret in every town in Holland was a fool's errand. "She must have told someone where she was going," she said gently. "We can't just abandon our lives to search for her."

Denys's face darkened, but he nodded slowly. "Perhaps not alone," he said. "We can work together. You stay here and keep your ears open, while I search the towns in Holland. We'll find her, Catherine, I promise." He paused, thinking aloud. "And she won't be far from here. Three people can't travel as quickly as one."

Catherine smiled, seeing the sense in his plan. "Yes, that makes sense," she said. But then she turned serious. "Before you go, let's visit Demoiselle Van Eyck. She and Margaret were close friends, and she might know where Margaret has gone." Denys was already standing, eager to get started, and Catherine took his arm, smoothing her apron as she stood up beside him.
