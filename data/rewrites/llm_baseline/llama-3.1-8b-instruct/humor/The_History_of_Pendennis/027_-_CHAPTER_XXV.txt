In the esteemed company of Captain Bragg, who had overseen the voyage that brought Miss Snell from England to Calcutta, where her father, a respected merchant and part-owner of several vessels, including the Ramchunder East Indiaman, awaited her arrival, Lady Clavering had garnered a reputation for her unrefined nature. Her limited education, acquired in a London suburb she persistently referred to as Ackney, seemed woefully inadequate preparation for the intricacies of life, particularly in the complex social hierarchy of the British Empire. It was during this formative period, aboard the Ramchunder, that she first crossed paths with her future husband, Mr. Amory, who served as third mate on the vessel.

While we shall not delve into the entirety of Lady Clavering's past, it is worth noting that Captain Bragg, entrusted with the care of Miss Snell during her passage to India, found himself obliged to confine her husband, Mr. Amory, to the brig upon discovering his rebellious behavior. By the time they docked at the Cape, Captain Bragg had relinquished responsibility for the errant officer, delivering his ward, Lady Clavering, safely into the hands of her father in Calcutta. The voyage had proven arduous, with the Ramchunder and its cargo facing numerous perils that threatened their safety.

Several months later, Mr. Amory reappeared in Calcutta, having successfully worked his way out of indentured servitude from the Cape. He married the wealthy attorney's daughter, despite her father's reservations, and embarked on a series of ill-fated business ventures – first as an indigo planter, then as an agent, and finally as the editor of the Sunderbund Pilot. Throughout these endeavors, Mr. Amory found himself at odds with both his father-in-law and his wife, who bore the brunt of his constant quarrels. His eventual downfall forced him to abandon Calcutta for the distant shores of New South Wales.

It was during this tumultuous period that Mr. Amory likely crossed paths with Sir Jasper Rogers, the respected Judge of the Supreme Court of Calcutta, who would later bear witness to Mr. Amory's transgressions.
