The gloom beneath the olden sea was a suffocating weight, a horizonless blackness that pressed against the ribs. But now, the pressure lifted. The darkness receded, revealing a landscape that stretched to the right of my entry point, a terrain marked by scattered vents of fire. Seeing them warmed my chest, though prudence demanded caution; these fires were gathering points for life, and life here was rarely benign.

I looked down at the Maid. She gazed up, stepping closer until our shadows merged. She possessed a delicate sweetness, yet her pallor betrayed a deep exhaustion. A pang of guilt struck me—I had carried her too far, a burden of iron shoulders against her tender frame. She offered no rebuke, only fixed her beautiful eyes upon me. My arms closed around her, and I kissed her forehead. Then, I scanned the land ahead, plotting the route.

From this vantage, the blue shimmer I had tracked across the seabed extended before me, distant but undeniable. That luminescence had guided us through the abyss, a broad beacon ensuring we did not circle blindly in the night. After a moment's calculation, I oriented myself: the Gorge lay to my left, but not directly so. To the west, the red glow of the Giants' Hole burned low on the horizon. We must navigate between these extremes, avoiding the red pit while staying clear of the blue.

I wrapped an arm around the Maid, who stood silent and still during my survey. Pointing vaguely to the northwest, I indicated the direction of the Gorge, noting that it lay beyond my current line of sight, identifiable only by the landmarks I recalled from my descent.

She had observed the surroundings intently during my deliberations, gaining a spatial awareness of her captivity. Turning to me, she asked for my intended path. I replied that we would proceed straight, maintaining a careful distance from both the blue shimmer and the fiery red pit.

"Look," she commanded softly.

I followed her gaze. Beyond the immediate foreground, faint green halos surrounded several fire-holes. "That is the tract of bad gas," she explained, her voice steady despite her fatigue. "Poisonous to breathe. In the Lesser Redoubt, their instruments mapped it precisely. Where the gas flows, the flames turn green."

She traced a line extending far to the northwest, mapping the invisible boundary of the toxic zone. "The great blue shine burns across all of that sector," she added. "They call it simply 'The Shine.'"

My internal companion, Mine Own, grew urgent, urging me to halt our advance toward The Shine. Her logic was stark: the Fixed Giants dwelled within its borders, concealed by the radiance except when rolling mists parted. I recognized the parallel immediately—the Fixed Giants were akin to the Great Watchers guarding the Mighty Pyramid, forces of profound and terrible evil. The memory surfaced instantly: the monstrous visage I had glimpsed amidst the bright smoke upon my arrival. This was one of them, a sentinel of horrid malice.

"How far does the gas extend?" I asked.

Mine Own gestured, clarifying the expanse. Realization dawned on me. If I adhered strictly to the gas boundary, the direct path to the Gorge's mouth became impossible. The geography forced a detour.

Then, Naani interrupted my frustration. "How did you cross the land when searching for me?" she asked.

I recounted the journey: a fortunate drift along the outer fringe of the gas field, leading naturally to the Olden Sea bed and freedom from the poison.

At this revelation, the Maid proposed a solution. We would descend once more into the seabed, traveling southwesterly for several hours until we cleared the gas zone entirely. Only then would we ascend, exercising extreme vigilance against the Giants patrolling the Great Red Fire-Hole. This route would lead swiftly to the entrance of the Upward Gorge.

It was a sound strategy, logical and efficient. I am not slow in such calculations, yet the Maid's eagerness pleased me. Hearing her voice, her thoughts forming plans, revealed the workings of her mind—a rare intimacy. To share her burdens, to think alongside her, was a comfort I cherished above all else.

We adopted her plan. However, seventeen hours had elapsed since our last rest, and the Maid's weariness was evident. I advised her that sleep was imperative before continuing. She agreed quickly, her energy depleted.

We ventured toward a fire-hole situated slightly to our right, northward in orientation. It proved farther than anticipated; nearly an hour passed before we reached its rim. It was a massive vent, glowing red-hot as it erupted into the night sky from a rocky hollow.

Approaching the edge, I signaled silence. Retrieving the Diskos from my hip, I moved forward alone, dropping to my hands and knees. I beckoned Mine Own to follow suit. Crawling to the precipice, we peered down.

Below, a fierce fire raged within the earth. Yet, strangely, no monsters circled the blaze. This absence brought a fleeting sense of relief, though I knew better than to linger near the fires of this land, where all creatures congregated.

I watched for a long time. The Maid crept close, resting her head on my elbow, sharing my vigil. We listened keenly to the night, but the air remained undisturbed. Speaking quietly, I reiterated the necessity of keeping our distance. But the Maid was freezing, her teeth chattering violently. She begged us to descend into the hollow, seeking warmth even briefly to counteract the biting cold.

I was chilled to the bone, my strength failing. Yet, witnessing her shivering compelled me to override my caution. Heart overriding head, we descended into the hollow and approached the fire.

It seemed contradictory, this desire for warmth after fleeing the very source of danger. Perhaps my spirit was already whispering warnings, a subtle alarm I chose to ignore. Experience teaches that danger feels distant when one is far removed from it, especially when the alternative is the paralyzing cold. The peril of the fire-holes seemed abstract, unreal, while the frost felt acutely, painfully real. But as we neared the flame, the truth of those dangers surged back, sharp and undeniable.

Once inside the hollow, I navigated among the rocks, scanning for hidden threats. I found nothing large, only three snakes and two scorpion-like creatures. These arthropods did not flee; they remained coiled in their rocky crevices, unmoving.

This observation confirmed my suspicion: sleeping near the fire-hole was ill-advised. The creeping things craved the heat and would emerge during our slumber. I resolved to seek a safer location for rest, though I held my tongue regarding the venomous inhabitants. I wished the Maid only rest and peace, intending to explain the danger later, hoping she would then willingly relocate. If she refused, I would enforce obedience, for her safety was my sole priority.

Soon, the Maid's body adjusted to the ambient heat. She slipped the scrip from my shoulder, producing food and drink with swift efficiency.

We ate and drank in silence. As she consumed her second tablet, her expression softened, a quiet sweetness returning. I sensed the memory of my earlier correction weighed upon her, yet she accepted it utterly. She was mine.

Throughout the meal, I kept watch, scanning the perimeter for intruders. When we finished, the Maid noticed my unease, turning sharply to peer behind her. Almost simultaneously, she spotted a snake moving among the rocks. Panic flared in her eyes, and she insisted we find shelter immediately.

We searched briefly but settled for a small cave nestled in an upright rock formation within the hollow. The rock stood perhaps a hundred feet from the fire, and the cavern itself was vast. The opening rose three times my height, dry and sweet-smelling, devoid of hiding spots for predators.

Inside, the cave felt like a sanctuary. The fire's glow illuminated the interior, casting a warm light that promised refuge. Yet, the fear of the Land persisted, heavier in my heart than hers. Mine Own trusted me implicitly, believing I possessed the power to protect her from any monster. This faith was a balm to my soul, though it did not quell my own terror for her survival.

We slept that night as we had before, sharing a single cloak. The fire provided little actual warmth, yet the air was less bitter than the surrounding darkness. By the time sleep claimed us, twenty hours had passed since our last rest. Exhaustion was total, yet our minds remained alert, poised to awaken at the slightest threat.

Seven hours later, a sudden disturbance shattered our repose. I woke instantly, as did the Maid. A high-pitched scream tore through the night, a sound of pure human terror that froze our blood. It was the cry of someone dying in the dark. I could do nothing but wait, my duty bound to the Maid, forbidding any rash movement.

Shaken, I prepared to descend from the rock and climb out to investigate, to offer aid to those in distress. But I could not abandon her.

Immediately, the night filled with roars. First in one section, then another. Soon, the roars echoed in response. These were the guttural sounds of huge voices, belonging to men as tall as houses, running and shouting in the dead of night.

The Maid began to tremble, collapsing into my embrace. I pulled her back into the cave's shadow. She shook like a broken vessel, recalling the sounds of those nights she had wandered alone.

So did I. For these were the shouts of the Giants. The horror of hearing their voices was etched into my very bones.
