As I descended into the great hall, the scent of roasting meats wafted through the air, mingling with the faint tang of salt and herbs. Catherine's transformation was palpable, her eyes now calm and serene, her features softened by the gentle warmth of the fire. She stood tall, a vision of serenity, her dark hair cascading down her back like a waterfall of night.

"Denys," she said, her voice low and melodious, "I've received my instructions. I'm to read Gerard's letter to his kin."

Denys's eyebrows shot up in surprise. "Ye will never do that?"

Catherine's lips curled into a faint smile. "Ay, I will."

A flicker of understanding danced across Denys's face. "There's something in the letter that's touched ye, hasn't it?"

Catherine's expression turned wistful, her gaze drifting into the distance. "Not a jot, Denys, not a jot. But even if I hated them as much as poison, I wouldn't disobey my love. Denys, it's so sweet to obey, especially when it's someone who's far, far away and can't enforce my duty, but must trust in my love. Ah, Gerard, my darling, if he'd told me to wade into the raging sea and read his letter to the sharks, I'd do it. Therefore, Denys, tell his mother I have his letter, and if she and hers would hear it, I'm their servant; let them choose their time, and I'll seat them as best I can, and welcome them as best I may."

Denys hastened to relay the message to Catherine's family, who were gathered around the dinner table. As he shared the news, their faces lit up with joy, and they eagerly awaited the letter. But Catherine's words had been carefully chosen, and she would not relinquish the letter easily. When Denys returned with the family's request, her expression turned firm.
