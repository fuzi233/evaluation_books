Two hours later, I emerged from the depths of the Gorge and stood at last upon the threshold of that strange and luminous land. Though my heart was troubled by its strangeness, a fierce joy surged within me at the sight of its brilliance—this sudden realm of fire and water, glowing beneath the eternal dark.

Before descending fully, I paused high within the mouth of the Gorge and gazed out across the expanse. Twenty-seven volcanoes loomed in the distance, their peaks crowned with flame, not counting the two vast ranges of fire-mountains that smoldered far to my right. Countless lesser fires dotted the land, flickering like scattered embers. It was a place where earth and water seethed with restless energy, where steam coiled over boiling seas, and the very air trembled with the deep, ceaseless mutterings of the fire-hills.

To my left rose a monstrous black mountain, its slopes lost in the upper darkness. Upon its flanks, two great volcanoes burned—one halfway up, the other near its summit, their flames like smoldering suns suspended in the night. Below them, slopes of ash and scorched rock sprawled outward, monuments to eruptions older than memory.

To my right stretched the seas—three smaller ones, and a vast, endless ocean that vanished into the crimson haze of the horizon. Islands rose from the waters, crowned with fire, while in other places the flames sprouted directly from the waves. The near sea steamed as if perpetually boiling, its surface broken by geysers and unseen turmoil.

The land hummed with life, not of beasts, but of the earth itself—a ceaseless, throbbing energy that filled the air with sound. It was the World-Noise of an age long dead, yet here it lived again, louder and more insistent than in the quiet of the Underground Fields.

I drank in the sight, mapping the way ahead. To the right lay only the sea; to the left, the shores curved inward, offering passage between water and forest. Satisfied, I descended fully into that fiery country, my spirit alight with both wonder and wariness.

By then, four-and-twenty hours had passed without rest. Weariness tugged at my limbs, and I sought refuge in a hollow between three great trees, where a basin of warm, dry rock promised shelter. After eating three of the tablets and drinking sparingly, I lay down, thoughts drifting to Naani—until sleep claimed me before I could dwell long upon her.

I woke abruptly, drowning. Hot water surged around me, filling the basin, steaming and pungent. I scrambled upright, coughing, and saw that a fissure in the rock had belched forth boiling water. The pool drained slowly, as it must have done for ages, and I realized I had slept through its cycles.

Stripping off my armor, I spread my garments upon heated stone to dry, then bathed in the warm pool, keeping the Diskos close. Though I sensed no evil here, the memory of the Night Lands kept me wary. Refreshed, I dressed again and slept six hours more, lulled by the rhythmic gurgling of the rising water.

At last, I rose, ate, and set forth, determined to cover great distance. The forest loomed to my left, the sea to my right. Often the trees grew so thickly that I waded through their shadows, their scent wild and sweet—so unlike the tame groves of the Underground Fields. Life stirred in the undergrowth; eyes gleamed in the dark, watching, retreating. Nothing attacked, yet I kept the Diskos ready.

Three times that day, I passed small fire-hills, their eruptions shaking the earth. Around each lay a wasteland of dead trees, yet already new growth crept inward, reclaiming the scorched earth between eruptions. Thirty-seven boiling springs I counted, some roaring like beasts, others silent but for their hissing steam.

By the eighteenth hour, exhaustion forced me to halt. I chose a spot against a great rock, where nothing could approach unseen, and slept—or so I thought. Yet my spirit remained alert, sensing danger.

I woke with a jolt. The luminous air revealed six hunched figures crouched at the forest’s edge, their eyes gleaming like beasts’. They watched, motionless. I gripped the Diskos, heart pounding, but before I could act, they vanished—gone as silently as shadows.

Uneasy, I ate and drank, then pressed onward, moving swiftly. For thirty hours I marched, pausing only to eat and drink at intervals. Twice, I felt unseen things tracking me through the woods, matching my pace. Fear drove me faster.

At last, I reached a river flowing into the sea. An island stood midstream, a possible refuge. With no way to swim, I lashed fallen trees into a crude raft and poled across, collapsing on the island to sleep nine hours of dreamless exhaustion.

The next day, I crossed the remaining stretch of river and continued, the land unfolding in wonders and perils. Fire-hills rumbled, creatures lurked, yet none attacked. My thoughts flickered often to Naani, though danger left little room for longing. Every step was a prayer for her.

Near evening, I climbed a great rock, seeking shelter. At its summit, I found not mere stone, but something stranger—a long, shaped mass like a fallen pillar, its underside familiar, its top overgrown. A place to rest, I thought, and slept—until instinct woke me.

Two Humped Men scaled the rock, swift and silent. Their thick necks and shoulders bulged with brute strength, their eyes fixed on me. I readied the Diskos. One must die quickly, lest the other strike from behind.

The battle for my life was at hand.
