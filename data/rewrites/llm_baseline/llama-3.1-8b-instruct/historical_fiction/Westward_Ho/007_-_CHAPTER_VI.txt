The moon cast its silvery glow over the westernmost extremity of England, where the Devon and Cornish highlands converged, giving birth to a series of secluded valleys. These natural amphitheaters, each unique yet eerily similar, unfolded like emeralds against the rugged backdrop of the Atlantic Ocean. Their walls, a tapestry woven from rich oak woods, dark green furze, and weathered black cliffs, stretched towards the horizon, punctuated by the occasional castle, spire, or jagged outcropping of iron-stone. Each valley boasted its own microcosm of life: a narrow ribbon of fertile meadow, a crystal-clear brook that wound its way across the landscape, and a graying stone mill, its wheel a blur of motion as it harnessed the water's kinetic energy.

Among these valleys, one stood out – Marsland, the fabled stronghold of the enigmatic Lucy Passmore, known to the locals as the White Witch. It was here that Sir Richard Grenville had surmised the Jesuits would find refuge. Yet, on this particular evening, the moonlit beach was home to an unlikely pair: Rose Salterne, driven by a morbid curiosity and fascination with the unknown, and her counsellor, the White Witch herself. As the clock struck midnight, they stood poised on the weathered shingle, the waves caressing the shore with a soothing melody.

"You're quite safe here tonight, Rose," the White Witch said, her voice a gentle whisper carried away by the breeze. "My father's slumbering, and we're alone – save for the occasional mermaid's song."

Rose gestured towards the shoreline, where their boat lay nestled in the sand. "Where's our vessel, Lucy? It should be waiting for us here, ready to depart."

The White Witch's gaze followed Rose's pointing finger, her eyes narrowing slightly as she took in the sight of the boat, its oars still in place. "Ah, that scoundrel of a father of mine has been out fishing for pollock, no doubt. He's left the boat unattended, and I daresay it's a wonder it hasn't been pilfered already. I'll have a word with him when I return, mark my words."
