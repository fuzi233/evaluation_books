As I descended from the palace at Helium, the darkness of the Martian night enveloped me like a shroud. Two hours had passed since I'd set out, and now, with Kantos Kan, Xodar, and myself at Hastor, we were poised to embark on a perilous journey. Our companions, Carthoris, Tars Tarkas, and Hor Vastus, had preceded us to Thark on a separate cruiser, while the transports prepared to set sail. The fleet of battleships would catch up with us by the next morning, forming a long, sinuous line that would stretch for miles towards the south.

Within the hour, the first battleship took to the skies, followed by a steady stream of vessels, each one a marvel of engineering and design. As I watched, transfixed, the last ship vanished into the darkness, leaving behind a trail of faint lights that dwindled into the distance. It was then that I turned to Kantos Kan, seeking to gauge the passage of time. He revealed to me, with a somber expression, that three hundred and sixty-five days had elapsed since my confinement in the pits of Zat Arras. The weight of this knowledge settled heavy on my heart, for I realized that our expedition was no longer one of rescue, but of vengeance. The thought of Dejah Thoris, lost to me forever, hung like a specter over our mission.

Yet, I chose to keep this burden to myself, aware that my friends had shared enough of my sorrows in the past. Instead, I focused on the larger purpose of our quest: to expose the cruel deception that had been perpetrated upon the people of Barsoom for centuries, and to guide them towards the hidden Valley Dor, a land of untold riches and bounty. There, amidst the icy wasteland, lay the only naturally fertile area on the dying planet, where dew and rain fell in abundance, and an open sea beckoned. But this promised land was guarded by the fierce warriors of the therns, who had barred access to its beauty and fertility, condemning the red men to a life of hardship and toil.

As we set sail, I knew that our success depended on breaking down the barriers of superstition that had held the red races captive for so long.
