The evening sun cast a warm glow through the windows of the Thornton household, illuminating the quiet domestic scene within. Margaret's departure had barely registered, but Mr. Thornton's arrival sent a ripple through the atmosphere. His face aglow, he entered the dining room with a sense of urgency, his eyes scanning the space as if searching for something or someone. His mother, busily rearranging the furniture, didn't immediately respond to his inquiry about Margaret's whereabouts.

"Where is Miss Hale?" he asked, his voice low but insistent.

"Gone home," his mother replied, her tone a shade cooler than usual.

"Gone home!" he repeated, his brow furrowed in concern. "She could not have been fit for it."

"She said she was; and Mr. Lowe confirmed it," his mother said, her movements economical. "I went to fetch him myself."

Mr. Thornton's gaze lingered on his mother, a fleeting expression of gratitude flickering across his features. "Thank you, Mother," he said, his voice tinged with warmth. But she didn't acknowledge the gesture, her focus fixed on the task at hand.

"The Irish workers...?" he ventured, his tone changing to one of practicality.

"I've sent for food, and Father Grady is speaking with them, trying to persuade them against leaving in a group. Luckily, I caught him just in time." Her words trailed off, and she paused, her attention drawn to the spot where Margaret had departed. "How did Miss Hale get home?"

"She had a cab. Everything was done properly, even to the paying. Let's speak of something else. She's caused quite enough commotion already."

Mr. Thornton's expression darkened, his eyes flashing with emotion. "I don't know where I'd be without her," he muttered, his voice low and intense.

His mother's eyes narrowed, her tone sharpening. "Are you so helpless that you need a young woman to defend you?" she asked, a hint of disdain creeping into her voice.

He reddened, his chest heaving with suppressed emotion. "Not many women would have taken the blows meant for me, with such kindness and intention," he said, his words laced with a mix of pride and vulnerability.

"A woman in love will do many things," his mother retorted, her voice clipped.
