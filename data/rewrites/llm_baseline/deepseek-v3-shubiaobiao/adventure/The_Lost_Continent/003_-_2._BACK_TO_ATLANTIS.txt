Tatho’s words haunted me that night, a restless tide in my mind. For the first time, I questioned my decision to bury myself in the governance of Yucatan, cut off from the shifting politics of Atlantis. The mariners who crossed the seas had long spoken in guarded tones, their words clipped by the Empress’s will. But I had been too consumed by my duties to pry, too principled to torture secrets from men bound by orders.

Now, the iron discipline of the Priestly Clan left me no choice. My office had been stripped from me in an instant, my authority dissolved. By dawn, I would be a guest in the pyramid I had once ruled, and by sunrise, I would be gone—hurried across the sea to report to Atlantis.

Tatho, ever the loyal friend, came to me before first light, offering whatever aid he could. My needs were few.

“A ship,” I said. “That’s all I ask.”

He frowned. “My vessels are slow, fouled from the voyage. Why not take your own navy? The ships in the harbor are swift, well-manned.”

“They belong to Yucatan, not to me.”

His laughter was sharp. “Deucalion, you *were* Yucatan. For twenty years, this colony answered to you alone.”

I stiffened. “The ships are owned by merchants or the State. I claim none of them.”

Tatho shook his head. “A dangerous policy. A man in power should always keep a fleet at his back. What if your successor had been less… honorable?”

“I have no fear of thieves,” I said coldly.

“Because you trust *me*. But another might have plundered your coffers without a second thought.”

“There are no coffers to plunder. Look at me, Tatho. I own the clothes on my back, a chest of medicines, and three slaves—two scribes and a cook. My salary? A soldier’s ration, nothing more. If any man claims I took so much as a bronze coin from this land, hang him for a liar.”

Tatho studied me, caught between admiration and disbelief. “We heard tales of your austerity in Atlantis, but this… You live like a hermit, not a viceroy. Money is power, Deucalion. Without it, even a man like you can be broken.”

“The Gods decide my fate.”

“Fate favors those who prepare.” He sighed. “I won’t rule as you did. Yucatan will prosper under me—but so will Tatho. My ships, my guard, my household—they must be fed. A people should pay richly for their prince.”

“We’ll never agree on this.”

“No,” he admitted. “But at least let me send you home with dignity. Take my flagship, the *Bear*, and an escort. You’ll not sail alone like some exiled beggar.”

I relented. And so, a month later, I stood on the deck of Tatho’s finest vessel, the Atlantean fleet slicing through the waves around me. No timid coastal crawl this time—we struck eastward, straight across the open sea, guided by instruments and the cold precision of modern mariners.

The sailors were a superstitious lot, sacrificing to obscure gods before departure, feasting until they stumbled aboard drunk. But once at sea, their skill was undeniable. They navigated by star and compass, harnessing the Sun’s power to drive us forward.

Yet the ocean’s dangers were ever-present. Storms lashed us; flaming stones hissed into the waves. And then, the lizards came.

Three of them, monstrous and sleek, their necks towering like masts. The *Bear* was fast, but not fast enough. They surrounded us, jaws gaping, reeking of salt and rot. The crew fought with axes, fire, sheer desperation—but their hides turned blades like stone.

I refused to hide. A lance in hand, I baited the largest beast, letting it strike before driving the weapon deep into its eye. It fled, blinded, bellowing into the depths. The others left only after Tob, the captain, sacrificed two men to sate their hunger.

A grim necessity. But as the horizon swallowed the last of them, I felt the old fire in my veins. The sea was still the domain of beasts—but man, when he dared, could still draw blood.

And Atlantis awaited.
