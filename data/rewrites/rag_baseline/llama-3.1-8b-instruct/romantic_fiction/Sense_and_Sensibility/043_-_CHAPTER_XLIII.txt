Marianne rose the next morning at her usual hour, though her body protested the effort. She forced a smile as she replied to her sister's inquiries, insisting she was on the mend, and attempted to demonstrate her recovery by engaging in her customary activities. However, a day spent shivering over the fire, struggling to focus on the pages of a book, and lying listlessly on the sofa, told a different story. When she eventually retired to bed, her condition worsened, and Colonel Brandon's concern deepened.

Elinor, observing her sister's decline, chose to heed Mrs. Jennings's counsel and summoned the local apothecary, Mr. Palmer's physician. He arrived, assessed Marianne's condition, and though offering some encouragement, hinted at the possibility of infection, causing Mrs. Palmer's anxieties about her baby to escalate. As the gravity of the situation became clear, Mrs. Jennings, who had initially underestimated the severity of Marianne's illness, grew increasingly somber, and her daughter Charlotte's fears were confirmed.

Mrs. Palmer's distress was compounded by her desire to protect her child, and Mr. Palmer, though skeptical of the concerns, ultimately yielded to his wife's entreaties and agreed to join her at her relative's estate a few miles outside Bath. Within a short span of time, Mrs. Palmer set off with her infant and nurse, accompanied by a servant from Mrs. Jennings's household. As they departed, Elinor found solace in Mrs. Jennings's unwavering commitment to caring for Marianne, and together they worked tirelessly to nurse her back to health.

Marianne, her body wracked by pain and her spirits crushed, could no longer cling to the hope of a swift recovery. The impending delay in their planned return home weighed heavily on her, as it would have been the day they were to embark on their journey, accompanied by a member of Mrs. Jennings's staff, and surprise their mother on the following morning. Her melancholy thoughts were a testament to the gravity of her condition, and Elinor's efforts to lift her spirits fell flat. The next day brought little change in Marianne's state, and their small party was further diminished as Mr. Palmer prepared to follow his wife, despite his reservations. Even Colonel Brandon, moved by Marianne's suffering, began to consider joining them, but Mrs. Jennings intervened, recognizing the timing of his departure would coincide with the turmoil in his own life.
