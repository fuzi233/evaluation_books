As the sun-drenched afternoon wore on, Mrs. Portman found herself drawn to the tranquil surroundings of Fairoaks, her mind still abuzz from a most stimulating conversation with Mrs. Pybus. The warm breeze carried the sweet scent of blooming flowers, and the gentle rustle of leaves seemed to conspire in harmony with the soft murmur of the nearby stream. It was as if the very essence of the natural world had conspired to create an atmosphere conducive to the exchange of secrets, and it was in this idyllic setting that Mrs. Portman revealed to Mrs. Pendennis a most intriguing tale regarding the Curate, Mr. Smirke.

"Ah, my dear," Mrs. Portman began, her voice barely above a whisper, "you will hardly believe the news I bring. It appears that our esteemed Curate has harbored a secret attachment, one that has lain dormant for quite some time." Her words hung in the air, suspended like the delicate petals of a flower, as Mrs. Pendennis's eyes widened in surprise.

"Pray, tell me, to whom does Mr. Smirke's heart belong?" Mrs. Pendennis asked, her tone tinged with a mix of curiosity and trepidation.

Mrs. Portman's smile was a gentle, knowing curve of the lips. "Why, it seems that when he first arrived among us, people whispered that he was enamored with Myra. But alas, it was not so. Nay, his affections lay elsewhere, in the person of Miss Thompson from Clapham Common, a lady of great beauty and refinement."

Mrs. Pendennis's expression shifted from surprise to indignation, her voice rising in protest. "Fie upon it! How dare they suggest such a thing? Mr. Smirke came to us as Arthur's tutor, and I daresay, his station is far above such frivolous gossip."

Major Pendennis, seated nearby, set aside his newspaper and eyed his sister with a mixture of amusement and exasperation. "Good heavens, Helen, really, must we indulge in such trivialities?"

Mrs. Portman's countenance remained serene, her voice unwavering. "I fear it is only natural, my dear. After all, a married curate is a far more pleasant companion than a bachelor, don't you agree? And I daresay, Myra will be delighted to hear the news, for it removes a certain...unpleasantness from our midst."
