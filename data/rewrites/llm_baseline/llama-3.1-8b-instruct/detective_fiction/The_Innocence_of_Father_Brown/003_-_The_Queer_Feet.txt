In the heart of London's Belgravia, nestled in the corner of a quaint square, stood the Vernon Hotel, a bastion of exclusivity and refinement. Its proprietor, the enigmatic Mr. Lever, a man of considerable means and taste, had cultivated an air of mystique around his establishment. It was an institution where the rarefied atmosphere was as much a draw as the exquisite cuisine and fine wines that graced its tables.

The hotel's exclusivity was carefully crafted to be both a blessing and a curse. Only twenty-four guests could dine within its hallowed halls at any given time, and the coveted terrace table, perched on a veranda overlooking a breathtaking garden, was reserved for those who could secure a spot. The warm summer evenings were the only time this gem was accessible, making each meal a highly prized experience.

As one entered the Vernon Hotel, the soft murmur of conversation and the clinking of fine china created an atmosphere of elegance. The waiters, handpicked by Mr. Lever himself, moved with the stealth and poise of gentlemen's servants. Their training was a testament to the hotel's dedication to excellence, and it was said that becoming a waiter at the Vernon Hotel was a far greater accomplishment than securing a seat in Parliament.

It was here, within these hallowed walls, that the enigmatic club known as The Twelve True Fishermen gathered annually for their dinner. This reclusive group prized their luxurious privacy above all else, and the thought of sharing the space with another club would have been anathema to them. As they dined, surrounded by the finest the city had to offer, they would engage in conversations that were as much about the art of living as they were about the art of eating.

Their evening coats, a deep, rich green, served as a subtle identifier, setting them apart from the common diners and the staff alike. A simple inquiry as to their choice of attire might elicit a wry smile and the explanation that it was to avoid being mistaken for a waiter – a jest that would leave the uninformed observer feeling bewildered, yet intrigued.
