It was a yearning that echoed the fervent prayers I'd offered to the great god of Mars, my beloved patron deity, to transport me back to the love I'd lost.

Not since that fateful night in 1866, when I lay entombed in the Arizona cave, my body shrouded in the semblance of death, had I felt the irresistible call of Mars' red light. My outstretched arms seemed to yearn towards the star, as if pleading for a return of the extraordinary power that had once swept me across the vast expanse of space, carrying me away from the world I knew.

I stumbled forward, my senses reeling, and crashed to the ground, mere inches from the bluff's precipitous edge. The world around me blurred, and I felt the memories of that forsaken cave flood back, the echoes of the unspeakable terrors that lurked within its shadows. My muscles, once more, refused to obey my will, and I felt the creeping numbness spreading through my limbs, as if I was being pulled back into the very anaesthesia that had claimed me all those years ago.

With a Herculean effort, I shook off the paralysis, and my vision snapped back into focus. As I struggled to my feet, the faint scent of blooming wildflowers wafted through the air, a stark contrast to the arid vastness of Mars. My gaze drifted back to the star, my hands reaching out, imploring the cosmos to grant me passage once more.

It wasn't long before my plea was answered. In an instant, I vanished, leaving behind the familiar world I'd known for so long. The cold, dark void enveloped me, and I felt the disorienting rush of weightlessness, a sensation eerily familiar from my previous journey.
