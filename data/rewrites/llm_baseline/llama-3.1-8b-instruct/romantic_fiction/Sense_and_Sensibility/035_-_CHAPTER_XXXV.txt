Though she couldn't help but feel a twinge of relief that one significant impediment stood between them, she acknowledged the difficulties that would have arisen from navigating the complexities of Mrs. Ferrars's character.

As she pondered the intricacies of family dynamics, Elinor found herself pondering the enigmatic Lucy Steele. How could someone so enamored with civility be so blinded by the faint praise bestowed upon her by Mrs. Ferrars? Elinor observed that Lucy's excitement seemed almost inexplicable, especially considering the clear intentions behind Mrs. Ferrars's actions. It was as if Lucy's interest and vanity had created a distorted lens through which she viewed the world, convincing her that the attention she received was genuine, rather than merely a product of her own circumstances.
