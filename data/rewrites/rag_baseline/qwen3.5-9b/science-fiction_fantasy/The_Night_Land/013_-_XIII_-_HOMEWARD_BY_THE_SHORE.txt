The air changed instantly. We stepped out of the Upward Gorge, that sad, inward-facing wound in the great mountains, and halted on the flat ground beyond its mouth. Behind us lay the darkness; ahead, the light.

Naani stopped moving, then started again, her chest rising and falling in rapid, shallow gasps. She spun in a slow circle, taking in everything—the horizon, the sky, the sheer scale of the land. Her eyes were wide, burning with a raw, unfiltered wonder. For the first time in her life, she was not running from dread, but standing inside it, watching it dissolve.

She turned upward, staring into the black throat of the Gorge, trying to comprehend the depth of the shadow she had just escaped. Fear flickered across her face, sharp and sudden, before she forced herself to look back at the opening. A wave of relief washed over her, followed immediately by the dizzying realization of how vast the open country was. The Great Sea stretched out before her, a blue expanse that defied logic. She laughed, then cried, the sounds mingling in a single, broken chord of amazement. She kept turning, left and right, inhaling the wide air as if it were medicine she had starved for years. Never before had she known a place so broad, so light.

We realized we no longer needed to whisper. The silence of the gorge was gone, replaced by the ambient noise of a living world. Naani shouted, testing the acoustics like a child playing with an echo. Her voice traveled far, dissolving into the distance, swallowed by the Country.

Almost instantly, a response came back from the dark mountains behind us. It sounded like an echo, but it lacked the rhythm of reflection. Was it a trick of the wind? A supernatural call from the gloom? Or simply the mind playing tricks on itself? Unsure, we ran a few more steps downward, seeking solid ground beneath our feet. Only when we felt the weight of the Gorge lift completely did we stop.

We sought a flat rock for sustenance and found one high above the lower lands. Climbing up, we settled onto the stone to eat and drink, sitting close together. Happiness radiated between us, but it was a guarded happiness. We scanned the perimeter constantly, wary of the Humpt Men or any other lurking danger.

Naani stared out over the landscape, transfixed by the sight of the sea. It stirred something ancient and painful within her—a mixture of vague, dreamlike memories that hurt and pleased her simultaneously. Suddenly, tears welled in her eyes. She pulled away from the view and pressed herself into my arms, needing the physical contact to anchor herself, to remind her of who she was. In my embrace, she found her natural joy again.

I spoke softly about the clarity of the air, noting how it felt to her. To me, it felt familiar, a sensation I recognized from my own time trapped in the Dark Land.

Abruptly, Naani broke from her reverie. She spoke in fragments, words that seemed to belong to an eternity ago, memories so old they shimmered like moonlight. Then, in a heartbeat, she was present again, her entire being focused on me, wrapped in a solemn, intimate silence.

The ocean called to her with a voice that sounded ancient, waking her partially. I was half-woken too, though not as thoroughly as I had been during my initial journey. Yet, her presence stirred me, bringing my own dormant memories and dream-thoughts rushing back to the surface.

We sat there, shaken by these shared visions of the olden world, lying beneath the terrible, massive roof of the night that covered the Country. I found myself unable to articulate the turmoil and the peace swirling in our spirits.

Far below, miles away where the shoreline curved, a great mist and steam rose from the earth. This was the same vapor I had navigated on my outward journey. Naani asked about it, and I explained what I knew: we would have to pass through it soon.

She marveled at the volcanoes burning in the sea, scattered across the wide Country. Their height and grandeur filled her with a strange humility. Taking advantage of her vulnerability, I drew her into my arms. I kissed her because she was utterly sweet, lovely in her naturalness. She kissed me back, asking questions between the presses of her lips, eager to learn more of the Country, perhaps also driven by a sweet, playful boldness born of pure joy.

She kissed me three times, passionately and warmly. Then she grabbed my shoulders with her small hands, which looked delicate against the metal of my armor. She shook me gently, demanding a faster response, her expression full of innocent mischief. She needed to be kissed hard.

I answered her, speaking a word between each kiss. Quickly, she slipped her hand between our mouths. I kissed her palm instead, noting its small size and prettiness. Without warning, she opened her fingers and kissed me through the gaps, then snapped them shut, stopping me mid-kiss.

Later, I made her stand on the rock and freed her hair from its binding, letting it fall over her shoulders. I removed her boots, revealing her bare, pretty feet. At first, she hesitated, refusing slightly, but then she became compliant, standing obediently while I worked my will. She was shy, blushing brightly, which only added to her charm.

Once satisfied, I stepped back to look at her. She gazed at me with a quaint, naughty expression, then turned abruptly, pretending to be a stiff, motionless dummy. When she faced me again, looking serious and sedate, she suddenly extended her foot, pressing her pink toes against my lips. Caught off guard, I froze, unable to react before she snatched her foot away. She sprang into my arms, wanting to be hugged and loved deeply. I laughed tenderly, telling her I wished I had a large enough pocket to carry her always close to my heart. She laughed mischievously, threatening to tickle and pinch me if I tried to carry her that way. I shook her gently, and she kissed me naughtily amidst the movement. What does a man do with someone like that?

She then demanded to be seated properly so she could reach the pouch on my back. She retrieved the comb and began grooming her hair. I sat beside her, talking and joking, feeling lighter than I had in ages. Though I dreaded the Humpt Men and the monsters of the Sea Country, I felt no lingering horror. Everything possessed a natural order; there was no evil force, only things that existed.

After she finished combing her hair and bound it atop her head, I asked her to let it hang loose over her shoulders. She smiled, delighted by my preference.

We organized our gear. I put her boots back on, and we prepared to continue our descent into the Country of the Seas. We walked at a steady pace, not tiring quickly, for I intended to rest before reaching the boiling steam near the shore.

For six hours, we walked along the base of the mountains toward the steam vents. We were still an hour away from the source. Our pace was slower than my previous outward journey, which had been frantic and intense. By the time we reached the eighteenth hour of travel, we were exhausted and ready for sleep.

We found a tall, hard rock with a flat summit large enough for two. Once safely atop, we ate, drank, and slept, using the cloak as Naani wished, though the warmth of the Country made it unnecessary.

We woke after seven hours of uninterrupted sleep. Sitting upright, we looked at one another, seeing each other anew in the morning light. A fresh joy sparked in our gazes. Naani moved into my arms, kissing me, needing the affection. We both hungered for the connection, deeper than mere comfort.

I prepared breakfast. The water fizzed vigorously, surprising us. We ate and drank, happy and content, chatting about trivialities while Naani surveyed the wonders of the new land. I kept my eyes on the immediate vicinity, scanning for threats.

My gaze drifted to the mountains framing the Gorge. In the calm light, they appeared truly monstrous, a wall rising endlessly until it vanished into the deathly upper night, lost forever. These were the same thoughts I had held vaguely on the way out, but now, with rest and conversation, they seemed clearer. I mentioned this to illustrate how differently restfulness altered perception compared to the rush of escape.

Our meal and conversation dragged on, but eventually, we hurried to finish, sensing a tendency toward lethargy. We descended from the sleeping rock quickly and resumed our journey at a brisk pace.

Soon, we heard the distant hissing of steam and the roaring bursts of boiling water. The sound was strange, yet familiar to me. I assured Naani, and she approached me. Together, we entered the steam.

We walked for over three hours. I placed Naani directly behind me, shielding her from the disorienting maze of the steam and preventing her from stumbling into hidden boiling pools. I used the shoreline to my left as a guide, though visibility was nil. We could not see the sea, only walking inches from the water's edge.

The sea seemed to boil in patches, creating indistinguishable hot pools. We navigated this uncertainty with extreme caution. Around us, the water erupted with shrieks, whistles, and roars, sounding occasionally like great monsters. The ground trembled beneath our feet. Sometimes, however, there was only the hiss of steam and a lonely, low piping from a distant crack in the earth.
