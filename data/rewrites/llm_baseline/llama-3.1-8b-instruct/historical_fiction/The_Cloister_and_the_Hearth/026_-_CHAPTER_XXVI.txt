As the warm sunlight streamed through the windows of the cloister, Gerard shifted uncomfortably in his bed, his irritability from the previous day still simmering beneath the surface. Denys, his trusted companion, hastily rose to attend to his needs, promising to procure a refreshing lemon to soothe his parched throat. The search proved more arduous than expected, for lemons were a rare commodity in the North, and Denys returned after a lengthy absence, his brow furrowed with concern.

Just as Gerard was about to voice his impatience, a dignified figure entered the room. The old gentleman, resplendent in a long, sober gown adorned with rich fur trim, cherry-colored hose, and pointed shoes, exuded an air of authority. A sword hung at his side in a luxurious leather scabbard, and a majestic white beard framed his stern features. His head was crowned with a four-cornered cap, its fur border a testament to his refined taste. A boy trailed behind him, carrying a basket filled with an assortment of medical supplies, which seemed to be a magnet for Gerard's gaze.

The old gentleman approached Gerard's bedside with measured steps, his voice low and soothing. "How fares the young man?" he inquired, his eyes scanning Gerard's face with a practiced intensity. Gerard replied that his wound had begun to lose its sting, but his throat remained parched and his head heavy.

"A wound, you say?" The doctor's expression turned thoughtful. "I wasn't informed of this earlier. Allow me to take a look." He gently examined the injury, his fingers probing the edges of the wound. "Ah, yes, a clean bite, indeed. The mastiff's teeth must have been quite sharp to inflict such a mark."

Gerard interjected, "Sir, it was no dog, but a bear that attacked me."

The doctor's expression turned severe. "Bear, you say? Young man, I implore you, do not trifle with the man of science who has dedicated his life to healing. A bear's paw, perhaps, but certainly not the work of a wild beast. Think carefully before you speak, lest you mislead the physician." He paused, his eyes narrowing. "I assure you, I have dissected many animals in my time, and I can confidently say that no bear's claw would have inflicted this type of wound."

Gerard protested, "But, sir, I said 'a bear,' not 'a bear's jaw.'
