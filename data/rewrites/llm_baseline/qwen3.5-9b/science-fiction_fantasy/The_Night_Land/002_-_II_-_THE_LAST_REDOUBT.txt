Since Mirdath, my beloved, died and left me alone in this world, I have endured an agony and a longing so profound and terrible that no words could ever describe it. For I, who possessed the entire world through her sweet love and companionship, who knew every joy and gladness of life, have known a loneliness so crushing that merely thinking of it stuns me.

Yet, I turn once more to my pen. Lately, a wondrous hope has taken root within me. At night, in my sleep, I awaken into the future of this world, witnessing strange marvels and tasting the gladness of life anew. I have learned the promise of tomorrow and visited in my dreams those places where, in the womb of Time, she and I shall meet again—tearing asunder in dreadful pain, only to reunite after strange ages in a glorious, mighty wonder.

This is the utterly strange tale of what I have seen. Truly, I must recount it, unless the task proves too great, so that in doing so I may find a little ease for my heart. Perhaps, too, I may offer a glimmer of hope to another poor human suffering from a longing just as dreadful for their own lost love.

Some will read this and deny its truth; others will argue. To them all, I say only: "Read!" Once you have read what I have written, you too will look toward Eternity alongside me, right up to its very gates. So, I begin my telling.

In this final epoch of my visions, I did not feel as though I were *dreaming*. Rather, it was as if I *awoke* there, plunged into the dark, *within the future of this world*. The sun had died. For me, newly awakened into that Future, looking back upon our Present Age was like gazing into dreams my soul knew to be real, yet which appeared to my newly opened eyes as a distant vision, strangely sanctified by peace and light.

Whenever I awoke into the Future, into the Everlasting Night that enveloped the world, I saw near me, encircling me entirely, a blurred greyness. Soon, however, this greyness would clear and fade like a dusky cloud, revealing a world of total darkness illuminated here and there by strange sights. With my awakening into that Future, I did not wake to ignorance, but to full knowledge of the things lighting the Night Land. Just as a man wakes each morning knowing instantly the names and nature of the time that has birthed him, so I knew those things. Simultaneously, I retained a subconscious awareness of this Present—the early life I now lead so utterly alone.

In my earliest recollection of *that* place, I was a youth of seventeen. My memory tells me that when I first woke—or rather, arrived—as it might be said, in that Future, I stood in one of the embrasures of the Last Redoubt. This was the great Pyramid of grey metal that sheltered the last millions of this world from the Powers of the Slayers.

So complete is my knowledge of that Place that I scarcely believe anyone here does not know it. Because of this familiarity, I may speak too casually of things requiring explanation for readers in our present day. There, standing and looking out, I was less the aged man of this era than the youth of *that* time, possessing the natural knowledge of *that* life gathered over seventeen years. Though, prior to my first vision, the man of this Age knew nothing of that other, future existence, I woke to it as naturally as a man wakes in his bed to the shining morning sun, recognizing it by name and meaning everything else. Even as I stood in that vast embrasure, I carried a knowledge, or memory, of this present life deep within me, touched with a halo of dreams, yet accompanied by a conscious longing for One known even there in a half-memory as Mirdath.

As stated, in my earliest memory, I recall standing in an embrasure high on the side of the Pyramid, peering outward through a peculiar spyglass toward the North-West. Yes, filled with youth, adventure, and a half-fearful heart.

In my brain resided the knowledge accumulated during all the years of my life in the Redoubt. Until that moment, however, the Man of this Present Time knew nothing of that future existence. Now, I stood there, suddenly possessing the knowledge of a life already spent in that strange land, while deeper within me lingered the misty knowings of our current Age—and perhaps others as well.

Looking North-West through the spyglass, I saw a landscape I had studied for all those years, naming every feature and calculating its precise distance from the "Centre-Point" of the Pyramid—a dimensionless sphere of polished metal located in the Room of Mathematics, where I studied daily. Through the glass, I saw clearly the bright glare of fire rising from the Red Pit, shining upward against the underside of the vast chin of the North-West Watcher—the Watching Thing of the North-West. *"That which hath Watched from the Beginning, and until the opening of the Gateway of Eternity"* came to my mind, recalling the words of Aesworpth, the Ancient Poet (though incredibly future to our time). Suddenly, these words seemed flawed. Looking deep into my being, I perceived, as in a dream, the sunlight and splendor of *this* Present Age. I was amazed.

Here, I must clarify: just as I awoke from *this* Age suddenly into *that* life, so too must the youth of that future time have awakened to the knowledge of *this* far-back life of ours. To him, it seemed a vision of the very beginnings of eternity, in the dawn of the world. Oh! I dread making this sufficiently clear: I and he were both *I*—the same soul. He, of that far date, vaguely seeing the life that *was* (which I now live); and I, of this time, beholding the life that I yet shall live. How utterly strange!

Yet, I do not claim holy truth in saying that I, in that future time, had *no* knowledge of *this* life before that awakening. I woke to find myself standing apart from other youths, possessing a dim, visionary knowledge of the past that confused and angered the scholars of that age. More on this anon. What I do know is that from that time onward, my knowledge and assurance of the Past became tenfold, confirmed by the memory of that life.

Thus, continuing my tale. Before proceeding, one other thing must be spoken: In the very moment I awoke from that youthfulness into the assured awareness of *this* our Age, the hunger of my love flew across the ages to me. What had been a mere memory-dream grew into the pain of Reality. I knew suddenly that I *lacked*; and from that moment, I went on listening, as my life is spent even now.

It was that I, freshly born in that future time, hungered strangely for My Beautiful One with the full strength of that new life, knowing she had been mine and might live again, just as I. As I said, I hungered, and found that I listened.

Now, returning from this digression: I was amazed to perceive, in memory, the unknowable sunshine and splendor of this age breaking so clearly through my previously vague and hazy visions. The ignorance of Aesworpth was shouted at me by the things I now *knew*.

From that time onward, for a little while, I was stunned by everything I knew, guessed, and felt. All the while, the hunger grew for that one I had lost in the early days—she who had sung to me in those faery days of light, that *had been* in verity. The special thoughts of that age looked back with keen, regretful wonder into the gulf of forgetfulness.

Presently, I turned from the haze and pain of my dream-memories back to the inconceivable mystery of the Night Land, which I viewed through the great embrasure. On none did it ever come with weariness to gaze upon all the hideous mysteries; so old and young watched, from early years to death, the black monstrosity of the Night Land, which this our last refuge of humanity held at bay.

To the right of the Red Pit lay a long, sinuous glare, which I knew as the Vale of Red Fire, and beyond that, for many dreary miles, the blackness of the Night Land. Across this expanse came the cold light from the Plain of Blue Fire.

Then, on the very borders of the Unknown Lands, lay a range of low volcanoes. These lit up, far away in the outer darkness, the Black Hills, where shone the Seven Lights. They neither twinkled nor moved nor faltered through Eternity; and even the great spyglass could make no understanding of them. Nor had any adventurer from the Pyramid ever returned to tell us anything of them. Here, let me note that down in the Great Library of the Redoubt were the histories of all those who ventured out into the monstrousness of the Night Land, risking not only their lives but the very spirit of life.

Surely, it is all so strange and wonderful to recount that I could almost despair contemplating the task ahead. There is so much to tell, and so few words given to man to make clear that which lies beyond sight and general knowledge.
