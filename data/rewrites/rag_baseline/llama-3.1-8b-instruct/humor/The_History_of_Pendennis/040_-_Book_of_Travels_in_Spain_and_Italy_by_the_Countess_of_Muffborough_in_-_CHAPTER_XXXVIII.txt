In the rarefied world of high society, where pecuniary influence often eclipses birthright, the Begum Clavering's impending arrival in London was met with a mixture of fascination and trepidation. Rumors of her immense fortune, accumulated through a labyrinthine network of indigo factories, opium clippers, and investments in native princely states, had preceded her, fueling whispers of awe and speculation among the metropolitan elite. Her account at the London bank was said to contain a sum so staggering that even the most discerning observers struggled to grasp its magnitude, reduced as it was to a cipher-ridden enigma that only added to the mystique surrounding her name.

As news of her imminent arrival spread, a flurry of diplomatic activity unfolded, with the Nawaub of Lucknow's envoy, Colonel Altamont, seeking to negotiate the sale of the fabled nose-ring diamond, 'the light of the Dewan.' A man of singular experience and intellect, Colonel Altamont had navigated the complexities of Mahometanism and undergone numerous perilous adventures, earning him a reputation as a shrewd and cunning diplomat. His connection to the Nawaub was said to be rooted in a shared history of adventure and mutual respect, with the Colonel having, at one time, served as the Nawaub's confidant and advisor.

Meanwhile, in the quieter sphere of social politics, the Chevalier Strong found himself drawn into the orbit of the Clavering family, as he oversaw the preparations for their townhouse in London. With an eye for detail and a passion for refinement, Strong supervised the arrangement of the interior, consulting with the city's leading upholsterers to create an atmosphere of elegance and sophistication. As he worked, he also saw fit to furnish his own chambers, stocking his cellar with fine wines and preparing for the select gatherings that would soon take place under his roof. Strong's friends praised his impeccable taste, and those who joined him for dinner found themselves treated to an exceptional claret, a testament to his growing prosperity.

As Strong's fortunes continued to rise, so too did his connection to the mysterious Colonel Altamont, who had taken up residence in the Chevalier's lodgings at Shepherd's Inn.
