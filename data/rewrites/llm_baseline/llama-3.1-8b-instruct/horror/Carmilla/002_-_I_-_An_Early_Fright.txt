The somber grandeur of our castle stood sentinel over the rolling hills of Styria, its turrets and spires reaching towards the sky like skeletal fingers. Though modest by some standards, our estate was a veritable kingdom, with an annual income of eight or nine hundred pounds – a sum that seemed princely in this rural corner of the world. My father, an Englishman, had acquired it through his service in the Austrian army, purchasing it with a combination of his pension and patrimony.

The castle itself was a treasure trove of Gothic charm, its stone façade adorned with numerous windows that seemed to wink like eyes in the fading light of day. A drawbridge, rarely raised, spanned the tranquil moat, where swans glided effortlessly across the surface, their snowy feathers glistening in the sunlight. Beyond the castle walls, the forest stretched out in every direction, its trees whispering secrets to one another in the gentle breeze.

From the hall door, the landscape unfolded like a canvas of emerald and gold. To the right, the forest marched off into the distance, unbroken for miles. To the left, the road wound its way through the trees, crossing a babbling brook that bled silver threads into the silence. Our nearest neighbor, the village of – well, it was hard to call it that – existed seven miles to the left, its inhabitants huddled together in a cluster of thatched roofs and chimneys. And if one looked hard enough, the ruined village of Karnstein lay three miles westward, its crumbling church a testament to the transience of human endeavor.

My family, a tiny coterie, inhabited these halls. My father, kind and gentle, grew older with each passing year, his eyes clouding over like the winter skies. I, too, was growing up, nineteen years old at the time of my tale, with eight years of memories stretching behind me like a rich tapestry. My mother, a Styrian beauty, had passed on when I was but an infant, leaving behind a void that only grew larger with time. But her absence was somewhat alleviated by Madame Perrodon, our devoted governess, whose round face and benevolent smile had become a fixture in my life. She was a woman of great kindness, a native of Berne who had brought a touch of Swiss warmth to our isolated world.
