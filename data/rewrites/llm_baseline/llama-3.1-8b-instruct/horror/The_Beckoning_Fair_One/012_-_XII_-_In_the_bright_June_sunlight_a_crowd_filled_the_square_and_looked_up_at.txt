As the warm June sun cast its golden rays upon the cobblestone square, the crowd gathered before the old house seemed to stretch on forever, their faces upturned towards the weathered façade with its telltale signs of age: faded insurance marks etched into the red brick walls, and the rustic wooden boards bearing the notice of the local agents, hung like makeshift lanterns above the narrow alleyway entrance. Two burly constables stood sentinel at the broken gate, holding back the throngs of curious onlookers, while the women lingered at the periphery, their whispers weaving an intricate tapestry of speculation. The children, sensing the somber mood, remained indoors, hidden behind closed doors.

A lanky figure stood at the center of a small cluster of listeners, regaling them with a tale that seemed to captivate the assembly. His eyes sparkled as he spoke, his voice rising and falling with the cadence of a seasoned storyteller. A stout, wide-eyed man, who had been trying to interject his own tale involving a mysterious key, found himself drowned out by the long-nosed man's animated retelling.

"...And I recall it as if it were yesterday," the man said, his voice dripping with conviction. "I was walking down the street, minding my own business, when I saw her emerge from that very doorway. Her face, dear heavens, it was as pale as alabaster, and her eyes... Ah, her eyes seemed to hold a world of sorrow within them."

A gentle murmur rippled through the crowd, and one of the listeners asked, "What was she like, Mr. Barrett?"

Barrett's eyes clouded over, and he began to speak in hushed tones, "It was as if she were the very embodiment of the biblical 'woman accused'—her skin as white as freshly fallen snow. I approached her, and with a firm but gentle tone, I said, 'Jane, this must cease at once; we are bound by a higher authority to eschew evil, and it is time for this to end.' And she replied, 'John, it costs four-and-sixpence a week.'"

The crowd fell silent, mesmerized by Barrett's words. Then, a soft inquiry broke the stillness, "Did Mrs. Barrett ever... see anything unusual, perhaps?"
