The moon cast a silver glow on the tranquil waters of the Thames as Daniel Deronda glided effortlessly through the ripples, his oars dipping in perfect syncopation. It was a summer evening, late July, and the air was alive with the sweet scent of blooming flowers and the gentle lapping of the water against the shore. The city's din and bustle seemed to fade away, leaving only the soothing rhythm of the river and the quiet solitude that came with it.

Deronda had grown accustomed to these evening strolls, finding solace in the stillness and the freedom to let his thoughts wander. A year had passed since his return to England, and though he'd begun to study law as a way to appease his uncle, Sir Hugo, he couldn't shake the feeling of indecision that had settled within him. The world beyond his narrow horizon beckoned, yet he felt unprepared to take his place in it. His love of boating had revived in this new life, providing a respite from the pressures of his family's expectations and the uncertainty that plagued him.

As he rowed, the stars began to twinkle in the darkening sky, and Deronda felt a sense of peace settle over him. He wasn't a sentimental soul, nor was he prone to flights of fancy, but rather a young man grappling with the weight of his own doubts. He questioned whether the world's battles were worth joining, whether the struggles and hardships were truly worth the cost. This was a concern shared by many of his contemporaries, those who felt suffocated by the responsibilities thrust upon them, yet lacked the courage to break free.

Sir Hugo, ever the pragmatist, found it puzzling that Deronda, so full of promise and potential, should be hampered by such thoughts. The young man's appearance, too, seemed at odds with his introspective nature – the dark-blue shirt, the clipped curls, and the soft waves of beard framing a face that had once been seraphic, now firmly terrestrial and manly. Yet, it was a face that commanded attention, not just for its physical beauty, but for the quiet dignity that shone within.

As Deronda passed under Kew Bridge, the city lights casting a kaleidoscope of colors on the water, he felt a sense of anonymity wash over him.
