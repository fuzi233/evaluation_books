As the moon cast its silvery glow over the landscape, Kantos Kan, Xodar, and I arrived at Hastor, the designated staging point for our mission to the south. Carthoris, Tars Tarkas, and Hor Vastus had departed ahead on another cruiser, leaving us to prepare for our journey. The transports were to depart immediately, moving cautiously southward, while our fleet of battleships would overtake them the following morning.

Upon our arrival, I was struck by the meticulous planning that had gone into every detail of the operation. Kantos Kan had left nothing to chance, and within a short span of time, the first transport lifted off, followed by the rest of the fleet, which formed a majestic procession stretching across the horizon. The night air was filled with the hum of engines and the soft whoosh of the vessels taking to the skies.

As I entered the cabin of Kantos Kan, a pang of dismay washed over me as I realized just how much time had passed since my imprisonment in the pits of Zat Arras. Three hundred and sixty-five days had slipped by, and I was faced with the cruel truth: we were too late to save Dejah Thoris. Our expedition had shifted from a rescue mission to one of vengeance.

I chose not to share this knowledge with my companions, knowing they had already borne enough of my burdens in the past. Instead, I focused on the broader goal of our mission – to expose the deceit that had been perpetrated upon the people of Barsoom for centuries, sparing countless lives from the terrible fate that awaited them at the end of their voluntary pilgrimages. By opening the Valley Dor to the red men, we could bring fertility to the parched lands and unlock the secrets of the Land of Lost Souls.

As we set out, the morning sun casting its golden light upon the ice cap, I couldn't help but think of the El Dorado hidden beneath our feet – a verdant oasis amidst the barren expanse of Barsoom. The only place where the rains and dew fell freely, where an open sea stretched out before us, and water flowed in abundance. And yet, this haven was inaccessible to the red men, locked away by the remnants of two once-great civilizations and the stronghold of superstition. If I could break down these barriers, it would be a fitting tribute to Dejah Thoris' memory – a testament to her courage and my own unwavering dedication to serving Barsoom.
