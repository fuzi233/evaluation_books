As the evening deepened into darkness, the star-studded sky was shrouded by a veil of gray rain-clouds, their color indistinguishable from the dull hue of the clouds that would dominate the day. Malone's footsteps echoed through the deserted streets, his gaze fixed on the horizon, where the fiery glow of the Stilbro' ironworks cast an eerie shimmer. He didn't pause to ponder the absence of the constellations or the tranquil atmosphere that usually filled the crisp air. Instead, he pressed on, his worn boots squelching in the muddy ruts that lined the causeway.

His destination was Redhouse Inn, its warm glow beckoning him like a haven from the chill and rain. As he approached, the scent of roasting meat wafted out into the night air, mingling with the murmur of laughter and the clinking of glasses. Malone's stomach growled in anticipation of a hot meal and a dram of whisky, but his thoughts were quickly interrupted by the sight of familiar faces within. He recognized the parishioners of Mr. Helstone, and his plans for a warm supper were foiled once more. With a sigh, he continued on his way.

Beyond the village, the highroad gave way to a series of fields, stretching out before him like a vast, flat expanse. Malone took a direct path through the soggy terrain, leaping over low stone walls and dodging hedges. The only sound was the patter of raindrops on the leaves and the distant rumble of water in the valley below. As he walked, the fields began to slope downward, and the sound of running water grew louder. A faint light flickered in the distance, drawing him toward it.

He spotted a small, white cottage and knocked on the door, the sound echoing through the stillness. A young servant answered, her face illuminated by the candlelight within. Malone's eyes adjusted to the warm glow, taking in the neat, crimson-draped doors and the strip of carpet leading up the stairs. "Is Mr. Moore at home?" he asked.

"Yes, sir, but he's not here," the servant replied, her voice cheerful despite the gloom outside.

"Not in? Where is he, then?" Malone pressed, his patience wearing thin.

"He's at the mill, in the counting-house," she said, before nodding toward the stairs.
