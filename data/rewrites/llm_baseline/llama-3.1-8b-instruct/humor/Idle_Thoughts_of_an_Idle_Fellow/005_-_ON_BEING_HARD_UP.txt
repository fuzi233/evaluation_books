As I settled into my favorite armchair, I found myself pondering the peculiarities of being hard up. It was a notion that seemed to settle over me like a shroud, weighing heavily on my mind, despite my initial intention to craft something witty and original. My fingers instinctively drifted into my pockets, a habit I'd cultivated over the years, and one that my sisters, cousins, and aunts had long since grown accustomed to teasing me about. They'd often remark that it wasn't becoming of a gentleman, that it lacked refinement. But I couldn't help but wonder – what exactly was the harm in placing one's own hands in one's own pockets? Perhaps it was the principle behind it that bothered them, rather than the act itself. Still, I couldn't deny that I felt more at ease with my hands tucked away, a sense of security that came from knowing I had a small stash hidden within reach.

In my younger days, when my finances were as precarious as a performer on a tightrope, I'd splurge on a penny just to hear the coins jingling in my pocket. It was a paltry sum, to be sure, but it brought me a fleeting sense of satisfaction. And when my income dwindled to a mere shilling, I learned to appreciate the value of every penny. I'd change it into smaller denominations, just to savor the sound of the coins clinking together. It was a quirk, perhaps, but one that gave me a strange sense of comfort.

My experiences as a provincial actor and a writer for the press had schooled me in the art of living on very little. I recall the weeks I spent subsisting on 15 shillings, rationing every penny to make it last. I remember the fortnight I eked out on a single greatcoat, the makeshift meals, and the endless walks to save a few pence here and there. And through it all, I gained a profound appreciation for the simple things in life – the taste of a well-brewed cup of coffee, the warmth of a fire on a cold winter's night, and the quiet contentment that came from wearing a collar for four consecutive days.

If only more people could experience the simplicity of living on a shoestring, I thought to myself. It would temper their tastes, make them more mindful of their expenses, and teach them to appreciate the little things.
