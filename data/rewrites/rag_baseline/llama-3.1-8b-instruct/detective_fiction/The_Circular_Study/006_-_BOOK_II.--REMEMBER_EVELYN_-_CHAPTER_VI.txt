The morning sunlight filtering through the leaded panes of the townhouse's parlor cast intricate patterns on the polished wood floor, a dance of light and shadow that seemed to mirror the turmoil within Lady Amelia Butterworth. She stood before the fireplace, her slender frame tensed as if poised to flee, yet rooted to the spot by the weight of her own secrets. Her eyes, a piercing green, locked onto those of Detective Gryce, their intensity a challenge he had grown accustomed to over the years. Yet, today, something about her demeanor struck him as off-kilter, a discordant note in the familiar melody of their encounters.

"Amelia, what does this mean?" he asked, his voice low and even, a counterpoint to the rising tide of her agitation. "Have you---"

There, the single word burst forth, a tiny explosion of sound that sent a faint tremor through the air. "You've caught me at my old tricks, and I'm correspondingly ashamed, and you, triumphant." A wry smile twisted her lips, a fleeting glimpse of the woman she once was, before the mask of reserve slipped back into place. "The parasol you sent to my house, sir, it wasn't mine. I was in the room where you picked it up, as you've so cleverly concluded, and since it's useless for me to feign innocence, I've come to confess."

Gryce's chair scraped against the floor as he drew it closer to hers, his eyes never leaving hers. "You were there," he repeated, his tone measured, "and when? I don't presume to ask for what purpose."

But Amelia Butterworth had already embarked on her confession, her words tumbling forth like a dam released. "I sought this case, sir, despite my better judgment. Not that I relished the prospect of entanglement, but the circumstances conspired against me. I recognized the exigencies of the occasion and appreciated your surprise at finding a stranger like myself, unconnected to Mr. Adams, drawn into this web of intrigue. It's a curious sensation, sir, to find oneself in a position where one's own actions seem to mock one's intentions."

As she spoke, the tension in her frame began to dissipate, replaced by a sense of resignation, as if she'd finally shed a burden she'd carried for far too long. Gryce watched, fascinated, as the layers of her character unfolded before him, revealing the complexity beneath the surface
