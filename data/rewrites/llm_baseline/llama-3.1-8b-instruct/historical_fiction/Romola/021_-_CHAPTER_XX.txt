The Carnival's final week descended upon Florence like a vibrant tapestry, woven from threads of laughter and music. In the streets, masqueraded processions marched to the rhythm of newly introduced songs, their melodies carrying the essence of Lorenzo the Magnificent's legacy. The air vibrated with the sound of rigoletto, a traditional dance performed in the piazza under the radiant blue sky. Amidst the festivities, jests of all kinds abounded – from playful pranks of tossing comfits to the rowdier sport of stone-throwing, a spectacle that, though rough, added a layer of excitement to the celebrations. As the boys and young men, an integral part of the Florentine crowds, reveled in their unbridled energy, they would often bar the way, demanding tributes of food and bonfires to fuel their merriment. It was a custom as old as the city itself, one that allowed them to express their exuberance and playfulness, their actions often veering between harmless antics and, occasionally, more serious consequences.

As the morning sun cast its warm glow over the city, Tito Melema and Bernardo Dovizi strolled through the Por' Santa Maria, their conversation animated and engaging, yet seemingly unrelated to the lively atmosphere surrounding them. Though they laughed and gestured enthusiastically, their eyes would frequently dart around, scanning the crowd. Tito's gaze drifted towards the Lung' Arno, where the Ponte Rubaconte stood, and in a fleeting glance, he caught sight of someone he would rather not encounter at that moment. Preoccupied with the upcoming events of his life, Tito was preparing for a significant milestone: his betrothal, scheduled to take place that very evening. The decision had been made relatively hastily, given that preparations for the marriage had been underway for some time, largely thanks to Tito's contributions towards furnishing the rooms in Bernardo's dwelling. Originally, it was intended to wait until after Easter, when Tito's period of probation would be complete, but an unexpected opportunity arose when Bernardo Dovizi requested Tito's assistance in Rome, arranging a library for Cardinal Giovanni. The prospect of advancement was too enticing to resist, and Tito had pressed for an earlier betrothal, knowing it would ease his departure and provide a sense of security for his future with Romola.
