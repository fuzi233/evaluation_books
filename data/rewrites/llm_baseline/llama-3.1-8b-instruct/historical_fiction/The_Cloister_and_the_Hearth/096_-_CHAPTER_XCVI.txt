As the first light of dawn crept over the sleepy streets of Rotterdam, Margaret slipped out of the house, leaving behind the warm bundle of joy that was her little Gerard. She entrusted Reicht, the loyal and sturdy servant, with a special mission: to accompany Gerard to Gouda and present him to the kindly parson who had agreed to care for him until she returned. With precise instructions and a gentle smile, Margaret bid Reicht farewell and set out for Jorian Ketel's humble abode.

The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air as she arrived at the cozy cottage, and Margaret's thoughts turned to the wise and kind-hearted man within. "He is the closest I've ever met," she mused, "and I know I can trust him." Together, Margaret and Jorian hatched a plan, one that would prove to be a clever and discreet maneuver, but one that would also leave Margaret's conscience to grapple with the weight of its consequences.

As she avoided the Gouda parsonage, Margaret couldn't shake the feeling that Gerard's father had misjudged her. "What did he think I was?" she wondered, her voice rising in indignation. "Did he truly believe I could so easily forget the love we shared?" The sting of Gerard's words lingered, and Margaret's pride and modesty conspired against her, whispering that she should steer clear of the parsonage from now on.

Days turned into weeks, and Margaret's maternal heart yearned for her son. The ache of separation grew unbearable, and she found herself sneaking back to the parsonage under the cover of darkness. Lurking outside, she waited for Reicht to emerge, and when he finally did, she whispered urgently, "Reicht, for goodness' sake, bring him out to me unseen." The devoted servant soon concocted an excuse to take Gerard outside, and the reunion was a joyful, tearful affair.

As the days passed, Margaret and Reicht devised a plan to reunite Gerard with his mother. They arranged for Reicht to escort him halfway to Rotterdam each day, where Margaret would await their arrival, her heart swelling with anticipation. During these clandestine meetings, the three would steal glances at one another, their eyes locking in a language of love and longing. As Gerard played alone at their feet, the two women would steal away to discuss a matter of great import – the proposed marriage between Margaret and Luke Peterson.
