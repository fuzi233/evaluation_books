As the sun beat down upon the Cordillera's rugged spine, casting dappled shadows on the forest floor, Amyas and his companions settled into their makeshift camp, nestled among the tree-ferns that swayed gently in the breeze. A fortnight's toil had passed, and though the trials they faced were nothing new, they pressed on, undeterred. The verdant expanse of the eastern plains had given way to the imposing city of Santa Fe, its gardens and grandeur a tantalizing prospect that remained forever out of reach. Yet, their journey was not without purpose. The Indian lad, whose eyes sparkled with a keen understanding of the land, had unearthed the presence of a gold-train making its way down from the city towards the Magdalena.

As they waited patiently by the rickety road, a serpentine path that wound its way through the hills, they pondered the enigmatic roar that had followed them since leaving the banks of the Meta. Some attributed it to jaguar or monkey, but none could explain its persistence. Meanwhile, their efforts to procure sulphur and nitre had yielded nothing, forcing them to rely on their swords and arrows – a reliance that Yeo found distasteful. Still, the memory of Drake's triumph at Nombre de Dios and the gold-train he claimed there offered a beacon of hope.

Their vigilance was rewarded when, as night began to fall, a sharp crack split the air, followed by a mournful cry. "That was a whip's crack," Yeo observed, "and a woman's wail. They're close by, lads!" Amyas's eyes narrowed as he scanned the surroundings, his gaze drawn to the glint of metal in the distance. "Men?" he whispered, his voice barely audible. "I trust you all not to shoot until I give the signal. Then, let us offer them one arrow." The men nodded, their faces set in determination, as they waited for the approaching party to emerge from the darkness.
