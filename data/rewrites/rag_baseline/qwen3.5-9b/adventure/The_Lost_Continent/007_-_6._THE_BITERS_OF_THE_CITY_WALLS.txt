The air in the banquet hall tasted of stale meat and unresolved tension. I had returned to the capital of Atlantis, stepping onto soil that felt less like a home and more like a stage where the script had changed overnight. My own contribution to this evening was negligible—a limp, a hesitation, a deliberate refusal to act. The old loyalties, the rigid hierarchy where serving the King meant serving the Priesthood, had dissolved into a muddy gray. Phorenice was a new variable, a self-made ruler who seemed to exist outside the old equations. If Zaemon spoke for the Priests, and the Priests had placed her at odds with them, how was a man of simple honesty to calculate the trajectory of his allegiance?

Logic offered a sterile solution: support whichever government promised the greatest stability for the collective whole. That was the path I chose. But logic also pointed elsewhere. Whispers of rebellion had reached my ears, of a faction strong enough to besiege the very heart of the city. Before committing fully, I needed data. I needed to weigh the rebels against the crown. So, on the very night I had planted my boot back on Atlantic soil, I decided to gather intelligence.

Inside the pyramid, no one challenged me. The feast had ended prematurely, cut short by the grotesque tableau of Tarca writhing on the floor, exposing the leprous scars that marred his flesh. Even the most voracious guests had lost their appetites. Phorenice glowered at the remaining diners, her silence heavier than any accusation, until her gaze finally settled on me. Her expression had softened, just a fraction.

"You may be an honest man, Deucalion," she said eventually, "but you possess a chilling indifference. When will you thaw?" She smiled, a thin, brittle thing. "Perhaps soon. For tonight, I bid you farewell. Tomorrow, we shall seize this nation by the shoulders and reshape it."

She departed, followed by Ylga. Taking precedence over my station, I exited the hall while the others remained, standing in respectful silence. I paused first at Tarca, placing a hand on his diseased skin. "An unfortunate man," I murmured. "Yet I admire a brave soldier. If relief can be found for your plague, I will use my influence to secure it."

His gratitude emerged as a broken whisper from ruined lips, eliciting shudders of feigned disgust from those nearby. I turned my dark gaze upon them. "Your charity, my lords, appears as scarce as your courage. You feign a disdain for Zaemon's doctrines, a mocking contempt for the priesthood, yet when it comes to touching the afflicted, you display a discretion we would have labeled ugly in the old days. I would sooner be Tarca, covered in filth, than any of you standing there in your pristine indifference."

With that dismissal, I waited for the customary salutations, received them with cold detachment, and walked out of the hall without granting anyone else a second glance. I proceeded to the grand gate of the pyramid, summoned the guard officer, and requested passage. The man was deferential but hesitant.

"Has your retinue not yet arrived?"

"I travel alone."

"Do you comprehend the condition of the streets?"

"I have navigated them twenty years ago. I can find my way."

"The city is under siege," the officer insisted. "The populace is starving. They roam in packs after dark. While I have no doubt you could overcome any obstacle in combat..."

"Exactly. I seek no brawl tonight. Provide me with sufficient escort. You know best what is required. I am accustomed to walking through streets of peace."

Twenty men were assigned, forming a double file on either side. We marched from the perfumed interior of the pyramid into the cool, moonlit expanse of the city. My destination was the walls; I intended to assess the disposition of the rebels.

However, the gods seemed determined to offer me a different lesson first. The city I observed was no longer the familiar accumulation of ages I remembered. The splendor remained, amplified. Entire districts had been razed to accommodate new palaces and towering pyramids for the wealthy. The architectural brilliance was undeniable, a testament to human ingenuity that bordered on the divine. It was easy to understand why 'barbarians' worshipped such structures, viewing them as miracles beyond human capability.

Yet, the cost was visible. Every magnificent pile cast a shadow over a hundred displaced families, now homeless and scattered across the open spaces. Consequently, every niche held a sleeper, every shadow harbored a band of desperate predators ready to strike.

I am not a champion of the common folk. If a man starves, he works; if he cannot, he dies, and the state is relieved of a burden. But here, amidst the grandeur, the evidence of blind oppression was stark: corpses bloated and skeletal, sprawled in the gutters. Save for the elite, the capital was a prison of misery.

Though I despised the sight, I could not protest. Under ancient law, all were servants of the king, subject to his will, and Phorenice occupied that throne. I suppressed my outrage, lifting my eyes from the squalor to the purple night sky, searching the stars for guidance on my future course.

The winding streets eventually led us to the walls. As we paced alongside the ramparts, answering the drumbeats of the sentries, we reached the great gate guarded by the garrison captain.

There was commotion. Laughter echoed into the stillness, punctuated by the snarls and roars of great beasts and the screams of the injured. This was not a surprise encounter. The capital's entrances were fortresses within fortresses. Four pairs of massive stone valves, each a block two men high and thick, blocked the way. The wall itself was doubled, creating an enclosed arena between the layers. Each gate had an inner and outer door, linked by hidden machinery so that one could not open unless the other was shut. To force them without siege engines was futile.

My escort sounded the horn. A warder appeared at an arrow-slit, inspecting us. His recitation of the ancient protocol was lengthy, drowned intermittently by the noises from within. Finally, he released the stone bolts and swung open one massive valve.

We crowded into the chamber between the walls. The warder returned, bowing low.

"I require no token," I stated bluntly. "Perform your duty. Open the other door."

"With respect, my lord, it is safer to announce your presence first. There is a contest underway in the circus below, and the tigers are currently untamed."

"Tigers?"

"If you allow them the name. They are baiting prisoners with two great beasts sent by the Empress to reinforce our defenses. However, there are wardrooms leading off this passage, with galleries overlooking the arena. From there, you may observe the spectacle undisturbed."

The desire to kill repulsed me, yet suspicion gnawed at me regarding the Empress's orders. Curiosity won out. I entered the warder's lodge and ascended the galleries commanding the circus. These archaic structures served as a secondary defense line; if the outer doors were breached, enemies trapped in the arena could be slaughtered without retaliation. Yet, they were also excellent viewing boxes.

The arena was illuminated by moonlight, the air thick with the acrid scent of blood. What transpired was not sport, but slaughter. I am not prudish regarding combat. Grant a prisoner weapons, place him in a pit with reasonably matched opponents, and let him fight to the death—I can applaud the ferocity. A warrior earns his end in hot blood rather than by the executioner's blade. Watching a valiant struggle is a noble pastime. But this was no fair duel.

The beasts were unfair adversaries. Twenty armed men might flee. When the warder mentioned 'tigers,' I assumed the great cats of the forest. Instead, I beheld the terror of the deep caves: colossal felines of such ponderous strength that, in hunger, they could drag down a mammoth. How they had been captured baffled me. Hunting such creatures requires infinite caution and stratagem; trapping one alive and chaining it to a city was beyond my wildest schemes. Yet, here they were, proof of Phorenice's genius.

Their purpose was clear. While loose in the arena, no living creature could traverse from one gate to the other. They were living barricades. Bronze collars encircled their throats, attached to massive chains anchored to the wall, controlled by windlasses in the hidden galleries. Normally, they were tethered at opposite ends, leaving a corridor open.

But now, the chains were slackened. The great striped brutes prowled the arena, their links clanking softly. Lying on the pavement were the bodies of eight men, dead and largely untouched. The tigers paused occasionally to nuzzle or bat them with playful paws, showing no intent to eat. The cruelty had become routine; they awaited fresh victims.

Sure enough, a valve of the distant gate swung back. A prisoner, struggling violently, was shoved into the arena. He fell face-down, staring at the ground, passively awaiting his fate. The gate slammed shut. The tigers turned. Wagers rippled among the spectators behind the slits.

There was a calculated cruelty to this game. The windlasses cranked, drawing the chains tight by several links. One tiger crouched, lashing its tail, launching a terrifying spring.
