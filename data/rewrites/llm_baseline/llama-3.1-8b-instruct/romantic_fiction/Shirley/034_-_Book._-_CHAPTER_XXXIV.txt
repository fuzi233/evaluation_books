As Martin settled into his chair, the scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, mingling with the soft hum of the morning sun peeking through the windows of the Yorke household. His stomach growled with anticipation, eager to be filled with the hearty meal that awaited him. But his mind wandered to the upcoming Sunday, a day he had always dreaded. Not for the reasons one might expect – he didn't dislike the ritual of attending church itself – but rather for the lengthy sermons that followed, which often left him feeling restless and uninspired.

This particular Saturday, however, held a different promise. A thick layer of snow blanketed the landscape outside, casting a serene silence over the village. As his mother, Mrs. Yorke, poured steaming hot water over his breakfast porridge, she suggested that the children stay indoors, forgoing their usual trip to church. Instead, they would spend the morning listening to John Wesley's stirring sermons, a tradition that both she and her husband held dear.

Martin's gaze drifted back to the pages of his book, a collection of fairy legends that transported him to far-off lands and exotic culinary delights. He savored each bite of his bread and milk, the simple pleasure a welcome respite from the monotony of his studies. When his mother asked him to join the others in reading the sermons, he politely declined, his eyes never leaving the words on the page.

"It's not fit weather," his father chimed in, but Martin remained silent, lost in his own world. His mother chuckled knowingly, aware of her son's stubborn streak. "Martin hates to go to church, but he hates still more to obey," she teased, her voice laced with affection.

Martin looked up, his expression unyielding. "I am not swayed by mere perverseness," he declared, his voice barely above a whisper. His parents exchanged a knowing glance, accustomed to his dramatic flair. "By a complicated mix of motives, which I dare not attempt to explain, lest I unravel the very fabric of my thoughts," he continued, his words dripping with sincerity.

His father laughed heartily, envisioning his son's future as a skilled orator. "Nature has gifted him with the makings of a fine lawyer – brass, self-assurance, and words, oh so many words!"
