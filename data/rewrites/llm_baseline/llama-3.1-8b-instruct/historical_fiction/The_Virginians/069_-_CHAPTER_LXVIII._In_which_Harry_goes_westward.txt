As the time for departure drew near, a melancholy settled over the household like a veil of silence. It was a feeling familiar to many, a bittersweet reminder of the impermanence of bonds and the vast expanse of the unknown. Harry Warrington's thoughts, like those of countless others, were consumed by the ache of leaving behind loved ones. His brother George, too, felt the weight of parting, though he stood steadfast as a pillar of strength.

As they waited for a favorable wind, the brothers found themselves in Portsmouth, surrounded by the bustling activity of the harbor. The sound of ships' bells and the distant rumble of cannons served as a poignant backdrop to their farewell. George's eyes lingered on his brother's pale face, etched with a mix of sadness and resolve. The moment passed, and George returned home, his heart heavy with the knowledge of what lay ahead.

In the quiet of his lodgings, George couldn't help but feel a pang of sadness as he gazed upon the small room that had once been Harry's sanctuary. The ashes of a half-burned paper in the grate served as a poignant reminder of the memories they had shared. As he made his way to Dean Street, the flickering candlelight casting eerie shadows on the walls, he found solace in the gentle voice of his beloved Theo. Her words, laced with a hint of melancholy, spoke of the selfishness of finding happiness amidst sorrow. "Don't," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the crackling flames. "It's wicked of me to be glad when Hetty is so unhappy."

The arrival of the evening brought a welcome distraction, as servants entered with supper and lit the lamps. The conversation turned to the destination of the fleet, and the collective hopes pinned on General Wolfe's success. Though his health seemed precarious, his spirit remained unbroken, and the nation held its breath in anticipation of his triumph. As the night wore on, the family gathered around the table, their faces illuminated only by the soft glow of the candles. In the midst of the warmth and camaraderie, the ache of parting lingered, a reminder that even as the sun rose on a new day, the hearts of those left behind would forever remain tied to the loved ones who had set sail into the unknown.
