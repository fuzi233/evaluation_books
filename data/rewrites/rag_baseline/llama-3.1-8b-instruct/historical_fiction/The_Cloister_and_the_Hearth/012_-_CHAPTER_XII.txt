The faint scent of woodsmoke wafted through the narrow corridors, a reminder of the fire crackling in the hearth below. In the dimly lit antechamber, Ghysbrecht Van Swieten waited, the key to Gerard's prison clutched tightly in his fist. His plan was to visit at ten o'clock, when hunger's pangs might render his captive more pliable. As he ascended the stairs, a crusty loaf and a pitcher of ale accompanied him, along with his loyal servant, well-armed against any unforeseen circumstances. Ghysbrecht paused at the door, listening intently. The silence within was oppressive, heavy as a weight that threatened to crush him.

A wry smile spread across his features, a calculated gamble playing out in his mind. "By this time," he thought, "he'll be as downtrodden as Albert Koestein, broken and worn down by the relentless drudgery." He pushed open the door, and his gaze swept the cell. Empty. The rope, the iron bar – all lay strewn about, a testament to Gerard's cunning escape.

Ghysbrecht's massive frame seemed to freeze, his body stiffening in a manner both unnatural and unsettling. After a moment, he stumbled forward, his eyes fixed upon the chest, now lying open and vacant. His servant, Dierich, crept closer, peering over Ghysbrecht's shoulder, and let out a low exclamation of dismay. But his master's reaction was far more extreme. Ghysbrecht dropped to his knees, his hands fluttering wildly over the chest as if searching for some hidden catch, some secret compartment that might yet reveal the truth.

Dierich watched in confusion, his brow furrowed in concern. "What ails you, Master?" he asked gently.

Ghysbrecht's lips moved, but no words emerged. His hands fell to his sides, and he stared into the chest, his eyes burning with a mixture of desperation and despair. "What's the point of staring at that empty box?" Dierich ventured. "See here! Note the cleverness of the young rascal; he's removed the bar, and—"
