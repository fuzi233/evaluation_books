With less than a minute to spare for reflection, an instinctive dread seized me: I must conceal my experiences from my Wife. It was not that I feared she would betray my secret, but I knew that to any woman in Flatland, the narrative of my adventures would remain utterly unintelligible. Thus, I endeavored to reassure her with a fabricated tale, claiming I had accidentally fallen through the cellar's trapdoor and lain stunned.

The Southward attraction in our land is so slight that even this improbable story struck my Wife as extraordinary. Yet, her good sense far exceeded that of her sex; perceiving my unusual excitement, she did not argue but insisted I was ill and required repose. Glad of an excuse to retire to my chamber, I sought quiet to ponder the impossible. As drowsiness claimed me, I struggled to visualize the Third Dimension, specifically the construction of a Cube from a Square. The image remained hazy, but I recalled the crucial phrase: "Upward, and yet not Northward." Determined to let these words serve as my guiding clue, I repeated them mechanically, like a charm, until I sank into a sound, refreshing sleep.

In my slumber, I dreamed I stood once more beside the Sphere, whose lustrous hue signaled that his wrath had transformed into perfect placability. Together, we drifted toward a bright, infinitesimally small Point, which my Master directed my gaze upon. As we neared, a faint humming emanated from it—a sound akin to a Spaceland bluebottle, yet so subtle that even in the vacuum's stillness, it reached our ears only after we halted some twenty human diagonals away.

"Look yonder," said my Guide. "In Flatland thou hast lived; in Lineland thou hast received a vision; thou hast soared with me to the heights of Spaceland; now, to complete thy range of experience, I conduct thee downward to the lowest depth of existence: the realm of Pointland, the Abyss of No Dimensions."

"Behold this miserable creature," the Sphere continued. "That Point is a Being like ourselves, confined to the non-dimensional Gulf. He is his own world, his own universe; of anything else, he forms no conception. He knows not length, breadth, or height, for he has never experienced them. He has no cognizance of the number two, nor any thought of plurality; for he is his own One and All, being truly Nothing. Yet observe his perfect self-contentment. From this, learn that to be self-satisfied is to be vile and ignorant, and that to aspire is superior to blind, impotent happiness. Now, listen."

Silence fell, broken only by a tiny, low, monotonous tinkling rising from the buzzing creature—like a phonograph recording a single note. From it came the words: "Infinite beatitude of existence! It is; and there is none else beside It."

"What does this puny creature mean by 'It'?" I asked.

"He means himself," the Sphere replied. "Have you not noticed that babies and childish souls, unable to distinguish themselves from the world, speak of themselves in the third person? But hush!"

"It fills all Space," the Point continued, soliloquizing. "And what It fills, It is. What It thinks, It utters; what It utters, It hears. It is the Thinker, the Utterer, the Hearer, the Thought, the Word, the Audition; it is the One, and yet the All in All. Ah, the happiness! Ah, the happiness of Being!"

"Can you not startle this creature from its complacency?" I urged. "Reveal to it the narrow limitations of Pointland as you did to me, and lead it upward."

"That is no easy task," my Master said. "Try yourself."

Raising my voice to its utmost, I addressed the Point: "Silence, contemptible creature! You call yourself the All in All, yet you are the Nothing. Your so-called universe is a mere speck in a Line, and a Line is but a shadow compared to—"

"Hush, hush! You have said enough," the Sphere interrupted. "Listen now, and mark the effect of your harangue on the King of Pointland."

The Monarch's luster beamed brighter than ever upon hearing my words, proving his complacency remained unshaken. Hardly had I ceased when he resumed his strain: "Ah, the joy! Ah, the joy of Thought! What can It not achieve by thinking! Its own Thought returning to It, suggestive of Its disparagement, thereby enhancing Its happiness! Sweet rebellion stirred up to result in triumph! Ah, the divine creative power of the All in One! Ah, the joy, the joy of Being!"

"You see," my Teacher observed, "how little your words accomplished. So far as the Monarch understood them, he accepted them as his own—for he cannot conceive of any entity except himself—and he plumed himself upon the variety of 'Its Thought' as proof of creative power. Let us leave this God of Pointland to the ignorant fruition of his omnipresence and omniscience; nothing you or I can do can rescue him from his self-satisfaction."

As we floated gently back to Flatland, I heard the mild voice of my Companion drawing the moral of my vision, stimulating me to aspire and to teach others to do the same. He had confessed that he was initially angered by my ambition to soar above the Third Dimension, but since then, he had gained fresh insight and was no longer too proud to acknowledge his error to a pupil. He then proceeded to initiate me into mysteries even higher than those I had witnessed, showing me how to construct Extra-Solids from Solids, and Double Extra-Solids from Extra-Solids, all strictly according to analogy. These methods were so simple and easy that they were patent even to the Female Sex.
