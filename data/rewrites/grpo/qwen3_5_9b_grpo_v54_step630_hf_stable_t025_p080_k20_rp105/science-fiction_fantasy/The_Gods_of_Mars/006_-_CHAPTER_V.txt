The darkness of the storeroom had held me for hours, a heavy, suffocating blanket that seemed to stretch endlessly. I awoke not to the gentle light of dawn, but to the jagged, staccato rhythm of gunfire echoing through the subterranean veins of the city. The sound was deafening, a chaotic symphony of violence that shattered the stillness.

Before my mind could fully orient itself to the nightmare unfolding around me, I was already on my feet. Through the haze of confusion, I saw the scene: a dozen lesser therns stood blocking the doorway, their wicked rifles lowered but ready, their faces twisted in a mask of panic and chagrin. Scattered across the floor lay the lifeless forms of my companions, save for Thuvia, Tars Tarkas, and myself, who had found refuge in our slumber.

"Master of the Tenth Cycle!" one of the therns cried out, his voice trembling. "Have mercy! Is Sator Throg to be murdered by his own vassals?"

The men edged backward, desperate to flee the presence of the mighty one they had just failed.

"What means this?" I roared, my voice cutting through the tension like a blade. "What is your mission here?"

"Two fugitives from the outer world are loose within your dominions," the thern stammered, casting a fearful glance toward Tars Tarkas. "We hunted them by the Father of Therns' command. One was white with black hair; the other, a huge green warrior."

Thuvia stepped forward, her voice steady despite the chaos. "Here stands the green warrior. And if you examine the corpse by the door, you will recognize the other. We were tasked with capturing them to secure our liberty for Sator Throg. Instead, your incompetence has resulted in the slaughter of my friends and nearly cost Sator Throg his life."

The therns looked sheepish, their bravado evaporating under the weight of their failure.

"Remove the bodies," I commanded, my voice devoid of emotion. "Throw them to the plant men and return to your quarters."

As the men dragged the corpses away, I noticed a subtle shift in the demeanor of the one carrying Sator Throg's body. As he lifted the dead man, his gaze lingered on the upturned face, then darted furtively toward me. A suspicion, sharp and dangerous, flickered in his eyes—a silent accusation he dared not voice. As he bore the body out, a cunning smile of triumph briefly graced his lips before he vanished into the shadows.

Only Tars Tarkas, Thuvia, and I remained. Our chance for freedom had been snatched away by the fatal marksmanship of our captors.

"The outcome is sealed," Thuvia whispered, her eyes scanning the corridor. "Even if this fool dares not accuse you openly, those above hold the power to demand a closer scrutiny. That, Prince, would be our death sentence."

I shrugged, the weight of despair settling upon my shoulders. My wounds throbbed, a constant reminder of the blood I had lost, and the lack of medicinal aid made every step a trial. I longed for the miraculous salves of the green Martian women, which could have restored me in an hour. Hopelessness, a feeling I had never known, now gripped my heart.

Then, a stray draft caught the long, yellow locks of the Holy Thern, Matai Shang, blowing them across my face. Might they still offer a path? If we acted swiftly, could we escape before the general alarm tore the fortress apart?

"How long before they return?" I asked.

"He will go straight to Matai Shang," Thuvia replied. "As a thorian, he will not wait long for an audience. If the Father of Therns believes his tale, the galleries and courts will be filled with searchers within the hour. We must move now."

"Where is the way out?"

"Up the cliffs, through the gardens, across the temples to the outer court," she said, her voice grim. "But the ramparts are impregnable. Since time immemorial, the therns have added stone to stone, building a fortress where a million warriors stand ready. None can pass undetected."

"Then we face it," I said.

"Night offers a slight advantage," Tars Tarkas suggested. "Fewer guards abroad."

"It is nearly sundown," Thuvia noted. "The sun set two hours ago. Come, let us verify."

She led us through winding passages until we reached an opening overlooking the Valley Dor. Below, the sun dipped beneath the western range of Otz, a massive red orb vanishing into the horizon. On Mars, there is no twilight; the transition from day to night is abrupt, plunging the world from brilliant light into utter darkness as if a single lamp were extinguished.

Beneath us, the Holy Thern stood watch on his balcony, his scarlet robes pulled tight against the coming cold. Below the trees, herds of plant men fed, their talons pruning the leaves, revealing the true nature of their civilization. Further down, a canoe carried a dozen doomed souls from the outer world to the river's edge. As the eyes of the herald on the balcony fell upon them, the shrill, weird wail of the demons echoed through the valley. The plant men poured from the grove, their huge, repulsive shapes covering the victims, their sucking mouths fastening onto the flesh.

"The great white apes will finish the meal," Thuvia observed as the monstrous beasts ran across the valley toward the river. "Darkness falls now."

She led us upward, through the maze of subterranean corridors. Twice, great banths blocked our path, but Thuvia's low command silenced their snarls, causing the beasts to slink away.

"You command these fierce brutes as easily as you breathe," I remarked, smiling at her. "How do you do it?"

"I do not know," she admitted, laughing and shuddering. "Years ago, I angered Sator Throg and was thrown into a pit of banths. Instead of killing me, they fawned at my feet. I know them all by name; they are the scavengers of this underworld."

An idea struck me. "Why not release a number of banths ahead of us to draw attention?"

"It would distract them," she agreed.

She began to sing a low, singsong tune, half-purr, and soon soft, padded footsteps followed us. Great green eyes shone in the shadows, and a sinuous, tawny form crept from a diverging tunnel. One by one, the ferocious creatures answered her call. Soon, we were surrounded by fifty brutes, walking like well-schooled guards, their lathering jowls and hungry eyes fixed on Tars Tarkas and myself. It was a strange procession: naked human feet and padded paws moving in unison, golden walls splashed with jewels, dim radium light, and the massive, maned beasts crowding around us. Leading the way was Thuvia, beautiful and commanding.

We entered a great chamber filled with the grotesque hybrids of the underworld—offspring of prisoners and Martians, deformed, maimed, and mostly sightless. They sprawled on the floor like corpses from Dante's Inferno, a veritable hell of lost souls. The banths sniffed hungrily at the defenseless prey, but we threaded our way through, passing other chambers filled with chained prisoners and beasts.

"Why are there no therns here?" I asked.

"They fear the banths," Thuvia explained. "They seldom traverse the underworld at night. By day, the corridors are alive with guards and slaves, but tonight, it is a hunting ground for the beasts."

We reached the upper galleries safely, emerging at the foot of a steep ascent leading to the inner gardens.

"Above us lies the gateway to the outer ramparts," Thuvia said. "Four miles of danger await us. Guards patrol every inch."

"Why such a massive force for a fortress so hidden by superstition?" I questioned.

"They fear the black pirates of Barsoom," she answered solemnly.

The door swung open, and the scent of growing things filled my nostrils. The banths rushed past us, swarming across the gardens beneath the lurid light of the nearer moon. Suddenly, a cry of alarm erupted from the temple roofs, a warning that raced east and west, sounding like a distant echo.

Tars Tarkas drew his long-sword, and Thuvia shrank back to my side as the fortress prepared for war.
