Sunday's chill had seeped into the bones of the countryside, and Newland Archer felt it in every step as he walked through the park. The crunch of gravel beneath his boots was a monotone accompaniment to the steady beat of his heart. He had borrowed a cutter from the Chiverses, and the drive to Skuytercliff had given him time to collect his thoughts. The Italian villa loomed ahead, its pale green and white façade seeming to absorb the winter sky like a faded watercolor. As he approached, the sound of the wind rustling through the conifers above the lake created a melancholy harmony that matched the mood that had settled over him.

He rang the bell, and the long tinkle echoed through the stillness, a solitary note that seemed to hang in the air. The butler's response was hesitant, as if he had been woken from a dream. Archer identified himself, and the butler's expression changed, relaxing into a mixture of relief and caution. "The Countess Olenska is out, sir," he said, "having attended afternoon service with Mrs. van der Luyden. However, Mr. van der Luyden is in, and I believe he is either taking his nap or reading yesterday's Evening Post."

Archer thanked the butler and decided to meet the ladies. As he walked through the park, the crunch of gravel beneath his boots grew softer, until he reached the high-road. A groom was leading the cutter back to the stables, and Archer followed him. The village of Skuytercliff was just a mile and a half away, but he knew that Mrs. van der Luyden never walked, and that he must keep to the road to meet the carriage. The thought of seeing Madame Olenska again brought a flutter to his chest, and he quickened his pace.

As he turned a bend in the road, he spotted a figure in a red cloak walking towards him. A big dog ran ahead, its tail wagging furiously. Archer hastened forward, and Madame Olenska stopped short, a smile spreading across her face. "Ah, you've come!" she exclaimed, drawing her hand from her muff. The red cloak made her look vibrant, like a burst of color in a winter landscape. He laughed as he took her hand, and she replied, "I came to see what you were running away from."
