As the storm raged outside, casting an eerie glow over the dimly lit chamber, Martin Wittenhaagen stood at the foot of the stairs, his arrow drawn to the head and his knife at the ready. The flickering flames of the candles danced across his weathered features, illuminating the mixture of surprise and wariness etched upon his face. His companions, however, were equally perplexed by the sight of him, their eyes scanning his tense posture as if searching for hidden meaning.

"What's gotten into the old fellow?" one of them asked, his voice tinged with amusement. "Was the old chap preparing to take aim at us?"

Martin's expression smoothed out, and he forced a chuckle, attempting to deflect suspicion. "Good heavens, no! I was simply testing my new string. There, I'll unstring my bow, if you think that."

Dierich's skeptical gaze lingered on Martin, his brow furrowed in concern. "There's more to you than meets the eye, my friend," he said, his tone laced with a hint of warning. "Let's get a fire going and warm ourselves up before we proceed."

As the flames crackled to life, the men huddled around the warmth, their sodden clothes and long hair slowly drying in the heat. It was then that the shrill cries pierced the air, echoing from Margaret's room above. The men sprang to their feet, one of them snatching the candle and racing up the stairs towards the bedrooms.

Martin's reaction was swift, driven by a mix of confusion and foreboding. He attempted to intercept the man, fearing that his curiosity might lead to disaster. But Dierich swiftly intervened, ensnaring Martin in a bear hug and ordering the others to restrain him.

In the ensuing chaos, Martin found himself pinned to the ground, his struggles futile against the combined strength of his companions. As they finally managed to subdue him, binding him with a rope they'd brought for Gerard, Martin let out a pained groan. He knew that the man had managed to slip away, making his way to Margaret's room while Martin was helpless to intervene.

Dierich's triumphant cry echoed through the chamber, his chest heaving with exertion. "Ah, grind your teeth, you old scoundrel!" he exclaimed, his voice dripping with malice. "You won't be causing any more trouble."
