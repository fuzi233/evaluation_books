Elinor's eyes widened in dismay as she took in the sight of Willoughby standing before her, his expression unreadable. Her initial instinct was to flee the room, but she hesitated, her hand hovering over the door handle. It was then that he stepped forward, his voice taking on a tone of quiet urgency.

"For just a little while, Miss Dashwood, please stay," he implored, his words dripping with a mix of desperation and formality. "Just half an hour, or even ten minutes, if you prefer."

Elinor's firm response was immediate. "No, sir. I'm afraid I must decline. You seem to have mistaken the time or the house. Mr. Palmer is not in residence."

Willoughby's reaction was vehement. "Even if they had told me that Mr. Palmer and all his family were away, it wouldn't have deterred me from seeking you out. My business is with you, and only you."

The sudden intensity of his words left Elinor taken aback. "With me?" she repeated, her voice laced with incredulity. "Well, sir, if you must know, be brief and try to compose yourself."

Willoughby nodded, seeming to collect himself. "I'll try," he said, his voice still rough around the edges. "Please, sit down."

Elinor hesitated, weighing her options. The prospect of Colonel Brandon arriving and finding her alone with Willoughby was daunting, but she had promised to hear him out. Her curiosity and sense of duty finally won out, and she took a seat, her movements economical.

For a long, tense moment, neither spoke. Then, with a hint of brusqueness, Willoughby broke the silence. "Your sister is out of danger," he said, relief etched on his face. "I've just heard from the servant. Thank God, she's going to be all right!"

Elinor's response was guarded. "We hope so."

Undeterred, Willoughby stood up and paced across the room. "If only I'd known earlier... But since I'm here now, let's put the past behind us. For once, Miss Dashwood, let's be civil to each other. I'm in a strange mood, one that I don't often experience. Tell me honestly, do you think me more a fool or a knave?"
