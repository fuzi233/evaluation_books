The morning mist swaddled Heston in a damp, gray shroud, as if attempting to stifle the lively hum of industry that pulsed through its narrow streets. The air was heavy with the scent of coal smoke and damp earth, a far cry from the crisp, salty tang of the sea that lingered in Margaret's nostrils as she gazed out at the horizon. Her father's decision to leave the seaside resort of Heston had been met with reluctance, for she had grown accustomed to the soothing rhythms of the waves and the gentle breeze that caressed her skin. Yet, the letters from Mr. Bell and Mr. Thornton had made it clear that their sojourn here was merely a temporary reprieve.

As they prepared to depart, Margaret's thoughts turned to the unknown landscape of Milton-Northern, a place her father had spoken of with a mix of trepidation and hope. She had caught glimpses of the town's sprawling factories and rows of brick houses during their journey, their uniformity a stark contrast to the charming, ramshackle cottages of Heston. The air grew thick with the acrid smell of smoke and industry as they approached the outskirts of Milton, a noxious cloud hanging low over the horizon like a malevolent specter. Her mother's hand tightened around hers as they navigated the straight, unyielding streets, the houses seeming to stretch on forever in a monotonous, brick-and-mortar expanse.

Margaret felt a pang of unease as they passed by the factories, their belching chimneys a constant reminder of the unforgiving nature of the industrial world that lay ahead. Her father's words echoed in her mind – "a new beginning, a chance to rebuild our lives" – yet she couldn't shake the feeling that they were leaving behind the very essence of their being, the comfort and familiarity of Heston's gentle rhythms, for a harsher, more unforgiving reality. The future loomed before them, shrouded in uncertainty, its outlines indistinct, yet unmistakably imposing.
