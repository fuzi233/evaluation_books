The night air clung to the wooden beams of Martin Wittenhaagen's home like a damp shroud, heavy with the scent of rain and woodsmoke. As if this had been a deliberate signal, the back door was struck with a force that shook the very foundations of the cottage. The household was hemmed in. Yet, amidst the chaos, Margaret seemed to regain a semblance of composure. She whispered to Gerard, "Say he was here, but is gone," and with a resolute grip, she dragged him up the creaking stairs to her father's sleeping chamber. Her own quarters lay adjacent, a haven of darkness.

The blows on the door continued, a staccato beat that echoed through the narrow corridors. "Who dares knock at this hour?" Martin's voice rose, a low growl from the depths of his chest. "Open, and you shall see!" The response came from the other side of the door: "Open, Martin Wittenhaagen, or you shall rue it." The speaker's voice was unmistakable – Dirk Brower, a name that conjured memories of sun-drenched days and laughter. "Why, that is Dirk Brower's voice, I trow," Martin muttered, a flicker of unease dancing across his face. "What brings you so far from Tergou?" The voice on the other side of the door grew more insistent: "Open, and you shall know."

With a slow deliberation, Martin drew the bolt, and the door swung open, admitting a draft of cool night air. Dierich and four others flooded into the cottage, their faces illuminated by the faint glow of a distant candle. "Now, Martin, where is Gerard Eliassoen?" Dierich's question cut through the din, his eyes narrowing as he scanned the room. "Gerard Eliassoen? Why, he was here, but now!" Martin's words hung in the air, a fragile thread of deceit.

Dierich's countenance fell, his expression a mask of disappointment. "And where is he now?" The question hung like a challenge, and Martin's response was a carefully crafted web of half-truths. "They say he has gone to Italy. Why, what is to do?" He spoke with a nonchalance that belied the turmoil brewing within him.
