As Deronda settled back into the rhythm of city life, he found himself pondering the enigmatic Mrs. Grandcourt. Their previous encounter in Leubronn had left him with a lasting impression, but it wasn't until their meeting at Diplow that he began to see her in a different light. Gone was the hardness, the self-assurance that had initially intimidated him; in its place, he observed a softer, more vulnerable side to her nature. Her eyes, once bright and unyielding, now seemed to hold a quiet melancholy, a sense of longing that stirred something within him.

Sir Hugo, noticing the change in Deronda's demeanor, couldn't resist a jest. "Be careful not to overstep your bounds, Dan," he cautioned, his voice laced with playful warning. "If you manage to ruffle Grandcourt's feathers, it may cast a shadow over my plans for the coming Christmas gathering at the Abbey."

Deronda smiled wryly, aware that his uncle's words were tinged with a mixture of humor and genuine concern. He knew he was not one to court danger, nor did he intend to stir up trouble. Yet, as he reflected on his past behavior, he realized that his interactions with women often seemed to be misinterpreted by those around him. It was as if his words and actions were seen through a distorted lens, one that amplified his intentions far beyond what he had ever intended.

In his upcoming visit to Mrs. Meyrick's household, Deronda felt a sense of trepidation. He was eager to reconnect with Mirah, whose letters to him had spoken of her happiness and contentment. Her words had touched a chord within him, and he couldn't help but feel drawn to her quiet strength and resilience. As he prepared to meet her again, he steeled himself against the temptation to reveal too much of his own emotions. After all, he had learned that some things were best left unsaid, even in the most innocent of gestures.
