As Deronda stood in the back drawing-room, surrounded by the familiar yet transformed landscape of his past, he couldn't help but feel a sense of disorientation. The soft glow of the afternoon sunlight streaming through the windows cast an ethereal light upon the space where they had shared moments of music and laughter, where Gwendolen's words had first whispered secrets of her heart. The gentle smile of Lady Mallinger's portrait seemed to watch over him, a reminder of the memories etched upon these walls. But Deronda's thoughts were elsewhere, suspended in a world where the melodies of Mirah's voice still lingered, intertwined with the haunting cry of Gwendolen's plea – _Per pietà non dirmi addio_.

The air was heavy with unspoken emotions as he paced the room, the silence punctuated only by the soft ticking of the clock. It was a place he had known intimately for years, yet now felt like a threshold to a bygone era. His life had undergone a metamorphosis, one that had awakened a deep sense of responsibility and compassion within him. The lions on the chimneypiece, their faces urbane and human, seemed to regard him with a knowing gaze, as if they too sensed the weight of his inner turmoil.

Gwendolen entered the room, her presence a poignant reminder of the transformations that had taken place in both their lives. Her mourning dress was a somber testament to the losses she had endured, yet her expression revealed a quiet resolution, a determination that spoke of a soul tempered by fire. As their hands clasped, a shared understanding passed between them, a bond forged in the crucible of their experiences. They sat down, the silence between them a palpable thing, heavy with the weight of their unspoken thoughts.

"I asked you to come because I need your counsel," Gwendolen began, her voice steady yet laced with a hint of vulnerability. "I want you to tell me what I should do. Don't hesitate to speak your mind, even if it seems difficult. I've made up my mind to follow my heart." She paused, her eyes searching his face for reassurance. "I used to fear poverty, the thought of being dependent on others. That's why I married. But I've borne greater hardships since then. I think I could endure poverty, if you believe it's the right path."
