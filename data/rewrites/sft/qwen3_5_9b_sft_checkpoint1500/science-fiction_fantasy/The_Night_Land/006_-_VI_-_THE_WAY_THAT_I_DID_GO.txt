I pressed forward, deliberately keeping my gaze fixed ahead, refusing to glance backward. To look back would invite despair; instead, I needed to summon every ounce of my strength and courage. Before me lay a terrifying unknown, a journey required to reach the distant Maiden in the World's gloom, or to face my inevitable death. Steadfast, I moved, acutely aware of the heavy emotions swelling within me—the wrenching pain of leaving my mighty home, and the profound, gentle wisdom that had governed our curbed existence.

My heart remained warm toward Master Monstruwacan for the honor he bestowed by descending in armor to join the Full Watch, standing beside me to fortify my spirit at this critical juncture. As I walked, the night air swirled around me, a turbulent ether charged with the chaotic thoughts and blind hopes of the millions I had just abandoned. Soon, however, I felt the biting chill of the outside air settle over me. It tasted different on my lungs, sharper on my palate, filling my mouth and throat with a density that the air inside the Thousandth City lacked. Here, near the earth, the atmosphere possessed a physical weight absent from the higher plateaus where humanity dwelt. Not everyone thrived at altitude; some, contrary to nature's usual logic, required the lower levels. In the deep fields, the air was a variable marvel—sometimes charming, sometimes sorrowful—but for those who wandered with reason, it offered a suitability that the rigid rules of the Cities denied.

Thus I continued, a vessel of new thoughts and ancient memories, brimming with fresh wonders yet carrying the shadows of doubt and fear. It was a cunning strangeness to step onto the "outward earth" of the Night Land—a realm where my childhood fantasies had often played, yet where I had never physically tilled soil or breathed the raw wind until now. This fact, perhaps, would seem quaint to those living in the present day.

At last, I approached the Circle encircling the Redoubt. To my astonishment, it was not the massive barrier I had reasoned it should be. Instead, it appeared as a slender, clear tube, barely two inches thick, yet it emitted a brilliant, powerful light that deceived the eye into seeing it as immense. I recalled with a jolt how often, as a boy and a man, I had watched monsters peer over this tiny ring of light. We, the children of the city, kept vigil through the embrasures, hoping to be the first to spot a beast gazing inward at the Mighty Pyramid. They would appear frequently, only to slink back into the night, seemingly repelled by the illumination. We took pride in spotting the ugliest horrors, believing victory belonged to whoever identified the most fearsome brute. Now, standing alone, that childish game brought a half-familiar shudder to my heart, mingled with a secret joy in the safety the light had once promised.

These memories surged forth, making me stumble briefly, unthinking of my position. A sudden imagination seized me—that something terrible might emerge from the Dark beyond. Only after a brief pause did I regain my composure, finding the courage to bid farewell. Turning, I beheld the wondrous Home of the Last Millions. It was an awe-inspiring sight, a mountain of life rising measurelessly into the night, spreading wide enough to seem to hold the entire world in its grip. On its nearer slopes lay three hundred thousand great embrasures; in total, twelve hundred thousand lines of defense circled the four sides. Half a mile upward sat the lowest tier, followed by countless others stretching into the distance. A great light poured from them, allowing me to distinguish individual openings for miles, until they faded into a constant, glimmering fire that shaped a shining peak against the black heavens. That was the Everlasting Monument.

Clustering against these embrasures were the countless millions of the Redoubt. Though visible as insect-like specks against the internal glow, I knew they looked out at me through their spy-glasses. I gazed upward again along the grey metallic slope, following it to the little star crowning the wonder—the Tower of Observation. There, recently, I had spent my life. I knew with certainty that Master Monstruwacan bent the Great Spy-Glass upon me, the lens through which I had so often peered. Raising my Diskos, I saluted and bade farewell, though I saw nothing through the vast expanse.

My heart swelled, yet my soul grew stronger. Then, a murmur rose from the night, dim and distant. I saw the tiny shapes of the people in the lower embrasures moving constantly. Instantly, I understood: the Multitudes were taking my salute, crying out, waving their farewells, or pleading for my return. Alone before this mountain of metal and life, I realized the danger of lingering. Staying longer would expose my plight. I raised the Diskos, reversing it as befitted a message from one young man to all the Millions. I looked swiftly upward through eight great miles of night toward the Final Light, ensuring my friend knew I thought of him in this final moment. Perhaps the invisible millions high in the Upper Cities interpreted this too as a farewell, for a faint, vague wind-like sound drifted down from the monstrous heights.

Lowering the Diskos, I turned away. I breast the Air Clog, stepped across the Circle, and entered the loneliness of the Night Land. I looked behind no more; the sight of my home weakened me, so I resolved to keep my back turned for a long while. Yet, as I walked, the world's aether surged constantly around me, telling me that my people and kin held continual thought of me—in prayer, in wishes, in perpetual watching. This gave me a feeling of companionship, but soon a dread crept in: this disturbance might signal an Evil Force that I was abroad. Who had the power to stop such a broadcast? If I had explained the danger to the multitude, their collective focus alone would have created a fatal ripple in the ether.

Initially, I walked blindly, intent only on putting a good distance between myself and the ache in my heart. But soon, I slowed and applied thought to my path. I reasoned that I must try a new route, for the area trodden by the Youths might be under intense surveillance. I did not head directly north. Instead, I turned northwest, intending to circle around the back of the North-West Watcher, then approach the North of the Plain of Blue Fire. From there, I hoped to find a straight way north, clearing the House of Silence, which terrified me more than any other horror in the land.

This detour lengthened my journey, but survival demanded caution over haste. My confidence in heading north stemmed from an inward knowing and studies within the Pyramid, combined with observations of the world's truths. A few months prior, I had stumbled upon a strange, ancient metal book forgotten in a hidden corner of the Great Library for perhaps a million years. Much of it read like fairy tales, dismissed by the modern age as myths. Yet, I perceived a kernel of ancient truth beneath the shell.

The book recounted a time of monstrous antiquity when the world shattered in a vast earthquake, rending the globe for a thousand miles. A mighty chasm opened, swallowing an ocean, shaking every city, and laying a heavy mist over the earth for days accompanied by torrential rains. While the people of the Pyramid treated this as mere entertainment for students, the book spoke with a strange gravity, quoting witnesses to these events. It described a huge valley emerging from the west, sweeping southeast, then turning north, a thousand miles wide and a hundred miles deep. The sun stood in the western end, casting a red gloom, while the bottom held great seas and awesome, plentiful beasts.

I pieced together the truth: in a bygone eternity, when the world was still light, a great earthquake burst the earth along a weak curve. An ocean fell into the world's furnace, instantly becoming steam, tearing the earth apart in its violent uprising. This created the mist and confusion the book described. Later, as the sun grew old and the upward earth cooled, the Mighty Chasm calmed, forming a deep valley holding seas, hills, mountains, forests, and lands of health mixed with poisonous fires and sulphur clouds. Down in that depth, unseen save by powerful lenses, lived Great Beasts bred anew by Nature's inward forces in the warmth of the valley.

The book revealed that humanity had softened over ages of ease, only to find the world growing cold and hostile due to the sun's decline. A hardy race emerged, fighting for survival. They perceived the Mighty Valley as a place of warmth and life, venturing down the wondrous heights. Generations climbed and worked the Road, the sole intent of their people, unaware of the depths below until finally arriving. They fought monsters, built cities, and constructed roads connecting them, even navigating the shadowed corners where the sun failed to shine. Yet, the horror of the Valley was too great. Driven back by foul powers, they retreated to the cities bathed in the red light of the western sun.

For a hundred thousand years, they lived wisely, experimenting with harmful forces in ignorance, seeking knowledge. But they opened a gateway for these evils, bringing harm and pity that came too late to prevent. Centuries later, as twilight descended on the world, strange wildness took hold of the peoples. Cities were attacked by monsters from the west, sparking pandemonium. An age of sorrows and hardening followed, breeding a determined generation. A leader arose, gathering the sound millions to battle all foulness. They drove enemies down and up the valley, scattering them.
