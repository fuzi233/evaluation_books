It was in vain. I had abandoned the rhythmic, meditative grinding of the ancestral root-spices—a domestic routine that had grounded my lineage for generations—and brought my hardest right angle into violent collision with the Stranger. I pressed upon him with a force sufficient to crack the sturdy rind of a winter gourd, fully expecting the satisfying, tactile resistance of a physical body. But I could feel him slowly, unarrestably slipping from my contact. There was no edging to the right nor to the left; rather, he seemed to dissolve out of the world's very pan, evaporating like the fragrant steam of a clarified broth, until he vanished into nothing.

Suddenly, my carefully cultivated acoustic ecology was shattered by a deafening, sterile blankness. The comforting sizzle of the hearth, the subtle, percussive chopping from the neighboring kitchens—all were swallowed by this unnatural void. Yet, through the oppressive, industrial dissonance, the Intruder’s voice boomed, trampling over the delicate phonetic nuances of our native dialect.

"Why will you refuse to listen to reason?" the Sphere resonated, his tone devoid of the rich, sensory timbre of shared experience. "I had hoped to find in you—as a steward of your people's culinary history and an accomplished curator of their tastes—a fit apostle for the Gospel of Three Dimensions. This mythic cycle brings me here but once in a thousand years to seed this truth, but your stubborn adherence to the surface prevents you from grasping my lexicon. Stay, I have it. Deeds, not your dying words, shall proclaim the truth. Listen, my friend. I have told you I can see from my position in Space the inside of all things that you consider closed. For example, I see in yonder curing-cupboard near which you are standing, several of those hollow vessels full of your fermented currency, and beside them, the two slate tablets detailing your family's oldest seasonal recipes. I am about to descend into that cupboard and bring you one of those tablets. I watched you lock its heavy door half an hour ago as you set the yeast to proof, and I know you possess the key. But I descend from Space; the doors, you see, remain unmoved. Now I am in the cupboard, smelling the sharp tang of the aged preserves. Now I am taking the tablet. Now I ascend with it."

I rushed to the larder, the discordant echo of his voice still ringing in the acoustic chamber of my home, and dashed the door open. One of the ancestral recipe tablets was gone—a fragile piece of our cultural memory erased in a single, inexplicable heartbeat. 

With a mocking laugh that grated like a rusted iron blade on a ceramic whetstone, the Stranger materialized in the opposite corner of the prep-room. Simultaneously, the missing tablet clattered onto the floor tiles, violently disrupting the kitchen's quiet hum. I took it up. There could be no doubt—it was the missing inheritance, redolent with the familiar scent of pressed thyme and dried oil. I groaned with a deep, existential horror, doubting my own sensory faculties, mourning the ease with which our material and linguistic reality could be bypassed.

The Stranger continued to impose his dominant syntax: "Surely you must now see that my explanation, and no other, suits the phenomena. What you call Solid sustenance is really superficial; what you call Space is really nothing but a great Plane. I am in Space, and look down upon the insides of the things of which you only see the outsides. You could leave this Plane yourself, if you could but summon up the necessary volition to abandon your limited palate. A slight upward or downward motion would enable you to see the entirety of the feast.

"The higher I mount, and the further I go from your Plane, the more I can see, though the sensory details naturally grow faint. For example, I am ascending; now I can see your neighbour the Hexagon and his family in their several dining apartments, consuming their evening roast; now I see the inside of the Theatre, ten doors off, where the last resonant echoes of the choir are fading; and on the other side, a Circle in his study, sitting among his dusty tomes. Now I shall come back to you. And, as a crowning proof, what do you say to my giving you a touch, just the least touch, in your stomach? It will not seriously injure you, and the slight pain you may suffer cannot be compared with the mental expansion your vocabulary will receive."

Before I could utter a single word of native remonstrance to defend the sanctity of my own body, I felt a shooting pain deep within my digestive tract. It was as if a drop of pure, corrosive lye had bypassed my tongue and throat entirely, blooming into a discordant, agonizing bass note directly in my gut. A demoniacal laugh seemed to issue from within my own internal acoustics, a horrifying ventriloquism of my physical self. A moment later, the sharp agony ceased, leaving behind only the dull ache of a spoiled meal, and the Stranger began to reappear, gradually increasing in size.

"There, I have not hurt you much, have I?" he said, his industrial hum vibrating against the pantry walls. "If you are not convinced now, I don't know what will convince you. What say you?"

My resolution was taken. It seemed intolerable that I should endure an existence subject to the arbitrary, mythic visitations of a Magician who could thus play cheap parlor tricks with one's very stomach, entirely bypassing the sacred, gustatory rituals of ingestion and physical sustenance. The sheer untranslatability of his intrusion was an affront to everything I held dear. If only I could somehow manage to pin him against the hearth wall until help came!

Once more I dashed my hardest angle against him, disrupting the quiet evening with my cries for aid. I believe, at the moment of my onset, the Stranger had sunk below our Plane, struggling against the dense, olfactory gravity of our world. He remained motionless. Hearing the rhythmic, familiar vibrations of help approaching along the floorboards, I pressed against him with redoubled vigour, desperate to preserve our way of life from this dominant, alien lexicon.

A convulsive shudder ran through the Sphere.

"This must not be," I thought I heard him mutter, his voice losing its confident resonance. "Either he must listen to reason, or I must have recourse to the last resource of a superior civilization."

Then, projecting his voice at a frequency that rattled the hanging pots, he hurriedly exclaimed, "Listen: no stranger must witness what you have witnessed. Send your Wife back at once, before she enters the kitchen. The Gospel of Three Dimensions must not be thus frustrated by the mundane routines of your domestic life. Not thus must the fruits of one thousand years of waiting be thrown away. I hear the acoustic timbre of her approach. Back! back! Away from me, or you must go with me—whither you know not—into the Land of Three Dimensions!"

"Fool! Madman! Irregular!" I exclaimed, clinging fiercely to the language of my ancestors. "Never will I release thee; thou shalt pay the penalty for trampling upon the sanctity of our hearth!"

"Ha! Is it come to this?" thundered the Stranger, his voice becoming an overwhelming, oppressive roar of industrial noise that drowned out all native sound. "Then meet your fate: out of your Plane you go. Once, twice, thrice! 'Tis done!"
