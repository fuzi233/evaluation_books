As the sun began to set over the rolling hills of Hampshire, casting a warm orange glow over the landscape, Lieutenant Sutch drove his pony along the winding road, the scent of heather and gorse filling the air. He was lost in thought, his mind preoccupied with the disappearance of Harry Feversham, a concern that had been weighing heavily on his heart. Like many a man who lived much alone, Sutch often found himself talking aloud, and as he rode, he muttered to himself, "I knew there would be trouble. From the start, I foresaw trouble."

The road dipped down into a valley, and Sutch caught sight of the railway station below, its red-tiled roof glowing warmly in the fading light. The sound of a distant engine whistle pierced the air, and Sutch's pony slowed to a stop as he gazed out at the approaching train. As the white smoke billowed towards him, he wondered what had brought Durrance to this remote corner of the countryside. He flicked the pony with his whip, and they began to move forward, the sound of the wheels on the gravel road the only noise breaking the silence.

As the train pulled into the station, Sutch watched as Durrance and his servant stepped out onto the platform. He hailed Durrance, who made his way towards the pony trap, his footsteps light and sure despite his blindness. Sutch's tone was cordial, but his words were laced with a hint of wariness, a feeling that Durrance was aware of. The lieutenant had been expecting this visit, and he knew that Durrance had come to ask questions about Harry Feversham, questions that Sutch was determined to avoid.

As Durrance reached for the step of the trap, Sutch caught him by the arm, helping him up into the vehicle. He felt a pang of sympathy for the blind man, and his thoughts turned to Ethne Eustace, who had also been worried about Durrance's situation. As they began to drive up the hill, Sutch spoke softly, trying to gauge Durrance's mood. "I was sorry to hear about your accident, Durrance. I know how it feels to be set back when you're making progress – it's a hard blow to take."
