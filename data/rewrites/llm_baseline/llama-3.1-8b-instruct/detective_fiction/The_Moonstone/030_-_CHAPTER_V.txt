As I stood hidden behind the curtain, my fingers instinctively released their grip, but my thoughts were consumed by a far greater concern than the awkwardness of my situation. My sisterly affection for Mr. Godfrey was still simmering within me, and the absence of his presence at the concert was the last thing on my mind. No, my thoughts swirled around the ominous words that had tumbled from his lips – the words that had sent shivers down my spine. He would do it today, he had said with an air of resolute conviction. But what would he do? Was it something that would tarnish his good name even further? Would he abandon our beloved charity, the Mothers' Small Clothes, or relinquish his remarkable oratory skills at Exeter Hall? The very thought of such calamities unfolding was too distressing to bear, and I felt an overwhelming urge to rush out from my hiding spot and beg him to explain himself. But just as I was about to act on this impulse, another voice pierced the air, a voice that was loud, bold, and lacking in feminine charm – the voice of Rachel Verinder.

"Why have you come up here, Godfrey?" she asked, her tone straightforward and unadorned. "Why didn't you join the others in the library?"

Mr. Godfrey chuckled softly and replied, "Miss Clack is in the library." Rachel quickly settled onto the ottoman in the back drawing room, her eyes fixed intently on Godfrey. "You're right, Godfrey. Let's stay here."

In that instant, I felt a sudden chill run down my spine, and my doubts vanished. To reveal myself after what I'd overheard would be impossible; retreating, save into the fireplace, was equally unthinkable. A trial awaited me, and I steeled myself to face it with the resolve of a primitive martyr. I discreetly adjusted the curtains, ensuring I could observe and listen without being seen. And then I steeled myself to witness the ordeal ahead.

"Don't sit on the ottoman," Rachel instructed Godfrey. "Bring a chair, Godfrey. I prefer to speak to people facing me." He rose to fetch a nearby chair, which, given his tall stature, made him appear comically out of proportion. As he sat, I noticed his long legs stretched out before him, a sight I'd never seen before.
