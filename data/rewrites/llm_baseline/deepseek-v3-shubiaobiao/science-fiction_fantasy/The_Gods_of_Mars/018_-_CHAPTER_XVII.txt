The morning sun cast long shadows through the arched corridors of Helium as a detachment of Zodangan guards arrived at our quarters. Their armor gleamed coldly in the pale light, their faces set in grim resolve. They were not escorts—they were jailers.

We marched in pairs down the Aisle of Hope, flanked by rows of Zodangan soldiers, their spears a bristling wall of hostility. The great hall of the Temple of Reward loomed before us, its vaulted ceiling echoing with the murmurs of thousands. The air was thick with tension, with the unspoken understanding that justice here would be anything but blind.

The judges awaited us—thirty-one nobles, their expressions unreadable. Not a single friendly face among them. Zodangans, all. Men whose city had fallen beneath the swords of Tharks, whose pride had been shattered by the very man now standing trial. John Carter, Prince of Helium, was not walking into a court. He was walking into vengeance.

The crowd fell silent as we took our places upon the Throne of Righteousness. Zat Arras, his golden circlet gleaming, rose with the smug assurance of a man who already knew the verdict.

"John Carter," he intoned, voice dripping with false solemnity, "stand upon the Pedestal of Truth and hear the crimes for which you must answer."

He turned to the assembly, weaving a tapestry of heresy. "He has returned from the Valley Dor—from the very Temple of Issus! He has spat upon our sacred traditions, mocked the Holy Therns, and defied the Goddess of Death herself. By the ancient law, he who dies must remain dead. To defy this is to invite eternal silence."

A judge’s voice cut through the stillness like a blade. *"Death."*

Then—chaos.

Kantos Kan surged forward, his voice a thunderclap. *"Justice!"* He vaulted past the guards, defiance in every line of his body. "Will you condemn a man without defense? Without witnesses? Is this the law of Helium—or the whim of Zodanga?"

The crowd erupted. *"Justice! Justice!"* The cry became a roar, shaking the very pillars of the temple.

Zat Arras, his face a mask of fury, had no choice. He gestured sharply. "Speak, then. But remember—the sacred things of Barsoom are not to be blasphemed."

I turned not to the judges, but to the people.

"Men of Helium," I called, "do not ask me to seek fairness from those who crave my blood. I speak not for myself, but for *you*. For your wives, your daughters—for all who might one day be dragged into the horrors of Issus’ domain. I have walked the Valley Dor. I have seen the lies behind its divinity. The Therns are not holy—they are butchers. The Temple is not salvation—it is slaughter."

The silence was absolute. Even the judges leaned forward, caught despite themselves.

"You know me," I continued. "You know I do not lie. Would John Carter, who bled for Helium, who fought for her freedom, now spin tales to deceive her people? I ask only this—let me save you, as I once saved you from the sword. Let me tear the veil from your eyes before more are lost to the darkness of Dor."

A noble stood, his voice ringing. *"Speak, Prince of Helium!"* The crowd took up the cry, their will a force even Zat Arras could not deny.

For two hours, I laid bare the truth. The Therns’ cruelty. The false paradise. The fate that awaited all who journeyed south. And when I finished—

Zat Arras rose, his face twisted. "You have heard his blasphemy. The verdict is clear."

*"Death!"* The judges’ swords flashed upward in unison.

But the people of Helium had other ideas.

Kantos Kan tore his scabbard free and flung his blade at my feet. "There is *my* judgment."

Like a tide, they came—soldiers, nobles, citizens—pushing past Zodangan spears, their own swords clattering before me. A hundred. Then more. The hall trembled with the weight of rebellion.

Zat Arras screamed orders, but his voice was lost. The crowd’s fury was a living thing, hungry and barely leashed.

I raised a hand. *"Enough."* The word carried, and against all odds, they listened. "One drop of blood spilled here will drown Helium in war. I will not be its cause." I turned to Zat Arras, my voice low. "Take your victory. But mark this—if you value your life, declare a stay. A year. No more."

His jaw worked, but he was no fool. He knew the mob would tear him apart if he refused.

"By my mercy," he spat, "the sentence is delayed! One year—or until Tardos Mors returns. Now *go*."

No one moved.

It was *my* nod that finally parted the crowd. They left, but their anger lingered like smoke in the air.

Kantos Kan gripped my shoulder. "To your palace, my Prince. Let Zodanga choke on its triumph."

We marched through the streets, a procession of defiance. Blades rained at my feet, a silent oath from the people of Helium.

And then—a cry from the gates. A messenger. A thoat charging through the crowd.

Sola.

Her face was streaked with dust and desperation. She stumbled from her mount, her voice breaking.

"Dejah Thoris—they took her! The black pirates of Barsoom have stolen your princess!"

The world went silent.

Gone.

Not dead.
