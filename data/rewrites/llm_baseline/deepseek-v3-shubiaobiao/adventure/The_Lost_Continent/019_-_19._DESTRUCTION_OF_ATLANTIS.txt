The old priest approached, his robes whispering against the stone. His voice was thin but urgent. "The Three have sent word, my King. They know of what comes."

I tightened my grip on my sword. "Then they know I stand here to meet it. When the fight is done, I’ll greet the Gods beyond the stars—and welcome them when they follow."

He shook his head. "They forbid it. You are not to die here. They have one last use for you."

I turned, fury rising. "I take no such order. If I am king, if I am a priest, if I am a soldier of Atlantis, then I hold this gate until my last breath. Go back and tell them that."

A faint smile touched his lips—a soldier’s envy. "I said you would refuse. So they gave me this." He held out a jewel, its facets catching the dim light.

My breath caught. "Nais wears that."

"Then you understand. She waits inside the Ark of Mysteries. The Three command you to join her."

For a heartbeat, the battle-cry of the coming storm sang in my blood. But Nais’s face rose before me, and I bowed my head. "I obey."

To the priests who remained, I gave my farewell. "Fight well, brothers. I envy you the glory. But the Three speak with the voice of the Gods, and I am bound to it."

They saluted, blades flashing, and I left them to their final stand.

---

The mountain shuddered beneath my feet as I climbed. The gates below would not hold much longer. The sea roared, devouring the land, swallowing the city whole. The skies burned.

Zaemon met me at the sacred boundary, his face carved from stone. "You see it," he said, gesturing to the drowning world below.

"I see."

"The Gods’ patience is spent. Atlantis falls. Only the Ark endures."

"Then let me join Nais and be done with it."

"There is a rite," he said, and though the ground trembled and the enemy howled at our heels, the old priest would not be rushed. The words of the highest initiation rolled from his tongue, binding me to the mysteries, to the last duty of the priesthood.

When it was done, he gripped my arm. "Go now. The Ark waits. You and Nais alone are chosen to carry what remains of us beyond this ruin."

---

I climbed into the Ark’s belly, sealing the door behind me. Nais stood in the dimness, her face pale but steady.

"They would not come?" she whispered.

"No."

She swallowed, then lifted her chin. "Then we will remember for them."

Outside, the world tore itself apart. The mountain groaned. The seas rose like ravenous beasts. Through the Ark’s thick hull, we heard the screams of the drowning, the final cries of a dying empire.

Then the waves took us.

The Ark lurched, spun, and tore free of the earth. We were weightless, adrift in the blackness, the last of Atlantis.

Nais pressed close. I held her as the ocean roared its victory.

And then—silence.

Only the dark. Only the two of us.

And the future.
