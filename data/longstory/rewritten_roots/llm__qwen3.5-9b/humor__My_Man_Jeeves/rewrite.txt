In my early days of knowing him, Bobbie Cardew was arguably the most notorious "rotter" within a four-mile radius. People have called me a simpleton, but I was never in the same league as Bobbie. Yet, she insists on being a hopeless invalid, forcing the doctor to agree. She holds a fixed belief that New York would kill her, despite it being her lifelong ambition to visit."

"Reminiscent of the chap whose heart was 'in the Highlands,' Jeeves?"

"The parallels are striking, sir."

"Therefore, since I cannot experience the city's marvels myself, I shall enjoy them vicariously through you. Yesterday, reading a poignant poem about a man who achieved his desire only when too frail to enjoy it, I was deeply moved. As you will receive my inheritance upon my passing, I have decided to provide you with an allowance now. I have instructed New York lawyers to remit a substantial monthly sum. My sole condition: reside in New York and indulge yourself as I wish. Be my representative. Plunge into the vibrant life. Attend brilliant suppers. Above all—and I insist—you write me letters at least weekly detailing your activities, so I may enjoy the city at second hand. Expect full details; no triviality is too insignificant. Your affectionate Aunt, ISABEL ROCKMETTELLER."

"What about it?" Rocky asked.

"What about it?" I echoed.

"Yes. What on earth shall I do?"

Here, I truly grasped the absurdity of his situation. An unexpected windfall had descended from a clear blue sky, an occasion worthy of jubilation. Instead, the man sat there as if Fate had struck his solar plexus.

"Aren't you excited?" I asked.

"Bucked?"

"If I were in your shoes, I'd be thrilled. This is incredibly generous."

He emitted a yelp, stared at me, and then proceeded to describe New York in terms reminiscent of Jimmy Mundy, the reformer. Jimmy had recently toured the city, expressing disdain for its inhabitants, yet Rocky made him sound like a travel agent for the metropolis.

"Precious soft!" he cried. "To live in New York! To abandon my cottage for a stifling, smoky apartment in this heaven-forsaken Gehenna! To mingle nightly with a mob convinced life is a St. Vitus's dance, drunk on noise and gin! I loathe New York, Bertie. I wouldn't approach it unless forced to see editors. It's blighted with moral delirium tremens. The mere thought of staying more than a day makes me ill. And you call this generous?"

I felt akin to Lot's friends when their host began criticizing Sodom. I was unprepared for Rocky's eloquence.

"It would kill me," he continued. "To breathe the air of six million souls! To wear stiff collars daily! To—" He paused. "Good heavens! I suppose I must dress for dinner? What a ghastly prospect!"

I was horrified.

"My dear chap!" I reproached.

"Do you dress for dinner every night, Bertie?"

"Jeeves," I said coolly. The man stood motionless by the door. "How many evening suits do I possess?"

"We have three full sets, sir; two dinner jackets—"

"Three."

"For practical use, two, sir. We also have seven white waistcoats."

"And shirts?"

"Four dozen, sir."

"And white ties?"

"The first two shelves of the drawer are entirely filled, sir."

Turning to Rocky, I gestured.

"You see?"

The young man writhed like an electric fan.

"I won't do it! I can't! I'll be hanged if I do! How can I dress like that? Most days I don't leave my pajamas until five, then throw on a sweater!"

Poor Jeevis winced; such revelations wounded his sensibilities.

"So, what is your plan?" I asked.

"That is precisely what I wish to know."

"You could explain to your aunt."

"I might—if I desired her lawyer to disinherit me instantly."

Seeing his logic, I turned to Jeeves.

"Jeeves?"

Jeeves cleared his throat respectfully.

"The crux, sir, is that Mr. Todd is obligated to submit detailed reports of his movements. Since Mr. Todd intends to remain in the countryside, the only solution is to engage a second party to gather the required experiences and compile a report, which Mr. Todd can then expand into correspondence using his imagination."

Silence followed. Rocky looked bewildered.

"Can you clarify, Bertie?" he asked. "It flickered in meaning. What is the idea?"

"My dear old man, it is simple. Get someone to tour the city, take notes, and you draft the letters. That is it, Jeeves?"
