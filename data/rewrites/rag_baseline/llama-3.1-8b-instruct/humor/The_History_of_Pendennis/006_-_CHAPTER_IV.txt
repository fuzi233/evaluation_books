As Mr. Foker and his esteemed guest sat down to a sumptuous repast in the coffee-room of the George, the air was alive with the subtle nuances of refined conversation. The atmosphere was thick with the weight of established relationships, where familiarity bred a certain comfort and informality. Mr. Foker, known for his discerning palate, launched into an impassioned defense of the sherry, proclaiming it to be a far superior vintage to its more pedestrian counterparts. His companion, Pen, listened intently, his eyes sparkling with amusement as Foker proceeded to dissect the merits of each libation with the precision of a connoisseur.

Their discussion was punctuated by the arrival of the London coaches, which, in those early days, would depart from the George with a fanfare of trumpets and a burst of excitement. Foker, ever the social butterfly, flung open the coffee-room window, greeting the guards and coachmen by their Christian names, inquiring after their families, and even mimicking the tooting of the horns with impressive verisimilitude. The ostler, Jem, chuckled good-naturedly as he whipped off the horses' cloths, and the carriages drove off with a flourish, leaving behind a trail of dust and anticipation.

As they prepared to take their leave, Foker turned to Pen and exclaimed, "A bottle of sherry, a bottle of sham, a bottle of port and a shass caffy – it ain't so bad, hay?" The two men rose from their seats, their faces flushed with the warmth of good fellowship, and made their way towards the theatre, where they paid their respects to Mrs. Dropsicum, Bingley's mother-in-law, a grande dame of the theatrical world, renowned for her portrayal of Lady Macbeth. Foker, ever the gentleman, tipped his hat in deference, and the two men slipped into their seats just as the play was about to begin.

The theatre, though not yet at full capacity, was abuzz with the gentle hum of conversation and the occasional burst of laughter. The performers, a talented troupe of actors, took to the stage with ease, their lines delivered with conviction and flair.
