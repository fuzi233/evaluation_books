A roar erupted overhead, shaking the canopy above our heads. Moments later, the Little Ones began to descend from the foliage, their faces alight with the urgency of discovery. They reported that scaling a sentinel tree, towering above the rest, they had spotted a singular anomaly on the flank of a distant mountain—a peak that seemed to defy gravity, rising endlessly until the sky itself appeared to buckle under its weight, snapping its summit in half.

"It may be a city," they declared, "but it bears no resemblance to Bulika."

I ascended to verify their claim. What met my gaze was a metropolis ascending into the azure stratosphere, a place where the distinction between geology and habitation dissolved. Mountain and sky, cloud and dwelling, merged into a chaotic tapestry of fractured shadow and blinding brilliance. Palace spires and precipitous cliffs intertwined in a dizzying dance of light and dark. Descending, I joined the Little Ones, who surged ahead with renewed vigor, leading the way without a backward glance. As we moved, the river transformed before our eyes, becoming increasingly exquisite until I realized I had never witnessed water of such purity. Nothing in this mortal realm could truly compare to it.

From the plain, the city remained visible amidst the high clouds, but attention shifted to a colossal tower—or perhaps a monolithic rock—piercing the skyline above the urban sprawl. Near the mountain's crest, the atmosphere churned. Clouds of gray, charcoal, and violet writhed in contradictory currents, tossing up a vaporous foam while swirling spots mimicked the motion of whirlpools. Suddenly, a blinding flash detonated, momentarily illuminating the Little Ones dancing before us. Darkness swallowed the scene instantly, yet their voices cut through the gloom, low with ecstatic wonder.

"Did you see?"
"I saw!"
"What did you see?"
"The beautifullest man!"
"I heard him speak!"
"I didn't: what did he say?"

The smallest voice, belonging to Luva, answered clearly. "He said, 'All mine's, 'ickle ones: come along!'"

I had witnessed the lightning but heard no words; Lona, however, perceived both sight and sound alongside the children. A second flash struck, and though my ears remained deafened, my vision cleared. The great quivering light coalesced into a mosaic of angelic visages. They manifested briefly, then vanished. A third eruption followed, this time composed entirely of humanity.

"I see my mother!" I cried out.
"I see lots o' mothers!" Luva exclaimed.

Once more the cloud flashed, revealing a menagerie of impossible creatures: horses and elephants, lions and dogs—beasts of such majesty—and birds of breathtaking variety. Great avians displayed wings shimmering with every hue of the sunset and rainbow; tiny birds whose feathers sparkled as if dusted with the earth's most precious gemstones. Silvery cranes, red flamingos, opal pigeons, peacocks resplendent in gold, green, and blue, and jewel-toned hummingbirds filled the air. Great-winged butterflies and lithe, voluminous reptiles completed the spectacle.

"I see that serpents grow birds here, just as caterpillars once became butterflies!" Lona observed.
"I saw my white pony, the one that died when I was a child," she continued. "I needn't have mourned so bitterly; I should simply have waited!"

There had been no thunder, no rolling crash. Instead, a gentle rain began to fall, infusing the atmosphere with a caressing coolness. We breathed deeply, our strides strengthening. The falling droplets refracted the colors of awakened terrestrial gems, and a magnificent rainbow arched over the city. The blue clouds thickened; the rain poured in torrents, yet the children exulted, running wild as we struggled to keep them in view.

With a silent, radiant surge, the river swept onward, filling its smooth, yielding channel to the brim. Unlike any earthly stream, it flowed not over rock, shingle, or sand, but across lush grass carpeted with a starry multitude of flowers: primroses, daisies, crocuses, narcissi, pimpernels, and anemones, all large and vibrant beneath the brilliant water. The river bore no turbidity from the storm, not even a hint of yellow or brown; the delicate mass shone with a pale, berylline luminescence rising from its deep, dainty bed.

Drawing closer to the mountain, we saw the source: the river emerged directly from the peak, rushing in full volume through the city's main thoroughfare. It cascaded down to the gate via a staircase of deep, wide steps forged from porphyry and serpentine, extending to the mountain's foot. Upon arrival, we found shallower steps flanking both banks, leading upward toward the gate and along the ascending street.

Without hesitation, the Little Ones sprinted straight up the stairs to the gate, which stood wide open. Standing on the landing outside was the portress, a woman-angel of dark visage, leaning her shadowed brow against her idle hand. The children rushed her, burying her in affectionate embraces. Before she could comprehend their presence, they had seized heaven by surprise and were already within the city, continuing their ascent beside the descending torrent.

A great angel, accompanied by a host of shining beings, descended to intercept them. Yet, the children danced merrily, evading capture. Soon, a group of female angels descended upon them in joyful pursuit, and in an instant, they were embraced in heavenly arms. The radiant hosts carried them away, and I lost sight of them forever.

"Aha!" proclaimed the mighty angel as he continued his descent to meet us, who were now nearing the gate and within earshot of his voice. "This is excellent! These are soldiers ready to storm heaven itself! I hear reports of a horde of black bats on the frontiers; these will dispatch them swiftly!"

Noticing the horses and elephants struggling up behind us, he added, "Escort those animals to the royal stables. Tend to them, then release them into the King's forest."

Turning to us, he bowed with the sweetest smile. "Welcome home!"

Immediately, he turned and led the way higher. The scales of his armor flashed like fragments of lightning. No thought can adequately describe the sensation of being thus received by the officers of heaven. Whatever I desired, whatever I did not yet know, was surely en route to me.

We paused for a moment at the gate, from which the radiant river roared forth. I could not identify the origin of the stones forming the archway, but among them I recognized the prototypes of every gem I had cherished on Earth—far more beautiful than their earthly counterparts, for these were living stones. Within them, I perceived not merely the concept, but the creator; not just the idea, but the active embodiment, the operative sender. Nothing in this kingdom was dead; nothing existed merely as an object. Everything possessed agency.

We proceeded upward through the city and exited its bounds. There was no wall guarding the upper side, only a massive pile of shattered rocks sloping upward like the moraine of an eternal glacier. Through gaps between the stones, the river billowed outward. Perched atop these rocks, I dimly discerned what appeared to be three or four great steps of a staircase, vanishing into a cloud white as snow. Above the steps, using only my mind's eye, I visualized a grand, ancient chair—the Throne of the Ancient of Days. From over, under, and between those steps issued the River of Life, flowing ceaselessly and abundantly.

The great angel could guide us no further; those remaining steps we must ascend alone. My heart pounding with hope and longing, I gripped Lona's hand tighter, and we began to climb. Soon, however, we released each other's hands, utilizing our limbs fully for the arduous ascent of the colossal stones.

Eventually, we drew near the cloud hanging down the steps like the hem of a garment. Passing through its fringe, we entered its deep folds. A hand, warm and strong, grasped mine and pulled me to a small door secured with a golden lock. The door opened; the hand released mine and gently pushed me through. I turned quickly, seeing the board of a large book close behind me. I stood alone in my library.
