Mirah's footsteps echoed through the quiet streets as she turned out of Knightsbridge, the memory of her recent concert still lingering in her mind. The simple black dress and dust cloak she wore didn't draw unwanted attention, but her thoughts were consumed by a different kind of unease. As she walked, the sound of footsteps kept pace with hers, and her imagination ran wild with the possibility that the person following her might be the one she least wanted to see.

Her mind flashed back to her father, and the mere thought sent a shiver down her spine. She couldn't bring herself to turn and face him, fearing the torrent of emotions that would flood her upon recognizing him. The idea of having to acknowledge him, of having to confront the past, was almost too much to bear. Yet, a sense of duty nagged at her, urging her to prepare for the inevitable encounter.

As she approached the entrance of the small square where her home lay, Mirah steeled herself for the confrontation. But just as she was about to make her decision, a gentle grasp on her wrist stayed her hand, and a low, persuasive voice whispered, "Mirah!" She froze, her heart pounding in her chest, as the expected voice and eyes locked onto hers. Her own face remained impassive, like a mask of stone, while his was a picture of soothing calm, a desperate attempt to mitigate the hurt that lay beneath.

His face, once handsome and full of life, now bore the lines of time and hardship. His bright color had faded, replaced by a sallow pallor that spoke of years of struggle. Yet, there was something about him that still exuded a sense of youth, a quality that belied the truth of his advanced age – a mere fifty-seven winters. His worn clothes seemed to reflect the state of his soul, and Mirah's heart ached with a mix of emotions as she gazed upon him. Shame and grief wrestled with repulsion and pity, a painful reminder of the world she had built for herself, where there was no room for him, no space for the love and companionship they once shared.
