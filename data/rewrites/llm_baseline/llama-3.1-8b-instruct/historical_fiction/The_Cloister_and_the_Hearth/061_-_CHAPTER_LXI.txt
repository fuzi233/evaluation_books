As the sun cast its golden rays through the windows of the palace, Princess Claelia summoned Gerard to her chambers, requesting a portrait of herself. Gerard, eager to oblige, suggested employing his friend Pietro Vanucci, a renowned artist, to capture her likeness. However, the princess declined, stating that she would wait until she was entirely satisfied with the outcome.

Gerard, confident in his own draughtsmanship, yet aware of his limitations in color, proposed an alternative. He asked the princess to pose for him as a Roman statue, showcasing his ability to render marble-like textures on paper. At first, she consented, but soon grew hesitant when Gerard explained that she would need to don the traditional attire of the ancients – a tunic, toga, and sandals.

"Why must I dress like a relic from another era?" Claelia asked with a hint of amusement.

Gerardo, undeterred, emphasized the elegance and nobility of the ancient garb, which would complement her classical features and statuesque physique. Claelia agreed to consider his proposal, requesting that he return on the next Saturday to finalize their plan.

In the interim, Gerard devoted his time to crafting the traditional garments, carefully selecting fabrics and patterns that would do justice to the princess's refined beauty. As he worked, he pondered the intricacies of art and the fleeting nature of time, lost in thought.

While strolling through the winding streets of Rome, Gerard chanced upon a small shop, its window display catching his attention. Amidst the assortment of books and scrolls, one caught his eye – a copy of Lactantius, its pages open and inviting. Gerard's excitement turned to dismay as he realized that the text was not handwritten, but printed. His thoughts racing, he recalled the rumors of the printing press spreading across Europe, and the implications it held for artisans like himself.

The shopkeeper, noticing Gerard's distress, assured him that there was no printing press in Rome, and that the Lactantius must have been printed elsewhere. Gerard's curiosity got the better of him, and he inquired about the origin of the book.
