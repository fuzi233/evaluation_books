Some traversed the landscape of years with the speed of a greyhound, leaving behind a trail of transformation and upheaval, while others moved with the slow, steady persistence of a limpet, making incremental progress that only revealed itself with time.

In the Harleth household at Pennicote, the past year had wrought a profound change in Gwendolen, once the radiant and self-assured beauty of the Archery Meeting. Her fall from triumph to humility had left an indelible mark, tempering her spirit and imbuing her with a newfound sense of contrition. Yet, amidst this metamorphosis, the family's daily routines remained largely unchanged – the rectory's tranquil atmosphere, the vibrant blooms of the peonies and hollyhocks, the rector's steadfast commitment to his duties, and the patriarch's affection for his children.

The rector's life, in particular, was a study in consistency, his confidence in the benevolence of their patrons undiminished by the reduced circumstances that had befallen the family. His scholarly pursuits, too, continued unabated, as he composed articles on ecclesiastical matters, their anonymous publication a testament to his dedication to his craft. Though some might suspect that his writing was motivated by a desire for advancement, the rector saw it as an expression of his genuine passion for learning, unencumbered by the weight of external expectations. His delight in his son Rex's return, accompanied by Anna, was a balm to his spirits, a reminder that even in adversity, there were still moments of joy to be savored.

Rex, for his part, had undergone a transformation of his own, one that seemed to have tempered his ambition, imbuing him with a sense of purpose that went beyond his initial heartbreak. Though his father viewed the setback as a mere adjustment to the natural ebb and flow of life, Rex's actions spoke otherwise – his renewed connection with his family, his engagement with the world around him, and the quiet confidence that now characterized his demeanor all hinted at a more profound change.
