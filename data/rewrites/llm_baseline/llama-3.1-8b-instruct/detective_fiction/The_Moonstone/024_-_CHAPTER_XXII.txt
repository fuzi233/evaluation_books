As the morning sun cast its warm rays through the windows of Black's Hotel, I found myself pondering the enigmatic figure of Sergeant Cuff, who sat hunched in a cozy corner of the lobby, his eyes fixed intently on the pages of his memorandum book. His lips curled into a wry smile as he scribbled notes, the scratch of his pen on paper the only sound breaking the silence.

"Lost in thought, Sergeant?" I ventured.

Cuff looked up, his piercing gaze meeting mine. "Not entirely, Betteredge," he replied, his voice low and measured. "Merely checking my schedule for the day."

I nodded, intrigued. "You think the case is closed, then?"

A hint of a smile played on his lips. "Lady Verinder is a clever woman, to be sure. And roses, I've found, are far more captivating than diamonds." He set his pen down and rose from his seat, striding purposefully towards the conservatory.

I watched as he disappeared into the lush greenery, the scent of blooming flowers wafting out to greet me. The sound of raised voices carried on the breeze, and I caught snippets of their conversation – the dog-rose, once again, at the center of the dispute.

Meanwhile, I had my own errand to attend to. I sought out Mr. Franklin, whose decision to delay his departure had left him with a surplus of idle hours. As I entered the library, I found him standing before the map of Modern Italy, his eyes scanning the parchment with a look of intense longing.

"Still wrestling with your doubts, sir?" I asked, trying to keep my tone light.

Franklin turned to me, his face a picture of woe. "I have aspirations, Betteredge – noble ones, I assure you. But how can I realize them without Rachel's guidance?" He spoke of his thwarted ambitions, his words tumbling forth in a torrent of self-pity.

I listened attentively, searching for a way to ease his distress. And then, a notion struck me – the timeless wisdom of Robinson Crusoe. I excused myself, hurrying to retrieve the book from my quarters. When I returned, Franklin was nowhere to be found, but his presence lingered in the room, a ghostly echo of his discontent.
