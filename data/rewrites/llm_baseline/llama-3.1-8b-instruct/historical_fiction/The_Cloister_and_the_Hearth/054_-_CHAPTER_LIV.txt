As Margaret's eyelids fluttered open, she found herself cradled in Catherine's gentle arms, surrounded by the warm faces of her family, their encouraging voices a soothing balm to her battered spirit. Giles, ever the charmer, boomed out reassurances, his deep voice rumbling like a contented bear. "Don't take it so hard, lass," he said, his eyes shining with warmth. "Gerard's letter says he's alive and well, and by the saints, what a marvel of writing it is! The smallest thing, but full of life and beauty."

Margaret's gaze drifted to the letter in Eli's hands, her fingers twitching with longing to hold it again. "Forgive me, Mother," she whispered, her voice barely audible. "Why did I let him go? Why did I not chase him back home?" She tried to slide off the chair, but Catherine's firm grasp held her fast. With a soft sigh, Margaret leaned against her mother's shoulder, her arm wrapping around Catherine's neck like a vine.

"Let Eli read it, Richart," Catherine suggested, her voice low and soothing. "His eyes are younger, and he'll do it justice." Eli handed the letter to Richart, who took it with a curious expression. As he began to read, Margaret's thoughts wandered to the day she'd woken in the litter, her head throbbing and her leg throbbing in agony. Her merchant, a kind and gentle soul, had held her hand and coaxed her back to consciousness.

She remembered the taste of wine on her lips, the feel of his soothing touch as he tended to her injuries. "Bless him!" Catherine breathed, her voice full of admiration. Eli shot her a warning glance, but Margaret just smiled weakly. As she drifted off to sleep, she felt a sense of peace wash over her, a sense of being safe and cared for.

She'd told him about her psaltery, how it lay broken and useless beside her. To her surprise, he'd smiled and patted her cheek, promising to find her a new one.
