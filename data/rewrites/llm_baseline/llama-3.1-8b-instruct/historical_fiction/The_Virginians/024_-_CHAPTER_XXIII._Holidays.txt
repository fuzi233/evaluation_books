The melody of Mrs. Lambert's daydream had faded away, leaving behind a gentle melancholy. Her daughter, Theo, and her mother exchanged knowing glances, their unspoken thoughts a testament to the tender bond between them. As they sat together in the parlor, Mrs. Lambert's fingers absently stroked the intricate patterns on the armchair, a habitual gesture that betrayed her deep affection for her child.

"I'm afraid, dear," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the ticking clock on the mantle, "that our hero has proven to be rather... ordinary." The maternal kiss on Theo's cheek was a balm to her daughter's wounded heart, and Mrs. Lambert's eyes sparkled with a hint of mischief as she continued, "But fear not, my dear, for I have every confidence that you shall find a true love, one who will cherish you above all else. I, myself, was once as young as you, and I recall the Bury Assembly, where I first met your father. I vowed then and there that I would never settle for anything less than perfection."

As she spoke, her thoughts drifted to the royal court, and the dashing Prince of Wales, whose handsome face and noble bearing had captured her heart. For a moment, she allowed herself to imagine that it was he who had ridden through the gates, and that fate had smiled upon her child in a similar manner. But reality soon reasserted itself, and she smiled wryly, aware that such fantasies were the stuff of novels, and not the substance of life.

Meanwhile, Colonel Lambert and his younger daughter, Hetty, were engaged in a lively repartee, their laughter and teasing a stark contrast to the sentimental atmosphere that pervaded the room. Hetty, ever the provocateur, delighted in needling her mother and sister, drawing them into a game of witty banter that left them both breathless and giggling. She regaled them with tales of far-off lands, of Prussia and Persia, and the wars that raged across their borders. Her questions were laced with a playful malice, and her mother and sister played along, their good-natured teasing a testament to the warmth and camaraderie that filled their little household.
