Up to this point in my account of Flatland, I have been taking for granted a truth that ought perhaps to have been stated as our world’s most fundamental, unnegotiable law: every living human being in Flatland is a Regular Figure, built to exact, symmetrical standards.

To be precise, this means a Woman is not just a line segment, but a perfectly straight line. An Artisan or Soldier will always have two equal sides. A Tradesman, our lower-middle-class Triangles, have all three sides of equal length. Lawyers (of which quiet, unassuming class I am a proud member) have four perfectly matched sides, and so on up the social order: every Polygon, no matter how many sides he claims, will have every edge identical in length. Raw side length of course varies with age: a newborn female is barely an inch long from tip to tip, while a tall, full-grown adult woman can stretch to a full foot. For all male classes, a rough, widely accepted rule of thumb holds that the total sum of an adult’s sides adds up to roughly two feet, perhaps a hair more. But raw size is irrelevant to our discussion here. It is the *equality* of sides that matters, and it takes very little reflection to see that every thread of Flatland’s social order is woven around this unshakable fact, written directly into the will of Nature herself.

If our sides were unequal, our angles would inevitably grow crooked. Suddenly, it would no longer be enough to feel a single angle, or catch a quick blurring glimpse of one shape edge by our Sight Recognition art, to confirm exactly who you are standing next to. You would have to trace every single edge, measure every last angle one by one, just to confirm the full shape of a stranger. No one lives long enough to waste that much time on every casual interaction. Our entire, hard-won science of Sight Recognition would perish overnight. The ancient, carefully cultivated art of Feeling, learned by every child through years of gentle practice, would not survive it by much longer. All social intercourse would become perilous, even impossible. No one would trust their neighbors, no one could plan even the most trivial daily errand, and every trace of civilization would collapse straight back into barbarism.

I do not imagine my readers in Spaceland need much convincing to see how unavoidable these conclusions are. One small example from daily life will be enough. Suppose you are walking down your street, spot two familiar Tradesmen Triangles, glance once at their sharp points and fast-fading far edges to confirm their identity, and invite them back to your home for lunch. Right now, you can do that with perfect confidence: every adult Triangle only ever occupies a predictable, easily estimated couple square inches of space. But imagine that same respectable, sharp Tradesman vertex is dragging behind it a lumpy, unnameable shape with a twelve or thirteen inch diagonal? What do you do when that monster wedges itself immovably in your front door?

I do not mean to insult your intelligence by piling on more obvious details. Already, navigating a crowded public market without accidental collisions stretches the sagacity of even the most well-educated Square. If no one could trust that a single person in the crowd was regular, predictable, shape-stable? Total chaos would reign. The smallest, most trivial panic would leave dozens of people with serious stab wounds, and if any sharp-edged Women or pointy Soldier-class Triangles happened to be in the throng, you could see mass loss of life in seconds.

Practical expediency therefore lines up perfectly with Nature’s design to demand perfect regularity of form, and our Law has never hesitated to enforce that shared mandate. In Flatland, “Irregularity of Figure” is considered a worse failing, a graver threat to the public good, than any combination of moral depravity and outright criminality that you Spacelanders can imagine.

There is, admittedly, a loud camp of paradox-spouting radicals who argue there is no inherent link between geometric lopsidedness and moral corruption. “The Irregular child,” they protest, “is shunned at birth by his own parents, mocked by his siblings, ignored by domestic staff, treated with universal suspicion by all of society, and barred from every position of trust, responsibility, or meaningful work. The police dog his every movement until he comes of age for his official state inspection. If his deviation from the regular standard is too extreme, he is summarily destroyed. The ones just under the allowed margin of error are locked away in menial seventh-class government clerk roles, forbidden to marry, forced to drudge at meaningless work for a starvation stipend, required to live and eat on the office premises, and even forced to take their vacations under constant surveillance. Is it any wonder that even the kindest, purest of natures would turn bitter, warped, and malicious under such treatment?”

This seemingly reasonable line of reasoning has never convinced me, nor has it swayed the wisest of our Statesmen. Our ancestors were entirely correct to lay down the unshakable policy axiom that any toleration of Irregularity is fundamentally incompatible with the safety of the State. The life of an Irregular is undeniably harsh, but the interests of the vast majority require that harshness. Think: if some creature with a perfectly sharp triangular front and a lumpy, uneven multi-sided back was allowed to live, and to produce children even more misshapen than himself? What would become of every art and custom that makes our life livable? Should we reshape every house door, every shop entrance, every church archway to accommodate these monsters? Would our theater ticket collectors be required to measure the full perimeter of every patron before allowing them to take a seat? Should Irregulars be exempted from militia service? And if we do draft them, how do we stop them from weaving through the ranks, tearing and stabbing through their comrades by accident or design? Think of the irresistible temptation to fraud such a shape offers: it would be trivial for an Irregular to walk into a shop facing the cashier with a broad, perfectly regular multi-sided front, pass himself off as a high-ranking Polygon, and order a cartload of expensive goods before anyone thinks to circle around and see the grotesque lopsided mess of his back.

Let the advocates of this false, short-sighted “philanthropy” scream as loud as they like for the full repeal of our Irregular penal laws. I have never in my life met an Irregular Figure who was not exactly what Nature designed him to be: a hypocrite, a misanthrope, and eager to perpetrate every manner of mischief his warped form allows.

That said, I do not endorse the extreme measures some of our more ruthless states enforce, where any infant whose angles deviate as little as half a degree from perfect regularity is destroyed immediately at birth. Some of our nation’s most brilliant, highest-achieving geniuses began their lives with deviations as great as three quarters of a degree, and destroying them at birth would have been an irreparable national loss. Our Healing Arts have scored some of their most glorious triumphs in carefully calibrated compression therapies, gentle shape stretching, surgical trepanning, bone binding, and specialized dietary regimens that can fully or partially correct even severe Irregularity in very young children.

I advocate therefore for a clear via media, a middle path. No arbitrary, unforgiving hard line of demarcation that dooms slightly lopsided gifted children to death. But once a child’s shape has fully set, and the official Medical Board has unanimously confirmed that no further therapy or surgery can correct his deviation from the regular standard, I submit that the merciful, painless disposal of that Irregular offspring is the only responsible choice for the safety of all Flatland.
