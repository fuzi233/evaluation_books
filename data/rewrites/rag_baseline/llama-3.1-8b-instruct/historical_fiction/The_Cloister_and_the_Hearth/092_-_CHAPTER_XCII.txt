In the stillness of the night, he cried out to the heavens, "Ah, Jerome, how well you knew me, and yet how little I knew myself!" His words hung in the air like the scent of incense, heavy with the weight of his regret.

With the clarity of one who has gazed into the depths of their own soul, he recognized the root of his turmoil – the unyielding passion that had consumed him. "I love her more than God," he whispered, the admission piercing his heart like a dagger. "Better than the Church, from such a love, what can spring to me or to her?" He recoiled at the thought, his mind reeling with the implications. "Let the strong resist temptation; it is the weak who must flee. And I, a sinner, what am I but a fragile vessel, prone to shatter at the slightest touch?"

Years of contemplation in a distant convent would temper his passions, rendering him a stronger, wiser monk. A pang of guilt gnawed at his conscience, and he found solace in the thought of providing for her comfort and security. "What will she think of me?" he whispered, his heart heavy with foreboding.
