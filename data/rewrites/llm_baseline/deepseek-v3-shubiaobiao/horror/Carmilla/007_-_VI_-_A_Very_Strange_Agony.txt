The drawing room was warm, bathed in the flickering glow of candlelight as we settled with our coffee and chocolate. Carmilla, as usual, abstained, but her mood had lifted—her enigmatic smile returning as Madame and Mademoiselle De Lafontaine joined us for cards. Papa wandered in later, claiming his “dish of tea,” and once the game concluded, he took a seat beside Carmilla on the sofa.

“Have you heard from your mother?” he asked, a thread of unease in his voice.

“No,” she replied softly.

“Do you know where she might be reached?”

A shadow passed over her face. “I cannot say. But I’ve thought of leaving you—you’ve been too kind. I’ve imposed enough. Tomorrow, I should like to take a carriage and seek her out.”

My heart clenched at the thought.

“Nonsense!” Papa protested. “I won’t hear of it. Not without your mother’s express instruction. Besides—” He hesitated. “The reports of this illness plaguing the countryside grow worse by the hour. I wouldn’t dream of sending you away unprotected.”

Carmilla lowered her eyes, that faint, unsettling smile returning. “You are too generous. I’ve never been happier than here, in your home, with your dear daughter.”

Papa, ever the gentleman, kissed her hand. The gesture should have been charming, but something in her stillness made my skin prickle.

Later, in her chamber, I lingered as she readied for bed. The fire cast long, trembling shadows over the walls.

“Will you ever trust me completely?” I asked.

She turned, her smile widening, but silent.

“You won’t answer?” I pressed.

“You have every right to ask,” she murmured. “But I am bound by vows—tighter than any nun’s. Soon, you’ll know everything. And you’ll despise me for it.” Her voice dropped to a whisper. “Love is selfish, darling. The fiercer it burns, the more it consumes. You must come with me, loving me unto death—or hating me, and still coming. There is no middle ground.”

A coldness slithered down my spine. “You’re speaking madness again.”

“Am I?” She laughed, low and musical. “Have you ever been to a ball?”

“No. What does that matter?”

“I remember mine… dimly. Like watching through dark water. Something happened that night. Something that left me wounded.” Her fingers brushed her chest, just above her heart. “A cruel love. Love always demands blood.”

Her eyes gleamed in the half-light, tracking my every movement. I bid her goodnight and fled, my pulse unsteady.

Sleep did not come easily. I had taken to locking my door, infected by Carmilla’s paranoia. That night, I dreamed—or perhaps I did not.

I was in my bed, the candle still burning, yet the room darkened unnaturally. Something moved at the foot of the bed—a shape black as pitch, long and sinuous, like a monstrous cat. It paced, its eyes burning into me. The air thickened. I couldn’t scream.

Then it leaped.

Twin needles of pain pierced my chest. I woke with a gasp—and saw her.

A figure stood at the bedside, draped in shadow, hair spilling like ink over her shoulders. Motionless. Breathless.

As I stared, she drifted toward the door—then through it, though it never opened.

The room was empty. The door remained locked.

I curled into myself, trembling, until dawn crept through the curtains.

Something had visited me that night.

And I feared it was no dream.
