As I lay on the frozen earth, I became aware of existence in a profound, infinite cold. It was a blessing, one that I knew I had experienced before, though the memory of warmth had long since faded. The cold had a soothing quality, dissolving every care, comforting every sorrow. COMFORTED? Nay; sorrow was consumed by the gentle, life-giving force that drew near, restoring every good and lovely thing a hundredfold. I breathed in deeply, taking in the damp scents of the earth, aware of the quiet presence of primroses, daisies, and snowdrops, patiently waiting for the promise of spring.

The stillness was a balm to my weary soul, and I lay there, full of quiet expectation. I had no need to stir, no need to move – only to rest, to let the cold seep into my bones and calm my racing thoughts. I grew colder, yet welcomed the chill more and more. My consciousness expanded, and I became increasingly aware of bliss, unimaginable yet palpable. It was not something I had sought or prayed for; it was simply mine, a gift of existence itself.

And then, the dreams began to arrive, crowding in upon me like the first wisps of morning fog. I stood on a snow-capped peak, surrounded by a sea of white mist that seemed to stretch on forever. The cold moon hung low in the sky, casting an ethereal glow over the landscape. I was a figure from another time, a figure from a mythic past – Adam, waiting for the breath of life to stir within me. I was a child cradled in the arms of a mother, her skin radiating a soft, otherworldly light. I was a young rider, galloping across a sky of deepest blue, bound for a destination unknown, yet certain.

For an eternity, I rode, lost in the realm of dreams, where time held no meaning, and the seven dimensions blurred together. I was in the heart of the universe, where love reigned supreme, and every sorrow was washed away. I drifted on the currents of a melting glacier, the spectral moon drawing closer, the wind and water whispering a haunting melody in my ear. I listened, entranced, as the song of redemption drew near.
