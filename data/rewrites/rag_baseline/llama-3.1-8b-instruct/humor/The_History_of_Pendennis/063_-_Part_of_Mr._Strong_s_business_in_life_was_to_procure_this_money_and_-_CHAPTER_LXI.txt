As the complex web of relationships within the Clavering household continued to unravel, a most peculiar phenomenon became apparent. The intricate dance of patronage and obligation that had long sustained Sir Francis Clavering's lavish lifestyle was now beginning to fray at the edges. The Begum, Lady Clavering, had reached a breaking point, her patience exhausted by her husband's reckless financial proclivities and duplicitous nature. In a dramatic turn of events, she declared her intention to sever ties with Sir Francis, leaving him to confront the consequences of his own actions.

Meanwhile, the erstwhile gambler had vanished, seeking refuge in the anonymity of a humble inn in the Blackfriars Road. His coachman, aware of the precarious situation, discreetly disseminated the news among the local coaching community, fueling speculation about Sir Francis's potential departure for the Levant. As the days passed, a steady stream of creditors arrived at Grosvenor Place, each bearing their respective claims against the errant baronet. The tradespeople who catered to Lady Clavering's refined tastes – the French cook, Messrs. Finer and Gimcrack, and Madame Crinoline – presented their invoices, accompanied by the substantial accounts of Miss Amory, a lady of considerable means.

Strong, ever vigilant in his capacity as Mr. Clavering's procurer, was soon to discover the accumulating evidence of his employer's pecuniary misfortunes. Upon visiting Grosvenor Place, he found the study cluttered with the weight of Sir Francis's unpaid obligations. The atmosphere was heavy with tension, as the loyal but outraged Mrs. Bonner, the Begum's trusted confidante and housekeeper, confronted Strong regarding the Baronet's predicament. "We've given him everything," she exclaimed, her indignation palpable. "We've lifted him from gaol, paid his debts, and provided for his every need. Yet still he hides, afraid to face his wife or the world. It's time we cut our losses and disavow him."
