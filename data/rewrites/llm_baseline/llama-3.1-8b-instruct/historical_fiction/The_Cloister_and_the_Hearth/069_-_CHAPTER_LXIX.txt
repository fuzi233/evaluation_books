As Gerard lay in the guest chamber of the Dominican convent, the warmth of the large wood fire danced across his weary face, casting flickering shadows on the walls. His clothes, hung to dry beside the hearth, rustled softly in the gentle breeze. Beside him, a gigantic friar sat in silence, his eyes fixed on Gerard as he read pious collects from his breviary. The patient's gaze wandered, drifting between the monk's calm features and the flames, his eyes closing occasionally as he succumbed to exhaustion.

The friar rose from his knees, his face touching the ground in prayer, and bid Gerard farewell. "Daybreaks," he said, "I must prepare for matins." Gerard's weak voice croaked out a question, "Good Father Jerome, before you go, how came I hither?" The friar's response was laced with a hint of mystery, "By the hand of Heaven. You flung away God's gift. He bestowed it on you again. Think on it! Hast tried the world and found its gall. Now try the Church! The Church is peace. Pax vobiscum."

As the friar departed, Gerard slipped into a doze, his mind reeling with the weight of his past transgressions. When he awoke, a new nurse stood beside him – a layman with an eye that sparkled with a mix of curiosity and concern. "How do you feel, signor?" he asked, his voice low and soothing. Gerard's gaze drifted to the stranger's face, searching for recognition. "Very weak. Where have I seen you before, messer?" The layman's expression turned serious, his small, restless eye locking onto Gerard's. "None the worse for my gauntlet?" he inquired, his words tinged with anxiety. "I was fain to strike you withal, or both you and I should be at the bottom of Tiber."

Gerard's memories began to stir, fragments of a desperate plea for help, a plunge into the river, and a stranger's intervention. His eyes snapped open, locking onto the layman's face. "What, 'twas you saved me? How?" The layman's smile, a mixture of relief and wariness, was both familiar and yet, utterly foreign.
