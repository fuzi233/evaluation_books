A murmur rippled through the crowd, swelling rapidly into shouts. "Kneel!" someone whispered urgently. "Kneel, sir, or you will be seen." Another voice roared, "Kneel, you without a beard! Pay obeisance to the only Goddess, or by the Old Gods, I will seize the rites and butcher you!" The whispers became a roar of fanaticism.

Yet, amidst the clamor, the name "Deucalion" began to circulate, bringing a sudden cooling to the enthusiasts' zeal. While few knew the man who fled Atlantis twenty years ago to rule Yucatan, everyone remembered the Deucalion who had ridden beside the Empress just days prior in the golden castle beneath the canopy of snakes. As the weight of his potential vengeance settled over the mob, the shouts died away, leaving them limp and silent.

When the silence returned, Phorenice turned to find me standing alone among the prostrate worshippers. I stepped forward, passing between two massive stones, and crossed the circle to where she stood by the altar. I did not prostrate myself. Instead, at the prescribed distance, I offered the salute she had commanded when I was named her chief minister, greeting her with formal decorum as Empress.

"Deucalion, man of ice," she retorted.

"I still adhere to the Old Gods!" I replied.

"I was not referring to that," she said, looking at me with a sidelong smile.

Just then, Ylga approached us, her face pale, her hand trembling. She threw herself in supplication for my life. "If he will not abandon the Old Gods yet," she pleaded, "surely you will pardon him? He is a strong man; he does not become a convert easily. You may change him later. But think, Phorenice—he is Deucalion! If you slay him here for this single offense, there is no other man within the marches of Atlantis worthy enough to serve..."

The Empress seized the words from her lips. "You slut!" she shrieked. "I keep you near me to manage my wardrobe and carry my fan, and do you dare meddle with my policies? Get back outside this circle, or I'll have you whipped. Yes, and I'll do more. I'll serve you as Zaemon served my captain, Tarca. Shall I point a finger and smite your pretty skin with sudden leprosy?"

The girl bowed her shoulders, cowed, and retreated. Phorenice turned to me. "My lord," she said, "I feel like a young bird in a nest that has suddenly found its wings. Wings have so many uses that I am curious to try them all."

"May each new flight be for the good of Atlantis."

"Oh," she said, flashing an eye, "I know what occupies your thoughts most. But let us return to the pyramid and discuss this at leisure. Pray, my lord, conduct me back to my riding beast."

Thus, I was absolved for refusing to worship her, thereby questioning her divinity publicly. Ylga's interference was deemed untimely. Though I could not fully grasp the reasoning behind these decisions, I accepted them, recognizing that they advanced the scheme Zaemon had entrusted to me.

As the Empress extended her fingers—warm, delicate, yet skilled in grasping weapons of war—I took them gravely and led her out of the great circle, which she had defiled with her tricks. I had expected the Lord Sun to strike vengeance upon the profanation while it lasted, but none came. I knew He would choose His own moment for retribution and appoint whatever instrument He deemed fit, without requiring me to raise a puny arm to guard His honor.

I guided this lovely, sinful woman back to the huge red mammoth, which waited tamely nearby. The scent of the sacrifice trailed us as we walked. She climbed the stairs to the golden castle atop the shaggy beast and bid me mount beside her. The spot reserved for the fan-bearer was empty, allowing our conversation to remain unheard by the crowds below.

She was eager to learn of my ordeal at the gate. I recounted the tale, emphasizing Nais's worthiness and suggesting that with care, the girl might be won back to allegiance. However, I withheld the specific commands Zaemon had given me in the sacred tongue, for it is unlawful to repeat such matters except before the High Council of Priests sitting before the Ark of Mysteries.

"You seem to possess an unusual kindness for this rebel, Nais," Phorenice observed.

"She revealed herself to me as more clever and thoughtful than the common herd."

"Aye," she sighed, perhaps genuinely, "an Empress loses much that a lesser woman takes as her due."

"In what particular?"

"She misses the honest wooing of her equals."

"If you set yourself up as a Goddess—" I began.

"Pah! I wish to be no Goddess to you, Deucalion. That role was for the common people; it grants me power with them and aids my schemes. All seven of the high priests know the trick of calling down fire, and I merely filched it. Cannot you be generous and admit that a woman can be as adept at discovering these natural laws as your musty elders?"

"It remains that you are Empress."

"And nor am I. Consider this: there is a woman seated beside you on this cushion, Deucalion. Look upon her and say the first words that come to your lips. Put aside ceremony and statecraft. Are you willing to wait until your manhood withers? It is wise not to rush unduly; I agree with you there. Yet, who but a fool watches a fruit grow ripe and then leaves it to rot past its prime?"

I gazed upon her glorious beauty, yet it left me cold. Remembering my command, I forced a smile. "I may have been fastidious," I admitted, "but I do not regret waiting this long."

"Nor I. But I have played my life as a maiden long enough. I am a woman, ripe and full-blooded, and the day has come when I should be more than I have been."

I clenched my hand around hers. "Take me to husband, and I will be a good man to you. But speaking now to Phorenice the woman, not the Empress, I offer fair warning: I will be no puppet."

She glanced at me sideways. "I have been master so long that I suspect it will be an enjoyment to be mastered occasionally. No, Deucalion, I promise—you shall be no puppet. Indeed, it would take a lusty lung to pipe if you danced against your will."

"Then, as man and wife, we shall dwell together in the royal pyramid. We shall rule this country with all the wisdom the High Gods have bestowed upon us. These miserable differences shall vanish; the rebels shall return to their homes to hunt and fight beasts in the provinces, and the Priest's Clan shall be pacified. Phorenice, you and I shall throw ourselves, brain and soul, into governance. We will make Atlantis rise as a nation surpassing all others in peace and prosperity."

Petulantly, she withdrew her hand. "Oh, your conditions and your Atlantis! You carry a crudeness in these colonial manners, Deucalion, that palls after the first blunt flavor wears away. Must I do all the wooing? Is there no thrill of love beneath all your ice?"

"In truth, I do not know what love is. I have had little speech with women during these busy years."

"We were a pair when you landed, though I have heard sighs and protestations from every bearded man in Atlantis. Some tickled my fancy, but none moved me deeply. Nor have I learned the nature of love from my own feelings. But, sir, I think you will teach me soon if you persist in your coldness."

"From what I have seen, love is for the poor, the weak, and those of flighty emotions."

"Then I would rather another woman were Empress, and I were some ill-dressed creature of the gutter whom a strong man could seize by force and carry home for sheer passion. Ah! How I could revel in it! How I could respond if he caught my whim!" She laughed. "But I would lead him a sad life if my liking were not stronger than his."

"We are as we are made; we cannot change our inward natures."

She looked at me with a sullen glare. "If I do not change yours, my Deucalion, there will be more trouble brewed for this poor Atlantis than you imagine. There will be ill-doings in our coming household if my love grows for you while yours remains unborn."

I believed she would have had me fondle her there in the golden castle on the mammoth's shabby back, before the city streets packed with curious onlookers. She had little appetite for privacy. For my part, I could not comply. The Gods knew I was earnest about my task, and they knew how it repelled me. I was a true priest that day, having set aside all personal inclination to fulfill the Council's commands. Had I known how to navigate her mood, I might have joined her, but lacking the dozen words necessary, I remained frozen.

There was no choice but to leave everything unsaid. Diplomacy was my training, and on most matters I possessed a glib tongue. But paltering with women was a lightness I had neglected, and if I had invented flowery speeches born of clumsy inexperience, Phorenice would have seen through the fraud
