Silence hung heavy in the small, dimly lit room like the weight of a forgotten melody. Harry's eyes fluttered open, his gaze drifting across the cracked plaster ceiling as the faint scent of damp earth and decay wafted through the air. A soft knock at the door broke the stillness, followed by the appearance of Gumbo, his expression a mixture of curiosity and concern. "A lady has come to see Master Harry," he announced, his voice barely above a whisper.

The door creaked as it swung open, and a figure clad in a worn capuchin cloak stepped forward. Her features were etched with worry, her eyes red-rimmed from crying. She rushed towards Harry, her movements urgent and desperate, as she wrapped her arms around his neck in a tight, maternal embrace. The air was thick with unshed tears as she pressed her lips to his cheek, her voice trembling as she spoke.

"Oh, Harry, my dear, I never thought to see you here." The words tumbled out in a rush, each one laced with genuine emotion. She pulled back, her hands grasping for his, her touch warm and gentle. For a moment, the only sound was the soft rustle of her breathing, the only movement the subtle tremble of her fingers.

Harry's response was hesitant, his words barely above a whisper. "I...I didn't expect anyone to come." The weight of his words hung in the air, heavy with the unspoken pain and shame that lingered beneath the surface.

The lady's reaction was immediate, a wave of tears washing over her face as she clutched his hand tighter. Her voice cracked with emotion as she spoke, each word a testament to the depth of her feeling. "Oh, my dear, my dear, I couldn't bear to think of you in such misery." Her words were a balm to Harry's wounded soul, a reminder that, despite the hardships and struggles, he was not alone.

As she continued to speak, her words spilled out in a torrent of love and concern. She confessed her role in his troubles, acknowledging the part she played in his downfall. "Some of the debts, I fear, were owing to my extravagance," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "You bought trinkets and jewels to give me pleasure. Oh, how I hate them now!" The admission was a small step towards redemption, a gesture of contrition that spoke to the depth of her regret.
