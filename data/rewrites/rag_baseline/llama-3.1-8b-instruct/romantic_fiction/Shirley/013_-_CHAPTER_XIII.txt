As the afternoon sun waned, casting a warm, golden light upon the lush grass, Shirley settled into a state of languid repose. She lay at the base of a sturdy oak, its gnarled branches a gentle canopy above her. Her eyes, like two shimmering pools, reflected the deep blue sky, while the soft rustle of leaves in the breeze lulled her into a world of tranquility. In this serene atmosphere, she found solace, unencumbered by the burdens of the world. The only sounds that broke the silence were the gentle hum of a bee and the occasional distant rumble of thunder.

Her thoughts, however, were not entirely tranquil. A subtle melancholy crept in, like a whispered secret, and her eyes began to shine with a faint, humid light. Caroline, noticing the change, approached her, concern etched on her face. "Shirley, why do you weep?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Shirley's smile, a gentle, enigmatic curve, was the only response. "Because it pleases me," she said, her words barely audible over the soothing rustle of the leaves. "My heart is both heavy and light, yet I find solace in the freedom to feel." Caroline's brow furrowed, puzzled by the enigmatic phrase, and Shirley continued, "You, dear friend, might weep tears of gall, if you choose, but I merely shed tears of joy, soon to be wiped away."

Caroline's confusion deepened, and she pressed for an explanation. "Why should I weep gall?" she asked, her voice tinged with a hint of trepidation. Shirley's laughter, a melodic trill, echoed through the clearing, and she sat up, her curls cascading down her back like a tumble of nightshade berries. "Mateless, solitary bird," she said, her words dripping with a hint of wistfulness.

Caroline's eyes narrowed, sensing a hidden meaning behind the phrase. "And are you not, too, mateless, Shirley?" she asked, her voice laced with a touch of irony. Shirley's smile faltered, and for an instant, her eyes clouded over, as if a shadow had fallen across her face. "At heart—no," she replied, the words barely above a whisper.
