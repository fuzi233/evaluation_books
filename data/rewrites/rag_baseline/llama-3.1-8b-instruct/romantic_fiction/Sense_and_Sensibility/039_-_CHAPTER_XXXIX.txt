As the weeks dragged on, Marianne's longing for the rolling hills and tranquil air of the countryside grew more pronounced. Her impatience to return to Barton was palpable, and she often found herself lost in wistful reveries, envisioning the sweet scent of blooming wildflowers and the gentle rustle of leaves in the breeze. Elinor, though equally anxious to be reunited with their mother, was more pragmatic in her approach, weighing the difficulties of a lengthy journey against the comforts of their current circumstances.

In an effort to alleviate Marianne's discontent, Elinor proposed a compromise – a trip to the Palmer's estate in Cleveland, Somersetshire, where they would spend the Easter holidays with their friends. The prospect of visiting a new region, rich in history and culture, was intriguing, and Elinor hoped it would provide a welcome distraction from their current hardships. However, Marianne was resolute in her refusal, citing the region's proximity to the place where she had initially envisioned spending her summer.

Elinor, sensitive to her sister's feelings, chose not to press the issue, instead focusing on the practical benefits of their plan. She pointed out that Cleveland was situated near Bristol, making it a relatively short journey back to Barton, and that their mother's loyal servant could easily meet them there to escort them home. A stay of only a week or so in Cleveland would allow them to return to their mother's side in a little over three weeks' time. Though Marianne's love for her mother was genuine, Elinor knew that the prospect of a brief respite in a new and unfamiliar setting might be just the thing to steel her against the imagined evils of their current situation.

Mrs. Jennings, who had grown fond of the Dashwood sisters, was dismayed by their decision to leave, but Elinor was resolute in her plans. As they made arrangements for their departure, Marianne found solace in mapping out the days that lay ahead, counting down the hours until they could return to the peaceful rhythms of country life.

Colonel Brandon, who had grown accustomed to the lively presence of the Dashwoods, was visibly affected by their impending departure. In a quiet moment, he sought out Elinor, and together they strolled to the window, discussing the finer points of a rare print she was preparing to copy for a friend. As they conversed, Mrs. Jennings watched with interest, sensing that the colonel's attentions might be more than merely social.
