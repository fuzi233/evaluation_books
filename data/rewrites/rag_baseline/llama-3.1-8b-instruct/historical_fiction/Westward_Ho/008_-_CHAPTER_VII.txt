As we navigate the labyrinthine history of the Oxenham family, it becomes increasingly evident that the dichotomy between tradition and innovation has played a pivotal role in shaping the trajectory of their fortunes. Our attention is drawn to the ancestral stronghold at Stow House, a bastion of antiquity situated on the northern slope of a majestic combe, its origins dating back to the earliest epochs of British history. The edifice, a testament to the ingenuity of its medieval architects, presents a paradoxical blend of fortification and domesticity, its sturdy walls adorned with machicolated turrets, loopholes, and narrow openings for discharging projectiles, yet also featuring a tranquil flower-garden replete with ornate statuary and meticulously pruned foliage.

This dichotomy is reflective of the era in which it was constructed, an epoch marked by the gradual transition from the tumultuous Middle Ages to the nascent Renaissance. The architectural style, a fusion of Norman and Tudor influences, bespeaks the tumultuous historical landscape in which the Oxenham family found themselves situated. Theirs was a legacy forged in the crucible of conflict, with successive generations leaving their indelible mark on the annals of English history.

As we explore the genealogical tapestry of the Oxenhams, it becomes apparent that the family's storied past is inextricably linked to the vicissitudes of their native Devon. The exploits of Sir Bevil, that most illustrious of ancestors, who vanquished the Parliamentarian forces at Stratton and met a hero's death at Lansdowne, serve as a poignant reminder of the family's martial prowess. Yet, it is the quieter moments, those infused with a deep sense of introspection and cultural exchange, that provide the richest insights into the Oxenham psyche. In the tranquil atmosphere of the Stow House gardens, where Sir Richard and Amyas stroll beneath the warm October sun, we catch glimpses of a more contemplative side to this storied family, one attuned to the nuances of language, history, and the subtle interplay of power and tradition.
