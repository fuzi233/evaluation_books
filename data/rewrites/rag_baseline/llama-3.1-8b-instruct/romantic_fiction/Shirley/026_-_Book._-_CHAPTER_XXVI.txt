It was a moment of serenity, a fleeting respite from the turmoil that had beset the rectory in recent days. Yet, even as the garden gate creaked open, and Shirley's familiar form appeared, the tranquility was short-lived. Her arrival was like a spark to dry tinder – it ignited a blaze of emotions within her that would soon engulf everyone around her.

As she entered the rectory, her eyes scanned the room, homing in on Caroline, who lay pale and fragile on the couch. A single glance, a brief, tender kiss, and Shirley's demeanor shifted. Her words, usually so fluid and effortless, faltered, replaced by a studied restraint. "You are better," she said finally, her voice low and husky, as if the words themselves were a struggle to release.

In the moments that followed, Shirley's conversation flowed effortlessly, a torrent of words that masked the turmoil beneath. Her eyes, however, betrayed her – darting towards Caroline, then to Mrs. Pryor, as if searching for answers to unspoken questions. A flicker of concern, a glimmer of suspicion, and a dash of curiosity – all these and more danced across her features, a kaleidoscope of emotions that left Caroline's mother bewildered.
