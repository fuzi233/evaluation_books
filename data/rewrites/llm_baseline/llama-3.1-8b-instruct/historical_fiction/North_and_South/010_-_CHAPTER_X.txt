The evening sky outside was a deep shade of indigo, a somber hue that seemed to seep into the drawing-room as the church-clock struck half-past seven. Mr. Thornton stepped into the warm glow of the lamp, its soft light dancing across the faces of the gathered company. The room itself was a testament to the warmth and hospitality of its inhabitants, a space filled with the gentle comforts of domestic life. A davenport stood in the window, a cozy nook where one could settle in with a good book, while a nearby stand held a delicate china vase adorned with a lush arrangement of English ivy, pale green birch, and copper-colored beech-leaves. The scent of baking wafted through the air, mingling with the faint tang of fresh tea leaves.

Margaret stood by the tea-table, her slender fingers moving deftly as she poured steaming cups for her father and their guest. Her light-colored muslin gown rustled softly as she moved, the subtle pink undertones a perfect complement to the warm tones of the room. A delicate bracelet glinted on her wrist, catching the eye of Mr. Thornton as he watched her with an intensity that bordered on fascination. Her father, oblivious to his son-in-law's gaze, chuckled and playfully teased Margaret, using her small fingers as makeshift sugar-tongs to sprinkle sweetness into his tea.

As the evening wore on, the group settled into a comfortable rhythm, their conversation flowing easily as they savored the warmth and camaraderie of the moment. Mr. Thornton found himself drawn to Margaret, his attention fixed on the gentle way she smiled as she worked, her eyes sparkling with a quiet light that seemed to hold a world of its own. Despite the lingering ache in her head, she was determined to be the epitome of gracious hostess, pouring tea and offering sweet treats with a quiet pride that belied her true feelings. As the night deepened, the shadows cast by the flickering lamp only added to the sense of intimacy, a feeling that this small, ordinary moment was somehow both fleeting and eternal.
