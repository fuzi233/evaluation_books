October thirtieth, 1496, dawned clear and crisp, with an autumnal breeze rustling through the trees of Florence. Yet, the city's residents were oblivious to the gentle caress of the morning air, their minds preoccupied by the tempests brewing at sea. The fierce gales that had ravaged the coast of Leghorn, driving away the ships from Marseilles laden with soldiers and grain, seemed to have united with the other forces of fate to undermine the Frate's reassuring words that Heaven watched over Florence.

The city was on the brink of disaster, its people plagued by hunger and its territories threatened on all sides. The French king, that mighty and ambitious monarch who had marched into Italy in triumph, conquering Naples without resistance, had departed fifteen months prior, leaving behind a power vacuum that seemed to grow more precarious by the day. It was whispered that his grief over the loss of a newborn son had sapped his will to return and restore order to the Church. In response, a Holy League had been formed, with the Pope at its helm, to "drive out the barbarians" occupying the fortress of Naples. On closer inspection, however, this noble endeavor seemed little more than a pact among wolves to chase away other predators, only to fight among themselves for the spoils. And Florence, with her storied history and proud heritage, found herself viewed not as a fellow member of this pack, but as a prized carcass waiting to be devoured.

Of all the major Italian states, Florence stood alone in her refusal to join the League, choosing instead to maintain her alliance with the French. This decision would prove costly, for Pisa, still fighting valiantly for its freedom, was now bolstered by the presence of the German Emperor Maximilian, who had joined forces with the Pisans in an effort to capture Leghorn, while the coast was blockaded by Venetian and Genoese ships. If Leghorn fell into enemy hands, Florence's very existence would be imperiled, her access to the sea cut off and her people left to starve.

In this hour of need, the government of Florence displayed remarkable resilience, rallying the people and summoning new resources to meet the challenges ahead. Despite the mounting shortages, they chose not to turn their backs on the hungry and destitute who flocked to the city's gates, driven by desperation and poverty.
