That evening, as the shadows cast by the setting sun crept across the walls of their home, Archer descended to the drawing-room, expecting to find May already seated, awaiting dinner. Instead, he found the room empty, the silence a stark reminder of the emptiness within. All family engagements had been postponed due to Mrs. Manson Mingott's illness, and Archer's punctual wife had, unusually, not preceded him downstairs.

He recalled hearing her movements in her room while he dressed, and his mind began to wander through the labyrinth of possibilities. He had grown accustomed to indulging in such flights of conjecture as a means of anchoring his thoughts to reality. At times, he felt as though he had stumbled upon a hidden truth – that even his father-in-law, Mr. Welland, may have known the allure of escapism, conjuring entire worlds of domesticity to shield himself from the turmoil within.

May entered the room, her beauty momentarily eclipsed by the faint pallor of her skin. Her hair was styled in its signature coils, but her eyes, once shining like sapphires, now seemed dull and lackluster. Yet, as she approached him, her gaze sparkled with a familiar warmth, and Archer's heart swelled with affection.

"Where did you go?" he asked, trying to keep the concern from his voice. "Ellen said you'd been called away on business."

"I received some letters I needed to attend to," he replied, the words feeling hollow. "I didn't want to disturb you."

May's expression turned wistful, and she paused before responding. "I wish you'd come to Granny's. I was waiting for you."

Archer's guilt pricked at him, and he felt a pang of irritation that such a trivial matter could weigh on him. He had forgotten their plan to meet at her grandmother's, lost in the currents of his own thoughts. The weight of their predictable routine bore down on him, a never-ending cycle of sameness that threatened to suffocate the passion they once shared.

As they sat down to dinner, the conversation meandered through the usual topics, but Archer couldn't shake the feeling that May's reserve was a mask hiding deeper concerns. She made no mention of Madame Olenska, nor of her grandmother's reception of her, and Archer sensed an unspoken tension between them.
