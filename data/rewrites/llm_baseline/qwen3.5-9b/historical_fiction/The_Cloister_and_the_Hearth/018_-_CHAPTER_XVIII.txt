Ghysbrecht Van Swieten knew no rest throughout the long, tempestuous night. His mind was a churning sea of dread, so consumed by anxiety that he dared not join the party scouring Peter's dwelling while the heavens roared above. Only when the final rumble of thunder faded into silence did he stir. With trembling hands, he crept down the stairs, saddled his mule, and rode swiftly toward the "Three Kings" inn at Sevenbergen.

There, he found his men slumbering in every conceivable posture—some sprawled upon chairs, others atop tables, and still more curled upon the bare floor. Ghysbrecht roused them with furious shouts, demanding answers for their failure. Their responses were a disjointed tale of unsuccessful searching, punctuated by boasts of their own zealous efforts.

"Fool!" the burgomaster barked, his voice cracking with rage. "To send you out without me! My life depends on whether he is there this very moment. Did you look beneath the girl's bed?"

"No," came the reply, hesitant and sheepish. "There was simply no room for a man there."

"How can you claim such certainty unless you looked?" Ghysbrecht snarled, his face contorted with fury. "You should have crawled under her bed, pried within its depths, and prodded every panel with your knives until something gave way. Now, rise! I will teach you the true art of searching."

Dierich Brower scrambled to his feet, shaking off the dust of his fatigue. "If you locate him, call me a beast and no man," he challenged, though his eyes betrayed a flicker of doubt.

Within moments, Peter's house stood once more encircled by the burghers. The fiery old man left his mule in the care of Jorian Ketel and, accompanied by Dierich Brower and the rest, burst through the door. Inside, the air was still and silent. Not a soul stirred—not even Peter. They ascended the stairs, hearts pounding with anticipation, but the upper rooms remained vacant.

Then, abruptly, one of the men let out a piercing cry, pointing a finger through the open window of Peter's chamber. The others turned to gaze outside. There, at a short distance, walking calmly across the golden fields alongside Margaret and Martin, stood the fugitive they had hunted so desperately.

Seizing the moment, Ghysbrecht erupted in an exultant yell. He dashed down the stairs and threw himself onto his waiting mule. With a collective surge of adrenaline, he and his men spurred their horses forward, launching themselves in hot pursuit of the fleeing trio.
