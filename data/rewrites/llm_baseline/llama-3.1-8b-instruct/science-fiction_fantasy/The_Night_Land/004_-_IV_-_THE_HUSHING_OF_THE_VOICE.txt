But before I surrendered to the allure of sleep, the air around me began to vibrate with an otherworldly energy, a phenomenon I had grown accustomed to in those quiet nights. However, this time the sensation felt different – it resonated deeply within my soul, like the gentle thrumming of a harp string plucked by an unseen hand.

As I listened intently, I became aware of a voice, soft and familiar, speaking directly to my innermost self. It was Naani's voice, yet it was not; it was as if the instrument of our communication, the Master-Word, had taken on a life of its own. My mind, attuned to the subtleties of this mystical language, responded instinctively, sending the question of the Master-Word into the darkness. The answer, a steady heartbeat in the night, echoed back, and I asked Naani why she chose to speak to me now, when all was still and the watchful eyes of the Monstruwacans slumbered in their Pyramid.

But Naani's response was not forthcoming; instead, she conveyed a fragment of thought that sent shivers coursing through my being. The words, a gentle refrain, seemed to awaken a long-forgotten memory, a tender moment from a bygone era when I had crafted a poem for Mirdath, my beloved, in the distant past. The lines, "Dearest, thine own feet tread the world at night—," danced in my mind, transporting me back to a time when love and loss had woven a tapestry of sorrow and longing.

My heart, stirred by the memory, overflowed with emotion, and I summoned the courage to reach out to Naani once more, seeking an explanation for this enigmatic message. But she remained silent, merely repeating the words, allowing them to settle around me like a soft blanket. In that instant, a realization dawned on me – it was not Naani's voice I heard, but that of Mirdath, my Beautiful One, speaking to me from beyond the veil of time.
