After the massacre that swallowed the ten thousand souls who had ventured forth from the Great Redoubt, there could be no remaining illusion that rescue was possible. The youths who had followed the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk were already far beyond any mortal aid we could offer, their fates tangled in the unseen forces that ruled the endless dark outside the pyramid walls.

We dared not sound the Home-Call, that thunderous world-spanning voice broadcast from the great machine atop the sealed apex of the Mighty Pyramid. To trigger it would have alerted every watching, crawling, waiting horror in the Night Land that humans were abroad outside the redoubt’s sheltering walls. We could only cling to the frail hope that the dark evil Powers had not yet marked the small band of youths for their own; no soul within the pyramid could ever claim to know the full scope of the dark things’ awareness or their blind gaps of ignorance.

Even then, we had long detected a strange, soft, pervasive influence drifting across the dark land, faint enough that our instruments could do no more than trace its passage, no more. We had long suspected it emanated from the accursed House of Silence, perched on its low bleak hill far north of the Great Road. Many of the Monstruwacan observers feared it was reaching out to fasten upon the travelling youths, though no proof could be had to confirm the dread. I laid my uneasy premonitions before the Master Monstruwacan, who trusted my strange sight and intuitive awareness of the aether’s tides — for he too had long suspected a secret, unnameable power dwelt within that silent house.

At last the influence swelled, roiling across the land, and through the Great Spy-Glass I watched the youths veer sharply off the road, breaking into a wild, unthinking run straight for the distant House of Silence, as if their feet had no will of their own. Without hesitation the Master Monstruwacan gave the order, and the Home-Call boomed out across the dead world, its thunder reaching even the poor doomed young men who hastened unknowing to their destruction. Accompanying the great voice was a direct visual message, warning them they were being dragged to their deaths by a force originating within the House of Silence, beseeching them to marshal every shred of their spirit to fight the pull, and if they could not break its hold, to slay themselves swiftly before they crossed the house’s threshold to utter, irreversible annihilation.

A hush fell over the millions crowded in the pyramid’s five hundred thousand observation embrasures and before every public View-Table, for the roar of the Home-Call carried a weight of doom no soul could miss. Unbidden, the combined will and prayers of every living soul in the Mighty Pyramid wove into a single great counter-force that surged through the aether, so powerful it thrummed against my spirit, and for a heartbeat the youths stumbled to a halt, huddled confused in a crowd, as bewildered as sleepwalkers woken mid-step at the edge of a precipice.

A roar of triumph rose from the pyramid’s inhabitants, loud as a gale, but the hope was premature. The dark force emanating from the House of Silence snapped the collective psychic surge apart, and the youths were pulled once more into their mindless run. A shaken, sorrowful silence fell, broken by weeping — but in that same instant, billows of mist bloomed before the young men: pure, pale shining mist made of cold fire that cast no light, a barrier raised by one of the gentle, ever-watchful Powers of Good that warded human spirits from utter ruin. The Monstruwacans reported that the dark influence vanished entirely from their instruments, the House’s hold cut clean away.

The Master Monstruwacan sounded the Home-Call a second time, beseeching the youths for the sake of their mothers, for the sake of all they loved, to turn homewards while the blessed barrier held and sanity was returned to them. For a moment it looked as if they would comply — but their chosen leader, Aschoff, champion athlete of the Nine-Hundredth City, driven by bold, unthinking courage, strode forward and leapt straight through the shimmering wall of cold fire.

The mist dissolved instantly into nothingness, and Aschoff turned once more to run for the House of Silence, all two hundred and fifty of his pure-hearted, wayward comrades following faithfully at his heels. They scrambled up the low hill, through the ageless gaping doorway that had stood open since the world first fell to everlasting night, a doorway that exuded a thick, palpable silence that could be felt across the breadth of the Night Land. They vanished through the uncased, lightless windows and that open archway, and never were seen by human eyes again.

Their mothers and fathers, watching from the pyramid’s embrasures, saw every moment of that horror play out. A profound, unearthly quiet fell across the entire Night Land — a stillness so thick and dreadful that I would have welcomed the distant roar of the giants, the baying of the blind hounds, the uncanny great laughter that rolled across the dark from time to time, even the thin whine that drifted up from the Silver-fire Holes near the Thing that Nods. Any of those familiar, dreadful sounds would have been a mercy next to that utter silence, for no sound of the youths’ suffering ever reached even my Night-Hearing, not in that moment nor all the long years that followed. They had passed into a silence no mortal heart could bear to imagine.

It was plain now to every soul in the Great Redoubt that no force could ever pierce the dark to rescue the people of the Lesser Redoubt. The thought settled heavy on my own heart like lead: for months I had clung to a foolish, bright hope that I might slip out secretly into the Night Land, find that tiny distant redoubt, and step out of the dark before Naani, my love who had been mine through a hundred forgotten eternities, and speak the Master-Word that would tell her I had come at last.

The thought that even then she might be facing some nameless horror at the hands of the dark things was enough to drive me half-mad. I seized my Diskos weapon, ready to run out into the dark half-prepared, to either find her or cast my life away in the attempt. Night after night I sent the Master-Word beating through the aether, calling out to her, warning her never to answer any voice that spoke to her from the dark unless it carried that exact Word, that no true friend would ever lure her out of her redoubt’s shelter. Again and again false voices mimicked her tone, whispering my name from the black, but none could ever speak the Master-Word back to me, and I silenced each with the Word before turning my mind back to thoughts of her.

My health wasted away under the strain, the Master Monstruwacan — who loved me as his own son — chided me gently to rest, but my heart ached too fiercely to heal. Then one night I woke from a tormented sleep, certain I heard Naani calling my old, forgotten love-name, her voice thick with anguish and supplication. I sent the Master-Word surging out into the dark, and for a heartbeat it echoed back, faint and trembling on the edge of my perception. I called to her again and again, but no more came, only that faint, thrilled vibration of the aether that told me she still lived, and was in great peril.

That was the moment I swore my oath: I would no longer hide safe within the Mighty Pyramid while the soul that had been mine through all time faced horror alone. I dressed swiftly, ran up to the Tower of Observation, and laid my plan before the Master Monstruwacan. He begged me long and hard to abandon the foolhardy quest, warned me I would be torn apart by the dark things within days, but he saw that my resolve was unshakable. He did the only thing he could do to keep me safe: he bade me undergo the full Three Days of Preparation, the sacred ritual designed to purify the spirit so that the dark Powers would have less hold over the soul that ventured out into the night.

The Preparation revealed to me all the hidden, unspeakable horrors of the Night Land that were never spoken of to the young — mutilations, abasements of the soul too terrible to set down in any book, horrors that made me wonder any man ever chose to leave the redoubt at all. But the fire of my love burned all the weakness and dross out of me, and I came through the three days and three nights cleansed, no fear left in my heart.

On the fourth day, my grey alien armour was brought to me, lined beneath with a close-woven heat-retaining suit that would fend off the killing cold of the Night Land. My rations — concentrated food and drink, prepared to sustain life for weeks on end — were stitched all about with the Mark of Honour, sewn by the loving hands of the pyramid’s women, to speed me on my way. I stood fully clad, ready at last to step out into the everlasting dark.
