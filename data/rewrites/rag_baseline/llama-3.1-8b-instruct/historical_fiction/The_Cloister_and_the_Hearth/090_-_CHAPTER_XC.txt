The scent of burnt offerings wafted through the narrow alleys of Hoog Straet, mingling with the heavy aroma of damp earth and woodsmoke. It was the day after the storm, and the air felt heavy with foreboding. Catherine moved listlessly, her eyes red-rimmed from weeping, as she made her way to the convent in search of Gerard. Her heart still reeled from the previous day's events, and the weight of her emotions threatened to consume her.

Upon arriving, she encountered the porter, who struggled to understand her request. It took several attempts for him to grasp that she sought to see Gerard, only to learn that he had departed the night before and was not expected to return. Catherine's sighs echoed through the halls as she relayed the news to Margaret, who sat idle, her son clasped tightly in her arms. The boy's small form seemed to be the only thing keeping her grounded in a world that had lost its meaning.

As Catherine implored Margaret to join her at Hoog Straet, the younger woman's response was tinged with resignation. "What for?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper. "You cannot but say to yourselves, she is the cause of all." But Catherine's words were laced with a hint of desperation, and Margaret's expression softened slightly. "Nay, nay," Catherine said, "we are not so ill-hearted, and Eli is so fond of you; you will maybe soften him."

Margaret's weary sigh was a testament to her own emotional state, but she eventually relented, agreeing to accompany Catherine. Upon their arrival, they found Eli and a carpenter engaged in a somber task – replacing the names on the door. The new sign bore the name "Margaret Brandt," a painful reminder of the animosity between the siblings. Catherine's reaction was like a knife cutting through her very soul – a stark reminder that the world was not always just.

As the day wore on, Giles arrived, his prejudices firmly entrenched against the black sheep of the family. He listened to Catherine's tale, his expression unyielding, and dismissed her words with a wave of his hand. "I'll show him," he declared, "what 'tis to have a brother at court with a heart to serve a friend, and a head to point the way."
