The night air was heavy with anticipation as Margaret lay hidden beneath the guise of her chest, a makeshift sanctuary crafted by Gerard to conceal her presence. The wooden slats creaked softly, a testament to its clever design, for a small section had been discreetly sawn away, allowing her to slide in and out unnoticed. The chest itself rested precariously on the rafters, its contents carefully arranged to create the illusion of a shallow bed, when in truth, it was a hiding place that swallowed her whole, two feet deep beneath the floorboards. As the men departed, their footsteps echoing through the house, Margaret waited, her ears straining to detect the slightest sound.

The silence was oppressive, punctuated only by the distant hooting of an owl outside. She knelt, her hands clasped together in gratitude, as if praying to the darkness itself. Then, with a gentle whisper, she called out, "Gerard?" Her voice was barely audible, a soft caress carried on the breeze. But there was no response. She tried again, her voice growing slightly louder, but still, there was only silence. Panic began to creep in, her fingers tracing the contours of the chest as if searching for a hidden compartment, a secret message from her beloved. She reached out, her hand brushing against the lid, and that was when she felt it – a stillness that seemed to seep into her very bones.

With a jolt, she flung the lid open, and a faint scent of wood polish wafted out. Gerard lay motionless, his face ashen, his body limp. Margaret's hands fluttered over him, as if trying to revive a sleeping child. She felt his heart, her fingers pressed against his chest, and horror gripped her like a vice. It was still. The mind recoiled from such a terrible truth – Gerard, who had slipped into the chest mere minutes before, could not be dead. Yet, there he lay, his eyes closed, his skin cold to the touch.

Tears streamed down her face as she begged him to speak, to rouse himself from this deathlike slumber. But there was no response. In desperation, she began to babble, a litany of apologies and pleas, her words tumbling forth like a river in flood. "Oh, Gerard, I'm so sorry... I didn't mean to... Forgive me, forgive me..."
