In the remote western extremities of the realm, where the rugged coastline of Devon and Cornwall converged with the limitless expanse of the Atlantic, existed a series of idyllic glens, each carved into the landscape by ancient rivers and sculpted by the relentless pounding of the ocean. These enclaves, often described as "mouths," were unique and yet eerily similar, boasting towering cliffs, verdant valleys, and crystalline streams that wound their way from one hillside to another. Each was an intricate tapestry woven from the threads of geological history, with layers of rich oak wood, dark furze, and smooth turf unfolding like a palimpsest. The shoreline itself was a jagged mosaic of black rock, striped with pink shell sand and laced with foamy waves that stretched outward in parallel lines, a testament to the primal forces that had shaped this unforgiving yet breathtaking landscape.

It was within one such glen, known as Marsland, that the mystique of the White Witch, Lucy Passmore, held sway. A place of eerie allure, where the rational and the irrational blurred, and the boundaries between reality and myth dissolved. Here, on a crisp October evening, two individuals, Rose Salterne and Lucy Passmore, stood poised on the wind-swept shore, their figures silhouetted against the silver glow of the full moon. Rose, her senses heightened by curiosity and superstition, felt drawn to the very essence of this forsaken place, as if the secrets hidden within its ancient stones held the key to unlocking the mysteries of the universe.

As the night wore on, the conversation turned to the subject of the boat, which lay beached some forty yards away, its presence a testament to the carelessness of Rose's companion, her husband. Lucy's words dripped with exasperation as she berated the absent figure, her voice carrying on the wind. "He's more plague to me than ever my money will be!" The goodwife's ire was palpable, yet beneath it lay a deeper sense of unease, a feeling that the very fabric of their lives was fragile, susceptible to the whims of fate and the capricious nature of the sea. As the moon climbed higher in the sky, casting an ethereal glow over the landscape, Rose and Lucy stood poised on the threshold of a world where the rational and the irrational coexisted, a realm where the laws of physics were bent and twisted, and the impossible became possible.
