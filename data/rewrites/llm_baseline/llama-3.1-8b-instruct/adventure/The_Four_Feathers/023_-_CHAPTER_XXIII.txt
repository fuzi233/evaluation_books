Durrance handed Ethne over to their servant, who was waiting patiently by the carriage, and then turned to Mrs. Adair.

"I want to speak to Colonel Durrance before he goes," Ethne said, her voice barely above a whisper.

Mrs. Adair nodded, and Durrance descended the steps, his footsteps echoing through the stillness. As they reached the bottom, Mrs. Adair fell into step beside him, her pace slower than his. The garden, usually a vibrant tapestry of colors and scents, seemed dull and lifeless today, the flowers wilted and the grass parched.

"I'm glad you're leaving," Mrs. Adair said, her voice heavy with emotion. "It will be so quiet here without you." She paused, her eyes scanning the garden as if searching for something lost. "We'll all be scattered now – you at Wiesbaden, Ethne at Glenalla. It will be hard to bear."

Durrance placed a reassuring hand on her arm, but Mrs. Adair shook it off, her gaze fixed on some distant point. "The house will feel empty without all of us," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "We shall come back, I suppose," Durrance replied, trying to offer some comfort.

As they walked through the garden, Mrs. Adair's thoughts turned to the meal she had prepared earlier – a simple but nourishing soup made with fresh vegetables from the garden. The aroma still lingered in the air, a reminder of the simple pleasures in life. "How long will you stay at Wiesbaden?" she asked, trying to sound nonchalant.
