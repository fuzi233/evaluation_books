A chaotic symphony shattered the silence, wrenching me back from the fog of unconsciousness into the jagged reality of survival. For a fleeting second, the world dissolved into a blur of noise; I could not orient myself, nor pinpoint the source of the commotion. Then, the sounds coalesced into a terrifying tableau: the dragging shuffle of heavy boots, the guttural snarl of primal beasts, the metallic clatter of armor, and the ragged, labored breath of a man engaged in mortal combat.

Pushing myself upright, I cast a frantic glance around the cell. The space was a grim sanctuary for captives, lined with chains that bound men and beasts alike. They lay prostrate near the far wall, their eyes fixed on me with a kaleidoscope of emotions—curiosity, sullen fury, shock, and a flickering ember of hope.

Among them, the hope burned brightest on the countenance of a young woman of the Red Race. She possessed the ethereal beauty of her kin, a species physically akin to humanity's noblest strains but distinguished by skin of a luminous, copper-red hue. Unadorned and stripped of status, she sat as clearly a prisoner, perhaps a slave, her features intelligent and striking. Her cry of alarm, the one that had likely spared my life moments prior, etched a permanent line of gratitude across her face.

It took several agonizing seconds for the tumult beyond the partition to register fully in my mind. Slowly, the pieces fell into place: it was Tars Tarkas, locked in a desperate, solitary struggle against a horde of wild beasts or savage foes.

"Fight!" I shouted, my voice cracking with urgency. I hurled my full weight against the seamless wall separating us, hoping to breach the barrier. The impact was futile, as solid as the falling cliffs of Hell's Bottom itself. Desperation clawed at my throat as I frantically searched for the mechanism of the hidden revolving panel, but my fingers found nothing. Just as I prepared to draw my longsword against the sullen guards guarding the door, the woman's voice cut through the din.

"Save thy blade, O Mighty Warrior!" she called out, her tone urgent yet composed. "It shall serve thee better elsewhere! Shatter it not against senseless metal that yields to the slightest touch of one who knows its secret!"

"Do you know the secret?" I demanded, my heart hammering against my ribs.

"Yes," she replied, rising swiftly. "Release me, and I will open the gate to the chamber of horrors where Tars Tarkas fights. If you seek aid, I can provide it. However, why would you return to face the banths? Why risk yourself again in that trap?"

"Because my friend fights there alone," I answered, already moving to scour the carcass of the fallen custodian. There, upon the oval ring of keys, I found what I needed. Many keys hung there, but the fair Martian maid quickly identified hers—a slender, needle-thin instrument. She slid it into an almost imperceptible aperture in the wall. With a soft, mechanical click, the section of floor beneath me swiveled, carrying me into the chamber where the battle raged.

Inside, the scene was a nightmare of violence and endurance. Tars Tarkas stood backed against a corner, his massive frame a fortress against a semi-circle of six enormous monsters. These were the banths, their fur matted with blood, their jaws dripping with saliva, waiting for any opening. The scars on his glossy green hide told the tale of a brutal fight; legs, arms, and chest were torn to ribbons by sharp talons and cruel fangs. He stood barely able to support his weight, leaning heavily on the wall, drained by exertion and loss of blood. Yet, he remained erect, a monument to the indomitable spirit of his people, embodying the ancient proverb: *"Leave to a Thark his head and one hand, and he may yet conquer."*

When he sensed my entry, a grim smile touched his lips. Whether it signaled relief at seeing me alive or amusement at my own battered state, I could not tell.

Before I could launch myself into the fray with my sword, a gentle hand settled on my shoulder. Turning, I found the young woman standing behind me, having followed me into the lion's den.

"Wait," she whispered urgently. "Leave them to me."

She pushed past me, stepping forward unarmed and defenseless, directly into the circle of snarling beasts. I tensed, expecting her to be torn apart instantly, but instead, she halted mere paces from the monsters. In a low, peremptory tone, she spoke a single word in Martian.

Instantly, the effect was miraculous. The great beasts, usually ferocious and untamed, froze. They slunk backward like frightened puppies awaiting a whip, their aggression vanishing in the face of her command. She spoke again, words too low for me to hear, and then began to lead them. One by one, the six mighty monsters followed her obediently, trailing through the secret panel into the adjacent room. When the last beast had vanished, leaving us staring in wide-eyed astonishment, she turned and offered a small, enigmatic smile before passing through the portal herself, leaving us alone with the green warrior.

For a moment, silence reigned. Then Tars Tarkas spoke, his voice rough but calm.

"I heard the fighting beyond the partition," he said, looking at me with piercing eyes. "But I did not fear for you, John Carter, until I heard the crack of a revolver. I knew no man on Barsoom could stand against you with bare steel, yet that shot stripped the last thread of hope from me, for I knew you lacked firearms. Tell me, how did you survive?"

I recounted the tale, and together we moved toward the other secret panel at the far end of the chamber—the one intended for escape. To our dismay, the lock proved equally elusive; the mechanism seemed to mock our efforts. The prisoners within remained securely chained, suggesting that no easy exit existed from this infernal labyrinth.

Discouragement began to settle over us as we turned from one baffling door to another. Just as despair threatened to consume us, the golden panel at the far end rotated silently. Standing beside it was the young woman who had tamed the banths.

"You dare to attempt escape from the Valley Dor?" she asked, her gaze intense. "And what is your mission, knowing the death you choose?"

"I chose no death, maiden," I replied, straightening my spine. "I am not of Barsoom, nor have I taken the pilgrimage to the River Iss. My friend here is the Jeddak of all the Tharks, Tars Tarkas. Though he has not voiced a desire to return to the land of the living, I intend to bring him with me from this nightmare. I am John Carter, Prince of Helium. Perhaps rumors of me have leaked into your hellish domain."

A smile played on her lips. "Naught that happens in the world we have left is unknown here. I have heard of you, many years ago. The Therns often wonder where you fled, since you neither undertook the pilgrimage nor could be found upon Barsoom."

"Tell me," I pressed, "who are you, and why a prisoner? Yet you wield power over these ferocious beasts, a familiarity and authority far beyond that of a slave."

"A slave I am," she answered, her voice trembling slightly. "For fifteen years, a slave in this terrible place. Now, they have grown weary of me and fearful of the knowledge I possess regarding their ways. I am recently condemned to die."

"What death?" I asked.

"The Holy Therns eat human flesh," she revealed, shuddering. "But only flesh that has first died beneath the sucking lips of a Plant Man—their blood drained, rendering it 'clean.' To this cruel end, I have been sentenced. Had your arrival not interrupted their plans, I would have been consumed within hours."

"Were it the Holy Therns who fell to John Carter's hand?" I asked.

"Oh, no," she corrected gently. "Those you laid low are lesser Therns, servants of the same cruel race. The Holy Therns dwell upon the outer slopes of these grim hills, harvesting victims from the broad world below. Labyrinthine passages connect these caves to the luxurious palaces of the High Ones. Through them flow the lesser Therns, hordes of slaves, prisoners, and fierce beasts—the grim inhabitants of this sunless underworld.

"There are men, women, and beasts born within this dim, gruesome subterranean realm who have never seen the light of day, nor ever shall.
