It was a chilly winter evening, and the household had settled into its nocturnal routine. The study lamp in the refectory cast a warm glow, a beacon of tranquility amidst the din of the day's activities. I had managed to slip away unnoticed, securing a temporary reprieve from the demands of my duties as an English teacher.

My destination was the dormitory, a space I had grown accustomed to traversing under the cover of darkness. However, upon arrival, I was met with an unwelcome surprise: Mistress Ginevra Fanshawe, my pupil, lay abed, feigning slumber. Her nocturnal vigilance was well-known, and I suspected she was merely biding her time, awaiting an opportunity to pounce on some unsuspecting conversation. My own desire for solitude was paramount, for I had a letter to attend to—a missive from Dr. John, penned in his characteristic benevolent tone.

Undeterred by the prospect of encountering Ginevra, I descended to the classes, only to find them in disarray. The hebdomadal ritual of sweeping and purification had left the room in a state of chaotic disarray, the air thick with dust and the scent of coffee grounds. Disheartened but resolute, I retreated, determined to find a sanctuary for my private reading. I retrieved a key from its hiding place and ascended to a secluded garret, a space that offered solace from the prying eyes of my colleagues and superiors.

The air was biting, and I wrapped my shawl tightly around me as I lit the candelabra on the worm-eaten chest of drawers. As I broke the seal on Dr. John's letter, a thrill of anticipation coursed through me. Would it be long or short? Cool or kind? The silvery dimness of the wintry evening seemed to heighten my senses, and I savored the sweet promise of the words within. The letter, penned in Dr. John's familiar handwriting, spoke of our shared experiences, our conversations, and the places we had visited together. The language was infused with a warmth and benevolence that transported me to a realm of unadulterated joy.
