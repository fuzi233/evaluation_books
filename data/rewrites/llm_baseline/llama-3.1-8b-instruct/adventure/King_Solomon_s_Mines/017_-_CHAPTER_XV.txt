The darkness closed in around us like a shroud as we stumbled into Twala's hut, the exhaustion of battle weighing heavily upon our shoulders. Sir Henry and Good, battered and bruised, were laid upon the traditional karrosses, their bodies wracked by the brutal force of the Kukuana warriors' clubs and spears. I, too, felt the weight of fatigue bearing down upon me, the old wound from the lion's claws throbbing with a dull ache. My head still reeled from the blow that had sent me crashing to the ground, the memory of it lingering like a ghostly presence in my mind.

Foulata, the beautiful Kukuana woman who had risked her own life to save ours, moved with quiet efficiency, her dark eyes shining with a fierce determination as she helped us remove the chain shirts that had protected us from the worst of the carnage. Beneath the metal, our skin was a mass of contusions, the marks of the brutal blows that had been dealt out that day. The green leaves she brought forth from the shadows, their pungent aroma wafting through the air, proved a balm to our battered bodies, easing the pain and bringing a semblance of calm to our ravaged souls.

But even as we found solace in Foulata's ministrations, our attention was drawn to the more serious wounds that had been inflicted upon us. Good's leg, once a proud and powerful limb, now hung limply from its socket, a gaping hole carved into its fleshy surface. Sir Henry, too, had suffered a grievous blow, a deep gash slicing across his jaw, a testament to the ferocity of Twala's battle-axe. Good, ever the skilled surgeon, set to work with a steady hand, stitching the wounds with a practiced ease that belied the turmoil that raged within him. The faint light cast by the Kukuana lamp danced across his face, illuminating the lines of concern etched upon his features.

As he worked, Foulata busied herself preparing a nourishing broth, its rich aroma filling the air and stirring our appetites. We ate in silence, our bodies and minds numbed by the horrors of the day's events. Later, as the darkness deepened, we collapsed onto the piles of karrosses, our weary heads sinking into the soft fur as if drawn by an unseen force.
