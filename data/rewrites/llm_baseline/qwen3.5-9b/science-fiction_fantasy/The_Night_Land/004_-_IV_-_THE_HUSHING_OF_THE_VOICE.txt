*"Dearest, thine own feet tread the world at night—
Treading, as moon-flakes step across a dark—
Kissing the very dew to holier light...
Thy Voice a song past mountains, which to hark
Frightens my soul with an utter lost delight."*

One evening, toward the end of the sixteenth hour, as I prepared for sleep, the aether around me began to thrill—a phenomenon common in those days. Yet tonight, this vibration possessed a strange, profound power. Within and without, the voice of Naani rang clearly in my soul.

Though I recognized it as hers, I did not answer immediately. Instead, I projected the Master-Word into the night, seeking confirmation. Directly, I heard the response: the steady, rhythmic beat of the Master-Word echoing through the darkness. Then I questioned Naani: Why speak with me via the Instrument during the deep silence of the night, when all slept and the watch was set among the Monstruwacans? Their sleep cycle began at the eleventh hour; by the sixteenth, they were five hours into their waking phase. She should have been resting, confined to the Tower of Observation with her father. Surely, she had spoken through the Instrument, her voice resonating sharply in my mind.

To this query, she offered no verbal reply. Instead, she imparted a sensation to my spirit that made me tremble. Words formed in my consciousness, beginning: *"Dearest, thine own feet tread the world at night..."*

Perhaps it was the weight of those words that caused my shaking. As they swirled around me, a memory-dream surfaced: I recalled crafting these exact lines for Mirdath the Beautiful in the long-gone Eternity of our Age, when she had died, leaving me utterly alone in a world stripped of color. Overwhelmed by the tumult of this emotion, I grew weak. In a moment, I urgently summoned Naani's presence in my mind, demanding an explanation for the utter turmoil she had wrought in my heart.

Once more, she gave no direct answer. She simply repeated the words across the vast darkness of the world. Suddenly, the realization struck me: It was not Naani speaking. It was Mirdath the Beautiful, emerging from the everlasting night itself. I cried out, "Mirdath! Mirdath!" projecting my cry into the void. Lo! A far, faint voice responded through the eternal darkness, repeating those same words.

Though the timbre was unmistakably Mirdath's, it was also Naani's. My heart knew the truth: this union was real. She had been reborn into this world to mate with me once more, in the living time of that Only One whose spirit and essence had been joined to mine through all ages. I poured every ounce of my strength into calling to Naani, but there came no answer—not a sign of hearing, despite hours of pleading.

Exhaustion seized me, yet neither peace nor sleep could claim me. Eventually, however, I succumbed to slumber.

When I awoke, my first recollection was of the miracle that had occurred during the sleep-time. No one in this world could have known those words unless it was the spirit of Mirdath, my Beloved, gazing down from above my shoulder in that lost era, as I had whispered them to her from a broken, aching heart. The voice was Mirdath's; and Mirdath's voice was Naani's. What can anyone say to such a mystery save what lies in the heart?

Immediately, I called to Naani, once, then twice. Soon, the throbbing of the Master-Word filled the air, beating solemnly in the night. I sent the Word forward as a token of assurance, and instantly, Naani's voice returned. It was slightly weaker than usual, lacking the amplification of the Instrument, transmitted instead through her raw brain-elements.

I answered her, questioning eagerly about her nocturnal utterances. She disclaimed everything, insisting she had no memory of speaking and had slept soundly through the entire period, dreaming only a very strange dream.

Confused and meditative, unsure of how to interpret this, I suddenly sensed again that Naani's voice thrilled the aether surrounding us. She intended to recount the dream that had haunted her mind.

She described seeing a tall, dark man, built massive and clad in unfamiliar garments. He stood in a small room, overwhelmed by sorrow and loneliness. Naani approached him. Presently, the man began to write, seeking to ease his pain by expressing his grief. Naani could read the words, though the script was alien to her waking spirit. She could not recall the content except for one short line: the word *Mirdath* written above. She marveled at dreaming of that name, assuming I had planted the seed with my earlier calls.

With a tremor in my spirit, I asked Naani to describe what else she remembered of the big, sorrowful stranger's writing. Almost immediately, her distant voice echoed around me: *"Dearest, thine own feet tread the world at night..."* That was all she retained.

Yet, this fragment was sufficient. With a sense of mad, strange triumph surging through my soul, I projected the remaining words of the phrase into her mind. My spirit felt them strike Naani's consciousness with the violence of a blow. For a brief moment, she stumbled, rendered dumb by the sheer novelty and certainty of it. Then her spirit woke fully, and she nearly wept from the fright and the sudden, overwhelming wonder of the revelation.

Instantly, her voice thrummed around me—a blend of Naani and Mirdath. I heard the tears of her spirit purify the bewildered, burgeoning gladness in her distant tone. She asked, as if someone who had just unlocked the Gates of Memory, if she might truly be Mirdath. Utterly weak and shaken by this splendor of fulfillment, I could offer no instant answer. She asked again, using my old term of endearment, with a surety in her far voice. Still, I was strangely mute, the blood thundering peculiarly in my ears. Silence passed, and then speech came swiftly.

Thus began our meeting of spirits across the everlasting night.

Picture this scene: Naani standing in the world of that far eternity, her spirit conversing with mine, looking back through partially opened gates of memory into the past of our current life and Age. She saw more than was granted to me in that Age; she perceived other instances, other reunions, confusing and half-understood by me. Yet regarding our present Age and life, we spoke as of a sacred yesterday.

As one might expect, the certainty of this bond stirred me fiercely toward its completion. All my heart and spirit cried out to be with that one who was Mirdath, now speaking through Naani's voice.

But how could this be achieved? None of the learned men in the Mighty Pyramid knew the location of the Lesser Redoubt. Neither could the World's Records and Histories provide this knowledge; they merely suggested a general belief among the Students and Monstruwacans that it lay between the North-West and North-East. No one possessed surety, nor could anyone conceive of the distance separating us from that refuge.

Counting this uncertainty, there remained the incredible danger of the Night Land and the hunger and desolation of the Outer Lands, often termed the Unknown Lands.

I discussed the matter extensively with Naani. Neither she nor her father, the Master Monstruwacan of the Refuge, knew our position. They only knew, according to their Records, that the Builder of the Lesser Redoubt had emerged from the Southern World in the Beginning.

Naani's father set an ancient Compass to bear. Explaining through the Instrument, he noted that such a powerful Earth-Current must belong to us; perhaps our very force was affecting the pointer's steadiness. Indeed, the needle swung in an arc between North and South, within the Western sector. This behavior had been consistent for them, rendering it a helpless guide. Save for the possibility that the Earth-Current accompanying us might pull the needle toward us. If true, we calculated the Lesser Redoubt lay to the North; they deduced we were to the South. Yet, this was all built on shifting sands of guesswork, insufficient to risk life or soul upon.

Curious, though a million times this had been attempted in past ages, we placed the compass before us, retrieved from the Great Museum. As ever in that age, if we stirred the needle, it spun wildly, stopping nowhere with surety. The flow of the Earth-Current from the "Crack" beneath the Pyramid exerted a power to deflect it from the North, causing it to wander. This may seem strange to the present Age, yet to them, it was the apparent nature of reality. Harder to believe was that it ever pointed steadfastly, proving a reliable guide.

Be it known, we knew the cardinal directions by tradition, inherited from the ancient time when, in the Half-Gloom, they constructed the Pyramid. They had mastered the ancient compass and used the sun to name the positions. We, dwelling in that far future day, had forgotten the origins of these directional names, using them only because our fathers had done so for a million years or more. Likewise, we adhered to the traditional names of days, nights, weeks, months, and years. Though there were no visible markings of these cycles—only the everlasting night—the concept felt entirely natural to that people.
