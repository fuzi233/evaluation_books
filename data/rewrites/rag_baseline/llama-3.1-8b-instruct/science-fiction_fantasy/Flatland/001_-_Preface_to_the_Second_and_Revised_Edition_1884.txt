In the flickering candlelight of his small, damp cell, Aethon's eyes drifted towards the worn pages of his memoirs, the scratchy script a reminder of the years spent in Spaceland, where the air vibrated with an otherworldly music. His thoughts, however, dwelled on the present, and the weight of his own diminished form, a shadow of the man he once was. The years of confinement, the mocking gazes of his fellow Flatlanders, and the inexorable march of time had all taken their toll. Yet, as he pondered the words he would write to his readers in Spaceland, he felt a spark within him stir.

He knew that his dear friend, the Editor, would speak on his behalf to address two criticisms that had arisen since the publication of his memoirs. The first, a clever objection, posited that a Flatlander, beholding a line, must perceive its thickness, its substantiality, just as surely as its length. And thus, it followed, that his countrymen must be not only long and broad, but also, in a very slight degree, thick or high. Aethon recalled the countless times he had wrestled with this idea, the frustration of being unable to convey the essence of the unseen dimension to those who dwelled in the world of two dimensions.

As he poured over his thoughts, a memory surfaced – a conversation with the Chief Circle, the High Priest of their land, who had come to inspect the State Prison and, for the seventh time, asked Aethon if he had found redemption. Aethon had attempted to demonstrate the presence of the third dimension, to convince the Circle that he, too, existed in a realm beyond the familiar boundaries of length and breadth. But the Circle's response had been the same as always: "Measure my 'high-ness,' and I will believe you." Aethon's failure to provide a tangible answer had left him defeated, the Circle departing the cell with an air of triumph.

Aethon's thoughts turned to the Editor, who would soon take up the challenge of responding to this and another objection, one of a moral nature. He trusted in the Editor's ability to convey the complexities of his world, to make the invisible visible to those who dwelled in the realms of Spaceland.
