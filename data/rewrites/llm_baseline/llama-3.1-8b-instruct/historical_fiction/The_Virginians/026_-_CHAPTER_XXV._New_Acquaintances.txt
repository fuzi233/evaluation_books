As the afternoon sun cast its warm glow through the windows of Madame de Bernstein's drawing room, Cousin Maria made her entrance, accompanied by two gardeners carrying baskets overflowing with vibrant flowers. The air was filled with the sweet scent of roses and sweet-williams as Maria expertly arranged the blooms in vases throughout the room. Her fingers danced across the surfaces, leaving trails of delicate petals and gentle touches that bespoke a deep affection for her surroundings. The soft chime of the grandfather clock in the corner added a soothing melody to the ambiance, a stark contrast to the lively chatter and clinking glasses emanating from the Pantiles fair outside.

In the midst of this tranquil scene, the soft murmur of conversation between Madame de Bernstein and her niece was interrupted by the arrival of the footmen, their livery a testament to the grandeur of the occasion. They carried with them an assortment of card tables, carefully arranged to showcase the Baroness's exquisite taste. The flickering light from the candelabras cast a warm, golden glow across the room, highlighting the intricate patterns on the wallpaper and the delicate lace trim adorning the furniture.

As the ladies chatted, the sounds of the fair drifted in through the open windows, mingling with the soft hum of conversation and the occasional burst of laughter. The scent of roasting meat wafted from the kitchen, teasing the appetites of the guests and adding to the anticipation of the impending dinner. In a bygone era, the night-life of society had been shrouded in darkness, lit only by the faint glow of tallow candles, their smoke and stench permeating every corner of the city. But now, with the advent of gaslights and the wonders of modern illumination, the evenings were transformed into vibrant gatherings of music, laughter, and warmth.

Madame de Bernstein's voice cut through the gentle din, her words carrying across the room as she addressed her nephew, Harry. "Where are you planning to dine, dear Harry?" she asked, her eyes sparkling with a hint of mischief. "I fear you should join us for dinner in the little parlour, but I think it would be more enjoyable for you to dine at the White Horse at three. We shall hear the bell ringing soon, and I daresay you wouldn't want to miss the opportunity."
