Newland Archer sat in his small, dimly lit office, surrounded by the familiar rhythms of dusty law books and worn leather chairs. His gaze drifted out the window, where the city's sounds blended into a soothing melody – a distant hum of traffic, the chirping of birds, and the occasional clang of a street vendor's bell. The monotony was a comfort, a reminder that some things remained constant despite the turbulence of life. His reverie was interrupted by the rustle of papers on his desk, followed by the soft clearing of a throat. Old Mr. Letterblair stood before him, his white whiskers and rumpled hair a testament to the weight of years and responsibility.

"Newland, I've called you here today to discuss a matter of some delicacy," the older man said, his voice measured and deliberate. "It concerns the Mingott family, specifically their granddaughter, the Countess Olenska." He paused, his eyes clouding over as he collected his thoughts. "She's requested a divorce from her husband, and Mrs. Manson Mingott has asked me to consult with you on the matter."

Archer's fingers drummed a staccato beat on the armrest as he listened, his mind wandering to the opera, where he'd seen the Countess Olenska just a fortnight prior. Her presence had been a fleeting memory, a brief, shining moment in his life. Since then, May Welland had reclaimed her place in his thoughts, and the notion of divorce had receded into the background. He hadn't given it much consideration, dismissing it as idle gossip. Yet, here he was, being asked to delve into the intricacies of the Mingott family's affairs.

Mr. Letterblair handed him a packet of papers, his expression expectant. "These documents outline the case. I'd appreciate it if you could review them before we discuss further." Archer's fingers closed around the papers, feeling the weight of responsibility settle upon him. He hesitated, unsure if he wanted to become entangled in the complexities of the Mingott family's drama. The thought of refusing, however, was equally unappealing, given the expectations placed upon him as a prospective member of the clan.
