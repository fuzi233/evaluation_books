As I stood in the warm sunlight, gazing out at the tranquil expanse of the Salcombe estuary, I felt a sense of foreboding settle within me. The months of July and August had passed, each day bleeding into the next, yet my anxiety had grown with an insidious persistence. My friend, Ethne, had voiced her fears, and I watched as she stood poised at the threshold of Mrs. Adair's window, her slender figure silhouetted against the golden light.

"What troubles you, dear one?" I asked, my voice barely above a whisper, as I rose from my chair and approached her.

Ethne's gaze drifted across the stone-flagged terrace, her eyes tracing the broad, shallow flight of steps that led down to the lawn. The sun danced upon the dew-kissed blades of grass, casting a mesmerizing pattern of light and shadow. "It's Colonel Durrance," she replied, her voice laced with a hint of trepidation. "He's growing more perceptive by the day. Quick to read between the lines, to infer what I don't say, to weave together the threads of my thoughts like a tapestry."

Mrs. Adair, seated in her armchair, smiled quietly, her eyes never leaving the pages of her book. I, too, couldn't help but notice the way Ethne's words hung in the air, like the scent of freshly baked bread wafting from the nearby kitchen. "And his eyes," Ethne continued, "they no longer hold any secrets for me. It's as if he can see right through me, to the very heart of my being."

I nodded sympathetically, aware that Ethne's words held a grain of truth. Since Durrance's blindness, our roles had shifted, much like the ebb and flow of the tide. We, who once navigated the complexities of our relationship with ease, now found ourselves lost in a sea of uncertainty.

Mrs. Adair's eyes flickered up from her book, her gaze meeting mine with a hint of curiosity. "Is there something you're keeping from him, Ethne?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Ethne's response was immediate, her words tumbling forth like a dam breaking. "Yes, there is something. Something I must keep hidden, no matter the cost." Her eyes darted towards the lawn, where Durrance was making his way towards us, his footsteps steady and purposeful.
