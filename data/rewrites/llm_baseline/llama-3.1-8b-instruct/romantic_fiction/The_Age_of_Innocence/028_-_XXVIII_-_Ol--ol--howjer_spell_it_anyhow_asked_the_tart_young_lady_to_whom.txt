As Newland Archer stepped into the Western Union office, he handed his wife's telegram across the brass ledge to the clerk, his eyes meeting those of a tart young lady behind the counter. She looked up at him with a hint of curiosity, her gaze lingering on the scribbled message before her. 

"Olenska—O-len-ska," Archer repeated, studying the foreign syllables etched above his wife's hasty handwriting.

The sound of a low, smooth voice broke the momentary silence, and Archer turned to find Lawrence Lefferts standing beside him, his moustache twitching as he pulled it into a nonchalant pose. "How's that for an unusual name in this part of town?" he remarked, not quite meeting Archer's gaze.

Archer nodded curtly, pushing his telegram through the lattice slot. "I'm here to send a wire to the Mingotts. Heard about their situation, I suppose?" Lefferts' question hung in the air, and Archer hesitated, his thoughts momentarily tangled.

Lefferts continued, his tone laced with a mixture of concern and disdain. "I did hear it's serious, and I assumed you'd come from there. You know, I've always found it rather...unsettling to see how some people handle these sorts of situations."

Archer's jaw clenched, but he bit back the retort that rose to his lips. He knew Lefferts was trying to provoke him, and he wouldn't give in to the impulse. Instead, he chose his words carefully. "Yes, it's quite serious, I'm afraid."

As they stepped out into the street, Lefferts launched into a stream of conversation about the latest gossip. Archer listened attentively, his mind still on the telegram and the events unfolding within the Mingott household.

Later that day, the news of the Beaufort failure spread like wildfire through the city's social circles. The papers splashed the story across their front pages, and the repercussions were immediate. Old Catherine Mingott's stroke was all but forgotten in the face of Beaufort's betrayal. His actions had left a trail of devastation, with many of his clients, including some of the city's most influential families, suffering significant losses.
