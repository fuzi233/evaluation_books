After the destruction of the Ten-thousand and the grim reminder of the Night Land’s terror, all thoughts of rescue faded. The youths who now walked the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk were beyond our reach.

We might have signaled them with the Home-Call, that thunderous voice from the Machine atop the Pyramid’s sealed base. But to do so would betray their presence to the monstrosities lurking in the dark. We could only hope those malevolent forces remained ignorant—though in truth, none could fathom what those Powers knew or ignored.

Yet something stirred in the Land, subtle and ominous. The Instruments barely registered it, but we sensed it: an Influence, cold and quiet, emanating from that accursed House of Silence upon its low hill north of the Great Road. Many among the Monstruwacans feared it sought the youths, though certainty eluded us. We could only watch and wait.

By then, the doomed company had neared the bend where the Road turned sharply northward, drawing ever closer to that abhorrent House. Soon, the Influence swelled, its power undeniable. I felt its origin in my bones—the House of Silence. The Master Monstruwacan shared my dread.

Debate flared among us. Some urged sending the Home-Call, warning the youths of the peril, begging them to turn back while sanity remained. But the Master refused. The Home-Call would roar across the Night Land, revealing their presence to every horror. That ancient signal had not been used since the days of flying ships; to unleash it now risked all.

A day and night passed. Multitudes gathered at the embrasures, watching, whispering. The Influence had grown; the youths no longer rested or ate, driven relentlessly northward at a pace that would soon break them. Their frenzy confirmed our worst fear—they were ensnared.

Then came the moment. A Monstruwacan reported a surge in the Influence. Through the Great Spy-Glass, I saw the youths suddenly veer from the Road, sprinting toward the House. The Master acted at once. The Home-Call bellowed across the world, shaking the Pyramid’s foundations. His message followed, etched in light upon the night:

*"You are drawn to destruction by the House of Silence. Fight its grip! If you cannot break free, end your lives swiftly—do not enter that place!"*

Silence swallowed the Pyramid. Millions prayed, their collective will surging outward, a counter-force against the House’s pull. I felt it—a deafening plea vibrating through the aether. For a breath, the youths halted, dazed, as if waking from a dream. A roar of hope rose from the watchers.

But the counter-force shattered. The House’s will seized them anew. They turned, resuming their desperate run—until a barrier flared before them: billows of pale fire, cold and shimmering. A Power of Goodness interposed itself, shielding them. The Influence faltered; the Instruments fell silent. The House’s grip had been broken, if only for a moment.

The Master sent the Home-Call again, pleading: *"Return now, while you are shielded! For your souls, for your mothers’ love!"*

Some hesitated. Their leader, Aschoff of the Nine-Hundredth-City, stepped forward—and leapt through the fire. The barrier dissolved. The spell reclaimed them. They ran.

They reached the hill. They climbed. Two hundred and fifty hearts, once full of life, now marched toward the yawning doorway that had stood open since the Beginning. The light within was cold, the silence absolute. Aschoff entered first. The others followed.

They were never seen again.

The Land fell into a silence more terrible than any roar. No agony echoed back, no whisper of their fate. Only an endless quiet, thick with horrors unspoken.

In that stillness, I knew the truth: no help would come for the Lesser Redoubt. No more attempts would be made. The cost was too great.

And yet—my soul burned. Naani, my lost love, called to me from the darkness. Visions of her suffering tormented me. Vainly, I sent the Master-Word into the night, receiving only the faintest reply. Mocking voices tried to deceive me, but I knew her true cry when at last it came—weak, desperate.

I could wait no longer.

Clad in the grey armor of the Night, I prepared to leave. The Master Monstruwacan pleaded, but my path was set. For three days, I endured the Room of Preparation, purging fear and weakness from my spirit. On the fourth day, I donned my armor, took up my scrip of provisions, and stepped toward the embrasure.

The Master watched in sorrow. He did not speak. None dared.

I would go into the Night Land—to find Naani or die in the attempt.
