While the world turned without him, a silent war raged within the depths of a soul hardened against all human warmth. It was a conflict born of his own cold temperament and the cruel circumstances that had forged him. I can only whisper of its toll; some tragedies exist beyond the reach of mortal understanding. We know of this one only through fragments—shredded letters addressed to no one, their existence betrayed solely by the trembling hand that wrote them. They were discovered long after Felix Cadwalader's death had been explained, hidden beneath the floorboards of Bartow's room. How they arrived there, or by whom, remains a mystery lost to time.

"Madness!

I have seen Eva Poindexter again, and heaven and hell have wrestled for my soul since that moment. Eva! Eva! The girl I once viewed merely as our quarry. The girl I have surrendered to my brother. She is too radiant for him; too perfect for any man unless he is one who has never known the thrill of a woman's voice or the power of a face capable of awakening such fierce affection. Is this love? Can I, Felix, whose heart has known but a single obsession, harbor even a secret ember in this breast of iron? Never! I am either the victim of frenzy or the toy of devils. Yet, only the inhabitants of a celestial realm seem to shine brighter when I recall those half-lifted eyes, those delicately parted lips, so free of guile, that innocent bearing, and that divine tenderness mixed with strength, which commands admiration and ignites love. I must flee. I must never see her again. Thomas's resolve is unshakeable. He must never perceive that mine trembles like an idol struck by lightning.

If Thomas had not been raised in Paris, he too... But I am the only weak link. Curses on my...

'Did I say I would flee? I cannot, not yet. One final glimpse, merely to confirm that this madness has cause. Perhaps I was merely startled yesterday to find celestial beauty where I expected pale emptiness. If my emotion stems from my own frailty rather than her superiority, I should acknowledge my folly before it destroys me.

I will stay and...

Thomas will not, shall not...

Dexter's daughter...

Hate, hate for Thom...

'My self-esteem is restored. I have seen her again—him—they were together. There was true love in his gaze. How could I expect anything less? And I managed to conceal my agony, imposing his duty upon him. She loves him—or he believes she does—and the work continues. But I will not remain to witness its completion. No, no.

I confessed my story tonight, disguised as a past experience. Oh, the devils must laugh at us men! They have every right to. Sometimes I wonder if my father, in the clarity of his new vision, joins them in their mirth.

Home, with my unholy secret! Home, where nothing distracts me from my gnawing griefs and almost unbearable thoughts. I pace the floors. I cry out her name. I scream it even beneath the portrait of Evelyn. There are moments when I am tempted to write to Thomas—to forbid him...

'Eva! Eva! Eva! Every fiber in my wretched body screams that one word. But no man shall ever know. Thomas shall never know how the thought of her fills my days and nights, turning my life into a torment and the future...

'I await his letters (sparse, cold) as a condemned criminal waits for the executioner. Does she truly love him? Or will that exquisite, soulful nature demand a stronger mate, a more concentrated temperament, a—a...

'I thought I saw my father rise from his grave in one of my dark hours to curse me. Oh! He might curse forever if...

'What have I said about no man knowing? Bartow knows. In his silence, his deafness, he has uncovered my secret, revealing his discovery through his piercing stares, his dissatisfied manner, and a jealousy at which I could laugh aloud if I were not driven to shriek in torment. A mute servant, picked up somewhere I have nearly forgotten, jealous of my weakness for John Poindexter's daughter! He was never jealous of my feelings for Evelyn. Yet, until the day I dared fate by seeking her a second time, her name often echoed through these rooms, and my eyes fixed on but one spot: where her picture hangs, a woeful beauty that has become my reproach.

'I have received a great shock. The starling, trained to murmur Evelyn's name, shrieked out today, 'Eva! Eva!' My first impulse was to twist its neck; my next, to take it from its cage and hide it in my bosom. But I did neither. I am still a man.

'Bartow will twist that bird's neck if I do not. This morning I caught him with his hand on the cage, a murderous light in his eye that I understood instantly. Yet he cannot hear the word the wretched bird murmurs. He only knows it is a word, a name, and he is determined to suppress it. Shall I hang the cage out of this old fellow's reach? His deafness, his inability to communicate with others, the precision with which he obeys my commands via my colored slides, his almost animal devotion to my person—these are necessities for me now. But the bird... Ah! There it goes again, 'Eva! Eva!'

'Is it hate or love I feel? Abhorrence or passion? Love seeks to save, but I have no intention of saving her, since she has acknowledged her love for Thomas, and since he... Oh, it is not now for Evelyn's sake that I plan revenge, but for my own! These nights and days of torture—the revelation of my own nature—the consent I was forced to grant to a marriage that promises bliss to them and anguish beyond measure to me—all this demands vengeance, and they shall not escape, these two. I have laid my plans deep. I have provided for every contingency. It has taken time, thought, money. But the result is satisfactory. If they cross the threshold of my circular study, they must submit to my will or perish here, along with me. Oh, they shall never live and be happy! Thomas need not suspect it. John Poindexter need not suspect it! I might have forgotten the oath sworn on my father's crossed arms, but I will never forget the immeasurable griefs of these past months or the humiliation they have brought me. My own weakness is to be avenged—my unheard-of, my intolerable weakness. Remember Evelyn? Remember Felix! Ah, again! Eva! Eva! Eva!"
