Harry Bland stood before the great oak doors of Castlewood, the heavy wood still warm from the summer sun, though a chill wind had already begun to stir the leaves of the surrounding forest. He was a man of twenty summers, his face open and unlined, save for a sharpness around the eyes that spoke of a mind constantly at work. To call him merely a gentleman of leisure would have been a grave injustice; he possessed a restless spirit, a hunger for knowledge and action that often made the quiet life of his family estate feel like a cage.

His mother, Mrs. Bland, watched him from the window with a mixture of pride and unease. "Go gently, Harry," she called out, her voice carrying over the rustle of the trees. "Do not let your curiosity outrun your judgment. You are leaving home, but you must never forget where your roots lie."

"I will not forget, Mother," Harry replied, turning back to face her. His tone was respectful, yet there was a steel beneath it. "But the world is vast, and if I am to understand it, I must step outside these walls. Father says the colonies need men who can think clearly, not just those who can inherit land."

Mrs. Bland sighed, a sound that seemed to carry the weight of years. She knew her son well enough to know that words were not enough to bind him here. "Then go," she said finally, stepping aside. "But remember, when you return, you bring something back with you. Something good."

Harry bowed slightly, a gesture of old-fashioned courtesy, and then turned to walk down the long drive. As he moved away, the silence of the estate settled over him, broken only by the occasional cry of a bird or the crackle of dry branches underfoot. It was a somber departure, yet there was a certain exhilaration in the air, the feeling of a new chapter beginning.

As he walked, Harry reflected on the lessons his father had taught him. Sir William Bland, a man of stern demeanor and deep convictions, had always emphasized duty above all else. "A man's place is where his honor demands it," his father had said. "And honor is not found in idleness, nor in the comfort of home alone." These words echoed in Harry's mind as he approached the edge of the woods, where the path grew rougher and less traveled.

He paused for a moment, looking back at the house. For now, it was just a memory, a point of origin rather than a destination. But soon, he would be far from it, venturing into lands unknown, seeking answers to questions he could not yet formulate. And somewhere along the way, he hoped to find not only truth but also friendship—a rare commodity in a world so often divided by class and circumstance.

With a final glance behind him, Harry stepped forward, disappearing into the shadows of the forest, ready to embrace whatever lay ahead.
