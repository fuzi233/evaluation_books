The truth lay hidden along the riverbank, its story written in shadows and silence—an event so singular it might never find its equal in all the ages of the world. Yet it passed unseen, unread, by those who lacked the eyes to decipher it.

Gerard had made his choice. With a resolve as cold as the water that would claim him, he penned his farewell to Pietro and Andrea, then stole toward the river as dusk deepened into night. He carried no hesitation, only the weight of copper and silver in his pockets to drag him down faster. The spot he chose was quiet, forgotten—a place where no curious eyes lingered.

Or so he thought.

A figure stood at the mouth of an alley, motionless. Gerard stiffened. Had he been followed? Watched? He forced himself to walk casually past, then circled back. The man was gone—only to reappear moments later, bending as if to adjust his shoe. Moonlight glinted on steel. A gauntlet. A bravo.

Yet Gerard, blind to the thought of enemies, saw only an instrument. He approached, desperate.

*"Sell me your blade,"* he urged, pressing his coins into the man’s grip. *"One stroke—that’s all I ask. Must I beg? Look, I’ll make it easy for you."* He tore open his doublet, baring his throat.

The assassin stared, uncomprehending.

*"What good are you?"* Gerard spat. *"Too cowardly to strike a man who faces you? Then turn away and let me do it myself!"*

With a final snarl, he flung himself into the black water.

The splash shattered the night. And then—

A second plunge. The killer, moving at last, surged after him into the river.

What happened next would be told by the assassin himself.
