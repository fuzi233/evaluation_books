As the dense November fog rolled in, shrouding the landscape like a damp shroud, Margaret Hale gazed out upon the bleak vista from the windows of her new home, Milton. The once-vibrant colors of the rooms now seemed muted, a dull reflection of the melancholy that had settled over her family. The thick yellow mist swirled about the edges of the house, seeping into every crevice, and casting an unwholesome gloom over the already disordered interior.

Her mother, Mrs. Hale, stood beside her, her eyes wide with dismay, as if the very walls of Milton might close in around them at any moment. "Oh, Margaret," she whispered, her voice barely audible above the sound of the fog's insistent drumbeat, "are we to live here?" The question hung in the air, a poignant reminder of the sacrifices they had made in leaving behind the familiar comforts of London and the warmth of their friends.

Margaret's heart echoed her mother's despair, but she forced a weak smile to her lips. "The fogs in London can be far worse, Mama," she said, trying to reassure herself as much as her mother. But even as she spoke, she felt the weight of their isolation bearing down upon her. Here, in this remote corner of England, they were cut off from the world, their lives reduced to a series of mundane routines and stifling conventions.

Dixon, the stalwart housekeeper, hovered nearby, her eyes darting nervously between the two women as she attempted to offer what little comfort she could. "Indeed, ma'am, I'm sure it will be your death before long, and then I know who'll—stay!" she ventured, her words trailing off as she realized the inappropriateness of her remark.

Margaret's response was cool, detached, as she turned to Dixon and said, "Not at all, thank you, Dixon. The best thing we can do for Mama is to get her room quite ready for her to rest." And with that, she excused herself, leaving her mother to the tender ministrations of the housekeeper, while she stepped out into the fog-shrouded night to fetch a cup of coffee.
