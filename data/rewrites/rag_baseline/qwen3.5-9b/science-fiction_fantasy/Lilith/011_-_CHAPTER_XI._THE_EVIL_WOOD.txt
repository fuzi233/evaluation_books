I collapsed into a fitful slumber, waking only as the sun breached the eastern horizon. I climbed to the ridge again and scanned the hollow I had traversed under the moonlight; it was utterly desolate, devoid of any sign of life. Could the seemingly calm expanse ahead teem with creatures of insatiable greed? I turned my gaze toward the terrain stretching before me, the only route available. It resembled a vast, arid desert, save for a distant patch of anomalous color that suggested a forest. There was no indication of habitation—no smoke, no dust clouds, no shadows cast by cultivated fields. The heavens were a piercing blue, unblemished by a single cloud or the thinnest veil of haze.

Descending, I pushed onward toward the imagined treeline. Something living surely existed within it; beyond it, the landscape offered nothing but emptiness. Upon reaching the plain, my vision confirmed the barrenness: rock stretched as far as the eye could see, alternating between flat, channeled expanses and humped, pinnacled formations. This was clearly the dried bed of a vanished river, scarred by countless watercourses that now ran dry. Some channels were carpeted in brittle, gray moss; others bore lichens hardened to the consistency of stone. The air, once rumored to be filled with the pleasant murmur of flowing water, was now silent as a grave.

It took the entirety of the day to reach the wooded area—a dense forest, yes, but lacking even a trickle of stream or rivulet. Yet, bathed in the glare of midday, I remained haunted by an auditory hallucination, hearing the roar of many waters with such clarity that my eyes' testimony seemed absurd. As the sun began its descent toward the horizon, leaving the riverbed behind, I entered the woods. Sunken below the canopy, filtering through the pillar-like trunks, the light revealed a world of blessed shadows awaiting my arrival.

I had anticipated a pine grove, but the flora here defied expectation. Trees of myriad species crowded the space; some bore striking similarities to known timber, while others displayed marvelous, alien differences. I sought refuge beneath the boughs of what appeared to be a blossoming eucalyptus. Its flowers possessed a hard calyx resembling a skull, the apex acting as a lid to release a frothy, brain-like bloom that overflowed its cup. From the shade of its falchion-shaped leaves, my gaze drifted deeper into the gloom.

Soon, however, the forest began to seal itself off. Doors and windows closed, shutting away aisles, corridors, and broad glades. Night descended instantly, bringing a cold that was sharp and biting. How can I convey the wild, spectral quality of this night to you? The tree upon which I leaned rose high before branching, yet its limbs drooped so low they threatened to cage me against the smooth trunk. As twilight faded, the varied outlines of the clumping foliage began to shift, assuming—or rather, suggesting—shapes foreign to their own forms.

A light wind stirred, setting a neighboring tree rocking. Every branch, twig, and leaf synchronized its motion with the sway of the limb, creating a fluid, undulating mass. Within this shifting tapestry, I perceived a pack of wolves struggling violently to break free from a wizard's invisible leash—their tension savage, far exceeding that of any greyhound. Watching them, my interest intensified as the wind gained strength and their movements acquired a semblance of life. Another, denser mass of leaves presented a tableau of horse heads and forequarters, projected caparisoned from imaginary stalls. Their necks bobbed up and down with impatient jerks, only to be disrupted by a wilder, side-to-side swaying as the wind intensified.

What grotesque heads they were! Gaunt, strange, several appearing as mere bare skulls. One lacked its lower jaw, hanging low in an expression of unutterable weariness, yet occasionally lifting high as if easing a nonexistent bit. Hovering above them, at the terminus of a branch, stood the form of a woman, waving her arms in an imperious command. The sudden definiteness of these illusions startled me, followed quickly by unease: what if they overwhelmed my sanity with deceptive reality?

Then, the twilight dissolved into absolute darkness. The wind died, sealing every shape within the void. I slept.

When consciousness returned, I was aware of a distant, confused rushing noise, mingled with faint cries. The sound grew, swelling into a tumult akin to gathering multitudes filling the wood. Simultaneously, from all directions, the noises drew nearer; the spot where I lay felt like the epicenter of a commotion extending throughout the entire forest. I barely moved a muscle, terrified of betraying my presence to hostile entities.

Finally, the moon approached the forest edge and spilled slowly into the canopy. With her first gleam, the noises escalated to a deafening uproar, and dim shapes emerged. As she ascended, brightening the gloom, the cacophony intensified and the figures clarified. A furious battle raged around me. Wild cries, roars of rage, the shock of impact, and prolonged struggles merged with articulate speech, surging in my ears. Curses and creeds, snarls and sneers, laughter and mockery, sacred names and howls of hate collided in a chaotic interpenetration.

Skeletons and phantoms fought in mad confusion. Swords swept through the phantoms, causing them only to shiver. Maces crashed onto skeletons, shattering them hideously; yet not one fell or ceased fighting so long as a single joint held two bones together. Bones of men and horses lay scattered and heaped, ground and crushed underfoot by the combatants. Everywhere, bone-gaunt white steeds charged; everywhere, on foot or mounted on wind-blown mist-horses, raged and ravened the indestructible spectres. Weapons and hooves clashed and crushed, while skeleton jaws and phantom throats swelled the deafening tumult with the war-cry of every opinion, good or evil, that had ever bred strife, injustice, or cruelty in any world.

Even the holiest words accompanied the most hateful blows. Distorted truths flew hurtling through the wind of javelins and bones. Moment by moment, allies would turn against comrades, fighting more wildly than before, screaming, "THE TRUTH!" One figure I noted wheeled endlessly in a circle, striking outward on all sides. Exhausted pairs would sit side by side for a minute, then rise to renew the fierce combat. No one stooped to comfort the fallen, nor stepped aside to spare him.

The moon shone until the sun rose, and all night long I witnessed glimpses of a woman moving at will above the strife-tormented multitude, shifting fronts, one arm urging the fight, the other pressed against her side. "Ye are men: slay one another!" she shouted. I saw her dead eyes and the dark spot upon her brow, recalling the vision from the previous night. This was the battle of the dead, which I observed and heard while lying beneath the tree.

Just before sunrise, a breeze swept through the forest, carrying a voice that cried, "Let the dead bury their dead!" At the command, the contending thousands dropped silently. When the sun peered in, it found no bone, but only here and there a withered branch.

I rose and resumed my journey through a wood as quiet as any grown from peaceful earth. The morning wind had ceased with the appearance of the sun, and the trees were silent. No bird sang, no squirrel, mouse, or weasel showed itself, and not a belated moth fluttered across my path. Yet, I maintained a vigilant watch over myself, daring not to let my eyes rest on any forest shape. Throughout the trek, faint sounds of mattocks, spades, and hurling bones echoed in my mind; any moment, my eyes might open to reveal horrors I would not wish to see. Prudence whispered that perhaps ten thousand phantoms waited only for my consenting fancy to manifest them.

By mid-afternoon, I emerged from the wood to find myself facing a second network of dry watercourses. Initially, I feared I had strayed from my intended line and reversed direction, but I soon realized otherwise, concluding I had simply reached another branch of the same ancient riverbed. I immediately began crossing it, finding myself in the bottom of a wide channel just as the sun set.

I sat to await the moon, and as drowsiness claimed me, I stretched upon the moss. The instant my head touched the ground, I heard the sounds of rushing streams—all manner of sweet, watery noises. The veiled melody of this molten music lured me into a dreamless sleep. When I awoke, the sun was already high, casting the wrinkled countryside into wide visibility. Covered in shadows, the land lay striped and mottled like the skin of a wild beast. As the sun climbed higher, the shadows diminished, as if the rocks were re-absorbing the darkness that had seeped forth during the night.

Hitherto, I had valued my Arab mare and my books more than any living man or woman. Now, at last, my soul thirsted for human presence, longing even for those inhabitants of this alien world whom the raven had vaguely described as closest to my own kind. With a heavy yet hopeful heart, my mind haunted by doubts regarding my direction, I continued wearily traveling "north-west and by south."
