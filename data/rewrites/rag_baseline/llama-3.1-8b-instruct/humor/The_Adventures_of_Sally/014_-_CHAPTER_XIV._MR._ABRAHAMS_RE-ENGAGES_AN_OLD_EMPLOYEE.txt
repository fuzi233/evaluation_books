In the silence of her apartment, Sally sipped her coffee, her gaze drifting to the newspaper article on the kitchen counter. The headline read: "Lew Lucas Knocks Out Bugs Butler." A faint smile crept onto her face as she recalled the fight's aftermath. The jubilant voices of the two Wise Guys, the office-boy's whoop in the subway, and the quiet satisfaction that had settled over her own shoulders all blended together in a melancholy harmony. 

The telephone rang, shattering the stillness. She picked up the receiver, her voice brightening at the familiar tone on the other end. "Ginger!" she exclaimed. "I'm so glad you're back. I just got your letter this morning."

Ginger's voice was laced with a hint of embarrassment. "I'm awfully sorry I didn't come to see you sooner. I've been...preoccupied."

Sally's response was a gentle rebuke, tempered with warmth. "Don't worry about it, dear. I know you've been through a lot. What happened?"

Ginger hesitated before launching into a halting account of his encounter with Bugs Butler. Sally listened attentively, her expression softening as the details emerged. When he finished, she let out a sympathetic sigh. "Poor thing," she murmured, glancing at the photograph of Lew Lucas on the wall. "You must have been a terrible sight to behold."

There was a pause on the other end of the line, and for a moment, Sally wondered if Ginger had hung up. Then, his voice drifted back, hesitant and uncertain. "I don't know what you see in me, Sally."

She laughed, a gentle, soothing sound. "Oh, Ginger, you know exactly what I see. Now, stop talking and come over. I want to see you for myself."

As she waited, the minutes ticked by, each one marked by the steady beat of her heart. Finally, the doorbell rang, and Sally's hands fluttered to her hair, smoothing out any tangles. She opened the door to find Ginger standing in the hallway, his bruised face a testament to the fight's aftermath. A faint flush rose to her cheeks as she took in the extent of his injuries, but her smile remained unwavering. "Oh, Ginger," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the pounding of her own heart.
