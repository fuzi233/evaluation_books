It would indeed have been strange if, with such barren data as they possessed, those men could have read the handwriting on the river’s bank. The mud smelled of fermented silt, cold clay, and the sharp, tannic tang of decomposing water-weed—an olfactory landscape that was about to bear witness to an event perhaps without parallel in the history of mankind, an occurrence that may remain mythically singular to the end of time. But it shall be told here in a very few words, partly by me, partly by an actor in the scene.

Gerard, having penned his brief adieu to Pietro and Andrea, had stolen down to the river at nightfall. His measures were taken with the dogged resolution of a man starved of his vital essence. He filled his pockets with all the silver and copper he possessed, weighting himself to sink the surer. The coins smelled of old yeast, human grease, and the brassy taint of shallow commerce; he stripped them of their superficial market value, reducing them to utilitarian stones meant only to drag him down. So provided, he hurried toward a stretch of the stream where the water’s low, rhythmic churn offered an acoustic refuge from the dissonant, grating noise of the cobblestone streets—a harsh urban clamor that had steadily eroded his psychological well-being. 

There are some, especially women, who look about to make sure there is somebody at hand. But this resolute wretch looked about him to make sure there was no one to disrupt the final silence. To his deep annoyance, a visual and sonic intrusion broke the quiet: he observed a single figure leaning against the corner of an alley, the faint scrape of a boot marring the nocturnal soundscape. So Gerard affected to stroll carelessly away, moving to the rhythm of his own pulse, but soon returned. Lo! The same figure emerged from a side street and loitered. 

“Can he be watching me? Can he know what I am here for?” thought Gerard, tasting the metallic bitterness of fear at the back of his throat. “Impossible.” 

He went briskly off, walked along a street or two in a wide, circular pacing that mimicked the cyclical turning of the seasons, made a detour, and came back. The man had vanished. But lo! On Gerard looking all round to make sure, there he was a few yards behind, apparently fastening his shoe. Gerard saw he was watched, and at that moment observed in the moonlight the glint of a steel gauntlet in his sentinel’s hand. 

Then he knew it was an assassin. 

Strange to say, it never occurred to him that his was the life aimed at. To be sure, he was not aware he had an enemy in the world, so entirely had his existence become an untranslatable subjective experience of grief. He turned and walked directly up to the bravo. 

“My good friend,” said he eagerly, desperately trying to bridge the gap between their disparate lexicons, “sell me thine arm! A single stroke! See, here is all I have.” He forced the heavy, copper-scented money into the bravo’s hands. “Oh, prithee! Prithee! Do one good deed, and rid me of my hateful life!” Even while speaking, he undid his doublet and bared his bosom—skin that remembered the enveloping heat of the hearth, the fragrant steam of simmering bone broths, and the sensory richness of a life now entirely hollowed out. 

The man stared in his face, struck dumb by an offering he could not comprehend. 

“Why do ye hesitate?” shrieked Gerard, his voice a raw, unvarnished sound tearing through the quiet acoustic ecology of the riverbank. “Have ye no bowels? Is it so much pains to lift your arm and fall it? Is it because I am poor, and can't give ye gold? Useless wretch, canst only strike a man behind; not look one in the face. There, then, do but turn thy head and hold thy tongue!” 

With a snarl of contempt that tasted of ash, he ran from the assassin and flung himself toward the deep, brine-scented water. 

“Margaret!” 

It was a single cry, yet it held the entirety of a dying language—a native dialect of one, carrying the profound cultural memory of shared sustenance, flour-dusted hands, and the wild-honey sweetness of their lost domestic routine. 

At the heavy, concussive plunge of his body shattering the river’s sonic surface, the bravo seemed to recover from a stupor. He ran to the muddy bank, and with a strange, guttural cry—an untranslatable vocalization of sheer shock—the assassin plunged into the dark, swirling current after the self-destroyer. What followed will be related by the assassin.
