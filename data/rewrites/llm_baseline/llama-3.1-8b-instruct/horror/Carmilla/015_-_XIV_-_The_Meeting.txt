Their hushed tones carried through the open window, a philosophical discussion quickly devolving into a heated exchange.

I knocked at the door, my presence silencing the cacophony of argument. The two physicians rose from their seats, their faces flushed with emotion. The elder man's words dripped with conviction, while his counterpart scoffed, his eyes gleaming with amusement. As I entered, their disagreement dissipated, replaced by an awkward silence.

"My dear colleague," the first physician said, his tone tinged with annoyance, "it seems you believe we need a charlatan, not a healer."

The old physician from Gratz's expression darkened, his voice laced with a hint of reprimand. "I shall present my views in due time, sir. For now, I fear I am of little use."

As he spoke, he seated himself at a nearby table, his fingers moving deftly over the parchment as he scribbled a few lines. My disappointment threatened to overwhelm me, and I made a hasty bow, departing the room. The second physician caught my eye, his gaze flicking toward his companion before raising an eyebrow in a knowing gesture.

The consultation had yielded nothing, leaving me feeling lost and forsaken. I wandered the gardens, my footsteps echoing through the stillness. The Gratz physician soon overtook me, his apologetic smile a testament to his desire to press one final point.

"I must confess, Monsieur le General," he said, his voice low and urgent, "I am convinced that no natural affliction exhibits such symptoms.
