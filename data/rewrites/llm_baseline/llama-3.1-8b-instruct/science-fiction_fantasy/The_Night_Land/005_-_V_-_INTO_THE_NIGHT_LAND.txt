As the days passed since the calamity that befell the Ten-thousand, and with the ominous presence of the Night Land weighing heavily on our minds, it became clear that any notion of sending aid to those brave Youths venturing forth on the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk was futile. Their fate, like the mysteries of the Night Land itself, seemed forever shrouded in uncertainty.

Still, some among us pondered the possibility of summoning the Home-Call, that powerful and far-reaching voice emanating from the Machine above the sealed base of the Mighty Pyramid. However, this notion was quickly dismissed, for to do so would be to invite the monsters that lurked in the shadows, revealing to them the presence of those who dared to venture forth from the safety of the Great Redoubt. It was a risk we could ill afford to take, especially given the unpredictable nature of the Evil Forces that governed the Night Land.

Yet, despite this trepidation, we couldn't shake the feeling that an otherworldly influence was stirring, one that seemed to emanate from the enigmatic House of Silence perched atop a low hill to the north of the Great Road. Many among the Monstruwacans sensed that this influence was directed towards the Youths, but its true purpose remained a mystery, leaving us to wait and watch with bated breath.

As the Youths drew closer to the fateful bend in the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, where the landscape shifted dramatically to the north, we grew increasingly uneasy. Our sentries, peering through the Great Spy-Glass, reported that the Youths had not rested nor eaten, their determination unwavering despite the perils that lay ahead.

Meanwhile, I confided my growing unease to the Master Monstruwacan, who shared my conviction that the House of Silence held secrets we were yet to uncover. Some among us advocated for issuing the Home-Call, hoping to warn the Youths of the dangers that lay ahead, but the Master Monstruwacan wisely vetoed this idea. To do so would be to put the Youths' lives at risk, and we could not afford to act without concrete evidence of their fate.
