The grand hall of the temple was bathed in a warm, golden light, the scent of incense wafting through the air like the promise of a forgotten feast. Our procession moved with deliberate slowness, a ritualistic dance through the Aisle of Hope, as I stood alongside my companions, our footsteps echoing off the stone walls. The Zodangan guards flanked us, their armor glinting like polished obsidian, their faces expressionless as they led us toward the central platform.

As we reached the raised enclosure, I caught sight of the thirty-one judges, their faces a stern, unyielding mask. I had expected to see some semblance of familiarity among them, a nod or a glance that might hint at a shared history, but instead, their countenances were as alien as the dusty, red landscape of Barsoom itself. Nobles, all of them, yet their eyes seemed to hold no spark of compassion, no flicker of recognition. It was a harsh reminder that, in this place, justice was a commodity to be bought and sold, not a virtue to be cherished.

The circular coliseum was packed to its limits, a sea of faces stretching out before us like a canvas of expectant hunger. All classes, all ages, and both sexes were represented, their eyes fixed upon us with an unnerving intensity. As we halted on the platform, the silence that fell over the assembly was oppressive, a heavy blanket that suffocated the air.

Zat Arras, the presiding magistrate, sat in the golden chair, his eyes gleaming with a calculating intelligence. He called my name, his voice carrying across the hall like a summons to the gods. "John Carter," he declared, "take your place upon the Pedestal of Truth, where your acts will be weighed and your reward determined."

As I stepped onto the smaller platform, I felt the weight of the judges' gaze upon me, their faces a stern, unyielding presence.
