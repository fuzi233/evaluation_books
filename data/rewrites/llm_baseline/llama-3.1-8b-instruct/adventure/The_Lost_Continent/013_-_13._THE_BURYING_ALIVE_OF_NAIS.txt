As the first light of dawn crept over the city of Atlantis, the air was filled with the sound of hammers ringing against stone, a rhythmic cadence that seemed to echo the beating of the city's very heart. Phorenice's edict had gone forth, and the people had responded with haste, their usual concerns and pursuits forgotten in the face of the Empress's will. The city's skilled artisans – masons, labourers, stonecutters, sculptors, engineers, and architects – had converged upon the designated site, their tools at the ready.

In the open square before the royal pyramid, a monumental throne was taking shape. Seven tiers of stone, each a knee-height deep, formed the groundwork, their fronts adorned with three steps apiece. Above, a cavity awaited the body of Nais, and the mighty block that would serve as the throne's seat loomed large, poised precariously on the uppermost layer. As the sun rose higher, the torches that illuminated the scene cast long shadows across the square, making the workers' labours seem all the more frenzied.

Throughout the night, relay after relay of stonecutters, masons, and labourers had toiled, their sweat and grime mingling with the dust of the city's ancient stones. The engineers had constructed elaborate machines to lift the massive blocks into place, and the architects had carefully ensured that each stone fit perfectly into its assigned spot. Whips cracked, and men stumbled, exhausted, but their places were quickly filled by others, driven by Phorenice's unyielding will.

As the crowd began to gather, drawn by the commotion and the promise of spectacle, the chippings and scaffolding were cleared away, revealing the imposing throne in all its glory. Its lower section towered above the ground, its stones weighing more than twenty men combined; the upper part, still aloft on its lifting rams, was a marvel of craftsmanship, its carvings depicting the royal snake entwined around the throne, the great hooded head looming overhead. The air was heavy with anticipation, the people's faces upturned in awe as they waited for the final act in this grand pageant.
