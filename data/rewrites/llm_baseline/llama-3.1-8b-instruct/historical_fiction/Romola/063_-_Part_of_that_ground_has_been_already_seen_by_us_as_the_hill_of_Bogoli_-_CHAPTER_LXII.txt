February 27th dawned over Florence, its morning light casting a warm glow across the cobblestone streets of the city. As the sun climbed higher in the sky, the throngs of people flowing through the streets began to shift, drawn inexorably towards the Piazza di San Marco. Today marked the final day of Carnival, and the air vibrated with anticipation – not just for the evening's festivities, but for the event that would soon unfold in the heart of the square.

In front of the Old Palace, the familiar spectacle of the Bonfire of Vanities loomed large, its flames crackling with a fierce intensity. Yet, despite its presence, the true focus of attention lay elsewhere. A sea of humanity surged through the narrow alleys and side streets, all converging upon the Piazza di San Marco like a great, unstoppable tide. As the morning wore on, the crowd grew denser, its members pushing and shoving in a desperate bid to claim a spot near the front.

The semicircular barrier in front of the church of San Marco stood firm against the pressure, its wooden slats weathered to a soft silver by years of exposure to the elements. Behind it, the Dominican Brethren of San Marco were gathering, their black mantles a somber reminder of their austere vows. Though the crowd's gaze was fixed upon the barrier, their eyes were not yet drawn to the makeshift pulpit that loomed above the church door – a symbol of the momentous occasion about to unfold.

For today, Girolamo Savonarola would take the pulpit once more, his words a beacon of hope and challenge to the people of Florence. Though the Pope's censure and threats hung heavy over the city, the people still flocked to hear him preach, drawn by the conviction that in his words lay a deeper truth. As they waited, the air seemed to vibrate with anticipation – not just for the words that would soon flow from the pulpit, but for the miraculous sign that might confirm the prophet's mission.

Some whispered that Savonarola held within him a power that transcended the mundane, a gift that allowed him to tap into the very fabric of the divine. Others spoke of the miracles he had wrought, of the healings and the conversions that had taken place under his guidance.
