As I wandered the halls, the silence following my mistress's departure allowed me to reflect on Sergeant Cuff, now engrossed in his memorandum book in a cozy corner of the hall. He curled the edges of his lips with a mixture of frustration and curiosity. "Making notes of the case?" I asked, trying to gauge his mood. He shook his head, "No, I'm checking my schedule for my next engagement." I couldn't help but wonder if he thought the case was closed. "You think it's all over here, then?" I ventured. Sergeant Cuff's eyes narrowed, "I believe Lady Verinder is one of the most cunning women in England. I also think a garden rose is far more captivating than a diamond. Where is the gardener, Mr. Betteredge?" He seemed to have lost interest in unraveling the mystery, instead fixating on the gardener's whereabouts. I watched as he rose from his seat, intent on finding the elusive gardener, and disappeared into the conservatory with the dog-rose as the focal point of their argument.

Meanwhile, I had to determine whether Mr. Franklin intended to depart by the afternoon train. Following the conference in my lady's room, he decided to wait for news from Frizinghall. This unexpected change of heart proved to be a catalyst, unleashing a torrent of restless energy within him. As an Italian-Englishman, he floated between the rooms, speaking of nothing but Miss Rachel's treatment of him and addressing no one but me. I chanced upon him in the library, where he sat beneath the map of Modern Italy, searching for solace in conversation. "I possess noble aspirations," he began, "but what am I to do with them now? I am brimming with dormant virtues, if only Rachel would have helped me to unleash them!" His words tugged at my heartstrings, leaving me at a loss for words to comfort him. Suddenly, an idea struck me – a solution inspired by the timeless wisdom of _Robinson Crusoe_. I hastened to my quarters and returned with the book, hoping to find some respite for Mr. Franklin's troubled mind.

I checked the library first, but he was nowhere to be seen. The map of Modern Italy stared back at me, and I, in turn, gazed at the map, searching for a thread to guide me through the labyrinth of his thoughts.
