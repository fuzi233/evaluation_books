In the quiet morning hours, the Castlewood household stirred within its spacious townhouse on Kensington Square. The soft sunlight streaming through the windows cast a warm glow upon the gathering of family members, each absorbed in their individual pursuits. My Lord Castlewood, still clad in his bedgown and nightcap, made a leisurely appearance, his eyes heavy with the remnants of a restful night's sleep. As he took his seat at the breakfast table, his sister, Maria, rose from her chair, her countenance bearing an air of somber reflection.

Their morning repast consisted of chocolate, served alongside delicate pastries and an assortment of fruits. Mr. Will, resplendent in his court suit, joined the assembly, recounting a tale he had heard the previous evening at the Star and Garter. His words were met with anticipation, as the family awaited the revelation of some exciting news.

"What's this that Will has brought home?" my lord inquired, his voice tinged with mild curiosity. He picked up the Daily Advertiser, scanning the pages for any mention of the latest gossip.

"Ask Maria," Lady Fanny suggested, her eyes sparkling with amusement. Maria, however, wore a face of portentous sadness, her complexion pale and drawn.

"It's one of Will's fanciful tales," she said, attempting to dismiss the notion. But Will shook his head vigorously, his expression unyielding.

"I assure you, it's the truth," he declared, his voice laced with conviction. "Tom Claypool of Norfolk witnessed it himself at Ranelagh, and Jack Morris overheard the story from Harry Warrington's own lips."

A hush fell over the room as the weight of Will's words sank in. My lord's countenance darkened, his eyes narrowing in contemplation. "It seems our esteemed Virginian, the Fortunate Youth, may not be who we thought him to be," he mused aloud.

"A second son, perhaps?" my lord ventured, his tone tinged with a hint of incredulity. Will nodded, his face set in a determined expression.

"That's right," he said, his voice firm. "And I must confess, I'm relieved to learn the truth. I never had much regard for the man, and his duplicity only serves to reinforce my opinion."

Maria's face flushed with indignation as she rose from her seat. "How could you, Will? How could you speak so callously of someone in such distress?"
