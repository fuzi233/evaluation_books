The average citizen of Flatland measures roughly eleven inches in span, a dimension that defines the physical limits of our existence, though twelve inches marks the absolute ceiling of growth. Yet within this two-dimensional plane, hierarchy is not measured by height or volume, but by the precision of one's edges. At the bottom of the social ladder lie our Women, mere Straight Lines, devoid of angles and thus invisible to the untrained eye unless they move. Above them stand the Soldiers and the lowest stratum of laborers: Isosceles triangles. These figures possess two long, equal sides stretching nearly eleven inches, converging at a vertex so acutely sharp and formidable that it cuts through the air with terrifying efficiency. When their base shrinks to a microscopic fraction—sometimes no more than an eighth of an inch—they become indistinguishable from the lines they are destined to serve, their points blindingly fine.

As one ascends the social strata, the geometry shifts toward symmetry and stability. The Middle Class comprises Equilateral Triangles, figures of perfect balance where all three sides meet in harmony. Above them sit the Professionals and Gentry, represented by Squares and Pentagons, shapes that command respect through their broader, more deliberate presence. The Nobility begins with the Hexagon and climbs steadily upward, each additional side signifying greater refinement and status, until the figure approaches the ultimate ideal: the Circle. This final form, composed of so many infinitesimal sides that the boundary dissolves into a seamless curve, constitutes the Priestly order, the apex of Flatland's spiritual and political hierarchy.

Nature, in her cruel logic, dictates that a male child inherits one more side than his father, ensuring that each generation theoretically rises a rung on the ladder of nobility. A Square fathers a Pentagon; a Pentagon, a Hexagon. However, this law of ascent holds little sway for the Tradesmen, Soldiers, and Workers, whose asymmetrical forms disqualify them from the promise of progress. An Isosceles father begets an Isosceles son, trapped in a cycle of perpetual degradation. Yet, hope flickers faintly in the darkness for these lower castes. Through a confluence of military valor, diligent labor, and specifically engineered intermarriages orchestrated by the Priests, the more intellectually inclined artisans may experience a subtle metamorphosis: their obtuse base widens while their lateral sides contract, gradually approaching the equilateral ideal.

Such a transformation is a national event, celebrated for miles around. Once an infant born of Isosceles parents is rigorously examined by the Sanitary and Social Board and certified as a Regular Equilateral, a solemn ceremony ensues. The child is immediately severed from his biological roots, removed from the sorrowful embrace of his serf-born kin, and adopted by a childless member of the Equilateral class. This adoptive parent swears an iron oath to sever all ties with the child's origins, fearing that unconscious mimicry of his humble heritage might cause the newly minted nobleman to regress into his ancestral state of servitude.

This mechanism serves a dual purpose. For the oppressed masses, the emergence of a True Equilateral is a beacon of light piercing the monotony of their squalor, a tangible proof that advancement is possible. For the Aristocracy, however, these rare anomalies function as a sophisticated safety valve. The higher classes understand that a populace utterly devoid of hope is ripe for radicalization, capable of electing leaders strong enough to challenge the Circles. Instead, Nature provides a counter-intuitive safeguard: as the working classes cultivate intelligence, virtue, and knowledge, their defining characteristic—their acute, dangerous angle—diminishes. As they grow mentally sharper, they become physically softer, their penetrating power waning as their angles approach the harmless钝ness of the Equilateral.

How admirably this Law of Compensation preserves the divine order of Flatland! By exploiting the very aspirations of the oppressed, the Polygons and Circles ensure that potential revolutionaries are neutralized before they can take shape. The State physicians further assist this grand design through artificial manipulation, compressing or expanding the limbs of rebellious leaders to force them into Regularity, thereby granting them entry into the privileged classes. Those deemed insufficiently advanced are consigned to honorable lifelong confinement in State Hospitals, while the most obstinate and hopelessly irregular are executed. Ultimately, the wretched rabble of the Isosceles, stripped of leadership and united by jealousy, are either dispatched by the loyalists paid by the Chief Circle or driven into internecine warfare by the skillful incitement of the Circular party. History records no fewer than one hundred and twenty major rebellions, alongside hundreds of minor outbreaks, and they have all concluded in this manner, extinguished by the very hopes they sought to fulfill.
