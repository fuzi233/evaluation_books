The late afternoon sun cast a warm glow over the modest household as Tito returned home, his footsteps echoing through the narrow corridors. Romola, seated across from the cabinet in her cozy room, set aside her quill and ink, the soft scratching of her pen the only sound as she paused in her work. The fading light of day had begun to wane, casting a gentle orange hue over the space, and she was about to surrender to the shadows, but her husband's timely arrival stayed her hand.

As Tito entered, his purposeful stride announcing his presence, Romola felt a mix of emotions swirling within her. The previous day's exchange, though born of a genuine concern for the Frate's safety, had left her with a lingering sense of doubt. Now, with Tito's steady gaze upon her, she sensed a shift in his demeanor – a quiet resolve that spoke of unspoken words and unresolved tensions. Without removing his cap or mantle, he leaned against the cabinet, his dark eyes fixed intently on hers, as if daring her to confront the weight of their unspoken past.

Romola's thoughts had been occupied with the possibility of reconciliation, with the notion that a candid admission of her own hasty judgment might clear the air between them. She recalled the silences of the past two years, the unspoken words that had accumulated like unripe fruit, their sweetness lost to the bitter taste of disappointment. As she gazed up at Tito, her expression softened, a gesture of submission etched on her face, but it was swiftly replaced by a growing unease.

For in Tito's face, a subtle transformation had taken place, a tightening of the muscles beneath his skin that spoke of a deep-seated tension. His words, spoken in a calm, measured tone, cut through the air like a knife, leaving Romola breathless. "Romola," he began, "it is time that we should understand each other." A pause followed, heavy with unspoken meaning, and Romola felt her heart quicken in response.

"I wish you once for all to understand," Tito continued, his voice unwavering, "that such collisions are incompatible with our position as husband and wife. I wish you to reflect on the mode in which you were led to that step, that the process may not be repeated."
