As the last wisps of daylight dissolved into the grey Arno twilight, a lone figure emerged from the shadows of the city, his footsteps echoing across the deserted stretch of riverbank. The warehouses and granaries, their stone facades worn smooth by time, stood sentinel-like, casting long shadows that stretched towards the water's edge. A narrow strip of grass and rushes, fed by the slow trickle of gutters, beckoned the man to settle, and he sat down, his eyes drawn to the swift, dark waters that flowed through the channelled slope beside him.

It was here, in this secluded spot, that he had once received small kindnesses – a crust of bread, a raw carrot, and the rinds of an apple – courtesy of the gentle runlets that flowed into the Arno. The anonymity of the place was a comfort, for it allowed him to wait in silence, avoiding the shame of begging on the church steps beyond the Porta San Frediano. For Baldassarre, the thought of begging was a bitter pill, one he would rather swallow poison than take. Yet, he clung to life, driven by a glimmer of hope – a possibility that refused to fade, like a lighthouse beam cutting through the darkness.

Years had passed since he'd left the convent hospital, where the monks of San Miniato had nursed him back to health. Since then, he'd lost sight of the wife who was meant to aid him, and the pestilence that ravaged Florence had all but extinguished his faith in a better future. His knife was gone, and his body was weak, his mind clouded by the weight of his own despair. Still, he yearned to live – to cling to existence, even if it meant waiting for a purpose he couldn't quite define. A formless longing stirred within him, like the first stirrings of dawn, when the world was still and dark.

As he sat by the river, the Compagnacci moved unseen, their dark masses dispersing along the city streets. The smaller group made for the Ponte Rubaconte, the larger for the Ponte Vecchio, but all knew their destination – the Via de' Bardi, where a wealthy merchant lay sleeping, unaware of the fate that awaited him. The Compagnacci leaders had whispered a signal, and the mob moved with a single purpose, driven by a hunger for plunder and revenge.
