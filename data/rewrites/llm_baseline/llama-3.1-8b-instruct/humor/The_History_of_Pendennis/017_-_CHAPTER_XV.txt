As the enemy's forces retreated beyond the boundaries of Fairoaks, Major Pendennis resolved to maintain a vigilant presence within the village. Though he refrained from openly scrutinizing his nephew, Pen, he subtly wove a web of observation, employing various agents to monitor the young man's activities. Every movement, every gesture, and every conversation of Arthur's was meticulously noted by the Major's watchful eyes.

It is a universal human experience to have been thwarted in love at some point or another, by the capricious hand of fate, the duplicity of women, or one's own inadequacies. Allow me to evoke the memories of your own romantic misfortunes, dear reader, and consider them as a reflection of Mr. Pen's anguished state. Imagine the sleepless nights, the feverish longing, and the maddening desires that crash against the unyielding rocks of rejection or indifference. Were we to compile a catalog of the sorrowful musings, lamentations, and curses of lovesick men throughout London, it would comprise a tome of unimaginable proportions. I wonder how many of our city's inhabitants will lie awake at dawn, counting the hours, their minds consumed by yearning and heartache. It is a pain that can be almost palpable, a weight that presses upon the chest, leaving one gasping for air. Though I've never known a man to succumb to love's cruel whims, I have witnessed a robust individual dwindle to a mere shadow of his former self, reduced to a mere specter of his former vitality. Perhaps, in time, the sting of heartbreak will subside, and a new affection will take hold, revitalizing the heart and restoring the body to its former vigor. We say this to comfort the ladies, lest they fret unduly over Pen's predicament. His mother, however, was not so easily reassured. Her maternal instincts drove her to worry and imagine the worst, and she implored the Major to take action, to extricate her son from the clutches of heartache.

"Leave him be, my dear," the Major counseled, with a reassuring smile. "We shall remove her from the village, and expose him to the world. He will soon discover that the pangs of love are fleeting, and that the human heart is capable of healing itself."
