As I stepped down from the porch, the cool evening air enveloped me like a damp shroud, heavy with the scent of wet earth and decaying leaves. Sergeant Cuff stood waiting on the steps, his weathered face a map of creases and lines, etched by the relentless march of time. I felt a twinge of unease, my dignity still reeling from the events of the previous day. Yet, despite my reservations, I found myself drawn to the Sergeant's presence, his words hanging in the air like the promise of a storm.

"What news from Frizinghall?" I asked, the words tumbling out of me like a confession.

Sergeant Cuff's eyes narrowed, his gaze piercing as he replied, "I've spoken with the Indians. They've been released, and their involvement in the theft of the Moonstone is now a mere footnote in the grand tapestry of events. But I warn you, Mr. Betteredge, their absence is a mere ruse – they will not be the last to seek the gem. The jugglers will return, and next time, they will not be so easily deterred."

Franklin emerged from his walk, his expression unreadable behind the mask of his hat. He passed us by, his footsteps echoing on the flagstones as he disappeared into the house. I, however, had already shed my dignity, and I pressed the Sergeant for more information.

"What about Rosanna?" I asked, my voice barely above a whisper.

Sergeant Cuff's expression turned grave, his eyes clouding over like a winter sky. "The mystery surrounding her remains as impenetrable as ever," he said, his voice low and measured. "I've tracked her to a shop in Frizinghall, owned by a linen draper named Maltby. She purchased a length of plain cloth, sufficient to make a simple nightgown. The material was chosen with care, and the quantity was precise – just enough to create a duplicate, should the original become... inconvenient."
