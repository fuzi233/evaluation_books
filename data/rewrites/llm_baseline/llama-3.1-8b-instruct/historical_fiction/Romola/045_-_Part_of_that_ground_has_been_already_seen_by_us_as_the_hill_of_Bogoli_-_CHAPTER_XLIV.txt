Cecco had been swept up with the others towards the Piazza, and she found Baldassarre standing alone by the church door, the horn-cup in his hand, awaiting her arrival. His expression had undergone a marked transformation: the vacant, dreamlike gaze of one struggling to regain consciousness had given way to a fierce intensity that flashed towards her like a trapped animal sensing its prey escaping.

Romola felt a shiver run down her spine as the gaze locked onto hers, and her initial thought was that he must have spotted Tito. A glimmer of hope arose within her that perhaps this man was the one responsible for the troubles plaguing her household, and that her husband might be innocent after all. If only she could bring Tito face-to-face with him and put her mind at ease.

"If you'll join me," she said gently, "I can provide you with shelter and sustenance until you've regained your strength." Her words hung in the air as she awaited his response.

"Yes," Baldassarre replied, his voice low and measured, as if he were speaking to himself rather than her. "I need to gather my strength."

Romola's eyes met his, and she sensed a deep-seated wariness emanating from him, a caution that seemed to intensify now that the haze of confusion was thickening with fatigue. Yet, her kind eyes held a quiet reassurance that slowly coaxed him into responding.

"I'm not from Florence," he said finally, his words hesitant, as if choosing each syllable carefully.

Romola's gaze lingered on him, her mind racing with questions she dared not ask, lest he become skittish and depart. She couldn't help but wonder what secrets lay hidden behind his enigmatic smile.

As they walked in silence across the Ponte Rubaconte, Romola's thoughts swirled with doubts that threatened to extinguish the faint hope that her husband was innocent. If this stranger had indeed wronged her family, what reason could there be for his trepidation?
