The darkness had lifted, yet the world remained bathed in a luminous twilight that felt less like fading night and more like a gentle, waking dawn. We breathed in the air of the spring, a vital, pulsating rhythm that seemed to sync with our own hearts. Something profound had shifted—not merely in the landscape around us, but within the fabric of our souls. Though the sky offered no harsh light to illuminate the path, every element of nature glowed with an inner radiance. Each heather bush, every slender shrub, and blade of grass emitted a soft luminescence, akin to the divine fire witnessed in ancient deserts, or perhaps it was simply the light reflecting from our awakened eyes. Shadows held no power here; instead, objects gently exchanged fragments of their glow, creating a seamless tapestry of illumination. As I gazed at the flora, I perceived not just their shapes and colors, but the essential ideas animating them—their true natures laid bare. My bare feet seemed to find a kinship with the earth they touched, vibrating with a silent love. In this moment, the boundary between the self and the cosmos dissolved. I lived within every leaf, and every leaf resonated within me. To perceive anything was to understand its entire existence, to trace its origins back to a singular, harmonious source. Long dormant senses surged awake, flooding me with experiences too vast for language to capture, too intricate for imagination to fully render. My consciousness expanded, an endless ocean welcoming infinite tides of joy. When a breeze brushed against a cluster of purple heather, setting them chiming, I felt the vibration resonate through my very bones. I was the joy of the bell, the joy of the wind, and the joy of the listening soul, all woven into one. I became a sanctuary for these simple pleasures, a peaceful sea where the swell of life's celebration created perpetual waves. Yet, beneath the shifting forms, the core essence remained constant—an eternal bliss. Life itself felt like a grand, cosmic holiday.

In this state of grace, I realized that truth and life were inseparable. Pure existence was nothing short of bliss; where there was no joy, there was no life, only a pale mimicry known as "living death." Even the dark winds that whispered through the reeds carried a sigh of gratitude. Finally, I existed. My life was inviolate, untouched by fear or sorrow. Walking beside me was my darling, and together we pressed onward toward the Father. The treasures we possessed even before the first sun rose were immeasurable, but what wonders awaited when the eternal day finally broke?

Our path led us to the terrible hollow, a place where monstrous horrors had once thrived. As I had dreamed, it had transformed into a serene and crystal-clear lake. Peering into its depths, I saw how a powerful whirlpool had scourged away the toxic soil where the creatures had burrowed. Resting at the bottom, illuminated by a faint, eerie green glow, lay the entire brood. They coiled in spirals, layered over one another, or sprawled in grotesque, tangled heaps. Their forms were so fantastical and ghastly that they dwarfed the nightmares of poets who had drunk too deeply of sorrow. Even those who had stared into the abyss of the Maelstrom would find these figures far more terrifying than the leviathans of myth. Tentacles twisted in convulsions, swollen lumps pulsed with malice, and glaring eyes burned with a sepia-hued deformity. Every head resembled a wicked flower, blooming from a vile stalk to perfect its cruelty.

Yet, as we passed, not a single creature stirred. They were not dead. As long as there remain minds corrupted by unwholesome thoughts, this lake shall remain haunted by their lingering shadows.

Then, the herald of the sun arrived—a wind carrying the notes of an auroral trumpet, announcing the Master-Minister of the Human Tabernacle. Before his prow gathered a massive wave, fretted with ripples of crimson and gold. He surged upward as if newly launched from the divine hand, pausing briefly to gaze down upon the world. He was a storm of white fury and molten metal, yet he was also a coal taken from the altar of the Father's endless sacrifice. I watched as every tiny flower straightened its stem, lifted its neck, and stood expectant with outstretched heads. Something greater than sunlight, something vaster than mere light, was approaching. It mattered not whether today, tomorrow, or ten millennia would pass; Life Himself and Love Himself were coming. Humanity stretched its necks to witness His arrival, promising to do so again and again until the end of time. Was this merely a vision drawn by the air? Would He truly find them waiting when He returned?

It was a glorious resurrection morning, prepared through the vigil of the night.

Ahead of us, the children gambolled, while the beasts trailed behind. Fluttering butterflies and darting dragonflies danced around our heads, a kaleidoscope of color and flashing wings. Sometimes they descended like a snowstorm of rainbow flakes, covering us in soft beauty; other times they rose into the humid air, forming clouds of embodied scent. It was a summer day, more ideal and perfect than any human who had never died could ever conceive. I walked upon this new earth beneath a renewed heavenscape. Everything I encountered remained fundamentally the same, yet now their minds opened to me. I saw directly into their souls. The spirit of every being greeted me warmly, declaring that we shared a common origin and purpose. They spoke of returning to Him with whom they had always been, of becoming the lightning that took shape as it flashed forth from His presence. The dark rocks drank the rain like thirsty sponges, soaking up the rays and sending them back as living light. Two fires of joy burned within Lona and me. The earth exhaled its sweet-smelling smoke heavenward, while we breathed our longings homeward. Our very consciousness was an act of thanksgiving.

We approached the channels, once parched and weary, but now they rushed and foamed with living waters that sang in their gladness. From as far as the eye could see, a roaring river dashed forward, vocalized by the rocks it struck. We did not attempt to cross this torrent; instead, we "walked in glory and in joy" along its right bank, ascending until we reached the great cataract at the foot of the sandy desert. Here, the river roared and swirled, dropping sheer before dividing into two majestic branches. Climbing to the height above, we discovered that the dreaded desert was gone. Through lush, grassy plains and between verdant banks flowed the deep, wide, and silent river, brimming to the top. For the Little Ones, the glory of God was revealed in the limpid flow of the water. Instinctively, they plunged into the stream and began to swim, soon joined by the beasts following in their wake.

Where the desert had once been barren, it now rejoiced and blossomed like a rose. Vast forests had sprouted overnight, their undergrowth thick with flowering shrubs teeming with songbirds. Every thicket birthed a rivulet, and every stream poured out a liquid melody.

The spot where the buried hand had lain offered no sign of disturbance. Beyond it, and stretching endlessly further, the river continued in full volume from afar. We ascended higher, traversing grassy margins and wandering through forests of gracious trees. With every step, the grass grew sweeter, the flowers more varied and lovely, and the trees grew larger. The wind filled with messages, whispering secrets of the journey ahead.

Finally, we arrived at a forest unlike any we had seen before. Its trees were towering giants, grander and more beautiful, their trunks acting as live pillars that upheld a thick, arched roof. Between the leaves and blossoms, scarcely a sunbeam filtered through. The children scrambled up into the rafters of this aerial vault, leaping and shouting to the unseen elephants below, who replied with deep, resonant trumpets. Lona understood the conversations taking place among them, though I could only guess at their meaning clumsily. The Little Ones chased the squirrels, who frolicked playfully, drawing them on until they allowed themselves to be caught and petted. Occasionally, a bird, radiant in plumage and form, would alight upon one of them, singing a prophecy of what was to come before flying away. Not a single monkey could be seen anywhere in this realm.
