The chamber was cold, the air thick with the weight of impending malice. Zat Arras regarded me with a serpent’s smile, his voice slick with mock courtesy.

“Ah, what brings the Prince of Helium so graciously into my presence?”

The gag was torn from my mouth, but I held my tongue, my gaze unwavering. Contempt curled in my chest, tightening like a fist. The silence stretched, thick as the tension between us, until Zat Arras’ face darkened with fury.

With a flick of his wrist, he dismissed the guards, leaving only his two sycophants and me in the dim-lit chamber. When the door sealed shut, his voice dripped like venom.

“John Carter,” he began, slow and deliberate, savoring each word, “by the law of our faith and the decree of the court, you are condemned. The people cannot save you—*I* alone hold that power. Your life is mine to spare or sever as I choose. None would question it.”

He stepped closer, his breath hot against my face. “Walk free in Helium for a year, and the people will forget your sentence. But know this: Tardos Mors will never return. Nor will Mors Kajak. Nor Dejah Thoris. The throne must be filled.” His eyes gleamed. “Zat Arras *will* be Jeddak. Swear your loyalty, and your chains vanish. Refuse…” He let the threat hang, sharp as a blade.

The choice was a knife in my ribs. Refuse, and Dejah Thoris’ fate would be sealed—her life torn apart in the arena of Issus. But to bow to this traitor? Never.

I met his gaze. “No alliance exists between a prince of Helium and a usurper. I do not believe Tardos Mors is dead.”

Zat Arras laughed, a sound like cracking ice. “Soon, your beliefs will matter as little as your life.” He clapped his hands. “To the pits with him.”

The guards dragged me through winding tunnels, deeper into the bowels of Helium, until they shackled me in a cavernous cell. The walls bore the grim remains of past prisoners—skeletons chained in eternal silence. The last guard took the light, leaving me in darkness so complete it pressed against my skin.

Time blurred. Sleep came fitfully.

Then—a flicker of light. A young man stood before me, bearing a bowl of gruel.

“Zat Arras sends greetings,” he said, his voice flat. “His offer stands. Accept, and walk free.”

I refused.

Days bled into months. The youth returned twice daily, silent as the grave. I scraped at my chains, desperate for escape, for word of Carthoris, of Dejah Thoris—was she still alive?

Then, a gambit. The boy—Parthak—had noble bearing beneath his shabby garb. I tempted him with promises of fine harness and a jeweled blade from my palace, if only he would carry a note to Carthoris.

He hesitated. Then, days later, he brought writing tools.

My message was simple: *Deliver to Parthak what I promised.* If Carthoris obeyed, I would know he lived. If Parthak wore the gifts, Carthoris would know *I* lived.

But Parthak never returned.

Despair gnawed at me. Thirty days remained before Dejah Thoris would face the arena. The image of her torn apart by beasts clawed at my mind—

Then, footsteps. Not Parthak. A new jailer, mute as stone.

Until—

A shadow moved in the dark. I lunged, chain raised—

And froze.

The chain struck, but not fatally. He lived.

“Father,” he gasped, clutching his head.

Relief and guilt crashed over me. He had come through Parthak’s betrayal, through Xodar’s cunning—Hor Vastus had played the Zodangan’s cellmate, extracting my location.

Now, freed, we ascended to my palace, where Kantos Kan, Tars Tarkas, and the others waited.

The fleet was ready. A thousand battleships at Hastor. A quarter-million warriors from Thark. A million Heliumites sworn to secrecy.

“We sail tonight,” I said.

But gunfire erupted in the gardens—a spy, cut down too late.

Zat Arras knew.

The clock was ticking.
