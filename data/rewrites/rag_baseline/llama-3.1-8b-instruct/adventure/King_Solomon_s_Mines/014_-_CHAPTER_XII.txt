As the last wisps of night's darkness receded, a fiery glow spread across the African horizon, casting an otherworldly light upon the landscape. The air vibrated with an expectant hush, as if the very earth itself was holding its breath in anticipation of the dawn. Sir Henry Morton, Good, and Infadoos, accompanied by a small band of warriors, emerged from the shadows, their footsteps echoing off the rocky outcroppings that dotted the terrain. The group navigated the winding path with ease, their knowledge of the land guiding them through the darkness. As the moon re-emerged from its partial eclipse, a brilliant silver streak of light burst forth, illuminating the darkening sky with an ethereal glow.

The sudden radiance cast long shadows across the landscape, and for a moment, the group stood transfixed, drinking in the beauty of the spectacle. As the stars began to fade, the light grew stronger, and they pressed on, their destination a large, flat-topped hill looming in the distance. The hill, known as the "Mountain of the Ancients," was a landmark familiar to the Kukuana people, its horseshoe shape a testament to the geological forces that had shaped the region over millennia.

As they climbed the steep slope, the air grew cooler, and the sounds of the night gave way to an expectant murmur. Reaching the summit, they found themselves amidst a sea of sleeping warriors, their bodies huddled together in a mixture of fear and uncertainty. The men, caught up in the spectacle of the lunar eclipse, had been roused from their slumber by the sudden light, and now stood frozen, their faces upturned to the heavens.

Infadoos led the group through the throng, his eyes scanning the crowd as he searched for the men who had been tasked with gathering their gear. As they reached the center of the camp, they spotted two figures waiting for them, their arms laden with the party's belongings. Among the items was a pair of long-lost trousers, which Good claimed with a look of unbridled joy, donning them with a flourish.

Infadoos smiled wistfully, his eyes crinkling at the corners as he remarked on Good's reluctance to display his "beautiful white legs." The Kukuana warriors, accustomed to Good's modesty, were content to admire his whisker, his piercing eye, and the mobile curve of his smile instead.
