As the last echoes of the departing cortege faded into the mist-shrouded forest, I watched the procession disappear from sight, the rhythmic beat of hooves and wheels gradually giving way to the stillness of the night air. My gaze lingered, reluctant to relinquish the fleeting glimpse of the mystery that had unfolded before us.

It was then that the young stranger stirred, her eyes fluttering open like petals unfolding to greet the sun. I caught only a glimpse of her profile, but her soft voice, like a gentle brook babbling through the trees, whispered out a question: "Where is Mama?" Madame Perrodon's soothing tones and reassuring words wove a gentle melody, calming the young girl's unease.

As the minutes passed, the stranger's memories began to resurface, like the first tender shoots of spring. She asked about her surroundings, her voice laced with a hint of uncertainty, and Madame Perrodon's kindly responses guided her back to the present. The young girl's eyes welled up with tears as she learned of her mother's temporary absence, and I felt a pang of sympathy for this fragile, lost soul.

Mademoiselle De Lafontaine's gentle hand on my arm stayed me from approaching, her voice a whispered warning: "Let her have some time alone, dear one; too much excitement might overwhelm her." I nodded, making a mental note to visit her once she was settled in her new surroundings.

As I sat with my family in our cozy drawing room, the soft glow of candles casting a warm ambiance, my father dispatched a servant to fetch the physician from the nearby village. A bedroom was being prepared for our guest, its furnishings a testament to the castle's rich history: old oak cabinets, crimson velvet cushions, and tapestries depicting scenes of ancient hunts and festive gatherings.
