Her words wove a tapestry of memories, each one a thread of connection that bound them together.

She had grown weary of the superficiality of society in New York, the relentless pursuit of novelty and conformity that left her feeling empty. In contrast, Washington offered a more nuanced landscape, where the complexities of human nature were laid bare, and the masks of propriety were shed. There, she had found a sense of belonging, and a chance to forge a new life for herself and Medora, who had been worn down by the expectations of others.

"But Dr. Carver – isn't he still a concern?" Archer asked, his voice low and cautious.

May's smile was enigmatic. "Dr. Carver's ambitions are nothing more than a passing fancy. He seeks a partner with a generous purse to fund his schemes, and Medora, dear girl that she is, has become a valuable asset in his eyes."

"A convert to what?" Archer pressed, his curiosity piqued.

"His radical ideas about social reform," May replied, her eyes sparkling with interest. "I find them more compelling than the stifling conventions that govern our own lives. It's almost as if we've discovered America only to replicate the same patterns of behavior we left behind."

The question hung in the air, and for a moment, May's eyes seemed to cloud over. Then, with a quiet intensity, she spoke: "I think it's because of you."
