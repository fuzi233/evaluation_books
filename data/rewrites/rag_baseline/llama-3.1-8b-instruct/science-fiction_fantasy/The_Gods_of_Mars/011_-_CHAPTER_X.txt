As I emerged into the outer gardens, the scorching sun cast its unforgiving gaze upon me, illuminating the faces of the gathered crowd. The air was heavy with the scent of blooming flowers and the distant tang of the sea. Xodar, once a formidable warrior, stood at the center of the commotion, surrounded by a cluster of nobles who hurled insults and curses at him with reckless abandon. Their hands moved swiftly, slapping his face, spitting upon his dignity. I watched, a silent observer, as the once-mighty Xodar absorbed the blows with a stoic silence, his eyes cast downward, his face a mask of unyielding resolve.

The crowd's attention shifted towards me, their gazes piercing, their voices raised in a cacophony of wonder and awe. A beautiful woman stepped forward, her voice like music, as she suggested that I, the mysterious stranger, should bind the noble Dator, Thurid. The suggestion was met with a chorus of agreement, and soon, a dozen voices were chanting his name, urging him to face me, to prove himself against the man who had bested Xodar.

As the crowd parted, a figure emerged from the shadows – Thurid, a towering giant, weighed down by an array of resplendent ornaments and arms. His eyes narrowed, his face twisted in a snarl, as he approached us. The air vibrated with tension as he spoke, his voice low and menacing, "What now? What would you of Thurid?" The crowd's voices swirled around him, a maddening torrent of words, until finally, he turned towards Xodar, his eyes flashing with a mixture of anger and contempt. "Calot!" he spat. "Ever did I think you carried the heart of a sorak in your putrid breast. Often have you bested me in the secret councils of Issus, but now in the field of war, where men are truly gauged, your scabby heart hath revealed its sores to all the world. Calot, I spurn you with my foot," and with the words, he raised his foot, ready to strike.

My blood boiled, a slow-burning fire that had been smoldering for minutes, fueled by the injustices heaped upon Xodar. I had no love for him, but the sight of cowardly cruelty stirred something primal within me, a fierce urge to act, to defend the defenseless.
