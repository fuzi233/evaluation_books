The scent of wet earth and damp stone filled the air as Durrance dismounted outside his lodgings, the rhythmic patter of raindrops on the cobblestones echoing through the morning stillness. Two years had passed since he'd received a mysterious summons from Dermod Eustace, drawing him to the Lennon River in Donegal, where he'd encountered the enigmatic Ethne, the master's daughter. Her melodies on the violin lingered in his mind, like a refrain of longing and wonder. The Melusine overture, in particular, had resonated with him, speaking of far-off lands, moonlit seas, and whispers from the desert's edge. The music had been a siren's call, beckoning him toward the unknown, and though Ethne's face had been turned away, he'd felt an inexplicable connection to her artistry.

As he stood beneath the eaves, the memory of that evening flooded back – the lilacs and laburnums swaying in the breeze, Ethne's profile silhouetted against the firelight, her music weaving a spell of mystery and enchantment. He recalled the thought he'd distilled from her playing: true music cannot complain. And yet, as he gazed out into the rain-soaked morning, he couldn't shake the feeling that something was amiss.

At half-past nine, Durrance waited by the familiar clump of flowers, but his friend Harry Feversham failed to appear. This was not the first time they'd missed their appointed meeting, and Durrance's puzzlement deepened. Had he lost his friend, or was it merely a matter of circumstance? The uncertainty hung over him like a mist, obscuring the path ahead.

As the rumors of war intensified, Durrance's thoughts turned to the distant horizon. He'd always been drawn to the unknown, and now, with Feversham's silence and the prospect of conflict looming, the allure of adventure seemed more irresistible than ever. It was against this backdrop that Feversham finally appeared, his face etched with a mix of envy and concern.

"I told you luck might look my way," Durrance said, his voice carrying above the rain's steady beat. "Well, she has. I'm off to Egypt on General Graham's staff. There's talk we may sail down the Red Sea to Suakin."
