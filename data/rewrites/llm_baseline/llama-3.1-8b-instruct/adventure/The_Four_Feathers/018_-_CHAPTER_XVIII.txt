The scent of jasmine wafted through the evening air as Ethne sat with her violin cradled against her knees, its delicate curves glowing softly in the moonlight that filtered through the garden. Her gaze drifted across the silver thread of the path, now lost in the shadows of the trees, and her thoughts lingered on the message that was to come. She had been waiting for this moment, and in her anticipation, the world around her melted away. The soft rustle of leaves and the distant hooting of an owl created a soothing melody that harmonized with the music that still echoed in her mind – the promise of Harry Feversham's words, spoken through the voice of Durrance.

"It was on the eve of my departure into the desert," Durrance began, his voice carrying the weight of longing and nostalgia. Ethne listened intently, her heart beating in time with the words, and she felt a pang of curiosity. What was it about that night that had left such a lasting impression on Durrance?

As she turned to him, her eyes sparkled with interest. "Yes, that was in February, wasn't it? The fifteenth, to be precise. Can you recall the exact day?" Durrance's response was immediate, and Ethne repeated the date to herself, her mind conjuring images of snowflakes gently falling onto the frozen landscape of Glenalla.

"I was there all February," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "But what was I doing on that particular day? It hardly matters now." A shiver ran down her spine as she recalled the sense of disconnection she had felt when listening to Willoughby's tale that morning. Why hadn't she sensed the significance of that meeting, the meeting between Feversham and Durrance on the fifteenth of February? It was only now, in the stillness of the summer evening, surrounded by the sweet fragrance of blooming flowers, that the truth began to unfold.

Remorse washed over her, and she chastised herself for her lack of intuition. If only she had trusted her instincts, she would have known at once. But the message brought no ill tidings; instead, it carried the promise of good news, of a completion that would round out the day in perfect harmony. "Well?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper, her eyes locked on Durrance's face.
