In the spring of 1582-3, a somber, grey sky hung low over the black mountain peaks, its austerity accentuated by the biting March wind that swept across the bog, still bearing the faint stains of winter frost. A lone, windswept knoll stood sentinel, its sparse, leafless oaks reaching towards the sky like skeletal fingers. Beside a desolate pool, the wind whipped the water into a frenzy, as a mournful cry echoed through the air, mingling with the howling gusts.

A group of English soldiers trudged along the bog's edge, their footsteps quiet on the spongy terrain, as they navigated the treacherous landscape. Clad in their standard issue gear, the men moved with a sense of purpose, their arquebuses slung over their shoulders, pikes at the ready. Two captains rode ahead, their contrasting attire a testament to their differing backgrounds and experiences. The taller, battle-hardened captain wore a worn, rust-stained suit of armor, while his companion, resplendent in a gaudy cuirass and helmet, seemed almost incongruous amidst the drab surroundings.

By a cord tied to the wrist of one of the pikemen, a bare-legged Irish kerne trotted alongside, his wild, unkempt hair framing his wary, sullen eyes. As the guide for this expedition, his role was crucial, and his loyalty was being carefully assessed. The stakes were high, for they were on the hunt for the elusive rebel leader, Baltinglas – and the consequences of betrayal would be dire.

As they journeyed, a conversation unfolded between the two captains, their words laced with a mix of camaraderie and tension. The grizzled officer, Amyas, quipped about the unforgiving landscape, to which the more optimistic captain responded with an unyielding passion for the region's untapped potential. "This soil, see, is fat enough," he declared, "if men were here to till it." His brother, Daniel, a skilled assayer, had assured him that the local rocks held secrets similar to those that yielded silver in Peru, fueling the captain's conviction that, with the right leadership, the area could flourish.
