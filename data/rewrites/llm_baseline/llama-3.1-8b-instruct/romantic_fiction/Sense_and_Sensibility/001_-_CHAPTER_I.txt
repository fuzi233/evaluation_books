The sun rose over the rolling hills of Sussex, casting a warm glow over Norland Park, the grand estate that had been home to the Dashwood family for generations. For years, the late Mr. Dashwood, a kind and gentle soul, had lived a comfortable life surrounded by his nephew, Henry, and his family. As the years passed, Henry's family grew, and his children brought joy to the old gentleman's life. He delighted in their laughter and the simple pleasures they shared together – a walk through the gardens, a game of cards by the fire, or a hearty meal in the dining hall.

Henry's first marriage had blessed him with a son, a steady and respectable young man who had inherited a substantial fortune from his mother. This legacy had allowed him to establish himself comfortably, and his subsequent marriage had further augmented his wealth. The inheritance of Norland estate, though significant, was not as crucial to him as it was to his three daughters from his second marriage. Their mother had brought little dowry, and their father's modest means would barely suffice to provide for their basic needs.

The old gentleman's death was met with a mix of emotions. His will, like those of his peers, brought both pleasure and disappointment. He bequeathed Norland to his nephew, but on conditions that diminished its value. Henry had hoped to use the estate to secure a better future for his wife and daughters, but the restrictions placed on it by his uncle's will left him little room for maneuver. The estate's valuable woods were tied up for the benefit of his four-year-old grandson, who had won over his uncle's heart with his endearing antics, imperfect articulation, and boundless energy. As a token of his affection for the girls, he left each of them a thousand pounds.

Henry's initial dismay gradually gave way to a sense of determination. With a cheerful disposition and a sanguine outlook, he knew that with careful management, he could still build a comfortable life for his family. However, fate had other plans. His time was short, and he soon passed away, leaving behind a meager legacy of ten thousand pounds, including the late bequests. His son was summoned as soon as news of his passing reached him, and Henry implored him to care for his mother-in-law and sisters, using all the strength and urgency his illness allowed.
