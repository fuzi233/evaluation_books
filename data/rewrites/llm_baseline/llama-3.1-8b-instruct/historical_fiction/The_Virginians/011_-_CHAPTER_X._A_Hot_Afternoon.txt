As the warm sunlight began to wane, casting a golden glow over Castlewood, General Braddock and the other distinguished guests were shown to their quarters, leaving the young brothers, Harry and George, to retreat to their own room. There, amidst the quiet of the evening, they poured out their thoughts and opinions about the day's events. The news of the proposed marriage between Madam Esmond and Colonel George, a man they barely knew, weighed heavily on their minds. The idea of their mother marrying a man who had been a land-surveyor, and who was significantly younger than herself, seemed preposterous. Harry was adamant that they should speak to their mother about their objections, urging that they return to their family estate and live independently if the union proceeded.

George, however, had a different plan in mind. "We must confront Colonel George," he declared, his eyes shining with conviction. "He may have fought alongside us, but that doesn't mean he's worthy of our mother's hand. We've been wounded, or perhaps even killed, by men who have earned our respect. We must challenge him." Harry's eyes widened as he grasped the wisdom in his brother's words. "Yes, George, you're right. Mother wouldn't marry a man who had hurt one of us. And if we duel him, it's settled."

As they sat in contemplative silence, the sound of the evening wind rustling through the trees outside seemed to carry the whispers of their ancestors, guiding them towards a path of honor and integrity. George, ever the elder statesman, cautioned his brother against hasty actions. "We mustn't insult a guest in our own home," he said, his voice steady and calm. "But we can certainly ride out with him, and then, when we're beyond the park gates, we can express our true feelings."

Harry's face lit up with excitement as he grasped his brother's hand. "By George, you're right! We'll do just that!" Their conversation was interrupted by the soft creaking of the wooden floorboards beneath their feet, and the sound of their own breathing, a reminder of the weight of responsibility that rested on their shoulders. As the night drew in, the brothers' resolve hardened, each determined to defend their family's honor and protect their mother from what they saw as an unsuitable match.
