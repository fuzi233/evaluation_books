Turning back to Eve, I spoke with a voice stripped of its usual defiance. "Mother, the couch beside Lona remains empty. Though I feel unworthy, may I not spend this night in your chamber, buried with the dead? Forgive me my fear, forgive my arrogance, and let me rest among them. I am weary of existing; I crave only sleep."

"The seat by Lona awaits you," she replied softly. "Yet there is a duty remaining before you can rest."

"I am ready," I said.

"How do you know you possess the strength?" she asked, a faint smile playing on her lips.

"Because you demand it," I answered. "Pray, tell me what it is."

Eve turned to Adam. "Husband, is he pardoned?"

"From the depths of my heart," Adam replied.

"Then instruct him."

Adam stepped forward, taking Mara's small hands gently in his. "Come, child. Let us go to the cottage. There, I shall show you what must be done."

As we moved, a sudden, violent gale swept through the courtyard, tearing at the roof with a deafening flap, only to subside abruptly into a low, mournful sigh. Once inside the death-chamber, Adam sat heavily while I stood before him.

"You recall," Adam began, his voice steady despite the gravity of his words, "that after leaving your mother's house, you encountered a dry rock marked by the scars of an ancient waterfall? You climbed it and found a sandy expanse. Go to that same rock now. From its summit, walk deep into the desert. Do not take many steps before lying down and placing your ear in the sand. If you hear the murmur of water beneath, proceed a little further and listen again. If the sound persists, you are moving in the right direction. At intervals of a few yards, stop, lie flat, and hearken. Should you ever find silence, you have strayed; you must scan every direction until the whisper returns. Follow the sound, ensuring you do not retrace your steps. Soon, the noise will grow louder, guiding you to its source—that is the spot you seek."

He handed me a shovel. "Dig there until you reach moisture. Place the hand within, covering it flush with the desert surface, and return. Be vigilant. Carry the hand with infinite care. Never set it down in any place that appears safe; let nothing touch it. Do not stop, do not turn aside for any obstacle attempting to block your path, and never look behind you. Speak to no soul, answer no call, and walk straight ahead. It is still dark, and the morning is far off, yet you must depart immediately."

Adam gave me the hand, heavy and cold, and placed the shovel in my grip. "This is my gardening tool," he noted. "With it, I have cultivated countless beautiful things into the sunlight."

I accepted the burden and stepped out into the crushing night. The air was biting, the darkness absolute. Falling would have been a terror, and the terrain treacherous even in daylight. Yet, the moment I began, I realized I was not abandoned to chaos. A pale, ethereal luminescence erupted from the earth with every step, illuminating exactly where to plant my foot. Through the heather and jagged stones, I traversed the landscape without a single stumble. The ominous burrow was silent; no waves crashed, no heads rose as I passed.

A moon descended, casting a silver path that guided me easily toward the break of dawn. By then, I was nearly across the dried channels of the first river branch, close to Mara's cottage. However, the path narrowed between towering rocks, where I found a figure veiled from head to toe in a mist of moonlight. I pressed onward, ignoring the presence entirely. Suddenly, the figure tossed aside the veil.

"Have you forgotten me already?" the voice echoed—whether the princess or a mimic remained unclear. "Do you not understand that wherever I choose to exist, I do? Take my hand; I am alive just as you!"

I stood on the brink of offering her my left hand, but a sudden thought stilled me. I held my peace and advanced steadily.

"Give me my hand!" she shrieked, her tone shifting to a manic edge. "Or I will tear you limb from limb! You are mine!"

She lunged at me. I shuddered violently but refused to falter. Her form dissolved into the shadows around me, leaving no physical trace.

Following the path, measured by my own weary rhythm, I filled it with the weight of armed men. They emerged from the gloom, and though I cannot say if they parted for me or were mere specters, they turned and pursued. I heard their clanking armor and felt their heavy tread at my heels, yet I cast no glance backward. Their sounds and the metallic clashes faded into the void.

Further along, where the moon hung low and shadows pooled deeply, I spotted a woman seated where the path was too narrow to bypass. Her face was muffled.

"Ah," she breathed. "You have arrived at last! I have waited an hour, perhaps more. You have served well! Your trial is complete. My father sent me to ensure you find some respite on your journey. Give me your charge, and lay your head in my lap. I will guard both of you until the sun rises high. I am not always bitter, nor am I cruel to all men!"

Her words struck with the force of a seductive trap. Exhaustion gnawed at me, and the offer of rest was intoxicating. Could it be true? Had I, through blind adherence to Adam's literal commands and a lack of spiritual vision, trampled the very essence of the Lady of Sorrows beneath my feet? My heart faltered, then hammered against my ribs as if seeking escape.

Yet, my will ironed itself over my trembling heart. My steps did not waver. I bit down hard on my tongue to prevent a slip of speech and continued forward. If Adam had sent her, he could not fault me for refusing to listen. Nor would the Lady of Sorrows resent me for standing firm, even against her influence.

Just moments before reaching the phantom, she peeled away the covering from her face. She was undeniably lovely, a vision of beauty, yet those eyes were not Mara's. No deception could ever truly or permanently mimic them. I moved forward as if she were invisible, and my foot found empty space where she had sat.

I had barely cleared the obstruction when a Shadow materialized before me—I believe it was *The Shadow*. A helmet seemed fused to his skull, but as I approached, I saw it was his actual head, twisted into a grotesque distortion that bore little resemblance to humanity. A cold, dank wind, repulsive as the air of a charnel house, smote me. My knees buckled, joints losing all firmness, my limbs trembling as if ready to collapse into a helpless heap. I thought I passed through him, but now I realize he passed through me. For a fleeting instant, I became one of the damned. Then, a gentle breeze, like the first breath of a newborn spring, washed over me, and before me, the dawn broke.

My path now led past the wide-open door of Mara's cottage. Upon the table inside lay a loaf of bread and a pitcher of water. Within or around the dwelling, there was no howl, no wail.

I ascended the precipice marking the vanished river and ventured deeper into the desert. Finally, after hours of listening in every direction, I pinpointed where the hidden water roared the loudest. Lilith's hand hung heavy around my neck as I began to dig. It was arduous labor; the sand was loose, requiring a vast excavation. Eventually, I heaved up a damp spadeful. I tossed the sexton-tool onto the ridge and laid down the hand. Water began to ooze slowly from beneath her fingers.

I leaped back and hurried to fill the grave. Utterly exhausted, I collapsed beside it and sank instantly into slumber.
