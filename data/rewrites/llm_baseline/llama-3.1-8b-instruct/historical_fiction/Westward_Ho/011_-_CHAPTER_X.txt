As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the quaint town of Bideford, Don Guzman settled into his new quarters, a prisoner on parole, yet one who had grown accustomed to the rhythms of life outside the castle walls. He had been with Richard Grenville for nearly a month now, and the English knight had proven to be a gracious host, treating him with the respect due to his noble birth. So when the Mayor of Bideford, old Salterne, arrived for supper, Don Guzman was not surprised to see the two men engage in conversation, the English custom of dining together seemingly a natural occurrence.

Don Guzman, though perplexed at first by the informality of the gathering, soon found himself drawn into the discussion, exchanging words with the Mayor and his daughter, the Rose of Torridge. As they spoke, he marveled at the Mayor's keen mind, his prudence, and courtesy, qualities he had encountered among the merchant princes of Genoa and Venice. Salterne's questions, though probing, were always respectful, and Don Guzman found himself answering freely, sharing tales of distant lands and exotic spices.

The Mayor's true intentions, however, were not entirely altruistic. A man of business, he sought to unravel the threads of knowledge hidden within his guests, and Don Guzman, a seasoned traveler, was a tantalizing puzzle to unravel. Salterne employed his considerable charm, pouring wine and asking clever questions, but the Spaniard remained cautious, choosing his words carefully, lest he reveal too much about the mysteries he had uncovered during his journeys.

As the evening wore on, the Rose of Torridge joined the conversation, her curiosity getting the better of her, and Don Guzman found himself drawn into a game of wit and words, exchanging stories and laughter with the Mayor and his daughter. Though he did not reveal any great secrets, he did offer glimpses into the world beyond their small town, a world of spice and gold, of ancient civilizations and forgotten lore. And as the night drew to a close, Don Guzman felt grateful for the evening's reprieve, a brief respite from the tedium of captivity, and the chance to engage in a dance of words with these English strangers.
