As the days passed since receiving the letter, Margaret's thoughts swirled like a simmering stew, refusing to settle. Her sister-in-law, Catherine, had scolded her for her foolish notions, and now she felt weighed down by the crushing weight of reality. It was then that Joan Ketel, a stout-hearted woman she had recently befriended, burst into the room, radiating warmth and an idea that would change everything.

"Margaret, I know just the thing to lift your spirits," Joan declared, her eyes sparkling with conviction. "The hermit of Gouda, a wise and gentle soul, possesses a gift – he can see into the future when the mood strikes him." Margaret's heart skipped a beat as she recalled hearing tales of the hermit's remarkable abilities. Joan coaxed her into joining her on a journey to the hermit's cave, and they packed a wicker basket with fresh butter and eggs, carefully arranged on a linen cloth.

Their unlikely friendship had begun several weeks prior, when Margaret had helped Joan care for her ailing husband, Jorian. The doctor had given up all hope, prescribing a series of unpalatable remedies that only seemed to hasten his decline. A boiled chicken broth, a broth infused with the thumb of St. Sergius, and even a poultice made from a precious relic had all failed to bring relief. It was then that Margaret, with her keen eye for observation, spotted a small, fragrant herb growing in the garden outside Jorian's window. She recognized it as sweet feverfew, a remedy known for its healing properties. Gathering a handful of the leaves, she brewed a soothing tea that worked its magic, coaxing Jorian's body back from the brink of death.

With the memory of that miraculous recovery still fresh in her mind, Margaret and Joan set off towards the hermit's cave, the scent of butter and eggs wafting through the air as they walked. Upon arriving, they presented their offering on a small wooden platform, and Margaret, with a sense of trepidation, called out to the hermit, "Holy hermit, we bring you the finest butter and eggs, and I, a humble woman, come seeking guidance. Tell me, is my beloved child alive, and will my husband keep his vows?"
