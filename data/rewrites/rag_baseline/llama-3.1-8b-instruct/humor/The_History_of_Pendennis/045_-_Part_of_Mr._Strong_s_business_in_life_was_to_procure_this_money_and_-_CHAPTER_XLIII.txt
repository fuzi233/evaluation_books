In the labyrinthine quarter of Alsatia, where the Inns of Court and Lincoln's-Inn Fields converge, a peculiar microcosm of London's social hierarchy unfolds. The streets, veiled from the outer world by the somber facades of Wych Street, Holywell Street, and Chancery Lane, conceal a hidden quadrangle – Shepherd's Inn – its entrance guarded by narrow, winding passages and smoky alleys that seem to have been forgotten by the sun. The air is thick with the cacophony of vendors peddling their wares: slop-sellers, brandy-ball hawkers, and purveyors of theatrical prints catering to the youth. Dingy furniture and bedding, more suited to a restless night's sleep, line the walls, alongside dealers in theatrical prints and ballad-singers who chant satirical songs against the ruling elite.

It is here, amidst the ceaseless din and life, that one finds the offices of the Polwheedle and Tredyddlum Copper Mines, the Registry of Patent Inventions, and the Union of Genius and Capital Company. Amidst this eclectic assortment of businesses, Mr. Campion's chambers stand out, their presence marked by a gleaming cab-horse and a gaudy harness adorned with heraldic bearings. As his vehicle halts before the door, the horse tosses its head, sending froth flying from its nostrils, while the groom's lustrous whites and the cab's shiny brass create a fleeting moment of radiance in this otherwise gloomy setting.

Captain Costigan, a familiar figure in this quarter, often spends his mornings lounging in the quadrangle, observing the comings and goings with a discerning eye. He would often stroll over to the porter's lodge, where he would engage Mrs. Bolton in conversation, reminiscing about the theatres and his daughter Leedy Mirabel. A veteran of the stage, Mrs. Bolton had danced at the Wells in her youth, part of Mr. Serle's esteemed troupe. Meanwhile, Costigan resides in the upper floors of No. 4, a suite previously occupied by the late Mr. Podmore, whose name still adorns the door – a testament to the transience of life in this ever-changing neighborhood. Charley Podmore, the celebrated tenor, T.R.D.L., would often perform at the nearby theatres, his melodious voice entrancing audiences.
