Cissister Village,

Gloucestershire,

England.



February 15th.

Dearest Ginger,

It's been three long months since I last wrote to you, and I daresay you'll think me a fragile creature if I haven't yet shaken off the lingering effects of my troubles. Though I'd like to claim victory, I must confess that the shadows still linger, but I've learned to keep them tucked away in a hidden corner of my mind. It's progress, I suppose.

I should regale you with tales of London, but the city's familiarity has lulled me into a state of complacency. It's as if I've lived here for years, watching the world go by without truly noticing its rhythms. You'll see from the address that Mr. Faucitt's estate sale remains pending. A wealthy gentleman with a discerning eye and a penchant for whiskers has been frequenting our lunches, and I suspect a deal is near. Mr. Faucitt yearns to escape the city's din and settle into a simpler life, surrounded by nature. He's grown disillusioned with London, finding it unrecognizable from the place he knew thirty years ago. The city's periodic transformations leave him disoriented, like Rip Van Winkle awakening to a strange new world. His initial shock came when he discovered the Empire Theatre had become a music hall, followed by the demolition of the Tivoli, and the transformation of the Rupert Street bakery into a dressmaker's shop. A fleeting magenta fog brought a momentary respite, reminding him of the city's enduring essence.

At Laurette et Cie., I'm kept occupied, thank goodness. I've taken on a role akin to yours at Fillmore Nicholas Theatrical Enterprises Ltd., serving as a sort of right-hand woman. I greet clients with a warm smile, often under false pretenses, and hand them over to the staff to handle the actual work. While I wouldn't mind continuing in this manner, Mr. Faucitt remains resolute in his decision to sell. It's a peculiar aspect of the English character – the longing to own a rural retreat, to return to a simpler, more rustic existence. Their hearts yearn for a place like Cissister, where I recently accompanied Mr. Faucitt on a visit. The rolling hills, old stone houses, and lush greenery transport me to a world of serenity.
