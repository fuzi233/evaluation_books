As Palm Sunday dawned over the olive groves of the Valdarno, the morning sun cast its golden rays upon the terracotta rooftops of Florence, illuminating the city's ancient stones. The air was alive with the sweet scent of blooming olives and the soft murmur of prayers rising from the churches. In the heart of the city, the piazzas were abuzz with activity, as families prepared for the traditional feast that marked the end of Lent. 

In the kitchens of the convent of San Marco, Brother Matteo stirred a massive cauldron of ribollita, the hearty soup that was a staple of Florentine cuisine during this time of year. The aroma of onions, vegetables, and bread wafted through the corridors, mingling with the incense from the chapel. As the monks prepared for the evening sermon, the sense of anticipation grew, for it was said that Fra Girolamo Savonarola would deliver a message of hope and renewal to the faithful.

But amidst the tranquil atmosphere, a sense of unease simmered just beneath the surface. The Compagnacci, a group of wealthy merchants and nobles, had grown increasingly hostile towards Savonarola and his followers, the Piagnoni. Their resentment had been building for months, fueled by the friar's uncompromising stance on reform and his criticism of the city's elite. 

As the day wore on, the tension escalated, and by the time the sun began to set, the city was ablaze with passion and fury. The Compagnacci, egged on by their leader, Lorenzo de' Medici, stormed the church of San Marco, intent on disrupting the evening sermon. The Piagnoni, determined to protect their beloved leader, stood firm, and the confrontation quickly turned into a violent affray. 

Pandemonium erupted as the crowd surged towards the convent, demanding that Savonarola be handed over to the authorities. The monks, armed and determined, fought back, and the sound of clashing steel echoed through the streets. The clash of swords, the crash of stones, and the scent of smoke filled the air as the battle raged on.
