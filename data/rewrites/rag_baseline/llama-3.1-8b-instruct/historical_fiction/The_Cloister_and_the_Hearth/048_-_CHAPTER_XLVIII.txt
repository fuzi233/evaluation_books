As Catherine sat amidst the warm glow of the hearth, her thoughts were consumed by the impending arrival of her son Gerard. Only a month, perhaps a week, remained before he would return home, and her mind was beset by the weight of uncertainty. How would she break the news to him that she had neglected to keep watch over his betrothed, the young woman whose fate hung precariously in the balance? The uncertainty sometimes took on a sickening form, a nagging dread that threatened to consume her.

"Oh, Kate," she whispered to herself, "what if she has made some rash decision?" The words echoed through her mind like a mournful sigh. Her daughter, Margaret, appeared beside her, concern etched on her face. "Mother, she would never be so foolish," she said softly.

Catherine's eyes clouded, her gaze drifting to the past. She recalled the countless times young women, desperate and headstrong, had leapt into the canals, driven by passion or despair. "My lass, you don't know what reckless fools young women can be," she said, her voice heavy with sorrow. "They're like moths drawn to flame, helpless against their own desires. And then there are those who suffer from the turmoil of their emotions, their feelings as mercurial as the wind. They'll jump into the water without a second thought, leaving their families to pick up the pieces."

Margaret's eyes widened in alarm. "Mother!"

Catherine's gaze snapped back to the present, her expression a mixture of pain and wisdom. "Men, too, can be monsters, creating these artificial canals that tempt women to their destruction. They know we won't take our own lives, that we value our flesh and blood above all else. But a jump into the canal is like slipping into bed – it's a choice, a surrender to the water's gentle touch. I remember when I was younger, full of life and passion, I once considered ending my own life in just such a way. I had quarreled with my husband, Eli, and was consumed by anger and hurt. I stood at the edge of our canal, the water lapping at my toes, and thought of the sweet revenge I'd exact on him if I were to leave this world behind."

Margaret's voice was barely above a whisper. "Nay, mother, I'll never believe it of you."
