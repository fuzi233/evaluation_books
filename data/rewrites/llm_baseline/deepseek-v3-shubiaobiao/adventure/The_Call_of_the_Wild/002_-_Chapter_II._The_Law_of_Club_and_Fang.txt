Buck’s first day on the Dyea shore was a plunge into savagery. Gone was the sunlit idleness of Judge Miller’s estate—here, survival demanded claws and cunning. The air hummed with violence, every snarl a promise of blood. These weren’t the tamed curs of civilization; these were wolves in harness, governed by a single brutal law: *Kill or be killed.*

The lesson came swift and brutal. Curly, ever friendly, approached a husky with a wagging tail. In a flash, teeth gleamed—a slash, a recoil—and her face split open. Before Buck could react, the pack swarmed, a writhing mass of fur and fury, burying Curly’s shrieks beneath their weight. Spitz, the white-furred demon, laughed with a lolling tongue. The men clubbed the dogs back, but too late. Curly lay in crimson snow, her body a ruin.

*So this was the way of it.* No second chances. No mercy. Buck’s muscles coiled tight. He’d never fall.

Then came the harness, rough straps biting into his fur. A sled dog now, hauling like a common mule. François’ whip taught obedience; Dave’s teeth corrected missteps. Spitz, the lead dog, snarled commands, yanking the traces to steer him. Yet Buck learned fast—*ho* meant halt, *mush* meant run—and the sled became an extension of his will.

New dogs joined—Billee, a whimpering fool; Joe, a snapping storm of teeth; Sol-leks, a one-eyed veteran who tore Buck’s shoulder for approaching his blind side. That night, the cold gnawed his bones. The tent spat him out, and the snow hid no warmth—until he stumbled upon Billee curled in a hollow. A stolen trick: Buck dug his own den, and survival grew easier.

Dawn revealed a world remade. The team lunged into the wilderness, glaciers yawning beneath their paws. Buck’s hunger sharpened. A meager ration of fish vanished too quick; soon, he stole like the others, swift and silent. Pike’s thievery taught him—bacon disappeared, and clumsy Dub took the blame. Morality was a luxury; hunger was law.

His body hardened. Muscles turned to iron, paws cracked ice without flinching. His senses sharpened—wind spoke of storms, the faintest snap of twigs betrayed danger. By night, his howl echoed ancestors long dust, their wildness singing in his blood.

Men called it progress. Buck knew better. It was an awakening—a shedding of softness. The North had whispered, and he answered with tooth and claw.

For gold had lured men north. And men, in turn, had unleashed the beast in Buck.
