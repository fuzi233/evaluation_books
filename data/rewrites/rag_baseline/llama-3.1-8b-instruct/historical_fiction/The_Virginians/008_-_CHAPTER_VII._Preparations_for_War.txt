The silence in Castlewood was oppressive, a heavy blanket that smothered all sound. Harry walked through the empty streets, his footsteps echoing off the trees. He had received a letter from his brother George, full of news from the north. The war was coming, and George was preparing to join the fight. Harry felt a pang of unease, a sense of disconnection from the world beyond their small community.

He thought of his brother's words, spoken in jest but tinged with a hint of envy. Harry loved the music of the soldiers' fifes and drums, the way it stirred something deep within him. He longed to join the ranks, to feel the rush of battle and the camaraderie of comrades. But duty bound him to Castlewood, to the quiet routine of life as a farmer.

As he walked, the sounds of the forest grew louder – birds singing, leaves rustling in the breeze. It was a soothing melody, one that calmed his nerves and reminded him of the beauty in the world. He felt a sense of peace settle over him, a sense of belonging to this place and its rhythms.

Back at the farm, his mother was busy in the kitchen, preparing dinner. The smell of roasting meat wafted through the air, mingling with the scent of baking bread. Harry's stomach growled in anticipation, and he made his way to the table. His mother smiled at him, her eyes warm with affection.

"News from George?" she asked, as he sat down.

Harry nodded, taking a seat beside her. "He's joining the war effort," he said, his voice low.

His mother's expression turned serious. "I know," she said. "I've heard rumors. But we'll manage here, Harry. We always do."

The conversation turned to the practicalities of farm life, the tasks that needed to be done and the crops that needed tending. Harry listened intently, feeling a sense of contentment wash over him. This was his life, and he was grateful for it.

Outside, the forest continued to sing its gentle song, a reminder of the beauty and simplicity of the world. Harry felt his worries fade away, replaced by a sense of peace and belonging. He knew that he would do his part, as a son and a farmer, to support his family and community through the trials ahead.
