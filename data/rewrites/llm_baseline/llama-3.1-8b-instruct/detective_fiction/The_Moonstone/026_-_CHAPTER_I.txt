As I sit in my small kitchen, surrounded by the simple pleasures of Brittany, I am reminded of the habits of order and regularity instilled in me by my dear parents, who are now gone. In the quiet hours of the morning, I begin each day by folding my clothes with precision, just as I did when I was a child. My fingers move deftly, smoothing out wrinkles and creases, as if performing a ritual. It's a habit that has remained with me through the years, a connection to a time when life was simpler.

Before folding, I take a moment to record the events of the previous day in my diary. The act of writing helps me process the world around me, and I find solace in the discipline of putting thoughts and feelings into words. As I write, the scent of freshly baked bread wafts from the oven, mingling with the sea air that carries the whispers of the Atlantic.

My life has taken a different turn since then. Gone are the carefree days of childhood, replaced by the weight of responsibility and the ache of loss. Yet, in these moments of solitude, I find comfort in the familiar rhythms of my daily routine. The diary remains a constant companion, a testament to the lessons learned from my parents and the importance of recording one's experiences.

A letter arrived yesterday, bearing news from England. Mr. Franklin Blake, a distant relative, has reached out, seeking my assistance in recounting a long-forgotten event – the scandal surrounding the Moonstone. He offers compensation, but it comes with a price: reopening wounds that had begun to heal. The prospect of revisiting painful memories is daunting, yet I feel an obligation to share my part in the story.

As I prepare to embark on this journey, I am reminded of the value of my diary. Without its entries, I fear I would not be able to recall the details of that fateful visit to Aunt Verinder's house in London. Every moment, no matter how insignificant, was recorded, and now it is time to share those stories. My commitment to truth remains unwavering, even as I navigate the complexities of reliving the past. I will recount everything, from the smallest particulars to the largest, without fear of consequence or favor. The lines between fact and opinion will remain clear, and I trust that Mr. Blake will respect the integrity of my narrative.
