A STRANGE HOSTESS

The moon cast an ethereal glow over the rolling hills as I traversed the winding path, her silvery light illuminating the delicate patterns of the dew-kissed grass. It was a night much like any other, yet the full moon seemed to hold a secret, a subtle smile that danced across her face as she dipped below the horizon. In the fleeting moment before her disappearance, a vision appeared within her disk – a small, rustic cottage with its door and window ajar, beckoning me to enter.

As the night deepened and the shadows grew longer, I decided to bide my time, finding solace in the quiet of the nearby ravine. I settled into the soft earth, the remnants of the fruit the children had given me earlier serving as a humble supper. The stars above twinkled like diamonds scattered across the velvet expanse, and I felt my eyelids growing heavy. Sleep claimed me soon after, my dreams infused with the scent of wild herbs and the distant hum of nocturnal creatures.

My slumber was short-lived, however, as I was abruptly roused by a presence. A figure sat atop a nearby outcropping, its back turned towards me. The darkness seemed to amplify its size, casting an air of mystery. As it shifted its head, I caught my first glimpse of its profile, its features softened by the moon's gentle light.

"Will you not join me?" a melodious voice asked, its sweetness a stark contrast to the unease that had begun to creep over me. I hesitated, weighing the comfort of my current surroundings against the unknown hospitality offered.

"I thank you," I replied, "but I'm quite content here. Where would you have me go?"

"There's no harm in the open air," she countered, "but the creatures that roam these lands at night are not ones you'd want as companions."

"I've not been bothered," I said, trying to sound confident.

"No, I've been keeping watch over you since you settled in," she replied, her back still turned towards me. "I see poorly in the daylight, but at night, the world is crystal clear. The shadow of my home would have hidden you, but its doors were open, and I chanced upon you from afar."

Intrigued, I asked, "How did you come to know I was here?"
