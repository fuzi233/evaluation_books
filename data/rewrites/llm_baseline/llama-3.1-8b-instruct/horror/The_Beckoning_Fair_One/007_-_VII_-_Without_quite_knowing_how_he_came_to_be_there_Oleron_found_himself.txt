Oleron descended the stairs, the worn treads creaking beneath his feet. He was bareheaded, the cool night air ruffling his hair as he stepped out into the darkness. A hazy memory began to stir, and he recalled leaving the candle burning on the table, slipping out into the night without a word. The door had closed softly behind him, and he'd made no noise, but the sudden chill of the evening air still lingered on his skin.

As he emerged into the square, the flickering gas lamps cast eerie shadows on the pavement. Oleron paused, his eyes drawn to the faint glow of light behind the blind in his flat. He'd left the window open, and the gentle breeze carried the scent of roasting meats wafting from the kitchen below. His stomach growled in response, and he felt a pang of hunger.

He turned away from the tempting aromas, his gaze drifting towards the small public house on the corner. The sign creaked in the wind, reading "The Golden Roast." Oleron had never ventured inside, but tonight he pushed open the door and stepped across the threshold. The barkeep, a stout woman with a kind face, greeted him with a nod as he slid a shilling across the counter.

"A dram of your finest brandy, please," he said, his voice low and rough from disuse.

The woman poured a generous measure into a waiting glass, and Oleron tossed it back, feeling the burn spread through his chest. He ordered another, and another, lost in the warm, golden liquid. As he sipped, he pondered the task ahead – collecting his belongings from the flat, navigating the treacherous staircase, and facing the unsettling memories that lingered within those walls.

The public house was quiet, the patrons huddled in the far corner, their voices muted. Oleron sat alone at the bar, his thoughts consumed by the prospect of returning to his old life. He'd lived with Mrs. Jenkins, his former landlady, for years, and the thought of seeking refuge with her again brought a sense of comfort. But for now, he'd stay here, lost in the warmth and the brandy, until the dawn broke.
