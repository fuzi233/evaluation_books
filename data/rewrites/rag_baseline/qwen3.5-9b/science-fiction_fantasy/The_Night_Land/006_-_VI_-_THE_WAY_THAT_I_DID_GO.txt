The path ahead demanded everything I possessed. I forced my gaze forward, refusing to glance backward, anchoring my resolve in a singular purpose: either reach the Maiden lost in the World's darkness or face my own inevitable end. With this clarity, I pressed onward, carrying the heavy weight of leaving my mighty Home behind. I felt the tender, terrible wisdom of the rules that had governed us, a curbed affection that now felt like a severing cord.

My heart warmed toward Master Monstruwacan for the honor of his descent. In armor, he joined the Full Watch to bolster my spirit at this critical juncture. Around me, the night's aether churned, vibrating with the chaotic thoughts and blind wishes of the millions I had just abandoned. As I steadied my emotions, the air shifted. It became unnervingly cold, tasting sharp and metallic on my tongue, filling my lungs with a substance denser than the thin atmosphere of the Thousandth City. This was the air of the deep fields, varied and strange, capable of charming one soul while sorrowing another, provided one wandered with reason as a companion.

I walked forward, a tapestry woven from new thoughts, old memories, and breaking wonders. Doubt and fear lingered, but I acknowledged them. To step onto the outward earth of the Night Land, even from this proximity, was a sensation of profound strangeness—a realization that would seem quaint to those living in the present day. Soon, I reached the Circle surrounding the Redoubt. It was smaller than my calculations suggested; a mere tube of glass, perhaps two inches thick, yet radiating a brilliance that deceived the eye into thinking it was massive.

This small aperture held a lifetime of memory. As a child, I had watched from the embrasures, hoping to be the first to spot a monster peering inward at the Mighty Pyramid. We played a game of identification, seeking the ugliest, most horrific beast to claim victory, unaware that our joy stemmed from the safety of the light keeping them at bay. Now, standing alone, those childhood games resurfaced. Memories of specific horrors flooded my mind, triggering a reflexive flinch. I hesitated, frozen by the sudden imagination of what might emerge from the dark beyond.

Only after releasing this tension did I summon the courage to turn fully toward the Home of the Last Millions. The sight stole the breath from me. It was a mountain of metal and life, rising measurelessly into the night, spreading its bulk across the horizon to encompass the entire world. On its nearest slopes, three hundred thousand embrasures gleamed; in total, a million and twenty thousand windows lined the four sides of the fortress. Half a mile up began the lowest tier, followed by countless levels ascending into the infinite dark. A great light poured from them, separating each opening into distinct points of luminescence until they merged into a single, shimmering peak piercing the heavens.

I realized then that the tiny clusters against the lower embrasures were the countless millions of the Redoubt. They were visible as insect-like specks, yet I knew they were staring back at me through spy-glasses. I lifted my gaze up the grey slope of metal, past the glimmering tiers, to the final point where the tower crowned the world. From within that observation deck, Master Monstruwacan bent his great lens upon me—the instrument through which I had so often spied. I raised my Diskos high, a silent salute and farewell, though I saw no face to greet it.

My heart swelled, strengthening my soul despite the emotion. Suddenly, a murmur rippled through the night, distant and indistinct. Looking down, I saw the shapes in the lower embrasures moving in unison. The multitudes had taken my gesture; they cried out, waving farewells or pleas to return. Standing there, a solitary figure beneath a mountain of steel and life, I felt the crushing isolation of my situation. Danger lurked in realizing my plight, so I raised the Diskos again, reversing it to signal to all the millions below. Then, I looked swiftly upward through eight miles of night toward the Final Light, ensuring my friend beyond the sight knew I thought of him in this last moment. Perhaps the invisible millions higher up interpreted this too as a farewell; a faint, vague wind of sound drifted down from the monstrous heights.

Lowering the Diskos, I turned away. I stepped across the Circle, breasting the strange resistance of the Air Clog, and entered the lonesomeness of the Night Land. I refused to look back; the sight of my Home weakened my resolve, and I determined not to glance behind for a long time. Yet, the aether continued to surge around me, carrying the prayers, wishes, and perpetual watching of my people and kin. This connection offered a fleeting sense of companionship, but it carried a peril: the disturbance signaled to some Evil Force that I was abroad. How could I stop it? If I explained the danger to the masses, their collective focus would create an even greater disturbance, rendering them powerless to act anyway.

At first, I walked blindly, intent only on creating distance to soothe the ache in my heart. But soon, I slowed, applying reason to my stride. I concluded that the area trodden by the Youths was likely under intense surveillance. So, I altered my course. Instead of heading directly north, I veered northwest, intending to circle the back of the North-West Watcher before reaching the Plain of Blue Fire. Only then would I seek a straight path north, avoiding the House of Silence, which terrified me more than any other horror in the Land. This detour lengthened my journey, but survival demanded caution over haste.

My assurance in heading north stemmed from an internal knowing and studies conducted within the Pyramid. These included a strange, ancient book of metal found forgotten in the Great Library, possibly buried for a million years. Its contents were largely dismissed as fairy tales by my contemporaries, yet I perceived a kernel of ancient truth beneath the shell of disbelief. The book recounted a cataclysm from a time vastly removed from our own: a monstrous earthquake that rent the world for a thousand miles, swallowing a great ocean into a yawning chasm. The resulting steam tore the earth apart, followed by mists and rains recorded in histories as mere quaint stories for students.

However, this particular text differed. Written with a grave authority suggesting eyewitness testimony, it claimed these events were truth. It described a valley emerging from the west, stretching a thousand miles, with sides a hundred miles deep. The sun hung low in the western end, casting a red gloom, while the bottom held great seas, strange beasts, and poisonous vapors. I reasoned that in a bygone eternity, when the world was still light, such an earthquake had occurred, bursting the crust along a weak curve and allowing an ocean to fall into the earth's furnace. The immediate reaction was steam and tearing rock, followed by the mist and rain.

The book's conclusion spoke of a later age, when the sun neared its dying and the upper earth grew cold and uninhabitable. By then, the chasm had settled into a deep, wondrous valley holding seas, hills, forests, and places of fire and sulfur. In this renewed primal world, nature bred great beasts unseen by ordinary eyes, creatures suited to the warmth of the depths. While the book's language was constrained and difficult, I translated its essence into my own understanding, preparing myself for the realities that awaited in the dark.
