The doctrine of the Circles distills itself into a singular, absolute maxim: "Attend to your Configuration." Whether applied to politics, ecclesiastical hierarchy, or moral conduct, their entire pedagogy serves the refinement of individual and collective shape, subordinating all other concerns to the perfection of the Circle. It is the supreme achievement of the Circles to have eradicated the ancient heresy that conduct stems from will, effort, training, or praise. Instead, they preach that Pantocyclus—the illustrious Circle who quelled the Colour Revolt—first revealed the truth: Configuration *is* the man. If you are born an Isosceles with unequal sides, you are destined for error unless corrected at the Isosceles Hospital; if you are a Triangle, Square, or Polygon bearing irregularity, you require the Regular Hospitals to cure your deformity, lest you meet the State Prison or the executioner's blade. Every fault, from petty misconduct to flagrant crime, is attributed solely to a deviation from perfect Regularity, perhaps caused by a childhood collision, improper exercise, or a sudden shift in temperature altering the frame. Thus concludes the illustrious philosopher: neither good nor bad conduct warrants praise or blame, for why admire a Square's integrity when one should only marvel at the precision of his angles? Why condemn a thieving Isosceles when the tragedy lies in his incurable asymmetry?

Yet, this theoretical purity possesses a jagged practical edge. When an Isosceles magistrate faces a plea of theft rooted in unavoidable unevenness, the reply is swift: precisely because he is a nuisance, he must be consumed. However, in the delicate theater of domestic life, where death is off the table, the doctrine falters. I confess that when one of my Hexagonal grandsons excuses his disobedience by blaming a thermal shock upon his perimeter, demanding I attribute the fault to his Configuration—which requires nourishment from the choicest sweetmeats—I find myself trapped. Logically, I cannot reject his premise; practically, I cannot accept it. Consequently, I adopt a compromise: I assume that a stern scolding possesses a latent, strengthening influence on his geometry, though I possess no empirical evidence for this belief. I am certainly not unique in this evasion; many high-ranking Circles, serving as judges, employ praise and blame with fervor, treating "right" and "wrong" as tangible entities. Within their households, they scold their children with the same vehemence, acting as if human figures truly possess the agency to choose between these fictions.

In doing so, the Circles invert the commandment governing parent-child relations in Spaceland. Where Spaceland teaches children to honor their parents, Flatland teaches a man to honor his grandson—or failing that, his son. Here, "honor" signifies not indulgence, but a reverent submission to their highest interests. The state doctrine dictates that fathers must subordinate their own needs to posterity, thereby securing the welfare of the State alongside their lineage.

However, the system's fatal flaw resides in its relationship with Women. Since irregular births threaten the social ascent, no woman with irregular ancestry may wed a man seeking elevation. While male irregularity is measurable, women appear visibly Regular due to their straight lines; thus, the State maintains meticulously guarded pedigrees to detect their "invisible" irregularities. Without certification, marriage is forbidden. Paradoxically, the higher a Circle ascends, the less scrupulous he becomes regarding his wife's lineage. An aspiring Isosceles fears a wife with even a single irregular ancestor; a Square or Pentagon checks only five generations; a Hexagon or Dodecagon is nearly careless. Yet, a Circle may deliberately marry a woman descended from an irregular great-grandfather, seduced merely by a superior lustre or a low, charming voice—a trait we prize above all others in females. These unions inevitably yield sterility or diminished sides, yet the loss of a few angles in a high-order Polygon goes unnoticed, often repaired by operations in the Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium. The Circles, prone to viewing infertility as a sign of superior evolution, fail to arrest this decay. Unless checked, the gradual erosion of the Circular class will accelerate until the race can no longer produce a Chief Circle, causing the Constitution of Flatland to collapse.

A further peril lurks in our treatment of women, decreed three centuries ago by the Chief Circle. Deemed deficient in reason but abundant in emotion, women were stripped of mental education, barred from reading, and denied arithmetic sufficient to count the angles of their kin. The result has been a generational decline in their intellectual power, a policy of enforced quietism that persists today. My deepest dread is that this well-intentioned suppression has backfired upon the Male Sex. We now live a bifurcated existence: with women, we speak of love, duty, pity, and hope—irrational fictions designed to curb feminine exuberance; among ourselves, we utilize a cold vocabulary of necessity, fitness, and anticipated benefit. We perform utter deference before women, convincing them that we adore the Chief Circle no more than they do, while secretly regarding them as "mindless organisms." Furthermore, our theology shifts entirely within the women's chambers.

This dual training places an unbearable strain on the youth. At age three, boys are torn from maternal care to unlearn the language of emotion and adopt the idiom of science. Already, I perceive a weakening in the grasp of mathematical truth among the current generation compared to the robust intellect of our ancestors three hundred years prior. I need not elaborate on the catastrophic risk if a woman were to surreptitiously learn to read and disseminate the truths of logic to her sex, nor on the danger of an infant boy revealing the secrets of dialectics to his mother. On the sole ground of the enfeeblement of the male intellect, I humbly appeal to the highest Authorities to reconsider the regulations of female education.
