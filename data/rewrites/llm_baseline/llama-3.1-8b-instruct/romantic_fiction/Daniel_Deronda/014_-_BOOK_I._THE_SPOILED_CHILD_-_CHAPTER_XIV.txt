Yesterday's doubts seemed to have dissolved, leaving behind only the gentle ripples on the surface of a tranquil lake. Her thoughts were already racing ahead, envisioning herself gliding effortlessly through the roving archery match in Cardell Chase, her movements fluid and carefree, like a wood nymph dancing under the ancient beech trees. The prospect of Grandcourt's approach, though not the ardent passion of a Daphnis for his beloved, held a certain allure, one that only added to her excitement.

With each passing moment, Gwendolen felt herself drawn closer to the realization of her desires. She foresaw the day unfolding like a tapestry, with Grandcourt's declaration of love woven into the fabric, and her response a gentle encouragement, guided by the rational conclusion she had shared with her uncle. As she broke her fast, a letter lay on her plate, one that brought a smile to her lips, and she handed it to her mother, who returned it with a warm smile, her eyes shining with renewed hope.

"You're not feeling the call of distant horizons?" her mother asked, her voice tinged with a hint of wistfulness.

"Not quite that far," Gwendolen replied, her voice light and carefree.

"It's a shame we didn't hear from you sooner. Can't you write now, before we depart this morning?"

Gwendolen hesitated, her expression faltering for a moment before she responded brusquely, "It's not pressing. Tomorrow will suffice. We'll be in Dover by then, and I need to send a letter to them."

Her mother's eyes sparkled with understanding, and she asked, "Would you like me to write for you, dear, if it troubles you?"

Gwendolen's gaze drifted away, her voice barely above a whisper, "No, it's fine. I'll write tomorrow." But as she spoke, a fleeting look of tenderness crossed her face, and she reached out to touch her mother's hand, saying with a playful smile, "Mamma, you're not old, you're still young at heart."
