In the verdant depths of Combe Valley, nestled between Stow and Chapel, a lone figure sat ensconced within the rustic walls of the farm-house beside the mill. Rose, a woman of striking beauty and sharp wit, seethed with frustration, confined to this remote corner of the world while the bustling town of Bideford unfolded its grandeur without her. Though her uncle and aunt, though wary of her associations with the enigmatic Eustace Leigh, a gentleman's son with ties to the local gentry, allowed him to visit frequently, ostensibly for mundane errands. Rose, ever the manipulator, skillfully wove a web of charm, ensuring her aunt remained present during their conversations, thereby safeguarding her own desires.

Eustace, aware of the formidable barrier posed by their differing creeds, chose a cautious approach. Rather than attempt to proselytize, he focused on establishing a rapport through commerce, purchasing goods from the mill and distributing them to the elderly women of Moorwinstow, who, in turn, commended his character as a pious young man. After consulting with Campian and Parsons, he devised a plan to attend church services with them, an act that, on the surface, appeared a gesture of goodwill. Yet, unbeknownst to Eustace, the congregation viewed his actions with skepticism, whispering among themselves that he had merely converted for the sake of Rose Salterne, the young Protestant maiden.

Meanwhile, the two Jesuits, masters of duplicity, played a delicate game of deception. While outwardly conforming to the status quo, they secretly plotted the downfall of their enemies. Since April, they had been navigating the complexities of England, waiting for the signal from Ireland that would set in motion the "rising of the West," a movement aimed at overthrowing the usurper, Elizabeth, who had usurped the throne and claimed the title of Queen of England. Convinced that the hearts of the English people lay with them, they were resolute in their conviction, disregarding the lessons of experience, as they had done throughout the centuries.
