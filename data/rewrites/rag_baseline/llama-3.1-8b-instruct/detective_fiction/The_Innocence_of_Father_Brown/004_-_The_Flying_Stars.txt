The Flying Stars


In the verdant heart of the countryside, a quaint village nestled among the rolling hills, its rustic charm still clinging to the fringes of modernity. It was Christmas Eve, and the crisp winter air carried the scent of roasting chestnuts and woodsmoke, transporting all who breathed it in to a bygone era. For Flambeau, a seasoned connoisseur of the art of deception, this was the perfect setting for his final, and most intricate, crime.

As he would often recount, his greatest achievement was not just the complexity of the plan, but the deliberate choice of location – a picturesque estate near the sleepy town of Putney, complete with a stately home, a carriage drive, and a tranquil garden adorned with a majestic monkey tree. It was a scene straight out of a Dickens novel, and Flambeau took great pride in his ability to craft a crime worthy of the great author's pen.

From the outside, the events that transpired were nothing short of inexplicable. The drama began on Boxing Day, when a young woman emerged from the house, her figure shrouded in a thick cloak, her face partially obscured by a veil of brown furs. Her eyes, however, sparkled with a warmth that belied the chill of the winter air. As she scattered bread for the birds, her gaze wandered to the adjacent stable, its wooden slats weathered to a soft silver, and the laurel-lined walkway that led to the garden beyond.

With a quiet reverence, she descended into the shadows of the evergreen plantation, where the scent of pine and earth filled the air. It was there that she chanced upon a sight that left her breathless – a young man perched atop the high garden wall, his dark hair standing on end, his features illuminated by the fading light of day. His sallow complexion seemed almost otherworldly, and the bright red tie he wore seemed a jarring note in an otherwise somber palette.

Undeterred by her warning, he sprang to the ground with an agility that belied his awkward appearance, landing with a thud that sent a faint tremor through the frosty air. With a nonchalant air, he declared, "I think I was meant to be a burglar," his words dripping with a quiet conviction.
