As I stepped out of the doctor's house, the warm sunlight streaming through the open doorway danced across the face of Ezra Jennings, Mr. Candy's assistant, making his gaunt features seem even more pronounced. His gypsy complexion, sunken cheeks, and dreamy eyes created an unsettling contrast, one that seemed to tug at my sympathies despite my initial reservations. It was as if his very presence was a whispered secret, urging me to listen to the tale he had to share.

"Are you headed in the same direction, Mr. Jennings?" I asked, noticing he held his hat in his hand. "I'm off to visit my aunt, Mrs. Ablewhite."

Ezra replied that he had a patient to attend to, and we were walking in the same direction. As we strolled down the sidewalk, I caught sight of the cheerful maid, who had greeted me warmly upon my departure, exchange a discreet message with Ezra. Her pursed lips and averted gaze spoke volumes about their complicated relationship. It seemed Ezra was not well-liked within the household, a notion reinforced by Betteredge's earlier warning.

As we descended the doctor's doorstep, I couldn't help but think that Ezra's life must be a difficult one. His silence during our ascent seemed to beckon me to take the lead in conversation, and I obliged, eager to discuss the doctor's condition.

"By the change I've witnessed," I began, "Mr. Candy's illness appears to have been more severe than I initially thought."

"It's nothing short of a miracle he survived," Ezra said, his voice laced with a hint of awe.

"Has his memory improved at all since his recovery? He's been trying to communicate with me," I ventured.

"His recollection of past events is hazy at best," Ezra replied. "Though he vaguely recalls planning and intentions, the specifics remain elusive. He's acutely aware of his limitations and struggles to conceal them from others. If only he could erase the past entirely, he'd be a happier man. Perhaps we all would, if we could forget our troubles so easily."

"There are moments in our lives, aren't there," I countered, "that we're reluctant to forget?"

Ezra nodded sympathetically. "Indeed, I hope that's true for most people, Mr. Blake. But for some, perhaps not. Do you think the memory Mr. Candy struggled to recall, while speaking to you earlier, was one you particularly wanted him to recover?"
