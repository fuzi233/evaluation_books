As the sun set over the bustling streets, Margaret Brandt found herself feeling more at ease among strangers than she ever had within her own family. That was until a sudden turn of events brought a new wave of happiness into her life. A prominent civic leader, known for his lavish spending and eccentric ways, fell gravely ill and summoned Peter, the town's skilled apothecary. Peter's unorthodox methods, which involved tossing out the leader's excessive medicines and concocting a gentle egg yolk elixir, proved surprisingly effective. Word of his success spread quickly, and soon Peter's fame grew as rapidly as his bank account.

However, his newfound wealth went largely to purchasing rare herbs and exotic spices, which he believed held the key to even greater cures. Margaret, who had grown wise from years of watching her father tend to their small garden, cautioned Peter against overspending, but he wouldn't listen. His victory was short-lived, as he soon found himself struggling to find the right words, and a few days later, he suffered a debilitating stroke that left him weak and unable to practice medicine.

When Margaret realized her father figure was paralyzed, she knew exactly what to do. She rushed out to find a trusted doctor, but when she returned, she was met with the unwelcome sight of her cousin William, who had taken to drinking heavily with his servant. Margaret's pride and hesitation to seek help from her kin had nearly cost her dearly, and now she felt a mix of emotions as she stood before her cousin, his servant, and the unmistakable signs of their indulgence.

"You may speak freely in front of your... companion," Margaret said, trying to keep her tone neutral. Her cousin's familiar address to his servant, however, made her cheeks flush with embarrassment.

"I didn't realize my cousin had taken to the local customs," Margaret said, attempting to brush off the awkwardness. But her words fell flat, and the servant's tart response only made things worse.

"What custom of the town are you referring to?" the servant asked, her tone sharp as a knife.

Margaret's patience wore thin. "You're too modest, mistress," she said, her voice rising. "Your words betray your true nature. It's only when the monkey has climbed the tree that its tail becomes visible." With a swift glance at her cousin, who remained silent, Margaret turned and walked away, her heart heavy with disappointment and frustration.
