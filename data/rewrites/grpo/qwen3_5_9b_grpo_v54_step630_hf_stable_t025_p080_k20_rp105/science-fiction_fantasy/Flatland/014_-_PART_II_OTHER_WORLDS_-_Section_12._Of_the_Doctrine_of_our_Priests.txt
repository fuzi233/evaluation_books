The entire theological edifice of the Circles rests upon a single, immutable axiom: "Attend to your Configuration." Whether the discourse concerns politics, morality, or ecclesiastical hierarchy, the ultimate objective remains the refinement of individual and collective shape, with the perfection of the Circle standing as the supreme ideal to which all lesser forms aspire.

The Circles claim the historic merit of having eradicated the ancient heresy that conduct is a product of will, effort, training, or encouragement. Instead, they preach that Configuration is destiny. It was Pantocyclus—the great Circle who quelled the Colour Revolt—who first illuminated humanity with this truth: that one's nature is written in one's angles. If you are born an Isosceles with unequal sides, you are doomed to error unless corrected in the Isosceles Hospital; similarly, a Triangle, Square, or irregular Polygon born with a defect must seek the Regular Hospitals for cure, lest they end their days in the State Prison or at the executioner's blade. Under this doctrine, every fault, from minor misconduct to flagrant crime, is attributed solely to a deviation from perfect Regularity—perhaps caused by a jostle in the crowd, improper exercise, or a sudden shift in temperature causing a frame to shrink or expand. Consequently, Pantocyclus concluded that neither good nor bad conduct deserves praise or blame. Why praise a Square for defending a client when one should merely admire the precision of his right angles? Why blame a thieving Isosceles when the true tragedy is the incurable inequality of his sides?

While theoretically sound, this rigid dogma presents practical difficulties. In the courts, if a criminal pleads that his unevenness compels him to steal, the Magistrate replies that such a nuisance must be consumed to protect society. Yet, in domestic life, where death is not an option, the theory falters. I confess that when one of my Hexagonal grandsons excuses his disobedience by claiming a temperature shift has compromised his perimeter, I find myself unable to logically reject his plea, yet equally unable to practically accept it. To resolve this, I adopt the convenient assumption that a stern scolding possesses a latent power to strengthen a child's Configuration—a notion I hold without evidence. Fortunately, I am not alone; many high-ranking Circles acting as judges employ praise and blame as if these concepts were tangible realities, believing fervently that a human figure possesses the agency to choose between them.

The Circles systematically invert the natural order of parent-child relations found in Spaceland. Where Spaceland teaches children to honor their parents, Flatland teaches a man to honor his grandson, or failing that, his son. This "honor" is not indulgence, but a reverent subordination of parental interests to the welfare of posterity, ensuring the State's advancement.

However, the system's greatest vulnerability lies in its treatment of Women. To discourage irregular births, no woman with irregular ancestry is deemed a suitable partner for a man seeking social elevation. While male irregularity is a matter of visible measurement, female irregularity is invisible; thus, the State maintains strict pedigrees, and marriage without certification is forbidden. Ironically, the higher a man climbs the social scale, the less rigorous his scrutiny of a wife's lineage. An aspiring Isosceles avoids any woman with a single irregular ancestor to ensure an Equilateral son; a Square or Pentagon looks back only five hundred generations; but a Circle, proud of his potential to sire a Chief Circle, has been known to marry a woman with an irregular great-grandfather solely for her superior lustre or the enchanting quality of her low voice—a trait we prize highly in females.

These ill-judged unions often result in barrenness or a reduction in sides. Yet, the loss of a few sides in a developed Polygon is easily masked by operations in the Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium, and the Circles are too complacent to view infertility as a sign of inferiority rather than a law of superior development. If unchecked, the gradual erosion of the Circular class could soon render the production of a Chief Circle impossible, collapsing the very Constitution of Flatland.

A more insidious danger, which I dare not openly propose a remedy for, concerns our relations with Women. Three centuries ago, the Chief Circle decreed that since women lack Reason but abound in Emotion, they must be denied mental education. They were barred from reading and taught only enough arithmetic to count the angles of their husbands and children. The result has been a generational decline in female intellectual power, a policy of enforced quietism that persists today.

I fear this policy, born of good intentions, has inflicted a heavier burden upon the Male Sex. We now live a bifurcated existence. With women, we speak of "love," "duty," "right," and "wrong"—irrational fictions designed to curb feminine exuberance. Among ourselves, however, we utilize a stark scientific vocabulary where "love" is the anticipation of benefits and "duty" is mere necessity. We outwardly display the utmost deference to women, leading them to believe they are adored as fervently as the Chief Circle himself; yet behind their backs, we regard them as little more than mindless organisms.

Our theology in the women's chambers differs entirely from our true theology. This dual training, requiring young males to unlearn the emotional language of their mothers before mastering the cold dialect of science, strains the developing intellect. I perceive a weakening in the grasp of mathematical truth among the youth compared to the robust minds of our ancestors three hundred years ago. My humble appeal to the highest Authorities is based on the enfeeblement of the Male intellect: if a woman were to secretly learn to read and transmit the secrets of logic to her sex, or if an infant male inadvertently revealed these truths to a mother, the consequences would be catastrophic. Therefore, I urge a reconsideration of the regulations regarding female education, lest the very foundation of our rational society crumbles under the weight of its own contradictions.
