As the last wisps of Carnival's festivities dissipated, Tito descended the worn marble steps of the Old Palace, his footsteps echoing through the stillness of a late March morning. His destination was the bustling streets of San Marco, where the scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, mingling with the whispers of the crowd. He chose not to take the direct route, opting instead to stroll through the Piazza di Santa Croce, where the faithful poured out of the grand church after the early sermon. It was here that the Lenten sermons had drawn the largest crowds, their fervor fueled by the controversy surrounding the ban on Fra Girolamo Savonarola's preaching.

The Dominican friar, once a fixture in San Marco, had been silenced by the Pope's edict, which threatened the city with an interdict if he continued to preach. Now, his words were met with vitriol and ridicule, particularly in the Franciscan church of Santa Croce. There, Fra Francesco di Puglia had issued a challenge, proposing to walk through fire to prove the truth of his doctrine, a challenge that had been eagerly taken up by Fra Domenico, a devoted follower of Savonarola. The people were enthralled by the prospect of a spectacle, one that would settle the question of Savonarola's prophetic claims once and for all.

Tito navigated the crowd with a carefree air, his eyes scanning the throng for any sign of interest. He had come to San Marco on a mission, one that required him to blend into the crowd and observe without drawing attention to himself. As he wove through the sea of faces, he caught sight of a large placard affixed to the wall of the Franciscan monastery. The official script was written in Latin, its meaning lost on most of the onlookers. Yet, they stood transfixed, their curiosity piqued by the tantalizing prospect of a spectacle that would soon unfold.
