The sun had long since dipped below the horizon, casting a deep indigo hue across the tranquil expanse of the Thames. Deronda, a young man with a quiet intensity, glided effortlessly through the water in his dark-blue shirt and skull-cap, his curls closely clipped, his mouth framed by a luxuriant beard that seemed to hold secrets of its own. The rhythm of the oars dipping into the river created a soothing melody, a counterpoint to the turmoil that churned within him. It had been a year since he'd returned to England, with the weight of expectation settling upon him like a mantle – the expectation that he would take his place in society, that he would find his purpose.

As he rowed, the city's sounds receded, leaving only the gentle lapping of the water against the hull of his boat. He felt a sense of liberation, of freedom from the constraints of his life, as if the river had become a reflection of his own inner landscape. The stars were beginning to twinkle above, like diamonds scattered across the velvet sky, and Deronda felt the universe stretching out before him like an endless sea.

His thoughts, however, were far from the tranquil beauty of the night. He was torn between the desire to immerse himself in the world, to take part in its struggles and triumphs, and the fear of being consumed by its demands. The weight of his own indecision bore down upon him, a constant reminder that he was still searching for a path, still unsure of the direction he should take. The notion that his appearance drew attention – a notion that others had pressed upon him with flattering intent – only served to deepen his unease. He had always felt like an outsider, a stranger in his own skin, and the pressure to conform to societal expectations only added to his sense of disconnection.

As he passed beneath Kew Bridge, the city's lights flickering like fireflies in the darkness, Deronda felt the weight of his own identity bearing down upon him. He was a young man, lost in a world that seemed determined to pin him down, to define him by the narrow parameters of its own making. But he knew that he was more than the sum of his parts, that he was a complex tapestry of contradictions, woven from threads of doubt and desire, of uncertainty and hope. And as he rowed on, the river a dark and mysterious presence beside him, he felt the stirrings of a deeper truth, a truth that would eventually set him free
