As the afternoon sun cast its warm glow through the smoked-filled air of the club, Captain Willoughby settled into his favorite corner, puffing on his cigar with the same detached air that had earned him a reputation as a bore among his fellow members. His monologues, though often tedious, were delivered with a vacant amiability that put off even the most patient of listeners. Those who knew him well learned to avoid him altogether, rising from their seats at the mere sight of his arrival.

But on this particular day, Willoughby found himself alone, the victim of his own relentless need for an audience. The waiter, anticipating his desire for company, approached him with a card, which Willoughby seized eagerly. His countenance, however, fell as he read the name: Colonel Durrance.

"Colonel Durrance," he muttered, scratching his head in a gesture of confusion. This was unexpected; they had never been friends, and Willoughby wondered if Durrance had somehow discovered his plans to travel to Kingsbridge. "Does Colonel Durrance know I'm here?" he asked the waiter.

"Yes, sir," replied the waiter, his expression neutral.

Willoughby's eyes narrowed as he nodded. He had no wish to become entangled in Durrance's affairs, particularly those concerning Miss Ethne Eustace. Feversham and Durrance could settle their differences without his interference. He eyed Durrance warily as the colonel entered the room, but Durrance seemed uninterested in discussing anything other than small talk.

"Ah, Durrance!" Willoughby exclaimed, rising from his chair. "I'll introduce you to my cigar, since you seem to appreciate a good smoke." He paused, then smiled wryly. "Though I suppose I'll have to let mine go out myself – I've grown accustomed to watching the embers dance." With a flourish, Durrance lit his cigar, the scent of burning tobacco filling the air.

"I'm glad to find you, Willoughby," Durrance said, settling into the chair beside him. "I'm only in town for the day, and I was eager to meet you. My oculist, Mather, spoke highly of you. You remember Mather, don't you? We served together in Suakin."
