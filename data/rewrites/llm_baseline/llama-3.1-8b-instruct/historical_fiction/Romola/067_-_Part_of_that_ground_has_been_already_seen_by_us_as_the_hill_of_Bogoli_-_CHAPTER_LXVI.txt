The scent of freshly pressed olives wafted through the air as the morning sun broke over the rolling hills of the Valdarno, casting a golden glow on the landscape. It was Palm Sunday, a day of reverence and contemplation, yet the city of Florence seethed with tension. The previous day's rain had washed away the dust, revealing the hidden fervor that simmered beneath the surface.

As the sun climbed higher, Savonarola ascended the pulpit in San Marco, his voice carrying out across the piazza. His words dripped with conviction, and though the congregation's eyes shone with devotion, their hearts harbored a mix of awe and trepidation. For in the silence between the lines, they sensed the weight of his own mortality. The preacher's eyes, once ablaze with fervor, now seemed to hold a quiet acceptance, as if he had gazed into the abyss and seen his own fate.

The benediction ended, and Savonarola descended the pulpit, his gaze lingering on the faces below. He knew that the storm brewing outside would soon engulf him, and he was willing to face it head-on. The crowd dispersed, their footsteps echoing through the empty streets as they made their way to the Duomo, where a heated confrontation awaited.

As the day wore on, the air thickened with the smell of woodsmoke and the murmur of angry voices. The Compagnacci, fueled by a hunger for revenge, sought to disrupt the evening sermon, and the Piagnoni, determined to defend their leader, stood firm. The clash began at the Duomo, spilling into the narrow streets surrounding San Marco. The call went up – "To San Marco! The fire to San Marco!" – and the mob surged forward, their numbers swelling with each passing moment.

Within the convent walls, the monks prepared for battle, their white habits a stark contrast to the chaos outside. They had long anticipated this moment, and their arms, hidden beneath their robes, were at the ready. Laymen who had chosen to stay fought alongside the monks, their swords flashing in the fading light. The sound of gunfire echoed through the cloisters, and hot embers rained down from the convent roof as the defenders held their ground.
