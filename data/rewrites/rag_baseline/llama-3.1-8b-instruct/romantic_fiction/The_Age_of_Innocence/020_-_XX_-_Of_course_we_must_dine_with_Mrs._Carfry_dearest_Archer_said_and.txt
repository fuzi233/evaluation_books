The dim light of the morning sun struggled through the fog-shrouded windows of Brown's Hotel. May Archer stared at her husband, her eyes narrowed in concern. "It's all very well for you, Newland; you know them. But I shall feel so shy among a lot of people I've never met." She toyed with the delicate handle of her teacup, her fingers drumming a staccato beat on the fine china. Across from her, Newland leaned back in his chair, a small smile playing on his lips. The gentle lapping of the Thames against the riverbank outside seemed to underscore the tranquility of the moment.

May's brow furrowed deeper as she contemplated the evening ahead. "And what shall I wear?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper. Newland's gaze drifted to the wardrobe, a vision of elegance suspended above the bed. The faint scent of lavender wafted from the closet, a reminder of the perfume May favored. His eyes returned to her face, and he smiled again, his eyes crinkling at the corners. "You'll be lovely, May. Whatever you choose will be perfect."

The stillness of the morning air was punctuated only by the soft ticking of the grandfather clock in the corner. Outside, the city stirred, its rhythms a muted counterpoint to the quiet intimacy of the Archer's breakfast table. May's anxiety, however, remained unsoothed. She pushed her chair back, the scrape of the wood on the floorboards a jarring note in the otherwise serene atmosphere. "I suppose we must go," she said, her voice a little firmer now. Newland nodded, his eyes never leaving hers. "Yes, dear. We must."
