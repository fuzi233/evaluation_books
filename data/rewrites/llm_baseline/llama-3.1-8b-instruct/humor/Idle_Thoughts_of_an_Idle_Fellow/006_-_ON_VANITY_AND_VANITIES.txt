The whispers of vanity danced through the morning air like the gentle rustle of leaves in an autumn breeze. My little niece, a whirlwind of energy and self-absorption, stood before me, her bright eyes fixed on the reflection of her new shoes in the windowpane. "Uncle, what do you think of my new shoes?" she asked, her voice a sweet melody of expectation. I hesitated, unsure of how to temper my true thoughts with the sugar-coated words she craved. They lacked the elegance and poise I'd come to expect from her mother, but I chose instead to lavish praise upon their awkward charm.

Her gaze sparkled with delight, and she skipped away, her words echoing through the hallway like a triumphant hymn: "Uncle says I look lovely – I must have two biscuits!" Her words were music to the ears of the other children, who gathered around her like a chorus of admirers, each one eager to bask in the glow of her reflected glory.

But I remembered another time, in a sun-drenched garden in the suburbs of London, when I'd witnessed a similar display of self-absorption. A shrill voice pierced the air, calling out to some unseen companion, "Mummy, mummy, look at me! I'm wearing my best dress – isn't it lovely?" The words were a testament to the universal language of vanity, one that transcended species and age.

Even the creatures of the wild were not immune to its allure. I recalled a majestic Newfoundland dog I'd seen in Regent's Circus, lost in admiration of his own reflection in a shop window. His tail wagged with an air of proud contentment, a sight that brought to mind the solemn faces of vestry members, their brows furrowed in devotion.

On a farm, during a festive celebration, I'd watched as a cow was adorned with a garland of flowers. The usually docile creature pranced about with an air of haughty dignity, only to sulk when the wreath was removed. And cats, those enigmatic felines, seemed to rival humans in their capacity for vanity, their whiskers twitching with indignation at the slightest criticism, their purrs a soothing balm for the ego.
