I had lost all sense of my bearings, wandering in a state of pure, helpless agitation, when suddenly I stepped directly into the path of a leopardess. Beneath her paws, a river of crimson blood surged forward, striking my ankles with the force of a mountain brook. Driven by a primal, formless dread regarding the origin of this gore, I hurried away from it. Yet, compelled by instinct, I stayed close to its rhythm, tracing the sound uphill toward Bulika.

Soon, reason cut through my panic. No leopardess, no elephant, not even the leviathans of our own world, could sustain such a torrent unless every vessel in their bodies burst open, draining the earth's lakes and forests faster than nature could refill them. This was not blood. I dipped a finger into the flow and confirmed it instantly: it was water. A soft, murmuring rivulet spilled aimlessly over the grass, singing a melody too sweet to ignore yet too dangerous to taste. I pressed onward, driven by the hope of dawn and the haunting familiarity of that alien sound—a stark contrast to the scorching heat of the day. The mere cooling of my feet revitalized me, allowing me to walk without fatigue until the encroaching twilight revealed the silhouette of a city against the pale aurora. These wall-towers seemed older than time itself.

Then, the brook vanished. For some time, I had noticed its volume fading, but I had stopped paying attention. Looking back, I saw the grass bent in the direction of its former course, dotted with small, shimmering pools. Beyond that point, heading toward the city, there was absolutely no trace of it. Near where I stood, the spring must have sputtered to a halt. Surrounding the fortress were vast gardens teeming with vegetables I did not recognize—no familiar flowers, no signs of animal life. The plots grew right up to the massive walls, separated only by towering mounds of gravel and refuse dumped from the ramparts.

I approached the nearest gate. It hung ajar, neither locked nor guarded, its hinges rusted in a state of perfect stasis, unable to swing further open or shut tighter. Stepping inside, I gazed down a long, ancient thoroughfare. Utter silence reigned; there was scarcely a sign of life. Was this a graveyard of a civilization? Turning back, I trudged over piles of dust, crossing several roads that all led to similar gates. I refused to return until I heard the stir of waking inhabitants. *What am I here for?* I asked myself. *What do I hope to find?* My purpose remained clouded, though one image burned clear: I needed to see the woman I had breathed life into once more. I desired no company from her; she had awakened terrifying doubts within me, making friendship—or worse, love—an impossibility. Yet, her presence exerted a strange, magnetic pull. I felt compelled to resist her influence while simultaneously dissecting it. I sought to pierce her inscrutable nature, to understand a mode of existence that defied imagination. How reckless! A man should not willingly court temptation for the sake of knowledge. Conversely, I had resurrected a malevolent force teetering on extinction. I was accountable for whatever chaos might follow. I had learned she was the enemy of children; perhaps the "Little Ones" were in peril. I had left them seeking answers, receiving only fragments of light. Now, I needed more. I needed to know how to shield them.

At last, a faint commotion drifted through the air. I moved through the next gate and down a narrow street lined with tall, looming houses, settling onto the base of a pillar crowned by a grotesque, bat-like statue. Soon, citizens began to saunter past. I addressed one, but he offered only a sneering glare and a harsh insult before moving on. Rising, I navigated alley after alley, which gradually filled with loiterers. As expected, I saw no children. Eventually, near a gate, a group of young men resembling the brutish giants I remembered surrounded me. They stared, shoved, hustled, and hurled debris at me. I endured it, desperate not to spark hostility where I intended to stay. I tried appealing to those who looked kinder, but no one paused to listen. I appeared destitute, and in Bulika, poverty was treated as a crime. Deformity and illness were taxed heavily, and nothing pleased their Princess more than laws designed to let poverty serve wealth. Finally, I fled, and no one pursued me beyond the threshold.

A burly figure sitting by the gate, chewing on a lump of bread, picked up a stone to hurl after me. In his clumsy zeal, however, he tossed the bread instead. I snatched it up; he dared not follow to retrieve it, for beyond the walls, everyone was a coward. I retreated a few hundred yards, collapsed into the grass, devoured the bread, and fell into a deep sleep. The hot sun restored my strength.

Night fell, and I awoke. The moon descended with a friendly gaze, reminding me of the companion that watched over me during my first terrifying night in this strange world. Its light was brilliant. A cold wind drifted from the gate, carrying a foul odor, yet it failed to chill me, for the sun's warmth still lingered in my bones. I slipped back into the city, joining the few souls crouching in corners to escape the biting blast.

Walking slowly down a long, narrow street, a huge white shape bounded across my path, flashing briefly in the moonlight before vanishing. Eager to catch a glimpse again, I turned down the next opening. It was a cramped lane, barely wide enough for passage, leading into a broader avenue. The moment I entered, I spotted the creature on the opposite side, lurking in the shadows. It was following me like a dog, mimicking a human figure. Every few moments, it glanced backward, but it said nothing and made no attempt to shoo away its pursuer.

Where it crossed a patch of moonlight, I realized it cast no shadow. It was itself a flat, two-dimensional shadow, yet an opaque one. It didn't just darken objects behind it; it rendered them invisible. Within the shade, it was darker than the darkness itself; in the moonlight, it appeared as if it had wrapped its own shadow around its form, leaving not a hint of gray beneath or beside it. Following closely at its heels was a leopardess, appearing as the white counterpoint to its blackness. As the pair moved from shadow into moonlight, the shadow-figure deepened into absolute void, while the beast flared into radiant brilliance.

I walked abreast of them on the opposite side, my bare feet clicking on the flat stones. The leopardess never turned its head or twitched an ear. The shadow-creature seemed to glance at me; for a split second, I lost its profile, seeing only a sharp, vertical line. In that instant, the wind caught me, blowing straight through my body. I shuddered from head to toe, and my heart hammered against my ribs like a pebble rattling in a child's toy.
