The warm glow of the fire crackled in the hearth as Captain Costigan summoned his trusted friend, Mr. Garbetts, to join him in a most pressing matter. Mr. Garbetts, a jovial and athletic figure, renowned for his impressive physique and charismatic stage presence, was a frequent fixture at the convivial gatherings at the Magpie Hotel. Their camaraderie extended beyond social circles, as they frequently collaborated on various business ventures, often relying on each other's signature to seal their agreements.

However, their friendship was not without its complexities. Mr. Garbetts' past infatuation with Miss Fotheringay had led to a tumultuous relationship, ultimately rejected by the young lady. This rejection had left Mr. Garbetts vulnerable to the jealousy of Mrs. Garbetts, who, it seemed, had become a formidable adversary in her own right.

As Captain Costigan awaited Mr. Garbetts' arrival, he pondered the delicate nuances of their shared acquaintance. He recalled the numerous occasions when Mr. Garbetts had regaled him with tales of his theatrical exploits, his booming voice and robust laughter filling the room. Yet, despite their close bond, Captain Costigan chose to omit certain details from their conversation, opting instead to focus on the pressing matter at hand.

Upon Mr. Garbetts' arrival, accompanied by a steaming flask of whisky-and-water, Captain Costigan outlined his plan to dispatch a stern letter to Major Pendennis, demanding that he acquiesce to the marriage between Mr. Arthur Pendennis and Miss Fotheringay. The letter, penned with calculated vitriol, threatened dire consequences should Major Pendennis refuse, including the use of a horsewhip to enforce compliance. The precise language of the letter remained confidential, known only to Captain Costigan and the intended recipient.

As Mr. Garbetts departed to deliver the missive, Captain Costigan busied himself with his dueling pistols, meticulously examining the intricate mechanisms and polishing the metal surfaces. His reverence for these relics of a bygone era was palpable, and he handled them with a mix of nostalgia and reverence. From a nearby drawer, he retrieved a collection of letters and poems penned by Pen, which he would typically peruse before sharing them with his daughter, Emily.

Twenty minutes passed before Mr. Garbetts returned, his countenance etched with concern. "You've seen him?" Captain Costigan inquired, his eyes narrowing in anticipation.
