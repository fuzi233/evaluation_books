Even as the novelty of two-tone and multi-hued body painting spread across Flatland, the core intellectual arts that had upheld our society for millennia began to rot from within.

Sight Recognition, that sacred skill that let us parse hundreds of overlapping shapes through subtle shifts of mist and edge softness without touching a soul, was no longer practiced by anyone who did not have a personal stake in preserving the old order. Geometry, statics, kinetics, and all the hard won sciences we had studied for centuries were soon written off as useless, frivolous relics, dismissed with sneers even in the halls of our most prestigious universities. The humble foundational art of Feeling, taught to every isosceles and square in elementary school to help them learn their own shape and the bounds of their neighbors, suffered the exact same fate, dropped from curriculums entirely within a single generation.

The Isosceles classes wasted no time exploiting this decay. They declared that the old annual tribute of labor and recruits from the criminal lower ranks to fund public education was no longer needed, and refused to pay it. Freed from that ancient burden that had once both tamed their brutal, sharp-edged natures and kept their overgrown population in check, their numbers swelled, and their insolence grew bolder by the day. Month by month, rank-and-file soldier triangles and artisan triangles grew louder in their demands — and their claims grew truer, too. What difference was there, they shouted, between a ragged three-sided workman and a twelve-sided noble polygon, when color coding let anyone of any intelligence tell one class from another at a glance, no complex math or careful visual parsing required?

They did not stop at merely letting the old skills fade. Boldly, they demanded the state ban all "aristocratic monopolist arts" entirely, and defund every endowment that supported the study of Sight Recognition, mathematics, and Feeling. Soon their demands escalated further: color, which had become a second nature for Flatlanders, had erased the old need for rigid class distinctions, so the law ought to follow suit. All people, of every rank, should be recognized as completely equal, with identical rights.

When the highest noble orders hesitated, uncertain how to push back, the leaders of the growing Chromatic Revolution went one step further. They demanded that every last class, Priests and Women included, must submit to being painted in official hues, to show their respect for the new universal order.

When critics objected that Women were mere straight lines, with no sides to paint at all, the revolutionaries had a ready retort: Nature herself dictates that every living Flatlander's front half — the side that holds the eye and mouth — must be distinct from the inert blind back half, and expediency only reinforced that rule. They brought a formal bill before an extraordinary all-states assembly of Flatland, laying out the rules: every Woman would have the half of her line that held her eye and mouth dyed bright red, and the opposite half dyed vivid green. The Priests, the perfect circular ruling class, would follow the exact same pattern: the semicircle centered on their mouth and eye would be red, the rear semicircle green.

There was devilish cunning to this proposal — no crude, sharp-edged Isosceles could ever have conceived it. It was the work of an Irregular Circle, a misshapen near-perfect shape that foolish elders had spared from culling in infancy, allowing him to grow into a force that would unleash untold ruin on our nation.

First, the bill was engineered to win every last Woman in Flatland over to the Chromatic cause. By assigning the exact same two red-and-green hues to Women that they assigned to the Priests, the revolutionaries guaranteed that from certain common viewing angles, any Woman would be indistinguishable from a member of the sacred ruling class, and be treated with all the deference and awe reserved for the Circles. It is easy to see why the entire Female sex threw their support behind the bill at once.

If you have not realized the trick yet, it is simple to demonstrate. Take a Woman painted per the new code: eye half red, back half green. Look at her edge-on, head on to her thin line body, and what you see is a straight clean line, exactly half red and exactly half green. Now take a Priest, a full Circle, painted so his front semicircle is red and his rear semicircle is green. If you line up your gaze exactly level with the dividing diameter that splits his red half from his green half, all you will see is a straight line, exactly half red and exactly half green.

Bear in mind that by this point the old skill of Sight Recognition was already rotting away. Women quickly learned to file and soften the two ends of their straight line bodies to blur them, so their line would taper exactly the same way a Circle's thin edge-on profile does. The slight difference in total line length between a tall Woman and a priest would be completely invisible to anyone who had forgotten how to parse subtle shape differences, distracted as they would be by the identical red-green color split.

The Women of Flatland could imagine all the joys that awaited them: they could linger in drawing rooms and hear state and church secrets their husbands would never have dreamed of sharing with a common wife, then march down the street and issue orders to shopkeepers and laborers in the full mistaken belief that they were speaking to a Priest. Any disrespect shown to them would be treated as blasphemy against the ruling class. They did not spare a single thought for the scandal that would tear the state apart when a frivolous young Woman's reckless antics were blamed on the Priestly Order, shattering the public's respect for the entire hierarchy. Even the wives of the highest Circles begged their noble husbands to support the new bill.

That was the second, darker purpose of the cunning Irregular Circle who had drafted the law. For all the rest of Flatland had abandoned intellectual rigor, the Circles still held fast to their old sharpness. Their homes had remained entirely free of color for generations, and their children were trained from infancy to practice Sight Recognition, honing their intellects from the day they could first see. The bill was designed to drag them down to the level of the rest of the population in one single blow. Forcing the Circles to paint themselves would pollute their once-colorless homes, stripping their children of the quiet, uncolored environment they needed to practice the sacred art of sight. Soon, every circular parent and child would be colored, and the only intellectual problems the young noble children would face from infancy would be trying to tell their red-and-green painted mother apart from their red-and-green painted father — a puzzle their mischievous mothers would constantly cheat and mislead them on, eroding their faith in clear logical deduction bit by bit.

Slowly but surely, the intellectual glory of the Priestly Order would fade. And once that last line of defense was gone, there would be nothing left to stop the mob from tearing down the old aristocratic order entirely, and submerging all of Flatland in total, chaotic equality.
