His passing had left her with a comfortable income, which she now devoted to maintaining a warm and welcoming home, filled with the aromas of roasting meats and baking bread.

As she sat in her parlor, surrounded by the soft glow of candles and the gentle hum of conversation, Mrs. Jennings's thoughts turned to the two young women who had recently become acquainted with her family. Elinor and Marianne Dashwood, with their quiet reserve and lively spirits, had captured her heart, and she longed to have them join her in town for the winter. She had already begun to make plans, envisioning the three of them driving together in her chaise, laughing and chatting as they navigated the bustling streets of London.

"I'm sure your mother won't mind you taking a short break," Mrs. Jennings said, her voice warm with enthusiasm. "In fact, I'm counting on it. My daughters will be delighted to have you girls to themselves, and I daresay, you'll find our company quite agreeable. We'll have a lovely time, just the four of us, and I promise not to put myself out in the least."
