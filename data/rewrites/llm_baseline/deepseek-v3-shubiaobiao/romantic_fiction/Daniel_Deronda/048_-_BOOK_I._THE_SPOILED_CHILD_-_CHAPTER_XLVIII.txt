The weight of Grandcourt’s existence was measured not in action but in possession—the passive sovereignty of inherited lands, which insulated him from the petty storms of politics or trade. The world’s turmoil reached him only as distant thunder, muffled by the walls of his wealth. He skimmed newspapers with languid disdain, dismissing entire nations and classes beneath the blanket contempt of "brutes," his silence a weapon more unnerving than any outburst.

Yet within the narrow sphere of his own interests, Grandcourt wielded a diplomat’s cunning. No flicker of Gwendolen’s attention toward Deronda escaped him—not from jealousy, for jealousy implied doubt, and doubt was a weakness he refused to acknowledge. His power lay in her understanding, as unshakable as iron, that her desires were irrelevant against his will.

He had not regretted his marriage. Gwendolen suited him: her beauty commanded admiration, her spirit made her conquest satisfying. A docile wife would have bored him; her simmering resentment, her helpless rebellion, were precisely what pleased his taste. He enjoyed the spectacle of her frustration, knowing she could not act on it.

Gwendolen flushed, recognizing the barb. She had no defense but pride.

Deronda, unaware of the tension he provoked, sought only to reassure her. He could not avoid her without cruelty, nor could he deny the pull of her unhappiness. She had become more than a puzzle to him; she was a responsibility, a claim on his conscience. Yet he knew his future lay far from her—a knowledge that made their encounters all the more bittersweet.

One morning, determined to defy Grandcourt’s stifling presence, Gwendolen announced at breakfast,

Grandcourt’s lizard-like gaze flickered over her. *"Why should a lady sing? Amateurs are grotesque."*

*"Then you won’t object if I engage her for our party?"* she pressed, testing him. *"Even Mr. Deronda approves of her taste."*

A faint sneer. *"Deronda praises her rather too often. Men see what his interest must be."*

Gwendolen went very still. *"What do you mean?"*

The implication struck like venom. She rose, her composure brittle, and retreated to her room, where fury and doubt warred within her. Was Deronda not the man she had believed him to be? Or was this another of Grandcourt’s manipulations?

She could not endure the uncertainty. Within the hour, her carriage was rolling toward Mirah’s lodgings.

---

Mirah received her with quiet grace, her calm face a contrast to Gwendolen’s agitation. The request for the performance was made hastily, but Gwendolen could not leave. The words burst from her:

Mirah’s eyes kindled. *"Who dares say otherwise? He saved me when I had nothing. He honors my brother, a man of learning and worth. If an angel accused him, I would not believe it."*

The answer was a balm. Gwendolen’s grip on Mirah’s hand tightened. *"Thank you,"* she whispered, then fled before Deronda could discover her there.

---

Grandcourt was waiting when she returned. *"You went to her, then. And asked about Deronda?"*

Gwendolen’s temper snapped. *"Yes. And what you said was a lie."*

*"Did she tell you that?"* His smile was glacial.

She had no proof but her own conviction, and under his gaze, even that withered.

*"It doesn’t matter if you have her sing,"* he said, *"but you will not return to that house. As my wife, you will conduct yourself as I deem proper."*

Each word was a chain. She was helpless.

Grandcourt had won again.
