As the seasons blended together in a haze of monotony, our existence within the fort became a tiresome routine. The commandant and I engaged in our usual spats, punctuated by card games and melancholy duets played on his wheezing flute and my battered guitar. Fawn endured her share of beatings and liquor rations, nursing her infant, the commandant's ailing self, or our prisoner, who struggled with the ague. Our lives trudged along until the arrival of a hunter bearing momentous news. The Marquis de Montcalm had dispatched a substantial force to bolster the French-held forts and occupy strategic positions in the British territories. The troops had departed from Quebec and Montreal, navigating the St. Lawrence and the lakes in bateaux laden with artillery and provisions. This development set the garrison abuzz with excitement. The men envisioned advancing into Pennsylvania and New York, seizing key cities like Albany and Philadelphia, and driving the British into the sea, thus claiming all of America from the Mississippi to Newfoundland for the French.

This prospect, however, failed to lift the commandant's spirits. 'It's all very well,' he said, 'but tell me, my young friend, what about your farm in Normandy? The cider press, the trippes à la mode de Caen?' His words dripped with a mixture of longing and envy.

I shrugged. 'The farm, yes, it's a pleasant thought, but what of your successor? Will they treat me with the same leniency as you have? You're a rare soul, commandant. Few men possess your compassion. When the garrison swells, will my new masters show me the same kindness?'

He shook his head. 'You'll be nothing more than a prized possession, locked away like a pig awaiting slaughter. Mark my words, you'll pay dearly for any harm befalling one of our officers. You'll be handed over to our allies, the Senecas. Don't you recall the fate of our prisoners taken during the Braddock expedition? They were treated with cruelty beyond measure.' He launched into a graphic description of the torments inflicted upon them – eyes gouged from their sockets, teeth and nails ripped out, limbs and bodies flayed. I winced, sparing my companion, Miss Theo, the gruesome details.
