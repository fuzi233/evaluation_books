As the morning light seeped through the windows of her small sitting room, Miss Butterworth's composure began to unravel like a thread pulled from a delicate tapestry. The encounter with Mr. Gryce had left her feeling unsettled, and she found herself fidgeting beneath the surface of her usual reserve. Her strict upbringing had instilled in her a sense of decorum, but the turmoil brewing inside threatened to upend her poise. She couldn't shake the memory of the young couple she had glimpsed fleeing the house, their faces etched with a mix of fear and desperation.

When Mr. Gryce returned, his smile seemed almost forced, and Miss Butterworth's unease only intensified. "Well, that is seen to," he said, his words hanging in the air like a challenge. "Now, all we need to do is wait for the outcome." His eyes sparkled with a hint of amusement, but Miss Butterworth wasn't fooled. Beneath the levity, she detected a glimmer of concern, a sense that they were indeed treading treacherous waters.

She drew a deep breath, steeling herself to confront the tangled threads of the mystery. "I would like to be assured of the value of my previous theory before venturing onto new ground," she said, her voice steady. "Shall we review the evidence and compare notes? For instance, why do neither of us view Bartow as the perpetrator?"

Mr. Gryce leaned back in his chair, steepling his fingers together. "I thought we'd exhausted that subject, but I suppose it bears revisiting. My reservations stem from the mute's behavior – the way he seems to be reenacting something he's witnessed, rather than something he's done. I've seen no reason to doubt this impression, and I'm inclined to trust it."

Miss Butterworth nodded, her thoughts racing ahead. "I was similarly influenced, and my doubts have shifted elsewhere. It's more plausible that a man would be driven mad by another's actions rather than his own." She paused, her gaze drifting to the window, where the sunlight danced across the panes like a thousand tiny sparks. "And did Bartow not love Mr. Adams?"

Mr. Gryce's expression turned thoughtful. "He doesn't seem to mourn him, but that's hardly conclusive. Emotions can be complex, and we shouldn't discount the possibility that Bartow's actions might be a performance, a carefully crafted act."
