As Flambeau drifted into the stillness of a Norfolk morning, the silence of the riverbank was like a soothing balm to his weary soul. His small sailing-boat, barely visible among the tall reeds, seemed to have become one with the landscape itself, gliding effortlessly across the water like a ghostly apparition. The gentle lapping of the waves against the hull created a lullaby that lulled him into a deep sleep, accompanied by the soft snores of his companion, Father Brown.

The night had been spent anchored in a secluded cove, surrounded by a tapestry of high grasses and pollarded trees that towered above them like sentinels. As the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, the men stirred, blinking away the remnants of slumber. The air was alive with the sweet scent of blooming flowers and the distant songs of birds, a symphony that echoed through the stillness of the morning.

Flambeau stretched his arms, feeling the stiffness in his muscles from a day of sculling. He gazed up at the sky, where the last wisps of night's darkness were being chased away by the golden light of dawn. The moon, now a silver crescent, hung low in the heavens, casting an ethereal glow over the landscape. It was as if the world was bathed in a soft, lunar light, imbuing everything with a sense of wonder and enchantment.

"By Jove!" Flambeau exclaimed, his voice barely above a whisper, "it's like being in a dream." Father Brown, already sitting up in the boat, nodded in agreement. The stillness of the morning had awakened something deep within them both, a sense of connection to the natural world and the simple joys of existence.

Their boat drifted on, carried by the current, as they sat in silence, taking in the beauty of the surroundings. The riverbed dropped away beneath them, revealing a world of hidden treasures – a secret garden of aquatic plants and flowers, a haven for fish and other creatures that called the river home. It was a world that existed outside the boundaries of time and space, a realm where the cares and worries of the world were left behind.
