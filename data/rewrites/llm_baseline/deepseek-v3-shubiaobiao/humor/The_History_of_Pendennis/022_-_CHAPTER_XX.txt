Before Mr. Foker bid farewell to Oxbridge (with more fanfare than a retiring opera diva), a new figure slunk into Boniface College—one Mr. Horace Bloundell, late of Camford University, where he’d been *politely* asked to leave after a series of *misunderstandings* involving tutors, a missing ceremonial mace, and a donkey.

Bloundell came from the ancient Suffolk Bloundells—Bloundell-Bloundell Hall, Bloundellshire, as the wags called it—though his lineage was more *debatable* than his gambling debts. He’d briefly worn the crimson pantaloons of a dragoon before swapping them for the sober black of a clergyman (a profession chosen, one suspected, because the living came with a particularly fine wine cellar).

When Major Pendennis visited Pen, he was introduced to a gallery of Oxbridge’s finest: Lord Plinlimmon (so polite he’d apologize to a chair for sitting on it), Magnus Charters (who declared republicanism between sips of his grandfather’s claret), and Bloundell, who wore more jewelry than a pawnshop window and exuded the charm of a cat in a fishmonger’s.

The Major, ever astute, took one look at Bloundell and whispered to Pen, *“That man is a walking bad decision.”*

Pen, of course, was dazzled. Bloundell had *seen things*—gambling hells, dubious clubs, and (allegedly) the inside of a Parisian jail. He spoke of lost fortunes and midnight escapades with the casual flair of a man who’d read too many penny dreadfuls.

Soon, Pen was swept into the seductive whirl of dice games, where Bloundell’s green wineglass became a chalice of ruin. The stakes? Mostly Pen’s allowance, his dignity, and (on one memorable night) a pair of boots he’d sworn were lucky.

Then came the London trip. Under Bloundell’s tutelage, Pen discovered the *cultural delights* of Covent Garden—namely, how quickly one’s purse could vanish between the theatre and a certain military club where men with waxed mustaches bet on cockroach races.

It was here Pen saw Miss Fotheringay again. Time had not been kind—or perhaps Pen’s eyes had simply cleared. Her once-mesmeric voice now struck him as a touch too *shrill*, her tragic sighs more *rehearsed* than heartfelt. The realization hit him like a bad oyster: *I pined for this?*

Yet Bloundell, ever helpful, assured him she’d had *dozens* of admirers, each more generous (and gullible) than the last. Pen, ever credulous, swallowed every word—along with far too much Maraschino.

By term’s end, Pen’s finances were in tatters, his reputation *seasoned* with scandal, and his only consolation was that he’d at least *lost* his money with panache.

Youth is a splendid fire—bright, warm, and prone to burning down the house.
