The morning mist swirled around the crumbling facade of the Hales' townhouse, like a damp, grey shroud that refused to lift. Within, Margaret Hale moved listlessly, her eyes red-rimmed from lack of sleep, her thoughts consumed by the weight of her family's sorrow. The task of arranging the house for the day ahead seemed a hollow exercise, a futile attempt to impose order on the chaos that had descended upon them. As she worked, the soft ticking of the grandfather clock in the hallway marked the passing hours, each one a reminder of the life they would never share again.

Her father, a man usually resolute and strong, moved through the rooms with a vacant air, as if his spirit had already begun to detach itself from the mortal coil. Frederick, on the other hand, struggled to contain his emotions, his forced attempts at cheeriness a thin veneer for the anguish that threatened to overwhelm him. Margaret knew that she had to be the anchor, the steady presence that held them all together, but it was a burden she felt acutely, weighed down by the responsibilities that rested on her shoulders.

As she moved from room to room, making sure that everything was in order, Margaret's thoughts turned to the funeral, the practicalities of which seemed to be falling squarely on her shoulders. She wanted to get it over with, to lay her mother to rest and begin the long, slow process of healing. But how to bring it about? Her father seemed lost, unable to make even the simplest decisions, and Frederick was a mess of tears and confusion.
