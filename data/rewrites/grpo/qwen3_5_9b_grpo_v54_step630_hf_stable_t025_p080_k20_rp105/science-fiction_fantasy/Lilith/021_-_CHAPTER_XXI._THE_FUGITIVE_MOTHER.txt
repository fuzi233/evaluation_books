The moon vanished beneath a heavy, bruised cloud, and from the sudden gray swallowed the world, a white figure erupted—a woman clutching a child to her heaving chest, her body bent in a desperate, driven arc. She moved on a parallel line to my own, a blur of terror and frantic speed, utterly blind to my presence.

"She is hunted!" I whispered to the silence. "A predator of this nightmare night stalks her!"

To pursue her would only ignite fresh panic. Instead, I stepped into her shadow, intending to intercept her pursuer. But before I could turn, a soft, swift rush erupted behind me. Something leaped over my head, striking me with brutal force against the forehead and sending me crashing to the earth. I surged back to my feet instantly, but all I caught was a flash of vanishing white. I chased the beast, blood already trickling down my face, but after only a few paces, a shriek of pure despair tore through the quivering night. I ran harder, though dread coiled in my gut that it was already too late.

Moments later, a low, white shape emerged from the vapor-dusted moonlight. At first, I assumed it was another hunter, but it approached with agonizing slowness, making strange, floundering leaps that spoke of immense pain. I drew aside, waiting. As it neared, I saw it hobbled on three legs, dragging its left fore-paw high above the ground. Its skin was shining white, marred by dark, oval spots, accompanied by a low, rushing sound like water cascading over grass. As it passed, I saw something streaming from the lifted limb.

"It is blood," I realized. "Someone braver than I has wounded this beast!" Yet, a sudden, crushing pity seized me at the sight of the suffering creature. Even if an axe had been in my hand, I could not have brought it down. In a broken succession of hobbling leaps, it vanished into the darkness, its blood seeming to flow back softly through the grass beside me. "If it bleeds like this," I thought, "it will soon be harmless."

I pressed on, hoping to find the woman and perhaps her deliverer.

A short distance away, I found her seated on the grass, the child resting in her lap.

"Can I help you?" I asked.

At my voice, she jumped violently, rising to flee. I threw myself to the ground.

"Do not be afraid," I said quickly. "I was tracking the beast when you found a closer protector! It just passed me, its foot bleeding so profusely that it must be dying!"

"There is little hope of that!" she replied, trembling. "Do you not know whose beast this is?"

Suspicious thoughts stirred within me, but I feigned ignorance. "What has become of her champion?"

"What champion?" she answered, her voice tight. "I saw no one."

"Then how did the monster suffer?"

"I smashed her foot with a stone—hard as I could strike. Did you not hear her cry?"

"You are a brave woman!" I said. "I thought that cry belonged to you!"

"It was the leopardess."

"I have never heard such a sound from an animal's throat! It was the scream of a woman in torture!"

"My voice was gone; I could not have shrieked to save my baby! When I saw that horrid mouth at my darling's little white neck, I grabbed a stone and crushed her lame foot."

"Tell me of this creature," I urged. "I am a stranger here."

"You will learn soon enough if you intend to go to Bulika!" she said. "But I must never return there!"

"Yes, I am going to Bulika—to see the princess."

"Be careful; you had best not go!—But perhaps you are...! The princess is a very good, kind woman!"

I heard a slight rustle. Thick clouds had gathered over the moon, obscuring my companion; I feared she was preparing to run again.

"You are in no danger from me," I assured her. "What oath would you require?"

"I know by your speech that you are not of Bulika," she replied. "I will trust you—I am not of them, either, for if I were, I could not escape. They never trust anyone. I only wish I could see your face! But I like your voice!—There, my darling is asleep! The foul beast has not harmed her!—Yes, it was my baby she sought!" She continued, caressing the sleeping child. "And then she would have torn her mother apart for carrying her off! Some say the princess keeps two white leopardesses; I know only one—with spots. Everyone knows HER! If the princess hears of a baby, she sends her immediately to suck its blood, and then the child either dies or grows up an idiot. I tried to leave with my baby, but the princess was away, and I thought I might wait until I was stronger. But she must have taken the beast with her, returning home just as I fled. I heard the SNIFF-SNUFF of the leopardess behind me, and I ran—oh, how I ran!—But my darling will not die! There is no mark on her!"

"Where are you taking her?"

"Where no one ever tells!"

"Why is the princess so cruel?"

"There is an old prophecy that a child will be the death of her. That is why she refuses all offers of marriage, they say."

"But what will become of her country if she kills all the babies?"

"She cares not for her country. She sends witches to teach women spells that repel infants and force them to eat horrors. Some say she is allied with the Shadows to end the race entirely. At night, we hear the questing beast and lie awake, shivering. She can sense the house where a baby is coming and lies in wait at the door. There are words of power to drive her away, but they do not always work. And here I sit talking, and the beast may have reached home already, with her mistress sending another after us!"

As she finished, she rose in haste.

"I do not think she will ever return home," I said, rising with her. "Let me carry the baby for you!"

She offered no answer. When I reached for the child, she clutched it tighter.

"I cannot understand," I said, walking beside her, "how the brute could bleed so much!"

"Take my advice and stay far from the palace," she warned. "There are sounds within it at night as if the dead are trying to shriek but cannot open their mouths!"

She bid me an abrupt farewell. Clearly, she desired no further company. I stood still, listening until her footsteps faded completely into the grass.
