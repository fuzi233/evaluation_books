Midnight, or roughly two hours after departing Helium, found Kantos Kan, Xodar, and myself hovering over Hastor. Carthoris, Tars Tarkas, and Hor Vastus had already departed on another cruiser for Thark. The transports were primed for immediate departure, moving slowly south, while the battleship fleet would intercept them by the morning of the second day.

Upon reaching Hastor, everything was in perfect readiness. Kantos Kan's planning had been flawless; within ten minutes of our arrival, the first great ship soared from its dock, followed thereafter at a rate of one per second, floating gracefully into the night to form a long, slender line stretching miles toward the south.

It was only after settling into Kantos Kan's cabin that I realized I had neglected to ask for the current date. Up until now, I had been unsure how long I had lain in the dark pits of Zat Arras. When Kantos Kan informed me, a pang of terrible dismay seized me. Three hundred and sixty-five days had passed. It was too late to save Dejah Thoris.

The nature of the expedition had shifted from rescue to revenge. I bit back the words, refusing to remind Kantos Kan of the tragic reality: before we could hope to breach the Temple of Issus, the Princess of Helium would likely be dust. As far as I knew, she might already be dead, for I did not know the precise date on which she first beheld the goddess Issus.

Would it serve anyone to burden my companions with my personal sorrows? They had shared enough of mine in the past. From this moment forward, I would keep my grief silent. Yet, the mission still held value. If we could teach the people of Barsoom the truth of the cruel deception practiced upon them for countless ages, saving thousands annually from the horrific fate awaiting pilgrims, we would have succeeded. If we could open the fair Valley Dor to the Red Men, revealing the naturally productive lands between the Mountains of Otz and the ice barrier, we would have done much. Here, at the bottom of a dying world, lay the only area capable of natural production, blessed with rain, dew, and an open sea. All else was blocked by the wicked remnants of two fallen races. If I could break the barrier of religious superstition holding the Red Men back from this *El Dorado*, it would be a fitting memorial to the immortal virtues of my Princess. Her martyrdom would not have been in vain.

By the second dawn, the great fleet of transports and their escorts lifted off. Soon, we were close enough to exchange signals. Note that radio-aerograms are rarely used in wartime, nor for secret dispatches, for nations exhaust every cryptographic possibility in a relentless race to intercept and translate messages. Consequently, no nation dares transmit important communications wirelessly anymore.

Tars Tarkas reported the transports were secure. The battleships pushed forward to an advanced position, and the combined fleets moved slowly over the ice cap, hugging the surface to avoid detection by the approaching Therns. Far ahead, a thin line of one-man air scouts protected us, flanking us on both sides, with a smaller group bringing up the rear twenty miles behind the transports.

After several hours progressing toward the entrance of Omean, a scout returned from the front, reporting that the cone-like summit was in sight. Simultaneously, another scout raced from the left flank toward the flagship, his speed betraying the urgency of his news.

"A great fleet of battleships, South-South-East, my Prince!" he cried, bounding up the stairs to the bridge. "There must be several thousands, bearing down directly upon us!"

"The Thern spies were not idle in the palace of John Carter," Kantos Kan observed. "Prince, give your orders."

"Dispatch ten battleships to guard the entrance to Omean," I commanded. "Let no hostile enter or leave the shaft. This will bottle up the Great Fleet of the First Born."

"Form the remainder of the battleships into a great 'V' with the apex pointing South-South-East," I continued, my voice steady despite the turmoil. "Order the transports, shielded by their convoys, to follow closely in the wake. When the point of the 'V' enters the enemy line, open outward at the apex. The battleships of each leg must engage fiercely, driving the enemy back to form a lane. Through this gap, the transports must race at top speed to gain a position above the Thern temples and gardens. Let them land and teach these Holy Therns a lesson in ferocious warfare they will never forget."

"It had not been my intention to be distracted from the main issue," I admitted, "but we must settle this attack once and for all, or there will be no peace while our fleet remains near Dor, and our chances of returning to the outer world will vanish."

Kantos Kan saluted and relayed the orders. In seconds, the formation changed. Ten ships sped toward Omean, while the troopships closed ranks, preparing for the final sprint.

Full speed ahead. The fleet sprang through the air like coursing greyhounds. In a heartbeat, the enemy ships came into full view—a ragged line extending as far as the eye could see, three ships deep. Our onslaught was so sudden it was like lightning from a clear sky.

Every phase of my plan executed with splendor. Our massive hulls cut a path entirely through the line of Thern battlecraft. Then the 'V' opened, revealing a broad lane through which the transports leaped toward the gleaming temples of the Therns. By the time the enemy rallied, a hundred thousand Green Warriors poured through their courts, while a hundred and fifty thousand others swung from low-hanging transports, raining deadly fire upon the ramparts.

Above the fiendish din of battle in the gorgeous gardens, the two great fleets engaged in a titanic struggle. Slowly, the lines of Helium's battleships joined, commencing the circling maneuver so characteristic of Barsoomian naval warfare. Under Kantos Kan, the ships moved in tight circles, presenting difficult targets. Broadside after broadside thundered as vessels aligned. The Therns attempted to rush in and break the formation, but it was futile, like trying to stop a buzzsaw with a bare hand.

From my vantage point beside Kantos Kan, I watched ship after ship of the enemy take the awful, sickening dive proclaiming total destruction. We maneuvered our circle of death until we hovered above the gardens where our Green allies fought. The order passed down for them to embark. Slowly, they rose to the center of the circle.

Meanwhile, the Therns' fire had practically ceased. They had had enough and were eager for us to depart in peace. But our escape was not to be so easy. Scarcely had we gained way toward Omean when a great black line topped the northern horizon. It could be nothing other than a fleet of war.

Who they belonged to or where they were bound, we could not guess. When they closed in, Kantos Kan's operator received a radio-aerogram, handing it instantly to Kantos Kan, who passed it to me.

*"Kantos Kan: Surrender, in the name of the Jeddak of Helium, for you cannot escape."* Signed, *Zat Arras.*

The Therns must have intercepted and translated the message almost simultaneously, for they immediately renewed hostilities, realizing we faced a new threat. Before Zat Arras could fire a shot, we were hotly engaged with the Thern fleet, and as he drew near, he unleashed a terrifying fusillade of heavy shot. Ship after ship reeled and staggered into uselessness beneath the pitiless barrage.

The situation could not last. I ordered the transports to descend again into the Thern gardens. *"Wreak your vengeance to the utmost,"* I signaled to the Green Allies. *"For by night, there will be none left to avenge your wrongs."*

Suddenly, I saw the ten battleships ordered to hold the shaft of Omean returning at full speed, firing stern batteries almost continuously. There could be but one explanation: they were being pursued by another hostile fleet. Well, the situation could not worsen. The expedition was already doomed. No man aboard would return across that dreary ice cap. I wished I could face Zat Arras with my longsword for just an instant before I died! It was he who had caused our failure.

Watching the oncoming ten, I saw their pursuers race into sight. Another great fleet! For a moment, I refused to believe my eyes, but I was forced to admit the most fatal calamity had overtaken us: the fleet I saw was none other than the Fleet of the First Born, which should have been safely bottled up in Omean. What a series of misfortunes! What awful fate hovered over me, thwarting my search for my lost love at every angle? Could it be possible that the Curse of Issus was upon me? That there existed some malign divinity within that hideous carcass? I would not believe it.

Throwing back my shoulders, I ran to the deck below to join my men in repelling boarders from a Thern craft that had grappled us broadside. In the wild lust of hand-to-hand combat, my old dauntless hopefulness returned. As Thern after Thern fell beneath my blade, I could almost feel that we would win success, even from apparent failure.
