The air was heavy with anticipation as I navigated the labyrinthine corridors of the pensionnat, my thoughts consumed by the enigmatic encounter with M. Paul the previous evening. His words, once laced with warmth and affection, now hung in the balance, leaving me to ponder the depth of our newly forged bond. As I ascended the stairs, the soft whisper of the convent's interior seemed to echo the doubts that plagued my mind: could I truly claim him as a brother, or was I merely a convenient recipient of his benevolent gestures?

I had always been drawn to the sincerity in his voice, the gentle cadence of his words, and the way he spoke of me with a tenderness that bordered on familial devotion. He had called me "sœur," and I had welcomed the title, yet a nagging sense of trepidation lingered, a fear that his professions of affection might be nothing more than a mere courtesy. The thought of being relegated to a secondary position, one that existed solely to serve as a placeholder until he found a suitable wife, was a prospect I dared not contemplate.

As the morning dragged on, I found myself lost in a sea of distractions, my attention fragmented between the mundane tasks of breakfast and prayer, and the more pressing concern of deciphering the nuances of M. Paul's intentions. I yearned for the arrival of the afternoon, when the lesson of literature would provide a respite from my inner turmoil. I hoped to glean insight into the workings of his heart, to discern the balance between brotherly love and platonic affection that seemed to govern our relationship.

The hours ticked by with glacial slowness, each one a testament to my growing unease. And then, as the clock struck the appointed hour, I made my way to the large second division, the designated meeting place for our lessons. The room was deserted, bathed in the cool shadows that crept in through the windows, but beyond the open double doors, the carré pulsed with life, its occupants a kaleidoscope of color and movement set against the vibrant backdrop of the setting sun. Its radiance cast an otherworldly glow, one that seemed to highlight the uncertainty that had taken up residence within me.
