The night air clung to the desert sands like a damp shroud, the chill of the north wind whipping across the dunes as Trench urged his camel forward. "Faster," he muttered, his teeth clenched against the biting gusts. "Idris may already be closing in."

Feversham glanced back towards the fading glow of Omdurman, his eyes straining to pierce the darkness. Though the drums had fallen silent, he couldn't shake the feeling of being watched, the oppressive stillness of the desert a heavy blanket that threatened to suffocate him. Every snap of a twig, every creak of leather, seemed amplified in the eerie quiet. Trench, however, rode with his gaze fixed on the horizon, his jaw set in a resolute line.

Fear gnawed at him, but he dared not look back, lest he betray his unease. Instead, his mind conjured images of the narrow streets behind them, the scattered fires casting flickering shadows on the walls, and the frantic search parties racing through the town, their torches casting wild silhouettes against the buildings. The thought sent a shiver down his spine.

For a moment, his focus wavered, and he turned to Feversham with a curt question: "What is it?"

"The fires of Omdurman," Feversham replied, his voice low and steady. "They're gone."

Trench's response was abstracted, his words tumbling out as if driven by a distant memory: "The golden blot, very low down?" Feversham didn't press for an explanation, sensing that Trench's thoughts were elsewhere, lost in a realm beyond the desert's grasp.

As they rode on, the darkness coalesced into two figures emerging from the shadows ahead, their forms indistinct until they drew closer. Abou Fatma's low cry – "Instanna!" – broke the spell, and Trench reined in his camel, his eyes narrowing as he assessed the newcomers.

The camels were changed, replaced with fresh beasts from the Anafi breed, known for their swiftness and endurance. As the saddles were transferred, Trench's anxiety grew, his questions tumbling out in rapid succession: "Are the arms ready? Where are they?" He scanned the ground, his fingers twitching with anticipation.
