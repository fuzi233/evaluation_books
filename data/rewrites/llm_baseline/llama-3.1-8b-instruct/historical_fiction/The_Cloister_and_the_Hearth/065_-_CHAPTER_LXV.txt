The stormy evening air clung to Gerard like a damp shroud as he stumbled into a humble tavern in one of Rome's lesser-known alleys. The scent of old wine and stale bread wafted through the air, drawing him in like a moth to flame. He called for a cup, and the rain drumming against the roof above seemed to wash away his cares, leaving him drowsy and disoriented. His head slumped onto his hands, which rested on the worn wooden table, as the patrons' raucous laughter and hushed conversations blended together in a soothing melody.

As the night wore on, the tavern grew busier, and the din of the crowd slowly roused Gerard from his stupor. His gaze drifted toward two figures huddled in the corner, speaking in hushed tones. One was a pardoner, his robes a deep crimson, his eyes gleaming with a mixture of conviction and calculation. The other, dressed in simple yet modest attire, kept his face hidden beneath a slouched hat, his beard a dark shadow across his chin.

Their conversation revolved around the exchange of indulgences, the pardoner enumerating the prices for various transgressions. Gerard's ears picked up snippets of their discussion – the cost of murdering a layperson, the disparity between the rates for killing a clerk, and the notion that certain crimes were more lucrative than others. Though he listened intently, his attention was dulled, his mind mired in his own sorrow and despair.

As the two men concluded their transaction and parted ways, Gerard's gaze followed the pardoner, his eyes narrowing into a scowl. A memory of Margaret, his lost love, flashed before him, and his thoughts turned bitter and dark. If she were alive, he vowed to confront the man, to throttle him for his cowardly crimes. But she was gone, lost to the world, and Gerard's anger dissipated, leaving only a hollow ache.

Outside, on the opposite side of the street, a lone figure walked briskly, his hat pulled low over his face. Gerard's eyes flickered toward him, but the sight held no significance, his mind too clouded by grief to grasp its importance. The rain continued to fall, washing away the world's troubles, but Gerard's sorrow remained, a heavy burden he carried alone.
