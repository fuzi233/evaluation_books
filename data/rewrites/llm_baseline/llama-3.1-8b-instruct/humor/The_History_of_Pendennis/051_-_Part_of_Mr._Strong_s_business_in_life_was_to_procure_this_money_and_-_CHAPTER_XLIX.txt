As Pen entered the dimly lit common room at Shepherd's Inn, his boisterous greeting cut through the stillness like a splash of cool water. "How d'ye do, Mr. Bows!" he exclaimed, shaking the older man's hand with a firm grip. A faint flush crept onto Arthur's face, and Fanny's cheeks followed suit, their faces mirroring each other like two halves of a single, rosy whole. Mr. Bows, however, seemed entirely unflappable, his eyes glinting with a knowing light behind his wire-rimmed spectacles.

Pendennis attempted to lighten the mood with a jest, launching into an imitation of Costigan's hectoring tones at Vauxhall Gardens the previous night. His performance was met with an uncomfortable silence, punctuated only by the creaking of the wooden floorboards beneath the ticket-porter's footsteps outside. Fanny's giggles were stifled, and Mrs. Bolton's brow furrowed in confusion, unsure whether she was witnessing a parody of the check-taker or the irascible Captain himself. Old Mr. Bows, meanwhile, looked as glum as a fiddler at a funeral, his eyes clouded with a mixture of sadness and memories.

As the silence deepened, Pen's words trailed off, extinguished like a candle flame in the draft from an open window. The air was heavy with unspoken tensions, the only sound the soft creaking of the wooden sign above the entrance, swaying gently in the breeze. Mr. Bows broke the spell, his voice low and measured, like the ticking of a grandfather clock. "You were coming to see me, sir. Won't you have the kindness to walk up to my chambers with me?"

Pendennis hesitated, his pride stinging from the rebuff, but Fanny's presence stayed his tongue. He smiled wryly, his eyes flashing with amusement. "Oh, I live in a garret myself, and Shepherd's Inn is twice as cheerful as Lamb Court," he said, his words dripping with a quiet defiance.

Mr. Bows' eyes narrowed, his voice taking on a subtle edge. "I knew you had third-floor apartments, and was going to say...the air up three pair of stairs is wholesomer for gentlemen than the air of a porter's lodge." Pendennis bristled at the implication, his candle-like temper flaring to life once more.
