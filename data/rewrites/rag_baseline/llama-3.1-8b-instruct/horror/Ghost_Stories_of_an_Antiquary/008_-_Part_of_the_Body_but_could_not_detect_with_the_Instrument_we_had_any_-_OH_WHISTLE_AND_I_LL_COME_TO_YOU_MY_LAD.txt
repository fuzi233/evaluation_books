As I stepped into the grand, high-ceilinged hall of St James's College, the scent of roasting meats wafted through the air, mingling with the soft hum of conversation. The Professor of Ontography, a man of precision and order, took his seat beside me, his eyes scanning the tables as if mentally mapping the evening's proceedings. I turned to him and said, "I suppose you'll be leaving soon, now that Full Term is over?"

"Yes," he replied, his voice crisp and measured. "My friends have been encouraging me to take up golf, and I plan to visit the East Coast – specifically Burnstow – for a week or ten days to hone my skills. I aim to depart tomorrow."

A neighbor to our left, an individual with a keen interest in antiquarian pursuits, leaned in, his eyes shining with enthusiasm. "If you're traveling to Burnstow, Professor, I'd be grateful if you could examine the site of the Templars' preceptory and share your thoughts on its potential for excavation. I'm considering taking my family there during the Long Vacation and thought it might be an opportunity to contribute to the field."

The Professor nodded, his expression thoughtful. "Of course, I'd be happy to provide an assessment when I return. Alternatively, if you'd care to share your location, I could write to you with my findings."

His neighbor smiled, his manner affable. "No need to trouble yourself, thank you. I simply thought it might be an interesting project, given the scarcity of planned excavations at English preceptories. As you know, the sea has made significant inroads along that stretch of coastline, and I suspect the site lies roughly three-quarters of a mile from the Globe Inn, at the northern end of the town."

I watched as the Professor's expression turned slightly skeptical, his eyes narrowing as he considered the proposal. "I've booked a room at the Globe Inn myself," he said finally. "It's the largest available, but it's a double-bedded room, and they've nowhere to store the second bed. Still, I need a spacious room for my research, and I'm willing to make do for the duration of my stay."
