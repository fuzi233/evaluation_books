The kitchen, ablaze with the warm glow of sizzling onions and the savory aroma of simmering stew, was a sanctuary where I often sought refuge from the turmoil that swirled outside. Today, however, it was not the haven I craved. My mistress, Lady Glyde, emerged from her chambers, her face ashen, and her eyes wild with fear. Mr. Franklin, the young gentleman who had recently become a fixture in our household, trailed behind her, attempting to offer what little comfort he could.

As we converged on the entrance hall, I caught sight of Sergeant Cuff, the stalwart lawman who had been tasked with unraveling the mysteries plaguing our household. His rugged features and imposing stature commanded respect, even in the face of my mistress's ire. "You are accountable for this!" she exclaimed, her voice shrill with anguish, as she confronted the Sergeant. "Gabriel, give that wretch his payment and release me from the sight of him!"

Sergeant Cuff, ever the paragon of composure, met her outburst with a firm but respectful demeanor. "I am no more responsible for this distressing calamity than you are, my lady," he said, his deep voice a balm to the stormy atmosphere. "If, in half an hour from now, you still insist on my departure, I will accept your ladyship's dismissal, but not your ladyship's payment."

His words, like a gentle breeze on a summer's day, soothed the tempestuous Lady Glyde, and she allowed Mr. Franklin to escort her back to her chambers. As the door closed behind them, Sergeant Cuff surveyed the assembled servants, his keen eye lingering on Penelope, my daughter, who stood frozen in tears. "When your father has changed his wet clothes," he said to her, "come and speak to us in his room."

I hastened to fetch dry clothes for the Sergeant, while Penelope, still shaken, made her way to our quarters. As she entered, I swept her onto my lap and prayed for her safety, my heart heavy with concern. She hid her face on my chest, wrapping her arms around my neck, and we sat in silence, the only sound the crackling of the fire and the Sergeant's distant murmur as he gazed out the window.
