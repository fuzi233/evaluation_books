As the morning sun cast its golden rays over the camp, François surveyed the scene with a mixture of relief and trepidation. Spitz, the dominant dog, was nowhere to be found, and Buck, his massive frame covered in wounds, stood watchfully beside the fire. The dog-driver pointed out the gashes and lacerations, his voice low and measured.

"Dat Spitz fight lak hell," Perrault observed, his eyes narrowing as he examined the damage.

"An' dat Buck fight lak two hells," François replied, a hint of a smile playing on his lips. "An' now we make good time. No more Spitz, no more trouble, sure."

As Perrault busied himself packing the camp equipment, François began to harness the dogs. Buck, sensing an opportunity, approached the vacant spot at the lead, where Spitz had once held sway. However, François, oblivious to the challenge, called Sol-leks to the position, deeming the old dog the most suitable lead-canine. Buck, undeterred, sprang forward, forcing Sol-leks to retreat, and claimed the coveted spot for himself.

"Eh? Eh?" François exclaimed, a gleam of amusement in his eye. "Look at dat Buck. Heem keel dat Spitz, heem t'ink to take de job."

But when François attempted to displace Buck, the younger dog resisted, his growls a clear warning. Undeterred, François tried to drag Buck aside, replacing Sol-leks in the lead position. However, Buck refused to yield, his eyes flashing with determination. The old dog, sensing the tension, grew increasingly agitated, its fear of Buck palpable.

François, growing frustrated, threatened to use his club, but Buck, remembering the previous beating, retreated cautiously. Though he did not attempt to attack, he circled warily, his gaze fixed on the driver, wary of another thrashing. The air was heavy with tension as François worked, Perrault watching with a mixture of concern and amusement.

After a time, François laid down the club, assuming Buck had retreated due to fear. But the younger dog was far from cowed. He wanted the leadership, and he was determined to claim it. Perrault intervened, and together they attempted to subdue Buck, but the dog dodged and weaved, his snarls a testament to his unyielding spirit.
