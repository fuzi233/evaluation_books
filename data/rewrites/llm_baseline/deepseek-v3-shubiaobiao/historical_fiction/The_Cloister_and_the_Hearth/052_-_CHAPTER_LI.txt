When Margaret descended the stairs, she was transformed. Though her eyes still glistened with tears, a quiet resolve had settled over her. The storm of bitterness had passed, leaving behind a woman determined in her purpose.

“Denys,” she said softly, “I have my orders. I am to read my love’s letter to his family.”

Denys blinked in surprise. “You’d truly do that?”

“Without hesitation.”

“Has the letter softened you toward them, then?”

“Not in the least,” she replied firmly. “But if I despised them with every fiber of my being, I would still obey Gerard’s wish. There is sweetness in obedience, Denys—especially when it is given freely to one who is far away, who cannot command but must trust love alone to ensure it. Ah, Gerard, my heart, if you were here, I might have resisted, for your family and I have no kindness between us. But now? If you bade me stand before a raging sea and read your words to the sharks, I would do it without fear. So tell his mother this: I have a letter. If she and hers wish to hear it, they need only name the hour. I will receive them as best I can.”

Denys carried the message to Catherine, finding the family gathered at dinner. The announcement of Gerard’s letter brought instant joy—until he added Margaret’s condition.

“What?” Catherine scoffed. “Does she think none of us can read but her? Let her send the letter, and we’ll manage well enough.”

Little Kate, ever gentle, ventured, “Perhaps she cannot bear to part with it. She loves him dearly.”

Cornelis sneered. “Or she seeks to worm her way into the family by this means.”

Denys shot him a look of contempt. “That’s a foul thought. The girl hates you all like poison—make no mistake. Yet for Gerard’s sake, she offers to read his letter to you herself. It’s a kindness, whether you see it or not.”

Eli, ever the voice of reason, silenced the squabble. “Enough. Tell her this: I, his father, set aside all past grudges and thank her for the offer. But if she would double my thanks, let her entrust the letter to you, Denys. I will read it to my blood and return it faithfully—on my honor as a free burgher of Tergou.”

Denys chuckled. “A man of sense! She’ll be glad to obey Gerard without facing you all—for she’d sooner sup with serpents than break bread here, no offense.”

But when he returned to Margaret, she refused. “Not to you, not to them, not to anyone living.”

Denys groaned. “What of the obedience you spoke of?”

She arched a brow. “Obedience to Gerard is sweet. Obedience to anyone else? Who ever claimed that was sweet?”

At last, she relented enough to give him a scrap of paper—a copy of Gerard’s own words, addressed to Kate.

*"If these lines reach you, test my family’s hearts. If they are softened toward me, let them hear this letter—but never let it leave your hand. Should they still turn from you, say nothing. Let them think me dead. Disobey me in this, and you prove your love false."*

The room fell silent as the words were read aloud.

Catherine pursed her lips. “Well. That changes things. Still, she should come to us, not we to her.”

Eli sighed. “Tell her we are too many to crowd into her home. Bid her come here—the sooner, the better.”

When Denys left, Eli muttered, “A bitter draught, this. To welcome under my roof the girl who has brought us such grief… But what choice have we? We must hear Gerard’s words.”

Catherine patted his arm. “Don’t fret. She won’t come.”

Yet soon enough, Denys returned with the answer: *"She will be here at three by the clock."*

The family exchanged glances, none speaking. The reckoning was at hand.
