Years had passed since our last meeting, the world outside changing as the seasons changed. I thought of my wife, Madam Esmond, her gentle nature a balm to my own rough edges. She had seen the best and worst of me, and still, she loved me. I wondered if she had ever seen the same hardness in herself, the same unwillingness to yield. Perhaps it was this very quality that had drawn us together, two souls forged in the fire of our own self-preservation.

As I worked, my mind wandered to the memories of my past, the faces of those who had shaped me into the man I was today. There was my uncle, a man of means and influence, who had pitied me with a condescending smile. His love for my wife and child had been genuine, but his affection for me was tempered by a sense of disappointment. He had always seen me as a failure, a man who had squandered his potential on petty squabbles and narrow-minded pursuits. I had never sought his approval, content to forge my own path, even if it meant walking alone.

The words on the page blurred together as I thought of my letter to the member of the House of Burgesses, a passionate outburst that had cost me my position as hackney-coach licenser. I had spoken truth to power, and power had struck back. But I had not regretted my words, even as I faced the consequences. It was a lesson I had learned early on: that some debts were too great to pay, and some wounds too deep to heal.
