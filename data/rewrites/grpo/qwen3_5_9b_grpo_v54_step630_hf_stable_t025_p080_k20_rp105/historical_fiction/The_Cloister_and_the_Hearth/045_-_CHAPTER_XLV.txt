The gathering at the hosier's dwelling paused mid-discourse as the mother entered, her presence instantly silencing the room. With a gaze that sliced through the air, she swept over the assembled men before laying a letter flat upon the table. She recited its contents from memory, tracing the invisible lines with a trembling finger, a desperate ritual to convince herself—and them—that she could still decipher the script. Then, her head snapped up, and her eyes locked onto Cornelis and Sybrandt. Their gazes dropped to the floor.

In that instant, the tempest that had been gathering in the cellar broke loose.

Catherine seemed to inflate with a fury akin to a hen ruffling her plumage, unleashing a torrent of wisdom and nonsense, of grand and petty invectives, that no man in history could have voiced in a single breath, let alone a woman. A sampling of her words follows, though she delivered them all in one relentless rush:

"I have long suspected that you kindled the fire between Gerard and your father, setting that old rogue, Ghysbrecht, to stoke it. Now, here are Gerard's own words to confirm your treachery. You have driven your own flesh and blood into a distant land, stealing from this mother who bore you her darling, the pride of her sight, the joy of her heart. Yet, you remain one and the same from start to finish. When we were all boys, my others were a comfort; you were a curse—mischievous and sly, requiring a woman half a day to mend your torn garments. Why? For labor wears cloth, but play cuts it. Prudence arrives with the beard, but it never came to you. You were the last to retire and the last to rise, for honesty sleeps early and industry wakes before dawn; where two sluggards dwell in a house, there dwells a pair of ne'er-do-wells. I have often sat watching your ways, wondering whence you sprang. You bear no resemblance to your father, and you are no more like me than a wasp is to an ant. Surely, you were swapped in the cradle, or a cuckoo dropped you upon my floor, for you lack our hands and our hearts. Of all my blood, none but you ever mocked those God afflicted. Often, with my back turned, I heard you sneer at Giles for not being as large as others, and at my lily Kate for lacking the strength of a Flemish mare. After that, you would rob a church! For in the eyes of Him who made both Kate and Giles, and in mine who suffered for them as I did for you, you paltry, unfeeling, treasonable curs, you can be no worse!

"No, I will not hush, my daughter; they have filled the cup to the brim. It takes a great deal to turn a mother's heart against the sons she has nursed upon her knees. Many times I have winked, choosing not to see too much, biting my tongue lest your father discover what I knew; he would have cast you out that very moment. But now, the cup is full. Where did you acquire this wealth? For the past month, you have rolled in it without a single stitch of honest work. I pray I may never hear from another mouth how you obtained it. It is since that night you stayed out so late, returning with a head swollen with pride, Cornelis. Sloth and greed are a fatal pairing, my masters. Those who love money must either sweat or steal. Well, if you robbed any poor soul of it, it was surely some woman, for I'll go bail a man would drive you with his bare hand. Be that as it may, it serves one purpose. It has revealed how you would manage our affairs if they ever fell to your keeping. I have watched you, my lads, for some time. You have spent a groat between you today. I spend scarce a groat a week and yet keep you all, good and bad. No, I give up waiting for the shoes that might walk behind your coffins. This shop and this house shall never be yours. Gerard is our heir; poor Gerard, whom you have banished and done your utmost to destroy. After that, never call me mother again! But you have made him tenfold dearer to me. My poor lost boy! I shall soon see him again, hold him in my arms, and set him upon my knees. Yes, you may stare! You are too crafty, yet not crafty enough. You severed the stalk, but you left the seed—the seed that shall outgrow you and outlive you. Margaret Brandt is quick, and the child is Gerard's, and what is Gerard's is mine. I have prayed the saints it may be a boy, and it will—it must. Kate, when I learned the truth, my bowels yearned over her unborn child as if it were my own. He is our heir. He will outlive us. You will not; for a bad heart in a carcass is like a worm in a nut, swiftly bringing the body to dust. So, Kate, take down Gerard's bib and tucker from the drawer you know of, and one of these days we shall carry them to Sevenbergen. We will borrow Peter Buyskens' cart and go to comfort Gerard's wife under her burden. She is his wife. Who is Ghysbrecht Van Swieten? Can he stand between a couple and the altar, and sunder those whom God and the priest have joined? She is my daughter, and I am as proud of her as I am of you, Kate, almost. As for you, keep out of my way for a while, for you are like the black dog in my eyes."

Cornelis and Sybrandt took the hint and slunk from the room, their hearts aching with remorse, impenitence, and a deep-seated hatred. They avoided her gaze as much as possible, and for many days thereafter, she spoke not a single word—good, bad, or indifferent—to either of them. She had finally freed her spirit.
