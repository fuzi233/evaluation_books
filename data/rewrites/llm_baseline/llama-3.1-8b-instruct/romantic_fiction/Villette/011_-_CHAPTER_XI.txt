In the sweltering summer heat, Georgette, the youngest of Madame Beck's children, fell ill, prompting a flurry of activity within the pensionnat. Désirée, suddenly freed from her own ailments, and Fifine were hastily dispatched to the countryside, where Madame Beck's sister resided, in an effort to prevent the spread of infection. As the situation grew more dire, Madame Beck opted to ignore the return of Dr. Pillule, instead summoning his English counterpart, Dr. John, to continue his visits to the ailing pupils. A few of the pensionnaires began to exhibit symptoms of a headache, leading me to believe that Dr. Pillule's expertise was sorely needed. "Now, indeed," I mused, "Madame Beck will have no choice but to recall Dr. Pillule; the prudent directress would never countenance the presence of such a young doctor among the pupils."

However, Madame Beck proved herself to be a woman of audacious conviction, introducing Dr. John to the school division of the premises and entrusting him with the care of the elegant Blanche de Melcy and the flirtatious Angélique. Dr. John seemed to derive a certain satisfaction from this mark of confidence, and if his demeanor were less suspect, he would have been amply justified in accepting the responsibility. Yet, in this land of convents and confessionals, a presence such as his was not to be tolerated without consequence. The school buzzed with gossip, the kitchen whispered, and the townspeople caught wind of the rumor, prompting parents to write letters and pay visits of remonstrance. If Madame Beck were weak, she would have been undone; a dozen rival institutions stood ready to capitalize on this misstep, should it be deemed such. But Madame Beck was not one to falter, and I found myself applauding her composure, her shrewd management, and her unwavering resolve in the face of adversity.

When confronted by the alarmed parents, Madame Beck presented a winsome, effortless charm, a quality she possessed in abundance, and which allowed her to defuse even the most tense situations with ease. "Ah, poor Dr. Jean!" she would exclaim, her hands fluttering in a gesture of affection, "he is such a dear young man, the best creature in the world!"
