The Piazza del Duomo was a canvas of vibrant blue and gold, its festive atmosphere palpable as the Advent season reached its crescendo. Amidst the throngs of people hurrying towards the Duomo, a sense of anticipation hung in the air, punctuated by the murmur of conversations and the rustle of fabrics. Yet, amidst this joyful chaos, a group of working-class men gathered at the corner of the Via de' Cerretani seemed detached, their rugged attire and worn tools a testament to their daily struggles. They stood under the fading light of the Piazza, their weathered faces a study in contrasts – some engaged in heated discussions, others intently listening to the words of Ser Cioni, a local notary, whose voice carried a hint of triumph.

As he spoke, his eyes scanned the crowd, his words dripping with conviction. "Florentines need fear no army in their own streets," he declared, his voice carrying across the Piazza. A nearby man, his arms and hands stained with crimson dye, nodded in agreement, his small hatchet at his belt a tangible symbol of the city's resilience. He recalled a recent incident involving French cavalry, who had been met with a fierce response from the locals. "I was carrying my cloth in Ognissanti when I saw them passing by, looking down on us as if we were inferior," he said, his voice laced with pride. "I told them, 'Stick out your stomachs; I've got a meat-axe in my belt that'll get the better of you.'"

The group listened intently, their faces a mix of curiosity and wariness, as Ser Cioni continued to speak. His words wove a tale of resistance and defiance, of a people who would not be intimidated by external threats. As he spoke, the sounds of the Piazza receded, replaced by the hum of a city on the brink of change. The air was alive with the scent of roasting chestnuts and baking bread, the aroma wafting from the nearby food stalls, a reminder of the simple pleasures that brought people together.

In the midst of this festive atmosphere, a young apprentice, Romola, navigated the crowded streets, her eyes scanning the market stalls for a glimpse of her brother, Tito. She had heard rumors of a potential job opportunity at the local silk factory, and she hoped to catch him before he began his shift.
