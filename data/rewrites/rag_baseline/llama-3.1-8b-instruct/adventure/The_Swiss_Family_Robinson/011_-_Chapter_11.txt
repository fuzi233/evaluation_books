As the sun rose over the horizon, casting a warm glow over the tranquil waters of the bay, I reflected on the logistical implications of our recent endeavors. The extraction of train oil from the blubber had been a necessary yet unpalatable task, one that required meticulous attention to detail and a robust system of distillation. My wife, ever the pragmatist, had astutely observed that our current location was ill-suited for such operations, given the lingering presence of the whale's putrid remains. She astutely proposed relocating to a new site, where we could establish a more sustainable colony, free from the depredations of marauding apes and other scavengers.

Her suggestion was met with enthusiasm from the children, who were always eager to explore new possibilities. However, I cautioned them against hasty action, citing the need for a more thorough cleansing of the island before we could safely reoccupy it. As we pondered our options, I found myself preoccupied with the idea of designing a mechanical aid to facilitate our navigation of the bay. The notion of a rowing-machine had been germinating in my mind, and I determined to bring it to fruition.

Utilizing an iron bar as the foundation, I constructed a rudimentary framework, incorporating ribbed machinery and navigational spokes to facilitate efficient propulsion. The jack, cleverly integrated into the design, enabled the machinery to rotate rapidly, thereby propelling the boat through the water. Although the prototype left room for improvement, a test run across the bay yielded promising results, and my family was overjoyed by the prospect of a future powered by this innovation.

The evening was spent preparing for an excursion to Cape Disappointment and the settlement of Prospect Hill, which would provide us with an opportunity to gather various plant species and begin establishing a new colony on Whale Island. We set out early the next morning, equipped with the necessary tools and supplies, including spades and mattocks for cultivating young coconut trees and shrubs. As we navigated the calm waters, the rowing-machine performed admirably, affording us a leisurely appreciation of the surrounding coastline.

Upon landing near Prospect Hill, we moored the boat and ventured into the nearby woods, where we collected fresh coconuts and various plant specimens. Before approaching our farm, we were greeted by the familiar sound of roosters crowing, which unexpectedly stirred a poignant recollection of past experiences, evoking a mix of nostalgia and trepidation.
