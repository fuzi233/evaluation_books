As the winter's chill began to thaw, John Thornton's partners had nursed him back to health before departing for Dawson, where they intended to fetch a raft of saw-logs. Though he still walked with a slight limp from his frostbitten toes, the warmer weather only served to hasten his recovery. And so, by the tranquil riverbank, surrounded by the soothing melodies of birdsong and the gentle hum of nature, Buck gradually regained his strength.

The prolonged repose following their arduous journey had done wonders for Buck, allowing his muscles to recover and his flesh to regain its former contours. His companions – John Thornton, Skeet the Irish setter, and Nig the gentle giant – were all waiting for the raft that would carry them downstream to Dawson. Skeet, with her compassionate nature, had taken it upon herself to care for Buck's wounds, gently washing and cleansing them each morning after his breakfast. As Buck's health improved, he grew to appreciate these tender gestures, almost as much as he cherished Thornton's affection.

Skeet's friendly advances had initially surprised Buck, who was still recovering from his ordeal. However, she soon won him over with her unwavering kindness, just as Nig had done. Though more reserved, Nig's boundless good nature and warm eyes put everyone at ease. The two dogs seemed to embody the same generosity and warmth that defined John Thornton, and Buck found himself drawn to their companionship.

As the days passed, Buck's convalescence transformed into a joyful celebration of life. Thornton, Skeet, and Nig would engage him in playful games, often involving ridiculous antics that left them all in stitches. Buck's heart swelled with gratitude for these new friendships, and he reveled in the love and acceptance he received from his companions. For the first time, he experienced the pure, all-consuming passion of love – a feeling he had never known during his time with the Judge's family in the sun-kissed Santa Clara Valley.

Thornton's unique bond with his dogs was a testament to his exceptional character. Unlike other men, who viewed their pets as mere assets or responsibilities, he saw them as beloved children, deserving of his unwavering devotion. He remembered every small gesture, every encouraging word, and cherished the quiet moments spent with them, which he affectionately referred to as "gas."
