The stench of the city’s corruption clung to us, but retreat was not an option. Lona’s disgust was palpable—she wanted to flee, to leave these hollow-eyed wretches to their fate. But I knew better. To run now would be to betray the Little Ones, to leave them vulnerable to the storm that would surely follow. And above all, it would deny the prophecy its due.

Mother and daughter *had* to meet.

Lona’s radiance might disarm Lilith’s cruelty. Or, if violence stirred, I would stand between them. If words failed, I would strike without mercy. The universe had woven this moment—doom coiled around Lilith’s throat, and we were the hands meant to tighten it.

I did not tell Lona the truth of their bond. She agreed to march to the palace regardless.

---

The princess had seen us coming.

At dawn, from her tower, she had watched the approach of the Little Ones—an army of innocence she had failed to obliterate. Terror, raw and choking, seized her. The prophecy was no longer a whisper in the dark. It was here.

She descended to the black hall, to the ellipse’s northern focus, beneath the open roof. Here, she *thought*—if one could call it thinking. To her, it was the act of shaping herself into the illusion she preferred, a queen unmarred by rot or regret.

Above her, unseen in the gloom, hung a mirror. She waited for the sun to climb, to illuminate her reflection, to gift her the vision of her own splendor. Shadows flitted at the edges of her sight, but she ignored them. Even the one that stood beneath the mirror, the silent attendant of her steps, went unnoticed.

The city fell. The people cowered. The Little Ones camped in the square, their strange mounts grazing on cobblestones. The sun rose, and for a fleeting moment, Lilith saw herself glorious—until the light shifted, and the mirror showed her the truth.

The black stain on her side had spread.

She recoiled, clutching her robe. The Shadow slipped away, and this time, she saw him go.

---

We rode to the palace at midday—twelve of the tallest boys alongside us, astride their small horses and elephants. The great hall waited, a maw of darkness.

Lilith sat in her chair, a shaft of sunlight crowning her, when the thunder of hooves shook the stones. Her breath hitched. No such sound had ever breached her sanctum.

We entered.

To us, she was a blaze in the gloom, a fallen star trapped in human form. To her, we were invaders, silhouettes against the light.

Then Lona leapt from her horse.

"Mother! *Mother!*"

The word tore through the hall, sweet and terrible.

Lilith’s face twisted. Hatchered her beauty into something monstrous. She stood, rigid, as Lona sprinted to the dais, arms outstretched—

And then, in a movement too quick to stop, Lilith seized her.

Lona’s body hit the marble with a sound that would haunt me forever.

I was too late.

I dropped to my knees, gathering her broken form against me. Blood slicked my hands, dripped onto the floor. The horses screamed, scenting it. Mine reared, bolted blindly into the dark, the others following in a panicked stampede. The Little Ones’ mounts crashed into the walls, their riders thrown clear.

Lona’s horse stood trembling over her, trunk outstretched as if to nudge her awake.

The princess slumped back in her chair, her face a corpse’s mask, her eyes burning. The rot had reclaimed her—her skin withered, the stain on her side a brand of damnation.

But I saw none of it. Only Lona.

"Mother…" she sighed.

Then nothing.

---

I carried her into the courtyard, the sun glaring down on her pallid face. Her head lolled. She was gone.

The world narrowed to silence.

I forgot the Little Ones. Forgot the murderess on her throne. Forgot even the weight in my arms. I wandered, numb, until the emptiness of the square jolted me back.

I had lost them too.

Staggering to the broken pillar, I collapsed at its base. And then—

A flicker. A ghost of a smile on Lona’s still lips.

I knew it was illusion. And yet I believed it.

---

The city was waking, its people gathering like vultures. They jeered, but none dared approach—not with the white leopardess slinking at my heels. I kicked at her. She hesitated, then followed.

At the gate, the crowd grew bold. A man shoved me, greedy hands reaching for my burden.

I broke his ribs with a kick.

Then—chaos. A roar from behind. The mob scattered as the Little Ones surged through, astride their elephants.

And there, bound atop two of the beasts, was Lilith.

Her eyes rolled, wild and unseeing. The elephants’ trunks probed her bonds, ensuring they held.

Behind her, on Lona’s horse, rode two more children.

We marched out of the city, victorious and ruined.

We had taken the princess.

And lost our queen.
