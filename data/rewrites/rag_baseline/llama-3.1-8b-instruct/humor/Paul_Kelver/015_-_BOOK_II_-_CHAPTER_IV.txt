As I stood beside the O'Kelly at the entrance of the narrow court leading to the stage door, the morning sunlight danced across the pavement, casting intricate patterns on the ground. It was eleven o'clock, and for a fortnight, the O'Kelly had been guiding me through the intricacies of music. The air was alive with the scent of blooming flowers from the nearby gardens, a sweet contrast to the staccato notes emanating from the American organ behind us. Mrs. O'Kelly, a woman with a stern demeanor and an unwavering commitment to sacred music, had banished the piano from their home, replacing it with this lugubrious instrument. Her hope was to steer her husband away from what she saw as sinful indulgences, and though the O'Kelly's talents had managed to coax a semblance of harmony from the organ, our practice sessions had become a laborious affair.

My rendition of "Sally in Our Alley" had taken on a melancholic tone, a far cry from the intended lightheartedness. The O'Kelly would often caution me to "keep it lighter," to eschew the soulful notes that seemed to reverberate through the instrument. As we practiced, I couldn't help but wonder about the couple's marriage. Was there a discordant note within the O'Kelly's nature that would clash with the carefree spirit of his beloved Sally?

Just as we were nearing a breakthrough, Mrs. O'Kelly returned from a meeting with the district visitors' committee earlier than expected. I was hastily hidden in a nearby conservatory, where I crouched behind a row of flowerpots, listening to the O'Kelly's hasty explanation for his earlier performance. His wife was not pleased, and her reprimand was swift and severe.

"William, do not prevaricate," she chided. "It was not a hymn."

"I was simply experimenting with a different interpretation, my dear," the O'Kelly replied, attempting to placate her.

Mrs. O'Kelly's expression remained skeptical. "William, pray in my presence not to play tricks with sacred melodies. If you have no respect for religion, please remember that I have. Besides, why should you be playing hymns in any time at ten o'clock in the morning? It is not like you, William, and I do not credit your explanation. And you were singing. I distinctly heard the word 'Sally' as I opened the door."
