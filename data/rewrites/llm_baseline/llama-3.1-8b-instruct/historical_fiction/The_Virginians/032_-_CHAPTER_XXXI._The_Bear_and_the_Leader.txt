As the warm sunlight cast its gentle rays upon the tranquil town of Tunbridge Wells, a subtle undercurrent of gossip swirled beneath the surface, concerning the young Virginian, Harry Warrington. Many of the town's matrons, particularly Madame de Bernstein, harbored suspicions about the young man's character, their imaginations fueled by whispers of his alleged escapades with the infamous Cattarina. Though the truth behind Harry's connection to the young woman remained unclear, Madame de Bernstein remained resolute in her conviction that her nephew was leading a life of reckless abandon, one that would have made even the most refined among them blush.

In her youth, Madame de Bernstein had been known for her fiery spirit, a true dragon of the Roman variety, feared and respected by all who knew her. But as the seasons passed, her vigor waned, and her once-sharp tongue grew dull. Her scales, once armor-plated with sharp wit and cunning, now felt soft and pliable, making her an easy target for those who sought to manipulate her. As she lounged in her chair, surrounded by the comforts of her well-appointed parlor, Madame de Bernstein found herself lulled into a state of complacency, her mind distracted by the trivialities of her own life – her dinner parties, her aching joints, and the latest gossip from the town.

The society at Tunbridge Wells, ever eager to indulge in the juiciest scandals, devoured every tale of Harry's supposed misdeeds, their imaginations running wild with speculation. They whispered of his alleged infidelities, naming names and sharing stories of his supposed conquests. Meanwhile, the reality of the situation remained shrouded in mystery, lost amidst the din of idle chatter and malicious rumors.

As the days passed, Madame de Bernstein's concerns about her nephew's relationship with Lady Maria gradually dissipated, replaced by a growing indifference. Her once-vigilant eye had grown dim, and her ears, attuned to the whispers of the townspeople, had begun to filter out the truth, replacing it with convenient half-truths and outright fabrications. In her twilight years, Madame de Bernstein had become a shadow of her former self, a relic of a bygone era, when dragons roamed the earth and their fiery breath could reduce reputations to ashes. Now, her malevolent powers were reduced to a mere whisper, a faint echo of a once-formidable force.
