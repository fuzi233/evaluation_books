The shadows cast by the morning sun danced across the walls of our estate as I stood beside Madame, our eyes fixed on the doorway where the doctor awaited us. My father had summoned him, and I had no inkling of what this visit portended. The servant who slept outside Carmilla's door had done her job well, for she did not emerge until the sun rose high in the sky.

As we made our way to the library, the doctor's presence weighed heavily on my mind. His somber demeanor and wispy white hair only added to the air of foreboding that clung to him like a damp mist. We found him seated in the window recess, his eyes red-rimmed from lack of sleep, his spectacles perched on the end of his nose. I recounted my tale to him, and as I spoke, I watched as his expression darkened, a mixture of concern and horror etched on his face.

Madame and I stood together, our ears straining to catch the hushed tones of the doctor's words, but the thick wall and window formed a barrier between us, rendering their conversation unintelligible. I felt a shiver run down my spine as I gazed at the doctor, his eyes boring into mine as if searching for something hidden deep within.

The minutes ticked by, and my father entered the room, his smile faltering as he caught sight of the doctor's grave expression. "I dare say, doctor, you've come to tell me I'm an old fool for bringing you here," he said, his voice laced with a hint of trepidation. But the doctor's stern visage brooked no jests, and my father's smile quickly gave way to a look of alarm.

The two men retreated to the window recess, their voices barely audible as they engaged in a heated discussion. I watched, my heart pounding in my chest, as they spoke in hushed tones, their words lost to me. My father's face appeared in the doorway, pale and troubled, and he beckoned me to join them.
