As the days passed since Edward's joyful visit to Colonel Brandon, Lucy's spirits soared to unprecedented heights. When Mrs. Jennings arrived the following day to offer her congratulations, Lucy radiated an unbridled enthusiasm that left the older woman in awe. For the first time in his life, Edward's countenance shone with an unwavering sense of contentment, and his friends couldn't help but notice the profound change.

In turn, Lucy's own happiness was palpable, and she eagerly anticipated the day when they would all gather at Delaford Parsonage. With a warm smile, she expressed her gratitude towards Elinor, crediting her with a selflessness that would not go unnoticed. "No exertion on your part," she said, "whether past or future, would ever surprise me, for I firmly believe you capable of doing anything for those you truly value." As for Colonel Brandon, Lucy's admiration knew no bounds – she sought to emulate the reverence with which he was treated, ensuring his every need was met, from the augmentation of his tithes to the utilization of his estate's resources, including his carriage, livestock, and poultry.

It had been nearly a fortnight since John Dashwood's visit to Berkeley Street, and as the days went by without any further acknowledgement of Fanny's condition, Elinor felt compelled to pay her a visit. However, this obligation came with little enthusiasm from her companions. Marianne, in fact, strongly dissuaded her sister from going, while Mrs. Jennings, despite the availability of her carriage, harbored such disdain for Fanny that even the prospect of seeing her again couldn't overcome her reluctance. As a result, Elinor found herself setting out alone to face the unappealing prospect of a tête-à-tête with someone she didn't particularly care for.

Upon arriving at the Dashwood residence, Elinor was met with a disappointing reception. Fanny was unavailable, but her husband appeared in the doorway, beaming with pleasure at the sight of Elinor.
