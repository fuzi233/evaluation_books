The history of England is often told in terms of kings and battles, yet there exists another chronicle written in the blood of the common man and the dreams of the adventurous spirit. It is a tale that begins not with a crown, but with a white bird seen by Mr. Oxenham; it follows Amyas as he returns home for the first time, bearing secrets that will alter the course of his destiny. We shall witness the exploits of two gentlemen from Wales who, though they hunted with hounds, found themselves running alongside deer—a metaphor for their own elusive lives.

Love, too, has its crooked paths, and we shall see how two hearts were entangled in ways neither could foresee. The narrative takes us to Clovelly Court in days long past, through the winding combs of the far west, and into the tragic life of John Oxenham of Plymouth. From here, the story expands to include the founding of the Noble Brotherhood of the Rose, the peculiar observances of Christmas by Amyas, and the Mayor of Biddeford's bold act of baiting his hook with his own flesh.

Our heroes encounter the Pope's legate, share meals at Annery House where the bridge itself seemed to dine, and welcome back the Golden Hind. Salvation Yeo performs deeds of valor against the King of the Gubbings, while Mr. John Brimblecombe learns the true weight of an oath. The chivalrous adventures of the Good Ship *Rose* carry them to Barbadòs, where they find no men, only mysteries. They seize pearls at Margarita, face perils at La Guayra, and engage in a fierce contest between Spanish bloodhounds and English mastiffs.

Under the trees at Higuerote, they take communion amidst danger, confront the Inquisition in the Indies, and stand upon the banks of the Meta River. Amyas faces temptation from the devil himself, helps secure a golden train, and captures a great galleon. Salvation Yeo reunites with a lost little maid, and Amyas makes his third return home. The Virginia fleet is halted by royal command, Admiral John Hawkins testifies against Croakers, and the shadow of the Great Armada looms large.

In the end, Amyas throws his sword into the sea, letting the apple fall westward—ho! This is the story of those who dared to go forth when others stayed behind, a saga of courage, faith, and the relentless pursuit of liberty across the known world.
