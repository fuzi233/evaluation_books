As the sultry afternoon air clung to the streets of Milton like a damp shroud, Margaret made her way through the crowded thoroughfares, her footsteps quickening with anticipation. She had promised to visit Betsy Higgins, whose fragile health had become a concern for the entire community. The heat seemed to press in around her, a palpable force that threatened to suffocate, but Margaret pushed on, driven by a sense of duty and compassion.

Upon arriving at the Higgins' humble abode, Margaret was greeted by Mary, who had attempted to tidy up the house in preparation for her visit. However, the air inside was thick with the smell of smoke and the oppressive heat emanating from the large fire burning in the grate. Margaret's eyes adjusted to the dim light, and she spotted Betsy lying on a small sofa beneath the window, her frail form a testament to the ravages of illness.

As Margaret took a seat beside her, Betsy's eyes fluttered open, and she gazed at Margaret's attire with a childlike wonder. "I never knew why folk in the Bible cared for soft raiment afore," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "But it must be nice to go dressed as yo' do. It's different from common." Margaret smiled, amused by Betsy's simplicity, and explained that she had acquired her gown in London, a city that seemed a world away from the close-knit community of Milton.

Betsy's eyes sparkled with curiosity, and she leaned back, shutting her eyes as if to receive a gentle breeze on a summer's day. Margaret's thoughts drifted to her own memories of Helstone, the forest home she had left behind. She had never spoken of it to anyone, save in passing, but there was something about Betsy's gentle nature that put her at ease. As she began to speak, the words flowed easily, like a gentle brook babbling through the forest.
