As I departed from my brother's side, my wife and young child returning to our family's estate in Richmond, I made it my duty to pay a visit to our Governor, who resided in his country house near Williamsburg. My intention was to discuss the unsettling developments unfolding across our province and beyond. Gentlemen, whose names would soon become synonymous with history, had been appointed from Virginia to represent us in the forthcoming General Congress in Philadelphia. In Massachusetts, the people and British troops stood poised on the brink of conflict, while in Maryland and Pennsylvania, we believed ourselves to be surrounded by a loyal populace. The Carolinas and Georgia, meanwhile, seemed steadfast in their devotion to the Crown, and it was unimaginable that our own Virginia would abandon its long-standing allegiance. Though our province boasted few troops, its nobility took pride in their heritage as descendants of the Cavaliers, and around our Governor, a swarm of confident Loyalists buzzed with anticipation, eager to draw their swords and vanquish the rebellious rabble.

In these gatherings, I was forced to endure scathing criticism of my brother, Harry. His wife, it was widely agreed, had led him astray with her radical views, and he had become enamored with the ideas of his acquaintance at Mount Vernon, which he fervently followed. "This is the man you've championed," the people surrounding our Governor said, "your friend, who has enjoyed your trust and shared in your hospitality!" While some of these words stung, I knew that what our zealous Loyalists deemed treachery was, in truth, a testament to Mr. Washington's and others' unwavering commitment to their allegiance to the Crown. They sought to exhaust every avenue of reconciliation before resorting to the desperate and perilous path of revolt and separation. Let the disloyal take up arms and the villains unsheathe their swords; we, however, would remain steadfast, relying on the invincible power of England to restore order and bring the wayward provinces back into submission. With fervent enthusiasm, we raised our glasses in toast to the King's health after our feasts, declaring our readiness to defend his rights and welcome a prince from his royal lineage to govern our ancestral lands.
