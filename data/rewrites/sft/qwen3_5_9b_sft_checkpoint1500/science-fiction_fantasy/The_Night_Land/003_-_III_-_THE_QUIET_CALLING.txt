For generations, a legend had circulated through the Great Redoubt—a myth whispered in the bustling markets of the thousandth floor and echoed in the quieter halls of the other three hundred cities comprising the Pyramid. It spoke of a Second Place of Refuge, hidden somewhere in the desolate expanse of the Night Lands, where the last millions of humanity had gathered to fight unto the end.

I had heard this story repeated endlessly during my travels through the cities of the Great Redoubt. These journeys began when I reached my seventeenth year and lasted for three years and two hundred and twenty-five days. By custom, I spent only one day in each city, a brief pilgrimage designed to train every child. It was a vast journey, fraught with encounters with countless souls whom I loved deeply before forever losing sight of them; life, after all, allows no space for lingering, and every citizen must attend to the duties required for the security of the Redoubt. Despite the brevity of our meetings, we traveled constantly, yet amidst the millions of inhabitants, time remained our scarcest resource.

Everywhere I went, the narrative of this other Refuge remained unchanged. In the libraries I managed to visit, scholars preserved great works asserting its existence with absolute confidence. In those bygone eras, this reality was unquestioned. However, the times had shifted. Now, these ancient records were studied only by doubters, who scrutinized the texts with skeptical eyes. And so it always is.

Yet, regarding the reality of this Refuge, I harbored no shadow of doubt. This conviction stemmed from the teachings of our Master Monstruwacan, who, alongside his assistants, commanded the Tower of Observation at the apex of the Pyramid. Between us, a bond of close friendship and natural affinity existed, transcending the gap between his mature wisdom and my youthful vigor. At twenty-one, he appointed me to the Tower of Observation, a position coveted by all. To serve there was the ultimate fulfillment for a human soul, allowing one's natural curiosity to align, however imperfectly, with the study of the stars.

Let me clarify, lest it be assumed I received this favor solely due to friendship, that my appointment was justified by a unique gift I possessed: what we termed "Night-Hearing." While a fanciful title for a phenomenon difficult to define, it was a singular ability. Throughout the millions of the Pyramid, no other individual shared this gift to a comparable degree. Because of it, I could perceive the "invisible vibrations" of the aether. Without relying on the mechanical recording instruments, I could intercept the continuous messages flowing through the eternal darkness—sometimes receiving them with greater clarity than the machines themselves.

Thus, I became accustomed to listening for a voice that had not resonated in my ears for an eternity, yet which sang with such sweetness and clarity in my memory-dreams that it felt as though Mirdath the Beautiful slept within my soul, whispering to me from the depths of time. On the fifteenth hour, when Sleep-Time commenced, I sat pondering this enduring love, marveling that my dreams retained the voice of a passion so ancient. Lost in thought, I imagined the aeon-lost figure whispering beauty into my ears, a sensation born of the vividness of my recollection.

Suddenly, standing as I did, communing with my internal musings, I was struck with a thrill, as if smitten by a physical blow. From the boundless night, a whisper had emerged, vibrating against my subtlest senses. Four long years had passed since my initial awakening in the embrasure, a mere boy of seventeen. Now, emerging from the world-darkness and the eternal years of a lost life, the whisper arrived. Instantly recognizing it, I refrained from answering by name, a lesson taught by wisdom. Instead, I transmitted the Master-Word through the night, projecting it via my brain-elements as strongly as I could. I knew that if the caller were truly the one I sought, she would possess the innate power to hear without instruments. Conversely, if this were merely a lure from the Evil Forces, or a trick of the House of Silence meddling with our souls, they would be unable to utter the Master-Word, a truth proven throughout the Everlasting.

As I trembled, striving to maintain a relaxed state essential for receptivity, the spiritual essence surrounding me began to vibrate with the rhythm of the Master-Word. It beat steadily in the night, a marvelous sound. With a surge of sweetness filling my spirit, I called out with my mind: "Mirdath! Mirdath! Mirdath!"

At that precise moment, Master Monstruwacan entered the section of the tower where I stood. Seeing my agitated expression, he fell utterly silent. Although he lacked the power of Night-Hearing, he was wise and thoughtful, highly attuned to the nuances of my gift. He had recently returned from the Receiving Instrument, where he suspected—but failed to properly detect—the faint thrum of the Master-Word. His search for me was driven by the knowledge that, as the gifted listener, I alone could discern it.

I recounted the entirety of my story, my thoughts, and the details of that awakening. As I spoke, he listened with profound sympathy and a heart troubled by wonder. In that age, a man might speak sanely of subjects that, in our current era, would be dismissed as madness or the ravings of insanity. Through the refinement of mental arts, strange experiments, and advanced learning, the people of that time could conceive of realities now beyond our comprehension. Just as we today might calmly consider tales of olden magic, they regarded my stories as valid truths.

While I spoke, I listened with my spirit. Save for a sense of faint, happy laughter wrapping around me, I heard naught else. Nothing more transpired that day.

It is necessary to record that, due to the influence of my memories and half-memories, I frequently disputed with the learned men of the Pyramid. They were plagued by doubts regarding the veracity of the ancient stories of the Days of Light and the existence of the Sun, despite hints of truth in our oldest records. I countered their skepticism with tales that seemed fairy-like to them, enchanting their hearts while angering their logical faculties, which refused to accept what their emotions readily embraced. Yet, Master Monstruwacan would listen to everything I had to say, regardless of the duration. Often, after talking for hours and drawing narratives from my Memory-Dreams, I would find myself transported back into the present of that Future. Suddenly, all the Monstruwacans would abandon their instruments and observations to gather around me, the Master himself so submerged in interest that he failed to notice them leaving. Nor did I notice, so completely absorbed was I in the realm of the past. When the Master eventually returned to the knowledge of the present, he would rouse and chide, causing the lesser ones to flee guiltily to their posts, their faces muddled with bewilderment and thoughtful hunger, ever questioning.

So it was with the other learned ones outside the Tower of Observation, those who disbelieved even as they hung on my every word. They would listen from the "dawn" of the first hour to the fifteenth hour of the "Sleep-Time." Occasionally, I encountered individuals of extraordinary learning who upheld my accounts as truths, creating a faction. Later, belief spread further, and though some still wavered, all were prepared to listen, willing to spend their days in discourse if only I had the leisure, for I had my duties.

However, Master Monstruwacan believed from the outset and remained wise, understanding the depth of my experience. I loved him for this, as for many other dear qualities. Consequently, among the millions of the Redoubt, I was singled out. My stories rippled downward through a thousand cities. Eventually, descending to the lowest tier of the Underground Fields—an hundred miles deep beneath the Redoubt—I found that even the ploughboys knew fragments of my tales. They would gather around me whenever the Master and I descended to discuss matters pertaining to the Earth-Current and our instruments.

Regarding the Underground Fields, which we simply called "The Fields," I must offer a brief description. They represented the mightiest work of our world; the Last Redoubt itself was but a small fragment compared to them. An hundred miles deep lay the lowest field, spanning an hundred miles from side to side. Above it stretched three hundred and six additional fields, each successively smaller in area, tapering until the topmost field, situated directly beneath the lowermost floor of the Great Redoubt, measured only four miles across. Thus, these layers formed a colossal, impossible Pyramid of Country Lands buried deep within the earth, extending an hundred miles from base to summit.

The entire complex was sheathed in the grey metal of which the Redoubt was built. Each field was pillared and floored beneath the soil with this same compound of wonder, rendering it secure against intrusion by monsters from without. All of this subterranean land was illuminated where necessary by the Earth-Current. This same life-stream fertilized the soil, imparting vitality and blood to the plants, trees, bushes, and all natural things.

Constructing these Fields likely required a million years. The waste products ("the dump") were cast into "the Crack"—the source of the Earth-Current—which extended beyond all soundings.
