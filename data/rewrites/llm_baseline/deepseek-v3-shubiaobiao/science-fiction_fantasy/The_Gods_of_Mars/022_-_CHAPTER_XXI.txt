Yersted’s warning left no room for hesitation. Time was slipping through my fingers like sand, and if I were to reach the Temple of Issus before Tars Tarkas’s assault at dawn, I had to move swiftly. The plan was clear: infiltrate its cursed walls, overpower the guards, and free my Princess. With Carthoris and our warriors at my back, victory was within grasp.

We began the arduous task of transporting our forces through the submerged passage leading from the submarine pool to the temple’s hidden gangways. Each trip was a gamble, but discipline held firm, and soon five thousand hardened fighters of Barsoom stood ready—red warriors, bred for war.

Carthoris alone knew the labyrinthine tunnels, forcing us to advance as one. Speed was our ally, but fate had other designs. As we prepared to leave the chamber, an officer pointed to the churning waters. At first, I thought another submarine pursued us—until I realized the truth. The water was rising.

Carthoris’s voice cut through the tension. *“The pumps of Omean have stopped. They mean to drown us. Move!”*

I gave the order, and the column surged forward, but the flood chased us like a living thing. The corridor sloped upward, yet the waters climbed faster, swirling at our ankles, then our knees. Panic flickered in the ranks—Martians feared drowning above all. But discipline held.

Then, disaster.

A branching corridor offered hope. I diverted the rear guard, ordering them into the new passage. The water reached our waists, then our chests. Men stumbled, gasped, vanished beneath the tide. Gur Tus, a stalwart dwar, stood with me as we dragged survivors forward. Djor Kantos joined the effort, and together we held the line until the last warrior passed.

But our respite was short-lived.

A cry echoed ahead—*“Fire!”*—followed by chaos. Flames blocked our path, smoke choking the tunnel. Retreat was impossible; the waters now lapped at our chins. Desperate, I led the survivors into another gallery, praying it led to safety. Two thousand made it through before the fire’s heat forced us back.

Then—betrayal.

A steel grating slammed down behind me, sealing my retreat. The First Born had anticipated every move. Trapped between fire and flood, I faced annihilation. The water rose, the smoke thickened. Only one path remained: dive into the blackness and swim.

The icy current swallowed me. Stroke after stroke, I fought toward what I hoped was salvation. My lungs burned. My vision dimmed. Just as despair took hold, my head broke the surface—air! Gasping, I staggered forward, driven by fury and purpose.

The Temple of Issus loomed ahead. A doorway beckoned. Without hesitation, I burst through—

And froze.

Before me stood not the horror of Issus, but the face I had crossed worlds to find.

Her eyes widened—terror, disbelief, then overwhelming joy. Words failed me. I could only step forward, arms outstretched, as she rushed into my embrace. The galaxy could have crumbled around us, and I would not have noticed.

She was alive.

And now, nothing would tear her from me again.
