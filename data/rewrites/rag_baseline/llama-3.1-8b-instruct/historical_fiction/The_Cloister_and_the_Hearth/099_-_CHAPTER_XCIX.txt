As I descended into the depths of my memories, I found myself in the presence of Jerome, his somber demeanor unchanged from the times I had known him. Yet, beneath the surface of his austere exterior, I sensed a glimmer of softening towards Gerard, a man he had grown to miss during his time in England. The tales of Gerard's unwavering stance against the Pope and the Minorites, particularly regarding the notion of the immaculate conception, only served to deepen Jerome's admiration for his brother's conviction.

However, it was the news of Gerard's illness that reignited Jerome's initial disdain. "Dying for a woman?" he exclaimed, his voice heavy with disapproval. Yet, Jerome knew that he had to intervene, to prevent the scandal that threatened to tarnish Gerard's reputation and the Church's image. He visited Gerard twice daily, employing all his persuasive skills to convince him to abandon his "unspiritual despondency" and preserve his piety and eloquence for the Church.

Gerard listened intently, his eyes filled with a deep sadness, as Jerome poured out his words of reason and encouragement. Gerard acknowledged his own weakness, but refused to relinquish his love, describing it as a deep affection, not passion. He spoke of the woman as his guiding light, the one who had sustained him in his duties and charity, and had been his inspiration in the pulpit. Her presence had intertwined itself with all that was good in his life, and Gerard couldn't bear the thought of living without her.

"I am no rebel against Heaven," Gerard said, his voice barely above a whisper. "It is God's will to release me. When they buried her, something within me snapped, irreparable. I heard it, and I felt it. From that moment, no food had any taste for me. I couldn't distinguish bread from flesh, even with my eyes closed."

Jerome's reaction was one of shock and awe. "Holy saints!" he whispered. Gerard continued, his words dripping with a newfound understanding. "I should have lived unhappy, but He said, 'No; enough is done, enough is suffered; poor, feeble, loving servant, your shortcomings are forgiven, your sorrows touch your end; come, thou to thy rest!' I come, Lord, I come!"
