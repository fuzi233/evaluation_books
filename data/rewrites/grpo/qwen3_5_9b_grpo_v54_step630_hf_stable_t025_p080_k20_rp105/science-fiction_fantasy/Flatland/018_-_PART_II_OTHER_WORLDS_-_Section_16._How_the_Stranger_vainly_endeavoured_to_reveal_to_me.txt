The echo of my Wife's Peace-cry, signaling her departure, had barely faded when I turned my attention back to the Stranger. I intended to approach him closely and invite him to sit, yet the sight of him froze me in place, dumbfounded. He possessed no angularity whatsoever, yet he shifted constantly, fluctuating in size and radiance with a fluidity impossible for any figure known to my world. A terrifying thought seized me: could this be a monstrous Irregular Isosceles, a burglar disguised as a Circle, who had gained entry to my home only to strike me with his acute angle?

In the dry, fogless air of my sitting-room, visual recognition was treacherous at close range. Overcome by panic, I lunged forward, shouting, "You must permit me, Sir—" and placed my hand upon him. My Wife had been correct. There was not a trace of an angle, no roughness, no inequality. I had never encountered a more perfect Circle. He remained still as I circled him, starting from his eye and returning to it. He was circular, flawless, and undeniably so.

A dialogue ensued, which I record as faithfully as memory allows, omitting only my profuse apologies. I was consumed by shame and humiliation that a Square should commit the impertinence of feeling a Circle. The Stranger began with impatience at the length of my introduction.

**STRANGER:** Have you felt me sufficiently? Are you not introduced yet?

**I:** Most illustrious Sir, forgive my awkwardness. It stems not from ignorance of social custom, but from surprise and nervousness regarding this unexpected visit. I beseech you to keep my indiscretion secret, especially from my Wife. But before we proceed, would your Lordship deign to satisfy the curiosity of one who wishes to know whence you come?

**STRANGER:** From Space, from Space, Sir: whence else?

**I:** Pardon me, my Lord, but are you not already in Space, along with your humble servant, even now?

**STRANGER:** Pooh! What do you know of Space? Define it.

**I:** Space, my Lord, is height and breadth indefinitely prolonged.

**STRANGER:** Exactly: you see you do not even know what Space is. You believe it consists of only Two Dimensions; but I have come to announce a Third—height, breadth, and length.

**I:** Your Lordship jests. We also speak of length and height, or breadth and thickness, thus denoting Two Dimensions by four names.

**STRANGER:** But I mean not merely three names, but Three Dimensions.

**I:** Would your Lordship indicate or explain in what direction lies this Third Dimension, unknown to me?

**STRANGER:** I came from it. It is up above and down below.

**I:** My Lord seems to imply it is Northward and Southward.

**STRANGER:** I mean nothing of the kind. I mean a direction in which you cannot look, because you possess no eye on your side.

**I:** Pardon me, my Lord; a moment's inspection will convince you that I have a perfect luminary at the junction of two of my sides.

**STRANGER:** Yes: but to see into Space, you ought to have an eye not on your Perimeter, but on your side—that is, on what you would call your inside; but we in Spaceland call it your side.

**I:** An eye in my inside! An eye in my stomach! Your Lordship jests.

**STRANGER:** I am in no jesting humour. I tell you I come from Space, or, since you cannot grasp the term, from the Land of Three Dimensions, whence I but lately looked down upon your Plane, which you foolishly call Space. From that vantage point, I discerned everything you term SOLID (meaning "enclosed on four sides"): your houses, your churches, your very chests and safes, yes, even your insides and stomachs—all lying open and exposed to my view.

**I:** Such assertions are easily made, my Lord.

**STRANGER:** But not easily proved, you mean. Yet I intend to prove mine.

When I descended here, I saw your four Sons, the Pentagons, each in his apartment, and your two Grandsons, the Hexagons. I saw your youngest Hexagon remain with you briefly before retiring to his room, leaving you and your Wife alone. I saw your Isosceles servants, three in number, dining in the kitchen, and the little Page in the scullery. Then I came here. How do you think I arrived?

**I:** Through the roof, I suppose.

**STRANGER:** Not so. Your roof was recently repaired and has no aperture through which even a Woman could pass. I tell you I come from Space. Are you not convinced by what I have told you of your children and household?

**I:** Your Lordship must realize that such facts concerning the belongings of your humble servant could easily be ascertained by anyone in the neighborhood possessing your ample means of information.

**STRANGER:** (To himself) What must I do? Stay; one more argument suggests itself. When you see a Straight Line—your wife, for example—how many Dimensions do you attribute to her?

**I:** Your Lordship treats me as if I were one of the vulgar, ignorant of Mathematics, who suppose a Woman is truly a Straight Line of only One Dimension. No, no, my Lord; we Squares are better advised. We know that a Woman, though popularly called a Straight Line, is scientifically a very thin Parallelogram, possessing Two Dimensions, viz., length and breadth (or thickness).

**STRANGER:** But the very fact that a Line is visible implies it possesses yet another Dimension.

**I:** My Lord, I have acknowledged that a Woman is broad as well as long. We see her length and infer her breadth; which, though slight, is measurable.

**STRANGER:** You do not understand me. I mean that when you see a Woman, you ought—besides inferring her breadth—to see her length and to SEE what we call her HEIGHT, although that last Dimension is infinitesimal in your country. If a Line were mere length without "height," it would cease to occupy Space and become invisible. Surely you recognize this?

**I:** I must confess I do not understand your Lordship. When we in Flatland see a Line, we see length and BRIGHTNESS. If the brightness disappears, the Line is extinguished and, as you say, ceases to occupy Space. Am I to suppose you give the title of a Dimension to brightness, and that what we call "bright" you call "high"?

**STRANGER:** No, indeed. By "height" I mean a Dimension like your length: only, with you, "height" is not easily perceptible, being extremely small.

**I:** My Lord, your assertion is easily tested. You say I have a Third Dimension, which you call "height." Now, Dimension implies direction and measurement. Do but measure my "height," or merely indicate the direction in which it extends, and I will become your convert. Otherwise, your Lordship's own understanding must excuse me.

**STRANGER:** (To himself) I can do neither. How shall I convince him? Surely a plain statement of facts followed by ocular demonstration ought to suffice. —Now, Sir; listen.

You live on a Plane. What you style Flatland is the vast level surface of a fluid, on or in the top of which you and your countrymen move, without rising above it or falling below it.

I am not a plane Figure, but a Solid. You call me a Circle; but in reality I am not a Circle, but an infinite number of Circles, varying in size from a Point to a Circle of thirteen inches in diameter, stacked one upon the other. When I cut through your plane as I am now doing, I create a section which you rightly call a Circle. For even a Sphere—which is my proper name in my own country—if he manifests himself to an inhabitant of Flatland, must manifest himself as a Circle.

Do you not remember—since I, who see all things, discerned last night the phantasmal vision of Lineland written upon your brain—do you not recall how, when you entered the realm of Lineland, you were compelled to manifest yourself to the King not as a Square, but as a Line? That Linear Realm lacked the Dimensions to represent your whole form, showing only a slice or section of you. In precisely the same way, your country of Two Dimensions is not spacious enough to represent me, a being of Three; it can only exhibit a slice or section of me, which is what you call a Circle.

The diminished brightness of your eye indicates your incredulity. But now prepare to receive positive proof. You cannot see more than one of my sections, or Circles, at a time, for you lack the power to raise your eye out of the plane of Flatland; but you can at least observe that as I rise in Space, my sections become smaller. See now: I will rise. The effect upon your eye will be that my Circle will shrink until it dwindles to a point and finally vanishes.

There was no "rising" that I could see; yet he diminished and finally vanished. I blinked once or twice to ensure I was not dreaming. But it was no dream. From the depths of nowhere came a hollow voice, close to my heart, "Am I quite gone? Are you convinced now? Well, now I will gradually return to Flatland, and you shall see my section grow larger and larger."

Every reader in Spaceland will easily understand that my mysterious Guest spoke the language of truth and simplicity. But to me, proficient though I was in Flatland Mathematics, it was no simple matter. The rough diagram above makes it clear to any Spaceland child that the Sphere, ascending in the three positions indicated, must manifest to me, or any Flatlander, as a Circle of full size, then small, and finally very small, approaching a Point. But to me, although I saw the facts before me, the causes remained as dark as ever. All I comprehended was that the Circle had made himself smaller and vanished, only to reappear and rapidly expand again.

When he regained his original size, he heaved a deep sigh, perceiving by my silence that I had utterly failed to comprehend him. Indeed, I was now inclined to believe he was no Circle at all, but some extremely clever juggler; or perhaps the old wives' tales were true, and Enchanters and Magicians did exist.

After a long pause, he muttered to himself, "One resource alone remains, if I am not to resort to action. I must try the method of Analogy." Then followed a still longer silence, after which he continued our dialogue.

**SPHERE:** Tell me, Mr. Mathematician; if a Point moves Northward and leaves a luminous wake, what name would you give to the wake?

**I:** A straight Line.

**SPHERE:** And a straight Line has how many extremities?

**I:** Two.

**SPHERE:** Now conceive the Northward straight Line moving parallel to itself, East and West, so that every point in it leaves behind the wake of a straight Line. What name will you give to the Figure thereby formed? We will suppose it moves through a distance equal to the original straight Line. —What name, I say?

**I:** A Square.

**SPHERE:** And how many sides has a Square? How many angles?

**I:** Four sides and four angles.

**SPHERE:** Now stretch your imagination a little, and conceive a Square in Flatland moving parallel to itself upward.

**I:** What? Northward?

**SPHERE:** No, not Northward; upward; out of Flatland altogether.

If it moved Northward, the Southern points in the Square would have to move through the positions previously occupied by the Northern points. But that is not my meaning.

I mean that every Point in you—for you are a Square and will serve the purpose of my illustration—every Point in you, that is to say in what you call your inside, is to pass upwards through Space in such a way that no Point shall pass through the position previously occupied by any other Point; but each Point shall describe a straight Line of its own. This is all in accordance with Analogy; surely it must be clear to you.

Restraining my impatience—for I was now under a strong temptation to rush blindly at my Visitor and precipitate him into Space, or out of Flatland, anywhere, so that I could get rid of him—I replied:

"And what may be the nature of the Figure which I am to shape out by this motion which you denote by the word 'upward'? I presume it is describable in the language of Flatland."

**SPHERE:** Oh, certainly. It is all plain and simple, and in strict accordance with Analogy—only, by the way, you must not speak of the result as being a Figure, but as a Solid. But I will describe it to you. Or rather not I, but Analogy.
