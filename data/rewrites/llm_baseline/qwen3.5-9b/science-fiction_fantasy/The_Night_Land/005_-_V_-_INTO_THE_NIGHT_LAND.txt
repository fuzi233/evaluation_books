Following the devastation that befell the Ten-Thousand, and the terrifying realization of what lay within the Night Land, we understood that no further aid could be offered. Those youths venturing onto the Road Where the Silent Ones Walk were now far beyond our reach.

Some might argue we should have signaled them using the Home-Call—the great Voice emanating from the Machine atop the sealed base of the Mighty Pyramid. We could not. To do so would alert the Monsters of the Night Land that survivors had left the Pyramid's protection. While we hoped the Evil Forces remained unaware of their presence, we knew the Powers possessed depths of knowledge and ignorance that no mortal could fathom.

We knew, however, of a strange, quiet influence spreading through the land, recorded by our instruments but originating from the House of Silence—a grim structure on a low hill north of the Great Road. Many Monstruwacans feared this influence targeted the youths, though we lacked certainty. We could only wait and watch.

Soon, the youths approached the section of the road turning sharply north, drawing near that horrific House. The influence in the land intensified, confirmed by my own senses as coming from the House, though proof remained elusive. I shared my conviction with the Master Monstruwacan, who trusted my judgment and shared his own suspicion of a secret power radiating from the House of Silence.

There were whispers of sending the Home-Call to warn the youths and urge their return. This was rejected by the Master. It was too dangerous to risk their souls prematurely; we needed absolute certainty that they were doomed before acting. Furthermore, the Home-Call was a voice of such magnitude that broadcasting it would instantly reveal our location and the fact that others had fled the Great Redoubt—a tactic useless in these ages, reserved for the era of great flying ships.

Days and nights passed while countless eyes peered into the Night Land, watching the youths approach their fate. Talk was rampant—foolish, fearful, yet well-intentioned—but no one possessed the courage to launch another rescue attempt. The land itself seemed awake, uneasy, filled with a sense of impending doom and low, distant roars. I should have written of these atmospheric shifts earlier, but the weight of my task has delayed my chronicle.

During this period, the youths slept not, ate rarely, and pressed onward at a woeful pace toward the North. Their exhaustion suggested they were under a spell from the distant House. Suddenly, a messenger reported a massive influx of influence into the land. Simultaneously, through the Great Spy-Glass, I saw the youths break from the Road Where the Silent Ones Walk, sprinting desperately toward the House of Silence.

The Master Monstruwacan hesitated not. He unleashed the Home-Call across the world, pleading with the doomed youths to resist the force pulling them to destruction. He warned them via the natural eye that they were being dragged by a power from within the House, urging them to fight for their souls or commit suicide before entering the house to face utter ruin.

A profound silence fell upon the Pyramid, broken only by the ominous bellowing of the Call. Millions across the land sensed the Master's plea, and unknowingly, a counter-force surged from the Pyramid, born of the collective prayers and wills of humanity. This spiritual wave stunned my spirit, creating a vast noise of supplication in the night. Trembling, I watched through the glass as the youths stopped their run, gathered in a confused crowd, as if waking from a nightmare to find themselves in a strange place.

Then, a roar erupted from the five hundred thousand embrasures and view-tables. It sounded like a triumphant wind, yet premature. The combined will of the millions broke the Evil Force's grip momentarily. But the barrier held only briefly; the Evil Force from the House reasserted itself, dragging the youths back to their flight. They ignored their salvation, turning once more toward the House.

Silence returned to the Pyramid, replaced by lamentation. Then, a new miracle occurred. Before the fleeing youths arose billows of mist—pure white fire, chilling yet offering no illumination. This mist halted their progress. We knew a Sweet Power of Goodness was fighting for their souls, shielding them from the forces of destruction. All the millions witnessed this, some clearly, others doubtfully, yet all spiritually heightened beyond the norm of this age.

Though none possessed the rare Night-Hearing to detect a soul speaking across the globe, I did. A Monstruwacan soon reported that the influence had ceased affecting our instruments. The connection between the House and the youths was severed, likely due to the intervention of that mighty protective power. Hope and fear mingled in the air; the youths had a chance to return.

While the youths wavered, the Master Monstruwacan sent the Home-Call again, imploring them to return home while the protective shield endured. Some looked toward the Pyramid, reading the Master's message. But in an instant, they turned. They obeyed a leader they had chosen: Aschoff, a great athlete from the Nine-Hundredth-City. Driven by unwarranted bravery, Aschoff leaped into the billows of white fire, destroying the barrier. Immediately, the fire vanished. Aschoff and his followers resumed their run toward the House of Silence.

They reached the low hill and ascended swiftly. Two hundred and fifty wholesome, innocent youths entered the great open doorway—"open since the Beginning"—through which flowed the cold, steadfast light and inscrutable silence of Evil. The uncased windows emitted an unholy desolation. Aschoff led them inside, and they followed, never to emerge or be seen by humans again.

Mothers and fathers gazed into the Night Land, witnessing the tragedy. The people fell silent, though some dared hope the youths would return. Deep down, everyone knew they had gone to destruction. The Night spoke horror to every soul, leaving behind a sudden, utter quiet.

To me, possessing the Night-Hearing, came a terrible fear—not of sound, but of the agony whispered into my spirit by the Quietness of the night. No sound reached my ears, then or in the years to come, for the youths had passed into a Silence the heart cannot comprehend.

This strange quiet, brooding over the land, was more horrifying than any previous roar. It was preferable to hear the distant thunder of the Great Laughter, the whining from the Silver-fire Holes, the bay of hounds, or the roar of giants. These sounds, though dreadful, were familiar. This silence held a depth of horror that defied comparison.

With no hope of succoring the People of the Lesser Redoubt, and no knowledge of their location, we accepted our inevitable end. It was a sad thought, compounded by the sorrow of mothers, wives, and kin who had already suffered for failed attempts to help. Now, the horror of the lost youths weighed heavily upon us.

For me, the knowledge that I could offer no aid to the Lesser Redoubt crushed my heart. I had harbored foolish dreams of secretly exploring the Night to find that lesser pyramid and rescue the thousands. Above all, I dreamed of stepping out of the mystery, putting my arms around Naani, and declaring, "I am That One." In my soul, I knew she would recognize me instantly, returning to me as she had in eternity.

To realize this reunion would never happen—that she might currently suffer under a foul monster—was akin to madness. Nearly seized by the *Diskos*, I considered rushing blindly into the Night to find her or die trying. Often, I called to Naani, sending the Master-Word through the night to assure her it was truly I, and not a monster, speaking to her spirit. I instructed her never to leave her Redoubt unless she received the Master-Word, trusting that no true friend would entice her into the dark.

I poured my soul into listening, sending thoughts through my brain-elements. It was doleful to speak into the void without hearing the answering beat of the Master-Word. Occasionally, the aether would thrill weakly, suggesting the faint pulse of the Master-Word, bringing temporary comfort that Naani still lived. Constant vigilance drained my health, forcing me to fight for sanity. My heart grew weary; life seemed insignificant compared to the loss I felt.

Voices claiming to be Naani's often spoke from the night, but whenever I responded with the Master-Word, they fell silent. I did not mock them but waited, shutting them from my mind to focus on truth, courage, and memories of Naani, which remained sweet and holy to my spirit.

Monsters outside tormented me, perhaps intending to lure me to destruction or simply plagued by malice. Considering my troubles and pouring my strength into Naani's defense caused me to grow visibly thin. The Master Monstruwacan, who loved me as a father, chided me gently, advising patience to regain my strength. But my resolve was unshakable. I explained that my leanness was a purification; fear had been burned away, leaving me lean, pure, and fearless. Love, I believed, purified the human heart. Because of this spiritual preparation, I was less vulnerable to the Forces of Evil.

The Three Days of Preparation were designed to cleanse the spirit, making it harder for Evil to harm. During this rite, initiates learned horrors not taught to the young—mutilations and soul-abasements that shook the heart. These secrets were guarded in the Room of Preparation, inaccessible to casual readers. When I finally entered, I wondered why anyone ventured into the Night Land, or why the room existed for those merely seeking knowledge rather than facing the unknown.
