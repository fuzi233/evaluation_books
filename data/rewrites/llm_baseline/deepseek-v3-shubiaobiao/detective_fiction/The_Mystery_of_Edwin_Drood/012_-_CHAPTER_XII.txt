When the weight of his own consequence grows too heavy even for a mind as profound as his, Mr. Sapsea takes to wandering the Cathedral Close as dusk settles over Cloisterham. There, amid the silent stones, he plays the benevolent landlord to the dead, bestowing upon them the dignity of his presence—and, more importantly, the honor of his inscription. A stray passerby might pause to read it; a stranger emerging too hastily from the churchyard must surely be retreating in blushing shame, as directed by marble decree.

For Mr. Sapsea is not merely a man of letters—he is now the Mayor of Cloisterham. And if mayors did not exist (preferably in great numbers), why, the very fabric of society—a phrase he is certain he coined—would unravel. It is a solemn duty, one that may yet earn him a knighthood. *Rise, Sir Thomas Sapsea!* The thought lingers pleasantly as he strolls, hands clasped behind his back, eyes sharp for any hint of reverence in the evening gloom.

His acquaintance with Mr. Jasper has deepened since their first meeting over port and epitaphs. The choir-master has proven himself a man of sound principles—patriotic, respectful of his elders, and admirably free of modern frivolities. Jasper’s singing, in particular, had stirred the mayor’s heart: none of those foreign ditties, but good, sturdy English hymns, the sort that make a man proud to smash foreign shores to splinters.

Tonight, however, his musings are interrupted. Rounding a corner, he nearly collides with the Dean himself, deep in conversation with Mr. Jasper and the verger, Mr. Tope. Sapsea’s bow is so deep it might scrape the cobbles.

*"You are writing a book about us, Mr. Jasper?"* the Dean remarks, amused. *"We are ancient enough to warrant one, though perhaps you’ll also note how neglected we are."*

Jasper demurs with a smile. *"No such ambition, sir. My curiosity is merely a whim—one Mr. Sapsea here encouraged."*

The mayor, caught off guard but never at a loss, seizes the opportunity. *"Ah! Durdles, of course! A singular character, Dean—one I’ve taken pains to understand."*

*"Then perhaps,"* the Dean replies dryly, *"you might persuade him not to break our choir-master’s neck. We’ve grown rather fond of Mr. Jasper’s head."*

Tope titters obligingly. Sapsea puffs up. *"I shall personally ensure Durdles exercises caution. Though I confess, I don’t yet see the danger."*

*"A midnight stroll among the tombs,"* Jasper explains. *"Your own suggestion, if you recall."*

*"Ah! Of course!"* Sapsea lies, with magnificent conviction.

Just then, Durdles slouches into view, a dusty specter with a bundle under his arm. At Sapsea’s pompous warning to *"take care of my friend,"* the stonemason scowls.

*"He can look after himself,"* Durdles grunts. *"And so can I. Stick to your own business, Mr. Mayor."*

Sapsea, undeterred, winks at the others—*See how deftly I handle him!*—before retreating in wounded dignity.

---

Night deepens. The lamplighter’s torch flickers like a will-o’-the-wisp along the Close. Jasper, having spent hours murmuring choir hymns in the dark, now slips into a pea-jacket, pockets a bottle, and steals out into the moonlight.

Durdles awaits him in his hovel of a workshop, already fortified by liquor. Lantern in hand, they set off—through the graveyard, past the quicklime pit (*"Eats bones, that does,"* Durdles remarks cheerfully)—until the shadow of Minor Canon Corner swallows them.

There, voices halt them. Crisparkle and Neville stroll into view, speaking in low tones. Jasper freezes, his grip tightening on Durdles’s arm.

*"Wait,"* he breathes.

Fragments drift to them: *"Christmas Eve... confidence... I answered for you..."* Neville’s face is earnest in the gloom. Then they pass on, vanishing into silvered light.

Jasper laughs—a sound that startles even Durdles.

---

The crypt is a maze of pillars and moonlight. Durdles, emboldened by the bottle, expounds on the *"old ’uns"* he’ll one day dig up. Jasper watches him, then the shadows, with equal intensity.

They climb the tower, steps worn smooth by centuries. At the summit, Cloisterham sprawls below, a patchwork of the living and the dead. Durdles, drowsy and drink-addled, wobbles perilously.

*"Ghosts of sounds,"* he mutters. *"Last Christmas Eve... a shriek, then a howl like a death-dog. Only I heard it."*

Jasper’s reply is razor-sharp. *"What do you mean?"*

Durdles shrugs. *"Ghosts. Why pick me? Dunno."*

Disdain flashes in Jasper’s eyes. *"Lead on,"* he snaps.

Down they go, Durdles stumbling, Jasper steady as a blade. In the crypt once more, the stonemason collapses against a pillar, begging for *"forty winks."*

Jasper paces. The moonlight stripes the floor. Somewhere, a shadow shifts—or perhaps it is only the wind.

*Unaccountable*, the night whispers. *Unaccountable indeed.*
