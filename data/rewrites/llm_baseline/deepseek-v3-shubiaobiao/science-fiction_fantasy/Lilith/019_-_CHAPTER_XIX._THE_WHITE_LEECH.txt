I woke in the dim glow of the cave, my hand throbbing. The skin was swollen, marred by a triangular wound—like the bite of some unseen predator. By evening, the pain had faded, leaving only a faint scar. I scoured the cavern, turning over stones, but found nothing.

Days dragged. The figure on the bed of leaves never stirred, never opened her eyes. Yet she was not dead—no decay touched her, no taint in the air. If anything, the sharp angles of her bones had softened, her parchment skin grown smoother. Life was returning, slow as the tide reclaiming the shore.

One night, restless, I rose to seek cooler air. Moonlight spilled into the cave, and I paused, gazing at her. My breath caught—had her eyes opened? Dark, fathomless, like voids cut from the night itself, they seemed to gleam beneath her lids. I crept closer, heart hammering.

But no. Her lashes were sealed shut. Hope had tricked me. I turned away, crushed, and wept into my hands.

Then—a thought. *Had* they opened, just for a moment? Perhaps she had blinked, then slept again. Or perhaps she watched me now, feigning stillness. The possibility soothed me. I slept.

That night, I was bitten again. A burning thirst woke me. By dawn, the wound had nearly healed. Some creature of the hot springs, I reasoned—a leech, drawn to blood. But as long as I was its target, she was safe.

Then, one morning, as I pressed a peeled grape to her lips, they moved—ever so slightly—closing around it. She lived.

I worked feverishly, crafting garments from the fibrous husks of desert plants, stitching layers into something modest, something she could rise in. Days passed. Her form filled out, her skin regained its luster—but her eyes remained shut.

And the bites continued. Every third night, without fail, the unseen thing found me as I slept. My strength waned; blood loss left me dizzy. But I didn’t care. She was waking. That was enough.

Then—a rustle. A voice, weak but clear: *"I caught the vile thing."*

I froze. *She spoke.*

"What creature?" I whispered.

"The one biting you."

"What was it?"

"A great white leech."

My skin prickled. "How big?"

"Six feet, perhaps."

"You saved me," I breathed. "How could you touch it?"

"I had to." Her voice was steel. "I threw it in the river."

"It’ll return," I said, then swayed, the cave tilting. I stumbled outside, collapsed.

When I woke, dawn lit the cave. She stood over me, clad in the rough garments I’d made, her eyes—*finally open*—black and depthless as a starless sky.

"You brought me here?" she demanded.

"Yes."

"From where?"

"The wilds. You had no clothes, no jewels—just bones and skin."

Her lip curled. "Why not leave me?"

"Because you were alive. And I was alone."

Her laugh was sharp. "You’d keep me like a trophy?"

"There was no beauty left to keep," I snapped.

Her face darkened. "Then why?"

"Because you were human."

"*Human?*" Contempt dripped from the word.

I met her glare. "I’d have done the same for anyone."

She turned away, silent. Then: "How long was I asleep?"

"Months."

Her fingers twined in her hair—long, untangled. "You bathed me in that river?"

"Yes. It was the only way."

A shudder ran through her. When she faced me again, her eyes were cold. "You’ve done me two great wrongs. You forced me back to life. And you’ve shamed me."

Her hand lifted. A gust of frigid air struck me—

Then nothing.

I woke on the ground, drenched and shaking. She was gone.
