As Gerard received the unexpected message, his face drained of color, and his jaw clenched in frustration. Like a wild boar, men of spirit would initially flee from a stronger opponent, but if cornered and harassed, they might suddenly turn fierce and retaliate. Gerard steeled himself, preparing for battle, but his expression faltered when he realized his trusty axe was back in Rhine. He had grown accustomed to its reassuring weight and reliability.

The group pondered their situation, weighing caution against hunger. Prudence urged them to steer clear of the village, but Hunger's persistent whispers won out. They decided to cross the fields, navigating through the tall grasses under the watchful eyes of the setting sun.

As the day wore on, they stopped at a haystack, borrowing bundles of hay to lie upon in a dry ditch hidden among the prickly nettles. They ventured out one by one, returning with turnips, which they ate at intervals during their retreat, savoring the simple pleasure of a hot meal.

As night descended, the group crept out into the rain-soaked darkness, their footsteps quiet on the wet earth. They made their way across the village, disappearing into the shadows. The tempestuous night was almost palpable, the wind howling like a beast as they navigated through the blackness. The air was heavy with moisture, and the only sound was the patter of raindrops on their cloaks.

When the moon finally broke through the clouds, they found themselves far from Dusseldorf, many leagues into the unknown. Undeterred, they pressed on, their footsteps steady in the darkness. It was only when they stumbled upon a grand structure that Denys' voice rang out, full of hope. "Courage! I think I recognize this place. Yes, it's a convent – we're in the see of Juliers, beyond the reach of Cologne's influence."

With those words, they slipped through the gates, finding safety within the convent's walls.
