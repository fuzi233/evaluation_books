As the sun cast its golden rays upon the cobblestone streets of Florence, Romola made her way to the Via Gualfonda, her destination the esteemed studio of Piero di Cosimo. It had been four days since she last set foot in this bustling quarter, where the city's residents and the French occupation forces coexisted in a delicate balance of tension. The air was alive with the whispers of discontent, as Florentines and Frenchmen eyed each other warily, their gazes a mix of curiosity and suspicion.

The streets, once filled with the joyful sounds of laughter and music, now seemed to reverberate with the weight of unspoken resentment. The French, having claimed superiority through their military might, had begun to assert their dominance, leaving the Florentines feeling increasingly uneasy. Yet, amidst this charged atmosphere, Romola moved undetected, shrouded in her black veil and mantle, accompanied by the loyal Maso.

Her purpose was twofold: to collect the long-awaited portrait of her father as Oedipus, which Piero had promised to complete, and to ensure that it was done with the care and attention it deserved. As a skilled artist, Piero was known for his mercurial nature, prone to abandoning projects mid-stream or losing interest in them altogether. Romola's anxiety grew with each passing day, for she knew that the loss of her father's image, captured in this portrait, would leave a void in her heart that could never be filled.

Upon arriving at the studio, Romola was granted special permission to enter unannounced. She gently pushed open the door, her voice like a gentle breeze as she called out to Piero in a flute-like tone, reminiscent of the little girl who had once served Tito. The painter responded swiftly, but his words were tinged with a hint of annoyance.

"Ah, Madonna Romola, is it you? I thought my eggs had arrived; I was craving them," he said, his tone a far cry from the warmth she had expected.

Romola smiled brightly, unfazed by his gruff demeanor. "I've brought you something far more delightful than hard-boiled eggs, Piero. Maso has prepared a basket of sweet cakes and confetti, just for you," she said, as she handed the basket to the painter.
