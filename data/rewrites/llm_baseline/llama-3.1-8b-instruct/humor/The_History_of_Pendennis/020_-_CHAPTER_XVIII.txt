As I reflect on the formative years of my acquaintance, Arthur Pendennis, memories of our shared past at the esteemed University of Oxbridge inevitably arise. Though our academic careers may have been marked by varying degrees of distinction, every individual retains a certain fondness for the camaraderie and intellectual pursuits of their university days. It is during this period, unencumbered by the weight of responsibility and the tribulations of adulthood, that one discovers the simple joys of learning and the thrill of exploration. The world, full of promise and possibility, lies before the young scholar like an uncharted text, waiting to be deciphered and interpreted.

Upon returning from a recent visit to Oxbridge, I found myself traveling alongside a young man, a current student at Saint Boniface, who had managed to procure an exeat for a day's excursion to London. His enthusiasm was palpable as he regaled me with stories and anecdotes throughout our journey, his infectious laughter and good-natured humor a balm to my own spirits. As we disembarked at the terminus, he insisted on hiring a hansom cab to hasten his arrival in the city, eager to plunge into the diversions awaiting him. Watching him disappear into the crowd, I couldn't help but feel a sense of nostalgia wash over me.

In contrast, when Arthur Pendennis attended Oxbridge, the university was accessible only by horse-drawn carriage. A well-appointed coach, filled with dons, young scholars, and their guardians, conveyed him to the hallowed halls of learning. Among the passengers was a stout gentleman from the City, who sat beside Major Pendennis, his pale-faced son opposite, and was visibly agitated upon learning that the coach had been driven for several stages by none other than the inimitable young Mr. Foker, a friend to all who knew him, and a skilled driver in his own right. Perched atop the coach, Arthur gazed about with wonder, his heart racing with excitement as the university came into view, its majestic spires and turrets rising above the surrounding landscape like a vision of academic excellence.
