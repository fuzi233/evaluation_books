The morning fog clung to the streets like a damp shroud, casting a mystifying veil over the city's grand facades. In a small, elegant townhouse on a quiet square, Lady Charlotte Windsor sat at her writing desk, the soft scratching of her pen on paper punctuated by the occasional snort from the fire crackling in the hearth. Her husband, Lord Edwin, stood before her, his broad shoulders silhouetted against the pale morning light filtering through the windows. His eyes, a deep, piercing brown, sparkled with amusement as he regarded her, and Lady Charlotte felt a flutter in her chest. They were an unlikely pair – she, a stalwart defender of tradition, and he, a man of science and reason – yet they had found a peculiar harmony in their marriage.

As she wrote, a sudden commotion outside caught her attention. A visitor, a stranger, had appeared at the door. Lady Charlotte's instincts flared, but she schooled herself to remain composed. She rose from her chair, smoothing her skirts as she approached the entrance. The stranger's features, however, did not conform to her expectations. He was...different. His form seemed to shift and ripple, like the surface of a pond disturbed by a stone. The notion struck her that he might be a trickster, a charlatan, but as she drew closer, she detected an aura of sincerity about him.

Without thinking, she reached out a hand to touch him, and the sensation was like running her fingers over the smooth surface of a polished sphere. Her thoughts reeled as she realized that this enigmatic visitor was, in fact, a circle. No angles, no edges, no discernible boundaries. She felt a flush rise to her cheeks as she recalled the countless times she had lectured her own children on the virtues of geometry, only to find herself confronting its most sublime manifestation.

As she stepped back, the stranger's voice, low and melodious, broke the silence. "Are you quite done examining me, my dear lady?" he asked, his tone tinged with a hint of patience. Lady Charlotte's face burned with mortification, but she managed a stammered apology, her eyes fixed on the stranger's tranquil face. "Forgive me, sir," she said, "but I...I was taken aback."
