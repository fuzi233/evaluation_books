While the world moved on, a silent war raged within the depths of a soul hardened against all compassion, shaped by both innate temperament and cruel circumstance. To speak of it fully would be impossible; some tragedies exist beyond human comprehension. We can only piece together this particular horror from fragments—torn letters addressed to no recipient, revealing their origin solely through the trembling handwriting of the author. Discovered long after the official account of Felix Cadwalader's death had been settled, these confessions lay hidden beneath the floorboards of Bartow's room. Their journey into Felix's possession remains a mystery, much like the secrets they guarded.

"Madness!" the words screamed across the page. "I have seen Eva Poindexter again, and heaven and hell have contended for my very existence since that moment. Eva! Eva! The girl I once viewed merely as our quarry. The girl I surrendered to my brother. She is far too radiant for him; she is too luminous for any man unless he is one untouched by the thrill of a woman's voice, blind to a face capable of stirring passion or awakening affection. Is this love? Can I, Felix—a creature defined by a single obsession, fueled by only one enthusiasm—retain even a microscopic corner of this iron breast for any woman, let alone his sister's daughter? Never! Am I the victim of frenzy or the plaything of demons? Yet, in my mind's eye, only the inhabitants of a higher sphere seem to shine brighter when I recall those half-lifted eyes, those delicately parted lips, so utterly free of deceit, that innocent carriage, and that divine tenderness mixed with such strength that she commands awe and ignites desire. I must flee. I must never see her face again. Thomas's resolve is unshakable. He must never perceive that my own foundation trembles like an idol struck by lightning."

"If Thomas had not been raised in Paris, perhaps he too... But I am the sole weak link. Curses on my..."

"Did I promise flight? I cannot leave, not yet. I crave one final glimpse of her visage, merely to confirm that this madness stems from reality. Perhaps I was merely startled yesterday to discover celestial beauty where I expected pale emptiness. If my turmoil arises from my own frailty rather than her superior nature, I should acknowledge my folly before it becomes my ruin. I will remain—and yet... Thomas will not, nor shall he... Dexter's daughter... hatred, hatred for Thom—"

"My self-esteem returns. I have seen them again—he and she—they stood together, and true love burned in his gaze. How could I expect anything less? And I managed to conceal my agony, imposing his duty upon him. She loves him—or believes she does—and the design proceeds. But I shall not linger to witness its completion. No, no."

"I confessed my tale tonight, disguised as a relic of the past. Oh, the devils must laugh at us men! They have ample cause. Sometimes I wonder if my father, in the clarity of his new sight, joins them in their mockery."

"Back home with my unhappiness sealed inside. Back to a place where nothing distracts me from my gnawing sorrows and nearly unbearable thoughts. I pace the floors. I scream her name aloud. I whisper it even beneath the portrait of Evelyn. There are moments when I am tempted to write to Thomas—to command him to stop..."

"Eva! Eva! Eva! Every fiber of my wretched being screams that one word. But no man shall ever know. Thomas must never understand how the thought of her consumes my days and nights, turning my life into torment and my future into ash."

"I await his letters (sparse, cold, distant) with the dread of a condemned prisoner awaiting the executioner. Does she truly love him? Or will that exquisite, soulful spirit demand a stronger mate, a more intense temperament, a—a—"

"In one of my darkest hours, I seemed to see my father rise from the grave to curse me. Oh! He might curse forever if—"

"What have I declared regarding no man knowing? Bartow knows. In his silence, his deafness, he has uncovered my secret, proving it through his piercing stares, his dissatisfied demeanor, and a jealousy so profound I could laugh in triumph if I weren't driven instead to shriek in agony. A mute servant, picked up somewhere I barely remember, jealous of my weakness toward John Poindexter's daughter! He was never jealous of my feelings for Evelyn. Yet, until the day I dared fate by seeking out and gazing upon the woman whose charms I had scorned, her name echoed constantly through these halls, and my eyes fixed themselves on only one spot: where her picture hung, a woeful beauty that has become my eternal reproach."

"I received a great shock today. The starling, trained to murmur Evelyn's name, shrieked out, 'Eva! Eva!' My first instinct was to twist its neck; my second, to snatch it from its cage and press it to my heart. But I did neither. I am still a man."

"Bartow will tear that bird apart if I do not prevent him. This morning I caught him reaching for the cage, a murderous light burning in his eye, easily understood. Yet he cannot hear the word the wretched bird whispers. He only perceives it as a sound, a name, and he is determined to suppress it. Should I hang the cage high, beyond this old fool's reach? His deafness, his inability to converse, the precision with which he obeys my commands delivered via my colored slides, his near-animal devotion to my person—all these necessities bind me to him now. As for the bird—Ah! There it goes again, 'Eva! Eva!'"

"Is it hate or love? Abhorrence or passion? Love seeks to save, yet I harbor no intention of saving her, having accepted her love for Thomas, and knowing that he—Oh, it is no longer for Evelyn's sake that I plot revenge, but for my own! These nights and days of torture—the revelation of my own nature—the consent I was forced to grant to a union promising them bliss and me endless anguish—all demands vengeance. They shall not escape. I have woven my plans deeply, accounting for every contingency. It required time, intellect, and gold. But the outcome is perfect. If they step across the threshold of my circular study, they must submit to my will or perish within, along with me. Oh, they shall never live happily! Thomas need not suspect it. John Poindexter need not guess it! I may have forgotten the oath sworn on my father's crossed arms, but I will never forget the immeasurable griefs of these past months or the humiliation they inflicted. My own weakness is what must be avenged—my unheard-of, my intolerable weakness. Remember Evelyn? Remember Felix! Ah, again! Eva! Eva! Eva!"
