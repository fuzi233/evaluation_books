The autumnal equinox still wrestled with its tempests, the wild rains of the day subsiding as the moon rose high in the night sky. Her silvery light danced across the dark blue canvas above, casting an ethereal glow over the deserted halls of Fieldhead. Louis Moore, ever the solitary soul, felt a sense of restlessness wash over him as he sat in the quiet schoolroom. The storm raging outside seemed to stir something within him, a yearning for connection, for human presence.

As he gazed out the window, the casements framing the vast expanse of the autumn night-sky, he felt an inexplicable pull to move, to explore the empty spaces of the house. He rose from his chair, leaving behind the subdued atmosphere of the schoolroom, and wandered through the deserted parlours, his footsteps echoing off the walls. The parlours were empty, each one a reflection of the others – cold, polished, and fireless, except for one.

In the oak room, a warm glow emanated from the hearth, where the cinders crackled and popped in the intense heat. A small work-table stood near the rug, a desk upon it, a chair beside it. It was here that Moore's gaze settled, his eyes drawn to the chair as if expecting someone to sit there. His face betrayed a hint of interest, a spark of curiosity that seemed to suggest he had stumbled upon a secret, a hidden treasure.

A small satin bag hung from the chair-back, the desk was open, the keys still in the lock. A silver pen, a crimson berry on a green leaf, and a delicate glove lay scattered about the table. The air was redolent with the scent of fresh fruit and ink. Moore's eyes lingered on the scene, his thoughts whirling with questions. Who could have left these things behind? And why?

He spoke aloud, his voice low and contemplative, "She's been here, careless and charming, leaving behind a trail of fascination. Why does she forget to tidy up, to leave things in order? What is it about her that makes me want to scold, yet can't help but smile?" The words hung in the air, a mixture of criticism and adoration, as Moore pondered the mystery of this enigmatic figure, whose presence had left such a lasting impression on him.
