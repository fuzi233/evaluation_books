The darkness had lifted, replaced by a soft, ethereal twilight. Yet, as we walked, we breathed in the crisp air of a spring reborn. A profound transformation had swept across the world—or perhaps, the miracle was far deeper, occurring within us. Though the sky offered no illumination strong enough to reveal distant shapes, every heather bush, every tiny shrub, and every blade of grass stood out with perfect clarity. This visibility came not merely from external light, but from a radiant glow emanating from the plants themselves, reminiscent of the burning bush Moses witnessed in the desert, or perhaps from the light radiating outward from our own awakened eyes. Shadows were nonexistent; instead, all things gently exchanged luminescence with one another. Each living thing projected its inner essence—its defining idea and spirit—through its unique shape and color.

My bare feet seemed to embrace the earth, loving every plant they touched. In this moment, the boundary between the world and my own being dissolved; its life became mine, and my life merged with theirs. The microcosm and the macrocosm had finally reconciled, harmonized into a single symphony. I inhabited everything, and everything dwelled within me. To perceive a thing was instantly to understand its life and my own connection to it, to realize our shared origin and ultimate destination. We existed because a Greater Being exists. Senses previously dormant suddenly awoke, awakening experiences too vast for language to capture, for there were no words or images adequate to describe them. My consciousness was overflowing, yet endlessly expanding, creating space to receive the influx of existence through countless open doors.

When a gentle breeze brushed against a cluster of heather, setting its purple bells ringing, I felt myself present in the joy of those bells, in the delight of the breeze that stirred them, and in the soul that perceived the sound. I lent my entire being as a sanctuary for these joys to revel. I was a peaceful ocean, constantly lifted by the swelling tide of living joy, though that joy remained eternally constant despite its myriad shifting forms. Life was a cosmic celebration. I realized then that life and truth were identical; pure life was bliss itself. Where being lacked bliss, it was not true life, but merely life-in-death. Every whisper of the dark wind carried a sigh of gratitude. Finally, I was alive! Nothing could harm my existence.

Beside me walked my beloved, and together we journeyed toward the Father. What we possessed even before the first sun rose upon our newfound freedom was immeasurable; what must the eternal day bring? We arrived at the terrifying hollow where monstrous creatures had once thrived. As I had dreamed, it was now a beautiful lake. Gazing into its crystal-clear depths, I saw how a great whirlpool had scourged away the soil where the deformed offspring had burrowed. At the bottom lay the entire grotesque brood, bathed in a faint, sickly green light that illuminated every hideous form. They coiled in spirals, folded in layers, knotted upon themselves, or sprawled "long and large" in motionless heaps. These shapes were more fantastical in their ghastly terror than any nightmare conjured by a wine-soaked poet's fevered mind. Even the diver who faced the swirling Maelstrom would find these horrors incomparable; their tentacled convulsions, swollen bulges, and glaring orbs of deformity appeared innocent compared to these incarnations of hatred. Every head resembled a wicked flower, bursting from an abhorrent stalk to complete its malevolence. None moved as we passed, yet they were not dead. As long as humans and women of unsound minds exist, this lake will remain populated by such loathsome things.

But listen! The herald of the sun, the auroral wind, softly trumpets his arrival! The Master-Minister of the human dwelling is near! Heaving a massive, rippling wave of crimson and gold before his prow, he surges upward, as if newly launched from the Creator's hand into the upper seas. He pauses, casting a gaze downward upon the world. A white-foaming storm of molten metal, he is but a coal drawn from the altar of the Father's endless sacrifice for His children. See how every little flower straightens its stem, lifts its neck, and stands expectant with head held high. Something greater than the sun, surpassing mere light, is approaching—coming inevitably, though the journey has been long. What does today, tomorrow, or ten thousand years matter to Life Himself, to Love Herself? She is coming, He is coming, and the necks of all humanity stretch forward to witness the arrival. Every morning they will extend themselves in anticipation; every evening they will droop in waiting—until He arrives. Is this merely a vision spun from air? Will He truly find them watching when He comes?

It was a glorious morning of resurrection. The night had been spent in preparation! The children gambolled ahead, followed closely by the beasts. Fluttering butterflies and darting dragonflies hovered around our heads, forming a cloud of colors and flashes. Sometimes they descended like a snowstorm of rainbow flakes, other times they rose into the humid air like a rolling vapor of embodied scents. It was a summer day more ideal, more perfectly itself, than any man who had not died had ever known. I walked upon the new earth under the new heaven, finding the inhabitants unchanged in appearance, yet transformed in nature. Now, they opened their minds to me, revealing their inner selves. The soul of everything I encountered came forth to greet me and make friends, declaring that we stemmed from the same source and shared the same purpose. They told me they were returning to Him with whom they had always been united, the One they had always intended to serve. They described themselves as lightning taking shape as it flashed from Him back to Him.

The dark rocks drank the showering rays like sponges; the great world absorbed the light and emitted life in return. Two fires of joy burned within Lona and me. Earth breathed heavenward, sending up fragrant smoke; we breathed homeward, exhaling our longing desires. Our very consciousness was an act of thanksgiving. We reached the channels, once dry and weary, now running, flashing, and foaming with living water that shouted in its jubilation. From as far as the eye could see, it was a rushing, roaring, dashing river, made vocal by the rocks it struck. We did not attempt to cross it, but instead "walked in glory and in joy" along its right bank until we reached the great cataract at the foot of the sandy desert. Here, the river roared and swirled, dropping sheer before dividing into two branches. We climbed the height—and found no desert awaiting us. Instead, through grassy plains and between verdant banks, flowed the deep, wide, silent river, full to the brim.

For the first time, the Little Ones beheld the glory of God in the limpid flow of the water. Instinctively, they plunged and swam, followed by the beasts. The desert rejoiced and bloomed like a rose. Vast forests had sprung up, their undergrowth filled with flowering shrubs teeming with songbirds. Every thicket gave birth to a rivulet, and every rivulet sang its own water-song. The site of the buried hand showed no trace of its former desolation. Beyond and still beyond, the river continued in full volume from afar. We ascended higher, sometimes along the grassy margins, sometimes through forests of gracious trees. The grass grew sweeter, the flowers more lovely and varied, the trees larger, and the wind richer with messages.

Finally, we arrived at a forest whose trees were greater, grander, and more beautiful than any we had yet seen. Their living pillars formed a thick, arched roof, filtering so few sunbeams through the dense leaves and blossoms that the canopy felt like an enclosed vault. The children climbed into the rafters of this aerial dome, scrambling and leaping through a landscape of bloom. They shouted to the unseen elephants below, hearing them trumpet in reply. While I could only guess at their meaning, Lona understood their conversations perfectly. The Little Ones chased squirrels, who frolicked playfully, drawing them on before eventually surrendering to be caught and petted. Occasionally, a bird, exquisite in plumage and form, would alight upon one of them, singing a prophecy of what was to come before flying away. Not a single monkey of any kind could they be found here.
