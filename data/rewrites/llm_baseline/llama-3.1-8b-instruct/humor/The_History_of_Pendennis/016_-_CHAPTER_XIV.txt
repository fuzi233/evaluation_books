In the dimly lit theatre, Mr. Bingley's rendition of 'Rolla' in 'Pizarro' struggled to captivate the sparse audience. It seemed that even the accomplished actor's passion for the role couldn't compensate for the lack of enthusiasm from the crowd. The theatre was so empty that one would think the part of Rolla held little allure for the people of Chatteris, unlike its hold on Mr. Bingley. Pen sat alone in the boxes, his eyes bloodshot from fatigue, leaning over the ledge as he gazed wistfully towards the stage. His attention was solely focused on Cora, and whenever she wasn't on stage, everything else faded into insignificance. The backdrop of Spanish and Peruvian landscapes, the tumultuous battles, and the procession of priests and sun virgins blurred together as he fixated on her. His longing for Cora had reached a fever pitch, and he confessed later that he was torn between love, rage, and despair – so consumed that he considered taking drastic measures, had it not been for the silent understanding and watchful presence of his mother, who, though unspoken, offered him solace in his desolate state.

Cora herself entered the scene, resplendent in a white gown adorned with a leopard skin, a golden sun emblazoned on her breast, and fine, glittering armlets that danced in the faint light. Her performance was met with muted applause, and though her eyes sparkled with their characteristic brilliance, they shone brighter still when directed at the two gentlemen in the adjacent box. Pen, oblivious to the sudden transformation that had taken place on stage, remained enthralled by Cora's beauty. Unbeknownst to him, a subtle yet profound shift had occurred – the actors, initially listless, now found renewed energy and focus, their voices lifted in a cacophony of sound as they attempted to capture the attention of the distinguished visitors. The usually reserved Bingley flung himself into the performance, his voice booming across the stage like thunder, while Mrs. Bingley, as Elvira, suddenly found her voice, her words carried on the wind. The supporting cast, too, made a concerted effort to impress the visiting dignitaries, each one vying for their notice.
