He stood outside the church of Santa Felicita, watching as the last of the pilgrims departed, their footsteps echoing off the stone façade. Only when the friar had vanished into the cool darkness of the church did Tito begin his own journey, strolling along the Via de' Bardi with a mind in turmoil.

If Fra Luca was indeed a Florentine, and if he chose to remain in the city, Tito knew that the truth about his benefactor's fate would soon be revealed. A new crisis loomed, one that threatened to upend the delicate balance he had carefully maintained. Yet, despite the unease that churned within him, Tito felt no need to rush into action. He had a visit to pay to Bardo, a necessary apology to offer for his recent absence. As he walked, the evening sun casting long shadows across the cobblestones, Tito pondered the path ahead. His talent for discretion, once a virtue, was now beginning to feel like a liability. How could he reconcile his past reticence with the truth about Baldassarre's existence, now that the secret was out?

For a moment, Tito considered the possibility of embracing the new reality, of acknowledging the truth and all its implications. But such a move would cast a harsh light on his earlier evasions, revealing him to be less a man of principle than a calculating pragmatist. His thoughts were a jumble of emotions, like a rich ragù simmering on the stove, as he weighed the pros and cons of his next move. The night was young, and he had all the hours to meditate on his decision. No one would know the exact moment when he received Fra Luca's letter, and so he chose to put off the inevitable, to let the darkness carry him forward.
