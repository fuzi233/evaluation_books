As the initial shock of their reunion began to subside, Hetty's words flowed forth like a gentle brook, its tranquil surface masking the turbulent emotions beneath. "Ah, Mr. Warrington, you've finally arrived to inquire about Theo's health, and yet you're surprised that your neglect has left her ailing for six long weeks. You've been absent for a year, and not a single word of concern has escaped your lips. How kind of you, sir."

Mr. Warrington's face contorted in distress as he struggled to find words. "Kind?" he repeated, his voice barely above a whisper.

Hetty's eyes sparkled with a fierce intensity. "I suppose you consider it kindness to devote yourself to her daily for a year, only to vanish without a word? My father's promise, you say? A promise, indeed, to make my dear sister ill and then abandon her without so much as a farewell. I wouldn't trifle with a child's heart, sir, and leave them heartbroken. What has she ever done to you but be foolishly enamored of you? By what right do you take her away from us, only to desert her because some distant relative disapproves?"

Her words cut deep, and Mr. Warrington's eyes widened in dismay. "Great heavens, Hetty, is it I who chose to leave? Wasn't it your father who forbade me from seeing her, and didn't he demand that I swear never to attempt contact again? What have I but my word, my honor?"

Hetty's face reddened as she shook her head. "Honor? You kept your promise to him, but broke it to her. Pretty honor, indeed! If I were a man, I'd show you what I think of your notion of honor. But alas, I'm bound by propriety and mustn't speak my mind." Her voice cracked, and she paused, struggling to contain her emotions. "Oh, George, don't you see the anguish I'm in? I'm torn apart by the thought of my sister's heart, broken by your departure. When Dr. Heberden told me you were unwell, I knew I had to come and see you for myself. I'm glad to see you looking so poorly, though I won't mention it to Theo. She still cares deeply for you, and can't bear the thought of your absence. That's a luxury reserved for men, isn't it –
