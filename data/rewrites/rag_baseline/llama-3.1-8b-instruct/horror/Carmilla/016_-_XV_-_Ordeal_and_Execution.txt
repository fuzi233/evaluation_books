The moon cast an eerie glow over the crumbling chapel, its stone façade seeming to absorb the faint light as if it were a living entity. Inside, the air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and decaying leaves. I stood at the back, watching as a stranger entered with an air of quiet confidence. He was a tall, gaunt figure, dressed in black, with a hat shaped like a broad leaf and spectacles perched on the end of his nose. His hair was long and grizzled, hanging down his back like a wild mane, and his skin was weathered to a deep, burnished brown.

As he moved towards the front of the chapel, his eyes seemed to dance with a quiet excitement, his long fingers waving in the air as he spoke to himself in a low, rumbling voice. The General, seated in a nearby pew, rose to greet him, his face creasing into a warm smile. "Ah, Baron," he exclaimed, extending a hand. "How wonderful to see you. I had almost given up hope of meeting you so soon."

The two men shook hands, their conversation flowing easily as they walked down the aisle, their footsteps echoing off the stone walls. The Baron pulled out a roll of paper from his pocket and spread it on the worn surface of a nearby tomb, his pencil scratching out lines and markings as he lectured the General about the chapel's architecture. He read from a small, leather-bound book, its pages yellowed with age, his eyes scanning the words with a practiced intensity.

As they moved further down the aisle, they began to measure distances, their feet clicking out a staccato rhythm on the stone floor. The General, the Baron, and my father stood together, examining a section of the wall with great care, pulling off the ivy that clung to it and rapping the plaster with the ends of their sticks. It was as if they were uncovering a hidden treasure, their faces set with a quiet determination.

At length, they discovered a broad marble tablet, its letters carved in relief into the stone. With the help of the woodman, who soon returned, they revealed a monumental inscription and a carved escutcheon. As they gazed upon the discovery, the General raised his hands to the heavens, his eyes closed in a moment of silent gratitude.
