As Eustace Leigh stood in the dimly lit corridor, the flickering lamp in his hand casting eerie shadows on the stone walls, he couldn't help but feel a sense of trepidation wash over him. The cell he stood before, its door slightly ajar, held a prisoner within—a man whose fate hung precariously in the balance. Eustace knew him well, having shared a bond of blood and loyalty. The prisoner, Frank, lay asleep, his high white brow and pale features illuminated by the faint light. Saved from the brutalities of the savage natives, Frank had been reserved for the more refined cruelties of the Inquisitors. His ordeal by torture had taken place mere hours ago, and now Eustace was tasked with persuading, or perhaps entrapping, him into recantation.

The stakes were high, and Eustace's conscience wrestled with his duty. He had long since abandoned any notion of personal gain or self-preservation, surrendering himself to the Inquisition's cause. Yet, as he gazed upon Frank's peaceful countenance, he found himself hesitant to disturb the fragile reprieve. The sound of Frank's gentle whisper, his mother's name and a higher, holier name, drifted through the air, and Eustace felt his resolve crumble.

"Let him rest," he whispered to his companion, a fellow Inquisitor. "My words will be of little use."

"I fear so too, sir," the other replied, his voice laced with skepticism. "This man is obstinate, even defiant. He mocked our holy office openly."

Eustace nodded, his mind reeling with the weight of the situation. The darkness within him seemed to deepen, and he felt the presence of the devil lurking, urging him toward the path of destruction. He knew that the Inquisitors were driven by a zealotry that bordered on madness, and he feared that their actions would ultimately lead to nothing but suffering and despair.
