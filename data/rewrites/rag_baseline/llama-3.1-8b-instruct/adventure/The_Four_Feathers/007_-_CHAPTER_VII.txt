As the last rays of dawn dissipated, Durrance cinched his field-glasses tightly, securing them against the rising heat within the leather case strapped to his hip. Captain Mather nodded in agreement, his eyes squinting against the glare.

"Forward," Durrance ordered.

The scouts ventured ahead, the troops reformed their ranks, and the seven-pounder mountain-guns fell into place behind them. Durrance's detachment of the Camel Corps descended from the rocky outcropping of Khor Gwob, thirty-five miles southwest of Suakin, onto the sun-kissed plateau of Sinkat. This was their final reconnaissance before the impending evacuation of the eastern Soudan.

Throughout the morning, the camels plodded steadily upward through the barren gulley, flanked by towering red rock formations that seemed to writhe in the flickering light. As the terrain unfolded, the landscape transformed into a desolate expanse of shattered stones and twisted mimosa branches. The air was heavy with the scent of dry earth and acacia. Then, like an oasis in the desert, the lush verdure of Sinkat beckoned in the valley below, a verdant haven that lifted the spirits of the weary troopers.

Their march across the plateau assumed a diagonal trajectory, guided by the imposing silhouettes of the Erkoweet mountains. The silence was almost palpable as they traversed the stillness, their footsteps muffled by the soft dust beneath their horses' hooves. At eleven o'clock, the sun ascended to the zenith of a cloudless sky, casting long shadows across the sand. The air was motionless, and the only sounds carried on the breeze were the metallic clinking of their gear, the soft thud of the camels' padded feet, and the occasional burst of pigeons startled by their approach.

Despite the seeming emptiness, life pulsed beneath the surface. As they navigated the sandy curves between the shrubs, the soldiers caught glimpses of gazelles bursting from the undergrowth, their coats a fleeting dance of brown and white as they vanished into the hills. It was as if the land itself was being reborn in this final hour before their departure.

Captain Mather spoke up, his voice carrying above the stillness. "Here the caravans once traveled southward to Erkoweet and the Khor Baraka. The Suakis built their summer residences amidst these very dunes."
