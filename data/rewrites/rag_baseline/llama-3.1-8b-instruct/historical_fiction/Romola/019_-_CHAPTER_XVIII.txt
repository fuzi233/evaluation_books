The scent of freshly baked panisettos wafted through the cobblestone alleys of Florence, mingling with the sounds of artisans hammering away at their wares. Tito strolled through the crowded streets, lost in thought, as he often was when pondering the intricacies of his family's past. His thoughts drifted to the ring he had sold, the memories it held, and the secrets it kept. Little Tessa's face floated before him, her bright smile and sparkling eyes etched in his mind like a fresco on a cathedral wall. He had grown accustomed to the idea of being free from the weight of that secret, but a pang of guilt still lingered, like the faint tang of a bitter amaro.

As he turned a corner, the imposing façade of Piero di Cosimo's workshop came into view. The artist's reputation for creating fantastical mythological designs had drawn Tito to him, and he had a plan to commission a work that would both ease his conscience and satiate his taste for the finer things in life. The heavy iron knocker on the door was wrapped in wool and secured with deftly tied cords, a testament to Piero's eccentricities and creative genius.

Tito's footsteps echoed through the courtyard as he approached the door, his mind racing with the possibilities of his latest scheme. He had resolved to use the fifty-five florins from the sale of the ring to fund a project that would allow him to maintain a distance from Romola's expectations, all while appeasing his own desires. A sense of liberation washed over him, but beneath the surface, a current of unease stirred, like the gentle lapping of the Arno against its banks.

As he pushed open the door, a bell above it let out a mournful clang, and Tito stepped into the warm, golden light of the workshop. The air was thick with the scent of linseed oil and turpentine, and the soft hum of conversation carried on the breeze. Tito's eyes adjusted to the dim light, and he spotted Piero, hunched over a canvas, his brushstrokes bold and expressive. The artist looked up, his eyes twinkling with curiosity, and Tito knew that he was ready to bring his vision to life.
