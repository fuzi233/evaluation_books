The encroaching twilight was not merely an absence of light, but a medium of profound connection. As we walked, the boundary between observer and observed dissolved. Every heather-bush, every blade of grass emitted its own luminescence, either radiating outward like the divine fire Moses witnessed or reflecting the intensified gaze of our awakened souls. Shadows ceased to exist; instead, objects exchanged photons in a seamless circuit of mutual illumination. Each plant displayed its intrinsic essence—the specific thought-form that constituted its being—projected clearly through its shape and color. My bare feet seemed to resonate with the flora they traversed, establishing a tactile communion. The microcosm and macrocosm had finally reconciled; I inhabited the universe, and the universe inhabited me. To perceive an object was to instantly comprehend its life force and my own shared origin, realizing that existence is defined by the presence of the Other.

Sensory faculties previously dormant surged awake, registering phenomena beyond linguistic description. Consciousness expanded infinitely, creating space for these new inputs. When a breeze stirred the purple bells of a heather bush, causing them to tinkle, I experienced the vibration not as external sound, but as a direct extension of my own spirit. I was the joy of the bell, the joy of the wind, and the vessel receiving both simultaneously. I became a vast, tranquil ocean, riding the swell of an eternal, shifting bliss. Life was revealed as a cosmic celebration. Truth and vitality were identical; pure existence was inherently blissful. Where being lacked this quality, it was merely death masquerading as life. Even the dark wind sighed in gratitude. I was alive, inviolate and complete. Beside me walked my beloved, and together we journeyed toward the Father. Before the first sun illuminated our newfound freedom, we already possessed the dawn.

We arrived at the hollow that had once harbored the earth's monstrosities. In accordance with my prior vision, it had transformed into a serene lake. Gazing into its crystal depths, I saw that a vortex had scourged away the substrate where the malformed creatures bred. Resting at the bottom, bathed in a faint, eerie green glow, lay the entire grotesque brood. They coiled in spirals, layered upon one another, or sprawled in massive knots—"extended long and large"—forming static heaps of nightmare. These shapes surpassed the wildest hallucinations of a fevered poet: tentacular writhings, swollen distortions, and glaring orbs of sepia deformity. To a diver in the Maelstrom, these would appear as innocuous compared to such incarnations of hatred. Every head resembled a malignant flower, bursting from a repulsive stalk to perfect its malevolence. None moved as we passed, yet they were undeniably alive. As long as humans of unsound mind persist, this lake remains populated by such loathsome entities.

Then came the herald of the sun, the auroral wind, trumpeting its arrival. The Master-Minister of the human tabernacle approached, heaping a massive, cresting wave of crimson and gold before his prow. He surged upward as if newly launched from the Creator's hand into the celestial sea, pausing to survey the world below. A white-raging storm of molten metal, he was but a single coal from the Father's endless altar-fire offered to his children. Watch how every tiny flower straightened its stem, lifted its neck, and stood expectant with head held high. Something greater than sunlight, surpassing mere illumination, was imminent. Its arrival was inevitable, regardless of the distance traveled. What mattered today, tomorrow, or ten millennia to Life itself, to Love itself? He was coming. Humanity stretched its necks to witness the event. Every morning they would extend themselves; every evening, they would droop in anticipation—until He arrives. Was this merely a visionary construct? Would He truly find them waiting thus?

It was a glorious resurrection morning, prepared during the preceding night. The children gambolled ahead, followed by the beasts. Butterflies fluttered and dragonflies darted above our heads—a cloud of colors and flashes, descending like a snowstorm of rainbow flakes or rising into the humid air as a vapor of embodied scents. This was a summer day more ideal than any mortal who had not died had ever known. I walked upon the new earth under the new heaven, finding the inhabitants unchanged physically, yet fundamentally altered spiritually. Now, they opened their minds to me; I saw into them. The soul of every entity I encountered emerged to greet me, declaring kinship and shared purpose. "We go to Him," they communicated, "with whom we always were and always meant to be. We are lightning taking shape as we flash forth from Him." The dark rocks absorbed the showering rays like sponges; the great world soaked up the light and exuded life.

Two fires of joy burned within Lona and me. Earth breathed heavenward, emitting sweet-smelling smoke; we breathed homeward, exhaling our longing desires. Our very consciousness served as an act of thanksgiving. We reached the channels, formerly dry and weary, now rushing, flashing, and foaming with living water that shouted in jubilation. From as far as the eye could see, it was a roaring river made vocal by its rocky banks. We did not cross it but ascended its right bank in glory and joy until we reached the great cataract at the foot of the sandy desert, where the river split into two branches. There, we climbed the height—and discovered no desert. Instead, through grassy plains and between verdant banks, flowed the deep, wide, silent river, full to the brim. Here, the Little Ones first perceived the glory of God in the limpid current. Instinctively, they plunged and swam, followed closely by the beasts. The desert rejoiced and blossomed like a rose. Vast forests had erupted, their undergrowth filled with flowering shrubs teeming with songbirds. Every thicket spawned a rivulet, and every rivulet sang its own water-hymn.

The site of the buried hand showed no trace. Beyond and further beyond, the river continued in full volume from distant sources. We ascended higher, alternating between grassy margins and forests of gracious trees. The grass grew sweeter, the flowers more varied and lovely; the trees increased in size, and the wind carried richer messages. Finally, we entered a forest of unparalleled grandeur. Their living pillars formed a thick, arched roof, filtering barely a single sunbeam through the dense canopy and blossoms. The children scrambled up into the aerial rafters, leaping through a landscape of bloom, shouting to the unseen elephants below and hearing their trumpeting responses. Lona understood their conversations, though I could only guess at their meaning clumsily. The Little Ones chased squirrels, who frolicked to draw them on, eventually surrendering to be caught and petted. Occasionally, a bird of exquisite plumage would alight upon one of them, singing a prophecy of what was coming before flying away. Not a single monkey of any kind was visible among them.
