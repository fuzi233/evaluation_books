Beneath the cold precision of Felix Cadwalader’s exterior raged a tempest no one could see—a war waged in silence, in the shadowed corners of a mind that allowed no witness. The evidence of it? Fragments of letters, torn and hidden, as if even the act of writing them was a betrayal. They surfaced later, tucked beneath the floorboards of Bartow’s room, their origins as obscure as the man who had hoarded them.

* * *

I saw Eva Poindexter again. And now, heaven and hell duel within me. *Eva.* The girl I dismissed as mere leverage, the one I handed to my brother like a pawn in our game. But she is no pawn. She is sunlight breaking through a storm—too radiant for him, too pure for any man who has not lived untouched by love until this moment.

Is this love? Can *I*, Felix—whose heart has been iron, whose purpose has never wavered—harbor such weakness? Impossible. And yet.

Her eyes, half-lowered yet piercing. Her lips, parted in unguarded honesty. The way she carries herself, tenderness woven with unyielding strength. She is ruin dressed in grace. I should flee. I should never look upon her again.

Thomas does not waver. He must never see that I do.

If only he had not been molded in Paris, perhaps he too would—but no. I alone am weak. A curse upon my—

Did I swear to leave? I cannot. Not yet. One more glimpse—to prove this madness is real, that it is *her* and not some flaw in my own mind. Perhaps yesterday was illusion. Perhaps I mistook radiance for the ordinary. I will stay. I will—

Thomas will not. *Shall* not—

Poindexter’s daughter.

Hate. Hate for Thomas.

* * *

My pride is salvaged. I saw them together. His gaze held devotion—how could it not?—and I played my part. I was stone. She loves him—or he believes she does—and the plan moves forward. But I will not watch it unfold. No. No.

Last night, I told him my story disguised as another man’s past. How the devils must laugh at us. Does my father, in whatever hell he inhabits, laugh too?

Home now, where silence amplifies the torment. I pace. I whisper her name like a prayer, even beneath Evelyn’s portrait. There are moments I consider writing Thomas—forbidding him—

Every nerve screams it. But no one will know. Thomas least of all. Let him never guess how she consumes me, how the thought of her is a blade twisting in my ribs.

I wait for his letters—brief, cold—like a condemned man awaits the gallows. Does she truly love him? Or does a soul like hers crave something fiercer, a will unbroken, a—

Once, in the depths of despair, I imagined my father rising from the grave to curse me. Let him. If only—

* * *

I thought no man knew my secret. But Bartow knows. Deaf, mute, yet he *sees*. His sidelong glances, his restless discontent—the jealousy of a servant toward his master’s weakness. He was never jealous of Evelyn. Her name once filled these rooms; my eyes fixed only on her portrait, that face of sorrowful beauty. But now?

A new horror: the starling, trained to murmur Evelyn’s name, now shrieks *"Eva! Eva!"* My first instinct—to crush its throat. My second—to press it to my chest. I did neither. I am still a man.

Bartow will kill that bird if I don’t stop him. This morning, I caught him hovering by the cage, murder in his eyes. He cannot hear the name, but he knows it is *hers*. Should I move the cage beyond his reach? His obedience, his isolation, his animal devotion—I need them now. But the bird—

Is this love or hate? Salvation or damnation? Love would seek to spare her. But I do not. Not now. Not when she has given her heart to Thomas. Not when he—

This is no longer about Evelyn. This is *mine*. The sleepless nights. The unraveling of my own nature. The agony of conceding their happiness while I choke on bitterness.

They will not escape.

I have planned it all—every step, every consequence. Time, money, cunning—it has cost me everything. Let them cross the threshold of my circular study. They will bend to my will or perish there. With me.

They will not live happily. Thomas will not. John Poindexter will not.

I might have forsaken the oath sworn over my father’s corpse. But I will not forget this torment. This humiliation.

And still—*Eva. Eva. Eva.*
