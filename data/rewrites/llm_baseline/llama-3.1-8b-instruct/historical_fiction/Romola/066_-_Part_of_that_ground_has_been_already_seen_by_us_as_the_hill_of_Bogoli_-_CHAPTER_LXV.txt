As the seventh of April dawned, the sun casting its warm rays upon the cobblestone streets of Florence, the Piazza della Signoria transformed into an arena unlike any other. The air was alive with anticipation, the crowd swelling like a living, breathing entity, eager to bear witness to a spectacle that would be etched in the memories of the Florentines for years to come. This was no ordinary gathering; it was a trial by fire, a test of faith, and a moment of reckoning that would determine the fate of the city's spiritual leaders.

The day began early, with the faithful streaming into the Piazza from all corners of the city, seeking the best vantage points from which to behold the drama unfolding before them. They perched on narrow ledges, grasping iron rods and pillars, their faces set with determination, as if the discomfort of their positions was a small price to pay for the privilege of witnessing the miraculous. The buildings surrounding the Piazza seemed to lean in, as if sharing in the collective excitement, their facades adorned with intricate stone carvings that told stories of a bygone era.

At the heart of this human tapestry lay the platform, a massive wooden structure, eight feet broad and twenty yards long, upon which the flames would soon dance. The fuel was carefully arranged, a pyre of dry oak branches, crackling thorns, and well-oiled rags, awaiting the spark that would set it ablaze. The air was heavy with the scent of woodsmoke and anticipation, as if the very atmosphere itself was charged with the electricity of expectation.

To one side of the Piazza, the five hundred hired soldiers of the Signoria stood at attention, their armor glinting in the sunlight, while to the other, the Compagnacci, led by Dolfo Spini, formed a formidable phalanx, their presence a testament to the gravity of the occasion. Meanwhile, beneath the Loggia of Orgagna, Marco Salviati's contingent of armed citizens stood guard, their eyes fixed intently on the Franciscans and Dominicans, who would soon take their places on the platform.

As the minutes ticked by, the air grew thick with tension, the crowd held its collective breath, waiting for the moment when the flames would be kindled, and the two monks would step forward to face their trial by fire. The sound of chanting drifted through the Piazza, a low, mournful melody that seemed to underscore the gravity of the occasion.
