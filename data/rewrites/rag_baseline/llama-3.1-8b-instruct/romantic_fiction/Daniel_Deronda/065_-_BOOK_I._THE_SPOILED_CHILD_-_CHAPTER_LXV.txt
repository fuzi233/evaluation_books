As twilight settled over the sprawling estate, its grand facade softened by the fading light, Deronda stood in the quiet of the back drawing-room, his footsteps echoing off the polished floors. This was a space where memories lingered, where the silken threads of their past entwined like the intricate patterns on the Persian rugs. It was here, at a musical gathering, that Gwendolen had first spoken of her fate being tied to his, her words hanging in the air like a melody – _Per pietà non dirmi addio_ – a poignant cry that still resonated within him.

The familiar surroundings, once a comforting haven, now felt like a threshold to a different life, one that had unfolded with the unyielding force of a river. The gentle smile of Lady Mallinger's portrait, the urbane faces of the lions on the chimney-piece, all seemed to belong to a bygone era, a past he revisited in his mind rather than lived. His own path had been forever altered, like the shifting tides of a sea, as he navigated the currents of his own destiny.

The sound of the door creaking open broke the silence, and Gwendolen entered, her presence both familiar and changed. The mourning dress accentuated the lines of her face, yet beneath the somber veil, a quiet determination shone like a beacon. Deronda's heart quickened as he met her gaze, the air thick with unspoken understanding. Each of them was weighted with remembrance, their minds racing with the possibilities that lay ahead.

As they seated themselves, the tension between them palpable, Gwendolen spoke, her voice barely above a whisper. "I asked you to come because I need your counsel. Don't hesitate to speak your mind, even if it seems difficult. I've made up my mind to act, but I'm uncertain. I was once terrified of poverty, of being beholden to others. That's why I married. But I've faced greater hardships since then. I think I could endure poverty, if you believe it's the right choice. Have you heard about my husband's will?"

Deronda nodded, his mind racing ahead to the question she would ask. "Yes, Sir Hugo told me," he replied, his eyes locked onto hers.
