I trailed behind him, plunging deeper into the pine forest until the sacred gloom seemed to wrap around us like a heavy cloak. We passed beneath trees that grew increasingly vast and ancient, their forms twisted into grotesque silhouettes by centuries of weather. As we pushed forward, the canopy thinned, revealing patches of sky above.

"You see that hawthorn?" my guide asked finally, extending a sharp, black beak toward the clearing ahead.

I peered through the melting line of woods onto the edge of an open heath. "I see a gnarled old man with a great white head," I replied.

"Look again," he commanded softly. "It is a hawthorn."

"It certainly appears to be an ancient hawthorn," I countered, "but this is not the season for blossoms!"

"The season for the hawthorn to blossom," the raven retorted, "is simply when the hawthorn chooses to blossom. That specific tree stands in the ruins of the church upon your home farm. Were you not intending to instruct the bailiff regarding its churchyard this morning, amidst the thunder?"

"I was planning to tell him I wished to transform it into a wilderness of rose-trees, ensuring the plough never ventured within three yards of it."

"Listen!" the raven whispered, holding his breath as if suspending the very air.

I strained my ears. Was it the sighing of a distant, musical wind? Or merely the phantom echo of joyous music long forgotten? Did I truly hear anything at all?

"They still go there," the raven stated flatly.

"Who goes there? And where do they go?" I asked.

"Some of the souls who once prayed within these walls still make the pilgrimage," he answered. "Yet, I fear they will not go often for much longer."

"What compels them to return now?"

"They require aid from one another to complete their thinking and hatch their feelings," he explained. "Thus, they speak and sing together. In doing so, they claim that the great thought lifts out of their hearts like a massive vessel riding the high tide of a river."

"Do they pray alongside their singing?"

"No," the raven shook his head. "They have discovered that each person prays most effectively in their own silent heart. Observe—there goes one!"

He pointed upward. A snow-white pigeon ascended rapidly, its wings beating with increasing speed, tracing an invisible spiral staircase into the ether. Sunlight fractured and shimmered against its feathers.

"A pigeon," I observed.

"Of course you see a pigeon," the raven agreed. "For there is the bird! Yet, I perceive a prayer ascending. I wonder now which heart serves as that dove's mother. Perhaps someone has awakened within my own cemetery."

"How can a pigeon constitute a prayer?" I pressed. "I understand how it might serve as a fitting symbol, but for a living pigeon to emerge directly from a heart..."

"It *must* puzzle you! It cannot fail to do so!"

"A prayer is a thought, a purely spiritual entity!" I insisted.

"True enough! But if you comprehended worlds beyond your own, you would understand your own far better. When a heart is truly alive, it generates living thoughts. There exists one heart wherein every thought is a strong, happy creature, and even the dreams are vibrant lives themselves. Some lift heavy burdens from the earth only to cast them back down; others launch their prayers in living shapes—the closest likeness they can forge. All living things began as thoughts and remain suitable vessels for those who think. When one addresses the Great Thinker saying, 'Behold, here is one of Thy thoughts: I am contemplating it now,' that act constitutes a prayer—a message from one small heart to the Big Heart."

His beak dipped suddenly, pointing downward toward the base of a granite block.

I looked and saw a tiny flower. Never before had I witnessed its kind, nor could I articulate the profound sensation it evoked through its graceful, trusting posture, its hue, and its scent, which smelled of a new world that remained intimately familiar. It resembled an anemone, painted in a pale rose shade with a golden center.

"That is a prayer-flower," the raven declared.

"I have never seen such a bloom!" I exclaimed.

"There is no other like it. No two prayer-flowers are ever identical," he replied.

"How do you distinguish a prayer-flower?" I asked.

"By its expression," he answered simply. "Beyond that, I cannot explain. If you recognize it, you know it; if you do not, you do not."

"Could you not teach me to identify a prayer-flower when I encounter it?"

"I could not. But if I could, what greater good would result? You would not know it through *yourself* and *its* essence! Why learn the name of a thing when you do not truly know the thing itself? Whose duty is it but yours to open your eyes? Indeed, the business of the universe is to reduce you to such foolishness that you realize you are alone, thereby beginning to become wise!"

Nevertheless, I acknowledged that the flower differed from any I had ever known. Therefore, I knew I was witnessing a shadow of the prayer contained within it. A profound awe settled over me as I considered the heart that listened to the flower.
