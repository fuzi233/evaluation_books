In the verdant heart of Spaceland, where the verdure seemed to stretch on forever, the inhabitants of Flatland moved with a studied precision, their very lives a testament to the rigors of regularity. For in this world, the unwritten law decreed that every being was a Regular Figure, a polygon with sides of equal measure. The Ladies, with their delicate lines, stood as paragons of this ideal, their slender silhouettes a whispered promise of refinement and poise. The Artisans, with their sturdy, two-sided forms, embodied the practical virtues of industry and discipline. And the Lawyers, like myself, with our four equal sides, represented the very pinnacle of rationality and decorum.

But what of those who defied this strict code? What of those whose sides, like the jagged edges of a shattered mirror, refused to conform to the norms of society? The thought sent shivers coursing through the collective spine of Flatland, for in such irregularities lay the seeds of chaos and disorder. A Woman with sides of unequal length, a Tradesman with a vertex that wobbled like a drunken compass, would be shunned, ostracized, and feared. For in a world where the mere glance of an eye could determine a person's station, character, and intentions, irregularity was a crime against the very fabric of existence.

As I sat in my study, surrounded by the comforting familiarity of my own four sides, I pondered the consequences of such a world. Imagine, if you will, a crowded marketplace, where the gentle rustle of silk and the murmur of conversation were punctuated by the awkward, stumbling attempts of the irregular to navigate the narrow thoroughfares. The very thought was enough to send a shiver down the spine, for in such a world, the most trivial encounter could become a potential minefield of misunderstandings and conflict.

And yet, as I delved deeper into the mysteries of our society, I began to realize that this rigid adherence to regularity was not simply a matter of expedience or convenience. It was a fundamental aspect of our very nature, a reflection of the harmony and order that governed the universe itself. The Law, that great and unforgiving arbiter of our world, had long ago decreed that irregularity was a synonym for moral obliquity and criminality, and so it was.
