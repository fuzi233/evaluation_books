As the young stranger strolled through the bustling Piazza, he couldn't help but feel a pang of hunger pangs in his stomach. The tantalizing aromas wafting from the nearby vendors only intensified his craving for a hearty breakfast. With a sigh, he reached into his worn leather pouch, only to find himself coming up empty-handed. "Not a single coin to my name," he muttered to himself in a language that wasn't Tuscan or even Italian, but one that held a hint of exotic mystery.

Undeterred, he continued his search for a humble breakfast spot where he might find solace in a simple meal. His eyes landed on a quaint corner of the market, tucked away from the chattering crowds. There, two mules stood patiently, adorned with vibrant red tassels and intricately woven collars. One carried a wooden crate filled with fresh milk vessels, while the other bore a pair of wicker panniers overflowing with an assortment of herbs and crisp greens. A young girl, no more than sixteen, sat astride the mule carrying the milk, her slender frame swaying gently in the morning light. Her bright red hood framed her face, which shone like a porcelain doll's, its beauty accentuated by the complete concealment of her dark locks.

The girl's eyelids drooped, her head nodding forward in a half-asleep, half-slouching posture, as if the weight of her laborious morning chores still lingered within her. Yet, when the stranger's soft voice whispered near her ear, she stirred, her blue eyes fluttering open like a butterfly emerging from a cocoon. Startled, she sat upright, taking in the stranger's striking features and his peculiar, melodious voice. Though she tried to turn away, her gaze drifted back, drawn to the stranger's warm, dark eyes.

"Scusa, bella," he said, his words dripping with the musical cadence of a troubadour. "Forgive me for waking you. My stomach growls with hunger, and the scent of milk has awakened a fierce longing within me." He chose his words carefully, knowing that the phrase "muoio di fame" – "I die of hunger" – would be familiar to her ears. As he spoke, his tone conveyed the lighthearted air of a street performer, rather than a supplicant.
