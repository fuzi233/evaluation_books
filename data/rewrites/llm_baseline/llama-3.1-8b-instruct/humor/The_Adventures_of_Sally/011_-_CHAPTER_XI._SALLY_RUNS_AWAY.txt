As the last wisps of summer's warmth lingered in the air, Ginger Kemp found himself drawn to the rooftop of Mrs. Meecher's establishment, where he could gaze out upon the breathtaking view of Washington Square. The tranquil scene, filled with the gentle rustle of laundry hanging from the clothesline, was a soothing balm to his frazzled nerves. It had been a trying few days since he'd parted ways with Sally, and Ginger had taken it upon himself to respect her need for solitude. Though it wasn't easy, he'd forced himself to stay away, recalling the times he'd faced his own struggles in silence, seeking solace in the quiet reflection that came with being alone.

However, by Saturday, Ginger felt a growing sense of restlessness. The day seemed tailor-made for rediscovery, and he decided it was time to take action. With his week's salary safely in hand – though a generous portion of it had already been claimed by Mrs. Meecher – Ginger pondered how best to console Sally. He considered the possibility of hiring a car and taking her to one of the charming roadhouses along the Boston Post Road, where he'd heard the music and laughter carried on the wind. The more he thought about it, the more appealing the idea became.

The Indian Summer weather, with its soft, golden light, only added to Ginger's resolve. As he stood on the rooftop, drinking in the beauty of the day, he couldn't help but feel that an open-air ride in a car would be the perfect way to spend such a lovely afternoon. The thought of Sally's face, alight with joy, as they sped along the winding roads, was almost too enticing to resist.

Ginger's reverie was interrupted by the sight of the Cunard liner making its way down the river, its four majestic funnels rising above the rooftops like giant sentinels. The Mauritania, the ship that had brought him to America, held a special place in his heart, and he watched, mesmerized, as it disappeared from view. His thoughts turned to Sally once more, and he knew he had to see her soon. He made his way downstairs, donned his hat, and set out to visit her, his heart beating with anticipation.
