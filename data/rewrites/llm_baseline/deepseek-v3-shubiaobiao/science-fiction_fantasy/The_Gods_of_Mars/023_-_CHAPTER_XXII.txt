Dejah Thoris trembled in my arms, her voice a whisper against my shoulder. “John Carter,” she murmured, “even now, I can scarcely believe my eyes. When Thuvia told me you had returned to Barsoom, I dared not hope—not after all these years of silence. And when I learned where I was imprisoned, I feared even you could not reach me.”

Her grip tightened. “Moons passed without word. I had resigned myself. But then—the sounds of battle in the temple. I prayed it was you, that Helium had come for me.”

“Our son was with me,” I told her. “Carthoris leads those men you hear.”

Her eyes darkened. “Where is Issus?”

Dejah Thoris shook her head. “She sent me here before the fighting began. She was afraid—angry. Now I understand why.”

The clash of steel and shouts of combat echoed through the temple halls. I needed to join the fray, but I would not leave her unprotected. Then I remembered the pits beneath us—a temporary refuge.

When I explained my plan, she resisted. “I cannot bear to be parted from you again,” she whispered. “Not here, where that monster might find me.”

But duty prevailed. With a final kiss, I guided her through the hidden door, sealing her inside. The act tore at me, but there was no choice.

---

The battle raged like a storm. Black warriors of the First Born clashed with the red men of Helium in a brutal dance of survival. This was more than a skirmish—it was the turning point of an era. The old ways against the new.

Carthoris fought at my side, his blade as sure as mine. The floor grew slick with blood, the dead piling beneath our feet. Then, through the great windows, salvation appeared.

A tide of green warriors surged across the gardens, their thoats thundering. At their head rode Tars Tarkas, his lance raised high. With a roar, his legion charged. The black line shattered.

The tide had turned.

---

But my heart was elsewhere. I sprinted back to the chamber where I had left Dejah Thoris, Carthoris close behind.

The room was empty.

A silk lay trampled on the floor. A dagger. The door to the pits stood ajar.

I called her name—no answer. Rage and terror warred within me.

“Issus,” I snarled. “This ends now.”

---

The throne room was chaos. Issus cowered upon her dais, her slaves shrinking from her. Before her guards could react, I was upon her, my dagger at her throat.

“Where is Dejah Thoris?” I demanded.

Her madness answered. Shrieking laughter, spittle flying. “The Temple of the Sun!” she howled. “Her and Thuvia and Phaidor—all together! A year they’ll wait. And when the food runs out—” Her grin was a nightmare.

I nearly killed her then. But a fouler fate awaited.

“See your goddess!” I shouted to the gathered First Born. “A withered crone, not a deity. Take her. Let her people judge her.”

I cast her down, and the vengeful swarm closed in.

---

Xodar led us through smoke-choked halls to the Temple of the Sun—a marvel of gold and gemstones, now a prison. Bars separated me from Dejah Thoris. The chamber was turning, the gap narrowing.

“Stop it!” I demanded.

Xodar vanished to seek the keeper of the mechanism.

Through the bars, Dejah Thoris pressed her hand to mine. “Go,” she urged. “Save yourself.”

Phaidor lurked nearby, her eyes venomous. Then, in a flash—

A dagger. Thuvia lunged. Smoke billowed. A single scream.

The wall sealed shut.

---

They dragged me away, though I fought like a madman. The temple burned. The pits flooded.

But none of it mattered.

Somewhere beyond that cursed wall, Dejah Thoris waited—alive or dead, I did not know.

And Phaidor’s final words haunted me.

Only time would tell.
