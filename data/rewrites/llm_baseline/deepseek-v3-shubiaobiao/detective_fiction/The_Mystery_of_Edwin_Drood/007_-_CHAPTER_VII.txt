The moon cast long shadows as Neville Landless and Mr. Crisparkle walked back through the quiet streets of Cloisterham. The young man’s voice was low, edged with bitterness.

“I know very little of my guardian, sir,” Neville admitted.

Mr. Crisparkle’s brow furrowed. “Almost nothing?”

“Nothing worth knowing.” Neville’s jaw tightened. “My sister and I were passed to him like unwanted burdens after our stepfather’s death. A miserly brute, that man—starved us of food, of kindness. Had he lived longer, I might have killed him.”

The Minor Canon halted mid-stride, his face a mask of shock. Neville flinched at his own words.

“I—I speak too harshly. Forgive me. But you’ve never seen cruelty like what my sister endured. She never wept before him. Never gave him the satisfaction.”

Mr. Crisparkle exhaled slowly. The boy’s rage was raw, untamed. Dangerous. Yet beneath it flickered something else—a plea for understanding.

“Why confide in me now?” the clergyman asked.

Neville’s gaze dropped. “Because for the first time, I feel I *can*.” His voice softened. “Because Cloisterham is quiet, and you… you listen.”

A pause. Then, grudgingly: “And because I need you to know the worst of me before you decide if I’m worth saving.”

---

Inside the Minor Canon’s house, laughter spilled from the drawing room. Jasper sat at the piano, fingers hovering over the keys as Rosa Bud sang—a mournful melody of parting. His eyes never left her lips.

Then, without warning, Rosa choked on a sob. “I can’t—!” Her hands flew to her face. “Take me away!”

Helena moved like lightning, catching her before she crumpled. “Hush,” she murmured, pressing a hand to Rosa’s trembling mouth. “It’s over. Breathe.”

Jasper’s hands stilled above the piano. He did not turn.

Edwin chuckled nervously. “Pussy’s not used to audiences. Or perhaps our Jack’s too stern a master.”

Helena’s dark eyes flashed. “*I* wouldn’t fear him.”

Jasper glanced over his shoulder, smiling faintly. “Your bravery does me honor, Miss Landless.” Then he rose and vanished into the shadows.

---

Later, in the hush of the Nuns’ House, Rosa clung to Helena like a child to a protector.

“He *watches* me,” she whispered. “His eyes—they follow my hands when I play, my lips when I sing. Even in silence, he *commands* me to keep his secret.”

Helena gripped her shoulders. “What secret?”

“I don’t know! Only that he… frightens me.” Rosa shuddered. “Sometimes, when his gaze goes distant, it’s worse—like he’s dreaming of something terrible. And I’m trapped there beside him.”

Helena’s fingers tightened. “Has he threatened you?”

“Never in words. But his looks are threats enough.” Rosa buried her face in Helena’s sleeve. “You won’t tell? Eddy adores him. Only you—you’re not afraid.”

Helena’s voice was steel. “No. I am not.”

Outside, the wind rattled the ivy on the walls. Somewhere in the dark, a shadow lingered—patient, hungry. Waiting.
