Gwendolen Harleth stood at the apex of her domain, her porcelain skin glowing like the first blush of dawn as she effortlessly drew back the string of her longbow. The archery ground, a sun-drenched haven of emerald and gold, seemed tailor-made for the leisurely pursuits of the aristocracy, its carefully manicured lawns and towering trees shielding the assembly from the world beyond. As the strains of the military band from Wanchester faded into the breeze, the atmosphere coalesced into a warm, golden haze, redolent of late summer afternoons and the promise of endless possibility.

To the right of the target, a cluster of ladies moved with the stately elegance of a procession of swans, their fans fluttering like wings as they exchanged laughter and whispers. Gwendolen's gaze danced across the sea of faces, her eyes lingering on the clusters of young men who stood transfixed by her presence, their faces aglow with a mixture of admiration and competitiveness. Amidst this sea of adoration, Gwendolen's own thoughts remained anchored to the subtle nuances of her surroundings – the way the sunlight caught the delicate folds of her gown, the precise angle at which the wind rustled her hair.

Within the enclosure, the only hint of commonness came from the occasional flash of a plebeian face, the wives and children of Lord Brackenshaw's tenants, who had been granted permission to attend.
