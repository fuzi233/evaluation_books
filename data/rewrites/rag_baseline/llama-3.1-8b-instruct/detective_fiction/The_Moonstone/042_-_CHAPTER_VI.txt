As I stepped out into the misty evening, the dim gas lamps casting an ethereal glow on the deserted platform, I felt a sense of trepidation wash over me. Gabriel Betteredge, my loyal companion, walked beside me, his footsteps a steady accompaniment to my racing thoughts. In my pocket, I carried the letter that would change everything, and in a small satchel, the nightgown, now a tangible reminder of the mystery that had been unfolding around me. Tonight, I would entrust these secrets to Mr. Bruff's investigation.

The silence between us was a novelty, a rare occurrence in my experience with Betteredge. I broke the stillness, my words spilling out like a pent-up tide. "Before I depart for London," I began, "I have two questions to pose to you, Betteredge. They concern my past, and I suspect they will surprise you."

Betteredge's eyes, weathered from years of service, met mine with a hint of curiosity. "If they will expunge that confounded letter from my mind, Mr. Franklin, they may ask anything else they please." He paused, inviting me to proceed.

I took a deep breath, the cool night air filling my lungs. "My first inquiry, Betteredge, is this: was I intoxicated on Rachel's birthday?" The question hung in the air like a challenge, and Betteredge's response was immediate.

"You, drunk?" he exclaimed, a hint of amusement dancing in his voice. "It's the singular failing of your character, Mr. Franklin, that you reserve your spirits for dinner, and eschew them entirely thereafter!" He chuckled, his eyes twinkling with reminiscence. "But that was a special occasion, I recall. You did indeed diverge from your habits that night."

I sensed a hint of hesitation in his tone, a moment's consideration before he continued. "You appeared wretchedly unwell, and we coaxed you into taking a sip of brandy and water to lift your spirits. I must confess, I poured you a generous measure of our finest Cognac – a full glass, actually – and, I'm ashamed to admit, diluted it with nearly a tumbler of cold water. Any child, let alone a grown man, would be hard-pressed to become inebriated from such a concoction!"
