A few kind souls who suffered through these scribblings in manuscript form remarked—with what I can only assume was pity—that they weren’t entirely dreadful. Meanwhile, certain relatives, possibly out of obligation or a misplaced sense of loyalty, swore they’d purchase a copy if it ever stumbled into print. With such overwhelming public pressure (if one can call two friends and a guilt-tripped aunt “the public”), who am I to withhold these utterly pointless musings from the English-speaking world?

Modern readers demand books that edify, enlighten, and uplift. This one would struggle to uplift a feather, let alone a cow. I cannot, in good conscience, recommend it for intellectual, moral, or even kindling-related purposes. However, if you ever weary of trudging through the so-called “Great Books” (and let’s be honest, who doesn’t?), you might flip through this for a bit. It’ll make a nice change—like swapping a three-hour lecture for a nap.
