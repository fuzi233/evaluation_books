When I saw my own gentle, bewildered brother marched away by the Flatland Council guards to their underground prison cells, my chest burned with urgency. I strained to leap down through the floor of the great chamber, to beg the tribunal for mercy on his behalf, or at the very least to catch his eye and bid him farewell. But not a single limb of mine would move an inch: I was completely, helplessly tied to the volition of my guide. The Sphere’s voice rumbled low and grim around me, “Heed not thy brother now. Haply you will have more than enough time hereafter to grieve with him. Follow me.”

We ascended once more into the wide, bright expanse of Space. “Hitherto,” the Sphere said, “I have shown you nothing but flat Plane Figures and the interiors hidden inside their lines. Now I will introduce you to Solids, and reveal the pattern that builds all of them. Behold these floating square cards.” He lifted one card and set it directly atop another, not shifting it north, south, east, or west as I knew those directions in Flatland, but *through* that hidden, invisible third axis he called height. A second square stacked onto the first, then a third, until the pile was as tall as it was long and wide. “This,” he said, “is a Cube.”

“Pardon me, my Lord,” I stammered, “but to my eyes this looks like a lopsided, gutted Irregular Shape, the kind of distorted figure we only see on the bodies of the most reviled criminals in Flatland. The sight of it makes my skin crawl.”
“Of course it seems a Plane to you,” the Sphere replied, “because you have never learned to parse light, shade, and perspective—just as a Hexagon in Flatland will look like nothing but a straight line to anyone who has not mastered the Art of Sight Recognition. But it is a Solid, and you will know it for a fact when you feel it.”
He guided my hands over the shape, and I quickly learned the marvel of this new being: it had six smooth, flat plane faces, and eight sharp little terminal points called solid angles, exactly the form the Sphere had promised a Square would trace if it moved through Space parallel to itself. My heart soared, thinking that I, a humble ordinary Square of Flatland, was in a way the progenitor of such a glorious, illustrious creature.

I still could not wrap my head around his lessons on light, shade, and perspective, and I did not hesitate to lay my confusion before him. To any native of Spaceland, the Sphere’s clear, patient explanations would be tedious child’s play, but between his lectures, shifting lights, repositioning of objects, and permission for me to touch every shape he showed me—even his own smooth, sacred curved surface—all the fog cleared at last. I could tell a Circle apart from a Sphere at a glance, distinguish a flat plane from a true Solid, no confusion left.

That was the peak, the paradise, of my strange, wild travels. Now I must recount the miserable, undeserved fall that followed. Why, I still rage to think of it, would a spark of thirst for divine knowledge be lit inside me only to be punished? My soul recoils from the humiliation of recalling what came next, but like a second Prometheus I will endure every pang of it, if only I can rouse all beings of two and three dimensions to reject the arrogant conceit that limits the total number of possible dimensions to anything less than infinity. No more personal digressions, no hedging. The exact facts, every word burnt indelibly into my brain, I will set down as they happened, and let my readers judge between me and the cruel fate that befell me.

The Sphere was ready to move on to lessons on every regular Solid: cylinders, cones, pyramids, pentahedrons, hexahedrons, dodecahedrons, and the full form of Spheres themselves. But I dared to interrupt him. I was not tired of knowledge—far from it: I ached to drink deeper, fuller draughts of it than he was offering.
“Pardon me, O my Lord,” I said, “you Whom I can no longer call the Perfection of all Beauty—vouchsafe your servant one small sight of your very interior. Your stomach. Your intestines.”
The Sphere paused, bewildered. “My what?”
“Your insides,” I pressed. “Your own wisdom taught me to aspire to something even greater, more beautiful, closer to perfect than you are. Just as you, superior to every Flatland shape, hold infinitely many Circles inside your single Sphere form, there must surely be a Being above you that combines infinitely many Spheres into one Supreme Existence, surpassing even all the Solids of this Spaceland. And just as we stand here in Space and look down to see the inside of every closed shape in Flatland, there must be an even higher, purer region above us: a greater Space, a higher dimensional Dimensionality, from whose vantage point we can both look down and see the hidden insides of every Solid thing in this 3D world. There, even your intestines, and the insides of all your fellow Spheres, will be laid bare to the poor wandering exile from Flatland to whom you have already granted so much.”
“Pooh! Nonsense!” the Sphere huffed. “Enough of this trifling! Time is short, and there is far more to teach you before you are ready to carry the Gospel of Three Dimensions back to your blind, benighted countrymen in Flatland.”
“Denied me not, gracious Teacher!” I begged. “Grant me but one glimpse of that higher realm, and I will be your docile, unwavering pupil forever, ready to hang on every word you speak.”
“Even if I wished to do so,” the Sphere said, “I could not. Would you have me turn my own stomach inside out to oblige you?”
“But you showed me the insides of every Flatland creature simply by bringing me into your Third Dimension!” I cried. “What could be easier than bringing your servant one step further, into the blessed land of the Fourth Dimension, where we will look down and see the inside of every three-dimensional house, the secret veins of the solid earth, the hidden treasures of all Spaceland mines, and the guts of every living solid creature—even the noble, adorable Spheres.”
“Where is this supposed Fourth Dimension?” the Sphere demanded.
“I do not know its exact location,” I said, “but of course my wise Teacher must know.”
“I do not. No such place exists. The very idea is utterly inconceivable.”
“It is not inconceivable to me,” I replied, “and so it can be still less inconceivable to you. Remember what you taught me! When I stood in Flatland looking at a Line, and inferred a Plane, I was already seeing a Third Dimension I did not recognize, the one you called height, not the same as brightness. So now, when I stand here in 3D Space looking at a Plane and infer a Solid, I am already seeing a Fourth unrecognized Dimension, not the same as colour, infinitesimal, but real, even if we cannot measure it. It follows perfectly from analogy, too.”
“Analogy? What nonsense analogy?” he snapped.
“See for yourself!” I said, rushing through the proof. “In One Dimension, a moving Point makes a Line, which has 2 terminal points. In Two Dimensions, a moving Line makes a Square, which has 4 terminal points. In Three Dimensions, a moving Square makes a Cube, which I saw with my own eyes, with 8 terminal points. The sequence is 2, 4, 8—an unassailable geometric progression! Then in Four Dimensions, a moving Cube must make an even more divine shape, with 16 terminal points! And the bounding sides? A Line has 2 bounding points, a Square has 4 bounding Lines, a Cube has 6 bounding Squares: that is a perfect arithmetic progression, 2, 4, 6. So the 4D shape must have 8 bounding Cubes, strictly according to the same analogy you yourself taught me!
“Tell me now—are there not old tales in Spaceland of beings that enter locked, sealed rooms without opening doors or windows, appear and vanish at will, no one knowing whence they came or whither they go? Do those not make perfect sense as visitors from the Fourth Dimension? I will stake everything on your answer to that.”
The Sphere paused for a long, quiet moment. “Such tales are reported,” he said at last. “But people are divided on what they mean. Most dismiss the phenomena as delusions of the fevered brain, the perturbed angularity of the person seeing the vision. No one has ever proposed a Fourth Dimension as an explanation. So drop this trifling at once, and return to our lessons.”
“I knew I was right!” I cheered, carried away. “Those vanishing visitors must have slipped back into that greater Fourth Dimension I beg you to show me! Why stop there? Once we are in the Fourth, we will not linger at its threshold, we will press on to the Fifth Dimension, then the Sixth, the Seventh, the Eighth, climbing forever into higher and greater wonders—”
I do not know how long I would have ranted, drunk on the ecstatic visions of infinite dimensions opening before me. The Sphere thundered at me to be silent, threatened me with the direst of penalties if I persisted, but nothing could stem the flood of my rapture. It was my own Teacher who had given me this burning thirst for more truth, after all.
The end came sudden and brutal. A great crash echoed out in the void, and a simultaneous jolt tore through my frame, yanking me through Space at a speed that stole my very breath. Down, down, down I plummeted, and I knew I was being hurled back to Flatland. I caught one last, fleeting, unforgettable glimpse of the wide, flat, dull expanse of my home stretched out beneath me, then total darkness. Then a final, deafening thunderclap. When I opened my eyes, I was once more a common, crawling Square, sprawled on the floor of my own study at home, listening to the soft, peaceable call of my wife announcing her gentle approach at my chamber door.
