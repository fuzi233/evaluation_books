I had wandered so long in the dark that I no longer knew where I was. My patience had worn thin when suddenly I stumbled into the path of the leopardess—her wounded paw left a trail of blood, a rivulet rushing against my ankles. The force of it surprised me, and a cold suspicion crept into my mind: *Whose blood was this?*

I followed the sound of the stream, letting it guide me toward Bulika, though I soon questioned how any creature, no matter how vast, could sustain such a flow. No leopardess, no beast of legend, could bleed so endlessly without emptying. Cautiously, I dipped a finger into the current—and found it was not blood at all, but water, clear and murmuring, flowing without a channel over the grass. Its song was sweet, yet I dared not drink. Instead, I walked beside it, letting the coolness on my feet revive me as I pressed on through the thinning dark.

At last, the first light of dawn revealed the silhouette of a city—ancient, its towers jagged against the pale sky. I glanced down, expecting to see the brook still at my feet, but it had vanished. Only bent grass and scattered pools marked its passage. The earth remembered what I could no longer see.

The city walls loomed ahead, surrounded by gardens choked with strange, unrecognizable plants. No flowers bloomed; no animals stirred. Heaps of refuse piled against the stonework, as if the place disgorged its waste but never tended to it. The nearest gate stood half-open, unguarded, its hinges rusted into stillness. I stepped through, into a street frozen in silence.

No movement. No voices. Had I wandered into a tomb? Unease prickled my skin. I retreated, circling outside the walls until I found another gate, another street—this one slowly filling with sluggish figures. Their eyes slid over me, indifferent or hostile. One man shoved me; others threw stones. None would speak. Here, poverty was a crime, and I wore its mark plainly.

When a lumbering brute by the gate hurled his bread at me instead of a rock, I seized it and fled. Beyond the walls, their courage withered. I ate, slept under the sun’s gaze, and woke to moonlight—the same moon, I fancied, that had watched over my first night in this world.

The wind carried a foul stench from the city as I slipped back inside. Shadows clung to corners, figures hunched against the cold. Then—movement. A flash of white. A leopardess, luminous in the dark, slinking after a man who cast no shadow. No, not a man—a *thing*, flat and black, a slice of darkness given form. Where moonlight touched him, he drank it in, turning deeper black, while the leopardess blazed like polished silver.

I froze as they passed. The shadow-man turned—or seemed to—his profile vanishing into a razor-thin line. And then the wind pierced me, a gust so cold it rattled my bones. My heart shuddered like a stone in a child’s rattle.

The city held its breath. And I stood alone, caught between the hunter and the hunted, wondering which I should fear more.
