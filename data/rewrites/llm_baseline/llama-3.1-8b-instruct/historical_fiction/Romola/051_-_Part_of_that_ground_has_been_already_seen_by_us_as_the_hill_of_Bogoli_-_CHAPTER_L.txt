The sun cast its warm rays upon the cobblestone streets of Florence as Tessa made her way through the bustling Piazza, her vibrant dress a splash of color amidst the crowd. She had left her former home on the hill of San Giorgio, a decision made by Tito after the events that transpired there, but one that allowed her to settle in a quieter, more serene neighborhood near the Porta Santa Croce. The scent of fresh bread wafted from the nearby bakery, mingling with the sounds of laughter and music carried on the breeze.

Tessa's thoughts were preoccupied, however, as she navigated the crowded square. She had been longing to see the Carnival, and Tito had been so carefree the previous evening, playing with their children, that she had hesitated to ask for permission. But now, as she approached the spot where the festivities would soon unfold, she felt a growing sense of restlessness. She had always been one to read people's faces, and Tito's expression had been particularly radiant that night – a far cry from the weariness that often clouded his features.

As she turned a corner, Tito's voice called out to her, and she spotted him sitting in his favorite armchair, Ninna perched on the floor beside him. The little girl's eyes sparkled as she watched her father, and Tito's face relaxed into a warm smile as he gazed at his daughter. Lillo, ever the attentive companion, leapt onto his father's lap, and Tito laughed as the child began to playfully thump Monna Lisa, who sat nearby, her hands moving deftly as she spun wool into yarn.

Tessa felt a pang of longing as she watched the scene before her. She had always known how to navigate Tito's moods, how to coax a smile from his lips without prying into his thoughts. And though she had grown accustomed to life with the children, she still cherished the moments when Tito's reserve softened, and he let his guard down. "Ninna is doing wonderfully without me now," she ventured, trying to sound nonchalant as she suggested a trip to the Carnival the following day.
