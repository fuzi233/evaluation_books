The afternoon light filtering through the windows of Castlewood's great hall cast a melancholy glow on the assembled servants. They milled about, their faces a mixture of sadness and expectation, as they awaited the departure of Madame de Bernstein. Gumbo, the charming young black man, stood apart, his eyes fixed on the floor, his thoughts a jumble of conflicting emotions. He had grown to love the household, and particularly the women, who had accepted him with kindness and generosity. Molly, the housemaid, had often slipped him secret gifts – a handful of fresh herbs, a small bunch of dried flowers – and Betty, the kitchenmaid, had even offered to cut off a lock of her chestnut hair in exchange for a token from Gumbo's woolly pate. He had laughed and joked with them, but deep down, he felt a pang of loss, a sense of being torn from the only family he had known. As he thought of leaving, he remembered the words of his father, a wise and powerful king from a distant land, who had told him that true royalty was not measured by blood or title, but by the love and acceptance of others. Gumbo's thoughts were interrupted by the sound of footsteps, and he turned to see the Baroness and her nephew, Harry Warrington, making their way towards him. The air was thick with unspoken words, and Gumbo knew that this was his moment to say goodbye. He took a deep breath, and began to speak, his voice barely above a whisper, but full of emotion. "Madame, milord," he said, "I will miss you both dearly. You have been my family, my friends, my home." The Baroness smiled, and reached out to touch his cheek. "We will miss you too, Gumbo," she said. "But you must go, and follow your heart. You are a true prince, and the world awaits you." As the three of them stood there, locked in a moment of tender understanding, the other servants watched, their faces a mixture of sadness and envy. They knew that Gumbo was leaving, and that they would never see him again. But they also knew that he would carry the memories of Castlewood, and the love of its inhabitants, with him always.
