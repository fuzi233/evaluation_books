In the dimly lit study, a lone candle cast flickering shadows on the walls, as if trying to keep pace with the turmoil brewing within the hearts of those gathered. The air was heavy with the weight of unspoken secrets and the stench of desperation. Inspector Gryce leaned forward, his eyes piercing through the veil of deception that shrouded Thomas Adams, whose words hung in the air like a challenge.

"Are we to believe that you left your brother's study without making a sound, without raising an alarm, and without proclaiming your relationship to him?" the inspector asked, his voice a low rumble that threatened to shake the foundations of the room.

Thomas's gaze drifted towards the floor, his eyes welling up with tears as he struggled to maintain his composure. "Incrimination is not what I fear now," he whispered, his voice cracking beneath the weight of his despair. "From the way you all look at me, I perceive that I am lost, for I have no means of proving my story."

Gryce's expression remained unreadable, but a flicker of surprise danced across his face. He turned to the young man's wife, who sat pale and wan in the corner, her eyes fixed on her husband with a mixture of fear and devotion. "Your wife can corroborate your words," Gryce said, his tone gentle but firm. "She can prove herself an invaluable witness in your favor."

Thomas's eyes snapped back to his wife, his face contorting in anguish. "Must she be dragged into this? So sick, so weak a woman? It would kill her, sir. She loves me—she—"

The inspector's words cut through Thomas's plea like a knife. "Was she with you in Mr. Adams's study? Did she see him lift the dagger against his own breast?" Gryce's question hung in the air, a gauntlet thrown down in challenge.

Thomas's response was barely audible. "No. She had fainted several moments previously, while the altercation between my brother and myself was at its height.
