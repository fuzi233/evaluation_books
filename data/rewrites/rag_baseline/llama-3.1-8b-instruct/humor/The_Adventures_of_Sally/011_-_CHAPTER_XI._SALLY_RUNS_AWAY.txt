The afternoon light filtering through the grimy windows of the boarding house cast a pale glow on the worn linoleum floor. Sally's small apartment, a cramped space with faded curtains and a single window, felt like a sanctuary. She moved quietly, avoiding the creaky stairs as she made her way to the front door. Outside, the sounds of the city swelled – distant horns, the hum of traffic, and the occasional shout of a vendor. The cool breeze carried the scent of roasting chestnuts from the street cart on the corner. 

She had been avoiding Ginger Kemp for days, and the thought of seeing him was a prospect she'd rather not face. Yet, as she stepped out onto the sidewalk, she caught sight of him lingering across the street. His eyes met hers, and for a moment, they just stared at each other. The tension between them was palpable, a heavy, unspoken weight that neither dared to acknowledge. 

Ginger's gaze drifted away, and he began to walk towards her, his long strides eating up the distance between them. Sally watched him, her heart rate quickening as he approached. The sound of his footsteps echoed off the buildings, a staccato beat that seemed to match the rhythm of her own anxiety. 

As he drew closer, she could see the faint lines etched on his face, the weariness in his eyes. It was a look she recognized, one she'd seen reflected in her own mirror. They stood there, suspended in an awkward silence, the only sound the soft crunch of gravel beneath their feet. The Mauritian liner, its smokestacks a familiar sight, chugged along the river, a constant presence in the background of their lives. 

Ginger finally broke the silence, his voice low and measured. "I was thinking of taking you out to dinner tonight," he said, his words a gentle probing, a tentative gesture. The idea was not new to Sally; she'd anticipated it, even dreaded it. Yet, as she looked at him, she saw something in his expression, a quiet vulnerability that softened her resolve. 

For a moment, they stood there, the city around them fading into the background. The sounds, the smells, the endless din of human activity – it all receded, leaving only the two of them, suspended in a fragile, uncertain moment. The possibility of connection, of escape, hung in the balance, a delicate thread waiting to be snapped.
