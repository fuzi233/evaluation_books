Two hours later, I had descended fully from the Gorge and stood amidst that strange country. Though my heart remained troubled and my mind bewildered, I felt a profound joy at the sudden, dazzling splendor of this new land.

Before reaching the base of the Gorge, I had paused at its great mouth to survey the landscape below. From that vantage point, I counted twenty-seven massive volcanoes, ignoring for the moment two colossal ranges of fiery hills burning in the distance to my right, nor the countless smaller vents scattered everywhere. Truly, it appeared to be a realm of fire and water. A solitary volcanic cone stood isolated within a sea, perhaps a mile away from where I stood, with dozens more spreading out behind it. There were three small seas visible, but one immense ocean stretched endlessly toward the red glow of the distant fire-hills, vanishing completely into the crimson light without any visible horizon.

Islands rose from these waters, crowned with volcanoes; elsewhere, the fiery peaks erupted directly from the sea itself. Over the nearest expanse, a thick blanket of steam hovered, suggesting the sea boiled periodically in various locations. Within the red haze of this atmosphere, a low, constant rumble shook the air—nowhere specific, then somewhere else—which I judged to be the voices of the volcanoes themselves, communicating through the fire that consumed them.

Everything was utterly alien to me. This land possessed a constant "Voice of the Energy of Life," making the chaotic World-Noise of my own age sound faint by comparison, yet sharper and more undeniable here than anywhere else.

Let me describe what immediately caught my eye. To the left of the Gorge's mouth loomed a gigantic, blackened mountain rising into the night sky, perhaps fifteen or twenty miles high. From its flank protruded a mighty, peaked volcano, estimated at five miles tall, situated on the far side. Above it, piercing the darkness, rose a second peak another nine or ten miles higher. Even more astonishing, atop the left crest of this black giant, two other immense fire-hills burned and glowed, appearing as strange, smoldering suns within the night. Below these towering infernos, vast mounds of ash and scorched debris spilled from the earth, cast forth by these perched volcanoes over eternity, building grey and somber monuments to the dreadful glory of Time.

To my right lay endless sea and the blinding red blaze of the fire-hills; to my left, however, stretched mighty forests, punctuated by additional monstrous fire-hills rising beyond the tree line. This was the initial impression that seared itself onto my brain and senses.

After descending from the Gorge, I encountered a dilemma: which path led to my destination? I searched for a long time, eventually climbing back up to reconsider. Foolish, I chided myself, for failing to map my route before coming down. Upon returning to the Gorge, I realized there was only one viable option. To my right were only the treacherous seas; to my left, where the shoreline met the water, a clear path seemed open for a distance. Perhaps, having traveled so far, I would discover a way forward. Thus, I descended again into the Red-Shining Country of Water and Fire.

Since leaving the height of the Gorge, it had been twenty-four hours since my last sleep. I needed to find shelter. I located a suitable spot where three large trees encircled a small, dry, and warm rocky basin. After consuming three tablets and drinking some water—my stomach still craving solid food—I made my bed in the rock basin and fell asleep, thinking briefly of Naani before drifting off.

Suddenly, I woke to find myself submerged in hot water. Fortunately, I had not drowned. Standing up, I discovered the basin was filled with steaming, pungent liquid pouring in from a smooth slit on the far side, gurgling and bubbling as if driven upward by a deep, boiling well beneath. Relief washed over me that the water hadn't boiled violently upon entry.

Realizing the water likely flowed in seasonally and receded through fissures in the basin floor—a cycle repeating roughly hourly—I stripped off my armor, retrieving my effects from the pool first. Finding a heated patch of rock nearby, I dried my clothes. Once clad again, I returned to the pool for a refreshing bath, feeling surprisingly secure. It seemed I had left all immediate dangers behind in the Night Lands. Still, I clutched the Diskos tightly at the pool's edge, unwilling to trust my safety entirely. As fate would have it, the country teemed with monstrous beasts, yet I felt no proximity to any Evil Force. These horrors, I surmised, congregated around the Mighty Pyramid, drawn by the spiritual density of thousands of humans packed so closely together, much like sharks following a ship carrying cattle. How such powers gained entry into this state of existence remains uncertain to me, though I have speculated on this before; ultimately, my reasoning offers no certainty.

My clothes dried, and I emerged from the pool, which was nearly draining back into the earth. Dressed and armored, my spirits lifted, though fatigue lingered. I slept for another six hours, occasionally roused by the grunting and bubbling sounds of the refilling pool. When morning broke, refreshed and fed, I prepared to resume my journey through the red-lit landscape.

All day, I marched at a brisk pace, keeping the nameless woods to my left and the sea-shores to my right. Often, trees grew directly into the water, forcing me to navigate through the forest itself. This was a marvel I had never experienced in the Underground Fields; there, the groves held a solemn beauty but lacked this wild, glad mystery. The scent of the woods was intoxicating to my spirit.

Life thrived in these shadowy woods. Living eyes peered out at intervals, retreating into the dark, leaving me unsure whether to fear or ignore them. Nothing attacked, however. Three times that day, I passed small fire-hills erupting with red fire and noise, their disturbances audible through the forest before I reached them. Around each site lay death and desolation where great trees had perished, yet tiny plants flourished in the gaps between eruptions. In a single day, I crossed thirty-seven boiling springs; some hissed silently, others roared loudly enough to sound like wild beasts from afar.

At the eighteen-hour mark, I stopped to eat two tablets and drink water that fizzed richly and quickly. Exhausted, I chose a spot against a large rock, shielded from rear attacks, and fell asleep instantly. Knowing strange creatures inhabited the woods, I resolved to sleep only lightly, guarding my physical form while my spirit remained alert. Before succumbing, I thought of Naani, whose spirit seemed to hover near, striving to communicate. I blessed her and vowed to increase my speed, hoping to reach the Lesser Refuge in the dead world sooner. Sleep claimed me immediately.

Abruptly, I woke. The brilliance of the country revealed my danger instantly, unlike the confusing grey shadows of the Night Land. Scarcely had I propped myself up when I saw figures crouching within the tree line, barely twenty paces away. My spirit had warned me. Gripping the Diskos, I stared in horror. Six squat men, hunched heavily at the neck and shoulders, crouched in a row, partially hidden by shadows. Their eyes shone with the cold light of beasts. Terror seized me, yet armed with the Diskos and my armor, my spirit steadied itself to conquer.

I rose, Diskos ready, but the Humped Men vanished. They had not moved visibly; I had watched that spot steadily. Was it possible they had never been there? No, I knew they were real.

I checked my watch mentally: I had slept five hours. Eating two tablets and drinking water while standing guard, I prepared to move. Anxiety gripped me; those humped men were likely just beyond the trees, ready to strike, or perhaps calling reinforcements. Securing my gear, I launched forward at a great stride, holding the Diskos close, scanning constantly. I felt lean and hardened, believing I could outrun them or anything else of their kind.

For thirty great hours, I maintained this pace, vigilant at all times. Every sixth hour, I ate and drank, pushing onward. I hoped to lose them, but my faith was fragile. Twice and thrice, shapes moved through the woods to my left, matching my speed perfectly yet remaining invisible. This shook my resolve deeply.

Unable to sleep easily without absolute security, I pressed on for another thirty hours, finding no safe haven. Finally, near the end of the thirtieth hour, I spotted water ahead, distinct from the eternal sea to my right. It was a river flowing *into* the sea, originating from the lands to my left. At its mouth lay a small island—a potential refuge from the Humped Men, who seemed to be hunting me relentlessly. Yet, crossing the river was necessary to continue along the main sea.

I knew I could not swim; the river was wide, warm, and undoubtedly infested with monsters. Climbing the bank to find a narrower crossing proved futile; almost a mile upstream, a second river joined the first, creating a complex trap: the sea on one side, the second river on the other, and the first river blocking my path. Perplexed, I scanned for a solution. The urgency forced my wit to sharpen. I sought a fallen tree. Many existed, but gathering two suitable ones exhausted me physically and spiritually.
