As the esteemed social elite gathered on Epsom Downs for the annual English festival, a confluence of acquaintances converged in a vibrant display of cosmopolitan flair. Mrs. Bungay, resplendent in attire reminiscent of the opulence of Solomon, sat alongside the demure Mrs. Shandon in a commodious open carriage, expertly navigated by a pair of fine horses. Amidst the lively atmosphere, Mr. Bungay, rejuvenated by a substantial luncheon, became increasingly agitated, his bald pate glistening with perspiration as he playfully deflected sticks. Meanwhile, Mrs. Shandon wandered among the throngs of patrons, interacting with various individuals connected to the publishing house, while Finucane remained attentively by their side.

As the social hierarchy of the event unfolded, Mr. Archer approached the ladies, offering a courteous greeting and sharing information about the notable attendees. He drew attention to the presence of the Prime Minister, who had recently advised him to support the horse Borax in the upcoming race, though Archer himself favored Munmeer. With a flourish, Archer pointed out numerous high-ranking officials and dignitaries, including the Chinese Ambassador and his entourage, who had been invited by Fou-choo-foo, a mandarin, via letters of introduction from the Governor-General of India, an intimate friend of Archer's. Notably, the Ambassador had brought his personal chef, who had caused a stir earlier by serving a dish made from the Ambassador's own cook's ill-fated canine companion, a beloved Blenheim spaniel named Fido.

Mrs. Bungay's reaction was one of shock and incredulity, and Archer went on to describe the incident in vivid detail, noting that the dish, when paired with green peas, bore a striking resemblance to duck. The discussion turned to the forthcoming marriage between Lady Flamingo and the Chinese Ambassador, with Archer commenting on the peculiar practice of foot binding, a custom he believed would only serve to cripple the young bride. As the conversation continued, Mrs. Bungay inquired about the identity of the carriage accompanying Mr. Pendennis, who had recently been spotted in the company of Mr. Warrington. Archer revealed that Pendennis's financial situation was quite comfortable, with an annual income of £18,000 in land and £22,500 in government securities.
