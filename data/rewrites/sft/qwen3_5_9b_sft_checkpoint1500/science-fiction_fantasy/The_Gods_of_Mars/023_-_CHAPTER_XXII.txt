"John Carter, John Carter!" Her voice was a shattered thing, tears cutting tracks through the dust of her cheeks as she buried her face in the hollow of my shoulder. "Even now, I cannot believe the testimony of my own eyes. When the girl, Thuvia, whispered that you had returned to Barsoom, I listened, but the truth felt like a cruel jest. How could such joy exist for a soul that had rotted in silence for so many years? Only when I realized the nightmare of my captivity, the hell-hole where I was kept, did I truly doubt that you could ever find me here."

"The moons passed, month after moon, bringing not a whisper of you. I resigned myself to the dark. And now... now you are here. For an hour, I have heard the thunder of conflict inside the palace. I knew not what it portended, but I clung to the faintest hope that it might be the men of Helium, led by my Prince."

"And tell me..." Her voice trembled, breaking on the name. "What of Carthoris, our son?"

"He was with me less than an hour ago, Dejah Thoris," I answered, the weight of the past settling heavily on my chest. "It must have been his warriors you heard battling within the temple precincts."

"Issus?" I asked, the name tearing from my throat like a jagged stone.

Dejah Thoris shrugged, a gesture of weary resignation. "She sent me under guard to this chamber just before the fighting began. She said she would summon me later. She was angry, yes, but also fearful. I have never seen her act with such uncertainty, with such terror. Now I understand. She feared you, John Carter. Prince of Helium, approaching to demand an accounting for the imprisonment of her princess."

Outside, the temple groaned. The clash of steel, the roar of voices, the frantic pounding of boots echoed from everywhere. I felt the pull of the battle, the need to join the fray, but I could not leave her. I could not drag her into the whirlwind of violence that swirled around us.

Then, a thought struck me—the pits from which I had just escaped. Why not seal her away there? Until I could return, secure, and bring her home forever from this place of nightmares.

I laid the plan before her.

For a heartbeat, she clung to me with a desperation that made my heart ache. "I cannot bear to be parted from you, John Carter," she whispered, her voice trembling. "Not even for a moment. I shudder at the thought of being alone again, where that monster might find me. You do not know her, John. None can imagine her cruelty unless they have lived it for six months. It has taken me all this time to piece together the horrors I have seen."

"I will not leave you, then, my Princess."

Silence stretched between us, heavy and fraught. Then, she drew my face to hers, pressing a kiss that tasted of salt and sorrow. "Go, John Carter," she commanded softly, though her eyes pleaded. "Carthoris is there. The soldiers of Helium are fighting for her honor. Where they are, you must be. I must think of them, of my husband's duty. I cannot stand in the way of destiny. Hide me in the pits, and go."

I led her to the heavy iron doors, the ones I had used to enter from below. I pressed her fragile form against mine, feeling the warmth of her life fading into the cold reality of separation. It tore at my soul, filling me with shadows darker than the night itself, yet I guided her across the threshold. One final kiss, then the click of the lock sealing us apart.

Without a second's hesitation, I sprinted toward the greatest tumult. Half a dozen chambers later, I arrived at the theater of a fierce struggle. The Black warriors, a tide of dark armor, massed at the entrance to a great hall, trying to block the advance of Red men seeking the inner sanctum.

I found myself trapped behind the Blacks. Without calculating odds, without weighing the madness of my venture, I charged across the chamber and fell upon them from the rear, my long-sword singing through the air.

"For Helium!" I screamed, the cry echoing like a war horn.

I rained cut after cut upon the stunned warriors. Outside, the Reds took heart, their shouts rising—"John Carter! John Carter!"—and before the Blacks could recover from the shock of my intervention, their lines broke. The Reds burst into the chamber.

The fight within that room would become legend, etched into the annals of Barsoom as a testament to the grim ferocity of her peoples. Five hundred souls fought that day, Black against Red. No quarter was asked, none given. It was a battle decided by the ancient law of the strongest surviving, a contest to determine who deserved to live.

I knew the stakes. Upon the outcome of this clash would hang forever the balance of power between these two races. It was a battle between the old ways and the new, yet I questioned nothing. Beside Carthoris, I fought for the freedom of Barsoom, for the liberation of its sons from the choking grip of a hideous superstition.

Back and forth we surged, the floor ankle-deep in blood, the air thick with the stench of iron and death. Dead men lay so densely packed that half the time we stepped over corpses as we clashed. Swinging toward the great windows overlooking the gardens of Issus, a sight met my gaze that sent a jolt of pure, unadulterated exultation through my veins.

"Look!" I roared. "Men of the First Born, look!"

For an instant, the carnage halted. Every eye turned to where I pointed, witnessing a vision no mortal had ever dreamed possible. Across the gardens, standing like a wall of iron, were the Black warriors. Beyond them, driving them back with relentless force, was a vast horde of Green warriors astride their terrible, armored beasts.

From the rear, one rider emerged, fiercer and more grim than any of his kin. It was Tars Tarkas, Jeddak of Thark. As he couched his forty-foot lance, clad in metal, his warriors mimicked the pose. His command was clear. Twenty yards separated the Greens from the Black line. With a wild, terrifying cry, the horde charged.

The Black line held, just for a moment, then shattered. The fearsome beasts, bearing riders of equal terror, passed completely through the ranks. Behind them poured endless waves of Reds. The Green horde broke loose, surrounding the temple. The Reds charged inward, and we turned to resume our interrupted battle. But our foes had vanished, scattered or slain.

My first thought, amidst the chaos, was of Dejah Thoris. "Carthoris!" I shouted, "I have found your mother!"

I launched myself toward the chamber where I had left her, Carthoris close at my heel, followed by the survivors of our little band.

The moment I crossed the threshold, I knew something was wrong. The air had changed. A length of silk lay upon the floor, discarded. Scattered around it were daggers and metal ornaments, torn free in a frantic struggle. But worst of all, the heavy iron door leading to the pits—the hiding place where I had locked away my Princess—stood slightly ajar.

A bound carried me to it. I threw it open and rushed inside, calling her name, again and again. There was no answer.

In that instant, the edge of sanity slipped away. I felt a rage rise within me, a manic fury that burned hot and blind. "Issus!" I screamed, the name ringing with terrible authority. "Issus! Where is she? Search the temple for her, but let no man harm her but John Carter! Carthoris, where are the apartments of Issus?"

"This way!" cried the boy, and without waiting for confirmation, he dashed off at breakneck speed, plunging deeper into the bowels of the temple. I followed, urging him on, driven by a desperate need.

At last, we reached a great carved door. Through it, Carthoris darted, a step ahead of me. Within, the scene was one I had witnessed before, yet twisted into a fresh horror—the throne of Issus, surrounded by reclining slaves, and encircled by rows of soldiery.

We did not wait. Too quickly, the guards drew their weapons. With a single, fluid motion, I struck down two men in the front rank. Then, using the sheer momentum of my body, I rushed through the remaining ranks, leaping onto the dais beside the carved sorapus throne.

The repulsive creature, squatting there in terror, tried to flee, leaping toward a trapdoor behind her. But I was not to be outwitted by such petty tricks. Before she could fully arise, I had her by the arm. Seeing the guard surge forward, I whipped out my dagger, holding it close to her vile breast.

"Back!" I roared, my voice cutting through the din. "Back! The first black foot upon this platform brings my dagger to Issus' heart!"

For an instant, they hesitated. Then an officer signaled them back. From the outer corridor, sweeping into the throne room at our heels, came a thousand Red men under Kantos Kan, Hor Vastus, and Xodar.
