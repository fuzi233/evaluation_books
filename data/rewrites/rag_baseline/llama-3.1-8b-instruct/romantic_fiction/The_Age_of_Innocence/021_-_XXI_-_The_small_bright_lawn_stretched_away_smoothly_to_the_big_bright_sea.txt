The lawn unfolded like a pale canvas before him, its edges softened by the late morning haze. The scent of blooming flowers wafted up from the geraniums and coleus that lined the winding path leading to the sea. A cluster of cast-iron vases, their chocolate-colored paint faded to a warm patina, stood sentinel along the path, their garlands of petunia and ivy geranium spilling over the neatly raked gravel.

Halfway between the edge of the cliff and the square wooden house, two large targets had been set up against a backdrop of shrubbery. Across from the targets, a real tent stood, surrounded by benches and garden-seats. Ladies in summer dresses and gentlemen in grey frock-coats and tall hats strolled about the lawn or sat on the benches, their conversations punctuated by the occasional burst of laughter. A slender girl in starched muslin emerged from the tent, bow in hand, and released her arrow, its trajectory watched with interest by the assembled crowd.

Newland Archer stood on the verandah of the house, his gaze drifting down to the scene below. To either side of the shiny painted steps, two large blue china flowerpots sat atop bright yellow china stands, each filled with a spiky green plant. A border of blue hydrangeas, edged with red geraniums, ran along the base of the verandah. Beyond, the French windows of the drawing rooms offered glimpses of glassy parquet floors, islanded with chintz poufs, dwarf armchairs, and velvet tables covered in trifles in silver.

The Newport Archery Club's August meeting was being held at the Beauforts', a tradition that continued despite the growing popularity of lawn tennis. For now, the bow and arrow remained a staple of social gatherings, allowing guests to display their elegance and poise. Archer's eyes lingered on the scene, struck by the realization that life seemed to be unfolding as it always had, even though his own perspective on it had shifted profoundly. The transition had begun in New York, where he and May had settled into their new home, a house with a bow-window and a Pompeian vestibule. There, he had fallen back into the familiar rhythms of his office work, finding comfort in the routine.
