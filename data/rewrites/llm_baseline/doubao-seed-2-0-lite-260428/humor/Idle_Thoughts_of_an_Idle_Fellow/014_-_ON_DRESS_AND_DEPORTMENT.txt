There’s a crowd of people who ought to be downright ashamed of themselves that love to spout the line: wearing an outfit that makes you feel like a million bucks gives you a bliss high no church sermon or well-meaning homily could ever match. And hate to break it to the prudes, those cynical sods are mostly right.

I still remember back when I was a spry, stupid young lad (the kind of “many, many years ago” storybooks reference to skip all the boring backstory bits), whenever life kicked my self-esteem right in the teeth, I’d drag out my absolute best duds and prance right out the front door. If my washerwoman dumped me for owing three months’ bills, or my 10th blank verse poem got returned with the editor’s dorky little note saying “regret no space to use your very kind offer”, or the lass I’d been head over heels for snubbed me in front of all her friends? I’d yank on that glossy new hat, slip into the pair of trousers with the perfect razor-sharp crease I’d spent three nights tucking under my mattress (not on the floor, you absolute heathen), and suddenly I was a new man. I remembered there were a dozen other washerwomen in the city, and a dozen more girls who’d absolutely fawn over how clever and handsome I was. I was ready to go flirt with every pretty maid I passed, and I swear that suit gave me the guts to do it.

Clothes do half the work when you’re courting, no question. Any young bloke will confirm that – he’ll spend two whole hours dithering over what to wear before he even leaves the house. The first half hour is just debating: light summer suit, cane, and drab little flat cap? Or black tails, top hat, and brand new never-used brolly? The Fates make sure he loses no matter what he picks. If he goes for the light suit and cane? It’ll bucket down out of nowhere ten minutes later, he turns up at her doorstep soaked, boots caked in mud, and spends the whole evening trying to tuck his feet so far under the sofa no one can see them. If he goes for the top hat and brolly? No self-respecting man dares go out in a top hat without an umbrella – it’s like letting a wobbly toddler toddle down the street alone without a nurse, for heaven’s sake. I hate top hats, by the way. Mine’s five years old now, everyone said it was hopelessly out of style last summer, turns out the old silly shapes are back, so I’m practically a trendsetter these days. But back to that poor courting sod: if he’s hauling the top hat and umbrella, the afternoon turns so sweltering the soap he used to slick his mustache melts, and that perfect little 20-minute curl he arranged on his forehead droops into a sad limp strand that looks exactly like a piece of soggy seaweed. And if by some absolute miracle he makes it to her door all crisp and put-together? She’s out with her cousin, won’t be back till 9 at the earliest.

No wonder poor young lovers stare at those old Christmas card gallants from 70 years ago with pure envy. Curly hair, neat little hats, tight breeches that show off their nice legs, dainty Hessian boots, ruffled frills, dangling watch seals. No wonder the lass in the giant poke bonnet and pale blue sash drops her eyes and melts instantly – you could win a dozen hearts in that getup. What chance do we have in baggy trousers and sad, shapeless monkey jackets?

Clothes shape your entire personality, you know. Stick a bloke in threadbare rags that haven’t seen a wash in months, he’ll skulk down the street staring at his feet like he’s sneaking out to buy a pint of beer behind his wife’s back. Deck that exact same man out in fancy threads and crisp starched linen? He’ll strut down the high street swinging his cane, winking at every lass that passes, as perky as a bantam rooster. Give a man a plumed bonnet, a dagger stuck in his belt, and all those fluffy ruffled bits down his sleeves? He’ll instantly be ready to pick a duel with a stranger over a bad look. Stick him in a heavy shapeless overcoat? He’ll duck behind the nearest lamppost and yell for a copper at the slightest weird noise. I’ll admit all the good, loyal, honest affection you can find under boring tweed and broadcloth is just as solid as anything they had under silk and velvet back in the knight days – but that chivalric spirit of riding a whole tournament just to make a lady smile? You don’t summon that out of a bloke wearing a stuffy black three-piece suit. You need clanking armor, swishing plumes, the rustle of bright satin.

This whole world’s gotten so middle-aged and boring, isn’t it? We went through the baby human phase, running around in loose robes barefoot, then the barbarian teen phase where we tattooed our whole bodies and never combed our hair, then the foppish young adult phase where we pranced around in scarlet doublets and curly wigs flirting and showing off. Now we’re all so sober, so serious, and some would say so stupid. The 19th century world is a stuffy middle-aged gentleman who’d clutch his pearls if he caught himself wearing anything fun. We all dress in black coats, black hats, black boots, acting like it’s deeply embarrassing that we ever pranced around as troubadours or knight-errants in bright colorful clothes. We’re so “sensible” these days, turns out sensible and dull are practically synonyms. And if you’re extra pious? You wear all black, even gloves and neckties, and soon they’ll all be wearing black shirts, mark my words. The halfway decent, moderately sinful folk wear light trousers on weekdays, some even rock a fun patterned waistcoat. And the absolute heathens? The worst sinners alive? They walk around in light summer suits, some even wear white hats. You can’t mention those people in polite company, I probably shouldn’t have named them here.

Talking of light suits, have you ever noticed how every stranger on the street gawks at you the very first time you wear a new light suit? By the third time you’ve worn it, the entire population of London is used to it. Not that I’d know, of course. I don’t wear those, those are only for bad people. But sometimes I look in the mirror at my two sad long baggy trousers, all rugged and worn at the knees, my stiff stand-up collar and scuffed flat cap, and wonder why I’m out here making God’s green earth look ugly. And then I get these wicked, wonderful thoughts. Why can’t I wear lavender tights, red velvet breeches, a bright green doublet slashed with yellow? A pale blue silk cape over my shoulder, a black eagle feather sticking out of my hat, a big sword at my hip, a falcon on my wrist, a prancing white horse under me? I could make every stranger’s day brighter just walking down the road. Why do we all have to look like a bunch of identical ants crawling over a dust heap? Why not dress a little fun? We’d be happier for it, wouldn’t we? Let the philosophers dress like old crows all in black, I want to be a butterfly.

Women definitely get to dress as pretty as they want, it’s their superpower. They’re the flowers of the earth, after all – the whole world would be unbearably dreary without their bright dresses and smiling faces. Those “relations” that pop round your dreary bachelor flat? They leave ribbons and laces and gloves and parasols scattered all over the place, it’s like a rainbow wandered in for tea. Summer’s best bit is seeing all the girls in bright pink, blue, white, bobbing between the trees, dotting the green fields, glinting in the sun. You can spot those bright dresses for miles. Right now I can see four white dresses climbing a hill three miles from my window, I thought at first they were mile markers that snuck out to have a laugh. Though that’s extra nice if it turns out those four dresses are your wife and your mother-in-law – you get a whole three miles to hide.

Speaking of long walks and stile posts, can we talk for a second about how every English woman’s boots are at least two sizes too big? I swear no woman in this island can find a boot that fits properly. I’ve lost count of how many times I’ve seen a lass plop down on the top rail of a stile and say she can’t walk another step, her boots are killing her. Every single one says they’re too big. We need a boot revolution, for the love of all that’s holy. In the name of every husband, father, and cousin in England, we demand more narrow size 2 boots in stock. Stop torturing our ladies. And don’t even get me started on waistbands! Dressmakers make them so loose the hooks and eyes pop off every five minutes with a bang that sounds like a thunderclap. Why do they put up with that? They’re obsessed with clothes, for goodness sake, they think about it 24/7. If you ever see two women huddled by a window chatting, don’t stand there thinking they’re talking about poetry or charity work. Move a little closer, you’ll hear one say “I took in the waist and let out one seam, it fits perfectly now” and the other go “Oh I’m wearing my plum colored bodice to the Jones’s house, with a yellow plastron, and Puttick’s has the nicest gloves for one and eleven pence!” Once I took two ladies for a drive through beautiful Derbyshire, all rolling hills and postcard-perfect views. I’d wave my umbrella and go “Look at that! The blue distant hills, that little white house in the woods is Chatsworth!” and one of them would go “Lovely. Hey, how about a yard of sarsenet for the skirt?” I’d point out a waterfall, they’d go “Charming. Have you seen my new handkerchief trim?” I swear if you marooned two women on a desert island, they’d spend all day arguing over whether sea shells or bird eggs make better dress trimmings, and come up with a brand new fig leaf fashion every single month.

Young lads care a whole lot about clothes too, but they never talk about it to each other. Fops get a totally unfair rap from other men. It’s a harmless little phase, it wears off fast. A 20 year old with a little bit of dandy in him is gonna turn into a 40 year old who wears clean collars and brushes his coat. You don’t want a bloke who’s too meek and humble. All that prattle about “modest worth” is rubbish, no one actually likes it. Uriah Heep’s dad was an idiot for telling his son everyone loves humble people. Nothing annoys people more. Half the fun of life is a good row, and you can’t have a proper satisfying fight with someone who just meekly apologizes. You get all riled up ready for a good yell, and they just sit there all soft, you can’t even let your anger out. Imagine being Xantippe, married to Socrates! The man was so infuriatingly calm she poured a full bucket of dirty water all over him, and he just sat there, smiled, and said something wise about rain. She probably spent ages trekking to get that extra muddy water, waited all afternoon for him to come home, thinking finally, finally we’ll have a proper argument. And nothing. She probably cried for an hour after. What good is a great philosopher husband if he won’t even have a silly little fight with you? Women’s lives are so boring, anyway! They can’t go to political meetings, they can’t join the local mock parliament, they’re banned from smoking carriages on the tube, no one lets them read comic papers. The least a good husband can do is give them a nice little row every once in a while for fun. They love that, it makes them tell all their friends what a wonderful, thoughtful man he was after he dies.

Everyone’s looking for the wrong thing in a husband, anyway. You don’t want some super clever literary or scientific genius who messes up the whole house trying to do experiments. What you want is a bloke who can run an errand perfectly without inserting his own stupid opinions, who can hold a baby the right way up, and who doesn’t complain when dinner is lukewarm mutton. That’s the ideal husband. Brains don’t get you any points at all in married life. Perfect example: there’s a little boy who wanted to be a sailor, he told the captain he could recite the multiplication table backwards, he knew how many times “begat” was in the whole Old Testament, he could recite all the classic poems. The captain said “Great, but can you carry lumps of coal?” That’s married life right there. Doesn’t matter how much fancy stuff you know, can you carry the coal?
