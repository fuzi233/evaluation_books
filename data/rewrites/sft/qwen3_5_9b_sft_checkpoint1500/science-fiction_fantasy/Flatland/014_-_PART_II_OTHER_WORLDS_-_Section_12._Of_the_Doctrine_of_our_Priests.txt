The entire theological framework of the Circles distills down to a singular, absolute axiom: "Attend to your Configuration." Whether the realm is political, ecclesiastical, or moral, every tenet serves the singular objective of refining individual and collective geometry, with the ultimate focus placed upon the perfection of the Circle itself. All other matters are merely subservient to this geometric imperative.

The Circles pride themselves on having eradicated the ancient, dangerous heresy—the notion that behavior stems from free will, effort, training, or encouragement. Instead, Pantocyclus, the legendary Circle who quelled the Colour Revolt, established the dogma that *Configuration is Destiny*. To be born an Isosceles with unequal sides is to be doomed to error unless corrected at the Isosceles Hospital; similarly, a Triangle, Square, or irregular Polygon born with flaws must seek the Regular Hospitals. Without such intervention, the inevitable fate is imprisonment or execution by the Angle of the State Executioner.

In this view, every fault, from minor transgression to heinous crime, is traced back to a deviation from perfect Regularity in the physical form. Such deviations might stem from a jostle in a crowd, insufficient exercise, overexertion, or a sudden shift in temperature causing a frame to contract or expand. Consequently, Pantocyclus argued that praising integrity or blaming deceit is nonsensical. One should not admire a Square's loyalty to a client, for that is simply the natural function of his precise right angles; nor should one condemn a thieving Isosceles, for his theft is merely the tragic symptom of his inherent inequality.

While this doctrine holds theoretical water-tightness, it falters in practice. If a criminal magistrate encounters an Isosceles pleading that his uneven legs compel him to steal, the response is grim and final: "Because you cannot help being a nuisance, I cannot help sentencing you to consumption." However, in the domestic sphere, where death penalties are absent, this rigid logic creates friction. I confess that when one of my Hexagonal grandsons excuses his disobedience by claiming a sudden chill distorted his perimeter—and thus requires sweetmeats to restore balance—I find myself paralyzed. Logically, I cannot refute the link between environment and shape; yet practically, I cannot accept his abdication of responsibility.

To navigate this paradox, I adopt a pragmatic compromise: I assume that a stern rebuke possesses a latent, reinforcing effect on a child's configuration. Though I lack scientific proof for this, it seems necessary. I am certainly not unique in this maneuver; even the high-ranking Circles who sit as judges employ the concepts of "right" and "wrong" with passionate vehemence when disciplining their kin, acting as if moral agency is a tangible reality, despite knowing deep down that a Figure is bound by its geometry.

By elevating Configuration to the supreme principle, the Circles effectively invert the Spaceland Commandment regarding parent-child relations. Where outsiders teach children to honor their parents, we teach them to honor their Grandson or Son—placing them, next to the Circles, above the father himself. Yet, "honor" here does not mean indulgence. It signifies a reverent dedication to their highest geometric interests. The father's duty is to submerge his own needs to ensure the posterity advances, thereby securing both the lineage and the stability of the State.

If I, a humble Square, may voice an observation, the system's fatal flaw lies in its treatment of Women.

Since preventing irregular births is paramount for societal ascent, no woman bearing the stain of irregularity in her bloodline may wed a man seeking elevation through regular degrees. While male irregularity is a measurable defect visible to the eye, women appear perfectly straight and regular externally. Therefore, the State maintains rigorous pedigrees, supervised by the government, to detect "invisible irregularities"—potential genetic flaws hidden within the female line. A marriage certificate without a verified pedigree is null and void.

Paradoxically, one might expect a Circle, obsessed with ancestral glory and the future production of a Chief Circle, to be the most scrupulous regarding a wife's lineage. Regrettably, the opposite is true. As social standing rises, vigilance regarding pedigree wanes. An ambitious Isosceles desperate for an Equilateral son will never tolerate a wife with a single irregular ancestor. A Square or Pentagon checks no further back than five generations. But a Hexagon or Dodecagon grows increasingly careless. Even a Circle, possessing supreme authority, has been known to marry a woman descended from an irregular great-grandfather, seduced by a superior luster or a melodious voice—a trait we consider "excellent" in females, often outweighing geometric purity.

These ill-advised unions frequently result in sterility or the birth of diminished figures. Yet, losing a side or two in a highly developed polygon is often imperceptible, and Neo-Therapeutic operations can sometimes repair the damage. The Circles, arrogant in their supremacy, tend to acquiesce to infertility as a natural law of superior evolution. If left unchecked, this trend threatens to accelerate the decline of the Circular class, eventually rendering the birth of a Chief Circle impossible and collapsing the Constitution of Flatland.

A second, equally ominous concern arises from the same source: the official policy toward female education. Three centuries ago, the Chief Circle decreed that women, lacking Reason but overflowing with Emotion, must cease to be treated as rational beings. They were barred from reading and denied arithmetic sufficient to count the angles of their husbands or children. The intended consequence was a sensible decline in their intellectual power across generations. This policy of enforced "quietism" remains the law today.

However, I harbor a grave fear that this benevolent intention has spiraled into a catastrophe for the Male Sex. We males are forced to live a bifurcated, dual-mental existence. With women, we speak a language of "love," "duty," "right," and "wrong"—irrational fictions invented solely to curb feminine excess. Among ourselves, in our studies and councils, we revert to a stark, clinical vocabulary where "love" is merely "anticipated benefit" and "duty" is "geometric necessity." Furthermore, we outwardly display profound deference to women, convincing them that we adore them as fervently as the Chief Circle. Behind their backs, however, we regard them as little more than "mindless organisms."

Consequently, our theology differs entirely within the female chamber versus the male sphere. My deepest anxiety concerns the cognitive load imposed on the youth. From the tender age of three, boys are ripped from maternal care and forced to unlearn the emotional lexicon used at home, replacing it with the cold, hard idioms of science. I already perceive a fragility in the current generation's grasp of mathematical truth, a softening compared to the robust intellects of our ancestors three hundred years ago.

I remain silent on the peril of a woman secretly learning to read and disseminating forbidden knowledge, or the risk of an infant boy accidentally revealing the secrets of logic to a mother. These are hypothetical terrors. My plea rests on a simpler, more immediate foundation: the enfeeblement of the male intellect caused by this contradictory upbringing. I implore the Highest Authorities to reconsider the regulations governing female education before the foundations of our geometry crumble under the weight of internal contradiction.
