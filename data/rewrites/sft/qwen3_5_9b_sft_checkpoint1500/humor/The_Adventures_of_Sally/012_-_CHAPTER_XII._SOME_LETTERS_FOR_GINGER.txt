My Dearest Ginger,—

I am recovering, though perhaps not as swiftly as the calendar suggests. Since it has been three months since our last missive, I assume you are already drafting a scolding regarding my apparent lack of mental fortitude. One might reasonably expect that any ailment, however severe, resolves itself within ninety days. Alas, I have not fully conquered my demons. However, I have successfully banished them to the cellar, leaving them to rot in the dark rather than parading them around my head like a neon sign. That, at least, is a victory, isn't it?

I find myself unable to offer you the usual whirlwind tour of London. Having lived here so long, the city has ceased to be a novelty and become merely "background noise." I feel as though I have resided here for centuries, despite knowing better.

Regarding Mr. Faucitt: the inheritance remains unsold, though rumors suggest a transaction is imminent involving a whiskered gentleman with eyes like polished flint. My employer is desperate to escape the concrete jungle and return to his pastoral dreamland of ducks and dirt. London, alas, has failed to recapture its former magic for him. For thirty years, he wandered the streets like a ghost, searching for landmarks from his youth. It seems that roughly once every three decades, London undergoes a violent metamorphosis—a shop front changes from blue to scarlet—and the returning exiles are driven to madness. Mr. Faucitt currently resembles Rip Van Winkle. First, he learned the Empire had devolved from a music-hall into a theatre; then, the Tivoli vanished entirely; finally, upon visiting the baker's shop in Rupert Street (where he once lodged in the eighties), he discovered it had been converted into a dressmaking salon. Only a magnificent magenta fog, reminding him that *some* traditions survive, offered him a glimmer of solace.

Fortunately, I am employed. At *Laurette et Cie.*, I serve as the feminine counterpart to your position at Fillmore Nicholas Theatrical Enterprises Ltd.—essentially, the Right Hand Woman. I hover like a gilded vulture, sidling up to patrons to deliver lines such as, "Chawming weather, moddom!" (which is usually a fib), before shuffling them off to the actual staff. I could endure this charade for years, but Mr. Faucitt is determined to liquidate the firm. Every Englishman I encounter shares a singular obsession: acquiring property in Loamshire, Hants, or Salop to "buy back the old place"—a estate invariably sold in Act I to fund an heir's gambling addiction.

Mr. Faucitt, when a boy, lived near Cirencester (now known as Cissister, a fact I am certain you did not know). After fifty years of forgetfulness, he has succumbed to the urge to spend his twilight years amidst pigs and poultry. I recently accompanied him on a pilgrimage. Oh, Ginger, the English countryside! I cannot fathom why anyone resides in towns. Imagine ancient, grey-stoned manors, golden haystacks, squelchy mud, towering oaks, and distant blue hills. It is a realm of profound peace. If I ever have to sell my soul, I demand forty years in a rural idyll as part of the bargain.

Do not assume my preoccupation implies I have forgotten you. To prove otherwise, I read the newspaper a week ago and spotted the headline "International Match." The meaning eluded me until I recalled your glory days as a "snip." I descended upon Twickenham to witness the carnage firsthand. An immense crowd nearly crushed me, but I endured it for your sake. I learned the English wear white and the Welsh wear red. When I asked a local which team played scrum-half, a dozen men in red tackled a diminutive player to the ground with ferocious enthusiasm. You are lucky to be safe in your office chair! Thank the stars you can summon a policeman if anyone attempts to jump on you. You certainly possess hidden depths, Ginger.

While riding atop a bus down Piccadilly, I glimpsed Mr. Carmyle. Fortunately, he did not spot me. I require no reminders of New York at this juncture.

Thank you for your previous updates, but pray, cease sending them. They only dredge up memories I prefer to keep buried. Here is the situation, Ginger: I must write to you because it soothes my spirit, but I beg you never to reply. Can you comprehend?

Speaking of relief, Fillmore and Gladys Winch are wed! Based on my observations, this is the finest decision Brother F. has ever made. She is delightful. I intend to pen him a note...

Dearest Ginger,—

Last week, a Sunday paper announced that *"The Primrose Way"* was a triumph in New York. Naturally, I rejoiced. Yet, I harbor a suspicion that newspapers publishing such trivia are playing fast and loose with reality. It is unsettling.

Feeling somewhat melancholic and isolated, I committed a folly typical of my condition. I visited your club and requested your presence. A kindly uniformed attendant, speaking with paternal warmth, informed me you had not been seen recently and likely resided abroad. He summoned a diminutive page, also in uniform, who skipped away chanting, "Mister Kemp! Mister Kemp!" in a piercing treble. Hearing your name echo across the distance filled me with shame. I tipped the boy two pence, presumably breaking regulations, though he seemed pleased.

Mr. Faucitt has sold the business and retreated to the country. Consequently, I find myself adrift.

Monk's Crofton,
(Meaning unknown)
Much Middleford,
(Slang for Shropshire)
Salop,

Dearest Ginger,—

"What is the use?" I ask myself repeatedly. I strive to flee New York, yet it relentlessly pursues me to this hiding place. A fortnight ago, strolling aimlessly down the Strand, I encountered none other than Fillmore, linked arm-in-arm with Mr. Carmyle. I could not evade them. First, Mr. Carmyle had spotted me; second, escaping Fillmore now requires a full day's journey. I flushed crimson for him. Ginger! I actually blushed for him in broad daylight on the Strand. In my darkest nightmares, I never imagined him so colossal. By what sustenance does this giant Feed fill his belly? Poor Gladys! Gazing upon him, she must feel akin to a bigamist.

Clearly, Fillmore remains buoyed by grandiose schemes, casually discussing the acquisition of various English plays. He has crossed the ocean, presumably to stage *"The Primrose Way"* here. He resides at the Savoy Hotel, where they escorted me to lunch, treating me like a stray sheep found in a barn. It was the nadir of my existence. Fillmore spoke incessantly of Broadway, and by the time he finished digesting French pastries and slumped back, panting slightly for coffee and liqueurs, he had induced a profound homesickness. If I had not wished to avoid a public spectacle, I would have collapsed and wept. It was insane for me to approach the Savoy. It is, effectively, an annex of Times Square. Americans sat at every table to the horizon. I might as well have been at the Astor.

If Fate insists on dragging New York to England solely for my humiliation, I suppose I must endure it. I allowed events to drift, and thus I am. Two days ago, I drifted here. Mr. Carmyle, seemingly enamored with Fillmore, extended an invitation to both us for Monk's Crofton. I possessed no excuse to refuse. Thus, I arrived, now seated in a cavernous bedroom with a roaring fire, plush armchairs, and every conceivable luxury. Fillmore is currently golfing. He departs for New York on Saturday aboard the *Mauretania*. I am horrified to learn, from his own lips, that he is now promoting a light-weight championship in Jersey City and guaranteeing astronomical sums to the boxers. Argument is futile; he simply cites figures regarding others' windfalls. Moreover, it is too late now. The bout is scheduled for within weeks. Still, the thought makes my skin crawl.

Enough with the gloom. Let us pivot. Are you acquainted with Monk's Crofton? Likely not, as I recall it being a recent acquisition purchased by Mr. Carmyle from a lord who lost fortunes on the Stock Exchange. I trust you have not seen it, for I wish to lavish upon it a description of epic proportions. I need to pour out my soul regarding this place. Ginger, what crime has England committed to deserve such paradises? I thought Mr. Faucitt's Cissister residence was charming, but it barely scratches the surface. His home is a standard country estate; this is a *Seat*. Monk's Crofton belongs in the pages of classic literature. "The sunset fell upon the walls of G---- Castle..." I can envision Tennyson's *Maud* inhabiting this sphere. It is one of England's stately homes, majestic and breathtaking.
