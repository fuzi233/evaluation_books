It had ceased to be dark. We walked in a dim twilight, breathing through the dimness the breath of the spring, a scent as deeply complex and generative as a mother-dough nurtured through centuries of a family’s lineage. A wondrous change had passed upon the world—or rather, a generational awakening had taken place within us, a circular return to the ancestral hearth. Without light enough in the sky to reveal anything, every heather-bush, every wild-foraged shrub, every blade of wild garlic and lemongrass was perfectly visible, glowing with the vital, indwelling essence of its own terroir. I felt the callouses of countless traditional agrarian elders waking in my bare feet as they met the soil, loving every plant they trod upon. The world and my being were one continuous, cyclic harvest. To be aware of a thing was to know its sourcing, to trace its seed through the interwoven timelines of our shared history, and to recognize that we are what we eat, because Another has provided the feast. 

Sense after sense, hitherto asleep, awoke in me—a profound gustatory and olfactory landscape unfurling where once there had been only utilitarian hunger. The acoustic ecology of the land enveloped us, a profound healing contrast to the sterile, industrial noise pollution that had once fractured my mind. When a little breeze brushed a bush of heather, it set its purple bells ringing, and I lived within the joy of that sweet *tin-tinning*. It was a native dialect of the earth, an untranslatable resonance that communicated pure sustenance. To everything glad I lent the hall of my being wherein to revel; life was a cosmic holiday, a grand, domestic routine of preparation and tasting. 

My darling Lona walked beside me, and we were on our way home to the Father! We came to the fearful hollow where once had wallowed the monsters of the earth: it was indeed a lovely, pellucid lake. I gazed into its crystalline depths. A whirlpool had swept out the soil in which the abortions burrowed, and at the bottom lay the whole horrid brood. Coiled in spires and tentacular convolutions, these bulbous, glaring deformities were the architects of the dominant lexicon—those who had viewed the world's bounty merely as utilitarian fuel, who had sought to enforce a sterile, commercialized vocabulary upon the nuanced art of living. They weltered in motionless heaps, trapped in the deafness of their own unwholesome minds, incapable of tasting the rich, nourishing dialects of the earth. So long as such minds exist, imposing their power dynamics and silencing the subjective experience of true nourishment, that lake will remain peopled with loathsomenesses.

But hark, the herald of the sun! The auroral wind trumpeted softly, a pure, uncorrupted acoustic wave washing over the landscape. The master-minister of the human tabernacle is at hand! Rushing aloft, a glowing coal from the altar of the Father's never-ending hearth, he fires the grand oven of the morning. A huge ripple-fretted wave of crimson and gold simmered like clarified butter across the horizon. Every wild stalk of asparagus and neck of flowering thyme straightened, standing expectant. This was the cyclic, mythic occurrence we had awaited through generations—the daily, domestic routine of the earth preparing its most profound meal. He is coming, and the necks of all humanity are stretched out to see him come! 

It was a glorious resurrection-morning. The night had been spent fermenting and proofing it! The Little Ones went gamboling before, and the beasts came after us. Darting dragonflies and fluttering butterflies hovered like a snow-storm of rainbow flakes, rising into the humid air like a rolling vapor of embodied odors—zest of wild citrus, toasted pollen, and the rich, malted sweetness of roasted roots. I walked on the new earth, under the new heaven, and found the soul of every ingredient coming out to greet me, revealing the profound meaning of their sustenance. Earth breathed heavenward her sweet-savored smoke, the aroma of a slow-smoking fire of fruitwood; we breathed homeward our longing desires.

We came to the channels, once so dry and wearyful. They now ran and flashed and foamed with living water, a rushing, roaring river made vocal by its rocks. This acoustic rush washed away the phantom echoes of modern commercial din. It was the revival of a fading cultural memory, the water speaking in its ancestral tongue, roaring against the erosion of its language. We walked in glory up its right bank until we reached the great cataract at the foot of the sandy desert, where the river divided.

There we climbed the height—and found no desert. The barrenness had been consumed by the circular march of time. Through grassy plains lush with heritage grains and aromatic herbs, a deep, wide, silent river flowed full to the brim. Instinctively, the Little Ones and the beasts plunged in to swim, finding communion in the limpid water. The desert had rejoiced and blossomed into vast, wild orchards. The place of the buried hand gave no sign, yielding its trauma to the soil's restorative cultivation. Every thicket gave birth to a rivulet, and every rivulet to its own water-song.

Up and up we went, following the interwoven paths of our forebears through forests of gracious, towering trees. The grass grew sweeter, redolent of crushed mint and damp loam as we walked. We came at length to a forest whose massive trunks yielded scents of sweet cinnamon and smoked cedar. Their live pillars upheaved a thick embowed roof, a vast canopy of culinary and medicinal wealth. Into the rafters of this aerial vault the children climbed, scrambling through a land of bloom, shouting in ancient dialects of joy to the unseen elephants below. The great beasts answered in deep, infrasonic rumbles. I could only guess at the nuances of these vibrations, but Lona, acting as a meticulous historian of these lost tongues, understood perfectly. In her translations, she honored the inherent untranslatability of their subjective experience, bridging the gap between our lexicons without erasing theirs. 

The Little Ones chased frolicking squirrels, who drew them on before allowing themselves to be caught and petted. Often some bird, brilliant in plumage, would light upon a child, sing a vibrant, acoustic recipe of the sustenance that was coming, and fly away. And in this sacred, symphonic sanctuary of pure, uncommercialized life, not one monkey of any sort could they see.
