As I sat in the quiet gardens of Warrington Manor, surrounded by the soothing melodies of the afternoon, my mind wandered back to those early days at home when Harry and I would often defy our tutor's patience. Little did our mother suspect, Mr. Esmond Warrington, a kindred spirit from Virginia, would one day become a close friend and confidant. My mother still shied away from discussing those tumultuous years, merely hinting at them as "those difficult times" and "the great rift between us." Yet, it was during this period that our lives began to intertwine with Mr. Warrington's, and though he would eventually send me barrels of his renowned Virginia ale, my mother would refer to him only as "your English friend" or "your generous patron," never uttering his name.

We brewed our own ale at Warrington Manor, but Mr. Foker's annual shipment of his finest brew from Ipswich was always eagerly anticipated. His son, a dashing young gentleman, had married an earl's daughter, and Mr. Foker himself was a worthy and kind man, whose acquaintance I had the pleasure of making. This chance encounter led to the receipt of some most welcome financial assistance, which was not merely a boon but also a symbol of the trust he placed in me.

It was not the monetary aid alone that mattered, but the sense of purpose and responsibility that came with the role of Governor, which I had taken upon myself. Mr. Foker's brewery, once situated near Pedlar's Acre in Lambeth, held a special place in my heart, and I cherished the memories of the surgeon, Dr. Voelker, who had tended to my wife during her confinement. A Bavarian by birth, Dr. Voelker had been introduced to me through Mr. Lance, the surgeon, and over time, he developed a deep affection for me and my family. He would often visit our garden, sharing pipes of Virginia tobacco and listening intently as I sang "Prinz Eugen the noble Ritter," a song passed down from my grandfather, who had brought it back from the Marlborough wars.

This shared interest in music sparked a lasting friendship between Dr. Voelker and myself, and his family soon became an integral part of our lives.
