I diverted into a service alley, seeking refuge within a stone archway. As I stepped into the gloom, I paused to survey the scene illuminated by the pale, indifferent moonlight. Before I could process the geometry of the space, a figure emerged—a woman, trembling violently, who froze mid-step to stare back at me. The silence stretched, taut and brittle, until a massive leopard erupted from the shadows. Its coat was a chaotic map of white fur marred by dark, irregular blotches, and it surged across the archway with predatory grace.

Instinctively, the woman pressed herself against my side, her breath hitching in ragged gasps. Pity, sharp and sudden, flooded my chest. I wrapped an arm around her rigid frame. "If that brute comes closer," I stated, my voice level despite the chaos, "I will engage it. You must flee."

"Thank you!" she whispered, her voice cracking. "Have you encountered this before?"

"Several times," she admitted, her knees buckling slightly under the weight of terror. "This is the Princess's pet. You are a stranger; otherwise, you would recognize her."

"I am a stranger," I confirmed. "Yet, is she permitted to roam freely?"

"She is confined to a cage, muzzle locked tight, paws encased in gloves of crocodile leather. She is chained, yet escapes with alarming frequency, draining the blood of any child she can catch. Fortunately, Bulika lacks many mothers." Her composure shattered instantly. "I wish I were at home!" she sobbed. "The Princess returned only last night, and the leopardess is already loose! How do I enter my own dwelling? She is hunting me—I know it! She will be crouching outside my threshold, waiting!—but I am a fool to discuss this with a stranger!"

"All strangers are not enemies," I countered, maintaining a defensive posture. "The beast cannot reach you until I have dealt with it. By then, you will be inside. You possess a roof over your head! What a ferocious gale it is!"

"Escort me home safely, and I shall grant you shelter from this storm," she pleaded.

"We must wait briefly," I instructed.

Her answers revealed a society built on stagnation and absurdity. She explained that her people subsisted solely on digging for precious stones in their cellars, remaining perpetually wealthy because labor was deemed a disgrace. Their ancestors had hoarded resources centuries ago, creating a static economy where wealth was inherited rather than earned. When funds ran low, they liquidated gemstones, convinced the earth held infinite reserves.

"But surely poverty exists?" I pressed.

"It is an abstraction we ignore," she dismissed. "When someone falls into destitution, we simply cease to acknowledge them. This is how we maintain our status. We intend to remain rich forever."

"And when the mines are exhausted? When the stockpile depletes?"

"We have untold quantities, and the ground yields endlessly," she insisted, her eyes fixed on something beyond my shoulder. "Besides, no foreign horde dares approach. They are paralyzed by fear of our Princess. She commands the air, the water, the earth—and presumably the fire. She rules absolutely, accountable to no one."

Periodically, she halted, scanning the darkness behind her with paranoid intensity. I questioned the xenophobia that permeated the city. "Why such animosity toward outsiders?"

"Their presence defiles the sanctity of our city," she declared. "We are ancient and noble compared to any other nation. Therefore, we expel all strangers before nightfall."

"How, then, can you admit me?"

"I shall make an exception for you."

"If no provision exists for housing travelers, why not build one?"

"Such a structure would be demolished, and its owner consumed by flames. How can purity be maintained unless we keep the base masses at a proper distance? Dignity is a fragile construct!"

She recounted that the Princess had reigned for millennia, wielding dominion over the elements themselves. Finally, she resolved to lead me to safety. We navigated a labyrinth of narrow passages, reaching her residence without encountering a single living soul. The building stood in a broader thoroughfare, flanked by towering structures, accessed via a steep, winding staircase.

As we ascended, her confidence evaporated. Fear seized her, and she bolted upward, scrambling up the final flights with frantic haste. I arrived moments later, only to find the door slamming shut in my face. I stood confounded on the landing, trapped in a narrow gap between opposing doors—just enough space for a man to lie prone. Exhausted and unwilling to soil the pristine air of Bulika further, I accepted the cramped shelter, resigned to the indignity of my position.
