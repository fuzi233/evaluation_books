Before dawn broke on the appointed morning, a phalanx of Zat Arras's officers breached our chambers, ushering us forth toward the Temple of Reward. We filed into pairs, marching down the Broad Aisle of Hope, flanked by armored guards and hemmed in by three solid lines of Zodangan soldiery stretching from the gate to the central rostrum.

Upon reaching the raised enclosure, I beheld the tribunal. By Barsoomian custom, thirty-one nobles were chosen by lot to judge peers. Yet, amidst those faces, I discerned not a single soul loyal to Helium. They were all Zodangans—the same nation that had crumbled under the green hordes and subsequently bowed as vassals to Helium. Justice, I realized with a sinking heart, was a farce for John Carter, for his son Carthoris, and for Tars Tarkas, the savage king who had swept through Zodanga's avenues like a storm of fire and blood.

The vast circular arena around us was packed to suffocation. Every stratum of society, young and old, male and female, filled the stands. As we entered, the hum of whispering died instantly. When we halted upon the Platform of Righteousness, a silence as heavy as death engulfed the ten thousand souls watching.

The judges circled the periphery of the large platform. We took seats with our backs to a smaller dais at the very center, positioning ourselves squarely between the bench and the roaring crowd. On that inner dais, each accused would stand during their trial.

Zat Arras, the High Judge, occupied the golden chair of authority. As we settled and the guards withdrew, he rose and bellowed my name.

"John Carter!" he cried. "Take thy station upon the Pedestal of Truth! Thou shalt be judged impartially by thy deeds and learn the recompense thou hast earned!" Turning to scan the assembly, he began his recitation, weaving a tapestry of crimes upon which my fate hung.

"Behold, ye judges and people of Helium," he intoned, his voice echoing off the stone walls. "John Carter, former Prince of Helium, hath returned from the Valley Dor and from the Temple of Issus itself. In the presence of many witnesses, he hath blasphemed against the Sacred Iss, against the Valley Dor, against the Lost Sea of Korus, and against the Holy Therns. Nay, he hath cursed Issus, the Goddess of Death and Life Eternal! Know ye further, by the testimony of thine own eyes, that he hath traversed these sacred precincts in defiance of ancient custom, violating the sanctity of our faith."

"He who is once dead may not live again!" Zat Arras thundered, pacing the platform. "He who dares return must perish forever. Judges, your path is clear. No testimony contradicts this truth. What penalty shall be inflicted upon John Carter for his transgressions?"

"Death!" screamed a judge, leaping to his feet.

Then, a man erupted from the crowd, raising a fist high above his head. "Justice! Justice! Justice!" It was Kantos Kan. Eyes swiveled toward him as he vaulted past the Zodangan line, landing on the platform beside me.

"What sort of justice is this?" Kantos Kan demanded, addressing Zat Arras. "The defendant hath not spoken! He hath had no chance to present a defense! In the name of the people of Helium, I demand fair and impartial treatment for the Prince!"

A roar of approval tore through the stadium. "Justice! Justice! Justice!" The clamor drowned out everything else. Zat Arras, though a tyrant, dared not defy the sheer weight of public opinion.

"Speak, then," Zat Arras growled, turning to me. "But hold thy tongue regarding the sacred things of Barsoom."

"Men of Helium!" I cried, turning my gaze from the judges to the throng, speaking over the heads of my captors. "Why should John Carter expect justice from the men of Zodanga? He asks for none! His appeal is not to them, but to thee, Helium! I speak not for myself, but for thee—for thy wives, thy daughters, and those yet unborn. I speak to save them from the unspeakable horrors I witnessed within the Temple of Issus! From the insatiable embraces of the plant-men, from the fangs of the Great White Apes of Dor, from the cruel lust of the Holy Therns, and from whatever dark destiny the cold, dead Iss awaits them after a life of love."

"Do any of you sit unaware of John Carter's history?" I continued, my voice rising. "How I came from another world, how I rose from a prisoner tortured by the green men to become a pillar of this realm! Never did John Carter lie in his own defense, nor speak ill of Barsoom, nor mock the strange religion he revered. Not a soul here owes his life to a greater debt than the single act wherein I sacrificed myself and the happiness of my Princess to ensure your survival. Therefore, men of Helium, I claim the right to be heard, to be believed, and to serve you as I saved you from the true death on that fateful day."

"I address you, Helium!" I proclaimed. "When I finish, let the men of Zodanga do their worst. Zat Arras has stripped me of my sword; they fear me no longer. Will you listen?"

"Speak, John Carter, Prince of Helium!" a noble voice rang out from the crowd, echoed immediately by thousands, shaking the foundations of the temple.

Zat Arras wisely chose silence, allowing the torrent of emotion to flow. For two hours, I spoke to the people of Helium.

When I concluded, Zat Arras rose, turning to the judges with a conspiratorial whisper. "My nobles, ye have heard John Carter's plea. Ye gave him ample opportunity to prove his innocence, yet he used the time only to further blaspheme. Gentlemen, what is your verdict?"

"Death to the blasphemer!" cried one, springing upright. Instantly, all thirty-one judges rose, brandishing swords in a unified display of condemnation.

Though the people may not have fully grasped Zat Arras's subtle maneuvering, they heard the gavel fall. A sullen murmur rippled through the packed arena, swelling into a roar. Kantos Kan, who had remained steadfast by my side, raised his hand for silence. When the noise subsided, he spoke to the crowd in a cool, measured tone.

"You have heard the doom the men of Zodanga decree for Helium's greatest hero. Perhaps it is duty to accept this sentence as final. Act according to the dictates of your hearts. Here is the answer of Kantos Kan, Head of the Navy of Helium, to Zat Arras and his false judges." With that, he unbuckled his scabbard and hurled his sword onto the floor at my feet.

Instantly, soldiers and citizens, officers and nobles alike, surged forward, pushing past the Zodangan blockade to reach the Throne of Righteousness. A hundred men climbed the platform, and a hundred blades clattered to the ground at my feet. Zat Arras and his officers fumed, yet they were powerless. One by one, I lifted the fallen swords to my lips, honoring them, and buckled them back onto their masters' waists.

"Come," Kantos Kan declared. "We shall escort John Carter and his retinue to his palace." They formed a protective ring around us and marched toward the stairs leading to the Aisle of Hope.

"Stop!" Zat Arras shrieked. "Soldiers of Helium, let no prisoner escape the Throne of Righteousness!"

The Zodangan troops were the only organized military force remaining in the temple, and Zat Arras believed his command would be absolute. However, he miscalculated the fury of the moment. As soon as his soldiers advanced toward the throne, chaos erupted. Swords flashed from every corner of the coliseum, and men charged threateningly at the Zodangans. A cry rang out—"Tardos Mors is dead! A thousand years to John Carter, Jeddak of Helium!" Seeing the hostile attitudes of the Heliumites toward Zat Arras's men, I knew that only a miracle could prevent a clash that would ignite a civil war.

"Hold!" I leaped onto the Pedestal of Truth, silencing the room. "Let no man move until I have spoken! A single thrust today may plunge Helium into a bloody abyss, turning brothers against fathers and sons. No life is worth such a sacrifice! I would sooner submit to the biased judgment of Zat Arras than be the architect of civil strife in Helium."

"Let us yield a point to the other," I proposed, my voice steady. "Let this matter rest until Tardos Mors or his son, Mors Kajak, returns. If neither is back within a year, a second trial may be held—a precedent exists." Turning to Zat Arras, I lowered my voice. "Unless thou art a greater fool than I presume, seize the chance I offer ere it is too late. Should that multitude of swords below draw against thy soldiery, no man upon Barsoom—even Tardos Mors himself—could avert the catastrophic consequences. What sayest thou? Speak quickly."
