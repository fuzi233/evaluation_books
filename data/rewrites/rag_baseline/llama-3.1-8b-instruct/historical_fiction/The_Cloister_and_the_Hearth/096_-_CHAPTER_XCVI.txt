As the last wisps of dawn dissipated above the Rotterdam rooftops, Margaret made her way back to the city, leaving behind the small form of her son, Gerard, in the care of Reicht Heynes. She entrusted the devoted servant with a vicar's grey frock and large felt hat, along with precise instructions on how to manage the boy's daily needs. Her heart still heavy with the decision, she hastened to Jorian Ketel's residence, drawn by an inexplicable affinity for the man. In his company, she devised a plan that, though shrouded in secrecy, would forever alter the trajectory of her life.

As she navigated the cobblestone streets, Margaret deliberately avoided the Gouda parsonage, where memories of Gerard's father lingered. The sting of Gerard's assumption that she could easily forget the priest who had once been her lover gnawed at her, and her pride joined forces with her modesty, urging her to distance herself further from the place. Yet, despite her best intentions, the anguish of separation proved unbearable. Each day without Gerard became increasingly torturous, until she found herself creeping back to the parsonage under the cover of darkness, her footsteps echoing through the empty halls like a thief's.

It was during one such nocturnal visit that she spotted Reicht Heynes in the kitchen, his features illuminated by the flickering candlelight. With a soft tap at the window, Margaret requested that the servant bring Gerard outside unnoticed. As the boy was led out into the night air, a scene of unbridled joy unfolded, followed by a poignant goodbye when mother and child parted once more. To alleviate the pain of their separation, it was agreed that Reicht would escort Gerard halfway to Rotterdam each day at a designated hour, allowing Margaret to reunite with her son.

These clandestine meetings became a bittersweet ritual, with Gerard often amusing himself nearby while Margaret and Reicht engaged in hushed conversations about Luke Peterson. One evening, as they spoke in hushed tones, Margaret revealed her intention to marry the young man, citing her promise to him. Reicht's response was laced with concern, and Margaret's words hung in the air, weighted with the knowledge that her servant had never harbored romantic feelings for the boy. However, their conversation was interrupted by Gerard's curious gaze, and they quickly silenced themselves, their faces a picture of composure.
