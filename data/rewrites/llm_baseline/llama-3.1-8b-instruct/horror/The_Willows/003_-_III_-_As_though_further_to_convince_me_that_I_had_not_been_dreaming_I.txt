As I struggled to shake off the lingering haze of a troubled slumber, I became acutely aware that time had passed since my previous fitful rest. My mind, however, refused to relinquish its vigilance, remaining poised and attentive beneath the surface of my sleep. It was this state of heightened awareness that allowed me to spring awake with a jolt, my heart racing with a primal fear.

The cause of my sudden arousal eluded me at first, but as my senses gradually asserted themselves, I became conscious of a multitude of faint tapping sounds emanating from outside. These gentle patterings had been building in intensity for some time, and in my somnolent state, they had coalesced into a discernible noise. I sat bolt upright, my breath coming in short, labored gasps, as if I had not slept at all. A clammy chill crept over me, despite the warmth of the night, and I shivered beneath the confines of my sleeping garment.

My thoughts turned to the various explanations for the strange sound – the wind, perhaps, rustling through the leaves or whipping the river into a frenzy. But my mind persisted in probing for a more concrete answer. I strained my ears, attempting to pinpoint the source of the sound, and my imagination began to run wild with possibilities. And then, like a flash of insight, a plausible explanation emerged: a branch from the lone poplar tree had broken free, caught in the wind, and was now poised to crush our tent with its next gust. I hastened to investigate, calling out to the Swede to join me.

However, as I emerged from the tent and took in my surroundings, I was met with a sense of disorientation. The wind continued to howl, the stars twinkled above, and the east was slowly lightening, but the tent seemed undisturbed. No bough threatened to come crashing down, no rain or spray beset us. I was left standing there, my heart still racing, my mind struggling to reconcile the discrepancy between my expectations and reality.

A grey, diffused light seeped through the underbrush, casting an ethereal glow on the sandy terrain. The fire had long since burned out, and I felt a creeping fatigue, the aftermath of a sleepless night. Yet, my nerves remained on high alert, my apprehension simmering just below the surface. The river had swelled, its roar filling the air, and a fine mist carried on the wind stung my skin
