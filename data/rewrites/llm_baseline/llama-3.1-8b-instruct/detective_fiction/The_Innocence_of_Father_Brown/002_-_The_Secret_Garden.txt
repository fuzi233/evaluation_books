As the storm clouds gathered outside, casting an eerie glow over the tranquil gardens of Aristide Valentin's estate, the Chief of the Paris Police found himself running late for his dinner party. His guests began to arrive, and his trusted servant, Ivan, greeted them warmly at the entrance hall, where he sat amidst a collection of ancient swords and firearms that adorned the walls. The hall itself was a testament to the peculiarity of Valentin's character, a blend of the old and the refined, much like the man himself. The estate, perched atop a hill overlooking the Seine, was a labyrinth of corridors and rooms, with high ceilings and tall windows that framed the view of the surrounding countryside.

Ivan, a gruff but kind-hearted man with a face as weathered as his years of service, reassured the guests that their host would join them shortly. In reality, Valentin was engaged in a more somber affair, attending to the administrative tasks that came with his position. As a champion of human rights and a fierce advocate for prison reform, he was often called upon to make difficult decisions regarding the fates of those in his custody. Despite the gravity of these duties, Valentin approached them with a sense of duty and compassion, a quality that earned him the respect of his peers and the admiration of those he helped.

When Valentin finally arrived, he was impeccably dressed in black, his dark beard flecked with threads of grey, and his eyes gleaming with a hint of weariness. He made his way to his study, which opened onto the gardens, and paused for a moment at the threshold, gazing out into the night. The moon struggled to pierce the clouds, casting an ethereal light on the meticulously manicured lawns below. Valentin's expression was contemplative, a rare sight in a man known for his logical nature. Some might say that such men are attuned to the subtleties of the universe, sensing the weight of the world's greatest challenges before they come to pass.

Shaking off the reverie, Valentin entered his drawing room, where his guests were mingling and sipping wine. The air was alive with the hum of conversation, and the soft clinking of glasses against the fine china.
