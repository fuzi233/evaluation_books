I decided it was time to bring the Monarch down from his lofty reveries and anchor him in reason. With that purpose, I resolved to reveal to him—however faintly—the truth about the world beyond Lineland: the nature of Flatland itself. So I began:

“Your Royal Highness, how do you distinguish the shapes and positions of your subjects? I myself, before entering your realm, observed by sight that some of your people are Lines, others Points, and that some Lines are longer than others—”

“You speak of impossibility!” he interrupted sharply. “You must have seen a vision. For no one can possibly detect the difference between a Line and a Point through sight. It is contrary to nature! But such distinctions are made by hearing—by sound. And by the same means, I know my own shape exactly. Behold me—I am a Line, the longest in all Lineland, over six inches of Space—”

“Of *Length*,” I ventured.

“Fool!” he snapped. “Space *is* Length. Interrupt me again, and I shall end our conversation.”

I apologized, but he continued with scorn, “Since you’re impervious to logic, let me show you how my two voices reveal my form to my wives—six thousand miles, seventy yards, two feet, eight inches away—one to the North, the other to the South. Listen now. I call to them.”

He chirped twice—once sharp, once delayed—and then resumed, smugly: “My wives hear one voice, then the second, separated by an interval during which sound travels 6.457 inches. From this, they infer that one of my mouths is 6.457 inches farther from them than the other—and thus know my full length. Of course, they don’t calculate this every time. They did it once, before marriage. But they *could*, if needed. In the same way, I determine the shape of any male subject through sound alone.”

“But what,” I asked, “if a man feigns a woman’s voice—or disguises one voice so it cannot be recognized as the echo of the other? Could not such deceptions cause chaos? And have you no way to verify identity by touch?”

This was, admittedly, a foolish question—touch could never work here—but I meant to provoke him. And I succeeded.

“What?” he shrieked, horrified. “Explain yourself!”

“Feel. Touch. Come into contact,” I replied.

“If you mean *feeling*,” he said coldly, “approaching so close that no space remains between individuals—know this, Stranger: such an act is punishable by death in my land. The delicate form of a Woman could shatter under such proximity. The State must protect her. And since Women cannot be distinguished from Men by sight, the Law forbids *anyone*—man or woman—to approach another so closely as to eliminate the space between them.”

“And what purpose would such illegal, unnatural closeness serve?” he went on, “when hearing already reveals everything more precisely, swiftly, and safely? As for fraud—impossible! Voice is the essence of being. It cannot be altered at will. Now suppose I had the power to pass through solid matter, touching each subject in turn—even a billion—measuring their size and distance by feeling. What a waste of time and energy! Yet with hearing, in a single moment, I survey the entire population: their location, form, mind, spirit—all at once. Listen—only listen!”

He paused, listening intently to a sound that seemed to me like the buzz of countless tiny insects.

“Truly,” I said, “your hearing serves you well. It compensates for many limitations. But still, your life must be profoundly dull. To see only a Point! Not even to glimpse a Straight Line! To know nothing of what a Line truly is! To possess sight yet be denied the very visions we take for granted in Flatland! Better to have no sight at all than to see so little!”

I admitted I lacked his refined auditory sense—what he heard as a symphony, I perceived as mere chatter. But I could, at least, *see* a Line distinct from a Point. And I would prove it.

“Just before entering your kingdom,” I said, “I saw you dancing from left to right, then right to left. Seven men and one woman were near you on the left; eight men and two women on the right. Is this correct?”

“It is correct,” he conceded, “as far as numbers and genders go. Though I do not understand what you mean by ‘left’ and ‘right.’ But I deny you *saw* these things. How could you see the inside of a man? You must have heard them—and imagined you saw them. And tell me—what do you mean by ‘left’ and ‘right’? Are they not simply North and South?”

“No,” I replied. “Beyond your motion north and south, there is another direction—one I call from left to right.”

“Show me,” he demanded. “Demonstrate this motion.”

“I cannot,” I said, “unless you step entirely out of your Line.”

“Out of my Line?” he gasped. “Do you mean out of the world? Out of Space?”

“Yes,” I said. “Out of *your* world. Out of *your* space. For your space is not true space. True space is a Plane. Yours is merely a Line.”

“Then describe this motion,” he insisted. “If you cannot move in it, explain it in words.”

“If you cannot tell left from right,” I said, “no words will help. But surely you must know such a simple distinction?”

“I do not understand,” he said flatly.

“Then consider this: when you move forward, does it not sometimes occur to you that you *could* turn—so that your side faces the direction you're moving toward? Instead of always progressing along your ends, do you never wish to move sideways—along your side?”

“Never,” he said. “And what do you mean? How can a man’s inside face any direction? Or how can he move in the direction of his inside?”

“Well,” I said, “since words fail, I’ll show you.”

And so I began to move—gradually lifting my body out of Lineland. As long as part of me remained within his world, he kept shouting, “I see you! I see you still! You are not moving!”

But when I fully stepped outside his line, he shrieked, “She is vanished! She is dead!”

“I am not dead,” I replied. “I am simply outside Lineland—outside your so-called Space—into the true Space where I can see things as they are. And now I can see your Line—your side, your inside—whatever you choose to call it. And I can see the men and women to your north and south. Let me name them—describe their order, size, and spacing.”

After listing them in detail, I triumphantly declared, “Does this not convince you?”

With that, I re-entered Lineland, returning to my original position.

But the King scoffed. “If you had any sense—though I suspect you’re not even a Man, given your single voice—you’d listen to reason. You demand I believe in another Line, another dimension, unseen and unproven. In return, you ask me to describe it—yet you offer no explanation, only magic tricks of vanishing and reappearing. And instead of revealing your new world, you merely recount facts known to every child in my capital. This is madness, arrogance!”

Enraged by his stubbornness—and especially by his ignorance of my sex—I retorted, “Blind fool! You think yourself perfect, when you are the most flawed, ignorant creature imaginable. You claim to see—yet see only a Point! You boast of inferring a Line—while I *see* Lines, angles, triangles, squares, pentagons, hexagons, even circles! I am not just a man—I am a Square, a being of two dimensions, a complete entity compared to your one-dimensional self. Even I, though superior to you, am but a minor noble in Flatland. I came to enlighten you—yet you reject the light!”

At these words, the King advanced, roaring as if to pierce me through the diagonal. Instantly, a thunderous cry rose from his subjects—growing louder, fiercer, until it echoed like a hundred thousand Isosceles charging, backed by the artillery of a thousand Pentagons.

Frozen in place, I could neither speak nor move. The noise surged. The King drew closer. Then—suddenly—I awoke. The breakfast bell rang, pulling me back to the real world of Flatland.
