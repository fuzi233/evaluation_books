As the days passed at the Terrace, Dr. John Graham remained aloof, rarely approaching the section of the room where I sat, his demeanor grave and preoccupied. My thoughts lingered on Miss Fanshawe, anticipating the moment he would speak her name. His restraint only fueled my expectation, and I found myself poised to receive the tender theme like a devoted recipient awaiting a long-awaited gift. It wasn't until after an inner struggle, which I observed and respected, that he finally broached the subject.

"Your acquaintance, Miss Fanshawe, has chosen to spend her vacation traveling," he began delicately, as if testing the waters.

A spark within me flared, but I tamed it, recognizing the need to accommodate his perspective. "Acquaintance, for the time being," I thought to myself, choosing not to contradict, though a correction hovered on my lips. Instead, I invited him to continue, intrigued by the subtlety of his approach.

He settled beside my worktable, his fingers deftly unwinding a reel of thread. "I've heard she's joined the Cholmondeleys on a tour through southern France."

"She has indeed," I replied, watching him carefully.

"Do you and she maintain a correspondence?" he asked, his gaze lingering on the thread as it unspooled.

The question struck a chord within me. "It might surprise you to learn that I've never considered initiating such an exchange."

He raised an eyebrow, but his expression remained courteous. "I've seen her letters to her uncle, M. de Bassompierre."

"They convey a sense of wit and innocence," he said, his tone tinged with a hint of wistfulness. "Her writing is like a gentle breeze – effortless and unassuming."

"I agree," I said, choosing my words with care. "Though, when writing to her wealthy relative, her letters often border on the practical, even mercantile."

His eyes sparkled with amusement. "And what of her handwriting? I imagine it to be elegant and refined."

"It is indeed," I replied, attempting to gauge his intentions.

"I daresay she excels in all she undertakes," Dr. John ventured, his voice low and sincere. "Can you think of a single area where she falls short?"

The question caught me off guard, and I hesitated before responding. "She excels in many areas," I said, deliberately avoiding a more candid answer.
