The sunlight filtering through the windows of Castlewood cast an eerie glow on the dusty floors. Madam Esmond stood at the edge of the room, surveying her domain with a critical eye. The air was heavy with the scent of decay and stagnation, a far cry from the vibrant tapestry she wove in her mind – a picture of perfection, of order, of control. She had always been a master weaver, expertly threading together the disparate threads of her life into a rich brocade of power and prestige.

As she walked, her footsteps echoed through the halls, a metronome marking the rhythm of her thoughts. Her children, those dear, malleable things, trailed behind her, their eyes cast down in reverence. They knew better than to meet her gaze, lest they be drawn into the vortex of her will. The domestics, black and white, moved unseen, their faces a blur as they scurried to fulfill her every whim. And the poor whites, those wretched creatures, they came hat in hand, their gratitude a palpable thing that hung in the air like a challenge.

Madam Esmond's thoughts turned to her husband, that poor, hapless soul. Cut down in his prime, he had been a mere cipher, a placeholder in the grand tapestry she was weaving. His name, once Warrington, now a relic of a bygone era, was all but forgotten. She was Madam Esmond now, a name that commanded respect, a name that spoke of power and influence. The Marquis's title, that grand prize, still lingered in her mind, a reminder of the heights she had yet to reach.

Her father, that wise and venerable figure, had always been a thorn in her side. His humor, that maddening, unpredictable thing, had kept her on her toes, had forced her to navigate the treacherous waters of his approval. But he was gone now, and with him, the one person who had ever dared to question her rule. The children, those pliable things, were hers alone to mold, to shape, to break. And she would not rest until they were as perfectly formed as the intricate patterns she wove into the fabric of her life.
