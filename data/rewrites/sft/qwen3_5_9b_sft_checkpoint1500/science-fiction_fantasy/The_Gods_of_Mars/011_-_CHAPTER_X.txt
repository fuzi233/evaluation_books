The guards led me through the outer gardens, a place of twisted vegetation and silent, brooding air, to find Xodar surrounded by a mob of nobles. They were not merely mocking him; they were tearing him apart with vitriol. Men slapped his face, leaving stinging marks, while women spat curses that his skin drank in silence. He stood like a statue carved from grief, absorbing the abuse without a word.

When I stepped forward, the jeering paused, turning to me with sudden, hungry curiosity.

"Ah!" cried a voice, sharp with malice. "So *this* is the beast who overcame the great Xodar bare-handed? Let us see how it was done."

Another laughed, a musical, venomous sound. "Bind Thurid! Let the noble Dator show this 'dog' what it means to face a true man."

"Do it! Bind Thurid!" a dozen voices chorused.

"He is here," someone called, pointing. From the shadows emerged a giant, a mountain of flesh adorned with resplendent ornaments that jangled softly. Thurid advanced with a bearing that spoke of ancient pride, though his eyes narrowed into slits of hatred as he spotted Xodar.

"What now?" Thurid barked. "What do you want?"

A dozen mouths moved in unison, explaining the insult. Thurid's gaze locked onto Xodar, and the air grew heavy with rage.

"Calot!" Thurid hissed, the name dripping with contempt. "I always suspected you carried the heart of a sorak within your rotting chest. In the secret councils of Issus, you often bested me, but here, on the field of war, where men are stripped of their masks, your scabby soul has finally revealed its ulcers to the world!"

"With those words, I spurn you!" Thurid shouted, winding up a kick aimed squarely at Xodar's ribs.

Something inside me snapped. For minutes, I had been boiling with a cold fury at the cowardly treatment meted out to this fallen comrade. Though I held no love for Xodar, I could not stand the sight of injustice, however disguised, without seeing the world turn red. Impulses I would normally suppress surged through me, hot and urgent.

Standing inches from Xodar, I saw the giant swing his leg. Before the blow connected, I moved. A swift, brutal strike landed on Thurid's shin, the impact sending a jolt of pain through his frame. It was enough to save Xodar from further humiliation, if nothing else.

Silence stretched taut for a heartbeat, then exploded.

Thurid roared, a sound like grinding stones, and leaped for my throat with the same ferocity he had shown on the cruiser's deck. The result was identical. I slipped beneath his lashing arms, dodging the killing grip, and countered with a terrifying right hook that smashed into the side of his jaw.

The massive warrior spun, his knees buckling under the force. He crumpled to the ruby-dust path, unconscious.

The black nobles stared in stunned disbelief, shifting their gazes between the prostrate form of the proud Dator and me.

"You asked me to bind Thurid," I declared, stepping over the fallen giant. I tore the ornate harness from his shoulders and swiftly bound his wrists and ankles, securing him tight. "Now, do unto him as you did unto Xodar. Take him before Issus, bound in chains, that she may see with her own eyes that there exists among you one greater than the First Born."

A woman, the one who had first proposed the binding, trembled. "Who... who are you?"

"I am a citizen of two worlds," I said, my voice ringing clear. "Captain John Carter of Virginia, Prince of the House of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium. Take this man to your goddess, as I commanded, and tell her this: as I have subdued Xodar and Thurid, so too can I defeat the mightiest of her warriors. With naked hands, with long-swords, or with short-blades, I challenge the flower of her fighting-men to combat."

An officer, the man who had escorted me, nodded sharply. "Very well. Come. My orders are absolute. There is to be no delay. Xodar, follow."

There was little mockery in his tone regarding me, and even less regarding Xodar. Having witnessed the ease with which I had dismantled Thurid, the officer's contempt for the disgraced Dator had evaporated. His respect for me, however, bordered on awe. During the return journey, he walked constantly behind me, a short-sword drawn at his hip, a silent sentinel.

We descended the awful shaft in the same car that had brought us up, plunging deep into the earth before entering the submarine. The long, crushing dive took us to a tunnel far beneath the surface world, emerging eventually at a pool that served as the gateway to the Temple of Issus. From there, we traveled on a small cruiser to the distant, hidden Isle of Shador.

Here, amidst the gloom of the buried sea, we found a crude stone prison guarded by six blacks. There was no ceremony, no fanfare. A guard opened the iron door with a massive key, and we stepped inside. As the door slammed shut and the lock grated, a terrible weight settled over me—the same crushing hopelessness I had felt in the Chamber of Mystery beneath the Holy Therns.

Tars Tarkas had been with me before, but now I was utterly alone. Despair began to coil in my mind as I wondered of the fate of the great Thark and his beautiful companion, Thuvia. If they had somehow survived, what hope did I have of reaching them? None on Barsoom dreamed of a place like this, buried beneath the waves of Omean. To try to penetrate this hidden sea against the navy of the First Born was suicide.

My case seemed hopeless. Yet, rising to my feet, I shook away the brooding darkness. I needed to explore.

Inside, the building was roofless, its walls soaring thirty feet high, broken only by two heavily barred windows halfway up. Partitions divided the space into several rooms. Near the center, Xodar sat on a low stone bench, his head bowed, silent since his degradation.

I wandered through the empty chambers until I found the last one. There, sleeping on a stone bench, was a young red Martian boy. He was the only other prisoner. His face was strikingly regular, his limbs graceful, though he was pale compared to the typical red man. I watched him sleep, knowing that rest in prison was a currency too precious to risk waking anyone for.

Returning to my cell, I found Xodar still huddled in the corner.

"Man," I cried, approaching him, "it profits you nothing to mope. It is no disgrace to be bested by John Carter. You saw how easily I dealt with Thurid. You saw me slaughter three of your comrades on the cruiser. Why do you sit in shame?"

"I wish you had killed me then," he muttered, his voice hollow.

"Nonsense!" I insisted. "Hope remains. Neither of us is dead. We are great fighters. Why not strive for freedom?"

He looked at me, eyes wide with confusion. "You know not what you speak," he warned. "Issus is omnipotent. Omniscient. She hears your words, she reads your thoughts. It is sacrilege to dream of breaking her command."

"Rot, Xodar!" I snapped.

He sprang to his feet, terror flashing in his eyes. "The curse of Issus will fall upon you!" he shrieked. "In an instant, you will be smitten down, writhing in horrible agony!"

"Do you believe that, Xodar?" I asked calmly.

"Of course! Who dares doubt?"

"I doubt it. I deny it," I said firmly. "Why do you say she reads my thoughts? The red men have possessed that power for ages, yet they also know how to shut their minds, to seal themselves from prying eyes. I learned the first secret years ago; the second, I never needed to learn, for on all Barsoom, there is no soul who can read the secrets of my brain."

"Your goddess cannot read my thoughts," I continued, leaning closer. "Nor yours, when you are out of sight, unless you will it. If she could read mine, I am certain her pride would have shattered when I turned at her command to 'gaze upon the holy vision of her radiant face.'"

"What do you mean?" Xodar whispered, shrinking back.

"I mean," I said, meeting his gaze, "that I thought her the most repulsive, vilely hideous creature my eyes have ever beheld."

For a moment, Xodar stared at me in horror-struck amazement. Then, with a cry of "Blasphemer!" he lunged at me.

I did not wish to strike him again, nor was it necessary. He was unarmed and therefore harmless. As he charged, I seized his left wrist with my left hand, swung my right arm around his left shoulder, and drove my elbow beneath his chin. I bore him backward, collapsing him across my thigh.

There he hung, helpless, glaring up at me with impotent rage.
