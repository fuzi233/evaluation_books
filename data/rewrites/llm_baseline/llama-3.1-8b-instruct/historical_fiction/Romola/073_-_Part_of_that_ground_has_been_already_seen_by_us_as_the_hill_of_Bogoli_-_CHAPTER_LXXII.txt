As Romola walked through the winding streets of Florence, she couldn't shake off the haunting words that echoed in her mind - the words spoken by Savonarola as he bid farewell to his brethren at San Marco: "Pray for me, for God has withdrawn from me the spirit of prophecy." The memory sent a shiver down her spine, and she felt an overwhelming sense of doubt creeping in about the way he viewed the past. The more she thought about it, the more uncertain she became.

The arrival of the Papal Commissaries on the 19th of May only added to her unease. They came bearing the weight of Savonarola's trial, their presence marked by the fervent cries of the people demanding his execution. The once-peaceful city was now abuzz with a frenzied energy, as if the very fate of Florence hung in the balance. The air was thick with the smell of freshly baked bread wafting from the nearby ovens, a stark contrast to the darkness that had settled over the city.

The next day, Romola heard the disturbing news of Savonarola's further torture. The implements of pain seemed to have left him shattered, and in a fit of convulsions, he retracted his previous confession, declaring that he had spoken falsely in denying his prophetic gift. His words were laced with passion, a desperate attempt to salvage what was left of his dignity. "The things that I have spoken, I had them from God," he pleaded, his voice barely above a whisper.

Despite his retraction, the torture continued, and Romola was filled with a sense of dismay and uncertainty. She wondered what lay ahead, and whether there would ever be a moment when Savonarola could speak freely, unencumbered by the fear of death or falsehood. It was then that she made a silent vow to be present at his final moments, to bear witness to whatever truth he might reveal.

Three days passed, and on the 23rd of May 1498, the city's main piazza was transformed into a somber arena. A long, narrow platform stretched out from the Palazzo Vecchio, its end marked by a massive pile of wood and a single gibbet with three halters, its truncated arm a grim reminder of the fate that awaited Savonarola and his companions.
