As the sun rose over the horizon, the cries of the islanders still echoed through the air, a cacophony of sounds that signaled the urgency of the moment. The beacons, ablaze with a fierce intensity, cast a golden glow across the waters, beckoning the inhabitants of Margarita and Cubagua to flee. For Amyas and his crew, however, the decision to depart was already made. With the alarm given, they had little time to indulge in the thrill of the hunt for another pearl prize. Instead, they set their sights on the open sea, charting a course southwest across the mouth of the great bay that stretched from the Peninsula of Paria to Cape Codera.

Tortuga and the meadow-islands of the Piritoos lay to their right, their green slopes rising like emeralds from the tideless sea. Yeo and Drew navigated the familiar waters with ease, their knowledge honed from years of trading with Hawkins. Before them, the majestic range of the Caracas mountains pierced the sky, its peaks towering above the hills that had previously seemed so imposing to Cary and Brimblecombe. Frank, accustomed to the grandeur of the Alps, and Amyas, familiar with the Andes, gazed upon this new landscape with a sense of awe.

As they sailed, the sea grew choppy, the waves crashing against the hull of their vessel. The gentle breeze, however, remained steadfast, and the crew pressed on, undeterred by the growing turbulence. To their left, the massive wall of Cape Codera loomed above them, its granite face illuminated by the soft light of the full moon. Night fell, and the stars twinkled like diamonds scattered across the velvet expanse, but the crew's focus remained fixed on their destination.

With the dawn, they spotted a ship on the horizon, its sails billowing in the gentle breeze. Yeo urged them to alter course and intercept the vessel, but Amyas and Frank demurred, their hearts set on reaching La Guayra. The wind, blowing directly off the land, made it an ideal day for a return journey, but Amyas's resolve was unshakeable. "We've come too far to turn back now," he said, his voice firm but laced with a hint of doubt. "Besides, we have a duty to fulfill, and La Guayra awaits."
