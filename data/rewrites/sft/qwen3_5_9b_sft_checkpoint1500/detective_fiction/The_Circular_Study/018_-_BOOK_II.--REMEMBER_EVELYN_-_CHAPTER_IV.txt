While the world turned outward, a silent, internal war raged within the depths of a nature hardened against all human sympathy. By temperament, Felix Cadwalader was impervious; by circumstance, he was isolated. I can only hint at the tragedy brewing here, a sorrow so profound it defies ordinary comprehension. We know its shape only through fragments—shredded letters, addressed to no recipient, surviving only because the handwriting betrayed their author. Discovered long after the official account of Felix's demise, these confessions lay hidden beneath the floorboards of Bartow's chamber. How Felix acquired them, or why, remains lost to history, yet they speak volumes of a mind breaking under the weight of its own contradictions.

***

*I have seen Eva Poindexter again, and heaven and hell have contended for me ever since. Eva! Eva! The girl I viewed merely as prey, whom I had sacrificed to my brother. She is far too beautiful for him—a creature too radiant for any man save one untouched by female allure, unaccustomed to voices that stir the blood or faces that ignite the heart. Is this love? Can Felix, whose soul has known but a single thought, maintain a fortress of iron strong enough to exclude a woman, let alone his sister-in-law, from a corner of his breast? Never! I am either the victim of frenzy or the plaything of demons.*

*Yet, when I close my eyes, the vision lingers: those half-lidded eyes, those lips parted in innocence, that guileless bearing, and that divine tenderness tempered with steel, commanding admiration and awakening a fire I cannot extinguish. I must flee. I must never look upon her face again. Thomas's resolve is unyielding; he must never witness mine wavering like an idol struck by lightning.*

*'If Thomas had not been raised in Paris, he too...'* But I am the sole weak link. Curses on my nature!

*'Did I vow to run?' I cannot. Not yet. I require one final glimpse, a mere confirmation that my turmoil stems from her superior divinity rather than my own frailty. If I am merely startled by finding celestial beauty where I expected pale mediocrity, perhaps I should acknowledge my folly before it consumes me.*

*'My pride is restored.' I have seen them together again—he and she. True love burned in his gaze. How could I deny it? And I managed to mask my agony, imposing his duty upon him with a straight face. She loves him—or believes she does—and the design advances. But I shall not linger to watch its completion. No, no.*

*I confessed my tale tonight, disguised as a distant memory. Oh, the devils must laugh at us men! They surely do. At times, I wonder if Father, in the clarity of his newfound vision, joins them in their mockery.*

*'Home, to my wretched secret.' Back to where nothing distracts me from gnawing griefs and thoughts nearly unbearable. I pace the floors. I scream her name aloud, even beneath the portrait of Evelyn. Moments come when I am tempted to write to Thomas—to forbid him...*

*'Eva! Eva! Eva!' Every fiber of this miserable body screams the one word. But no man shall ever know. Thomas must never learn how her image haunts my days and nights, turning life into torment and the future into ash.*

*I await his letters (sparse, cold) like a condemned prisoner awaiting the axe. Does she truly love him? Or will that exquisite, soulful nature demand a stronger mate, a more concentrated spirit, a...?*

*'What have I declared regarding secrecy?' Bartow knows. In his silence, his deafness, he has uncovered my secret, revealing himself through piercing glances, dissatisfied mannerisms, and a jealous rage at which I would laugh in madness if torment did not choke the sound. A mute servant, plucked from obscurity, jealous of my failing for John Poindexter's daughter! He was never jealous of my feelings for Evelyn. Yet, until the day I risked fate by seeking her sight a second time, her name echoed through these halls, and my eyes dwelt on but one spot—the woeful beauty of her portrait, now my living reproach.*

*I have received a shock. The starling, trained to murmur Evelyn's name, today shrieked, 'Eva! Eva!' My first instinct was to crush its throat; my second, to tear it from its cage and press it to my chest. I did neither. I am still a man.*

*Bartow will crush that bird if I do not. This morning I caught his hand on the cage, a murderous light burning in his eye, easily understood. Yet he cannot hear the word the wretched bird whispers. To him, it is merely a syllable, a name to be suppressed. Should I hoist the cage high, beyond this old fool's reach? His deafness, his inability to converse, the precision with which he obeys commands conveyed via my colored slides, his animal-like devotion to my person—these necessities sustain me now. As for the bird—Ah! There it goes again, 'Eva! Eva!'*

*'Is it hate or love? Abhorrence or passion?' Love seeks salvation, yet I harbor no thought of saving her, having acknowledged her affection for Thomas, and believing him worthy. It is not for Evelyn's sake I plan revenge, but for my own! These endless nights and days of torture—the revelation of my own nature—the coerced consent to a union promising them bliss and me eternal anguish—all cry out for vengeance. They shall not escape. These two.*

*I have woven my plans deeply, accounting for every contingency. Time, thought, and gold have been spent. The result is perfect. If they step across the threshold of my Circular Study, they must submit to my will or perish within, dragging me down with them. Oh, they shall never know happiness! Thomas need not suspect; John Poindexter need not guess! I may have forgotten the oath sworn on Father's crossed arms, but I shall never forget the immeasurable sorrows of these past months, nor the humiliation they wrought. My own weakness—the unheard-of, intolerable weakness of it—must be avenged.*
