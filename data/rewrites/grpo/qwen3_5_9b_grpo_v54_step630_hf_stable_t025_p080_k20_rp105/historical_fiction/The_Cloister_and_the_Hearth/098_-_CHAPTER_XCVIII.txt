Two weeks later, a pale, bowed figure made his way to the Dominican convent in the suburbs of Gouda, seeking an audience with Brother Ambrose. Ambrose governed the house as deputy, for the prior had recently passed away and his successor had not yet arrived. This visitor was Gerard, come to end his life as he had begun it. He had entered as a novice on probation, yet the truth was he was a failing man, aware of his own decline. He sought only to die in peace, surrounded by the gentle kindness of Ambrose and the other monks whose doors and hearts had always been open to him. His former home felt too heavy, too saturated with memories of her.

Ambrose, recognizing both the value of his friend and the depth of his sorrow, harbored a kindly hope that he might be cured and restored to his parish duties. To this end, he placed Gerard in a comfortable cell above the gateway and strictly forbade him from fasting or practicing any austerities. But within a few days, the new prior arrived, proving to be a man of stern, almost brutal discipline. Initially consumed with rooting out abuses and tightening the rules, his demeanor shifted abruptly when he learned that the vicar of Gouda had entered the convent as a novice. "It is well," the prior declared sharply, "but let him first resign his vicarage, or depart. I will have no fat parsons in my house." The prior then summoned Gerard, who obeyed and went before him. The moment their eyes met, they both recoiled in shock.

"Jerome!"
