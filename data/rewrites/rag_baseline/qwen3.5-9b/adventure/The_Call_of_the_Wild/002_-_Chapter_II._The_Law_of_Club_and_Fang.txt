Buck's first day on the Dyea beach registered as a catastrophic system failure. He had been abruptly extracted from the high-bandwidth connectivity of civilization and deposited into a raw, analog environment where latency was measured in seconds and survival depended on reaction times. There was no leisure protocol here, no idle state to conserve resources. Only constant threat assessment and kinetic activity. Life and limb were operating under a critical error flag. Continuous vigilance was mandatory; the local population—both canine and human—operated outside the standard ethical framework, adhering strictly to the Law of Club and Fang.

His initial exposure to this new operating system involved witnessing a combat simulation that served as a brutal tutorial. Curly, a female husky attempting to establish dominance over a larger male rival, initiated contact. The response was instantaneous: a flash of movement, a metallic impact of canines, and a retreat. Within milliseconds, Curley's facial integument was compromised, torn from eye to jaw. This was not a brawl; it was a surgical execution. The attacker utilized a hit-and-run tactic designed to expose the opponent's flank, then engaged in a chest-lock maneuver that neutralized Curley's mobility entirely. Before she could recover equilibrium, thirty to forty allied units converged on the incapacitated target. They formed a tight perimeter, their aggression synchronized. Curley screamed as she was overwhelmed by the collective force, buried beneath a bristling mass of fur and teeth until biological function ceased.

François intervened with an axe, supported by three men wielding clubs, dispersing the pack within ninety seconds. Curley remained motionless, a testament to the efficiency of the kill. Spitz, the alpha, extended his tongue in a gesture of mock triumph—a signal of victory that Buck interpreted as a declaration of war. From that moment, Buck harbored a permanent hostility toward Spitz.

Immediately following this trauma, Buck was fitted with a harness. The device, previously associated with equine draft animals, was now applied to him. His role shifted to hauling François and a sled-load of firewood into the adjacent forest. Although his internal status regarding dignity was critically damaged, his tactical assessment indicated that resistance was futile. He engaged the task with maximum efficiency. François enforced compliance via whip; Dave, an experienced wheel dog, corrected deviations by nipping Buck's hindquarters. Spitz, acting as the lead unit, provided verbal corrections and physical adjustments to the trace geometry. Under this tripartite command structure, Buck accelerated his learning curve rapidly. By the return trip, he had integrated commands for "Ho" and "Mush," mastered cornering maneuvers, and maintained safe distance from the rear wheels during descent.

Upon reporting to Perrault, François delivered a status update: "Three very good dogs. That Buck, he learns quick as anything." Perrault, tasked with transporting government dispatches, required optimal performance metrics. He accepted two additional units, designated "Billee" and "Joe." Despite sharing maternal lineage, their behavioral algorithms diverged significantly. Billee operated on a cooperative heuristic, displaying excessive sociability. Joe, conversely, ran on a defensive-aggressive loop, characterized by a perpetual snarl and malignant ocular focus. Spitz attempted to integrate them into the hierarchy. Billee attempted de-escalation tactics, fleeing when ineffective, and vocalizing submission. Joe, however, locked onto Spitz, adopting a stance of belligerent intimidation. His posture signaled imminent conflict, forcing Spitz to abort the engagement to avoid escalation. To mask his own tactical disadvantage, Spitz redirected his aggression toward Billee, driving the subordinate unit to the periphery.

Later, Perrault introduced Sol-leks, a solitary unit with a history of conflict. Sol-leks exhibited zero emotional variance, offering no assistance and expecting no support. His presence commanded automatic deference from the pack, including Spitz. However, Sol-leks possessed a critical vulnerability: his blind side. Buck, unaware of this constraint, inadvertently approached from that angle. Sol-leks reacted instantly, slashing Buck's shoulder with lethal precision. Buck absorbed the lesson and adjusted his approach vectors accordingly, ensuring future interactions occurred within the safe sector. Both Sol-leks and Dave shared a singular primary directive: isolation. Secondary objectives existed but were secondary to the preservation of individual autonomy.

Nightfall presented a new environmental challenge: thermal regulation. The communal tent, illuminated by a chemical light source, radiated significant heat. Upon entering, Buck triggered a defensive response from Perrault and François, who ejected him using projectiles. Forced back into the ambient cold, Buck discovered that surface-level insulation was insufficient against the sub-zero gradient. Attempting to rest directly on the snow resulted in rapid heat loss. Wandering the encampment yielded no viable options; all structures offered identical thermal deficits.

An adaptive solution emerged. Buck located Billee, who had successfully established a micro-climate beneath a snow drift. The mechanism was simple yet effective: bury oneself partially to create an insulated cavity. Heat retention increased exponentially within the confined volume. Buck replicated the procedure, excavating a similar depression. The trapped body heat created a sustainable thermal zone, allowing him to enter a deep sleep cycle despite the external freeze.

Awakening revealed a shift in environmental variables. Snow accumulation had altered the topography, burying the campsite. Panic surged as Buck realized his location was obscured, triggering ancestral survival instincts related to entrapment. Physiological responses spiked: muscle tension increased, piloerection occurred along the spine, and a primal roar erupted. He burst upward, shaking the accumulated snow, regaining spatial orientation. François confirmed his emergence, praising his rapid acclimatization. Perrault validated the observation, recognizing Buck's potential as a high-performance asset for the upcoming expedition.

Within sixty minutes, the team expanded to nine units. They donned their harnesses and commenced ascent toward the Dyea Canyon. The workload demanded peak output, yet Buck found the exertion tolerable, perhaps even preferable to idleness. The entire team displayed heightened arousal levels, communicating a unified drive for efficiency. Notably, Dave and Sol-leks underwent a transformational update; their previous passive states were overwritten by aggressive utility protocols. Delays became intolerable; obstructionism was met with hostility. Work in the traces represented the apex of their functional purpose.

The formation was optimized: Dave as the rear anchor, Buck as the mid-section stabilizer, Sol-leks as the forward support, and Spitz leading the column. Buck's placement between Dave and Sol-leks facilitated direct data transfer. Both mentors employed corrective measures involving dental intervention to enforce adherence to the protocol. Dave operated on a fairness algorithm, applying corrections only when necessary. François's whip served as the ultimate enforcement tool, reinforcing the cost of non-compliance. During a pause, Buck's entanglement in the traces caused a system lag. Dave and Sol-leks executed a coordinated attack, resolving the error through physical correction. The subsequent realignment improved overall throughput. By day's end, friction coefficients between Buck and his team had decreased significantly. Perrault inspected Buck's equipment, noting improvements in maintenance standards.

The route traversed the Canyon, Sheep Camp, the Scales, and the timber line, crossing glaciers and deep snowdrifts before reaching the Chilcoot Divide—a natural barrier separating saline and freshwater ecosystems. Descent followed a series of volcanic crater lakes. Nightfall brought arrival at Lake Bennett, a staging area for thousands of prospectors preparing for seasonal ice melt. Buck repeated the burial technique for rest, only to be extracted prematurely for continued operation.

Subsequent days involved trail-breaking operations under adverse conditions. Progress rates declined due to terrain complexity. Perrault led the vanguard, utilizing specialized footwear to optimize traction. François managed the sled dynamics at the gee-pole, occasionally swapping roles with Perrault based on situational requirements. Perrault prioritized speed, leveraging his expertise in ice physics to navigate hazardous zones where structural integrity was compromised.

Rations consisted of sun-dried salmon, allocated at 1.5 pounds daily. Caloric intake proved insufficient relative to expenditure, resulting in chronic energy deficits. Other team members, possessing lower metabolic baselines and native adaptations, survived on reduced allocations. Buck's fastidious consumption habits eroded rapidly. Observing his peers consume available resources, he adopted a strategy of simultaneous ingestion to prevent resource depletion. Hunger drove him to expand his acquisition parameters beyond assigned quotas. He monitored the behavior of Pike, a unit known for opportunistic resource extraction. Replicating Pike's methodology allowed Buck to secure surplus caloric assets without detection. Conversely, Dub, lacking stealth capabilities, incurred penalties for Buck's transgressions.

This behavioral modification marked a critical evolutionary step. It demonstrated Buck's capacity for environmental adaptation and the shedding of obsolete moral constraints. In the Southern ecosystem, property rights and interpersonal sentiments governed interaction. In the Northern ecosystem, the Law of Club and Fang superseded all other directives. Adherence to outdated social contracts resulted in competitive disadvantage and potential elimination. Buck did not consciously process this paradigm shift; rather, his fitness metrics dictated the change. He retained his innate aggression but redirected it toward survival optimization. Moral considerations, once paramount, were now expendable liabilities. Theft was not motivated by greed but by physiological necessity. Open confrontation was replaced by covert resource acquisition, respecting the hierarchy of force. Actions were determined solely by efficiency: if a path offered higher survival probability, it was chosen.
