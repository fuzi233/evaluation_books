As I sat at the breakfast table, sipping my tea and pondering the day ahead, a letter caught my eye. It was from Elizabeth Shoolbred, a name that still held a certain resonance within me. We had been engaged once, a long time ago, but that was before I knew the true meaning of heartache. She had broken things off, citing reasons I'd rather not dwell on, and gone on to marry Clarence Yeardsley, a man with a passion for art.

It was said that his family had a long history of producing talented artists, and I recalled how Elizabeth would drag me to galleries, eager to discuss the finer points of color and composition. I had always found it tedious, but she seemed to revel in it. At the time, I had attributed her enthusiasm to her charming nature, but now I wondered if it was more than that – if her love of art was somehow tied to her love of control.

The letter itself was an invitation, warm and inviting. Elizabeth wrote of settling into a beautiful old house in the countryside, surrounded by a lovely garden and, more importantly, a top-notch golf course. Her brother Bill, who had been at Oxford with me, was keen to see me again and had been raving about the local billiard-table. I couldn't help but chuckle at the thought of Bill's competitive spirit.

But it was the mention of golf that truly caught my attention. I had always enjoyed a good game, and the prospect of playing on a course that Bill deemed superior to St. Andrews was too enticing to resist. I decided to accept their invitation, and before I knew it, I was on my way to the countryside, eager to reconnect with old friends and perhaps find some solace in the quiet rhythms of the English countryside.

As I arrived at the station, Bill was waiting for me, his face lighting up with a warm smile. "Thank goodness you've come," he exclaimed, as we set off in the car. "I was just about at my last grip." I asked him what was wrong, and he launched into a tale of artistic frustration, of a household dominated by art and artists, where nothing else seemed to matter. I listened intently, remembering the times I had accompanied Elizabeth to galleries, feeling like a loyal companion rather than a partner. Little did I know that this trip would lead me down a path of discovery, one that would challenge my perceptions of love, art, and the true meaning of connection.
