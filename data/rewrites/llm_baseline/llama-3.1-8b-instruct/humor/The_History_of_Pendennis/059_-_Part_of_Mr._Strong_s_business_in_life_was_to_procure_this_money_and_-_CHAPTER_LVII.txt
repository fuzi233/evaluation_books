As they set off on their Continental journey, Worthy Major Pendennis fulfilled his promise to Warrington, easing his conscience and providing some solace to poor Helen regarding her son's attachment to the odious little gatekeeper. Arthur's vanity, initially bruised by the realization that his affections had not been reciprocated, slowly began to heal as he understood that no unfavorable consequences would arise from the brief and unfortunate entanglement. With this weight lifted, the entire party was free to embark on their long-planned excursion.

The rentier, Arthur Pendennis, traveled alongside his wife, Madame Pendennis, and Mademoiselle Bell, accompanied by George Warrington, a 32-year-old gentleman standing at six feet tall, with an unremarkable countenance, dark hair, and matching beard. They secured passports from the British consul in Dover and crossed the Channel to Ostend, where they leisurely made their way to Brussels and the Rhine, visiting the historic cities of Bruges and Ghent along the way. It would be tedious to recount the numerous sites they visited and the conversations they had about the local customs and traditions.

Laura's eyes sparkled as she marveled at the tranquil and ancient cities, while Helen's gaze was drawn to the mystical Beguine convents, where she witnessed the reverent black-veiled nuns kneeling before the ornate altars. The sounds of chanting and the vibrant colors of the ceremonial attire captivated her, yet also filled her with unease. As they walked through the cobblestone streets, they encountered barefooted friars and grand statues of saints and virgins, which only added to Helen's bewilderment. She couldn't help but feel that these scenes defied the written law, and the pomp and ceremony of the Catholic worship left her feeling perplexed.

Each evening, after their strolls or drives, the young men would return to find Helen engrossed in her devotional texts, her favorite passages from the psalms and sacred scriptures lying open on the table. Laura would often pause her reading, sensing the depth of her friend's emotional turmoil. The recent events surrounding her son had left Helen reeling, and she found herself increasingly preoccupied with thoughts of Arthur's changed demeanor. A secret hung between them, a painful awareness that she was no longer the sole recipient of his confidences.
