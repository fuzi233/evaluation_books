The Memory of Feasts Past.

As a young boy, I reveled in the simple pleasures of eating and drinking, much to the delight of my mother, who would often remark on my hearty appetite. I recall a particularly vivid encounter with a stern-looking gentleman who came to dine at our home. He watched me devour my food with an air of fascination, his eyes lingering on my plate as if mesmerized by the spectacle. When he turned to my father, his voice took on a note of concern.

"Do your son ever suffer from the pangs of dyspepsia?" he asked, his brow furrowed in a mixture of curiosity and anxiety.

My father smiled reassuringly, his response a testament to the robust health of his offspring. "No, Colly wobbles here is as sturdy as an oak, never complaining of such troubles." I chimed in, still chewing on a particularly succulent morsel, "What is dyspepsia, pa?" The gentleman's expression turned from concern to pity, as if beholding a fledgling bird about to take flight. His voice was laced with a hint of wistfulness as he replied, "You will learn, in time."

My mother's words of encouragement still echo in my mind, reminding me of the joy I brought her with each meal. As I grew older, I began to appreciate the art of dining, savoring the rich flavors and textures that danced on my palate. The notion of a hearty meal – a sumptuous spread of roasted meats, steaming vegetables, and warm bread – was a staple of my youth, one that never failed to leave me feeling content and at peace.

It is a peculiar thing, observing boys eat when one is not burdened with the costs of sustenance. Their idea of a satisfying repast is a veritable feast, replete with the finest ingredients and an abundance of delicacies. Roast beef, succulent potatoes, and a medley of fresh greens – all these and more comprise the makings of a true boyhood banquet. And yet, as the meal concludes, they are off, their bellies full, their spirits unbroken, ready to tackle the world with the vigor of youth.

How little do they comprehend the quiet joys of a well-fed adult, the sense of satisfaction that settles over one like a warm blanket on a winter's night. For us, a meal is not merely a necessity, but a source of comfort, a reminder of life's simple pleasures.
