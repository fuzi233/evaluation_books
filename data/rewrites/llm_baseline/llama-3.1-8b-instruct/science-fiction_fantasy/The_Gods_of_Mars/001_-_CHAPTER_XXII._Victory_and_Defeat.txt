As the sun set over the Hudson, casting a warm orange glow across the rolling hills, I stood in the quiet stillness of my uncle's old cottage, surrounded by memories of a lifetime. Twelve years had passed since I'd laid Captain John Carter's body to rest in that strange mausoleum in the Richmond cemetery, the one with the open casket and the intricate mechanism that only he could unlock from the inside. It was a peculiar request, one that had sparked my curiosity and fueled my imagination.

I recalled the remarkable manuscript he'd left behind, a tale of adventure and love that spanned centuries, continents, and even worlds. A man who remembered no childhood, yet was old enough to have held my great-grandfather on his knee. A warrior who'd fought alongside and against the green men of Barsoom, and won the heart of the beautiful Dejah Thoris, Princess of Helium. For nearly a decade, he'd been a prince of the house of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium.

The memories swirled in my mind like the scent of old spices wafting from the kitchen, where my aunt's famous apple pie was baking in the oven. The aroma transported me to a different time, a different place, and I found myself wondering if my uncle had truly passed on, or if he'd returned to the dead sea bottoms of Mars, seeking the fabled atmosphere plant he'd opened to save countless lives on that distant world. Had he found his princess, and their young son, waiting in the royal gardens of Tardos Mors? Or had he discovered he'd arrived too late, condemning himself to a living death on a dying planet?

These questions lingered, much like the flavors of a rich stew simmering on the stovetop, as I pondered my uncle's fate on that sultry August evening. Old Ben, my trusted body servant, interrupted my reverie with a telegram. I tore it open, my heart racing as I read the words: 'Meet me tomorrow at the hotel Raleigh in Richmond.' My uncle's name was signed at the bottom, a name that sent shivers down my spine.

The next morning, I boarded the first train to Richmond, my excitement building with every passing mile. Two hours later, I was ushered into a room where my uncle awaited me, his familiar smile lighting up his face like a warm fire on a winter's night.
