As I departed Bretton, little did I anticipate that it would be the last time I'd set foot within those tranquil streets. Six months had passed since Paulina's departure, and I'd grown accustomed to the idea of a prolonged absence. Upon returning home, I was met with the warm embrace of my family, and for a brief moment, I allowed myself to revel in the comfort of their presence. Yet, the tranquility was short-lived, for I was soon beset by memories of the tempestuous past – a period that I had sought to suppress, yet couldn't shake.

The recollection of that fateful voyage lingered, a constant reminder of the fragility of life. The howling winds, the icy grip of the waves, and the desperate struggle for survival still haunted me. Though I'd tried to keep the experience at bay, it often intruded upon my slumber, leaving me gasping for air as if the brine-stung waves still crashed against my throat. The memory of that protracted storm, which had ravaged our vessel for days on end, remained etched in my mind like an open wound.

I recalled the desolate days and sleepless nights, when the sky was shrouded in an impenetrable veil, and our efforts to save the ship seemed futile. The thought of the crew's demise still weighed heavily upon me, and I couldn't help but wonder if I, too, had narrowly escaped the abyss. Though I'd never spoken of these trials to anyone, the burden of secrecy only served to intensify the pain.

In the intervening years, the world had moved on, and Bretton had become a distant memory. Mrs. Bretton, once a beacon of warmth and kindness, had vanished from my life, her path diverging due to the unforeseen consequences of a failed investment. Her son, Graham, had chosen a profession, and the family had relocated to London, leaving behind a void that could never be filled. I was now entirely reliant on myself, with no one to turn to for support.
