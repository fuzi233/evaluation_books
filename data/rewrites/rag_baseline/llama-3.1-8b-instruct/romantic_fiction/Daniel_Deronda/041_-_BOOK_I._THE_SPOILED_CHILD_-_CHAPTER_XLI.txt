The evening's events still lingered, a palpable presence that refused to be shaken off, like the ghostly imprint of a hand on a cold windowpane.

In the mirror of his mind, Deronda beheld a young man torn asunder by conflicting desires – to surrender to the fervent certainties of Mordecai, to be swept up in the tide of the other's passion, and yet to remain steadfast in his own skeptical reserve. He recalled the words of Aristotle, 'It is a part of probability that many improbable things will happen,' and wondered if this, too, was not a truth applicable to his own life. Had he not, in his quiet, well-appointed world, felt the stirrings of a deeper longing, a yearning for connection, for a sense of purpose that eluded him in the sterile rituals of his daily routine?

As he pondered, the outlines of Mordecai's face rose before him, a haunting visage etched with the lines of suffering and the fire of conviction. Deronda's thoughts recoiled from the notion that he was somehow responsible for the other's fervor, that his presence had awakened a dormant spark within the consumptive Jew. And yet, as he delved deeper into the labyrinth of his emotions, he began to see the threads of connection that bound them – the fragile, gossamer strands that linked the isolated, the marginalized, and the fanatical.

He thought of the countless others like Mordecai, scattered throughout the city, each with their own peculiar vision, their own maddening certainty. The mathematician, the metaphysician, the prophet – all seeking validation, all craving recognition. And in their midst, Mordecai stood apart, a poet of the human condition, his words dripping with a pathos that was both heart-wrenching and strangely beautiful.
