My failed attempt to win my grandson over to the truth of the Sphere’s world did not make me abandon my mission, but it taught me the glib catchphrase “Upward, not Northward” would never be enough to shift the minds of my fellow Flatlanders. I would need a full, structured demonstration of the entire idea, laid out clearly enough for even the most skeptical mind to follow. To that end, I hid myself away for months to write a treatise, carefully dancing around the kingdom’s strict Anti-Dimension Laws to avoid immediate seizure. Rather than naming the physical third dimension outright, I framed the concept as a hypothetical “Thoughtland”: a realm beyond our flat 2D plane where any being could peer down to see every hidden vault, every sealed organ, every secret locked inside every shape in our world. I wrote of a form that could exist there, bounded by six perfect Squares, ending in eight sharp terminal points — a construction no Flatlander could ever render with our limited tools.

The work was hampered by a fundamental flaw, though: in all of Flatland, our “tablets” are nothing more than straight lines, our “diagrams” only distinguishable by shifts in brightness and the size of edge marks along that single linear surface. There was no way to draw, no way to visually communicate a 3D form. When I finished my book, *Through Flatland to Thoughtland*, I already suspected almost no reader would ever grasp what I was trying to show them.

A heavy cloud hung over my life from that moment on. No pleasure tasted the same. Every sunset, every polite social gathering, every quiet walk along our hexagonal city streets made me burn to speak what I’d seen: every 2D shape I looked at, I could not stop mentally picturing how it would look tilted into that hidden, third axis, and I could barely stop myself from muttering those comparisons out loud. I neglected my clients, abandoned my surveying work, and spent every spare hour trying to re-conjure the vision of Spaceland in my memory. Eleven months after the Sphere first carried me away to that luminous world, I closed my eyes and tried to summon the exact shape of a Cube — and I could not do it. I eventually pulled up a rough approximation of the form, but I never have been, and never will be, entirely certain it matches the real thing I saw that day. That doubt sank me deeper into melancholy. I would have gladly laid down my life if it could make even one soul truly believe, but if my own blood grandson would not listen, how could I ever sway the wisest, highest-ranked Circle leaders of our land?

Yet I could not keep the words locked in. I grew bolder and bolder, slipping dangerous phrases into even the most formal of upper-class gatherings. At public debates where officials discussed locking up “lunatics who claim they can see inside the bodies of their neighbors”, I quoted the ancient wise Circle who once noted that all prophets and inspired seers are called mad by the masses too narrow to understand their visions. I dropped references to “the all-seeing eye that pierces the inner walls of every shape”, and “the land that lies above our plane”. Once, I even let the forbidden terms slip out loud: “the Third Dimension” and “the Fourth Dimension”, clear enough for half the room to hear.

The final, fatal indiscretion happened at a meeting of our Local Speculative Society, held in the Prefect’s own palace. A stuffy, small-minded intellectual had just finished reading a three-hour long, over-researched paper “proving” that Providence had deliberately limited all reality to two dimensions, and reserved the power of omnividence exclusively for the Supreme Being, never to be granted to any mortal. The fire in my chest burst through all my caution. At first I pretended I was just recounting a fictional fable, but my enthusiasm quickly overthrew all disguise. I laid out every single fact of my story: the Sphere’s midnight visit, our voyage up into Space, the way the entire sprawling city of Flatland stretched out below me with every house, every body, every private secret visible at a single glance, every word the Sphere had spoken to me, my safe return home. I exhorted every soul in the hall to lay down their prejudices, follow the logic of analogy, and accept that the Third Dimension was real.

I was arrested on the spot.

The next morning, I was dragged to the Council chamber — the exact same spot where the Sphere had manifested before me months earlier. The first guard detail that arrived were all 55-degree angles: high-ranking, well-trained, irreplaceable officers. But the Council President waved them off, and had them replaced with low-ranking 2 and 3 degree policemen: clumsy, disposable, easily erased without loss to the state. I knew exactly what that meant. They planned to execute me, wipe my dangerous story from existence entirely, and did not want to waste their most valuable officers in the sealed chamber where my testimony would be contained and erased.

When I finished speaking my entire testimony, the President noted a handful of the young junior Circles at the back of the room were frowning, clearly moved by my obvious earnestness. He asked me only two questions:
1. Could I point to the direction I meant when I said the words “Upward, not Northward”, no tricks, no vague turns of phrase?
2. Could I describe or draw this thing I called a Cube, by any means other than listing imaginary sides and angles?

I told him I had no more proofs to offer, that I would put my faith in the Truth, which would surely prevail in the end. He smiled, and said he fully concurred with that sentiment. He sentenced me to perpetual life imprisonment. If the Truth was really on my side, he told me, the Truth would find a way to free me eventually, and see that my message spread. I would not be tortured, I would not be starved, and as long as I behaved myself, I would be permitted regular weekly visits from my brother, who had been confined in this same prison for unrelated minor sedition not long before.

Seven years have passed, and I am still a prisoner. The only visitor I ever get is my brother: the kindest of Squares, just, sensible, cheerful, brimming with fraternal affection. Every week when he comes to my cell, I retell the full story of the Sphere’s appearance, walk him through every sight I saw in Spaceland, and lay out all the arguments from analogy for the existence of the third dimension, step by step. But I hang my head in shame to confess: even he does not believe me. He was there in the Council chamber the day the Sphere manifested. He saw the glowing, shifting expanding and shrinking circles move across the room. He heard every explanation the Sphere gave to the assembled Circles. And still he laughs gently, calls me a little touched by old madness, swears there can be no such thing as a Sphere, no such thing as a third dimension.

I have no converts. For all I can tell, that glorious vision of Spaceland was gifted to me for nothing. Prometheus was chained to a rock for stealing fire from the heavens to give to mortals. I, poor Flatland Prometheus, sit locked in this tiny stone cell, with nothing to give my people, not even a single spark of that truth I once held in my hands. But I keep writing these memoirs on my prison line-tablet, in the faint, impossible hope that somehow, some way, these words will slip past the boundaries of our 2D plane, find their way to some mind in Some Dimension, and stir up a race of rebels who refuse to let their perceived reality be the only limit of what they can imagine.

That is the hope that carries me through my brighter days. But the dark days come, too. Some nights I wake up sweating, and I can no longer be sure the Cube I saw once was ever real. The phrase “Upward, not Northward” haunts me like a grinning, soul-devouring Sphinx, that unanswerable riddle I can never explain to anyone else. There are whole weeks when Cubes and Spheres feel no more substantial than the fairy tales of the one-dimensional Lineland, or the empty non-existence of the zero-dimensional Pointland. Sometimes even the stone walls of my cell, the line-tablets I write on, every supposed solid reality of Flatland itself, feels like nothing more than the baseless fabric of a madman’s dream, dissolving into thin air.
