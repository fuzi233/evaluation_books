The dust of the veldt clung to my boots like a second skin, a gritty reminder of the miles we had already traversed before the true journey began. It was here, amidst the sprawling, sun-baked plains of Southern Africa, that my path first crossed with Sir Henry Curtis. He was a man of considerable standing, a figure whose reputation preceded him like a shadow, yet he sought me out not for fame, nor for fortune alone, but because he needed a guide who understood the land better than any map could show.

Together, we pieced together the fragments of a legend older than the empire itself: the story of King Solomon's Mines. It was a tale whispered around campfires and dismissed by scholars alike—a myth of subterranean halls filled with untold riches, guarded by tribesmen who lived outside the bounds of conventional time. Yet, where others saw madness, I saw a pattern, a geography waiting to be mapped.

To navigate this unknown realm, we were joined by Umbopa, a warrior of the Matabele. His eyes held the sharp intelligence of a hunter, and though his people were fierce, his loyalty proved absolute once he entered our service. Our initial trials came swiftly; we engaged in a harrowing elephant hunt, testing our resolve against beasts of immense power, and then pressed onward into the vast, empty desert. There, the silence was deafening, broken only by the crackle of dry grass and the occasional cry of a bird. The thirst that followed was a physical torment, driving us to shout "Water!" until our voices were raw, pushing us toward a destination known only in stories: Solomon's Road.

As we ventured deeper into Kukuanaland, the landscape shifted, becoming stranger and more hostile. We encountered Twala, the King of the Matabele, a ruler whose ambition mirrored the ancient greed of the legends we pursued. What followed was a descent into chaos, marked by a witch-hunt that revealed the darkest undercurrents of tribal superstition. Trust eroded quickly as fear took hold; we gave signs of weakness, stood before an impending battle, and watched as the attack tore through our lines.

In the heat of combat, the Grey regiment made a desperate stand, holding back the tide long enough for some to escape, but the cost was high. Illness swept through the survivors, striking down Goodfalls, and soon we found ourselves at a place of death, surrounded by enemies and dwindling hope. Yet, within the depths of the earth, where the air grew cool and the walls shimmered with lost glories, lay the Treasure Chamber of Solomon. As the reality of our situation settled in, we abandoned hope for survival, clinging instead to the singular goal of securing what remained. In the end, Ignosi offered a final farewell, marking the close of an era for us, leaving behind a legacy etched in blood and gold. And then, finally, we found it—the proof that dreams, when backed by courage and endurance, could become reality.
