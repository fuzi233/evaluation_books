As I arose the following morning, I felt invigorated by a renewed sense of purpose and mental clarity. The lingering fatigue of the previous evening had dissipated, leaving my thoughts sharp and my will unencumbered. As I completed my morning ablutions, a knock at the door broke the silence, and I called out, expecting the chambermaid. Instead, a rugged individual entered, his features etched by the harsh lines of a life spent outdoors.

"Gif me your keys, Meess," he growled, his tone a stark contrast to the politeness typically associated with the inn's staff. I hesitated, sensing a subtle nuance beneath his gruff demeanor. "Why?" I asked, attempting to gauge the motivations behind his request. He replied curtly, snatching the keys from my hand, "All right! Haf your tronc soon." It soon became apparent that he was a customs official, tasked with collecting payment from travelers.

The realization alleviated my concern, and I proceeded to make my way downstairs, taking in the opulent surroundings of the inn. What struck me most was the juxtaposition between the grandeur of the common areas and the modesty of my assigned quarters. This disparity led me to ponder the economic realities governing the allocation of resources within the establishment. The inn's staff, it seemed, possessed an uncanny ability to assess the social standing and financial means of their guests, assigning accommodations accordingly. This pecuniarity sparked a train of thought, as I wondered about the underlying mechanisms driving such behavior.

As I navigated the spacious halls, I found myself drawn to the coffee-room, a space bathed in natural light pouring in through the skylights above. Despite my initial trepidation, I took a seat at a small table, where a waiter soon presented me with a meal. My unease lingered, however, as I struggled to shake off the feeling of isolation. The presence of numerous male patrons only served to exacerbate my discomfort. Yet, to my surprise, none seemed to regard my actions as unusual, attributing my behavior to the perceived eccentricities of a foreigner.

Following breakfast, I was faced with the daunting task of deciding my next course of action. An inner voice, likely prompted by a chance remark made by Miss Fanshawe, urged me to seek out Madame Beck in Villette, a town located some forty miles distant.
