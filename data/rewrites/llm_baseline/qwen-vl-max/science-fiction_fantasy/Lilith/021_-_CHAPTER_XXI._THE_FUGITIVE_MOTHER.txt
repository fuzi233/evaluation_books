The moon dimmed beneath a creeping veil of cloud, and from the sudden shadow emerged a figure—pale as bone, clutching a child to her chest. She ran with hunched shoulders, her movements frantic, every step trembling with fear. I moved along the same path, but she did not see me. Her breath came in ragged gasps; her eyes were wide with dread. "She’s being hunted," I whispered. "Something terrible is after her."

To chase would only terrify her further. Instead, I stepped into the trail ahead, blocking the unseen pursuer’s path.

For a moment, I stood still, watching her vanish into the mist. Then—behind me—a swift, silent rush. Before I could turn, something leapt over my head, striking my forehead with a sharp blow that sent me crashing to the ground. I rose instantly, blood trickling down my temple, but all I saw was a flash of white disappearing into the dark.

I gave pursuit, though my vision blurred with pain. But only a few steps brought a cry—raw, gut-wrenching—that tore through the night like a blade. My pace quickened. If it was too late… then nothing I could do would matter.

Soon, through the fog-softened moonlight, I saw a low, shuddering shape approaching. At first, I thought it another predator—crawling forward in broken leaps, dragging itself with unnatural effort. It moved on three legs, its left forepaw lifted high, trembling. Its skin gleamed white, dotted with dark oval marks. A faint rushing sound followed it—like water falling onto grass.

As it passed, I saw the source: blood streamed from the raised paw, trailing behind in a thin, red river that soaked into the earth beside me.

“It’s wounded,” I murmured. “Someone else must have struck it.”

Yet, at the sight of its suffering, pity gripped me. Even if I had held an axe, I could not have raised it.

It limped out of sight, bleeding still. “If it keeps losing blood,” I thought, “it won’t last long.”

I pressed on. Perhaps I could still help the woman. Maybe I’d find the one who had saved her.

A little farther, I found her—sitting on the grass, cradling the child. The baby slept, untouched.

“Can I help?” I asked.

She jumped, startled, reaching for her feet as if to flee.

I dropped to the ground. “Don’t be afraid. I was chasing the beast. But you’ve already beaten it—it passed me moments ago, bleeding so badly it can’t live long.”

Her voice trembled. “There’s no hope for that. Do you know whose beast she is?”

I hesitated. Strange thoughts stirred—whispers I’d heard, rumors of creatures bound to power beyond nature—but I said only, “I don’t know. Who is she?”

“The princess’s leopardess,” she replied. “And I killed her.”

“You? How?”

“I pounded her foot with a stone. As hard as I could. Did you not hear her scream?”

“I did. But I thought it was you.”

“No,” she said softly. “It was the leopardess. My voice was gone. When I saw her jaws near my baby’s neck, I grabbed a rock and crushed her paw.”

I stared. “You’re brave.”

She looked up, her face pale in the dim light. “I had no choice. That beast… she wouldn’t stop until she took both of us.”

“Tell me about her,” I said. “I’m new here.”

“If you’re going to Bulika,” she warned, “you’ll learn soon enough. But I will never return.”

“I am going to Bulika,” I said. “To see the princess.”

“Then beware. You’d better not go. But perhaps…” She paused. “The princess is kind—she says she is. But she has secrets.”

A rustle. The clouds thickened, swallowing the moon. I could barely make her out now.

“You’re not a threat,” I said. “What oath would you want?”

“She’s not of Bulika,” she whispered. “I can tell by your voice. And I’m not either. We trust no one there. They don’t believe in mercy. But I like your voice. And my baby sleeps. No mark. She’s safe.”

She touched the child’s cheek, her fingers shaking. “They say the princess has two white leopards. I’ve seen only one—the spotted one. Everyone knows her. If the princess hears of a newborn, she sends her. The leopard drinks the infant’s blood. Then the child dies… or grows up hollow, broken. I stayed because she was away. I thought I could wait. But she must have taken the beast with her—and returned just as I fled.”

She leaned forward. “I heard the SNIFF-SNUFF behind me. And I ran. Oh, how I ran!”

But the child lived. Unharmed.

“Where are you going?” I asked.

“To where no one ever speaks,” she said.

“And why does the princess do this?”

“She fears a prophecy,” the woman whispered. “That a child will kill her. So she refuses marriage. She sends witches to poison women’s dreams, to give them bitter herbs that prevent birth. Some say she trades with the Shadows to end the race. At night, we hear the beast prowling. She senses where babies are born—lies at doors, waiting. There are words that repel her… sometimes. But not always.”

She rose abruptly. “We must go. The beast may have reached the palace. She might send the other.”

“Let me carry your child,” I offered.

She didn’t answer. When I reached for the infant, she pulled her closer, shielding her with her body.

“I still don’t understand,” I said, walking beside her. “How could such a creature bleed so much?”

“Don’t go to the palace,” she warned. “At night, the walls whisper. Sounds like the dead trying to scream—but their mouths are sealed.”

She stopped. Turned. “Farewell.”

No more words. No glance back. Her footsteps faded into the grass, swallowed by the dark.

I stood alone, listening to the silence, wondering what waited in Bulika—and what fate awaited the child who had escaped the beast’s fangs.
