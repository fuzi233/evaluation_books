The capital of Atlantis greeted me with a spectacle of both splendor and decay, a city transformed under Phorenice’s rule. My return was met with hollow ceremony, and the feasting hall had emptied swiftly after the grim display of Tarca’s affliction. The Empress’s gaze lingered on me as the others recoiled, her words a mix of challenge and amusement.

*"You may be an honest man, Deucalion,"* she said, *"but you are a monstrous cold one. I wonder when you will thaw?"*

Her smile was a blade. I did not answer.

When she departed, I did not linger. The night air outside the pyramid was thick with unrest. The streets, once orderly, now teemed with desperation. The grandeur of new palaces cast long shadows over the starving and the dispossessed. The city’s spine had been broken and reforged—not for its people, but for the whims of its ruler.

I marched toward the walls with an escort of guards, their presence a necessity in these lawless streets. The capital was under siege, not just by rebels beyond its gates, but by the rot within.

At the great gate, laughter and roars echoed from the circus between the walls—a place designed for defense, now turned into an arena of cruelty. The warder hesitated before admitting me, his eyes flickering toward the spectacle beyond.

*"There is baiting underway, my lord,"* he warned.

I stepped into the gallery overlooking the pit. Moonlight spilled over the blood-slick stones. Two cave-tigers, monstrous and sleek, prowled on chains, their massive paws crushing the remnants of their victims. A fresh prisoner was thrust into the arena—a woman, bare-limbed and weaponless save for a sharpened bone.

She did not cower.

When the first tiger lunged, she struck like a viper, driving her makeshift weapon into its flesh. The beast roared, recoiling, and she seized her chance. With a sprint that defied fear, she crossed the pit in a desperate arc, leaping for the gallery where I stood. Her fingers clawed at the sill, slick with sweat and blood.

I caught her wrist before she could slip.

She did not beg. Instead, she twisted, driving the bone toward my throat with a snarl. I wrested it from her grip, pinning her until her fury stilled to ragged breaths.

*"That was an ungrateful blow,"* I said. *"But for my hand, you’d be dead."*

*"I’ll kill someone before I’m killed,"* she spat.

I called for the warder. *"Shorten their chains. Then help her down."*

As the tigers were dragged back, a figure stormed into the pit—a man armored and furious.

*"Who dares steal my sport?"* he bellowed.

The woman tensed beside me, but I stepped forward, shielding her.

This was Atlantis now: a city where mercy was weakness, and survival was bought with blood. And I would learn soon enough whether its salvation lay in the hands of its Empress—or its rebels.
