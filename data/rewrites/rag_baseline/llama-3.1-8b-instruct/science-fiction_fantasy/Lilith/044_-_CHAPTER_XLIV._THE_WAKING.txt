THE WAKING

As I slowly regained consciousness, I felt the weight of the house of death lift from my shoulders. It was as if I had shed a cloak heavy with the scent of damp earth and decay. I opened my eyes, and though the air was dark, I knew exactly where I was – in the sanctuary of the afterlife. Every moment I'd spent trapped within its walls had been a dream, and now I was finally awake. A wave of exhilaration washed over me, and I smiled, feeling my heart leap with joy.

Lona stood beside me, her presence a beacon of warmth in the darkness. I'd never truly lost her – only the sight of her. My relief was palpable, and I wrapped my arms around her, drinking in the radiance that emanated from her being. As I held her close, I felt the life force coursing through my veins, revitalized and renewed.

"I woke first," she whispered, her voice barely audible above a whisper.

"You did," I replied, my voice filled with emotion. "And woke me."

Her eyes sparkled with a knowing glint. "I only looked at you and waited."

The soft glow of a candle illuminated our faces, and soon, Adam and Eve joined us, their gentle smiles a testament to the countless awakenings they'd witnessed. The Mother's voice carried on the breeze, her words a soothing balm to my parched soul. "I hope you've had a pleasant darkness," she said.

I shook my head, still reeling from the experience. "Not very, but the waking from it is heavenly."

Adam's words echoed her sentiment. "It is but begun. You are hardly yet awake."

His arm wrapped around Lona, drawing her close, while he placed a gentle hand on my shoulder. "He is at least clothed upon with Death, which is the radiant garment of Life."

I felt a shiver run down my spine as I gazed at Lona, her beauty transcending the mortal realm. Adam's words hung in the air, a promise of the boundless potential that lay ahead.

"We shall meet you two again before long," he said, his eyes crinkling at the corners.

I turned to him, my mind racing with questions. "Will we have to die again?"
