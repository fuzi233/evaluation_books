The scent of fresh-baked scones wafted through the air as I entered the cozy sitting room where my lady sat, surrounded by the comforting familiarity of her favorite armchair and the warm glow of the fireplace. Her eyes widened slightly as I mentioned that Sergeant Cuff, the London detective, had requested an audience with her.

"Must I see him?" she asked, a hint of annoyance creeping into her voice. I couldn't help but feel a sense of curiosity; I had always known my lady to be composed and collected, but there was something about Sergeant Cuff that seemed to rattle her. "Can't you represent me, Gabriel?" she asked, her voice tinged with a subtle vulnerability.

I hesitated, unsure of how to respond. As I watched, my lady's expression softened, and she continued, "I'm afraid my nerves are a bit frayed. There's something about that detective that puts me on edge – I don't know why, but I have a feeling he's going to bring trouble and chaos into our lives." I nodded sympathetically, trying to reassure her, but she shook her head. "Foolish, I know, but I just can't shake the feeling."

I excused myself, leaving her to collect herself. As I stepped out into the garden, I spotted Mr. Franklin and Mr. Godfrey deep in conversation, their voices hushed and serious. I made my way to the kitchen, where the aroma of freshly baked pastries and roasting meats filled the air. Mrs. Michelson, our cook, was busily preparing lunch, her hands moving deftly as she expertly chopped herbs and vegetables.

Upon returning to the sitting room, I found Sergeant Cuff waiting patiently. My lady's eyes flickered towards him, and for a moment, I thought I saw a flash of unease. But she quickly regained her composure, asking if he had any objections to my presence. "I'm your trusted adviser, after all," she explained, her voice steady. "And in matters concerning the household, I value your counsel above all others." The Sergeant smiled graciously, accepting her invitation to sit.
