To those who might wonder after the truth of the places I describe, know this: St. Bertrand de Comminges and Viborg are real enough—stones and soil, history and hauntings. As for Felixstowe, let it linger in your mind when you read *“Oh, Whistle, and I’ll Come to You.”*

But do not trouble yourself with the scraps of false learning I scatter like grave dust across these pages. Every parchment cited, every tome invoked, is a phantom of my own making. There was never any such book as the one I name in *“The Treasure of Abbot Thomas.”* No scholar’s hand ever turned its pages, no library ever held its weight.

*“Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book”* first saw light in 1894, printed soon after in the *National Review.* *“Lost Hearts”* followed in the *Pall Mall Magazine.* The rest—those tales whispered by firelight to friends at King’s College—are harder to place. I remember only this: *“Number 13”* was penned in 1899, while *“The Treasure of Abbot Thomas”* took shape in the long, shadowed summer of 1904.

—M. R. James
