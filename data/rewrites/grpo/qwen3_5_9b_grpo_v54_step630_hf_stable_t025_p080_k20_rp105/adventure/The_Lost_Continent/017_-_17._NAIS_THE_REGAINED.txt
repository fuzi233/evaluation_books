The view from the crest of the Sacred Mountain stretched out before us like a map of destiny. Below, the city sprawled in the shadow of the harbor and the great estuary, while the distant hills smoked and fumed in their eternal haze. Dominating the center stood the great stone circle of our Lord the Sun, grim and bare, and rising behind it was the royal pyramid, its golden sides drinking in the morning light. In the square before that towering monument lay the granite throne, a mole's hill in the distance, where my love had slept for nine long years.

Old Zaemon followed my gaze, his expression heavy. "Ay," he sighed, "I know where your heart lies. Deucalion, when you first landed from Yucatan, was a man of iron. The King we have chosen—our best option—is a man of weakness."

"It can be forged into strength," I replied, my voice steady despite the ache in my chest. "Give me Nais, living and warm to fight for, and I shall be a warrior far superior to the cold viceroy you describe."

"I have already pledged her to you," Zaemon said, his tone shifting to something more conspiratorial. "But this rescue will cost you dearly in the stability of your new kingdom. Perhaps, in the chaos, we might also rid you of Phorenice and her brood. Yet, I doubt it. She is too cunning. Once we begin our game, she will likely deduce our intent and escape before harm can strike. The High Gods, who send these trials to refine us, have granted her a dangerous knowledge: how to trigger these earth tremors."

"I have seen her dance with them," I admitted. "But tell me your plan."

"It shall be revealed in due time. For now, you must sleep. Tonight, you descend into the city, retrieve your woman when she is freed, and bring her back to this mountain. You will need the strength of a titan for that task." He stepped back, scanning me from head to toe. "You no longer resemble the tidy, clean-chinned Deucalion. Your appearance is your armor tonight. You are hairy, shaggy, naked, and dirty—a wild savage fresh from Europe. Be careful that no fine citizen mistakes your thaws for servitude."

"I pity the fool who would enslave me," I retorted.

Zaemon laughed, a rare sound that quickly faded into gravity. "Indeed, I do too. Well, rest now, my King. Leave me to prepare in the Ark of Mysteries with another of the Three. Handling the powers we must unleash tonight is no light burden, and the danger is as great for me as for you. If we do not meet again beneath the stars to give account to the Gods, fare you well, Deucalion."

I slept that day with the discipline of a soldier, banishing all harassing thoughts. When the darkness fully descended, I roused myself and gathered the attendants for the rope. The moon was absent, and the air was thick with oppressive blackness. As they lowered me, I vanished from sight.

I reached the tumbled rocks at the cliff's base and moved with extreme caution. Phorenice's soldiers huddled around their campfires, their unease palpable, sensing the coming evil. I had no desire to alert them further. Creeping through the deepest shadows, I arrived at the passage entrance I had used before to breach the city walls.

The lamp was lit, and I moved swiftly through the windings. The air here was more noxious than before, heavy with vapors, signaling that Zaemon had already begun to stir the internal forces destined to convulse the city. However, finding an exit proved difficult. Not because of people blocking the way, but because the masonry had been altered. In ancient times, the Priest's Clan controlled architecture, ensuring secret passages remained intact. Now, the Priests were powerless, and architects, through sheer incompetence, had sealed many of these vital outlets.

Forced to navigate the tunnels wherever they led rather than where I wished, I emerged on the far side of the city, near where the outer wall meets the harbor. I exited unseen, meticulously erasing all traces of my passage. Beneath my feet, the earth felt less like solid ground and more like the deck of a ship in rough water. Though no tremor had yet shaken the sleeping populace, I could feel the solid deadness of the world dissolving; devastation was moments away.

*Gods!* Would I arrive too late to witness the unburial of my love? Would she be exposed to the public gaze, vulnerable to the rude, cruel hands of a terrified crowd? My mind conjured images of her helpless form being thrust upon by strangers, and my insides curdled at the thought.

I ran madly down the narrow, winding streets, driven solely by the need to reach the square before the royal pyramid. With exquisite cruelty, I had forced her alive into her tomb beneath the granite throne; if my strength and speed could save her, I would not fail.

Few disturbed my furious hurry. Here and there, a wretch in the gutter cried, "A thief! Throw a share or I pursue!" But none dared follow. As the earth's swing intensified, causing buildings to groan and shower dust, people fled in panic. Yet, they ignored the ragged, shaggy savage who ran past, flinging mud from his naked feet.

I reached the great square just in time. It was filling with a fleeing mob, choked with darkness, noise, and dust. Beneath us, the ground rippled like a sea, the undulations growing terrifyingly intense. Houses crashed unseen in the gloom, adding to the tumult. The great pyramid, however, was built to withstand such shocks. Its stones were dovetailed with marvellous cleverness and clamped by ponderous metal tongues. It was said one could dig half its foundations away, and it would still stand four-square under heaven.

Flickering torches revealed the great stone doors of the pyramid open, with frightened inmates scurrying out only to vanish into the gloom. But I cared little for the pyramid's fate, nor whether Phorenice was trapped or free. I crouched by the granite throne in the center of the square, hearing its stones grate together like broken bone as it rocked to the earth-waves.

In that night of dust and darkness, seeing was nearly impossible, yet the Gods, in requital for my long-suffering love, seemed to grant me special sight. I watched the great carved capstone of the throne shift slightly, then again, then again—a tiny jerk for each earth-pulse, but an appreciable movement. Moreover, it always moved to one side.

There was method in Zaemon's desperate work, a detail I had overlooked in my blind panic. I climbed the steps on the side where the capstone was sliding and clung there, aflame with expectation.

The earth-swing grew more violent, the din of falling houses and human screams rising into the night. Dust thickened until one coughed and strangled, blotting out the rare stars. Clinging to an angle of the granite throne, I could barely maintain my footing against the violence.

Yet the huge capstone, carved with the snake and the outstretched hand, still held my love locked in her living tomb. I wanted to bite the cold granite in my impotence. The people thronging the square were mad with terror, their prayers turning to the old Gods they had long spurned. Louder grew the crash of falling masonry. From the royal pyramid itself came sounds of grinding stones and cracking metal, confirming that even its majestic strength was breaking.

Then, a flash of lightning revealed a shock that stole the breath from my lungs: Phorenice stood safe in the square, not far from me.

She stood on a great flagstone, swaying her supple body to the shocks. Her face was calm, her loveliness untouched by the years. She brushed away settling dust from her short red curls. There was no fear on her face, only weariness, contempt, and a tinge of amusement. It took more than the crumbling of her royal pyramid to impress Phorenice with the power of her enemies.

*Gods!* How her cool indifference maddened me. I could have strangled her if she had been within reach. But she stood swaying easily, like a sailor on a ship's deck. Beside her, overcome with nausea, crouched Ylga, her fan-girl and twin sister to Nais. I owed Ylga an ancient kindness, but I had no leisure for her then. With each shock, the capstone jarred farther from its bed, bringing my love closer.

Panic-stricken, a knot of people thought the granite throne an oasis of safety and flocked toward it, dragging themselves up the steep steps on hands and knees. But I was in no mood to let the place be profaned. I beat at them with my hands, tearing them away and hurling them back down the steps. They asked what right I had to the place above them; I answered with blows and gnashing teeth. I cared not what they thought of me, only that they be gone. They wailed, calling me a devil of the night, for they had no spirit left to defend themselves.

Farther and farther the great stone slid out, its slowness driving me mad. A life's education left me in that moment; I possessed no stately patience. In my puny fury, I thrust at the great block with my shoulder and head, clawing at it until my muscles rose in great ropes and knots. The High Gods must have laughed at my helplessness. All was being ordered by the Three in Their good time. The work of the Gods may be slow, but it is done exceeding sure.

Finally, when the city was numb with terror, the threatened event occurred. The capstone slid out to its balance, and the next shock threw it with a roar and a clatter to the ground. Then, a strange tremor seized me.

After all the scheming and effort, what I had prayed for had come about. Yet, my insides sank at the thought of mounting the stone where I had stood before, to take my dear from the hollow where my hands had laid her. I knew Phorenice's vengefulness and her cleverness. Had she left Nais to lie in peace? Stolen her away to suffer indignities? Or ended her sleep with death, leaving a corpse as a grisly jest? I dared not guess. Never in my hard-fighting life have my emotions been so wrenched. Love for Nais had sapped my hardihood over a matter so privately hers.

However, the infernal uproar of the earth tremor began to slacken, as if Zaemon knew his work was done and wished to give the wretched city a breathing space. Taking my fortitude in hand, I clambered onto the flat of the stone. Lightning had ceased, leaving only darkness and stifling dust. If I were seen, shaggy and changed as I was, Phorenice would not be slow to guess my name and errand.

I confessed to myself that the idea of a fight was loathsome. I simply wanted my business done and to flee.

With shaking hands, I fumbled over the face of the stone, finding the clamps and bars of metal still in position where I had clenched them. Reverently, I let my fingers pass between them, feeling the curves of my love's body resting beneath. An exultation whirled within me. I did not know if she had been touched since I last left her, or if the drug would finally awaken her to warmth and sight. But dead or alive, she was there, and she was mine. I could have yelled aloud in my joy at her possession.

The earth still shook, and masonry roared into ruin. I clung to my place with one hand while unhasping the metal clamps that capped her prison with the other. Finally, I swung the upper half clear, leaving the pins holding her feet in place. I stooped and drew her soft body up onto the flat of the stone beside me, pressing my lips a hundred times to the face I could not see.

A mad thought took me, I believe, that the fierceness and heat of my kisses would bring her back to life. Indeed, I did but sorry credit to my training in calmness that night. But she lay in my arms cold and nerveless as a corpse. Gradually, my sober wits returned.

This was no place for either of us. Let the tremors cease, let daylight come, and let the panic subside, and all the great cost expended might be counted as waste. We would be seen, and it would not be long before someone identified Nais; then it would be easy to guess Deucalion under the beard and shaggy hair of the savage attending her. Tell of fright? By the Gods! I was scared as the veriest trembler who blundered amongst the dust-clouds when the thought came to me.

With ruin spread around, it was hopeless to think any secret galleries under the ground remained unbroken. But I had heard shouts from the frightened mob: "The city is accursed! If we stay here, it will fall on us. Let us get outside the walls where there are no buildings to bury us."

If they went, I could not see. But one gate lay nearest to the royal pyramid, and in their panic, they would not go farther than needed. I put the body of Nais over my shoulder, leaving my right arm free, and blundered off through the stifling darkness.

It was hard to find a direction, to walk over ground tossed like a frozen sea, and to keep footing as the earth heaved. I fell a score of times, but my dear burden received no bruise. Presently, I reached the skirts of the square and found a street I knew. The most venomous shaking was done; no more buildings fell, though enough lay sprawled to make walking a climb. Sweat rolled from me as I labored along.

There was no difficulty passing the gate. There was no gate. There was no wall. The Gods had driven their plough through it, and it lay flat. Proud Atlantis stood as defenseless as the open country.
