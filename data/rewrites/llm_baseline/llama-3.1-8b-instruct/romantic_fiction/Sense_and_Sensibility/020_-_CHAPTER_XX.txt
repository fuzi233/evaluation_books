As the sun cast its pale light through the misty windows of the grand estate, the Miss Dashwoods entered the drawing room, their footsteps echoing softly on the polished floorboards. At the same door, Mrs. Palmer burst in, radiating warmth and hospitality, her bright smile illuminating the space. She swept the room with her gaze, embracing each of the Dashwood sisters with affectionate enthusiasm.

"I'm so delighted to see you both!" she exclaimed, settling between Elinor and Marianne on the plush sofa. "I was worried you might not come, which would have been a terrible shame, given our short stay here. We leave tomorrow, you see, and the Westons will be visiting us next week. It was all so sudden – Mr. Palmer asked me to join him in Barton on short notice, and I only learned of it when the carriage arrived at our doorstep."

Mrs. Palmer's eyes sparkled as she continued, "But I'm thrilled to have had the chance to host you. I wish we could linger longer, but alas, duty calls. We'll meet again soon, I hope, perhaps in town?"

The conversation turned to plans for the future, and Mrs. Palmer's thoughts turned to the possibility of the Dashwood sisters joining her in London. "Not going to town?" she laughed, her voice like music. "I'd be utterly disappointed if you didn't! I've secured a charming house in Hanover Square, next door to ours. You simply must come – I'll be honored to chaperone you until I'm...indisposed, if Mrs. Dashwood doesn't mind venturing out."

Though they thanked her profusely, the Dashwoods politely declined, citing their mother's reservations about social engagements.

"Oh, my love," Mrs. Palmer appealed to her husband, who had just entered the room, "please, persuade the Miss Dashwoods to join us in town this winter!"

Her words fell on deaf ears, as Mr. Palmer merely bowed to the ladies before launching into a lament about the dreariness of the weather. "How wretched it all is!" he declared. "Rainy days like these make everything and everyone seem dull. One can hardly bear to be in the presence of acquaintances. And to think Sir John hasn't seen fit to install a billiards room in his home – how little he knows the meaning of comfort! Sir John is as dull as the weather itself."
