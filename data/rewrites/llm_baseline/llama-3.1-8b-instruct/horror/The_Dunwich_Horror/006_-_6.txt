In the sweltering summer of 1928, a sense of foreboding settled over the town of Arkham like a shroud. Dr. Armitage, a scholar of the occult, had been expecting the unthinkable, and it finally came to pass between Lammas and the equinox. He had heard whispers of Wilbur Whateley's frantic search for the forbidden tome, the _Necronomicon_, at the Widener Library in Cambridge. The librarians, aware of Armitage's warnings, had denied Whateley access to the book, but the young man's anxiety had grown more intense by the day.

As August dawned, the townspeople of Arkham began to sense that something was amiss. The night air was heavy with an otherworldly presence, and the stars seemed to twinkle with an eerie, malevolent light. It was on this night, in the small hours of the third, that Dr. Armitage was roused by the frenzied barking of the college watchdog. The animal's cries grew louder, punctuated by the anguished screams of an unseen entity. The sounds sent shivers down Armitage's spine, and he knew that something was terribly wrong.

Donning a pair of slippers and a robe, Armitage rushed across the lawn to the college buildings, his heart racing with anticipation. The campus was already abuzz with activity, and he saw several figures gathered near the library. The sound of a burglar-alarm still echoed through the night air, and the moonlight cast an eerie glow on the open window. Armitage's instincts told him that what was happening was not for mortal eyes to behold, so he pushed back the crowd with a firm hand and unlocked the vestibule door.

Among the onlookers were Professor Warren Rice and Dr. Francis Morgan, both of whom had heard Armitage's theories and warnings about Whateley's activities. Armitage motioned for them to follow him inside, and they entered the building with caution. The only sound that remained was the watchful whine of the dog, its ears perked up in alertness. Suddenly, a chorus of whippoorwills erupted from the shrubbery, their piping rising in a maddening rhythm that seemed to synchronize with the dying gasps of a man.
