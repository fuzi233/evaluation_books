Gerard stood frozen, the sudden news striking him with such force that his complexion flickered between pale fear and flushed anger. He ground his teeth, his spirit rising like a wild boar cornered by relentless hunters. He knew that if one pressed too closely upon a man of spirit, eventually that man would turn and charge, tearing through the very pack that sought to crush him. Yet, as he braced himself for battle, a crushing realization brought his face down again. "My axe lies in the Rhine," he whispered, his voice heavy with despair.

Denys and Prudence conferred quickly. Prudence urged caution, advising them to skirt the village entirely, while a gnawing hunger demanded they seek sustenance. In the end, hunger roared the loudest, and Prudence's logic proved most compelling. They resolved to cut across the open fields.

Stopping beside a towering haystack, they borrowed two bundles, using them as a makeshift bed in a dry ditch hidden from view, though stinging nettles pricked their skin. Taking turns venturing forth, they returned with turnips, munching sparingly throughout their flight until the sun dipped below the horizon.

Then, shivering violently, they crept out into the driving rain and encroaching darkness, emerging onto the road beyond the village. The night was a dismal abyss, black as pitch and whipped by a fierce wind. They were invisible, unheard, and unseen, moving like ghosts past their enemies, whom they almost forgot amidst the primal terrors of the storm. It felt as though they were groping and hacking their way through solid black marble. When the moon finally rose, casting its pale light over the landscape, they had traveled many leagues from Düsseldorf. Yet, they did not stop. Their weary feet carried them onward until they approached a massive structure looming in the gloom.

"Courage!" Denys cried, his voice cutting through the wind. "I believe I recognize this convent. Yes, it is so. We have entered the see of Juliers; Cologne holds no sway here."

In that instant, the walls of the sanctuary became their shield, and they stepped safely within.
