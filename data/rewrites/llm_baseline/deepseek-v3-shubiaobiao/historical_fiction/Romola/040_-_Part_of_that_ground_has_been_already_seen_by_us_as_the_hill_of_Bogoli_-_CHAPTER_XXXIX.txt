The pavilion glowed under the evening’s torchlight, its frescoed walls alive with flickering shadows. Tito’s sharp eyes quickly discerned the gathering’s true nature—beneath the veneer of conviviality, politics simmered. Good wine and rich dishes had long been trusted to loosen tongues and sharpen allegiances, and the Rucellai, among Florence’s most opulent families, knew well how to wield both.

Plato’s bust, salvaged from Lorenzo de’ Medici’s villa after his death, presided over the feast with silent irony. Once, it had overlooked gatherings of philosophers—Pico della Mirandola, Marsilio Ficino, Poliziano—men who had burned with ideas. Now, it watched as silver forks flashed and laughter rippled through the warm air, the weight of their legacy reduced to ornament.

Bernardo Rucellai, the host, welcomed Tito with marked favor, seating him between Lorenzo Tornabuoni and Giannozzo Pucci, both polished Medicean loyalists. The talk, at first, was light—jests about how Dante’s frugal Florentines would gape at such luxury, admiration for the craftsmanship of Pollaiuolo’s silver dish. Yet beneath the frivolity, tension hummed.

As the servants withdrew, the conversation sharpened. The Frate—Savonarola—loomed over them all, his shadow stretching even into this privileged enclave.

*"He loads the cannon,"* Rucellai remarked dryly, *"and the Frate lights the match."*

Tornabuoni leaned in. *"We must be prudent. The Medici will return—but until then, we play the Frate’s game. The alternative?"* He gestured with his cup. *"The Arrabbiati would tear us apart."*

Niccolò Ridolfi scoffed. *"All governments end the same—biting or groveling. But we keep our heads down, wait, and hold to our own."*

Rucellai’s voice was cool. *"I dissemble nothing. Yet for now, the popular party is our shield."*

The wine flowed; alliances murmured in corners. Tornabuoni and Pucci drew Tito aside.

*"You’re invaluable,"* Tornabuoni said, fingers drumming his ankle. *"Discreet, connected, yet unmarked. Play this well, and the future favors you."*

Pucci’s grip tightened on Tito’s shoulder. *"Win the Frate’s trust. Then, when the time comes—"*

A cardinal’s hat, Tornabuoni hinted. Power, unspoken but understood.

Tito smiled, mind already racing. *"Leave Spini to me,"* he said. *"I’ll know his plans before he does."*

Outside, in the cold dark, a figure circled—watching, waiting. But within, amid laughter and torchlight, none noticed the eyes fixed on Tito’s careless grace.

The game had begun.
