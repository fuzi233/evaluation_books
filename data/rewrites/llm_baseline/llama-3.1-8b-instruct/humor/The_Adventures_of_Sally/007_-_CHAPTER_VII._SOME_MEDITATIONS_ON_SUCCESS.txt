The news of the ban on the theatres potentially being lifted on Tuesday sent ripples of joy throughout the cast, with each person eagerly speculating on the possibilities of a swift return to the stage.

Teddy, resplendent in his bowler hat, regaled the others with tales of the lift-boy's insider information, while the cigar-stand girl at the Pontchartrain Hotel was quoted as saying that Toledo and Cleveland would reopen their doors the very next day. It was as if fate itself had finally relented, surrendering to the collective determination of the company.

Fillmore, once again the epitome of confidence, celebrated his newfound status by splurging on a new waistcoat and a cache of half-dollar cigars. His transformation was nothing short of remarkable – he seemed to have regained the weight he had lost during the lean times, and his very presence on the sidewalk outside the theatre was a testament to his renewed vitality. As he stood basking in the admiration of the passersby, the posters proclaiming his name – FILLMORE NICHOLAS PRESENTS – seemed to glow with an otherworldly light.
