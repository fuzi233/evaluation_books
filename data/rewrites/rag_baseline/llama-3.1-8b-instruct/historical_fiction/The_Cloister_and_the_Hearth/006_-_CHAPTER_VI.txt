As I stood before the cottage, the scent of baking bread wafted through the air, mingling with the fragrance of blooming wildflowers that lined the path. It was as if the very essence of home had beckoned me to this place. I had come to Sevenbergen out of morbid curiosity, seeking to unravel the tangled threads of a past that had left me feeling bereft. My heart, a stubborn and unyielding entity, had responded to my inquiry with a candid admission: I was here to confront the memory of Margaret, to prove to myself that her rejection had not shattered me, and that I remained whole despite the loss.

The thatched roof of Peter Brandt's cottage came into view, and I quickened my pace. A girl sat in the doorway, her slender fingers moving deftly as she worked a needle, while a sturdy figure stood behind her, leaning on a longbow and speaking in low tones. An inexplicable pang struck me at the sight of him, but the man's rugged features, etched by the lines of age and experience, seemed to hold a deep familiarity. I recognized him as an old soldier, one I had seen firing at the butts with impressive precision. A minute passed, and I stood before them, my footsteps echoing on the dirt path.

Margaret looked up, her eyes locking onto mine with a mixture of wariness and curiosity. She set aside her needlework, and a faint cry escaped her lips, followed by a flush that spread across her cheeks like the first blush of dawn. But these signs of emotion were swiftly suppressed, and she regarded me with a detachment that belied her earlier vulnerability.

"What brings you here, Master Gerard?" she asked, her voice tinged with a hint of wariness.

"I chanced upon the village and thought to pay my respects," I replied, trying to sound nonchalant despite the turmoil within me.

"My father will be here shortly," she said, her eyes never leaving mine.

"Then I shall wait," I said, attempting to appear casual, though my heart pounded in my chest like a drum.

As I stood there, I noticed a book lying open on the table – a worn leather-bound volume of the Vulgate. My fingers itched to touch its pages, to feel the raised letters and the weight of the parchment. But before I could reach for it, Margaret's hands came down, covering the page with a gentle firmness.
