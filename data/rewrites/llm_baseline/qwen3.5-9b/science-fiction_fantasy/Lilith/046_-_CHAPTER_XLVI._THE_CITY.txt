Lona and I, walking beneath the canopy, finally caught the echo of a tremendous shout drifting down from above. Within moments, the Little Ones began to descend from the high foliage, buzzing with news. They claimed that scaling the apex of a tree—taller than any other—they had spotted something strange on the flank of a lone mountain. This mountain, they insisted, climbed endlessly toward the heavens until the sky itself grew dense enough to cap it, knocking its summit clean off.

"It might be a city," they declared, "yet it bears no resemblance to Bulika."

I ascended to verify their tale. What met my gaze was a vast metropolis rising into the azure clouds, a place where mountain and sky, rock and dwelling, dissolved into indistinguishable unity. Clouds, peaks, and palaces merged in a chaotic symphony of fractured shadows and blinding light. Descending once more, I joined the Little Ones, and together we hurried forward, our pace quickening. Their spirits lifted further with every step; they led the way, laughing and never glancing back.

The river transformed before our eyes, growing increasingly magnificent until I realized I had never witnessed true water before. Nothing in this world merely *resembled* it; it *was* perfection. Soon, the city emerged clearly against the blue backdrop, nestled among the clouds. Yet, darker clouds swirled around a towering spire—or perhaps a sheer cliff face—that pierced the skyline near the mountain's crest. These clouds shifted in hues of gray, charcoal, and violet, writhing in confused, contradictory motions, tossing up a vaporous foam while swirling spots danced within them like miniature whirlpools.

Suddenly, a dazzling flash erupted, momentarily illuminating the Little Ones ahead. It was followed instantly by blinding darkness, yet through the gloom, their voices rang out, hushed with awe.

"Did you see?"
"I saw!"
"What did you see?"
"The most beautiful man!"
"I heard him speak!"
"I didn't. What did he say?"

A tiny, childish voice cut through—the voice of Luva.
"He said, 'All mine, little ones: come along!'"

I had witnessed the lightning but heard no words; Lona, however, perceived both sight and sound with the children. A second flash struck, and though my ears remained deaf, my eyes were suddenly opened. The great trembling light coalesced into faces of angels. They flared into visibility and then vanished. A third flash arrived, its substance and radiance distinctly human.

"My mother!" I cried out.
"I see many mothers!" Luva exclaimed.

Once more the cloud flashed, revealing a kaleidoscope of creatures: horses and elephants, lions and dogs—oh, such beasts! And such birds! Great birds whose single wings shimmered with every color of the sunset or rainbow; small birds whose feathers sparkled as if dusted with the precious stones of the entire earth; silvery cranes; scarlet flamingos; opal pigeons; peacocks resplendent in gold, green, and blue; jewel-toned hummingbirds; great-winged butterflies; and lithe, voluminous creeping things—all revealed in one celestial instant.

"I see that serpents grow into birds here, just as caterpillars once became butterflies!" Lona observed.
"I saw my white pony, the one that died when I was a child," I replied softly. "I needn't have mourned so deeply; I simply needed to wait."

There was no thunder, no clap, no rumble. Instead, a gentle rain began to fall, filling the air with a caressing coolness. We breathed deeply and took longer strides. The falling drops reflected the colors of all awakened earthly gems, and a magnificent rainbow arched over the city. The blue clouds thickened; the rain poured in torrents; the children exulted, running wild, making it difficult to keep them in view.

With a silent, radiant rush, the river surged onward, swelling to fill its smooth, soft, yielding channel. For instead of flowing over rock, shingle, or sand, it moved across lush grass carpeted with primroses, daisies, crocuses, narcissi, pimpernels, and anemones—a starry multitude, large and bright, glowing through the brilliant water. The river bore no turbidity from the rain, not even a hint of yellow or brown; the delicate mass shone with a pale, beryl-like gleam rising from its deep, dainty bed.

Drawing closer to the mountain, we saw the river originate directly from its peak, rushing in full volume through the city's main thoroughfare. It cascaded down to the gate via a staircase of deep, wide steps crafted from porphyry and serpentine stone, which extended all the way to the mountain's foot. Upon arrival, we found shallower steps on either bank leading up to the gate and along the ascending street.

Without pausing, the Little Ones sprinted straight up the stairs to the gate, which stood wide open. Outside, on the landing, sat the portress—a woman-angel of dark visage, resting her shadowed brow on her idle hand. The children rushed toward her, burying her in affectionate embraces. Before she could comprehend their presence, they had seized heaven by surprise and were already inside the city, still climbing the stairs beside the descending torrent.

A great angel, accompanied by a host of shining beings, came down to greet and receive them. Yet, the children merrily evaded everyone, continuing upward. Then, in a joyful dance, a group of woman-angels descended upon them, and in an instant, they were embraced and bound in heavenly arms. The radiant figures carried them away, and I lost sight of them forever.

"Aha!" proclaimed the mighty angel as he continued his descent to meet us, who were now nearly at the gate and close enough to hear his words. "This is excellent! These are soldiers ready to storm heaven itself! I hear reports of a horde of black bats on the frontiers; these will put an end to them swiftly!"

Noticing the horses and elephants scrambling up behind us, he added, "Escort those animals to the royal stables. Tend to them, then release them into the King's forest."

Turning to us, he offered a low bow with the sweetest smile. "Welcome home!"

Immediately, he turned and led the way higher. The scales of his armor flashed like fragments of lightning. No thought can adequately describe what I felt, thus welcomed by the officers of Heaven. Everything I desired, everything I did not yet know, was surely on its way to me!

We paused briefly at the gate, where the radiant river roared forth. I could not identify the origin of the stones forming the archway, but among them, I recognized the prototypes of every gem I had cherished on Earth—far more beautiful than their earthly counterparts, for these were living stones. In them, I perceived not merely the intent, but the creator; not just the idea, but the embodied artist, the active sender. Nothing in this realm was dead; nothing was merely an object; nothing existed without purpose or spirit.

We proceeded upward through the city and exited onto the upper level. There was no wall here, only a massive pile of broken rocks sloping upward like the moraine of an eternal glacier. Through gaps between the rocks, the river bubbled out. Perched atop these rocks, I could faintly discern what appeared to be three or four great steps of a staircase, vanishing into a cloud white as snow. Above the steps, using only my mind's eye, I envisioned a grand, ancient chair—the Throne of the Ancient of Days. From over, under, and between those steps issued the River of Life, fresh and newborn, ceaselessly and abundantly.

The great angel could guide us no further; those final rocks we must ascend alone. My heart pounding with hope and longing, I gripped Lona's hand tighter, and we began to climb. Soon, however, we released each other's hands to use our limbs fully in the laborious ascent of the colossal stones.

At last, we approached the cloud hanging down the steps like the hem of a garment. Passing through its fringed edge, we entered its deep folds. A hand, warm and strong, grasped mine and pulled me to a small door secured with a golden lock. The door swung open; the hand released mine and gently pushed me through.

I turned quickly, expecting to see the angel, but instead, I saw the page of a large book closing behind me. Standing alone, I found myself in my own library.
