As Calvin C. Bunner stepped into the room, a hint of ceremony accompanied him, evident in the slight hesitation before he removed an unlit cigar from his mouth. "At your service," he amended, his tone a delicate balance of formality and warmth. Trent's prompt response had caught him off guard, and he found himself momentarily disoriented by the Englishman's directness. "You're Mr. Trent, I presume?" he ventured, his eyes narrowing slightly as he sought to reestablish his footing.

Mr. Murch, seated nearby, nodded in acknowledgment of the greeting, which, to Trent's ears, sounded almost quaintly formal. "I was heading upstairs when I overheard a snippet about a pistol," Trent explained, "so I thought I'd drop in to investigate." Bunner chuckled, his expression relaxing into a more affable one. "You wondered if I might be eavesdropping, perhaps?" he suggested, his voice tinged with a hint of amusement. "No, sir, just a few hushed words about a firearm – this one, I believe – and nothing more."

Bunner's features, a testament to his Connecticut upbringing, were those of a man both refined and introspective. His pale, almost girlish face, framed by a wave of dark hair parted in the middle, seemed to radiate an aura of quiet intensity. His lips, usually occupied by a cigar, now hung slightly ajar, betraying a look of eager curiosity. When he did smoke or chew a cigar, however, this expression vanished, replaced by a mask of calm, calculated reserve – the demeanor of a seasoned businessman.

Having graduated from college, Bunner had entered the world of finance, where he quickly drew the attention of the enigmatic Manderson. The latter, impressed by the young man's skills, eventually offered him the position of private secretary. Bunner proved himself to be a paragon of reliability, methodical in his approach, and possessing an uncanny ability to navigate the complexities of the stock market.

As Trent and Bunner sized each other up, their gazes locked in a silent understanding. Both seemed content with what they saw. "It appears I'm being told," Trent observed, "that the discovery of a pistol potentially linked to Manderson's murder doesn't carry much weight. It's a common enough firearm back home, and it seems to have gained popularity here as well."
