RALLYING ROUND OLD GEORGE


As I stood at the rail of the _Circe_, gazing out at the turquoise waters of Monaco Harbour, my mind wandered to the events that had led us to this tranquil corner of the Mediterranean. It was an invitation from old sportsman Marshall that had brought us here, and I had, in turn, invited George Lattaker, a dear friend since our university days, to join us. His Uncle Augustus, a man of means, had written to inform George that he would be visiting London to hand over a significant inheritance – a bequest from one of George's aunts, which he had been managing as a trustee since childhood. However, I had suggested to George that it would be more convenient for him to travel to Monte Carlo instead, combining his business with a leisurely holiday. Thus, we found ourselves anchored off the coast of Monaco, awaiting Uncle Augustus's arrival.

As I pondered the intricacies of George's family affairs, the sounds of a heated argument drifted across the deck. A female voice rose above the din, its plaintive tone unmistakable – it belonged to Miss Pilbeam, the maid of Mrs. Vanderley. Her words tumbled forth in a cascade of despair: "Oh, Harold!" The male voice, raised in a manner most unbecoming, was that of Voules, my loyal butler. His usual stoic demeanor had given way to something approaching desperation.

"Voules!" I called out, my voice carrying across the water.

The altercation ceased abruptly, replaced by the faint sound of muffled sobs and the distant clinking of glasses. A tap at the door interrupted my reverie, and Voules entered, his composure restored, as always.

"Voules, are you under the impression that I'm hosting a masquerade ball?" I inquired, trying to keep my tone light. "It's only seven in the morning."

"I was summoned, sir," he replied, his expression unreadable.

I raised an eyebrow. "You were summoned to create a ruckus outside my cabin?"

"I apologize, sir. I lost control of my emotions in the heat of the moment."

I decided to let the matter drop. "Who was with you during this... episode?"

"Miss Pilbeam, sir. Mrs. Vanderley's maid."

"Ah, I see. What was the nature of your, ah, disagreement?"
