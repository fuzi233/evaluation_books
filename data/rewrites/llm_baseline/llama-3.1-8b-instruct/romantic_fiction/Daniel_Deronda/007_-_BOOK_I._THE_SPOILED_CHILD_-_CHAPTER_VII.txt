As the first wisps of snowflakes danced in the chill air, like delicate brushstrokes on a canvas of blue, Anna sensed a restlessness within her cousin Rex. Though he had never spoken of it to her, she felt an unspoken understanding that she was privy to a secret he kept hidden even from himself. It was a peculiar sensation, akin to the quiet moments when the family's tranquil facade was pierced by the whispers of a thousand unspoken thoughts. For the first time, Anna couldn't articulate the turmoil brewing within her, as if the words had become tangled in her throat like the intricate patterns on a Persian rug.

Anna's admiration for Gwendolen was genuine, but it was laced with a mix of trepidation and wariness. Her cousin's beauty and charm cast a spell that made Anna feel like a mere spectator, watching a majestic creature roam free in its natural habitat, untethered by the constraints of ordinary life. Gwendolen's indifference to Rex's affections was a painful reality that Anna couldn't shake, and the thought of her scorn sent a shiver down her spine. The knowledge that Gwendolen's attention was elsewhere, perhaps fixated on some distant horizon, left Anna feeling like a fragile leaf caught in a whirlwind, torn between the desire to protect her brother and the fear of losing him to the allure of something greater.

As the days passed, Anna found herself living in a state of suspended animation, waiting for the inevitable conversation that would confirm or deny her worst fears. She dreaded the moment her parents might broach the subject, sensing that their words would cut through the silence like a knife. The tension between them was palpable, a silent language that only they understood, and Anna's anxiety grew with each passing day. Meanwhile, the adults moved through their routine, oblivious to the drama unfolding beneath their noses, their attention fixed on the trivialities of the world outside – the latest news from the _Guardian_ or the _Clerical Gazette_ – while the inner lives of the young ones remained a mystery, a complex tapestry woven from threads of hope and longing.

"Where are you off to, Rex?" Anna asked one gray morning, as their father departed for the sessions with Mrs. Gascoigne, and she noticed her brother donning his antigropelos, a makeshift hunting outfit that hinted at his restlessness.

"To watch the hounds throw off at the Three Barns," he replied, his tone neutral.
