In the warm glow of the Dominican convent's guest chamber, a lone figure lay exhausted, his fragile form wracked by spasms of nausea that had finally subsided, leaving him as weak as a winter's breeze on a Holland canal. A massive fire crackled on the hearth, casting shadows on the stone floor as the patient's clothes hung nearby, steaming gently. Beside him, a towering friar read from his breviary, his voice a soothing melody that seemed to calm the turbulent waters of the patient's mind.

As the patient's gaze drifted towards the friar, he appeared to listen intently, his eyes locked onto the man's majestic form. But moments later, his eyelids drooped, and a low moan escaped his lips. The friar, sensing the patient's distress, knelt beside him, his face pressed against the cold stone, and prayed fervently. Rising to his feet, he bid the patient farewell, saying, "Daybreaks, my son. I must prepare for matins."

As the friar departed, the patient's eyes fluttered open, and he asked, "Good Father Jerome, how came I hither?" The friar's response was cryptic: "By the hand of Heaven. You flung away God's gift. He bestowed it on you again. Think on it! You've tried the world and found its bitter taste. Now try the Church! The Church is peace. Pax vobiscum." With that, the friar vanished, leaving the patient to his thoughts.

Gerard's mind reeled as he pondered the friar's words. He had indeed rejected the Church, and now, as he lay in this strange place, he wondered if he had made a terrible mistake. As he drifted into a doze, a new figure appeared beside him – a layman with an intense gaze that seemed to bore into his very soul.

The man inquired after Gerard's well-being, his voice laced with concern. "How do you feel, my son?" Gerard replied, his voice barely above a whisper, "Very weak. Where have I seen you before, friend?" The layman's expression turned anxious, and he asked, "None the worse for my gauntlet? I was forced to strike you, or we'd both have ended up at the bottom of the Tiber."
