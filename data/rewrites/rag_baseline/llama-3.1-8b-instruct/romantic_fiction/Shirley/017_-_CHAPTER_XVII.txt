The School Feast unfolded like a rich tapestry, woven from threads of anticipation and tradition. It was not a day for martial valor or the clash of arms, but rather a celebration of community and the passing down of cherished recipes. Under the guidance of their esteemed priest, the group set out, their footsteps echoing through the village as they made their way towards Nunnely Common. As they walked, the lilting rhythms of their music stirred something deep within the hearts of those who listened, particularly Miss Keeldar. Her eyes sparkled with a longing spirit, as if the melodies summoned a world beyond their tranquil valley.

Old Helstone, ever the keen observer, caught her gaze and couldn't help but chuckle. "There's no battle to be won today, my dear," he said with a gentle smile. "Our homeland is at peace, and we're merely taking a leisurely stroll. Let's keep our spirits in check, shall we?" His words were laced with a hint of amusement, but Shirley, ever the free spirit, shot back with a mischievous grin, "Take your own advice, Doctor." In a whispered aside to Caroline, she murmured, "We're not soldiers, bound for battlefields or blood-soaked glory. No, we're Lowlanders of Scotland, pilgrims on a sacred journey to the hills, where prayer and conviction might just precede the drums of war. This music stirs my soul, awakening a hunger within me – a yearning for a deeper purpose, for a love worth fighting for."

As they navigated the narrow confines of Royd Lane, the group's excitement grew palpable. The air was alive with the scent of blooming wildflowers and the distant tang of woodsmoke carried on the breeze. Boultby's spectacles glinted with a mixture of curiosity and trepidation, while Helstone's usually calm demeanor betrayed a hint of unease. The ladies, sensing the tension, exchanged knowing glances, their faces set with determination. Mr. Hall, ever the peacemaker, intervened, his staff gesturing towards the approaching procession. "What's amiss, my friends?" he asked, his voice low and soothing.
