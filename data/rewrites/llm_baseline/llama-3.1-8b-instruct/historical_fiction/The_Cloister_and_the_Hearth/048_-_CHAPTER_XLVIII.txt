Catherine's hands moved swiftly through the kitchen, chopping vegetables for the evening meal as she worried about Gerard's impending return home. It was a month, at most, before he would arrive, and she couldn't help but feel a sense of unease. What would she say to him about his betrothed, whom she had barely kept an eye on? The uncertainty of the girl's fate hung over her like a cloud, taking on a particularly unsettling form lately.

"Oh, Kate," she whispered to herself, "what if she's done something foolish?"

Her daughter, Little Kate, entered the kitchen, a look of concern etched on her face. "Mother, she wouldn't be so reckless."

Catherine's hands stilled, her knife hovering over the cutting board. "Ah, child, you don't understand the impulsive nature of young women without guidance. They're like unseasoned yeast – unpredictable and prone to leaping into the unknown. I've seen them fall deeply in love with one man one day and discard him the next, often for someone who would have been better suited to them all along. Their emotions can be as mercurial as the weather, and their decisions, well, they can be rash indeed."

Little Kate looked puzzled. "But, Mother, you're forgetting that Margaret loves Father dearly."

Catherine sighed, her eyes drifting to the simmering stew. "Yes, child, they often forget the ties that bind them in moments of crisis, when the world seems to be crumbling around them. I place more trust in the innocence and naivety of the unborn, who bring joy and hope to our lives, than in those who are already set in their ways."

As the two women worked together, the scent of roasting meat wafted through the air, mingling with the savory aromas of the stew. Catherine's thoughts turned to her own past, and a memory long buried rose to the surface. "I remember when Eli and I were newly married, we had our first disagreement. I was so angry that I considered taking a walk along the canal, which was a temptation I shouldn't have given in to, especially since I was still sewing my wedding dress. But I didn't. Instead, I went back inside, and Eli surprised me with words that I'll never forget – 'Let the flea stick in the wall, my lass, for I love thee.'"
