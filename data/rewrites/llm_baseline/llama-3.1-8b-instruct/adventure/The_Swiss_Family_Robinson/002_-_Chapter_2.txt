As the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, I was roused from my slumber by the melodic chirping of our sentinel rooster, whose nocturnal vigilance had given way to an enthusiastic salute to the rising moon. The warmth of the previous day had surrendered to an equally unforgiving chill, but we managed to rest comfortably, our bodies weary from the arduous tasks of the day before.

With the children still fast asleep, my wife and I took advantage of the quiet morning hours to deliberate on our precarious situation. Our thoughts centered on two pressing matters: inquiring about the fate of our departed companions and appraising the nature and resources of our unfamiliar surroundings. We concurred that Fritz and I should embark on an expedition to investigate these concerns, while my wife remained near our makeshift landing site with the three younger boys.

"Rise, my dear children," I called out cheerfully, shaking them awake. "Help your mother prepare breakfast, that we may fortify ourselves for the day ahead."

My wife smiled wryly, replying, "Alas, what choice do we have? We can merely boil some more soup."

"Ah, but what about Jack's fine catch?" I interjected. "Has it been safely stored?"

"It's been tucked away in a nearby crevice overnight," she explained. "I thought it prudent to keep it from the dogs, who seem to covet such delicacies almost as much as the agouti."

"A wise decision indeed," I agreed. "Even Jack will learn to think ahead in time." I observed that the lobster's size would serve us well, as we would need sustenance for our forthcoming excursion. At the mention of this prospect, the children's faces lit up with excitement, and they capered around me, their hands clasped in anticipation.

"Hold still, my young ones," I cautioned. "This expedition would be too perilous and taxing for you. Fritz and I will venture forth alone, accompanied by one of the dogs, while the other stays behind to safeguard you. Fritz, prepare the firearms, and secure Flora so she doesn't follow us."

Upon hearing the word "firearms," Fritz's face flushed with embarrassment as he fumbled with his rifle. I gave him some time to compose himself, observing with satisfaction that his frustration had instilled a valuable lesson.
