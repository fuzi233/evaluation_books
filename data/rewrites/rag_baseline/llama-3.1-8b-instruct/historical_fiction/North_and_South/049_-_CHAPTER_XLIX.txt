The mist-shrouded dawn crept over the sleepy town of Milton, its gentle fingers weaving a soothing melody through the streets and alleys. In the quiet grandeur of the Lennox estate, Edith stirred in her bed, her thoughts already drifting to the day ahead. As she rose to join her husband, Captain Lennox, in their chambers, a soft rustle of papers caught her attention. It was a letter from Margaret, the young woman whose life had become increasingly intertwined with their own.

"Is not Margaret the heiress?" Edith whispered to her husband, her voice barely audible above the morning silence. She leaned in close, her eyes sparkling with a mixture of curiosity and concern. Captain Lennox's expression remained puzzled, and Edith's brow furrowed in disappointment. For a moment, they stood there, suspended in the stillness of the early morning, the only sound the soft ticking of the clock on the mantle.

As the days passed, Edith's interest in Margaret's affairs only deepened. She would often steal away to share tidbits of news with her husband, her words spilling forth in a breathless torrent. "I've just received a lawyer's letter," she exclaimed one morning, "and it seems Margaret is the residuary legatee of her uncle's estate! Two thousand pounds and forty thousand, at the current value of the property in Milton!" Captain Lennox's eyes widened in surprise, and Edith's face lit up with a triumphant smile.

Their lives, it seemed, were now inextricably linked to Margaret's fortunes. As her legal adviser, Captain Lennox found himself drawn into the intricacies of the law, guiding Margaret through the complexities of her newfound wealth. He took great pleasure in explaining the mysteries of the law, his enthusiasm infectious and genuine. Edith watched with a knowing glint in her eye, her thoughts turning to the possibilities that lay ahead.

One day, as they sat together in the garden, Edith turned to her husband with a mischievous spark in her eye. "Henry, I wonder what you think these long conversations with Margaret will ultimately lead to." Captain Lennox's cheeks flushed, and he quickly looked away, his voice tight with restraint. Edith's smile faltered for a moment, but she pressed on, her words dripping with a subtle malice. "Oh, very well, then I won't mention it to Sholto, or Mr. Montagu's frequent visits to our home."
