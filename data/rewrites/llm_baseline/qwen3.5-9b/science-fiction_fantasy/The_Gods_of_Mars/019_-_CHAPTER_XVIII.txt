Once inside the palace walls, I escorted Sola to the dining hall. After she paid her father the customary respects of the Green Men, she recounted the tale of Dejah Thoris's pilgrimage and subsequent capture.

"Seven days ago," Sola began, "after her audience with Zat Arras, Princess Dejah Thoris attempted to flee the palace during the dead of night. Though I had not witnessed the outcome of her meeting with Zat Arras, I sensed that whatever transpired there had inflicted upon her the sharpest mental anguish. Discovering her slipping away from the palace, I needed no further confirmation of her destination.

"Hastily rousing a dozen of her most trusted guards, I laid my fears before them. Instantly, they rallied to my side, vowing to track our beloved Princess wherever she wandered—even to the Sacred Iss and the Valley Dor. We intercepted her just a short distance from the palace. Beside her walked only Woola, the faithful hound. When we caught up, she feigned outrage, ordering us to return to the palace. But for once, we defied her command. Realizing we would not allow her to embark on this final, solitary journey, she wept and embraced us. Together, we ventured out into the night, heading south.

"The following day, we encountered a herd of small thoats, mounting them allowed us to travel swiftly. We pressed hard due south until the morning of the fifth day, when we sighted a massive fleet of battleships sailing north. Before we could seek cover, we were spotted and surrounded by a horde of Black Martians. The Princess's guard fought with noble valor until the very end, yet they were overwhelmed and slain. Only Dejah Thoris and I remained alive.

"When she realized she was in the grasp of these Black pirates, she attempted to take her own life. However, one of the Blacks snatched her dagger from her hand, and subsequently, they bound both of us so tightly that we could not move our fingers.

"The fleet continued its northern course. It comprised approximately twenty large battleships, accompanied by numerous swift cruisers. That evening, one of the smaller cruisers, which had been far ahead of the main fleet, returned bearing a prisoner—a young Red woman they claimed to have seized in a mountain range, right under the noses of a trio of Red Martian battleships.

"Overhearing fragments of their conversation, it became clear that the Black pirates were hunting a group of fugitives who had escaped them several days prior. Their urgency regarding the young woman was evident from the lengthy and intense interrogation conducted by the fleet's commander upon her arrival. Soon after, she was bound and confined in the compartment with Dejah Thoris and myself.

"This new captive was a girl of striking beauty. She informed Dejah Thoris that many years ago she had undertaken a voluntary pilgrimage from the court of her father, the Jeddak of Ptarth. Her name was Thuvia, Princess of Ptarth. Curious, she asked Dejah Thoris who she might be. Upon hearing the answer, Thuvia fell to her knees, kissing the fettered hands of the Princess. She confessed that very morning she had been with John Carter, Prince of Helium, and Carthoris, his son.

"Dejah Thoris initially struggled to believe her, but as Thuvia detailed the extraordinary adventures she had endured since meeting John Carter—and relayed the accounts given by John Carter, Carthoris, and Xodar of their exploits in the Land of the First Born—Dejah Thoris recognized the truth. 'For who,' she declared, 'on all Barsoom other than John Carter could have performed the deeds you describe?' When Thuvia spoke of her love for John Carter and his unwavering loyalty to the Princess of her choosing, Dejah Thoris broke down in tears. She cursed Zat Arras and the cruel destiny that had torn her from Helium mere days before the return of her beloved lord.

"'I do not blame you for loving him, Thuvia,' Dejah Thoris said gently. 'And knowing the purity and sincerity of your affection, I can easily believe your confession.'

"The fleet pressed on north, nearing Helium itself. But last night, realizing that John Carter had indeed evaded them, they turned their course back toward the south. Shortly thereafter, a guard burst into our compartment and dragged me onto the deck.

*'There is no place in the Land of the First Born for a Green Man,'* he sneered. With those words, he delivered a violent shove that sent me tumbling off the edge of the battleship. Clearly, he believed this the simplest method of removing my presence and ending my life simultaneously.

"But fortune favored me. By a miracle, I survived with only minor bruises. The ship was moving slowly at the time. As I plunged into the darkness below, I shuddered at the certain doom awaiting me, for throughout the day the fleet had soared thousands of feet above the ground. To my utter astonishment, however, I landed not on jagged rocks or barren earth, but upon a soft mass of vegetation merely twenty feet beneath the deck. In fact, the keel of the vessel must have been skimming the surface of the terrain at that precise moment.

"I lay where I fell all night. The following morning revealed the providential nature of my escape. As the sun rose, I beheld a vast panorama of seabed and distant hills stretching far below. I stood atop the highest peak of a lofty range. The fleet, in the darkness of the previous night, had barely grazed the crests of the hills. During the brief interval when they hovered close to the surface, the Black guard had pitched me downward, intending my death, but instead granting me life.

"A few miles to the west lay a great waterway. Reaching it, I found to my immense relief that it flowed from Helium. From there, a thoat was secured for me—the remainder of your story is known."

For many minutes, silence hung heavy between us. The thought of Dejah Thoris in the clutches of the First Born sent a chill through me. Suddenly, however, the ancient fire of unyielding self-confidence surged within me. I sprang to my feet, throwing back my shoulders and raising my sword high. I took a solemn vow to reach her, rescue her, and exact vengeance.

A hundred swords flashed from their scabbards as a hundred warriors leaped onto the table-top, pledging their lives and fortunes to the expedition. My plans were already forming. I thanked each loyal friend, leaving Carthoris to host them, and withdrew to my private audience chamber with Kantos Kan, Tars Tarkas, Xodar, and Hor Vastus.

Here, deep into the night, we deliberated the specifics of our operation. Xodar was convinced that Issus would select both Dejah Thoris and Thuvia to serve her for a year.

"For that duration, at least, they will be relatively secure," Xodar reasoned. "We will know precisely where to look for them."

Regarding the equipping of a fleet capable of entering Omean, the details were entrusted to Kantos Kan and Xodar. The former agreed to rush the necessary vessels into the dry docks, where Xodar would oversee their modification with water-propulsion systems. For many years, the Black man had managed the refitting of captured battleships designed for navigation through Omean, possessing intimate knowledge of the propeller construction, housings, and auxiliary gearing required. Estimates suggested a six-month preparation period, necessitating absolute secrecy to prevent Zat Arras from discovering our intentions.

Kantos Kan expressed confidence that Zat Arras's ambitions were now fully ignited; nothing short of the title of Jeddak of Helium would suffice.

"I doubt," Kantos Kan observed, "that he would even welcome Dejah Thoris's return, for it would place someone closer to the throne than he. With you and Carthoris removed from the picture, there would be little obstacle preventing him from seizing the title yourself. Rest assured, so long as he remains supreme, neither of you is safe."

"There is a way," cried Hor Vastus, "to thwart him effectively and forever."

"What?" I asked.

He smiled enigmatically. "I shall whisper it here today, but someday I shall stand upon the dome of the Temple of Reward and shout it to the cheering multitudes below."

"What do you mean?" Kantos Kan inquired.

"John Carter, Jeddak of Helium," Hor Vastus murmured softly.

The eyes of my companions brightened, grim smiles of pleasure and anticipation spreading across their faces as each gaze turned to me, questioning. But I shook my head.

"No, my friends," I replied, smiling. "Thank you, but it cannot be—not yet, at least. Only when we know that Tardos Mors and Mors Kajak are gone, never to return; and if I am still here, then I shall join you all to ensure the people of Helium are permitted to choose their next Jeddak fairly. Whoever they elect may count upon the loyalty of my sword, nor shall I seek honor for myself. Until then, Tardos Mors is Jeddak of Helium, and Zat Arras serves as his representative."

"As you wish, John Carter," Hor Vastus conceded. "But—what was that?" he whispered, pointing toward the window overlooking the gardens.

Scarcely had the words left his lips when he had launched himself onto the balcony.

"There he goes!" he shouted excitedly. "The guards! Down there! The guards!"
