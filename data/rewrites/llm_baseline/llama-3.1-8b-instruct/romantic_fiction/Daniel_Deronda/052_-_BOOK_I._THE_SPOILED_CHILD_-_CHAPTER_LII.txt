As Mordecai sat across from Hans Meyrick, his piercing eyes seemed to hold a secret, a depth that only unfolded itself slowly, like the petals of a lotus flower. The words of La Rochefoucauld echoed in his mind: "The same firmness that resists love can also make it violent and enduring; and those who are weak, forever swayed by their passions, are rarely truly fulfilled." It was a truth that Mordecai knew well, for he had spent years perfecting the delicate balance between conviction and compassion.

Meanwhile, a letter arrived for Deronda, penned in the elegant script of Hans Meyrick. The words danced across the page, a testament to the artist's wit and insight. "My dear Deronda," the letter began, "in response to your observations on the tumultuous state of Italy and the world at large, I offer this: time will reveal its secrets, and the whispers of the past will shape the present. The recent scandals involving swindling telegrams have shed light on the causes behind last year's devastating cattle plague, vindicating the philosophers who had predicted such an outcome and justifying the compensation to the affected farmers."

Hans' thoughts turned to his own musings on the future, where a murrain would soon afflict the commercial class, and the truth behind it would emerge in the sudden popularity of rejected artworks. Some might dismiss this idea as a fanciful use of analogy, but Hans remained convinced that there were those who would stop at nothing to exploit even the most vulnerable among them. He smiled wryly, thinking of the irony that a bad critic might one day praise his own Berenice series, and that the men responsible for his misfortune would be punished for their neglect.

As Hans sat for Mordecai, he felt a sense of liberation, free to explore the mysteries of the Hebrew prophet's mind without the distraction of Deronda's presence. He found himself drawn to Mordecai's broad, philosophical nature, where the lines between allegory and mysticism blurred. The older man's sharp dialectic skills left Hans in awe, and he marveled at how easily his own ideas found a comfortable home within Mordecai's vast and complex mind. The task of reconciling their differing perspectives was Mordecai's to undertake, not his. Hans preferred to remain in the realm of mystery, where the search for truth was a lifelong pursuit, and the answers lay hidden, waiting to be uncovered.
