By the soft glow of the streetlamp, the evening shadows cast a serene tranquility over the narrow Via de' Bardi. As Romola strolled alongside her husband, Tito, accompanied by the quiet figure of Maso bearing the lantern, the gentle patter of raindrops against the cobblestones underscored the peaceful atmosphere. Their conversation was a studied dance, each step choreographed to avoid any discordant note. Tito, ever the pragmatist, spoke of the day's events, his words laced with a subtle warmth, yet never truly connecting with the depth of Romola's emotions. He knew her well enough to gauge her reactions, to sense the fine line between her submission and her hidden turmoil.

As they approached the loggia, the scent of damp earth and old stone wafted through the air, mingling with the fragrance of fresh bread carried on the breeze. Tito suggested they seek shelter beneath the loggia, his voice tinged with a hint of urgency. Romola raised an eyebrow, surprised by his sudden caution, yet willing to acquiesce. Under the lamplight, the painting of the Virgin seemed to radiate a gentle benevolence, its tranquil presence a soothing balm for the chill of the evening.

The sound of approaching footsteps and murmuring voices grew louder, a reminder that even in the seclusion of this quiet street, the city's rhythms persisted. As Tito guided Romola beneath the loggia, the loggia's arches rose above them like a protective canopy, casting dappled shadows on the stone floor. For a moment, they stood together, suspended in the stillness, the rain pattering softly around them. The world outside receded, leaving only the quiet intimacy of this fleeting moment, a moment that seemed to hold the promise of connection, of understanding, yet remained forever elusive.
