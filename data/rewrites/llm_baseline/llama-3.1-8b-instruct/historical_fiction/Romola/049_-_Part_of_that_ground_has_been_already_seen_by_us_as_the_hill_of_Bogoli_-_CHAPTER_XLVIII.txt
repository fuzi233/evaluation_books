As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow through the narrow window of Romola's small room, she set down her quill, the nib dipping into the inkwell with a soft splash. The fading light hinted at the end of another long day spent poring over documents, her mind consumed by the intricacies of the merchant's ledger. Tito's arrival caught her off guard, and she looked up to find him standing in the doorway, his features illuminated by the fading light.

In the silence that followed, Romola's thoughts turned inward. The tumult of emotions that had driven her to confront her husband earlier that day still lingered, and she couldn't help but wonder if she had been too hasty. Perhaps, she thought, her godfather's words about the duplicity inherent in politics had been a more significant factor than she had initially believed. The idea that Tito might have been drawn into the web of intrigue without intending to further the plot offered a measure of comfort, and she felt a pang of regret for her earlier outburst.

As she gazed up at Tito, she noticed a subtle shift in his demeanor, a tension that seemed to emanate from within. His eyes, usually warm and gentle, now held a sharp intensity that made her heart skip a beat. For a moment, they simply regarded each other, the air thick with unspoken words.

The stillness between them was palpable, a heavy silence that seemed to press upon Romola's shoulders. It was a silence born of two years of unspoken understanding, a silence that had grown like a thick crust on a well-used saucepan, hardened by the heat of unexpressed emotions. Tito's eyes seemed to hold a world of meaning, and Romola felt her own heart pounding in response.

"Romola," Tito began, his voice low and measured, like the gentle simmer of a sauce reducing on the stovetop. "It is time we understood each other." He paused, the weight of his words hanging in the air like the fragrance of a just-cooked risotto.

"That is what I most desire, Tito," she replied, her voice barely above a whisper. Her face, once flushed with indignation, now seemed pale and vulnerable, like a delicate soufflé that had fallen slightly, revealing the tender layers beneath.
