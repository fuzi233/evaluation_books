Tonight, we welcomed a messenger from our distant cousin in Gratz, bearing two large packing cases adorned with intricate carvings. The arrival sent ripples of excitement throughout our quiet household, and we gathered in the grand hall to witness the unloading of the precious cargo.

As the cases were opened, the soft murmur of conversation filled the air, punctuated by the occasional clang of tools and the rustle of fabric. Our family's esteemed portraits, worn by the passage of time, began to re-emerge, their faded hues restored to vibrancy. My mother's Hungarian heritage shone through in the numerous family portraits, each one a testament to our storied past.

Among the collection, one painting caught my eye – a small, nearly square portrait with a mysterious aura surrounding it. At the top, a faint inscription read 'Mircalla, Countess Karnstein, 1698.' I recalled seeing it before, though its details had been obscured by the ravages of time. The artist carefully revealed the masterpiece, and I felt a shiver run down my spine as I beheld the striking resemblance to Carmilla.

She sat motionless, her gaze fixed on me with an unnerving intensity, as if drawn into the world within the painting. "Papa, isn't this incredible?" I asked, hardly able to contain my excitement. "Look, it's as if she's alive!" My father's response was subdued, however, and he turned his attention to the picture cleaner, engaging in a discussion about the restoration process.
