The view from the crest of the Sacred Mountain offered no grandeur, only a sprawling diagram of decay. Below, the city unfolded like a stained map, its harbor and estuary gleaming with a sickly reflection. Beyond, the hills smoked in their habitual, suffocating rhythm. Dominating the center was the stone circle of the Sun, grim and bare, while nearby, the royal pyramid loomed—a massive accumulation of gold that caught the sun's glare, mocking the darkness. In the square before it stood the granite throne, reduced by distance to the size of a molehill, the resting place where my love had slept for nine years.

Old Zaemon tracked my gaze. "Yes," he sighed, the sound heavy with resignation. "I know where your attention rests. Deucalion, fresh from Yucatan, was a force of nature. The King we chose—the best available option—is merely a vessel of weakness."

"It can be converted," I replied, my voice tight. "Give me Nais, living and warm, and I surpass the cold viceroy you describe."

"I have pledged her to you," Zaemon said, his tone flat. "But at the cost of fracturing this new kingdom. Perhaps we also rid you of Phorenice and her brood. Unlikely. She is too cunning. Once the game begins, she will deduce the origin and conclusion, escaping before harm touches her. The High Gods, seeking our refinement, granted her knowledge of how to trigger these earth tremors."

"I have witnessed her manipulation. May I hear the plan?"

"It will be revealed in due course. For now, you must sleep. Tonight, you descend into the city, retrieve your woman—my daughter—and bring her here. You require the strength of a man." He stepped back, scanning me. "Few would mistake you for the tidy, clean-chinned Deucalion anymore, brother. Your current appearance serves as armor. Hairy, shaggy, naked, filthy—you resemble a savage newly landed from Europe. Be wary that no citizen mistakes your physique for servitude and claims you as property."

"I pity him if he does."

Zaemon laughed, a rare, brittle sound. "Indeed, I do as well." But the laughter vanished instantly. Laughter and Zaemon were incompatible companions. "Rest, my King. Leave me to the Ark of Mysteries to prepare with another of the Three. Handling the powers we unleash tonight is no trivial task. Danger awaits us both. If we do not meet again behind the stars to answer the Gods, farewell, Deucalion."

That night, I slept as a soldier sleeps: consuming hours, excluding thought. Weakness permits distraction; strength accepts silence. When the darkness fully settled, I roused myself and secured the rope attendants. The moon was absent; the air was thick with a suffocating blackness. As they lowered me, I disappeared from sight.

Reaching the tumbled rocks at the cliff's base, I proceeded with extreme caution. Phorenice's soldiers huddled around their fires, radiating a palpable dread. I had no desire to amplify their anxiety. Creeping through the deepest shadows, I navigated the tunnel system I had used previously to breach the city walls.

The lamp awaited. I moved swiftly through the windings. The air tasted more noxious than before, saturated with vapors, signaling that Zaemon had initiated the internal mechanisms destined to convulse the city. Yet, exiting proved difficult. Not due to crowds blocking the path, but because the masonry had shifted. In antiquity, the Priestly Clan regulated architecture, ensuring no conflict with secret passages. Today, priests held no sway; architects, prone to error, had sealed countless outlets.

Forced to navigate the network wherever possible rather than where intended, I emerged on the far side of the city, near where the outer wall meets the harbor. I exited unseen, meticulously erasing all traces of my presence. Beneath my feet, the earth felt like the deck of a ship in rough water. Though no visible movement had yet disturbed the populace, who slept unaware, I sensed the solid deadness of the ground had evaporated. Any moment, it could erupt into devastation.

*Gods.* Was I too late to witness the unburial of my love? Would she be exposed to the public gaze as terrified citizens swarmed into the open spaces? I imagined their crude, cruel hands thrusting upon her helpless form, and my insides curdled.

I ran blindly down the narrow, winding streets, driven solely by the urge to reach the square before the catastrophe consumed everything. With exquisite cruelty, I had placed her alive in her tomb beneath the granite throne. If my strength and speed allowed, I would reclaim her with the same hands.

Few interrupted my frenzy. Occasionally, a wretch in the gutter shouted, "Thief! Pay a toll or I pursue!" But none followed. My speed outpaced them. Soon, the earth's swing intensified, causing building facades to grate, groan, and spit showers of dust. People fled their homes in screams, but none stopped the ragged, shaggy savage running past, mud flinging from his naked feet.

Arriving at the great square, I found it filling with refugees fleeing the narrow streets. Darkness, noise, dust, and sickness permeated the air. Beneath us, the ground rippled like a sea, the motion growing terrifyingly slow and intense. Houses crashed unseen into the gloom, adding to the cacophony.

The royal pyramid, however, was built to withstand rudimentary shocks. Its stones were dovetailed with marvelous ingenuity, clamped by ponderous metal tongues. It was boasted that half its foundation could be excavated, and it would remain four-square under heaven, enduring longer than hills. Flickering torches revealed its great stone doors open, frightened inmates scurrying out only to vanish into the gloom. Whether Phorenice was trapped or escaped mattered little to me. I crouched by the granite throne in the center of the square, feeling its stones grate together like broken bone as it rocked on the earth-waves.

In that night of dust and obscurity, visibility was nil. Yet, perhaps as recompense for the ache of my long-held love, the Gods granted me special sight. Watching, I saw the carved rock forming the capstone shift slightly, then again, then again. A tiny jerk for each pulse, yet an appreciable displacement. Moreover, the stone moved consistently to one side.

There was method in Zaemon's desperation, something I had overlooked in my blind panic. I ascended the steps on the side where the capstone drifted, clinging there with feverish expectation.

The earth-swing grew more violent, though the gradations remained imperceptible. The din of collapsing houses, shrieks, and cries of pain rose into the night. Dust thickened until one coughed and choked, blotting out the rare stars. Clinging to an angle of the granite throne, crouched on the uppermost step below the capstone, I struggled to maintain my footing against the violence.

Yet the huge capstone, carved with the snake and the outstretched hand, held my love fast in her living tomb. I wanted to bite the cold granite in frustration at the barrier between us. The crowd thronging the square was mad with terror, their numbers rendering my situation more desperate. "Phorenice, Goddess, aid us now!" some cried. When the prayer yielded no relief, they fell to reciting old confessions of faith, turning in their hour of dread to gods they had spurned for decades. A curious critique of their true religion, if one cared to observe.

Loudly, the crash of falling masonry continued. From the royal pyramid itself, though invisible through the darkness, came sounds of grinding stone and cracking metal, indicating that even its majestic strength had reached its breaking point. Old Zaemon's threat echoed in my mind: *"You shall see... this royal pyramid... torn tier from tier, stone from stone, scattered as feathers before a wind!"*

The surging earth grew heavier. The square's pavement gaped and upheaved, crushing feet under grinding blocks. Screams sharpened. Now, the pyramid itself began to split, gape, and topple. Roofs collapsed, ponderous masonry shuttered down, filling the chambers. Thunder roared through the black night, joining the devil's orchestra, while vivid lightning flashes illuminated the flying dust clouds.

Perhaps naturally, I expected her to be buried. But a shock struck me when a lightning flare revealed Phorenice safe in the square, standing not far from me.

She occupied a large flagstone, swaying her supple body to the shocks. Her face remained calm, her loveliness untouched by years. Periodically, she brushed away settling dust from her short, curling red hair. No fear marked her features. Only weariness, contempt, and a tinge of amusement. More than the crumbling of her royal pyramid impressed Phorenice with the infinite power of her enemies.

*Gods!* How her cool indifference maddened me. I possessed the capacity to strangle her if she had been within reach. Instead, she stood swaying easily, like a sailor on a deck, while beside her, crouched on the same flagstone, was Ylga, overcome with nausea, once again her fan-girl. Ylga was Nais's twin sister, and I owed her an ancient kindness, but I had no leisure to act, nor much ability to do so. With each shock, the capstone jarred farther from its bed, bringing my love closer. She claimed all my service then.
