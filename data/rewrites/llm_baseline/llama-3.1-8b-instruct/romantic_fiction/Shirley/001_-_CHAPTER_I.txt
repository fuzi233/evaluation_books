The scent of simmering broth wafted through the crisp morning air as the sun rose over the rolling hills of the North Riding of Yorkshire. In the small village of Whinbury, nestled between the gentle slopes, a group of young men gathered at a wooden table, their faces flushed from a morning of brisk exercise. Mr. Donne, curate of Whinbury, had invited his colleagues, Mr. Malone from Briarfield and Mr. Sweeting from Nunnely, to share a humble repast at the home of John Gale, a skilled clothier. The aroma of freshly baked bread and steaming vegetables mingled with the sound of clinking spoons and lively conversation.

As we step into the cozy parlour, the warmth of the fire and the golden light streaming through the windows create an inviting atmosphere. The three young men sat around the table, their plates filled with a simple yet nourishing meal of cold lentils and vinegar, unleavened bread, and bitter herbs. The stark contrast to the richness of the lamb served at Easter feasts was intentional, a nod to the austere traditions of Passion Week.

It was the year eighteen-hundred-eleven-twelve, and the village was still recovering from the scarcity of curates that had plagued the region. The Pastoral Aid and Additional Curates' Society had yet to provide relief, and the burden of supporting these energetic young men fell squarely on the shoulders of their elderly rectors. Yet, amidst the scarcity, a few districts in the West Riding of Yorkshire boasted a bounty of talented young clergy. Our fortunate trio was part of this select group, and as they savoured their meal, their spirits were high, their conversation flowing easily.

Their host, John Gale, had kindly offered his home for this impromptu gathering, and the warm hospitality of the clothier's wife added to the convivial atmosphere. As we join the party, the air is filled with laughter and the clinking of cups. For now, the weight of their responsibilities as curates is forgotten, and they indulge in the simple pleasures of good company and a satisfying meal. As we listen in on their conversation, we catch glimpses of their lively personalities, their wit, and their camaraderie, a testament to the enduring bonds of friendship forged in the fires of shared endeavour.
