Ripening Schemes.

A month after the Carnival's fervor had dissipated, leaving behind a faint scent of rose petals and sugar, Tito descended the worn marble steps of the Old Palace, bound for the Basilica of San Marco. His errand was a private one, shrouded in the quiet intimacy of early morning. The direct route, a straight line from the palace, seemed too straightforward, too lacking in nuance, so Tito chose instead to stroll through the Piazza di Santa Croce, where the faithful would soon pour out of the grand church, their faces aglow with the fervor of the daily Lenten sermon.

Santa Croce, that magnificent temple of the Franciscans, had become the epicenter of Florence's intellectual ferment. The daily Lenten sermons, once the domain of the charismatic Fra Girolamo Savonarola, had grown increasingly contentious. His words, once a beacon of hope and reform, had become a lightning rod for criticism, drawing fire from the pulpit of Santa Croce, where the Franciscan friar, Fra Francesco di Puglia, had taken up the challenge of refuting Savonarola's prophetic doctrine. The gauntlet had been thrown, and the people, ever eager for spectacle, had taken notice.

As Tito navigated the crowded piazza, he spotted the throngs of onlookers converging on the Franciscan monastery, their attention fixed on a large placard, its Latin script a tantalizing enigma. The placard, posted by order of the Signoria, was a declaration of intent, a challenge to Savonarola to prove the divine origin of his doctrine by walking through fire. The crowd's curiosity was piqued, their hunger for drama and spectacle insatiable. And yet, as they strained to decipher the Latin text, a sense of unease settled over them, a feeling that they were missing something essential, something that lay just beyond their grasp.

Tito, however, was not there to indulge in the spectacle. His eyes, bright with a quiet intensity, scanned the crowd, searching for a particular figure, a figure that would soon emerge from the monastery, bearing the weight of a decision that would change the course of their lives forever.
