The night descended upon the house not as a blanket of rest, but as a siege of the subconscious. Their sleep was fractured, a troubled vigil where the boundary between dream and waking dissolved into a singular, paralyzing dread. Whether the terror of their slumber bled into their consciousness or their waking anxieties sank back into the void, they remained perpetually unrested. Something was moving within the walls—silent, terrible, and utterly unknown to them. No sound pierced the darkness; the silence was not an absence of noise, but a tangible entity, thick with terror.

At intervals, a frightful wind would fill the house, shaking its interior until the timbers quivered like a horse shedding water. Yet this wind made no moan, passing through their chambers like a soundless sob before vanishing. They slept again, only to wake with a violent start, convinced the house was flooding. Water surged from below, swelling rapidly until the garret was submerged to the rafters. It flowed with the eerie quiet of the earlier wind, leaving them dry and warm once it receded.

On another occasion, the air itself seemed to swarm with cats. They multiplied endlessly, climbing walls, skittering across floors, enveloping the room. Lady Mara's nightgowns felt the phantom pressure of claws seeking purchase, though none found flesh. By morning, not a single scratch marred their skin. Then, cutting through the suffocating dark, came the only auditory break: the distant, mournful howl of a massive grandmother-cat from the desert. Interpreting this as a mother calling her lost young, the swarm froze instantly. Silence reclaimed the room, absolute and heavy, allowing them to drift back into a coma-like sleep until sunrise.

This was the testimony of the children, innocent observers of a nightmare they could not comprehend. But I, present throughout the night alongside the veiled woman and the princess, witnessed fragments of the truth. Some I saw clearly; most I only felt as a crushing weight; others existed beyond the reach of sight, accessible only to a heart prepared to understand the abyss.

When Mara departed with the children, my gaze fell upon the white leopardess. We had believed her gone, yet she cowered in a corner, trembling with mortal terror at whatever lurked in the shadows. A lamp burned on the high chimney-piece, casting shifting patterns of cloud and shadow that danced across the room. The princess lay rigid on the settle by the wall, frozen in a posture of fearful waiting, unmoving hand or foot.

Upon Mara's return, she dragged the settle bearing Lilith to the center of the room, positioning herself opposite me across the hearth. Between us, a small fire crackled, a fragile beacon against the encroaching gloom.

Something terrible was advancing. The cloudy presences flickered and shook violently. From their midst emerged a silvery creature resembling a slowworm, crawling deliberately across the clay floor before slipping into the flames. We sat motionless, held in suspension. The entity advanced.

Hours stretched into eternity. Midnight approached, yet the scene remained unchanged. The night was still, devoid of sound—a rustle from the fire, a crack from the beams, or a shift in the earth. Occasionally, I felt a subtle heaving, unsure if it originated in the soil, the air, the waters beneath, or within my own soul. A profound sense of impending judgment settled over me. Yet, I felt no fear; I cared for nothing save the task that demanded completion.

Suddenly, the clock struck midnight. The muffled woman rose, turned toward the settle, and slowly unwound the long swathes concealing her face. The fabric pooled on the floor, and she stepped over it. The princess's feet pointed toward the hearth; Mara moved to her head, then turned to stand behind it. In that moment, I beheld her face. It was lovely beyond description—pale and sorrowful, a grief etched into both heart and soul, yet devoid of unhappiness. I knew she could never truly be unhappy. Great tears streamed down her cheeks; she wiped them with her robe, her countenance settling into a stillness that bordered on severity, softened only by the infinite pity in every line of her expression. She placed her hand upon the princess's head, resting on the low hairline, and stooped to breathe upon the sallow brow. The body shuddered.

"Will you turn away from the wicked things you have been doing so long?" Mara asked softly.

Silence. Mara repeated the question in the same gentle, inviting tone. Still, there was no response. On the third attempt, the seemingly lifeless form opened its mouth. Words formed, yet they were not mere sound; they framed themselves of something alien, transcending vibration to strike directly at my understanding.

"I will not," the voice resonated. "I will be myself and not another!"

"Alas, you are another now, not yourself! Will you not be your real self?"

"I will be what I mean myself now."

"If you were restored, would you not make what amends you could for the misery you have caused?"

"I would do after my nature."

"You do not know it: your nature is good, and you do evil!"

"I will do as my Self pleases—as my Self desires."

"You will do as the Shadow, overshadowing your Self inclines you?"

"I will do what I will to do."

"You have killed your daughter, Lilith!"

"I have killed thousands. She is my own!"

"She was never yours as you are another's."

"I am not another's; I am my own, and my daughter is mine."

"Then, alas, your hour is come!"

"I care not. I am what I am; no one can take from me myself!"

"You are not the Self you imagine."

"So long as I feel myself what it pleases me to think myself, I care not. I am content to be to myself what I would be. What I choose to seem to myself makes me what I am. My own thought makes me me; my own thought of myself is me. Another shall not make me!"

"But another has made you, and can compel you to see what you have made yourself. You will not be able much longer to look to yourself anything but what he sees you! You will not much longer have satisfaction in the thought of yourself. At this moment you are aware of the coming change!"

"No one ever made me. I defy that Power to unmake me from a free woman! You are his slave, and I defy you! You may be able to torture me—I do not know—but you shall not compel me to anything against my will!"

"Such a compulsion would be without value. But there is a light that goes deeper than the will, a light that illuminates the darkness behind it: that light can change your will, can make it truly yours and not another's—not the Shadow's. Into the created can pour itself the creating will, and so redeem it!"

"That light shall not enter me: I hate it! Begone, slave!"

"I am no slave, for I love that light, and will with the deeper will which created mine. There is no slave but the creature that wills against its creator. Who is a slave but her who cries, 'I am free,' yet cannot cease to exist!"

"You speak foolishness from a cowering heart! You imagine me given over to you: I defy you! I hold myself against you! What I choose to be, you cannot change. I will not be what you think me—what you say I am!"

"I am sorry: you must suffer!"

"But be free!"

"She alone is free who would make free; she loves not freedom who would enslave: she is herself a slave. Every life, every will, every heart that came within your ken, you have sought to subdue: you are the slave of every slave you have made—such a slave that you do not know it! See your own self!"

Mara withdrew her hand from the princess's head and retreated two paces.

A soundless presence, akin to roaring flame, seized the house—the same force the children had perceived as a silent wind. Instinctively, I turned to the hearth. Its fire had reduced to a still, small, moveless glow. Yet I saw the worm-thing emerge, white-hot and vivid as incandescent silver, the living heart of essential fire. It crawled along the floor toward the settle, moving with agonizing slowness. Even slower, it ascended the cushion and laid itself at the princess's feet, reluctant to proceed further. I rose and crept closer. Mara stood motionless, awaiting an event foreknown. The glowing entity crawled onto a bare, bony foot; it showed no suffering, nor was the settle scorched where it had rested. Very slowly, it traced a path along her robe until it reached her bosom, disappearing into the folds.

The princess's face remained stonily calm, her eyelids closed as over dead eyes. For several minutes, nothing occurred. Then, on her dry, parchment-like skin, drops began to appear, like the finest dew. Instantly, they swelled to the size of seed pearls, merged, and began to pour down in streams. I lunged forward to snatch the worm from the withered bosom and crush it with my foot. But Mara, Mother of Sorrow, stepped between us and drew aside the closed edges of the robe. No serpent was there; no searing trail marked the fabric.
