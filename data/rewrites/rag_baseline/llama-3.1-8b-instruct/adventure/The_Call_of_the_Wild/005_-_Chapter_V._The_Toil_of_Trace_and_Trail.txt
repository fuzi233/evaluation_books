Twenty-five hundred miles, traversed in a mere four months, had taken its toll on Buck and his companions. Their once robust forms now hung gaunt and weary, their muscles depleted from the relentless grind of the trail. Every fiber, every cell, felt the weight of exhaustion, the lingering ache of a thousand miles of slogging through snow and ice. As they stumbled down the main street of Skaguay, their feet dragging in the dust, the drivers' encouraging words only served to highlight the futility of their situation.

"Mush on, poor sore feet," the driver urged, his voice tinged with a mixture of pity and resignation. "This is the last stretch, then we'll get some well-deserved rest." But Buck and his companions knew better. The drivers' words were little more than a cruel jest, a desperate attempt to mask the harsh reality of their circumstances.

In the midst of this desolate scene, a peculiar phenomenon caught Buck's attention. A pair of men, strangers in this frozen outpost, strolled into town, their eyes scanning the crowd with an air of detached curiosity. One, a young man with a Colt's revolver strapped to his hip, seemed out of place among the rugged trappers and prospectors. His callow features and bristling cartridge belt betrayed a naivety that was both intriguing and disturbing. His companion, a middle-aged man with a weak, watery gaze and a mustache that seemed to twist in defiance of its limp lip, exuded an air of quiet confidence, as if he had seen it all before.

As Buck watched, the two men engaged in hushed conversation with the Government agent, their words punctuated by the rustle of paper and the clinking of coins. Buck sensed a transaction taking place, one that would alter the course of his life forever. He saw the money change hands, the Scotch half-breed and the mail-train drivers exchanging places with these newcomers. When driven to the new owners' camp, Buck's apprehensions deepened. The tent, half-stretched and disordered, seemed a reflection of the couple's slovenly lifestyle. A woman, Mercedes, flitted about the campsite, her presence a jarring note in the otherwise masculine atmosphere. Buck watched warily as the pair began to dismantle the tent, their labored movements a testament to their ineptitude.
