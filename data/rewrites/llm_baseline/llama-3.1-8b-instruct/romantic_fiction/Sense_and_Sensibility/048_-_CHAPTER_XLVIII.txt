But fate had other plans. The couple had tied the knot in town and were now making their way to Delaford, the very place that held a special significance for Elinor. She recalled the many stories her aunt had shared about the estate's rich history and the local culinary traditions that had been passed down through generations.

As she gazed out at the garden, Elinor's thoughts turned to the parsonage-house, where she envisioned Lucy, a woman driven by her own desires and ambitions. She pictured Lucy expertly managing the household, balancing thriftiness with a flair for style, always mindful of appearances and eager to curry favor with the influential Colonel Brandon and wealthy acquaintances. And then there was Edward – Elinor's mind struggled to reconcile her feelings towards him, torn between a deep affection and a sense of disillusionment. She felt a pang of sadness at the thought of him being trapped in this marriage, yet couldn't quite muster up the emotions to feel truly unhappy for him.

Days passed, and Elinor found herself growing increasingly restless, eager for news from London. She longed for someone to share the details of Edward and Lucy's life together, to provide insight into the inner workings of their relationship. Her mother's suggestion that they write to Colonel Brandon had sparked a glimmer of hope, and Elinor held onto the possibility that he might arrive soon, bearing tidings from the couple.
