It is high time to leave behind these fleeting, discursive notes on the mundane mechanics of Flatland and turn toward the central event of this narrative: my initiation into the mysteries of Space. That is the true subject; everything preceding it serves merely as a prologue. Consequently, I must omit countless details that, while potentially fascinating, are secondary to this revelation. Consider, for instance, our methods of locomotion and cessation without feet, or the ingenious ways we anchor structures of wood, stone, and brick despite lacking hands or the ability to leverage the earth's lateral pressure. I pass over the meteorological intricacies of how rain navigates the gaps between our zones to nourish the south without flooding the north, the geology of our hills and mines, the biology of our flora, the rhythm of our seasons, and the unique adaptations of our alphabet to linear tablets. These omissions stem not from neglect, but from a deliberate economy of the reader's time.

Yet, before unveiling the secrets of the third dimension, I cannot ignore the pillars of our constitution, the architects of our destiny, and the objects of our near-worship: our Circles, or Priests. Do not mistake my terminology; unlike in your world where "priest" implies a specific clerical role, among us, a Circle is the supreme administrator of every conceivable domain—trade, commerce, military command, architecture, engineering, education, statesmanship, legislation, morality, and theology. They perform no labor themselves; instead, they are the invisible causes behind every worthy deed executed by others. While the populace accepts the label "Circle" as literal truth, the educated elite understand the grim geometric reality: no Circle is truly circular. A Circle is merely a polygon with an immense number of infinitesimal sides. As the count of these sides grows, the shape approaches perfection. When the number reaches three or four hundred, the distinction vanishes to the untrained touch. However, let us be precise: it *would* be difficult to detect the angles if one possessed the capacity to feel them. Among the highest society, tactile recognition is forbidden; to physically feel a Circle is considered the ultimate act of sacrilege. This cultivated numbness allows the elite to maintain the sacred veil of mystery surrounding their perimeters.

With an average perimeter of three feet, a polygon of three hundred sides possesses edges measuring barely one-tenth of an inch. In the case of six or seven hundred sides, the segments shrink to the size of a pinhead from Spaceland. By convention, the current High Priest is assumed to possess ten thousand sides. Unlike the lower classes, whose advancement is strictly capped by natural law—one additional side per generation—the ascent of the Circle caste defies such arithmetic determinism. If nature alone ruled, the lineage would be predictable; the four hundred and ninety-seventh descendant of an equilateral triangle would inevitably be a five-hundred-sided figure. But nature imposes two antagonistic decrees upon this evolution. First, as the species climbs the developmental ladder, the rate of growth accelerates exponentially. Second, fertility diminishes in direct proportion. In the homes of polygons with four or five hundred sides, sons are rare; twins are unheard of. Conversely, the offspring of a five-hundred-sided parent may emerge with five hundred and fifty, or even six hundred sides.

Art intervenes to catalyze this higher evolution. Our physicians have mastered the terrifying technique of fracturing the tiny, tender sides of infants from the upper classes, resetting their frames with surgical precision. Occasionally, this risky procedure succeeds in leaping over two or three hundred generational increments, effectively doubling the noblety of the child's descent in a single stroke. Tragically, many promising lives are sacrificed in this gamble; fewer than one in ten survive the ordeal. Yet, driven by an insatiable parental ambition, nearly every nobleman places his firstborn in the Circular Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium within a month of birth. One year decides fate. Most often, the child returns to join the crowded rows of the Neo-Therapeutic Cemetery, a permanent addition to the tombstones. Rarely, however, does a procession bear the survivor back to exultant parents: no longer a polygon, but a Circle, at least by courtesy. Such miraculous instances fuel the desperate willingness of polygonal families to submit to these domestic sacrifices, hoping against hope for a blessed outcome.
