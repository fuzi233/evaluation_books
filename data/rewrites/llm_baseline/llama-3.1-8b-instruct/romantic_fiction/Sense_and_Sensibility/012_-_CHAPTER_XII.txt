As the warm sunlight streamed through the windows of the cottage, Elinor and Marianne strolled hand in hand through the lush green hills surrounding their home. Marianne's eyes sparkled with excitement as she shared the news of Willoughby's generous gift – a magnificent horse he had raised on his estate in Somersetshire. The animal was said to be particularly suited for a woman's gentle frame, and Marianne envisioned the two of them riding together across the rolling downs, feeling the wind in their hair and the sun on their faces.

Without a thought for the practicalities, Marianne had accepted the gift without hesitation, oblivious to the potential expenses that would arise from maintaining a horse. She brushed aside Elinor's concerns about the cost of an additional servant and a stable, convinced that their mother would never object to the arrangement. Besides, any horse would suffice for her brother John, who could easily obtain one from the nearby park.

Elinor tried to reason with her sister, cautioning her about the impropriety of accepting such a lavish gift from a man they barely knew. But Marianne was resolute in her defense of Willoughby, insisting that she had a deep understanding of his character, despite the short time they had spent together. According to her, it was not the length of acquaintance that mattered, but the depth of connection, and she felt an unshakeable bond with Willoughby.

Elinor recognized that further argument would only strengthen Marianne's resolve, so she chose to appeal to her sister's affection for their mother. By painting a picture of the inconvenience and expense that would burden their mother if she were to accept the gift, Marianne began to waver. Eventually, she agreed to decline the offer and inform Willoughby of her decision when he next visited.

When Willoughby arrived at the cottage later that day, Marianne spoke with him in hushed tones, explaining that she regretfully had to decline the gift. Elinor listened discreetly from the sidelines, taking note of the concern etched on Willoughby's face.
