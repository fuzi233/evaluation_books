The afternoon sunlight cast a warm glow over the Lebas family's drawing room, where Lady Violet sat amidst the soft rustle of silk and the faint scent of old books. Her fingers drummed a staccato rhythm on the armrest of her chair as she pondered the peculiar notion of love, a concept that seemed to both exhilarate and suffocate her at the same time. Like a firework bursting across the night sky, love was a fleeting spectacle, its radiance piercing and all-consuming, yet ultimately ephemeral. And yet, it was this very quality that made it so alluring – a tantalizing glimpse of transcendence, a whispered promise of the divine.

As she gazed out the window, watching the rain-soaked gardens below, Lady Violet's thoughts turned to her niece and nephew, Edwin and Angelina. Theirs was a love that seemed to burn with an intensity she couldn't quite understand – a flame that flickered brightly, only to dwindle and die out, leaving behind a residue of ashes and disappointment. She recalled the countless afternoons they'd spent together, the laughter and whispers, the stolen glances and secret smiles. And now, as the seasons passed, she watched with a mixture of sadness and resignation as their ardor cooled, their words growing strained, their gestures less tender.

But love, she mused, was not meant to be a lifelong companion, nor was it a reliable guide through life's twists and turns. No, love was a spark, a catalyst for growth and transformation, a reminder of the beauty and fragility of existence. It was a flame that, once kindled, could be fanned into a warm and comforting fire, one that could bring solace and warmth to those gathered 'round. And so, Lady Violet thought, it was not the grand passion of love that mattered, but the gentle, everyday affections that sustained us through the long, dark winters of life – the soft touch, the gentle word, the thoughtful deed. These were the things that could be cultivated, nurtured, and cherished, providing a steady, reliable warmth that never faltered, even in the face of adversity.

As the rain continued to fall outside, casting a melancholy spell over the Lebas household, Lady Violet rose from her chair, her movements slow and deliberate.
