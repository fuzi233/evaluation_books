As the sun dipped below the rooftops of Camden Town, casting a warm orange glow over the narrow streets, the confectioner's shop at the corner came alive with a kaleidoscope of colors. The sign above the door creaked gently in the evening breeze, beckoning passersby to enter and indulge in the sweet treats within. The shop's facade, adorned with mirrors and glittering lights, shimmered like a firework, drawing in the young and old alike. Among the crowd of children gathered outside, a young man stood out – a tall, burly figure with fiery red hair and a resolute face, yet a manner that seemed almost listless.

John Turnbull Angus, a skilled artist with a passion for social justice, stood transfixed by the shop's offerings. His flat, grey portfolio, filled with black-and-white sketches, hung loosely from his arm. Angus had found some success selling his art to publishers, but his uncle, an admiral, had disowned him for his radical views on economics. The experience had left Angus with a deep-seated desire to challenge the status quo.

Upon entering the shop, Angus approached the back room, a cozy pastry-cook restaurant where the air was thick with the scent of freshly baked bread. A young woman, Laura Hope, served behind the counter, her dark hair styled in a neat bob, her eyes sparkling with warmth. As Angus passed by, he doffed his hat, and Laura's gaze followed him into the inner room.

"I'd like a halfpenny bun and a small cup of black coffee, please," Angus requested, his voice steady and clear. Before Laura could respond, he added, "And I'd like to marry you."

Laura's expression froze, her dark eyes narrowing as she assessed the young man. "Jokes aren't welcome here," she said firmly, her voice a gentle rebuke.

Angus's eyes, a deep shade of grey, locked onto hers, and for a moment, they simply regarded each other. Then, with an air of quiet conviction, he spoke, "I assure you, my proposal is as sincere as the bun I've ordered. It's a matter of cost, just like the bun – one pays a price, and sometimes it's indigestible. But it's a risk worth taking."
