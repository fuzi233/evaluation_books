The air was heavy with the scent of dust and worn leather in the dimly lit room. Sally sat amidst the stacks, surrounded by volumes of forgotten melodies and half-forgotten composers. Her fingers danced across the keys of an old upright piano, coaxing out a melancholy tune that hung in the air like a mist. The music was a reflection of her mood – melancholic, introspective, and searching. Outside, the city hummed with the gentle thrum of traffic, a distant reminder of a world that moved without her. She played on, lost in the beauty of the notes, as the shadows cast by the fading light danced across the walls.

As she played, her thoughts turned to Ginger, now employed by Fillmore's theatrical enterprise. His duties were vague, but his gratitude was genuine, and Sally's heart swelled with pride. She had done it for him, secured a steady income and a sense of purpose in a city that often seemed devoid of either. But as she played on, the music began to take on a different tone, one of longing and wistfulness. The notes seemed to echo the emptiness she felt, the sense of disconnection that lingered despite the success that surrounded her.

Ginger's voice cut through her reverie, his footsteps heavy on the creaking floorboards as he carried a massive easy chair towards the room. "And what I mean to say is," he said, his words punctuated by the scraping of wood on wood, "if I didn't sweat about a bit and help you after the way you got me that job..." Sally's fingers stilled on the keys, and she smiled wryly. "Ginger, desist," she said, her voice low and husky.

The chair scraped to a halt beside her, and Ginger paused, his face creased in a mixture of concern and amusement. "Yes, but honestly..." he began, but Sally's eyes narrowed, and he fell silent. The air was thick with the weight of unspoken emotions, the unspoken understanding that Ginger's gratitude was a fragile thing, easily shattered by the slightest misstep.

As the silence stretched out, Sally's gaze drifted back to the piano, her fingers hovering above the keys. The music had stopped, but the notes still lingered, suspended in the air like a promise.
