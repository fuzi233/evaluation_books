The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the cloister, mingling with the scent of damp stone and old wood as Gerard moved mechanically, performing the last rites for his dear friend. His hands moved with practiced ease, the familiar motions a comfort in the midst of grief. Yet, as he finished, he felt a numbness wash over him, a sense of disconnection from the world around him.

In the kitchen, the sisters of the convent bustled about, preparing a feast in honor of the departed. Gerard's thoughts turned to the traditional funeral meal, one that would bring comfort to the mourners and sustain them through their time of sorrow. He envisioned the tender flavors of his mother's recipe for boerenkoolstamppot, a hearty dish of boiled kale and potatoes, served with a rich gravy.

As the sun began to set, a covered cart, its wooden slats worn smooth by years of use, was loaded with a coffin and a small, delicate wooden box containing Gerard's wife's favorite spices. A single horse, its coat gleaming in the fading light, was harnessed to the cart, and Gerard climbed aboard, his eyes fixed on the box. The cart's driver, a gruff but kind man, spoke softly to the horse as they set off towards Gouda, the sound of his voice a gentle accompaniment to Gerard's grief.

That night, as the cart stopped at an inn, the driver and the landlord attempted to coax Gerard inside, offering him warm bread and a hot meal. But Gerard pushed them away, his fingers clenched around the wooden box, his mind fixed on the spices that had been his wife's passion. The driver and the landlord exchanged a sympathetic glance, and the driver whispered something to the landlord, who nodded and retreated, leaving Gerard to his vigil.

The next morning, the cart arrived at the manse, where a group of parishioners had gathered to pay their respects. They gasped in shock at the sight of Gerard, his face pale and drawn, his eyes red-rimmed from lack of sleep. As they approached, Gerard's gaze focused on them, and he opened his mouth to speak, but only a faint cry escaped, a plea that was lost in the din of their collective grief.
