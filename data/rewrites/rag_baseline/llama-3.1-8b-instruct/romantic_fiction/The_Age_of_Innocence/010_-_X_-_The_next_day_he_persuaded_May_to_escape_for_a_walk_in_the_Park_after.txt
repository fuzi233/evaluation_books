The park was a frozen tableau, the snow-laden branches of the trees etched against a sky of faded blue. May's laughter floated through the stillness, a delicate melody that accompanied the crunch of snow beneath their feet. Newland Archer walked beside her, his eyes drawn to the delicate shape of her profile, the way the cold air seemed to heighten the pink of her cheeks. He felt a sense of ownership, a possessiveness that bordered on discomfort.

"I'm glad you like the lilies," he said, his voice low and even. "I remember you mentioned they came late yesterday." 

May's eyes sparkled with pleasure. "They always make me think of you, and the thought of you sending them is even sweeter than the flowers themselves." Her words were a gentle reminder of the rituals that governed their relationship, the polite gestures that underscored the quiet understanding between them.

As they strolled, Newland's thoughts drifted to Madame Olenska, whose visit earlier that day had left him feeling restless. He recalled the bouquet of yellow roses he had sent her, the thought of which now seemed awkward in the light of his conversation with May. He had meant to tell her about the roses, but the silence had hung between them like a challenge, a reminder of the secrets they kept from one another.

Their footsteps crunched through the snow, the only sound in the stillness. Newland's mind wandered back to his conversation with May, the way she parroted the phrases he used, the way her words seemed to echo his own thoughts. He felt a pang of unease, a sense that he was trapped in a cycle of expectations, a dance of social niceties that stifled genuine connection.

As they paused at a bend in the path, May turned to him with a radiant smile. "I'd love to travel with you," she said, her eyes shining with excitement. Newland's heart skipped a beat, but his response was tinged with doubt. He knew that her mother would resist such a notion, that the very idea of deviating from convention would be met with disapproval.

"We could be different," he said, the words feeling forced, like a recitation. May's face lit up, but he saw the faintest glimmer of uncertainty in her eyes. He wondered if she truly understood the implications of his words, if she grasped the depth of his desire to break free from the constraints that bound them.
