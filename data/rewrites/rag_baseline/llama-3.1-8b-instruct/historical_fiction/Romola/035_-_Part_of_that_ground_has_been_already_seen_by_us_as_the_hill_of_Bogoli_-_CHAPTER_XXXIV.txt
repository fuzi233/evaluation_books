No Place for Repentance.

The scent of woodsmoke and simmering ragù wafted through the narrow streets of Florence as Messer Naldo arrived on the evening of the twenty-eighth of November. Only eleven days had passed since his previous visit, a testament to his unyielding dedication to the hill of San Giorgio. A visit to the local panificio, where the warm aroma of freshly baked bread mingled with the evening air, led him to the Via de' Bardi, where a pivotal scene was about to unfold.

Tito's decision to provide for Tessa, a year and a half prior, had been a gesture born of genuine kindness, rather than a calculated move to further his own interests. His feelings towards her were paternal and gentle, though they lacked the intensity he experienced when in the presence of Romola. The recollection of her long, dark tresses sweeping across his cheek still resonated within him, a tactile memory that vibrated through his very being. His fibre was refined, his intellect sharp, and he was not one to succumb to base desires. All his youthful ardor had been ignited by Romola, and he felt an insurmountable obstacle between him and the truth – the web of deception he had spun around himself and Tessa.

As he navigated the cobblestone streets, Tito's thoughts were a jumble of self-justification and trepidation. He had convinced himself that his actions towards Tessa were motivated by kindness, rather than a desire to maintain the secrecy surrounding his true feelings for Romola. Every strong emotion forged its own moral compass, and Tito's devotion to Romola demanded a certain level of integrity. Though he had grown accustomed to living a double life, the weight of his secret began to bear down upon him.

In the days following his betrothal to Romola, Tito found solace in the idea that Tessa remained under the illusion of their marriage. His relationship with Monna Lisa, Tessa's caretaker, was one of convenience, with Tito paying her handsomely to keep her silent about the circumstances surrounding their union. Monna Lisa, deaf and reclusive, posed no threat to the secrecy of Tito's arrangement, and Tessa, though fond of her benefactor, had been thoroughly intimidated into silence. The delicate balance of their situation was one that Tito struggled to maintain, ever aware of the consequences should the truth come to light.
