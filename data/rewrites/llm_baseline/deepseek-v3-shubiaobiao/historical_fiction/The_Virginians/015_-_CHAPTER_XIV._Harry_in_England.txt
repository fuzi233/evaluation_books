When the young Virginian recounted his adventures to his aunt, Madame Bernstein, her stern heart softened in spite of itself. Though hardened by years of solitude and disappointment, she found herself strangely moved by the boy’s earnest simplicity. His voice, his manner—so unlike the polished artifice of the society she knew—stirred something long buried within her.

She listened as he spoke of his mother’s fierce protectiveness, of his own youthful jealousy toward Colonel Washington, of the vast forests and rivers of his homeland. And though she scoffed inwardly at sentiment, she could not deny the warmth that spread through her as he spoke.

*"If only I had had a son like him,"* she thought bitterly. *"But no—my life was not meant for such tenderness."*

Yet, for now, she allowed herself the indulgence.

Meanwhile, the Castlewood family received their colonial cousin with varying degrees of enthusiasm. Lady Castlewood, ever the gracious hostess, masked her reservations behind honeyed words.

*"How charming he is!"* she exclaimed to Madame Bernstein. *"So refreshingly unspoiled! One wonders how such manners could flourish in the wilderness."*

*"His mother was a woman of rare breeding,"* Madame Bernstein replied coolly. *"And his stepfather, Colonel Esmond, was a gentleman of the old school."*

*"Ah, but breeding alone does not make a man,"* Lady Castlewood sighed, casting a meaningful glance toward her daughters. *"One must have heart as well. And I confess, I live only for my children."* She clasped her hands dramatically, her bracelets tinkling. *"Should anything befall them, I should perish!"*

The young ladies, Fanny and Maria, were less guarded in their interest. Fanny, in particular, seemed eager to instruct Harry in the fashionable dances of the day, much to her brother William’s visible irritation.

*"Infernal savage!"* William muttered under his breath. *"Does he mean to turn Fan’s head?"*

Lady Castlewood, ever watchful, was quick to intervene.

*"Fanny, my dear,"* she purred, *"you must not monopolize our cousin. Surely Maria would like a turn as well?"*

Maria, seated at the harpsichord, played on with forced cheerfulness, while Harry—awkward but eager—attempted the steps under Fanny’s guidance.

*"I fear I am a poor student,"* Harry admitted with a sheepish grin.

*"Nonsense!"* Fanny laughed. *"You will learn quickly enough."*

But later, in private, Lady Castlewood’s tone sharpened.

*"Have you lost your senses?"* she hissed at her daughter. *"Encouraging that provincial boy? Do you imagine he is a suitable match for an earl’s daughter?"*

*"He is our cousin!"* Fanny protested.

*"Cousin or not, he is nobody!"* Lady Castlewood snapped. *"A backwoods upstart with no prospects. If you persist in this folly, I shall take you back to Kensington at once!"*

Fanny’s defiance wilted under her mother’s glare.

Meanwhile, Harry remained blissfully unaware of the undercurrents swirling around him. He rode with William, played cards with Lord Castlewood, and basked in the warmth of his newfound family.

For now, at least, he was welcome.

But in the grand halls of Castlewood, where ambition and deception walked hand in hand, his presence was already stirring whispers—and not all of them kind.
