Beyond the trees, a faint afterglow clung to the western horizon, casting a warm glow over the rolling hills, while a new moon hung low in the sky like a silver crescent above the old barn. Sally stepped out onto the porch, her eyes drinking in the heady scents of the evening, and felt a deep sense of contentment wash over her.

The shadows deepened, slowly swallowing the light, until the world was bathed in a soft, velvety darkness. The stars began to twinkle in the sky, like diamonds scattered across the fabric of the universe. In the distance, the occasional bark of a dog broke the stillness, and Toto, the small white dog, who had been quietly observing Sally from the sidelines, let out a high-pitched squeak, as if protesting the sudden commotion.

Ginger's footsteps echoed down the road, growing louder with each passing moment, and Sally's heart swelled with anticipation as she caught sight of her beloved in the distance. As he approached, she called out to him, her voice carrying across the lawn.

"Ginger!"

"What ho!" he replied, his voice cheerful and carefree.

Toto, sensing the excitement, darted down the drive, his tail wagging furiously as he bounded towards Ginger, only to be met with a cold greeting. Ginger, though he loved animals, had never taken to Toto, whom he had rescued from a life of idleness at Mrs. Meecher's. "It's a wonder you've managed to keep him from turning into a sloth," he said, as he eluded Toto's eager leaps.

"He's changed, Ginger," Sally said, smiling at the transformation. "He used to be so slow and sedate."

"A bit of dietary discipline did the trick," Ginger said dryly. "We'll be shipping him back to Mrs. Meecher soon enough."

"I'll miss him," Sally said, her voice tinged with sadness.

"Nearly missed him myself this morning," Ginger said, grinning mischievously. "He was helping himself to the bacon, and I had to take drastic action."
