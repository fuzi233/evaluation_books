As the ages passed, the once-venerable institutions of Flatland began to crumble. The Art of Sight Recognition, once the cornerstone of intellectual pursuits, slowly lost its relevance and was all but forgotten. The universities, once bastions of learning, saw the studies of Geometry, Statics, and Kinetics dwindle in importance, until they were relegated to the fringes of society. The Elementary Schools, too, suffered a similar fate, as the inferior Art of Feeling fell out of favor.

Meanwhile, the Isosceles classes, emboldened by their newfound freedom from the burdens of education, grew more numerous and insolent. They saw no need to contribute to the system that had once tamed their rough edges and kept their numbers in check. As a result, they began to assert their independence, and with it, their right to equal status with the Polygons.

The Soldiers and Artisans, who had long been relegated to the lower rungs of society, saw an opportunity to level the playing field. They argued that, with the advent of Colour Recognition, the distinction between themselves and the higher classes was no longer valid. They demanded that the government recognize their equality and grant them the same privileges as the Polygons.

The Revolution gained momentum, and soon the people began to clamor for a new law – the Universal Colour Bill. This bill proposed that every individual, regardless of class or station, be required to submit to a new form of identification. Priests and Women, who had traditionally been exempt from the usual tributes to education, would be included in this new requirement.

The Bill proposed that every Woman be painted with a distinctive coloration – the front half of her body, containing her eye and mouth, would be colored red, while the hind half would be green. Priests would be painted in a similar manner, with the semicircle containing their eye and mouth colored red, and the hind semicircle colored green. The reasoning behind this proposal was that this would create a visual distinction between the sexes, making it easier to identify each group.

However, not everyone was convinced of the wisdom of this plan. Some saw it as a clever ruse, designed to bring the Women into the fold of the Revolution.
