For in his lifetime, Manderson had amassed a vast fortune without cultivating a single devoted friend or leaving behind a deed that would immortalize his name.

Upon hearing the news, those entrenched in the frenetic whirlwind of commerce perceived the earth tremble beneath their feet. In the annals of America's storied financial history, no figure had cast a longer shadow than Manderson's. His influence had etched a unique niche within the bastions of trade, where titans of industry, their might bolstering the forces of capital, had long held sway. Yet, unlike these behemoths, Manderson's legend had been inextricably linked to a romantic aura, a quality cherished by his compatriots, which seemed incongruous amidst his reputation as the unyielding guardian of stability.
