The morning sunlight struggled to penetrate the dusty panes of the Durban library, casting a warm glow on the worn wooden floorboards beneath Lady Violet Lebas's chair. Her slender fingers drummed a staccato rhythm on the armrest as she pondered the peculiar notion that had taken hold of her: writing a history of her life's most extraordinary exploits. It was a prospect both thrilling and daunting, rather like navigating the treacherous sand dunes of the Natal coast – she'd lost count of the number of times she'd gotten lost in those shifting expanses. As a seasoned explorer and trader, Lady Violet had lived a life marked by moments of breathtaking beauty and unflinching danger, and yet, she couldn't shake the feeling that it was only through sharing these stories that she might finally grasp their significance.

As she sat amidst the musty silence of the library, surrounded by tomes bound in cracked leather and yellowed parchment, Lady Violet's thoughts turned to her nephew, Harry, a bright young doctor struggling to find meaning in the sterile halls of a London hospital. His letters, full of wit and curiosity, had kept her company during the long, dark nights spent watching the stars wheel above the African plains. She hoped that, by recording her adventures, she might create something that would captivate his imagination and offer a respite from the monotony of his duties.

But why, she wondered, had she decided to take up the pen? Was it merely a whim, a passing fancy born of idleness and a desire to relive the thrill of her past exploits? Or was it something more profound, a need to confront the demons that had driven her to seek out the unknown in the first place? The questions swirled in her mind like the dust devils that danced across the veldt, their answers as elusive as the wisps of cloud that clung to the distant mountains.
