To grasp the intricacies of Paul Oleron's existence, one must first immerse oneself in the culinary landscape of his life. At the tender age of forty-four, he had devoted himself to the art of writing, yet he rarely paused to reflect on the disparity between his ambitions and the reality of his circumstances. His daily routine was a symphony of precision, a testament to the methodical nature of his being. Every morning, the stout Welsh woman, Mrs. Barrett, would arrive at his doorstep with a steaming basket of freshly baked bread, her Merionethshire accent a warm reminder of the rolling hills and verdant valleys of her homeland. As he sipped his coffee, the aroma wafting from the kitchen would transport him to a world where the scent of roasting meats and baking spices filled the air.

As he moved about his new abode, arranging and rearranging the furniture to suit his needs, Oleron's thoughts turned to the forthcoming publication of his magnum opus, Romilly Bishop. The manuscript had taken on a life of its own, and he was determined to allow it to unfold at its own pace. In the cramped workroom he had left behind, the words had begun to flow effortlessly, like a rich sauce simmering on the stovetop. The flavors of his imagination mingled with the musty smell of old books and the faint tang of wood smoke, transporting him to a realm where creativity knew no bounds.

In the kitchen, the sunlight streaming through the window highlighted the rustic charm of the old powder-closet, now repurposed as a larder. The shelves, once used to store the elaborate wigs of a bygone era, now held an assortment of spices, herbs, and preserves, their colors and textures a testament to Oleron's growing passion for the culinary arts. As he rummaged through the closet, his fingers stumbled upon a pair of mushroom-shaped wooden wig-stands, their intricate carvings a testament to the craftsmanship of a bygone era. He pondered the mystery of their presence, wondering how they had come to be nestled among the shelves, awaiting discovery. It was a small, enigmatic treasure, much like the secrets hidden within the pages of his manuscript, waiting to be unearthed.
