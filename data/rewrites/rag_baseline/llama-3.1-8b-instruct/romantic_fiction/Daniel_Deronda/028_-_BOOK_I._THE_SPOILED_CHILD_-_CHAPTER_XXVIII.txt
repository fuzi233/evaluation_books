As twilight descended upon the rectory, casting a warm, honey-golden light through the windows, the family gathered around Gwendolen, their faces aglow with a mix of curiosity and expectation. Mrs. Gascoigne, ever the consummate hostess, poured steaming cups of tea, while her husband, the rector, leaned forward in his chair, his eyes shining with a benevolent intensity.

"My dear Gwendolen," he began, his voice like a gentle brook, "I must say, I am heartened by the news of your engagement. Your attachment to Mr. Grandcourt seems strong, and I daresay, a lifelong union is a solemn thing indeed." He paused, allowing his words to settle, like autumn leaves on a pond, before continuing. "And yet, I believe our recent trials have given us a glimpse of Mr. Grandcourt's character. His willingness to provide for your mother, should she need it, speaks well of his liberality."

Mrs. Gascoigne nodded in agreement, her fingers fluttering across the intricate lace of her gloves as she spoke. "Indeed, dear Gwendolen, I have no doubt that Mr. Grandcourt would have offered for you even sooner, had you not chosen to travel abroad. But now, with the benefit of hindsight, we see that his perseverance is a quality to be cherished."

Gwendolen's response was measured, her voice a delicate balance of gravity and wit. "I'm not entirely convinced, Aunt," she said, her eyes sparkling with a hint of mischief. "I'm still trying to understand what's proper, in this situation." Her words hung in the air, like a question mark, as the others waited for her to elaborate.

The rector's smile, a gentle, paternal beam, reassured her that she was not to be scolded. Anna, seated beside her, offered a tender kiss on the cheek, her eyes brimming with unshed tears. For in the days leading up to this moment, she had harbored a secret hope – a hope that Gwendolen's heart might soften, and that she and Rex might find a way to be together. But now, as she gazed upon her sister's resolute face, she knew that hope had been misplaced.
