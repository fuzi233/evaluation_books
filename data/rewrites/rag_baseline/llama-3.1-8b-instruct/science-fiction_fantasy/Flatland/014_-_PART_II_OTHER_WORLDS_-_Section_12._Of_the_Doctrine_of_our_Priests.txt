In the somber, honey-hued light of the library, Lady Violet Lebas's gaze drifted across the pages of the letter from Mr. Andrew Lang, her mind wandering to the peculiarities of the world beyond the confines of her estate. The rain lashed against the leaded glass, casting a rhythmic pattern on the floorboards as she pondered the notion that Configuration might hold the key to understanding the intricacies of human nature. Her thoughts turned to the doctrine of the Circles, an ideology that had been ingrained in the culture since the time of Pantocyclus, the illustrious Circle who had quelled the Colour Revolt.

According to the doctrine, all aspects of life – politics, morality, and even personal relationships – were reduced to a single, all-encompassing principle: attend to your Configuration. Every individual's shape and form determined their very essence, and thus their actions. A being born with irregular sides or angles was doomed to fail, unless they sought correction at one of the Regular Hospitals. The consequences of deviating from perfection were dire: imprisonment or execution by the angle of the State Executioner.

Lady Violet's mind strayed to her own grandson, a mischievous Hexagon prone to disobedience. When he claimed that a sudden change in temperature had left him unable to control his actions, she found herself torn between her duty to uphold the doctrine and her maternal instincts. While theoretically, Configuration was the sole determining factor in a being's behavior, in practice, it was far more complicated. In the face of minor transgressions, the strictures of the doctrine seemed to falter. She recalled instances where the highest Circles, even those who sat as judges, employed praise and blame in their interactions, as if the very concept of right and wrong held sway over the irregularities of human nature.

As she read on, Lady Violet's thoughts turned to the implications of this paradox. If Configuration was the sole arbiter of a being's fate, then what place did free will hold in the grand tapestry of existence? Was it possible that the Circles' rigid adherence to their doctrine had led them down a path of intellectual dishonesty, where they spoke of right and wrong as if they were tangible, malleable forces, rather than mere abstractions? The rain continued to fall outside, its rhythmic beat a reminder of the world's indifference to the quiet struggles of its women.
