Into the Night Land

The destruction that had fallen upon the Ten-Thousand left no room for further aid, and the fresh terror of the Night Land settled heavily upon us. Those youths who now ventured onto the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk were far beyond our reach. We considered signaling them with the Home-Call, the great Voice emanating from the Machine atop the sealed base of the Mighty Pyramid, but we dared not. To do so would alert the Monsters of that Land that others were still abroad from the Pyramid. We could only hope, with trembling faith, that the Evil Forces remained ignorant of their presence, for no mortal could truly know the extent of the Powers' knowledge or ignorance.

Yet, a strange and quiet Influence lingered in the Land, recorded by our instruments but felt more deeply in the spirit. We believed it originated from the House of Silence, a grim structure on a low hill to the north of the Great Road. Many Monstruwacans feared it targeted the youths, but certainty was impossible; we could only wait and watch. Soon, the youths drew near the road's northern bend, approaching that horrid house. The Influence in the Land grew stronger, and I felt assured it came from the House, though proof remained elusive. I shared this feeling with the Master Monstruwacan, who trusted my judgment and shared my belief in the secret power radiating from the House of Silence.

Some urged sending the Home-Call to warn the youths and urge their return, but the Master refused. It was not meet to endanger their souls until we knew they were lost without intervention. The Home-Call was a voice of immense power, capable of echoing across the world, a relic of the olden days when great flying-ships traversed the sky. Its use would immediately reveal our location and the plight of the youths to the entire Land.

Day and night passed, and countless eyes peered into the Night Land, watching the youths hasten toward their fate. Talk was rampant—foolish, kind, yet devoid of courage to attempt a rescue. The Land itself seemed awake, unquiet, filled with a sense of impending doom and low, distant roars. I realized I had omitted earlier accounts of these sounds, a failure in my telling that I now acknowledge.

During this time, the youths slept not, ate but once, and pressed onward at a woeful speed toward the North along the Great Dismal Road. This confirmed our fears: they were under a spell from the House of Silence. Suddenly, a Monstruwacan reported a mighty Influence entering the Land. Simultaneously, through the Great Spy-Glass, I saw the youths break from the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, running with desperate speed toward the House of Silence.

The Master Monstruwacan hesitated not. He sent the Home-Call across the world, pleading with the doomed youths to resist the force drawing them to destruction. He warned them that if they could not overcome the pull, they must slay themselves before entering the House. Immediately, a counter-force surged from the Pyramid, born of the prayers and soul-wishings of millions. A vast spiritual noise filled the night, stunning my spirit with its power. Tremblingly, I watched through the Spy-Glass as the youths stopped, gathered in a confused crowd, as if waking from a nightmare to find themselves in a strange place.

A great roar rose from the embrasures—five hundred thousand voices shouting in triumph. Yet, it was premature. The combined will of the millions broke against the Evil Force from the House, which drew the youths back to their running. The Pyramid fell into shaken silence, followed by lamentation. Then, a fresh wonder occurred: billows of mist, like pure white fire shining with a chilling light, appeared before the youths. This mist of cold fire halted their progress, a manifestation of the sweet Powers of Goodness striving to protect them. All the millions witnessed this, some with clarity, others doubtfully, yet all advanced in spiritual sight beyond the norm of their age.

Though none possessed the Night-Hearing to hear a soul's speech across the world, I did. A Monstruwacan reported that the Influence ceased working on the instruments, confirming that the Evil Force from the House was cut off and that a mighty Power fought for the youths' salvation. Hope and fear stirred the people, sensing a chance for return.

While the youths wavered, the Master Monstruwacan sent the Home-Call again, beseeching them to come home while the protective power shielded them. Some looked toward the Pyramid, reading the message of love from their mothers. But in a moment, they turned, obedient to a leader named Aschoff, a great athlete of the Nine-Hundredth-City. Unwittingly, Aschoff destroyed their souls; he leaped into the billows of bright, shining fire that formed the barrier. The fire sank into nothingness, and Aschoff, followed faithfully by all, resumed their run toward the House of Silence.

They reached the low hill and the great open doorway, which had been open since the Beginning, through which the cold, steadfast light and inscrutable silence of Evil had made a silence felt across the Land. Uncased windows emitted the utter silence of unholy desolation. Aschoff ran in, followed by the two hundred and fifty wholesome, innocent youths. They never emerged, unseen by any human thereafter.

Mothers and Fathers watched from the Redoubt, seeing the tragedy unfold. Though some hoped for their return, everyone knew in their hearts the young men had gone to Destruction. The night spoke horror to all souls, and a sudden, utter quiet fell upon the Land. To me, who possessed the Night-Hearing, came a great Fear—not of sound, but of the agony whispered into my spirit by the Quietness of the night. No sound reached my ears, for the youths had passed into a Silence the heart cannot conceive.

This strange Quiet, brooding within the night, was horrid beyond any roaring that had passed before. It offered my spirit no rest compared to the far-echoing thunder of the Great Laughter, the whining from the Silver-fire Holes, the Baying of the Hounds, or the Roaring of the Giants. These dreadful sounds could not offend me as this silence did.

No one thought they could succour the Peoples of the Lesser Redoubt, nor knew where it stood. Thus, they must suffer and come to their end alone, a sad and dreadful thought. The Great Pyramid had already suffered sorrow and calamity from previous attempts, bringing only failure and grief to mothers and wives. Now, this dread horror concerned the lost youths.

The sure knowledge that we could offer no aid weighed heavily on my heart. I had foolishly held vague hopes of secretly exploring the Night to discover the Lesser Pyramid and rescue the thousands. Above all, I dreamed of stepping out of the mystery and terror to put forth my arms to Naani, saying, "I am That One." Knowing she, who had been mine in a bygone Eternity, would surely recognize me instantly and come to me again. To think she might be suffering horror in the power of some foul Monster was like madness. Nearly, I could seize the Diskos and run unprepared into the evil and terror of the Night Land to find her or cast off my life in the attempt.

Often, I called to Naani, sending the Master-Word beating through the night to assure her it was indeed I speaking, and no foul thing spelling evil and lies. I instructed her never to be tempted from the shelter of the Redoubt by messages from the night, but to await the Master-Word, knowing no true friend would seek to entice her into the dark. I spoke with her via brain-elements, sending words silently into the dark. It was doleful and weariful to speak into the void without hearing the answering beat of the Master-Word or the sweet, faint voice whispering within my soul. Yet, once or twice, the aether thrilled weakly around me, and to my inward hearing, the Master-Word beat faintly in the night, offering a little comfort that the love-maid of my memory-dreams still lived.

Constantly putting forth my soul to harken, my health failed. I chided my being for lacking wiser control, fighting to remain sane. Day by day, my heart grew more weary and restless; life seemed a small matter against the great loss my heart felt. Often, a Voice spoke plainly out of the night, purporting to be Naani's. I always replied with the Master-Word, silencing it. I did not jeer at its failure to bewitch me but let it bide. The Voice would fall silent, then call again, but I never spoke with it, for therein lay danger to the soul. Instead, I shut it from my memory, thinking only of sweet and holy matters, often of Naani, which was both sweet and holy to my spirit.

Thus, Monsters without in the Night tormented me, perhaps intending to lure me to destruction or simply plaguing me with malice. Considering my trouble and giving my strength to Naani through the night worked upon me; I grew thin, plainly visible to those who loved me. The Master Monstruwacan, who loved me as a son, chided me gently with wise speech, making me love him the more, yet I gained no health. My heart destroyed me, as love held back and made always to weep does.

It may seem strange that my Mother and Father did not talk with me, but I had neither parent for many years—a fact I should have noted earlier so none would waste thought pondering it. Blame lies with my telling.

Concerning my love-trouble, a certain thing happened that gave me resolve. One night, I woke from a sorely troubled sleep, and it seemed Naani called my olden love name in a voice of utter anguish and beseeching. I sat up, sent the Master-Word into the Night with my brain-elements, and presently there was the solemn beat of the Master-Word answering, weak and faint, scarce audible. I called again to Naani, who was Mirdath, giving her assurance and urging her to tell me what was wrong and pitiful. Who shall be amazed that I was shaken with the eagerness of my spirit, for it had been so long since Naani spoke clearly within my soul?

Yet, though I called many times to the everlasting night, no more came the voice of Naani speaking strangely within my spirit; only at times a weak thrilling of the aether about me. At last, maddened with sorrow and the sense of harm about the maid, I stood upright, raised my hands, and gave word and honor to Naani through all the blackness of the night. I declared I would no longer abide within the Mighty Pyramid for my safety while she, who had been mine Own through Eternity, came to horror and destruction by the Beasts and Evil Powers of that Dark World. I gave the word with my brain-elements, bidding her be of heart, for until I died I would seek her. Out of the Darkness came naught but silence.

I clothed myself swiftly and went up quickly to the Tower of Observation to speak instantly with the Master Monstruwacan. My heart burned with intention to act speedily. I told him everything, declaring I meant no longer to suffer in quiet and to no end, but to make adventure into the Night Land to find Naani or perhaps find a swift peace from my long troubling.

When the Master Monstruwacan heard my words, it sat heavily upon him. He besought me long and many times to refrain, stating that none might achieve such a task and that I would be lost in my youth before many days passed. Yet to all his speech I said naught, save that this thing was laid upon me, and even as I had promised, so should I act. In the end, the Master perceived I was set to this thing and not to be moved. He pointed out my leanness due to troubling and advised wisdom to wait and regain full strength.

But even as I was, so would I go. I told him gently, showing how it was meet and helpful to the safety of my soul. My strength was still in me, yet I was sweeter in spirit because I stood lean and pure; much poor dross and littleness had been burned from me, so that fear was not in me. All I laid to the count of my love, which purifies and makes sweet and fearless the human heart. Because I was as I had said, I was less in trouble of the Forces of Evil, for long and sore had been my Preparation of Spirit. I knew none had ever gone forth into the Darkness so long withholden from that which weakens and taints the spirit.

Here, let me set down how the Three Days of Preparation, proper to those willing to go forth into the Night Land, had for their chief aim the cleansing of the spirit so that the Powers of Evil had less ability to harm. Also, as I have said, none should go forth in ignorance of the full dreadfulness of all that held the Night. During the Preparation, certain horrors were made known that were not told to the young—horrid mutilations and abasements of the soul that shook the heart with fear if whispered into the hearing. These things were not set down in any book easily come by but were warded and safely locked by the Master of the Preparation in the Room of Preparation.

Indeed, when I heard what I was soon to hear, I wondered in my heart that ever any went out into the Night Land, or that the Room of Preparation should have other than students who meant not to go forth but only to achieve knowledge of what had been done and might be done again. Yet, in verity, this is the way of the human heart; it has always been and will be so in all the years forever. For to adventure is the lust of Youth, and to leave Safety is the natural waywardness of the spirit. Who shall reprove or regret? It would be sorrowful if the Spirit of Man should cease. Yet, it must not be thought that I uphold fightings to the death or mutilation between man and man; rather, I sorrow upon this thought.

When the morrow came, I went unto the Room of Preparation. The Door closed upon me, and I underwent the Full Preparation to have full power and aid to come to success through all the terror of the Night Land.
