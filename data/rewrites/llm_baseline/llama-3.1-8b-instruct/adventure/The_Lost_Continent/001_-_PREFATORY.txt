As the first light of dawn crept over the rugged landscape, I stretched my stiff limbs, grateful for the gentle warmth that banished the chill of the dew-kissed air. My companion, Dr. Coppinger, was already engaged in a series of limbering exercises, using the rough, bristly lava rocks to loosen his joints. A man of boundless energy and insatiable curiosity, he had spent years honing his skills in the field, earning degrees in various disciplines, including medicine. His latest obsession was the study of ancient civilizations, and I had joined him on this expedition to explore the lost continent of the Guanches.

After a refreshing wash in the small stream at the bottom of the barranca, we sat down to a meager breakfast, our toothbrushes a welcome luxury in the midst of this rugged terrain. "We're running low on provisions," Coppinger remarked, his eyes scanning the sparse remains of our rations. "And this Spanish newspaper wrapping isn't doing much to preserve them."

I chuckled, shaking my head. "Your portion consists mainly of tobacco ashes."

Coppinger nodded grimly. "It'll only get worse if we don't find more food soon. We've got a long, arduous climb ahead of us."

Indeed, the trail to the fonda at Santa Brigida, where we had cached our gear, lay some ten miles distant. Coppinger was determined to collect as much data as possible before we departed, and I knew that our next meal would be a welcome respite from the rigors of our journey. As we sat by the stream, eating our meager repast, I couldn't help but think of the fine wines we might enjoy in the mountain villages, if only we could secure them with a bit of diplomacy.

The two mummies we had discovered so far were a paltry catch, their fragile goat skins crumbling to dust at the slightest touch. Yet Coppinger was undeterred, convinced that we were on the cusp of a major discovery – perhaps an ancient university or sacred site, akin to the one on the opposite side of the island. He would not rest until he had explored every cave in the area, his flashlight and camera at the ready. With a spring in his step, he grasped his trusty crowbar, and I shouldered the rope, preparing to tackle the treacherous ascent to the cliff's ridge, where the sun had scorched us the previous day.
