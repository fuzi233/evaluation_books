As Newland Archer sat down to dinner with his mother and sister, Janey's announcement caught him off guard. "Your cousin the Countess called on Mother while you were away," she said with a mischievous glint in her eye.

Newland raised an eyebrow, intrigued, and met his mother's gaze, which was fixed intently on her plate. Mrs. Archer's reserve was legendary, and Newland suspected she might be hiding something beneath her tranquil surface. "She had on a stunning black velvet polonaise with jet buttons and a tiny green monkey muff," Janey continued. "I've never seen her look more stylishly dressed."

Madame Olenska had arrived unaccompanied, early on Sunday afternoon, when the fire was already lit in the drawing room. She carried a new card-case, one that Newland hadn't seen before. "She said she wanted to see us because you'd been so kind to her," Janey said, her voice laced with a hint of curiosity.

Newland chuckled. "Madame Olenska always has a way of making friends sound grateful." He glanced at his mother, who seemed to be studying her hands rather than meeting his gaze. "I'm sure she's happy to be back among her own people again."

"Yes, so she told us," Mrs. Archer said dryly. "But I must admit, she does seem... pleased to be here." A hint of disapproval crept into her voice, and Janey picked up the thread, her eyes sparkling with amusement. "Mother doesn't think her simple, do you, Mother?"

Mrs. Archer's lips pursed in distaste. "It's just my old-fashioned sensibilities, dear May is my ideal." Newland watched his mother, sensing a deeper unease beneath her words.

The next day, Archer paid a visit to old Mrs. Mingott, carrying several messages from his family. She received him warmly, her gratitude evident as he recounted his efforts to persuade Madame Olenska to abandon her plans for a divorce. As he spoke of his hasty departure from work and his desire to see May, Mrs. Mingott let out a throaty chuckle and patted his knee with her plump hand.
