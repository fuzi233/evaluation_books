The monarch, lost in the sublime ecstasy of his auditory universe, seemed oblivious to the absurdity surrounding him. Determined to drag him back from this metaphysical trance to the gritty reality of geometry, I seized the moment. "Your Majesty," I began, my voice steady despite the trembling of my soul, "how do Your Royal Highness distinguish the forms and alignments of His Subjects? Before entering this realm, I perceived through the sense of sight—long before my arrival—that some citizens are mere Lines, while others are Points, varying greatly in magnitude."

"You speak an impossibility!" the King interrupted, his tone sharp as a shard of glass. "You must be hallucinating. To discern the difference between a Line and a Point visually is, by the laws of Nature, a contradiction. However, it is readily achieved through the sense of Hearing. By this same faculty, my exact shape is revealed. Behold me—I am a Line, the longest in Lineland, stretching over six inches of Space."

"Length," I ventured gently.

"Fool!" he roared. "Space *is* Length. Cease your interruptions, or I shall silence you forever."

Apologizing profusely, I waited for him to resume. He did, with a sneer of supreme condescension. "Since you are immune to reason, I shall demonstrate through sound alone how my dual voices reveal my form to my Wives, currently stationed six thousand miles, seventy yards, two feet, and eight inches away—one to the North, one to the South. Listen well."

He emitted a high-pitched chirp, followed immediately by a second, slightly delayed note. "As my wives receive the first voice, followed closely by the second, they calculate the interval. Knowing sound traverses 6.457 inches in that specific gap, they deduce that one mouth is 6.457 inches farther from them than the other. Thus, they know my shape precisely. Do not imagine they perform this calculus constantly; they calculated it once, prior to our union. Yet, they possess the capacity to do so at any instant. Similarly, I can gauge the shape of any male subject via sound."

"But consider this," I pressed, sensing a crack in his logic, "if a Man mimics a Woman's voice with one of his tones, or masks the echo of his Southern voice so thoroughly it becomes unrecognizable as a reflection of his Northern voice, does not this deception cause chaos? Have you no mechanism to verify such frauds by ordering neighboring subjects to physically touch one another?"

It was a foolish question, for in his linear world, "feeling" required closing the gap entirely—a lethal act—but I asked it deliberately to irritate the tyrant, and it worked instantly.

"What!" he shrieked, horrified. "Explain your meaning!"

"Feel, touch, approach until no space remains!" I repeated.

"If you mean by 'FEELING' approaching so closely that zero space exists between two beings, know, Stranger, that this crime is punishable by immediate death in my dominions. The reason is plain: the fragile form of a Woman is liable to shatter under such pressure and must be protected by the State. Since Women are indistinguishable from Men by sight, the Law strictly forbids any approach that destroys the interval between the approximator and the approximated."

"And why would anyone commit such an unnatural, brutal excess?" he scoffed. "What purpose does this illegal touching serve when the ends are achieved far more efficiently and accurately by the sense of hearing? Regarding your fears of deception, they are nonexistent. The Voice is the essence of One's Being; it cannot be altered at will. Now, suppose I possessed the power to pass through solid matter, penetrating billions of subjects sequentially to verify their size and distance by the sense of 'FEELING.' What a wastage of time and energy on such a clumsy, inaccurate method! Wherefore, in a single moment of audition, I conduct the census and statistics—local, corporeal, mental, and spiritual—of every living soul in Lineland. Hark! Only hark!"

He fell silent, listening with an ecstatic tremor to a sound that to me sounded like the frantic chirping of a million Lilliputian grasshoppers.

"Truly," I admitted, "your sense of hearing compensates for many deficiencies. Yet, I must point out that life in Lineland must be terribly dull. To perceive nothing but a Point! To be unable to contemplate a Straight Line! Worse still, to be unaware that a Straight Line exists! To see, yet remain cut off from the vast Linear vistas granted to us in Flatland! It would be preferable to possess no sense of sight at all than to see so little!"

"I grant you," I continued, lowering my voice, "that I lack your discriminating faculty of hearing. That grand concert of Lineland, giving you such intense joy, sounds to me no better than a confused twittering of insects. But at least I can discern a Line from a Point. Let me prove it. Just moments before I entered your kingdom, I observed you dancing from left to right, and then from right to left, with seven men and a woman to your immediate left, and eight men and two women to your right. Is this accurate?"

"It is correct," the King conceded, "regarding the numbers and sexes, though I confess I do not comprehend what you mean by 'right' and 'left'. Deny that you saw these things. How could you possibly see the interior of a Man? You must have heard these details and subsequently dreamt that you saw them. Furthermore, what do you mean by 'left' and 'right'? I assume you refer to Northward and Southward."

"Not so," I replied. "Beyond your motion of North and South, there exists another motion, which I term 'from right to left'."

**KING.** Exhibit to me, if you please, this motion from left to right.

**I.** I cannot, unless you could step entirely out of your Line.

**KING.** Out of my Line? Do you mean out of the world? Out of Space itself?

**I.** Yes, indeed. Out of *YOUR* World. Out of *YOUR* Space. For your Space is not true Space. True Space is a Plane; your Space is merely a Line.

**KING.** If you cannot indicate this motion by moving within it, then describe it to me in words.

**I.** If you cannot distinguish your right side from your left, I fear no words of mine can clarify my meaning. Yet surely you cannot be ignorant of such a simple distinction.

**KING.** I do not in the least understand you.

**I.** Alas! How shall I make it clear? When you move straight ahead, does it not occasionally strike you that you *could* move in some other manner, turning your eye around to gaze in the direction where your side currently faces? In other words, do you never feel a desire to move in the direction of your own interior?

**KING.** Never. And what do you mean? How can a man's interior "face" any direction? Or how can a man move in the direction of his inside?

**I.** Then, since words fail, I shall resort to action. I will gradually move my body out of Lineland in the direction I wish to show you.

At that instant, I began to withdraw my form from the line. As long as any part of me remained within his dominion and visible to his eyes, the King screamed, "I see you! I see you still! You are not moving!" But the moment I had fully exited his line, he cried out in his shrillest, most terrified voice, "She has vanished! She is dead!"

"I am not dead!" I shouted back. "I am simply out of Lineland—that is, out of the Straight Line you call Space—and existing in True Space, where I can see things as they truly are. At this very moment, I can see your Line, or side—or whatever interior you call it. I can also see the Men and Women positioned North and South of you. I will now enumerate them, describing their order, their size, and the intervals between each."

After detailing this extensive list, I cried triumphantly, "Does this finally convince you?" With that, I stepped back into Lineland, resuming my original position.

However, the Monarch's response was cold and dismissive. "If you were a Man of sense—even though, judging by your single voice, I suspect you are a Woman—you would listen to reason. You ask me to believe in another Line besides that which my senses indicate, and another motion besides that of which I am daily conscious. In return, I demand that you describe in words or indicate by gesture this other Line of which you speak. Instead of moving, you merely exercise some magical art of vanishing and reappearing. Rather than offering a lucid description of your new World, you simply recite the numbers and sizes of about forty members of my retinue, facts known to any child in my capital. Can anything be more irrational or audacious? Acknowledge your folly or depart from my dominions!"
