Since Mirdath, My Beautiful One, departed, leaving me solitary in this desolate realm, I have endured a torment and a longing so profound, so utterly dreadful, that words fail to capture its weight. In truth, I, who once knew the sweetness of the world through her love and companionship, who tasted the very joy of existence, am now stricken by a loneliness that numbs the soul.

Yet, I turn again to my pen. Lately, a wondrous hope has taken root within me. For in the nights, amidst my slumber, I awaken into the future of this world. There, I behold strange marvels and rediscover the gladness of life. I have grasped the promise of tomorrow and, in my dreams, visited those realms where, within the womb of Time, she and I shall meet again—rending asunder in agony, only to reunite after epochs of strangeness in a glory that transcends mortal comprehension.

Here begins the strange chronicle of what I have witnessed. If the burden of writing it proves too heavy, I shall stop; perhaps, in doing so, I may find a fragment of peace, and offer a glimmer of hope to another suffering soul who longs, as I do, for the one who has died. Some may read this and scoff, declaring it false; others will argue. To them, I say only this: "Read!" Once you have read what I have set down, you shall look toward Eternity alongside me, gazing at its very gates. Thus, I begin my tale.

To me, in this final epoch of my visions, it did not feel like *dreaming*. Rather, it felt as if I *woke* there, into the dark, *into the future* of this world. The Sun had perished. For me, newly roused into this Future, to glance backward at our Present Age was akin to viewing a dream that my soul recognized as real—a distant vision, strangely sanctified by a fading peace and light.

Whenever I awoke into the Future, into the Everlasting Night that enshrouds this world, I perceived a blurred greyness circling me, close at hand. Soon, however, this greyness would dissipate, vanishing like a dusk cloud, revealing a world swallowed by darkness, punctuated here and there by strange, eerie illuminations. With my awakening into that Future, I did not wake to ignorance. I awoke to a complete knowledge of the terrors lighting up the Night Land—just as a man rises from sleep each morning and instantly knows the names and nature of the time in which he lives. Simultaneously, I retained a sub-conscious, misty awareness of *this* Present—the early life I now lead, utterly alone.

In my earliest recollection of *that* place, I was a youth of seventeen. My memory confirms that when I first arrived, or rather, when I first became self-aware in that Future, I stood within an embrasure of the Last Redoubt—that colossal pyramid of grey metal that sheltered the last millions of mankind from the Powers of the Slayers.

So thoroughly do I know that place that I scarcely believe anyone here understands it. Perhaps my familiarity renders me overly casual, neglecting to explain things that require elucidation for readers in our current day. Standing there, I felt less the aged man of *this* era and more the youth of *that*, possessing the innate knowledge of *that* life, gathered over seventeen years of existence there. Until my first vision, I (of this Age) knew nothing of that other, future existence; yet I awoke to it as naturally as a man rising to the morning sun, knowing its name and the meaning of everything within it. Still, standing in that vast embrasure, I carried a knowledge, a memory, of *this* present life deep within me, touched by a halo of dreams, yet haunted by a conscious longing for One, remembered even there in a fragment of memory as Mirdath.

As I stated, in my earliest memory, I stand in an embrasure high upon the side of the Pyramid, peering outward through a peculiar spy-glass toward the North-West. Yes, full of youthful vigor, with an adventurous yet half-fearful heart.

Within my brain resided the knowledge accumulated during all the years of my life in the Redoubt. Yet until that precise moment, the Man of this Present Time possessed no knowledge of that future existence. Now, I stood there, suddenly aware of a life already spent in that strange land, while deeper within me lingered the misty knowings of *our* present Age—and perhaps, of others yet to come.

Gazing North-West through the spy-glass, I beheld a landscape I had scrutinized throughout those years, naming every feature and measuring its distance from the "Centre-Point" of the Pyramid—a void of polished metal situated in the Room of Mathematics, where I studied daily. Through the lens, I saw clearly the blinding glare of the fire from the Red Pit, burning upward against the underside of the vast jaw of the North-West Watcher, the Watching Thing of the North-West. *"That which hath Watched from the Beginning, and until the opening of the Gateway of Eternity"* flashed into my mind, echoing the words of Aesworpth, the Ancient Poet (though, ironically, *future* to our time). Suddenly, these words seemed flawed. I looked deep into my own being and saw, as in a dream, the sunlight and splendor of *this* our Present Age. I was astounded.

Let me make this crystal clear: just as I awoke from *this* Age into *that* life, so too must I—the youth standing in that embrasure—have awakened to the knowledge of *this* far-back life of ours. To him, it appeared a vision of the dawn of eternity, the beginnings of the world. Oh! I dread making insufficiently clear that I and he were both *I*—the same soul. He, of that distant date, vaguely glimpsed the life that *was* (which I now live in this present Age); and I, of this time, beheld the life that I *shall* live. How utterly strange!

Yet, I am unsure if I speak holy truth in claiming that, in that future time, I had *no* knowledge of *this* life and Age prior to that awakening. I awoke to find myself standing apart from the other youths, for I possessed a dim, visionary knowledge of the past. This confused, yet enraged, the scholars of that age. Though I shall elaborate on this matter anon, I do know this: from that time forward, my knowledge and assurance of the Past multiplied tenfold, as confirmed by this memory of that life.

Returning from this digression, it was at the instant I awoke from that youthfulness into the assured awareness of *this* our Age that the hunger of my love flew to me across the ages. What had been merely a memory-dream swelled into the pain of *Reality*. I knew suddenly that I *lacked*; and from that moment onward, I walked, listening, as I do now, spending my life.

Thus, fresh-born in that future time, I hungered strangely for My Beautiful One with the full strength of that new life, knowing she had been mine and might live again, just as I. As I said, I hungered, and I learned to listen.

Now, returning to my narrative, I remained amazed by perceiving, in memory, the unknowable sunshine and splendor of this Age breaking so clearly through my previously hazy visions. The ignorance of Aesworpth was shouted at me by the things I now *knew*. From that time onward, for a brief space, I was stunned by everything I knew, guessed, and felt. All along, the hunger grew for that one I lost in the early days—she who sang to me in those faery days of light, who *had been* in verity. The special thoughts of that age gazed back with a keen, regretful wonder into the gulf of forgetfulness.

Presently, I turned away from the haze and pain of my dream-memories, returning once more to the inconceivable mystery of the Night Land, which I viewed through the great embrasure. None ever grew weary of gazing upon all the hideous mysteries; so old and young watched, from early youth unto death, the black monstrosity of the Night Land, which this our last refuge of humanity held at bay.

To the right of the Red Pit lay a long, sinuous glare, which I knew as the Vale of Red Fire. Beyond that, stretching for many dreary miles, lay the blackness of the Night Land. Across it came the cold light emanating from the Plain of Blue Fire. Then, on the very borders of the Unknown Lands, stretched a range of low volcanoes. They lit up, far away in the outer darkness, the Black Hills, where shone the Seven Lights. These lights neither twinkled nor moved nor faltered through Eternity; nor could the great spy-glass comprehend them. Nor had any adventurer returned from the Pyramid to tell us anything of them. Let me say here that, down in the Great Library of the Redoubt, were the histories of all those who ventured out into the monstrousness of the Night Land, risking not only their lives but the very spirit of life.
