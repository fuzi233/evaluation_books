The mists of November shrouded the valley below, veiling the river's gentle sweep in a damp, gray veil. As Mrs. Hale stepped into her new home, the thick fog crept in, driven by the chill wind, and swirled about the open doors in wispy tendrils of damp air. The interior of the house, though partially arranged by Margaret and Dixon, still wore an air of disarray, the rooms waiting to be transformed by the touch of their inhabitants.

Margaret's eyes met her mother's gaze, and in the depths of Mrs. Hale's expression, she saw a reflection of her own dismay. "Is this to be our home?" Mrs. Hale asked, her voice tinged with a note of desperation.

Margaret's heart echoed the sorrow in her mother's words, but she struggled to find the words to comfort her. Instead, she offered a faint smile and a reminder that the London fogs could be just as unforgiving. But her mother's eyes remained fixed on the dismal prospect before her, and Margaret knew that nothing could dispel the gloom that had settled over them.

Dixon, ever the practical one, attempted to lighten the mood, but even her attempts at humor fell flat in the face of the oppressive atmosphere. Mr. Hale, too, sought solace in Margaret's company, his spirits as low as the fog that shrouded the landscape. He joined Margaret at the window, gazing out at the impenetrable veil that obscured the world beyond their doorstep.

As the darkness gathered, Margaret felt the weight of their situation settle upon her. They were here, in Milton, with no clear escape from the dismal conditions that surrounded them. The fog seeped into her room, a chill draft that seemed to cling to every surface. The window, a small rectangle of glass set into the wall, framed a view of nothingness – a blank wall that seemed to stretch on forever.

In the midst of this desolation, Margaret's thoughts turned to a different world, one that existed far beyond the confines of their bleak reality. A letter from Edith, written in her friend's characteristic style, danced in her mind's eye. Edith's words painted vivid pictures of their life in Corfu – the warm sun on their skin, the sound of laughter and music carried on the breeze, the beauty of the blue sea and the white cliffs that stretched towards the horizon.
