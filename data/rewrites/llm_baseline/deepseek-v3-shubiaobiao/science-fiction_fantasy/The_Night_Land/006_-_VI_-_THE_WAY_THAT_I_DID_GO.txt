With resolve hardening my spirit, I stepped forward into the desolation beyond the Circle. To look back would weaken me, and I dared not grant my heart that indulgence. Ahead lay the unknown—the terrors of the Night Land, the whispering abominations, and somewhere in that black waste, the Maid who had called to me across the abyss of time and distance.

The air tasted strange, sharper than the tempered breath of the Pyramid. It carried a metallic bite, thick with unseen currents, as if the very atmosphere of the outer world resisted intrusion. The difference was palpable—not just in the cold that gnawed through my armor, but in the weight of it, pressing against my lungs. I had known the air of the upper Cities, thin and effortless; this was heavy, laden with the breath of the earth itself.

Ahead, the Circle’s glow receded, a slender band of light no thicker than my wrist. How often as a child had I watched monsters loom over that fragile barrier, their grotesque faces half-lit by its radiance? We had made a game of it, we children, daring one another to spot the most hideous creature peering inward. Now, I was the one stepping beyond its sanctuary, and the game felt hollow.

I paused, forcing myself to turn for a final glimpse of the Last Redoubt.

It rose—titanic, immeasurable—a mountain of metal and light piercing the eternal night. Three hundred thousand embrasures studded its lower slopes, each a glowing eye where countless faces pressed close, watching my departure. Higher, the lights blurred into a haze, then a distant shimmer, until the apex vanished into the black, crowned only by the faintest star—the Tower of Observation, where the Master Monstruwacan surely trained his glass upon me. I raised the Diskos in salute, though I knew he could not see my face.

A murmur rippled through the aether, the thoughts of millions brushing against mine—prayers, fears, farewells. I dared not linger. With a final gesture, I turned my back on the only home I had ever known and crossed into the Night Land.

I did not take the northern path directly. Instead, I angled northwest, skirting the edges of the Watcher’s domain. To approach the House of Silence head-on was folly. Better to circle wide, to come upon it from behind, where its gaze might least expect intrusion.

The decision was not mine alone. Fragments of an ancient text had guided me—a forgotten record, brittle with age, speaking of a time when the world had cracked open, forming a valley so deep it swallowed oceans. The book was myth to most, but I had pored over its words until they burned in my mind. It told of a Road, built by a forgotten people, stretching through that chasm. If the Lesser Redoubt still stood, it must lie within that abyss.

Northward, then. Or so I hoped.

The Night Land stretched before me, a black plain studded with fire-pits and shifting shadows. Every step carried me farther from safety, deeper into a land where the very air hummed with unseen malice. Yet I walked without hesitation.

There was no other choice.
