The stormy weather that had beset the Hales' household was a fitting backdrop for the turbulent emotions that churned within Margaret's heart. Her mother, Mrs. Hale, had grown increasingly affectionate towards her, drawing her closer as a confidante, a role Margaret had long coveted. As they sat together in the parlor, the howling wind outside served as a poignant reminder of the absent brother, Frederick, whose fate had been a constant source of worry for their family.

Mrs. Hale's eyes, red-rimmed from lack of sleep, reflected the turmoil that had been plaguing her since the day Frederick set sail. The memory of that fateful departure still lingered, etched in her mind like the lines on a well-worn book. The wind's mournful sighs seemed to echo the anguish she felt, a sense of helplessness that clung to her like a shroud.

As they conversed, Margaret found herself drawn into the depths of her mother's sorrow, her own fears and uncertainties mirrored in the older woman's words. The dream that haunted Mrs. Hale, a vision of Frederick lost at sea, its glass-green waves and cruel foam, seemed to reverberate through the house, a haunting refrain that spoke to the desperation that gripped them all.

The letters, yellowed and worn from their journey across the ocean, lay in the japanned cabinet, a tangible connection to the brother they loved and missed. Mrs. Hale's hands trembled as she untied the silken string, releasing the contents into Margaret's eager hands. The scent of salt and seaweed wafted up, transporting them both to a different world, one of adventure and longing.

As Margaret read the letters, her mother's words poured forth, a torrent of emotion that threatened to overwhelm her. The stories of Frederick's early days at sea, his growth into a capable and brave young man, all served as a bittersweet reminder of what might have been. The names of Captain Reid and the Orion, the ship that had borne Frederick away from them, became a litany of memories, each one a painful reminder of the fragility of life and the capricious nature of fate.
