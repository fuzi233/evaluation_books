As the autumnal twilight settled over the windswept moors, casting a melancholy glow over the crumbling stone cottages of Milton, Nicholas Higgins stirred from his slumber. He rose from his chair, his worn boots scraping against the cold hearthstones as he made his way to the window. His daughter, Mary, sat nearby, her fingers deftly cutting the coarse fabric of a fustian coat, the soft clinking of her scissors punctuating the silence. The air was heavy with the scent of peat smoke and damp earth, a poignant reminder of the struggles that lay ahead.

Margaret Hale and her father, Mr. Hale, stood at the door, their faces aglow with the warmth of the evening sun. The weight of their recent loss still lingered, a subtle tension between them that only seemed to grow thicker with each passing day. As they entered the cottage, Nicholas's eyes met theirs, a mixture of welcome and wariness etched on his face.

"Sit down, sit down," he said gruffly, poking at the embers of the fire as if to distract from the somberness that filled the air. His black beard, several days' growth, made his pale face seem even more gaunt, and his jacket, though patched, hung awkwardly on his frame.

"We thought we'd catch you after dinner," Margaret said, her voice gentle as a summer breeze.

"Aye, dinner stretches across the day for me now," Nicholas replied, his words tinged with a mix of bitterness and resignation. "I'm not lacking for food, but work... that's a different matter."

His daughter, Mary, looked up from her task, her eyes meeting Margaret's with a hint of defiance. Mr. Hale's eyes, however, lingered on Nicholas, his expression a mix of concern and curiosity.

"Are you out of work?" Margaret asked, her tone measured.

Nicholas's response was curt, but a spark of something like hope flickered in his eyes. "I'm not seeking coin," he said finally. "I don't need it. Mary's doing well with her cutting, and Bess left us a small nest egg before... before she passed." His voice cracked, and he looked away, his jaw clenched.

Mr. Hale's words, spoken without thinking, hung in the air like a challenge. "We owe Mary some money," he said, but Margaret's swift pressure on his arm stayed his tongue.
