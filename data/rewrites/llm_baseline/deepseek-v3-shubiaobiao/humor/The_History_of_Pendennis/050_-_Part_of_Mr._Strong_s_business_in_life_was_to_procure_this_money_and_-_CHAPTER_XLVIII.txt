Arthur Pendennis awoke with an uncharacteristic spring in his step—no midnight brawl with Costigan, no indignant summons from Huxter—just the rare luxury of a London man greeting the day without the usual dread of ledgers, lawsuits, or literary deadlines. (Though, to be fair, Pen’s "labors" at the *Pall Mall Gazette* were hardly the stuff of martyrdom. Even Pegasus, when hitched to a daily column, becomes a glorified cab horse, more concerned with his oats than his odes.)

Still, Pen’s mind buzzed with the memory of little Fanny Bolton—her artless chatter, her blushes, the way her voice fluttered like a moth around a candle. *What a relief*, he mused, *after the calculated simpers of ballroom Blanche or the yawn-inducing graces of society misses*. That a creature so fresh could sprout in the soot-choked flowerpot of Shepherd’s Inn was a marvel. And old Bows, of all people, teaching her to sing! The thought amused him. Bows—that threadbare philosopher with the heart of a gentleman—was a character begging to be immortalized in print.

Thus resolved, Pen (having no pressing need to feign industry on a Saturday) set off for Shepherd’s Inn, ostensibly to call on Bows, though his boots, his gloves, and the rakish tilt of his hat suggested other motivations.

---

Meanwhile, in the Bolton household, Fanny had scarcely slept. Visions of Pen—his gleaming shirt-studs, his laugh, the way he’d *almost* kissed her—danced behind her eyelids. Compared to Emma Baker’s cheesemonger or Betsy Rodgers’ puffed-up attorney’s clerk, Arthur might as well have been a prince from the pages of Minifer’s Circulating Library. (Not that Fanny would admit to devouring those three-volume romances. Certainly not.)

So when Pen materialized under the archway, white-hatted and absurdly handsome, Fanny’s heart performed a quadrille in her chest. She nearly bowled over her siblings in her haste to the wicket. Mrs. Bolton, initially wary, soon thawed under Pen’s charm offensive—his inquiries about the undertaker business (!), his theatrical gossip, his *generous* offer of play tickets. (Astley’s! Fanny could already smell the sawdust.) By the time he perched on a child’s chair—*oh, the sacrifice!*—both women were his devoted allies, trading giggles like conspirators.

Then Bows appeared.

The old musician’s face curdled at the sight: Pen, holding court like Macheath among the innkeeper’s daughters.

"Ah, Bows!" Pen boomed, blissfully oblivious. "Just the man I wanted to see!"

Bows’ grip was limp, his smile a grimace. *Plague on him*, Fanny thought—though whether she meant Bows or Pen, even she couldn’t say.
