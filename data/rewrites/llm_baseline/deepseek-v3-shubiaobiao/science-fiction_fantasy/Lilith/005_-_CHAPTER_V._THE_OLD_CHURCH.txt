The raven led me deeper into the pine forest, where the air hummed with quiet reverence. We walked in silence, the ancient trees bending over us like sentinels of another age. Their trunks twisted into shapes that whispered of time long past, their bark etched with stories only the wind could read.

At last, the woods thinned, and the raven paused. His beak lifted toward the edge of the heath.

*"Do you see that hawthorn?"* he asked.

I squinted at the gnarled shape in the distance. "I see an old man, bent and white with years."

*"Look again,"* he insisted.

The image shifted—a thorned tree, its branches heavy with blossoms despite the season.

"But it isn’t spring," I protested.

*"The hawthorn blooms when it chooses,"* the raven replied. *"That one grows in the ruins of the old church on your land. You meant to tell your bailiff to leave its yard wild, didn’t you? Before the storm came."*

A memory flickered—the scent of roses, the quiet of graves untouched by ploughs.

*"Listen,"* the raven murmured.

I strained my ears. Was that music? A whisper of hymns long forgotten, or just the wind threading through stone?

*"They still gather there,"* he said.

"Who?"

"Why do they go?"

"Do they pray aloud?"

*"No. Prayer is quieter now. A thing of the heart."* His wing twitched upward. *"Look—there goes one."*

A white pigeon spiraled into the sky, wings catching the sun.

"A bird," I said.

*"A prayer,"* he corrected. *"Alive. Taking flight."*

"But a prayer is a thought—how can it have wings?"

*"You think only in words. But some hearts think in living things."* His gaze dropped suddenly to the base of a granite slab. *"And here’s another."*

A flower bloomed where none had been before—petals like pale fire, a center of gold. It was unlike anything I had ever seen, and it filled me with a longing I couldn’t name.

*"A prayer-flower,"* the raven said.

"How do you know?"

"Could you teach me to recognize them?"

*"No. That’s between you and the flower."* He tilted his head. *"The universe will make a fool of you until you learn to see for yourself."*

I stared at the blossom, its edges trembling in the breeze. It was more than a plant—it was a voice. And if I listened, perhaps I’d hear what it was saying.

Awe settled over me. Somewhere, a heart was speaking—and the earth was answering.
