As the drums of war beat louder in Europe, Harry Warrington's restless spirit yearned for battle. The Prussian king's conquests had captured the imagination of society, and the young man found himself drawn to the tales of heroism and valor. But fate had other plans for him. Instead of joining the fray, Harry spent his days wandering the military coffeehouses, his heart heavy with discontent. He devoured newspapers, searching for news of the conflict, but even the written word couldn't satiate his longing for action.

His love of literature was nonexistent, and he scoffed at the romantic novels that swept through the salons. The ladies' tears over Mr. Richardson's stories left him perplexed – what could they possibly find so captivating? He laughed, but his humor was always a little delayed, as if he'd missed the punchline. Why must every gentleman be expected to be a scholar? Should he be judged harshly for not turning a phrase?

Idle and reliant on his brother's charity, Harry's days blended together in a haze of listlessness. His hands, strong and capable, yearned for purpose, but found none. It was fortunate, perhaps, that he didn't succumb to more reckless behavior, like the legendary Achilles, who'd grown soft and complacent in the court of King What-d'ye-call-'em. The great warrior's descent into idleness had led to scandalous consequences. A young man of mettle shouldn't be coddled by the gentle hands of women; he needed challenge, adventure, and the rough test of battle to temper his spirit.

The Lambert sisters, with their boundless compassion and kindness, tried to fill the void in Harry's life. They soothed his troubled mind, calmed his anger, and lifted his spirits. Women, with their unique gift for empathy, had a way of healing the wounds inflicted by life's cruelties. Ah, how fortunate those who had such gentle healers by their side! Harry's friends, however, might not be so understanding. They'd see him as a Prometheus, forever bound to his rock, his creative spark stifled by the weight of his own discontent.
