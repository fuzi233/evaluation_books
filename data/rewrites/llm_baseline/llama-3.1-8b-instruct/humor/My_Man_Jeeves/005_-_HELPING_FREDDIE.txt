The memory of dear old Freddie Meadowes still brings a tinge of melancholy to my mind. Our long-standing friendship had weathered many trials, but nothing could have prepared me for the sight of him sitting alone in the dimly lit corner of the club, his eyes vacant, his spirit crushed. It was as if the very essence of him had been drained away, leaving behind a shell of the carefree, affable companion I once knew.

Jimmy Pinkerton, a fellow wordsmith, was by my side as we approached Freddie's table. Together, we probed gently, trying to coax him out of his despair. His tale unfolded like a slow-burning ember, revealing the depths of his sorrow. It was Angela West, the woman he had been engaged to, who had broken off their engagement, her heart hardened against him. No explanation was forthcoming, only a sense of finality that hung in the air like the scent of damp earth after a spring shower.

I knew the ache of unrequited love, having once been smitten with Elizabeth Shoolbred, whose indifference had left an indelible mark on my own heart. Freddie's pain resonated within me, and I knew I had to act. A change of scenery, I thought, might help lift his spirits. I proposed a trip to Marvis Bay, a quaint village in Dorsetshire, where I had rented a cozy cottage. The idea took root, and soon we were making plans for a quiet sojourn by the sea.

Marvis Bay, with its gentle rhythms and soothing landscapes, proved to be the perfect antidote to Freddie's malaise. We spent our days lounging on the sandy beach, watching the sun dance across the waves, and our evenings strolling along the shoreline as the stars began to twinkle in the night sky. The locals, accustomed to the gentle pace of life, welcomed us warmly, and Freddie, though still troubled, found solace in the simple pleasures of the place.

However, as the days passed, I began to realize that Freddie's presence was not exactly a boon to our little community. His somber mood seemed to draw the light out of the air, and even the normally vibrant atmosphere of the beach seemed to wilt under his gaze.
