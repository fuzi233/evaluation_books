The cacophony of violence shattered the dreamlike suspension of my consciousness, yanking me violently back into the suffocating reality of the living world. For a fleeting, disorienting instant, the sensory input was a chaotic blur; I could not orient myself within the claustrophobic geometry of the cell, nor could I parse the source of the clamor that had roused me. Only then did the fragmented sounds coalesce into a horrifying symphony: the wet, shuffling drag of shackles, the guttural snarls of primal beasts, the metallic clatter of weaponry, and the ragged, terrified panting of a man pushed to the brink of exhaustion.

Rising to my feet, I swept a frantic gaze across the chamber where I had previously received such a chilling welcome. Against the far wall, the captives and the savage brutes lay in their iron bonds, their eyes fixed upon me with a kaleidoscope of emotions—curiosity curdling into sullen rage, surprise warring with a fragile, flickering hope. That final spark of hope burned brightest on the countenance of the young red Martian woman, whose earlier cry of warning had been the sole barrier between me and certain death.

She embodied the pinnacle of her species, a race whose physical perfection mirrored the divine ideals of Earth, distinguished only by the luminous, copper-red hue of their skin. Stripped of all adornment, her attire—or lack thereof—offered no clue to her station, though the context of her imprisonment made her status as a slave or captive undeniable. Her beauty was stark, unadorned, and undeniably intelligent.

Seconds stretched into an eternity as the noises from beyond the partition jolted my fading faculties into clarity. Suddenly, the nature of the disturbance crystallized: it was Tars Tarkas engaged in a desperate, life-or-death struggle against wild beasts or savage men. With a surge of adrenaline, I hurled my body against the secret door, but the resistance was absolute, as futile as attempting to hurl oneself off the precipice itself. Desperation clawed at my throat as I frantically searched for the mechanism of the revolving panel, my fingers trembling against the smooth surface. Just as I prepared to strike the sullen gold with my longsword, the young woman's voice cut through the tension.

"Save thy sword, O Mighty Warrior," she urged, her tone urgent yet calm. "Thou shalt need it more where it will truly avail thee—do not shatter it against senseless metal that yields to the lightest touch of one who knows its secret."

"Do you possess the secret?" I demanded, my voice rough.

"Yes," she replied, stepping closer despite her chains. "Release me, and I shall grant you entry to the other chamber of horrors, if you so desire. The keys to my fetters rest upon the corpse of your fallen foe. Yet, why would you return to face the fierce banth, or whatever monstrosity they have unleashed within that trap?"

"Because my friend fights there alone," I answered, my movements swift and precise as I located the keys upon the carcass of the dead custodian.

The oval ring held many keys, but the fair Martian maid moved with practiced efficiency, selecting the specific instrument that snapped open the great lock at her waist. Freed instantly, she hurried toward the hidden panel. She reached for another key, this one slender and needle-thin, inserting it into an aperture nearly invisible to the untrained eye. Immediately, the door swung open on its pivot, and the section of floor beneath my feet rotated, carrying me into the chamber where Tars Tarkas battled for his life.

The great Thark stood backed against a corner of the walls, a statue of defiance. Facing him in a menacing semicircle were half a dozen colossal monsters, crouching and waiting for a fatal error. Their blood-streaked heads and shoulders bore testament to the ferocity of the assault, as did the glossy hide of the green warrior, torn and scarred by sharp talons and cruel fangs that had ripped his limbs and torso to ribbons. Exhausted by prolonged exertion and hemorrhaging, he leaned heavily against the supporting wall; without it, he likely would have collapsed. Yet, fueled by the indomitable tenacity of his kin, he faced his relentless foes, embodying the ancient proverb of his tribe: *"Leave to a Thark his head and one hand, and he may yet conquer."*

Upon seeing me enter, a grim smile touched the hard lines of his mouth. Whether it signaled relief or mere amusement at my own battered and disheveled state, I could not discern. Just as I prepared to leap into the fray with my blade, a gentle hand rested upon my shoulder. Turning, I found to my astonishment that the young woman had followed me into the chamber, unarmed and defenseless.

"Wait," she whispered urgently. "Leave them to me."

Pushing past me, she advanced directly toward the snarling banths. At close range, she uttered a single word in low, peremptory tones. Like lightning striking earth, the great beasts wheeled upon her. I braced myself to witness her torn apart, but instead, the creatures slunk to her feet like puppies awaiting a deserved reprimand. She spoke to them again, her voice too soft for me to catch the syllables, and then she began to march toward the opposite side of the chamber. The six mighty monsters trailed behind her like loyal hounds. One by one, she guided them through the secret panel into the room beyond. When the last had vanished, leaving us staring in wide-eyed amazement, she turned, offered a brief, enigmatic smile, and passed through herself, leaving us alone in the silence.

For a heartbeat, neither of us spoke. Then Tars Tarkas addressed me.

"I heard the fighting beyond the partition through which you passed," he rumbled, his voice thick with concern. "But I did not fear for you, John Carter, until I heard the report of a revolver shot. I knew that no man upon all Barsoom could face you with naked steel and survive, yet that shot stripped the last vestige of hope from me, for I knew you lacked firearms. Speak of it."

I recounted the tale as he requested, and together we turned our attention to the secret panel I had used to enter the apartment—the one situated at the opposite end of the room from where the girl had led her savage companions. Disappointment washed over us as the panel stubbornly refused to yield to our efforts. We reasoned that once beyond it, we might find a passage to the outside world. The secure chaining of the prisoners suggested that an avenue of escape must exist from the terrible creatures inhabiting this unspeakable place.

We turned from one door to another, from the baffling golden panel at one end of the chamber to its mate at the other, equally impenetrable. Hope ebbing away, the young woman appeared once more beside us, having returned from her task.

"Who are you?" she asked, her gaze piercing. "And what is your mission, that you dare attempt to escape from the Valley Dor and the death you have seemingly chosen?"

"I have chosen no death, maiden," I replied firmly. "I am not of Barsoom, nor have I undertaken the voluntary pilgrimage upon the River Iss. My friend here is the Jeddak of all the Tharks, and though he has not yet voiced a desire to return to the living world, I am taking him with me from the living lie that has lured him to this frightful place. I am of another world. I am John Carter, Prince of the House of Tardos Mors, Jeddak of Helium. Perhaps some faint rumor of me has leaked into the confines of your hellish abode."

A smile played upon her lips. "Yes," she replied softly. "Naught that passes in the world we have left is unknown here. I have heard of you, many years ago. The therns have oftentimes wondered whither you had flown, since you took neither the pilgrimage nor could be found upon the face of Barsoom."

"Tell me," I pressed, "who are you, and why a prisoner, yet possessing power over the ferocious beasts of this place—a familiarity and authority far beyond that expected of a slave?"

"Slave I am," she answered, her voice trembling slightly. "For fifteen years a slave in this terrible place, and now that they have grown weary of me and fearful of the power my knowledge of their ways has granted me, I have recently been condemned to die."

She shuddered visibly. "What death?" I asked.

"The Holy Therns eat human flesh," she explained, her eyes distant. "But only that which has died beneath the sucking lips of a plant man—flesh from which the defiling blood of life has been drawn. To this cruel end I have been condemned. It was to occur within a few hours had your advent not interrupted their plans."

"Was it then the Holy Therns who felt the weight of John Carter's hand?" I queried.
