The news of their return had been anticipated for several years, dating back to the widely publicized marriage of Francis Clavering, the only son of Sir Francis Clavering, to Jemima Augusta, the daughter of Samuel Snell of Calcutta and widow of the late J. Amory. At the time, it was rumored that Clavering had secured a substantial dowry through his union with the wealthy widow, whose family ties extended across the Indian subcontinent. The union was met with skepticism by the local populace, who whispered about the dubious nature of Clavering's past, including his stint in the dragoon regiment and his subsequent imprisonment in the Fleet.

Despite these whispers, the Claverings had established themselves as a prominent fixture on the European social scene, hosting lavish parties at the Poggi Palace in Florence and Schloss Schinkenstein on the Mummel See. Their trajectory took them to Rome, Lucca, Nice, and the casinos of the Rhine and Belgium, leaving a trail of gossip in their wake. Their final residence before returning to England was Paris, where they resided in opulent splendor following the news of Samuel Snell's passing.

Little is known about Sir Francis Clavering's ancestry that would not reflect poorly on the baronet. Born to an outlaw, he had attempted to launch his career with a military commission but soon found himself mired in debt after a series of ill-fated transactions at the gaming tables. He eventually sold out of the army, spent time in the Fleet, and joined his ailing father in exile in Belgium, France, and Germany. For several years, he drifted between billiard rooms, watering holes, and racing circuits, relying on the charity of others to sustain him.
