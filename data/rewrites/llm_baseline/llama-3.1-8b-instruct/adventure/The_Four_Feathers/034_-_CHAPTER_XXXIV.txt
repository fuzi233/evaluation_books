The warm sunlight danced across the stone floor of the Broad Place terrace as two old friends, General Feversham and Lieutenant Sutch, lingered over their morning newspapers. The elder man, with a twinkle in his eye, turned a page, his voice low and measured. "It seems Osman Digna has returned to Suakin, bringing with him the promise of conflict."

Sutch set his paper aside, his gaze drifting away from the rolling hills of the English countryside to the far-off vision of the Red Sea city shimmering in the haze. "I daresay it will amount to little more than a nuisance," he said, his words laced with a hint of amusement.

As they sat, the scent of freshly brewed coffee wafted from the nearby garden, mingling with the fragrance of blooming flowers. Feversham's eyes sparkled with a faraway look, and he leaned forward, his hands cradling his cup. "Suakin, my dear Sutch, is a city of contrasts – the blistering heat, the endless plains of golden grass, and the majestic hills that rise like sentinels from the horizon."

Sutch's eyes lit up with interest. "A place of mystery, indeed. Though I recall hearing tales of the garrison's excesses – shooting ibex by the dozen, leaving none for the local markets."

Feversham chuckled, his eyes twinkling with mirth. "Ah, yes! A place where one might find something to occupy oneself, even if it meant indulging in a bit of sport."

Their conversation was interrupted by the soft tread of a footman bearing a tray laden with letters. Feversham's eyes scanned the envelopes, his fingers deftly selecting one before he produced a pair of spectacles from a pocket case and settled in for a leisurely perusal. Sutch, ever the patient friend, set aside his newspaper, watching with interest as Feversham savored each word, his cigar burning slowly in the silence.

The letter, penned by a dear acquaintance from Ramelton, was a treasure to be savored, and Feversham indulged in the pleasure of its contents, pausing to relight his cigar and stretch his long frame in the warmth of the sun. As he read, a look of puzzlement creased his brow, and Sutch, sensing his friend's unease, asked, "Any news from Ramelton, Feversham?"
