The moment my brother was dragged away to prison, a desperate impulse seized me. I tried to leap from the balcony into the Council Chamber below, driven by a need to plead for his release or simply to bid him farewell. Yet, my limbs refused to obey. I was paralyzed, entirely dependent on the will of my Guide. He spoke in a somber, heavy voice, silencing my frantic thoughts: "Heed not thy brother. Haply, thou shalt have ample time hereafter to condole with him. Follow me."

We ascended again, rising from the flat expanse into the boundless void of Spaceland. "Thus far," the Sphere intoned, "I have shown thee nothing but Planes—their surfaces and their interiors. Now, I must introduce thee to Solids, and reveal the architecture by which they are built."

Before me hovered a vast array of movable square cards. With swift, precise movements, the Sphere began to stack them. "See how I place one upon another," he instructed. "Not northward of the first, as thou might expect, but *on* the first. Now a second, then a third." He piled them until a towering structure rose, equal in height, length, and breadth. "Behold," he declared, "a Solid complete. We name it a Cube."

"Pardon me, my Lord," I stammered, squinting at the bewildering arrangement. "To my eye, it resembles an irregular figure with its inside laid bare. I perceive no Solid, but a distorted Plane—like the monstrous criminals of Flatland, whose internal organs are exposed. The sight is agony to my senses."

"Truthfully," the Sphere agreed gently, "it appears to thee as a Plane because thou hast not yet learned the arts of Light and Shade, nor Perspective. Just as a Hexagon appears to a blind man in Flatland as merely a straight line, lacking the 'Art of Sight Recognition,' so too does the Cube deceive thy untrained vision. In reality, it is a Solid, a truth thou shalt verify through the sense of Feeling."

He guided my attention back to the object. Suddenly, the illusion shattered. Through the medium of tactile sensation, I grasped the reality. This marvel was not a flattened shape; it possessed depth. It was composed of six planar sides enclosing a volume, terminating in eight sharp points known as solid angles. A thrill coursed through me as I recalled the Sphere's earlier lesson: a Square, moving through Space parallel to itself, generates exactly such a creature. To think that something as humble as I—a two-dimensional square—could be the progenitor of such an illustrious, voluminous offspring filled me with a sudden, dizzying pride.

Yet, confusion clouded my joy. The concepts of "Light," "Shade," and "Perspective" remained slippery, elusive abstractions. I could not hold them in mind, and I felt compelled to voice my doubts.

If I were to recount the Sphere's full explanation of these phenomena, it would likely seem tedious to any native of Spaceland, who understands them instinctively. However, suffice it to say that through his lucid demonstrations, shifting objects and sources of illumination, and allowing me to physically feel various shapes—even his own sacred person—he finally untangled the knot within my mind. From that moment forth, I could distinctly distinguish between a Circle and a Sphere, and between a mere Plane Figure and a true Solid.

This revelation marked the climax, the paradise, of my strange and eventful history. What followed, however, would be the narrative of my miserable fall—most tragic, yet, I swear, most undeserved. Why should the thirst for knowledge be ignited only to be quenched with disappointment and punishment? My will recoils from the painful task of recalling my humiliation, yet, like a second Prometheus, I choose to endure this torment and worse. I must, if by any means possible, awaken a spirit of rebellion within the minds of both Plane and Solid humanity against the arrogant conceit that limits our existence to Two, or Three, or any finite number of dimensions short of Infinity.

Away, then, with all personal considerations! Let me continue to the end, as I began, without further digressions or anticipations, following the plain path of dispassionate history. The exact facts, the exact words—they are burned indelibly into my brain—and shall be set down without the alteration of a single iota. Let my readers judge between me and Destiny.

The Sphere would gladly have continued his instruction, indoctrinating me in the conformations of all regular solids: cylinders, cones, pyramids, pentahedrons, hexahedrons, dodecahedrons, and spheres. But I ventured to interrupt him. Far from being weary of knowledge, I thirsted for deeper, fuller draughts than he offered.

"Pardon me," I said, lowering my gaze respectfully, "O Thou Whom I must no longer address as the Perfection of all Beauty. Pray, vouchsafe thy servant a sight of thine interior."

**SPHERE.** My what?

**I.** Thy interior: thy stomach, thy intestines.

**SPHERE.** Whence this ill-timed impertinence? And what do you mean by stating that I am no longer the Perfection of all Beauty?

**I.** My Lord, your own wisdom has taught me to aspire to One even greater, more beautiful, and closer to Perfection than yourself. As you, superior to all Flatland forms, combine many circles into One, so doubtless there exists One above you who combines many spheres into One Supreme Existence, surpassing even the solids of Spaceland. Even as we, now in Space, look down upon Flatland and see the insides of all things, so of a certainty there exists a higher, purer region above us, whither thou dost surely intend to lead me—O Thou Whom I shall always call, everywhere and in all dimensions, my Priest, Philosopher, and Friend—from the vantage ground of which we shall look down together upon the revealed interiors of solid things. There, thine own intestines, and those of thy kin the Spheres, will lie exposed to the view of the poor wandering exile from Flatland, to whom so much has already been vouchsafed.

**SPHERE.** Pooh! Stuff! Enough of this trifling! Time is short, and much remains to be done before thou art fit to proclaim the Gospel of Three Dimensions to thy blind, benighted countrymen in Flatland.

**I.** Nay, gracious Teacher, deny me not what I know it is in thy power to perform. Grant me but one glimpse of thine interior, and I am satisfied forever, remaining henceforth thy docile pupil, thy unemancipable slave, ready to receive all thy teachings and to feed upon the words that fall from thy lips.

**SPHERE.** Well, then, to content and silence thee, let me say at once: I would show thee what thou wishest if I could; but I cannot. Wouldest thou have me turn my stomach inside out to oblige thee?

**I.** But my Lord has shown me the intestines of all my countrymen in the Land of Two Dimensions by taking me with him into the Land of Three. What, therefore, is more easy than now to take his servant on a second journey into the blessed region of the Fourth Dimension? There, I shall look down with thee once more upon this land of Three Dimensions, seeing the inside of every three-dimensional house, the secrets of the solid earth, the treasures of the mines in Spaceland, and the intestines of every solid living creature, even of the noble and adorable Spheres.

**SPHERE.** But where is this land of Four Dimensions?

**I.** I know not; but doubtless my Teacher knows.

**SPHERE.** Not I. There is no such land. The very idea of it is utterly inconceivable.

**I.** Not inconceivable, my Lord, to me, and therefore still less inconceivable to my Master. Nay, I despair not that, even here, in this region of Three Dimensions, your Lordship's art may render the Fourth Dimension visible to me. Just as in the Land of Two Dimensions, my Teacher's skill sought to open the eyes of his blind servant to the invisible presence of a Third Dimension, though I saw it not, so here, perhaps, a similar grace awaits.

Let me recall the past. Was I not taught below that when I saw a Line and inferred a Plane, I in reality perceived a Third, unrecognized Dimension—not the same as brightness, called "Height"? And does it not now follow that, in this region, when I see a Plane and infer a Solid, I really perceive a Fourth, unrecognized Dimension—not the same as color, but existent, though infinitesimal and incapable of measurement?

Besides this, there is the Argument from Analogy of Figures.

**SPHERE.** Analogy! Nonsense: what analogy?
