The narrow alley swallowed me as I stepped into its gloom, seeking refuge beneath a shallow arch. Moonlight pooled on the cobblestones like spilled mercury, and I hesitated at the threshold, watching. Then she came—a woman, slipping in behind me, breath ragged, her body pressed to the wall as if it could shield her. She trembled, her gaze fixed on the alley beyond.

A heartbeat later, the leopard came.

It was a streak of ivory and shadow, muscles coiled beneath a pelt mottled like old parchment. The woman gasped, her fingers digging into my arm. I pulled her closer.

"If it turns on us," I said, low and steady, "I’ll hold it. Run."

Her voice was a whisper, frayed with fear. "Thank you."

"You know this creature?"

Her nod was stiff. "She belongs to the princess. You must be a stranger—everyone in Bulika knows her."

"I am." My eyes tracked the beast’s vanished path. "They let it roam free?"

"A muzzled thing, they say. Gloved claws, chains." A bitter laugh escaped her. "Yet she slips her cage. Hunts children when she can. Few mothers remain in Bulika to mourn."

Her tears came then, silent and sharp. "I should never have left home," she choked. "The princess returned last night, and already the beast is loose. She waits for me—I know it. Watching my door like a specter." She wiped her face, sudden suspicion tightening her features. "Why am I telling you this? You could be anyone."

"Not all strangers bring harm," I said. The wind howled through the alley, a living thing. "You have a home to return to. That’s more than most."

She studied me, then nodded once. "Take me there safely, and I’ll give you shelter from this accursed night."

We waited in the hush, and I asked of Bulika—its people, its ways.

"They dig," she said. "Not for bread, not for trade. For gems in the dark beneath their houses. They are wealthy enough to let others labor for them."

"Why?"

"Work is a stain here. Only the lowborn toil."

"And when the gems run out?"

She scoffed. "The veins are endless. And if they weren’t—we are Bulika. The princess ensures our wealth, our safety. No outsider dares challenge her."

Every few words, her eyes darted past me, scanning the dark.

"Your people despise strangers," I noted.

"They defile us. We are ancient. Pure." Her chin lifted. "The princess has ruled for millennia. The earth obeys her. The air, the waters—perhaps even fire. She answers to no one."

When she deemed it safe, we moved, winding through serpentine passages until we reached her door—wedged between two towering houses, a steep stair leading up. Halfway, panic seized her. She fled the last steps, and before I could follow, the door slammed shut.

I stood alone on the narrow landing, the wind clawing at my back. Exhaustion weighed heavy. Let Bulika scorn me; I would rest here, in the space between doors, and wait for dawn.
