It was a brisk spring morning when the door to my bedroom burst open, and Rocky Todd's energetic form poked me in the ribs, shaking the bedclothes vigorously. As I blinked away the haze of sleep and struggled to gather my wits, I couldn't help but think that it was some peculiar dream. Rocky, a poet from Long Island, had previously confided in me that he never rose before noon, and often lingered in bed until the afternoon. His constitutionally lazy nature suited him well, for he'd discovered a lucrative career path that allowed him to indulge in this lifestyle. 

Rocky's daily routine revolved around a carefully crafted schedule. He devoted three days each month to writing poetry, leaving the remaining 329 days for rest and contemplation. His poetry often took the form of motivational pieces urging young men to live life to the fullest. He'd shown me one of his compositions once – a rousing call to action that began:

Be!
Be!
    The past is dead.
    To-morrow is not born.
        Be to-day!
To-day!
    Be with every nerve,
            With every muscle,
            With every drop of your red blood!

The poem had been featured prominently in a magazine, accompanied by an illustration of a robust figure basking in the glow of sunrise. Rocky claimed he earned a handsome sum of $100 for it, and spent the following month lounging in bed until the afternoon.

His financial stability was secured thanks to his wealthy aunt residing in Illinois, whom he'd named after in tribute. Upon inheriting her estate, Rocky planned to abandon all work, save for the occasional poem extolling the virtues of a leisurely life. This was the man now prodding me awake in the early morning light.

"Wake up, Bertie!" he exclaimed, brandishing a letter in the air. "Read this!"

I'm not a morning person, and I require a cup of tea and a cigarette to clear my head. I reached for the bell to summon Jeeves, my trusted butler and confidant.

"Tea, Jeeves," I said as he entered the room, looking remarkably refreshed despite the early hour.

"Very good, sir," he replied, his movements fluid and silent, like a liquid substance flowing across the floor.
