As the night of August 30 descended upon the small town of Donegal, the air was alive with whispers of Harry Feversham's disappearance. It had been a month since the ball at Lennon House, and yet the countryside remained abuzz with speculation. At dinner tables and in hushed conversations, the townsfolk indulged in spirited debates, weaving tales of Harry's possible whereabouts. Some claimed to have spotted him on the morning after the dance, hurrying along the causeway by Lennon Bridge, his white face a testament to his distress. Others posited that a drag-net was the only means to unravel the mystery, and Mr. Dennis Rafferty, who dwelled on the road to Rathmullen, refused salmon, proclaiming himself no cannibal.

Their theories, however, were as far removed from the truth as the mist-shrouded hills that loomed above the town. Two individuals alone held the key to Harry's disappearance, yet they moved through life with an air of quiet confidence, their demeanor unchanged. Ethne might have grown slightly softer, Dermod a tad more irascible, but these were the only discernible variations. As a result, gossip had free rein to spin its tale.

Meanwhile, in the distant city of London, a different kind of turmoil brewed. News of a great battle fought in the desert east of Ismailia had sent shockwaves through the streets. Messengers dashed to and fro, bearing tidings of victory and defeat. The moonlit charge of General Drury-Lowe's Cavalry Brigade had rolled up Arabi's left flank and seized his guns, leaving the outcome uncertain. An English general was rumored to have fallen, and the York and Lancaster Regiment had suffered heavy losses. As night descended, a sea of pale faces gathered beneath the gas lamps in Pall Mall, their eyes fixed on the lit windows of the War Office. The crowd stood in silence, its collective breath held in anticipation, until a figure moved across the blinds, sending a shiver of excitement through the throng.

Lieutenant Sutch, nursing his wounded leg, stood at the periphery, his back against the Junior Carlton Club's parapet. As a hand touched his arm, he turned to see Harry Feversham standing beside him, his face a map of worry lines, his eyes burning with an otherworldly intensity. Sutch's initial hesitation gave way to curiosity as Harry's words tumbled forth in a rapid, troubled stream.
