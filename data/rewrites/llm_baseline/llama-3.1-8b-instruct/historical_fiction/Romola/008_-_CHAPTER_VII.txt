In the warm May afternoon sunlight, Messer Bartolommeo Scala sat beneath the shaded loggia of his palace, his gouty foot propped up on a cushion as he gazed out upon the lush gardens and verdant lawn beyond. His beautiful daughter, Alessandra, and her husband, the Greek soldier-poet Marullo, sat beside him, while two lesser-known friends listened intently on the opposite side. The silk lucco had been cast aside, and a lighter mantle draped over his tunic, its loose folds rustling gently in the breeze. Before him lay a stack of papers, a correspondence between himself and the renowned scholar, Politian, their words dancing across the page like the intricate patterns of a Tuscan fresco.

It was a peculiarity of the era that men of learning would often share their disputes, and Scala had invited his friends to weigh in on the balance of right and wrong in the exchange. The quarrel, though begun in jest, had taken on a life of its own, with each side volleying epigrams and witty barbs. The dispute revolved around a matter of the heart – Scala's rejection of Politian as a suitor for his daughter's hand, and the scholar's subsequent jabs at the secretary's literary endeavors. For despite his many accomplishments – a twenty-year tenure as secretary of the Florentine Republic, a stint as Gonfaloniere, and a title as Knight of the Golden Spur – Scala harbored a secret passion for writing, and his poetry, though well-meaning, was far from polished.

As he sat, surrounded by the trappings of his success, Scala couldn't help but feel a twinge of guilt. His friends, men like Ficino and Landino, had always been kind in their praise, finding elegance in his Latin prose, but he knew the truth – he was a novice, a dilettante playing at the art of writing. And yet, he couldn't help but continue, driven by a desire to create something of beauty, even if it brought him little more than a fleeting sense of satisfaction. The papers before him seemed to mock him, a reminder of his own limitations, and he pushed them aside, lost in thought as the sun dipped lower in the sky.
