The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the kitchen as Shirley prepared for a visit to the nearby rectory. She had promised Mrs. Pryor that she would persuade Caroline Helstone to join her for a brisk walk across Nunnely Common. The morning sunlight streaming through the windows highlighted the dust motes dancing in the air as Shirley carefully sliced a ripe pear for the afternoon tea. Her thoughts, however, were with the lively tune that had taken up residence in her head – a melody she had first heard the day before, whistled by a gentleman in the field beyond their hedge.

Shirley's fingers moved deftly as she mixed the dough, her hands remembering the sensation of whistling that very same tune just hours before. She had learned to whistle years ago, in a long-forgotten summer, but it was only yesterday that the sound had come flooding back. She recalled the way the notes had danced on her lips, a joyous expression spreading across her face as she let the music fill the air.

As she worked, Mrs. Pryor entered the kitchen, her eyes twinkling with amusement. "My dear, you're whistling again," she said, a hint of a smile playing on her lips. Shirley looked up, startled, and laughed, her eyes sparkling. "Forgive me, ma'am," she said, her cheeks flushing slightly. "I forgot myself." She paused, her hands moving to the counter as she gathered her thoughts.

"Where did you learn to whistle, Miss Keeldar?" Mrs. Pryor asked, her curiosity piqued. Shirley's response was nonchalant, her voice matter-of-fact. "Oh, I picked it up years ago. I don't recall exactly how." Mrs. Pryor raised an eyebrow, her expression skeptical. "And what gentleman reminded you of it, if I may ask?" Shirley's eyes sparkled mischievously as she replied, "Why, Mr. Moore, of course. He's the only gentleman around here who isn't gray-haired, after all."

Mrs. Pryor's expression turned serious, her gaze drifting away from Shirley's animated face. "You doth like Mr. Helstone, ma'am," Shirley teased, her tone light and playful.
