Silence seeped into the worn armchair like the soft lapping of waves against a shore. Archer sat with a sheet of paper in front of him, a faint pencil mark tracing the outline of a forgotten melody. He had come to Boston to escape the stifling air of summer, to breathe in the city's languid rhythm and let the world's din recede. The Somerset Club's breakfast room was a refuge, its patrons shuffling through the morning haze like sleepwalkers. Archer joined the ranks, ordering a simple meal and immersing himself in the Commercial Advertiser's columns.

A cigarette smoke curl wafted across his eyes, carrying with it the scent of worn leather and yesterday's news. He recognized the faces of acquaintances drifting in and out of the room – familiar, yet detached, like figures in a watercolor painting. The world outside seemed to be unfolding in a different tempo, its rhythms no longer synchronized with his own.

As he glanced at his watch, a sense of restlessness stirred within him. Nine-thirty had come and gone, and with it, the promise of a new day. He rose, collected his thoughts, and made his way to the writing room. A few hasty lines spilled onto the page, followed by a summons for a messenger to collect a response from the Parker House.

The answer, however, was not forthcoming. "The lady was out," the waiter informed him, his tone a monotone whisper. Archer felt a spark of frustration kindle within him – why had he not sent the note earlier? He seized his hat and stick, venturing into the sweltering heat of the day. The city, once a vibrant tapestry, now unfolded before him like a map of unfamiliar territories.

He wandered across the Common, the grass beneath his feet a verdant expanse of uncertainty. On a bench beneath a leafy tree, he spotted her – a figure draped in grey, a sunshade cast over her head like a veil. Her profile, a study in melancholy, drew him closer. As he approached, she turned, her eyes locking onto his. For an instant, a flicker of surprise danced across her features, before yielding to a tranquil smile.

"Oh," she murmured, her voice a gentle breeze on a summer's day. Without rising, she beckoned him to sit beside her, creating a space between them like a pause in a melody. Archer found himself drawn into her presence, the world outside receding as they sat together
