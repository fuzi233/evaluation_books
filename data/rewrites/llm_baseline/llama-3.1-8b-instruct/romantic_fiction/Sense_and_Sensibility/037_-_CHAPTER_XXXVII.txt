The aroma of freshly baked bread wafted through the air as Elinor settled into her favorite armchair in the Berkeley Street drawing room. Her mother had grown accustomed to visiting Mrs. Palmer less frequently, now that her daughter's health had improved significantly. With a sense of purpose, Elinor began her daily routine of needlework, lost in thought as she carefully stitched intricate patterns onto a delicate handkerchief.

Just then, the door swung open, and Mrs. Jennings swept into the room, her eyes sparkling with excitement. "Elinor, my dear, have you heard the news?" she asked, hardly giving her time to respond before continuing in a breathless rush.

"Not yet, ma'am," Elinor replied, setting aside her work to focus on her visitor.

Mrs. Jennings settled onto the adjacent sofa, her hands fluttering as she spoke. "It's simply too extraordinary! I visited Mrs. Palmer this morning, and Charlotte was beside herself with worry over the baby. It was crying, and covered in pimples – just a case of the red gum, of course – but Charlotte was convinced it was something far more serious."

Mrs. Jennings paused, allowing Elinor a moment to absorb the information before continuing. "I suggested we call for Mr. Donovan, and fortunately, he happened to be nearby. He examined the child and agreed with us that it was merely the red gum. As he was leaving, I asked him if there was any news, and he...well, let's just say he seemed to know more than he was letting on."

Elinor's curiosity was piqued, and she leaned forward, her eyes locked on Mrs. Jennings' animated face. "What is it, ma'am? Is Fanny ill?"

Mrs. Jennings nodded, her expression turning solemn. "Yes, dear. That's exactly what I said. 'Lord,' I exclaimed, 'is Mrs. Dashwood ill?' And then it all came out. You see, Mr. Edward Ferrars – the young man I've spoken to you about on occasion – has been secretly engaged to my cousin Lucy for over a year now!"

Elinor's fingers stilled on her needlework as the news sank in. "Engaged? But how could this have gone unnoticed for so long?"
