The air in the Tower of Observation was thick with the static of a dying world. I sat at the console, the hum of the aether vibrating through the floorboards, a low-frequency thrum that usually signaled communication. Tonight, however, the vibration carried a specific weight. It was the voice of Naani.

She spoke clearly, bypassing the ears and striking directly at the center of my consciousness. I recognized the cadence instantly—the rhythm of her thoughts, the unique texture of her presence. Yet, I did not respond immediately. Instead, I projected the Master-Word into the void, a simple query designed to test the channel.

The answer returned with mechanical precision, a steady beat in the silence. My internal logic demanded a response. Why speak now? The Monstruwacans in the Little Pyramid were in their deep sleep cycle, beginning at the eleventh hour. By the current time, fifteen hours later, they should be waking. Naani should be resting, confined to her quarters, separated from the observation deck by the laws of physics and duty. Her voice, transmitted via the Instrument, was too distinct, too intimate for a routine check-in during a shift change.

I queried her directly. No verbal reply followed. Instead, a sensation bloomed in my mind—a tremor that started in the marrow and spread outward. Words formed, not as sounds, but as images etched onto the retina of my soul.

The phrase hung in the mental space, heavy and resonant. A memory surfaced, unbidden and violent. I recalled crafting these exact words for Mirdath the Beautiful eons ago, in a time before the Great Silence, when she had died and left me adrift in a universe stripped of color. The emotional residue of that loss washed over me, weakening my resolve. I reached out with my brain-elements, demanding clarification. Who was speaking? Was this a trick of the failing aether?

Again, no direct answer. The words repeated, drifting across the dark expanse of the world. Suddenly, the distinction collapsed. It was not Naani speaking. It was Mirdath. The voice belonged to the ghost of the past, echoing from the eternal night. I screamed her name into the void, *"Mirdath!"* and the response came back, layered and complex. It was the voice of Mirdath, yes, but simultaneously the voice of Naani. The realization struck with the force of a physical blow: I was being reborn into this world, reunited with the Only One whose spirit had mated with mine across the ages.

I called to Naani, pouring every ounce of my strength into the transmission. Silence. No acknowledgment. No sign of reception. Hours passed in a blur of futile projection until exhaustion claimed me, dragging me into a forced slumber.

Waking abruptly, the first image that seized me was the impossible truth of the previous night. How could anyone else know those words? Only the spirit of Mirdath, standing behind my shoulder in that lost eternity, could have supplied them. The voice was hers, and through her, Naani's. The convergence was undeniable.

I summoned Naani again. Twice. Immediately, the Master-Word pulsed through the tower, solemn and rhythmic. I sent the assurance signal, and her voice returned, weaker than usual, lacking the amplification of the Instrument. She relied solely on her brain-elements now.

I questioned her urgently about the earlier transmission. She denied everything. She claimed ignorance, stating she had slept soundly through the entire duration and had instead experienced a vivid, disturbing dream. Confusion gripped me, a fog of uncertainty. Yet, the thrill of the aether persisted around us, urging her to share the vision.

She recounted the dream. In it, she stood in a small, dim room facing a towering figure, a man built of shadow and sorrow, clad in garments foreign to her culture. He was writing, seeking to articulate his pain. Though the script was alien to her waking mind, she could read the intent. She remembered only one line, written above the rest: *Mirdath.*

The name triggered a cascade of recognition. I trembled as I asked her to recall the rest of the inscription.

That was all she retained. Yet, it was enough. A strange, manic triumph surged through me. I projected the remainder of the phrase back to her, completing the sentence. The impact was instantaneous and violent, shattering her stupor. She stumbled, mute before the sudden clarity, her spirit reeling from the shock of confirmation. Tears welled in her distant gaze, purifying the bewilderment into a radiant, overwhelming joy.

She asked, her voice trembling with newfound certainty, *"Am I truly Mirdath?"*

I was too shaken by the splendor of this fulfillment to answer instantly. She pressed again, using my old name of affection, her tone sure despite the distance. Still, I found myself paralyzed, the blood roaring in my ears, speech lagging behind the magnitude of the revelation. This was the meeting of our spirits, spanning the everlasting night.

I visualized the scene: Naani standing in that far eternity, her spirit bridging the gap to mine, peering backward through the cracked gates of memory into our shared history. She saw more than I did; her vision extended to other convergences, other lives lived and lost, confusing and fragmented to me but sacred to her. We spoke of our current age as if it were merely yesterday, hallowed and precious.

The certainty of this union ignited a fierce desire within me. My heart and spirit screamed to bridge the physical divide, to be with the one who was Mirdath and now spoke as Naani. But the path was blocked. None of the scholars in the Mighty Pyramid knew the location of the Lesser Redoubt. The historical records offered only vague speculation, placing it somewhere between the North-West and North-East, but no one possessed the coordinates or the confidence to navigate the treacherous Outer Lands.

We discussed the geography extensively. Naani and her father, the Master Monstruwacan, knew nothing of our position, only that the builder of the Redoubt had emerged from the Southern World in the Beginning. The ancient Compass, set to bear, offered no aid. Its needle swung wildly in an arc between north and south, rendered useless by the chaotic flow of the Earth-Current beneath the Pyramid. Even the Great Museum's instrument spun uncontrollably, defying the magnetic laws of our era. We navigated by tradition, relying on names of direction and celestial markers that existed only in the collective memory of ancestors who had lived under a sun we had forgotten.

Naani pleaded with me to come to her, her hunger for reunion palpable. Yet, she forbade it, warning that risking death in the darkness of the dead world was preferable to the spiritual communion we already enjoyed. Ignorant of the impossibility of finding her without a coordinate, I dismissed her caution. If only I knew the direction, the distance—even if measured in thousands of miles—would be surmountable.

There was, however, a phenomenon that defied explanation. Whenever I projected my speech into the night, regardless of my conscious orientation, I invariably turned toward the North. The Master Monstruwacan subjected me to rigorous testing, blinding me with bandages and enclosing me in sensory-deprivation screens. Even deprived of external cues, I turned Northward with instinctive certainty. Unable to speak if forced to face another direction, I seemed compelled by an internal compass pointing solely to her.

Naani reported no such anomaly in her experience. We noted the discrepancy with curiosity, attributing my condition to the gravitational pull of her spirit. I suspected she lay in that northern darkness, though it remained a hypothesis rather than fact. The Master Monstruwacan documented my behavior in the Hour-Slips of the Tower, and soon the news rippled through the great cities. My ability to hear across distances became a subject of intense scrutiny, drawing praise and annoyance alike from the populace.

While I contemplated the mechanics of our reunion, disaster struck.

At the seventeenth hour, while the millions of the Pyramid slept, I was on duty in the observation tower. The aether thrummed violently. Naani's voice cut through the noise, urgent and strained. I sent the Master-Word, and the answer returned, solemn and fading. Calling to her, I sensed a crisis. The people of the Lesser Redoubt were in mortal peril. The Earth-Current had failed catastrophically. They had summoned her, hoping for a signal from us, but the silence from our side confirmed their worst fears.

Joy had evaporated from the Redoubt in the span of an hour, replaced by a rapid, crushing despair. They interpreted the cessation of the Current as the final flicker before extinction. As we spoke, her voice drifted further away, becoming indistinct. The thought of losing her completely broke my heart.

Throughout the remaining sleep-cycle, I conversed with Naani as lovers preparing for a final separation. When the cities awoke, the news spread like wildfire, plunging the millions into mourning.

For perhaps a month, the connection deteriorated. Naani's voice grew so faint that even my enhanced Night-Hearing struggled to parse her meaning. Every syllable became a treasure, a tactile touch upon my soul. Grief consumed me; I could not eat, could not rest. The Master Monstruwacan chastised me, arguing that my health was essential if we were to attempt any rescue, noting that the recording instruments were now silent.
