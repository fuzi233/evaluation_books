The dim glow of the gas lamps cast a melancholic sheen over the rain-soaked streets of Hammersmith, as I stood before the door of a small, unassuming boarding-house. A placard nailed to the wall proclaimed 'No Vacancy,' and I felt a familiar pang of desperation as I knocked again, this time with a mixture of resignation and hope. The door swung open to reveal a pinched-faced woman, her eyes red-rimmed from the smoke of countless fires and endless meals cooked for the likes of me.

"No room. Full up," she intoned, her voice a monotone of tired familiarity.

I felt the weight of my hunger settle upon me like a shroud, a reminder that I'd spent the better part of the day scouring the streets for work, only to be rebuffed at every turn. The rain seeped into my bones, chilling me to the marrow, and I knew I couldn't afford to spend another night on the streets. The woman's gaze flickered towards the doorway behind her, where a meager fire crackled in the hearth, casting flickering shadows on the walls.

I recalled the words of a fellow traveler, a Swede named Magnus, who'd spoken of the simple pleasures of a well-cooked meal, the warmth of a fire, and the comfort of a soft bed. His stories had transported me to a world far removed from this bleak, unforgiving city, where the only solace lay in the promise of a hot meal and a dry roof. I pushed aside the thoughts, focusing instead on the practicalities of survival.

As I stood there, a sudden memory flashed before me – a snippet of conversation with a fellow traveler, a Mr. Wraxall, who'd regaled me with tales of his own travels in Scandinavia. His words painted vivid pictures of ancient manor houses, their grandeur and beauty a testament to the region's storied past. I recalled, too, his descriptions of the traditional Swedish cuisine – hearty stews and roasts, served with an array of pickled vegetables and fresh bread. My stomach growled in response, a reminder that, for now, such indulgences remained a distant dream.

The woman's gaze snapped back to me, her expression a mixture of annoyance and curiosity. "What can I do for you?" she asked, her voice a little softer now.

I hesitated, weighing my options. To beg for a place to sleep, or to continue my search for work?
