As Warrington settled into his armchair, his eyes fixed on the pages of his book, Pen strode into the room, a look of wistful nostalgia on his face. His hands fluttered about his waistcoat pockets, as if searching for something lost, before coming to rest on a sheaf of papers clutched tightly within. The manuscript, penned in his youth, lay scattered across the table, its pages yellowed with age and worn smooth by the touch of countless fingers.

This novel, born from the tumultuous passions of his own heartbreak and financial woes, had been a fervent exercise in Byronic despair and Wertherian despondency. The ink-stained pages seemed to whisper tales of a bygone era, when Pen's heart was aflame with the poetry of his favorite German authors. As he pored over the manuscript, memories long buried rose to the surface – the scent of freshly sharpened pencils, the feel of tears staining the paper, and the ache of unrequited love.

With a wry smile, Pen recalled the tender verses he had penned for Miss Amory, his supposed eternal flame, which he had hidden away in the hollow of a tree. The dedication 'Amori' seemed almost laughable now, a playful wordplay that spoke more of his naivety than any genuine ardor. Warrington, sensing his friend's melancholy, offered a sympathetic puff on his pipe, his eyes glinting with amusement.

"Ah, yes, 'Amori' – a clever play on words, indeed," Warrington drawled, reaching for the manuscript with the fire-tongs. "Let us hear more of this...rubbish." As he settled into the pages, his voice wove a spell of intimacy, drawing Pen back into the world of Walter Lorraine, where love and heartache wrestled with the darkness. "False as thou art beautiful! Heartless as thou art fair! Mock me not with thy lovely face, nor with thy words, which pierce my soul like a dagger's sting..."

As Warrington's voice danced across the room, Pen's eyes followed the words, tracing the paths of his own youth, now distant and faded. The blots on the page, like the impressions left by camels' hooves in the desert sand, spoke of a time when emotions ran deep and true.
