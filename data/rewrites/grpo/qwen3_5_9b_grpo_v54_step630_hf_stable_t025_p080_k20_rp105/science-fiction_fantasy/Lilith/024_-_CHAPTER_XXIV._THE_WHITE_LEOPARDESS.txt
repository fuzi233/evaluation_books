The moonlight spilled across the cobblestones at the foot of the stairs, illuminating a street choked by an unwholesome, inhospitable wind. Yet, within my retreat, the air remained still. As I composed myself for rest, my eyes snapped open. There, rising above the highest step, was the head of the shining creature I had witnessed trailing the Shadow. The instant our gazes met, she halted, then began to retreat, tail first. I lunged forward, but trapped against the wall, she executed a frantic backward somersault—head over tail—and scrambled down the stairs, vanishing into the gloom. I chased her to the street's edge, scanning the darkness, but she was gone. I returned to my hard couch, my mind racing.

Two evils prowled Bulika now: one spotted, one unspotted. Though I held little value for men or women in this city, the life of a child outweighed my own precarious existence. I resolved to guard the door until dawn.

Soon, the latch dragged slowly. I rose as the door creaked ajar. Standing there was not the woman I had aided, but the muffled figure from the desert. She said nothing, guiding me into an empty, stone-paved chamber and indicating a rug on the floor. Wrapped in its coarse weave, I lay down once more. She sealed the inner door, and the sound of the outer door closing echoed like a final verdict. Darkness pressed in, broken only by the pale moonlight filtering through cracks.

Sleep eluded me. A stifled moan drifted through the silence, growing into the cry of a child, which abruptly shattered into a terrible shriek. I sprang up and darted into the passage. From a side door emerged the white leopardess, clutching a newborn infant in her jaws, carrying it as if it were her own cub. I threw myself upon her, forcing her to release the baby. The infant hit the stone slabs with a piteous wail.

The muffled woman appeared instantly. She stepped over our tangled forms in the narrow corridor, scooped up the child, and vanished. Returning, she lifted me from the beast, opened the door, and pushed me gently out. The leopardess followed at my heels.

"She has failed me," I thought, anger flaring. "She has surrendered me to the beast to be settled with at her leisure! But we shall have a tussle for it!"

I ran down the stairs, fearing she would spring onto my back, but she moved quietly behind me. At the bottom, I turned to seize her, but she leaped over my head. When I spun to face her, she was crouching at my feet. I stooped, stroking her sleek white fur; she responded by licking my bare feet with a hard, dry tongue. I patted and fondled her, a well of tenderness swelling in my chest. She might be treacherous, yet if I rejected every display of affection lest it be feigned, how could I ever find the genuine love that must exist somewhere in every world?

She rose, standing beside me.

Suddenly, a bulky object thudded heavily into the middle of the street, a few yards away. I ran to it, discovering a pulpy mass, barely retaining the form of a woman. She must have been hurled from a neighboring window. I scanned the surroundings: the Shadow walked along the opposite side of the way, and the white leopardess trailed at his heel once more.

I followed, urging in my heart that the beast was likely not acting of her own free will. As I closed the distance, she turned and flew at me with a hideous snarl, forcing me to recoil. Instantly, she resumed her place behind the Shadow. I approached again; she attacked, her eyes blazing like live emeralds. A third time I tested her, and she snapped at me like a dog, biting my hand. My heart faltered, and I cried out. The creature paused, looking at me with a gaze that plainly asked, "Why WOULD you make me do this?"

Turning away, angry at my own foolishness, I realized I had wasted precious time since entering this place. It was night, yet I would go straight to the palace. From the square, I had seen it—a high fortress looming above the city's heart, circled by defenses that seemed more military than royal.

I found the fortifications much neglected, partly ruinous. For centuries, clearly, they had been ignored. Great gates stood open before a rocky chasm, with a drawbridge that looked unused, and it was hard to believe water had ever filled the hollow. All was so still that sleep seemed to permeate the structure, making the moonlight appear discordantly awake. I must enter like a thief or shatter a silence that made the mere thought of noise terrifying.

Like an outcast dog, I paced the walls until I reached a small recess with a stone bench. Taking refuge from the wind, I lay down and, despite the chill, fell fast asleep.

I was awakened by something leaping upon me, licking my face with the rough tongue of a feline. "It is the white leopardess!" I thought. "She has come to suck my blood! And why should she not? It would cost me more to defend than to yield!" I lay still, expecting pain. Instead, a pleasant warmth began to diffuse through me. Stretched at my back, she lay as close as possible, her body heat slowly penetrating mine, her breath—stripped of all wildness—swathing my head and face in a genial atmosphere. A full conviction that her intent was good took hold. I turned like a sleepy child, threw my arm over her, and sank into profound unconsciousness.

When I began to stir, I fancied I lay warm and soft in my own bed. "Is it possible I am at home?" I thought. The familiar scents of the garden seemed to crowd in. I rubbed my eyes and looked out: I lay on a bare stone, in the heart of a hateful city!

I sprang from the bench. Had I truly shared a bed with a leopardess, or had it been a dream? She had just left, for the warmth of her body lingered on my skin.

Leaving the recess, I carried a new hope, strong though shapeless. One thing was clear: I must find the princess. Surely I possessed some power over her, if not dominion. Had I not saved her life, and had she not prolonged it at the expense of my vitality? This reflection gave me the courage to face her, whatever she might be.
