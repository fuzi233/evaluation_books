As I stepped across the threshold, a chill, like that of an ice-house, enveloped me. The door creaked shut behind us, and the sexton murmured something to his wife, causing her to turn towards us. In that moment, her countenance transformed, as if the splendor of her eyes had overflowed, flooding her face with a radiant beauty reminiscent of a blooming rose. Her gaze seemed to hold the essence of life itself, immortal and eternal, shining with an unbroken light. Even her hands appeared luminous, like moonstones, each pearl-shell ornament glinting with a soft, ethereal glow. Her beauty was overwhelming, and I was grateful when she turned her attention away from me.

The faint candlelight barely penetrated the darkness, making it difficult to discern our surroundings at first. As we walked further, the light danced across a figure lying on the floor, partially raised from the ground. Could a living being truly rest in such a cold, unforgiving environment? The thought sent a shiver down my spine. A fainter glow emanated from beyond, and I caught glimpses of indistinct shapes all around us.

We strolled along an aisle of raised platforms, each one bearing a figure wrapped in a sheet as white as freshly fallen snow. My heart grew heavy with a sense of foreboding as I realized we were walking among rows of slumbering forms. Every step revealed more of the seemingly endless procession of couches, stretching out in every direction, disappearing into the shadows. Was this to be my resting place, surrounded by the silent, unmoving bodies of the departed? Was this the sexton's library, filled with the "books" of the dead?

"One of the cellars I'm tasked to watch," Mr. Raven whispered, his voice barely audible over the hush of the room, as if he feared disturbing the slumbering figures. "The wine laid here will age and mature." He glanced at his wife, and his tone softened. "But it's dark for a traveler such as yourself."

"The moon is rising," his wife replied, her voice low and melancholic, carrying the weight of centuries of sorrow. "She'll bring light soon."
