Inside the grandeur of Santa Maria del Fiore, the Duomo's majestic vaults rose above the throngs of Florentine faithful, their faces upturned in rapt attention towards the pulpit. Amidst the sea of expectant faces, one pair of eyes caught sight of a figure standing frozen at the entrance – a man bound by rope, his eyes a deep well of desperation. For a moment, they lingered on his weathered features, etched by the hardships of a life lived outdoors, and the faint scent of smoke clung to his tattered cloak like a reminder of a life on the margins. It was a face that commanded attention, yet the onlookers' gazes soon wavered, drawn back to the Dominican friar's impassioned words, his voice a force that electrified the air.

Romola, seated near the rear of the nave, had entered quietly through a side door, and from her vantage point, she took in the entire scene. Her gaze drifted towards the newcomer, lingering on the rope that bound his wrists, and the lines etched on his face told a tale of a life marked by struggle. She felt a pang of compassion for the stranger, her heart stirred by the sight of his grey hair and the deep sorrow that seemed to seep from his very pores. Though her eyes had met his for a fleeting instant, he had not noticed her, lost as he was in the turmoil of his own memories.

As he stood there, his mind reeled under the weight of the past, images of Tito's face haunted him like a specter, blending with the gnawing hunger and the parched dryness of his throat. His thoughts were a maelstrom of conflicting emotions, each one battering him with the ferocity of a tempest. The words of the preacher pierced the turmoil, resonating deep within him like the reverberations of a distant bell. The phrase 'The day of vengeance is at hand!' echoed through his mind, a promise of deliverance that seemed to stir a glimmer of hope within him.

Baldassarre's gaze snapped upwards, his eyes drawn to the preacher, though he could only make out the general outline of the man's features. He yearned to hear the words again, to feel the rush of emotion that had shaken him to his core.
