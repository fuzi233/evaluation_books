As the last of the French contingents departed Florence, Romola wrapped her mantle tightly around her, her eyes fixed on the flickering torches that illuminated the cobblestone streets. She stood beneath the loggia, a tranquil oasis amidst the vibrant bustle of the city, and listened intently to the distant peals of the bells as the departing troops and royal retinue passed through the Porta San Piero. The evening air was alive with the scent of roasting chestnuts and the soft murmur of conversation, a heady mixture that usually filled her with a sense of joy and community. Tonight, however, the atmosphere was tinged with an undercurrent of unease.

Her thoughts turned to Tito, whose distant anxiety had become a constant presence in her life. She recalled the haunting image of the prisoner grasping him, as depicted in Piero's vivid sketch, and felt a shiver run down her spine. Though she couldn't quite articulate why, she sensed that the departure of the French might somehow ease the weight of Tito's concerns.

As dusk gave way to night, Romola descended into the library, the warm glow of the lamp casting a golden light on the shelves lined with ancient tomes. She resolved to immerse herself in her work, the meticulous copying of the catalogue, in an attempt to shake off the lingering unease. Her fingers moved deftly over the parchment, the scratch of the quill a soothing accompaniment to her racing thoughts.

Before she knew it, the silence was broken by the soft creak of the door. Tito entered, his face aglow with the faint light of the setting sun. Romola's heart skipped a beat as she rose from her seat, her hands fluttering to unwind his becchetto. "Tito, dearest, I didn't expect you so soon," she said, her voice barely above a whisper.

Tito flashed his brightest smile, his eyes crinkling at the corners as he clasped her close. For a moment, Romola allowed herself to be enveloped in the warmth of his embrace, her fears momentarily forgotten. As he held her, she felt a spark of hope ignite within her – perhaps, just perhaps, the old happiness would return. Tito was trying to be like himself, to recapture the easy camaraderie they once shared, but Romola sensed a deeper anxiety beneath the surface.
