As I pressed northward and westward, I navigated with extreme caution, steering clear of the Great Watcher looming in the northwest. My mind raced through the logistics of this monumental journey. First, I calculated my pace, concluding that moderation was essential; the destination was unknown, and the path ahead was vast. Next, I organized my routine: eating and drinking every six hours, with a block of sleep from the eighteenth to the twenty-fourth hour. This schedule allowed for three meals and six hours of rest daily. While this regimen seemed ideal, the Night Land was fraught with peril, and I knew I would often be forced to abandon sleep to watch for threats.

True to human nature, I broke this discipline immediately. For twenty-one hours, I walked without rest, hiding and creeping through areas likely to reveal me to the Watcher. Hunger gnawed at me, making the thought of food repulsive. Only after those grueling hours did exhaustion force me to seek shelter. In the distance, I spotted a small fire-hole, a familiar sight from my earlier travels. It offered warmth against the biting cold and a potential dry spot for slumber.

Approaching cautiously, I found it to be a cheerful enclave amidst the gloom. A dull, bubbling fire filled the hole, emitting a faint sulfur smoke. Sitting nearby, I placed my Diskos on the rock beside me. For a while, I remained motionless, too weary to eat or drink, my gaze fixed on the Mighty Pyramid. Though I had traveled far, it still appeared terrifyingly close, its immense scale shaking me with the weight of the task ahead. Yet, there was solace in its proximity; I knew millions watched over me from within. I made no signal, for a constant farewell was inappropriate; the command was simply to *GO*. It was strange to behave so distantly from humanity while standing so near my home, yet I accepted this solitude. I was happy to imagine that Master Monstruwacan, my dear friend, might be observing me through the Great Spy-Glass at that very moment.

Realizing my hesitation to eat was foolish, I opened my scrip and consumed three tablets of dense, strong food. They were not filling, so I shook dust from a special tube into a cup. The air acted upon the dust chemically, causing it to boil and fizz until it transformed into simple water. Thus, my small pack sustained me for a long time, a necessary comfort in this dark, hungry land.

I reviewed my gear, retrieving a small compass crafted by Master Monstruwacan. He had warned me that I might lose my way in the vast darkness, but if the ancient principle within the machine still functioned—perhaps oriented toward the Pyramid rather than the North—it could guide me home. I set it on the ground, but it spun wildly. I realized I was still high above the Underground Fields, too far from the "Crack" for the Earth-Current to stabilize the needle. I wondered if Monstruwacan could see my test through the Spy-Glass, though the shifting lights and rising mists of the Night Land made visual confirmation unreliable.

Eventually, I packed the compass and prepared for sleep. I recalled that, after many days of travel, I had indeed found the needle drawn northward—a comforting thought. Later, far from the Redoubt, I discovered a new power: the compass pointed westward, indicating a Great Power in the Darkness swaying it. This revelation brought a childlike wonder; it suggested the ancient Northward Force was active, a knowledge felt in the heart rather than just understood by the brain. I wondered if the Lesser Redoubt was influencing the needle, signaling my approach to the journey's end, but I trusted the North.

Wrapped in my cloak, I lay by the fire-hole, the Diskos nestled against my chest. As I drifted into a drowsy state, I felt the aether surge around me, stirred by the millions of watchers in the Redoubt who sympathized with my spirit. Exhausted, I slept deeply, my last conscious thought of Naani, the sweet maid I sought. In dreams, she spoke to me, surrounded by fairy-light, freeing me from life's sorrow.

Suddenly, a mighty howl shattered the silence—the Home-Call. Swiftly, I slid from my cloak, gripping the Diskos. I knew a Terror was approaching; the Night Land had been awakened. Peering toward the Pyramid, I scanned the upper blackness where the Tower of Observation's Final Light shone. Then, I saw them: quick, darting flashes of green fire. In the Set-Speech, they spelled a urgent warning: a Great Grey Man had scent of me and was crawling through the moss-bushes behind the fire-hole. The message was sharp: leap left and hide.

I vanished into the shadows of a moss-clump, my heart trembling with cold terror. From the bushes beyond the fire, the Grey Man emerged. It was a great, grey creature, laying its head to the ground to spy along the earth, avoiding the fire's glare. It crept back and forth three times to each side, hunching its shoulders and thrusting its jaw like a nasty beast. Each movement shook my courage, fearing it might discover me from the rear. However, my heightened hearing proved vital. After its final sweep, it retreated into the bushes, seemingly failing to find me.

Then, a voice spoke plainly within my spirit, warning that the creature was circling to take me from the other side. I knew this was Master Monstruwacan, using his brain-elements to communicate via my Night-Hearing, confirmed by the solemn throb of the Master-Word beating in the night. Trusting the message, I leaped to another clump, crouching low with my spirit's ears open. Suddenly, a bush behind me moved. A great grey hand pushed aside the moss, followed by the Grey Man's head, which entered the clump where I had been hiding.

I knew I must strike. I leaped and smote with the Diskos. The beast fell to its side, its great grey legs twitching and drawing upward, while its head remained trapped in the bush. I stood back as it died, the Diskos spinning in my hand, sending forth fire as if alive, celebrating the slaying of the horrid monster. I moved to the far side of the fire-hole, holding the Diskos high and spinning to signal my victory to the Redoubt.

Master Monstruwacan spoke no further words, for it was not meet to disturb the aether unnecessarily. Calming my fear, I felt the gladness of the millions in the Redoubt vibrating through the night air, a wave of sympathetic fear and friendship. I gathered my wits, realizing I had slept through ten hours, violating my own rules. Resolving to obey reason in the future, I ate three more tablets and drank water, then circled the fire-hole before turning back toward the Pyramid. I made no salutation, wishing to avoid stirring the emotions of the Millions.

I moved swiftly and cautiously into the night, bearing the Diskos with a newfound love for the strange weapon that had befriended me. It seemed to live with the fire of the Earth-Current, a bond shared only by those of olden days who carried swords. The Redoubt knew that the Diskos belonged to its user; a stranger handling it would find it clumsy and dangerous. This affinity was a law of the land, rooted in the connection between man and his tool.

As I ventured deeper, the perspective shifted. Like a traveler ascending to the stars, the landscape rearranged itself. The fixed visions of my life within the Redoubt dissolved; the Night Land revealed new aspects with every step. Approaching the monstrous Watcher of the Northwest, I crept within a mile, only to be confounded by its appearance. Seen from the side, it resembled a vast cliff face hanging over the Red Pit, scored and weathered by the bloody shine rising from below. Lying prone, I stared into its monstrous bulk, a squat, rugged hill of blackness dotted with warts that glowed like lunar mountains under the red fire.

A profound unease settled over me. I felt the aether disturbed by the distress of the Millions, sensing their cunning awareness of my location. The thrilling vibration brought wisdom: I was foolish to linger. The Watcher might sense my trouble. Gathering courage, I retreated to a safe distance, feeling the quiet, steadfast life of the Monster surrounding me, a Great Evil Power gazing upon me. Yet, I commanded my soul to courage, moving quietly for two miles until I felt separate from its spirit.

At the eighteenth hour, I paused to eat and drink, looking back at the Watching-Thing, a silent, solitary sentinel against the red glow. I continued for six more hours, reaching "The Place Where The Silent Ones Kill." It was a barren, rocky plain where no bushes grew, offering no hiding. A faint, grey, lichen-like light covered the desolation, creating shifting shadows that hinted at the presence of Silent Ones. Sliding quietly through the moss-bushes to the north of this plain, I searched for movement but saw none.

The plain stretched out before me, ending abruptly. I spotted a small, bare patch and decided to run across it to save time. As I rose from the bushes, I saw vague figures in the greyness: a line of lofty, shrouded figures standing motionless, watching me. My heart failed, but they did not advance. Finding courage, I backed slowly into the bushes and circled wide to avoid the Plain of Destruction.

After twenty-four weariful hours, I sought a fire-hole for warmth. Near one, I heard a large, husky voice speaking ordinary things in a monstrous, house-like tone. Fear paralyzed me until a second voice answered, and the speech of elephant-sized men rose from the hollow—brutish and hoarse. I crouched, moving moss aside to peek down. There, in the center of the hollow, burned a large fire-hole. Surrounding it were great holes in the slopes where monstrous men slept, curled inward. Beyond the fire sat three giants, larger than elephants, covered in reddish hair and warts, skinning a massive Night-Hound. They held bloody stones, staring at the ground, listening intently.

I froze. They sensed me. As I tried to retreat, I kicked a little dust into the hollow. Instantly, the three giants looked up, their eyes shining red and green like animals. A roar erupted, nearly destroying my soul. All the sleeping giants woke and surged into the hollow. I was lost, but as I fled backward, the earth gave way beneath me. I fell into a hole among the moss-bushes behind me, choking on dust and ash. I lay still, refusing to breathe or cough. The search passed southward, saved by some unseen power.

Emerging shaken and weak, I lay in the bushes for ten hours, starving and freezing. Upon waking, I stamped my feet and warmed myself, then ate four tablets and drank double the usual water to replenish my emptiness. I resumed my journey, pausing to look at the Watcher's humped back, fueling my hatred for the monster. I gazed upward at the Great Redoubt, feeling doubly lost, but quickly turned away, gathering my courage.

Twelve hours later, I heard a strange, low humming noise from above, seemingly coming from a great distance yet close overhead. It was the Sound of the Doorways in the Night. History recorded that those who heard it usually met destruction by invisible evil forces. Panic seized me; I bared my arm to access the death capsule beneath my skin, ready to die instantly. I crept silently away, but the sound faded. I realized I had passed beneath the opening before it fully manifested. Shaken, I continued, hearing the sound again further ahead. I hid, then ran, only to realize I had passed it safely. The sound was a phantom of my fear.

By the eighteenth hour, I reached a safe hollow to sleep. Afterward, I walked through a bleak, desolate country, avoiding the Doorways. I passed nineteen great fire-holes and four small ones. At one, I saw a creature resembling a scorpion but squat and thick. I felt a restful spirit as I avoided monsters, growing bolder until I reached the North-West border of the Plain of Blue Fire.

This Plain was a terrifying void, a cold blue light rising from the earth that swallowed all vision. Hidden peoples of spirits wandered within it, calling to one another blindly. I kept two miles away, coasting along the edge for two days, sleeping under bushes and on rock ledges, bitter cold but unharmed. On the third day, emerging from the Plain, I saw the Mighty Pyramid again, a distant speck of light on my right. The aether stirred as news of my reappearance spread through the Redoubt. The Millions thought of me, their emotions rippling through the night, a sweet, homely sympathy that bolstered my spirit.

At the eighteenth hour, I found a boiling hot fountain shooting water northward. Following the steam, I found the water warm and bathed my tender feet. Suddenly, the bay of a Night-Hound echoed from the northwest. Horror struck me; the beast was tracking me. I leaped into the shallow stream, running down its middle. The Hound's bellow followed, closing the distance. I stopped, then dove into deeper water, lying on my belly, head above the surface. The Hound galloped past, tearing the earth, then bayed further up the stream. I stayed submerged, motionless, until the beast passed. I rose and followed the warm stream, keeping to the water to mask my scent, traveling twelve hours while the Hound searched endlessly.

The stream led me to the House of Silence. I was terrified, for my goal was to avoid it. The river breached the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk, leading directly to the House. I left the water, creeping through the moss-bushes westward, away from the House. Soon, the bushes thinned, revealing bare rock. I retreated, finding the Road bent dangerously close to the House. Above me, the monstrous, huge structure stood in the night, full of quiet lights, brooding over the land. The silence around it was an anguish of terror. I hid among the bushes, feeling the sheer dread of the place, remembering the youths who had entered it.
