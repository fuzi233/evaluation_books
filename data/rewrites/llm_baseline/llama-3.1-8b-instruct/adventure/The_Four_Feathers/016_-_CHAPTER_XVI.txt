As Ethne stood amidst the lush greenery of the garden, the scent of blooming jasmine wafted through the air, transporting her to a place where memories of Harry Feversham lingered like the fragrance on her skin. It had been years since Captain Willoughby had shared the story of the soiled feather, and in that moment, all thoughts of Colonel Durrance had faded from her mind like the ebbing tide. Yet, as she gazed at the vibrant flowers surrounding her, the weight of those five long years came flooding back – the years she had stood tall, a mask of courage firmly in place, greeting the world with a warm smile.

Her reverie was shattered by the rustling of leaves as Colonel Durrance emerged from the hedge. "Hush," she whispered to Willoughby, her face paling as a spasm of pain wracked her body. Though she had savored the brief respite from her duties during their conversation, reality beckoned, and she knew she couldn't afford to indulge her emotions. With a newfound sense of resolve, she steeled herself to hide her feelings, determined to keep up the charade of a life unmarred by the burdens of her past.

As Durrance stepped further into the enclosure, his presence seemed to fill the space, commanding attention. Ethne's eyes darted about, searching for an escape, but the creek stretched out before them, and the dense hedge walls confined them on all sides. She knew she'd find no respite. "Keep still," she whispered urgently, her voice barely audible. "Do you know him?" she asked, her gaze locked on Willoughby.

"We were comrades at Suakin for three years," he replied quietly, noticing the ease with which Durrance moved. "I'd heard he'd lost his sight, but it seems that's not the case." His words hung in the air, and Ethne's fingers tightened around his arm, a warning to remain silent.

"Speak softer," she cautioned, her voice low and urgent. "It's true; he's blind."

Willoughby's brow furrowed in concern. "What can I say to him?" he asked, his voice laced with empathy.

"Nothing," Ethne replied firmly.
