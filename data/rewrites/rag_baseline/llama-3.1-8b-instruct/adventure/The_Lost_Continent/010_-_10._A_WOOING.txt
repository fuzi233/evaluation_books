As I emerged from the crowd, the silence that had descended over the assembly seemed almost palpable, a physical presence that pressed against my skin. The Empress, Phorenice, stood beside the altar, her eyes fixed on me with a mixture of curiosity and amusement. I approached her with a measured pace, my footsteps echoing off the stone walls. At the prescribed distance, I performed the formal salute she had taught me, my movements stiff and precise.

"Deucalion, man of ice," she said, her voice husky and confident.

"I still adhere to the old Gods," I replied, trying to gauge her reaction.

Her sidelong smile hinted at a deeper meaning, one that I couldn't quite decipher. Before I could press her for an explanation, Ylga intervened, her face pale and her hand trembling as she begged for mercy on my behalf. "If he won't abandon the old Gods yet, surely you'll pardon him?" she implored. "He's a strong man, and it wouldn't be easy to convert him. You can try to change him later."

Phorenice's expression darkened, her eyes flashing with anger. "You dare to meddle in my affairs, Ylga?" she spat. "I have you to attend to my wardrobe and carry my fan – is that not enough? Get out of here, and don't come back until you've learned some respect!"

The girl bowed her head, backing away with a look of fear. Phorenice turned to me, her eyes narrowing. "My lord, I'm like a bird on the verge of taking flight. I want to test my wings, to see what they're capable of."

I nodded, trying to hide my unease. "May each new flight be for the good of Atlantis," I said, reciting the phrase she had taught me.

Her gaze lingered on mine, a spark of mischief dancing in her eyes. "We'll discuss this further in the pyramid," she said, her voice dripping with sweetness. "For now, please, my lord, escort me back to my riding beast."

As I led her out of the circle, the silence seemed to grow thicker, a heavy fog that clung to my skin.
