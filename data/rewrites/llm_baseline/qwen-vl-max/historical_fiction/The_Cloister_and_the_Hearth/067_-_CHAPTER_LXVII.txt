It would have been remarkable, indeed, if men with so little to go on could have deciphered the silent message written upon the riverbank. For there, in that very place, an event had just unfolded—one so singular in its nature that it may well stand unmatched in human history, and perhaps remain so for all time. Yet it shall be told briefly, in part by me, and in part by one who took part in it.

Gerard, after writing a short farewell to Pietro and Andrea, slipped away to the river at dusk. He had resolved upon his course with a grim determination not uncommon among those who seek their own end. To ensure he would not float, he filled his pockets with every coin of silver and copper he owned. Then, prepared, he hastened to a secluded stretch of the stream, one he knew was seldom visited.

Some people—especially women—look about them to make sure they are not alone. But Gerard scanned the darkness not for companionship, but for solitude. And to his irritation, he saw a solitary figure leaning against the corner of a narrow alley. He pretended to wander off casually, but returned soon after. There stood the same man, now emerging from a side street, idling as if by chance.

“Could he be watching me? Could he know what I’m here for?” Gerard thought. “Impossible.”

He walked briskly down a few streets, made a wide turn, and came back. The man was gone. But when Gerard glanced around once more, there he was—only a few yards behind, apparently tying his shoe.

Then Gerard understood: he was being watched. At that moment, moonlight caught the glint of a steel gauntlet in the stranger’s hand. He knew at once—it was an assassin.

Yet strangely, it never occurred to him that his own life was the target. After all, he did not believe himself to have any enemies in the world.

He turned and approached the man directly. “My good friend,” he said urgently, “sell me your arm! Just one blow! See—here is everything I possess!” He thrust the coins into the other’s hands. “Oh, please! Please do one kind deed—end this hateful life for me!”

Even as he spoke, he unfastened his doublet and bared his chest.

The man stared at him, motionless.

“Why do you hesitate?” Gerard cried. “Have you no pity? Is it so hard to raise your arm and strike? Is it because I am poor and cannot pay you gold? You useless creature—you can only kill from behind, not face a man like a man. Very well—turn your head and keep silent!”

With a snarl of scorn, he fled, leaping into the river.

“Margaret!”

At the heavy splash of his body hitting the water, the assassin seemed to awaken from a trance. He raced to the bank, and with a cry unlike any other, plunged into the current after the man who had thrown himself into death.

What happened next will be told by the assassin himself.
