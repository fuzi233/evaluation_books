The sun cast its warm rays upon the grand estate of Castlewood, illuminating the preparations for the long-awaited gathering in honor of Madam Esmond's esteemed guest, General Braddock. As the appointed hour drew near, Madam Esmond busied herself with final touches, ensuring every detail was in place to create an atmosphere of elegance and refinement. Her thoughts, however, were not entirely on the festivities; she couldn't help but feel a pang of melancholy, for the occasion marked a significant milestone – her son George's impending departure for the war-torn lands beyond the colonies.

The General's aide-de-camp, the illustrious Colonel Washington, arrived at the designated hour, his arrival announced by the soft clip-clop of his horse's hooves on the well-manicured lawn. As he dismounted, his servants efficiently led the majestic steeds to the designated stables, where they would be cared for until the end of the evening. It was a testament to Colonel Washington's reputation as a skilled horseman that his mounts were among the finest in the colony.

As the Colonel stepped into the covered gallery, he was greeted by Madam Esmond, resplendent in a gown that sparkled like the stars on a clear night. They exchanged pleasantries, discussing the weighty matters of state and the impending campaign, while the negro servants busied themselves with the preparation of the feast below. The air was filled with the enticing aromas wafting from the kitchen, mingling with the sweet scent of blooming flowers that adorned the grounds.

Colonel Washington's words brought a gentle smile to Madam Esmond's face as he spoke of her son's prospects under his care. "You will take good care of my boy, sir," she implored, her voice trembling slightly. "He looks up to you, just as his father did before him." The Colonel's eyes locked onto hers, his expression a picture of sincerity. "Madam, I would walk through fire for your family's sake. Your son is in good hands, I assure you."

As they conversed, the sound of carriage wheels echoed through the stillness, signaling the arrival of another distinguished guest – none other than Benjamin Franklin, the esteemed statesman from Pennsylvania. His humble beginnings as a printer's apprentice seemed a distant memory now, as he rode in the carriage of the Commander-in-Chief himself. Colonel Washington nodded in approval, his voice filled with admiration for the man's ingenuity and worth.
