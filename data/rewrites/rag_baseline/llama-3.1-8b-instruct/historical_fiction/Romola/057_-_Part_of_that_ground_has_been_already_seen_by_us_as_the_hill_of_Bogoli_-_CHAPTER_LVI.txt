As the morning sun cast its warm rays upon the cobblestone streets of Florence, Romola strolled along the walls of the city, taking in the tranquil atmosphere of the Borgo La Croce. The air was filled with the sweet scent of blooming jasmine, and the sound of distant laughter carried on the gentle breeze. She had just finished a leisurely walk along the Arno River, taking in the vibrant colors of the market stalls selling fresh produce and artisanal goods.

As she approached the intersection of Via del Proconsolo and Piazza della Signoria, Romola noticed a small, barefoot child, no more than three years old, pause from his energetic run and look around in confusion. His back, adorned with a patchwork of red and brown curls, was the first thing she saw, but as he turned towards her, his dark eyes welled up with tears, and his lower lip quivered in distress. The sight of a tall figure looming behind him sent the child's fear into overdrive, and a loud, anguished cry burst forth.

Romola's maternal instincts kicked in, and she gently knelt beside the child, enveloping him in a warm hug. As she spoke softly to him, her words seemed to calm the child's sobs, and his gaze locked onto hers, transfixed. "You've lost your way, little one," she cooed, "don't worry, we'll find your house soon." She suspected the child had slipped away from his mother's watchful eye, and that his mother would likely be searching for him soon.

With a gentle smile, Romola tried to lift the child, but he was surprisingly heavy, and she couldn't manage to pick him up. "Come, little one, you must walk by my side," she suggested, offering her hand. To her surprise, the child took it willingly, though his other fist still clung to his shirt with a fierce determination. As they walked, Romola asked, "Do you have a mother?" The child remained silent, but Romola detected a glimmer of recognition in his eyes.
