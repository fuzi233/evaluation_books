As we emerged from the dense thicket of acacia trees, the twin tusks of the slain elephants lay sprawled before us like skeletal fingers, their ivory gleaming in the fading light of day. Two days of grueling labor had yielded a bounty of nine majestic creatures, their tusks averaging a staggering forty pounds apiece. The massive bull that had taken Khiva's life weighed in at a colossal one hundred and seventy pounds per pair, its sheer size a testament to the unforgiving power of the African wilderness.

With reverence, we interred what remained of Khiva beneath an ancient baobab, the earthy scent of the ant-bear hole mingling with the sweet fragrance of blooming aloes. Our small band moved on, driven by a shared longing to return to this unforgiving yet majestic land. After three days of travel, the worn landscape gave way to the promise of Sitanda's Kraal, nestled beside the gentle waters of the Lukanga River. Memories of our arrival lingered vividly – the cluster of native huts, the rough-hewn cattle kraals, and the patchwork fields where the local people coaxed forth a meager harvest.

Beyond the settlement, the veld stretched out in undulating waves, a seemingly endless expanse of grasslands teeming with smaller game. To the left, the unforgiving expanse of the desert beckoned, its mysteries veiled behind an aura of mystery. A tiny stream babbled through our campsite, its crystal waters reflecting the vibrant hues of sunset as the desert itself was set aflame. Good oversaw the arrangement of our makeshift encampment, while I walked alongside Sir Henry, our footsteps carrying us toward the crest of a stony slope.

From this vantage point, the Suliman Berg rose like a spectral sentinel, its snow-capped peaks shimmering in the distance. I pointed toward the mountain range, my voice barely above a whisper. "There lies the wall surrounding Solomon's Mines – a barrier that has tested the mettle of many a brave adventurer." Sir Henry's gaze followed mine, his eyes burning with a quiet determination.

"I will find my brother," he vowed, his words echoing the unyielding resolve that had defined his life's journey. My response was measured, though my heart swelled with hope for the young explorer. "May it be so."
