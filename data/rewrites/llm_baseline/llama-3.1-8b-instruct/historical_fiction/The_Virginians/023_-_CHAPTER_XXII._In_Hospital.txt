As the warm sunlight streamed through the window, casting a gentle glow over the quiet room, Harry Warrington slowly regained consciousness. His host and hostess, the Lamurts, smiled warmly at him from behind the bed curtains. A rumbling stomach and the scent of roasting chicken soon reminded him of his hunger, and he gratefully accepted a meal prepared by the capable hands of Mrs. Lambert's daughter, Theo. 

A fleeting image of two young women with rosy cheeks and dark hair flitted through his mind, but it was quickly replaced by the memory of an older woman – lovely, yet somehow familiar. A pang of guilt and regret washed over him, followed by a sense of disconnection from his past. It had been a long time since he'd last thought about those artificial pearls and pinchbeck locks, relics of a bygone era when he was just a boy. Now, as a man, he felt the weight of years bearing down upon him.

"What seems to be troubling you, my dear child?" Mrs. Lambert inquired, concern etched on her face.

"Just a twinge in my shoulder," Harry replied, attempting to brush off the unsettling thoughts. "You've both been incredibly kind to me. I'm grateful for your hospitality."

"We're old friends, Mr. Warrington," Colonel Lambert explained. "Your father and I were acquainted, and your mother and I were schoolgirls together at Kensington. You weren't a stranger to us, even before your aunt and cousin informed us of your identity."

Harry's gaze wandered to his bandaged arm, and he recalled the events leading up to his injury. "Ah, yes... I remember now."

Mrs. Lambert smiled reassuringly. "You're recovering well, Mr. Warrington. My husband has done an excellent job. Perhaps, after breakfast, you can try getting out of bed? We'll send your valet to attend to you."

"I think that would be wise," Colonel Lambert agreed. "Though, if your man's devotion is any indication of his character, he must be a devoted servant indeed."

Theo, it seemed, had played a significant role in Harry's care, preparing the meal and even mending his sleeve to ensure comfort. Her father arched an eyebrow at the mention of her name, but Mrs. Lambert simply beamed with pride.

As Harry's eyes drifted to a nearby vase filled with freshly cut roses, he exclaimed, "What beautiful flowers! Who cut them?"
