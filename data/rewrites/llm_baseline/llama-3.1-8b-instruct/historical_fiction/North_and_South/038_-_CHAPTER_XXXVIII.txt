"Whatever you say, think what you may, you'll get no word from me!" Her words were not just a testament to her pride, but also a reflection of the secrets she kept. Mr. Thornton knew this, and the knowledge cut deep. He recalled the tender look that had passed between her and another man, the familiarity and intimacy that had left an indelible mark on his mind. It was a bittersweet reminder of all that he had lost, and the pain it brought was a constant companion, lurking in the shadows of his thoughts.

The memory of that moment, like a ghost, haunted him everywhere he went. Even as he navigated the quiet streets of Milton-Northern, the dusky twilight seemed to amplify its presence. He couldn't shake the feeling that Margaret had been swayed by the charms of another, that she had been lured into a world where love and deception entwined like the branches of a willow tree. And that falsehood, that one small lie, spoke volumes about the depth of her emotions, a truth that Mr. Thornton struggled to accept.

He was torn between his love for her and his conviction that she had betrayed him. Her words, though they had wounded him deeply, still lingered in his mind – "There was not a man in all that crowd for whom she would not have done as much, far more readily than for him." The irony was not lost on him; he had shared her desire to avoid bloodshed, but this unknown suitor had claimed her heart, body, and soul for himself. The injustice of it all seared his heart like a branding iron, fueling a fire of jealousy that threatened to consume him whole.

As he walked, his footsteps became a restless cadence, a manifestation of the turmoil within. He was irritable, his pride wounded by his own lack of control. He felt like a stranger to himself, a man driven by passions he could no longer tame. His mother, sensing his unease, watched him with concern, but he pushed on, trapped in a cycle of self-reproach and longing.
