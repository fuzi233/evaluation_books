As Madame Olenska swept into the room, her presence was like a shaft of moonlight illuminating the space. Every detail about her sparkled – the delicate folds of her gown, the intricate lace trim, and the soft sheen of her hair – all seemed to shimmer with a gentle light. She carried her head high, a quiet confidence emanating from her very pores, as if daring the world to challenge her beauty.

Mrs. Manson, seated nearby, rose to her feet, a hint of amusement dancing in her eyes. "We were just discussing a little surprise for you, my dear," she said, her voice barely above a whisper, as she gestured discreetly towards the bouquet of flowers on the table. 

Madame Olenska's gaze landed on the flowers, and for an instant, her composure faltered. A subtle flush rose to her cheeks, and her eyes flashed with a fierce inner light. "Who would be so absurd as to send me flowers?" she exclaimed, her voice laced with a mixture of annoyance and embarrassment. "Why tonight of all nights? I'm not attending a ball, nor am I some debutante to be wooed. Some people are simply too predictable."

Without another word, she strode to the door and called out for Nastasia, her parlour-maid. The girl appeared instantly, and Madame Olenska instructed her to take the flowers to the house three doors away, to Mr. Winsett's residence. His wife, she explained, was ailing, and perhaps the flowers might bring her some small comfort. As Nastasia protested, Madame Olenska firmly insisted, her voice low and even. "Don't bother to tell anyone they came from me. Just make sure they're delivered discreetly."

With a swift movement, she draped her velvet cloak over Nastasia's shoulders and dispatched her on her errand. Turning back to the room, she shut the door firmly, her chest rising and falling beneath her lace-trimmed bodice. For an instant, Archer wondered if she might burst into tears, but instead, a wry smile spread across her face, and she laughed softly. "And how are you two getting along?" she asked, her eyes glinting mischievously between Archer and the Marchioness.

"It seems you've given Mr. Archer plenty of time to get acquainted," the Marchioness replied, her voice dry.
