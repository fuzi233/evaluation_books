As Daniel Deronda walked towards the Cohen's household, the words of Zunz echoed in his mind: "If there are ranks in suffering, Israel takes precedence of all the nations...if a literature is called rich in the possession of a few classic tragedies, what shall we say to a National Tragedy lasting for fifteen hundred years, in which the poets and the actors were also the heroes?" He couldn't help but contrast this grand notion with the humble lives of the Cohens, whose struggles seemed to stem from mundane concerns rather than epic martyrdom.

Upon arriving at the Cohens', Deronda was greeted with warmth, their faces beaming with joy at his return. Mr. Cohen, a man consumed by his love of accumulating wealth, seemed almost effusive in his hospitality, assuring Deronda that he didn't mind the loss of a small sum, as long as the pleasure of his family's company outweighed it. His wife, Mrs. Cohen, was apologetic about the sleeping baby, but soon grew enthusiastic about Deronda's presence, urging him to join the children in the parlor, away from the confines of the shop.

As Deronda entered the cozy room, he spotted the grandmother engrossed in a game of cards with the children, who were creating "plates" with her guidance. The room was filled with laughter and the sound of clinking cups and balls. Jacob, Deronda's young friend, was practicing his throwing skills, and Deronda watched with a warm smile as he expertly tossed the plate, catching it mid-air without it breaking. The children's eyes sparkled with delight, and Deronda joined in, setting up a dance of paper figures on the table while Jacob concentrated on his cup and ball.

It was only after they had finished their games that Deronda's curiosity got the better of him. "Is Mordecai in just now?" he asked, trying to sound nonchalant despite his genuine interest. Mrs. Cohen nodded discreetly towards the closed door, and Deronda's eyes followed her gesture. "The fact is, sir," Mr. Cohen began, "we've been a bit concerned about him lately. He's been acting strangely, a bit...distracted." He tapped his forehead, indicating Mordecai's mental state. "Not entirely rational, like us, you know. But usually, he's quite industrious and devoted to the children. We're not sure what's gotten into him these past couple of days."
