The warm sunlight streaming through the windows of the quiet study cast a golden glow on Caroline's fingers as they moved deftly, sewing a delicate pattern onto a small, intricately woven basket destined for the local Jewish community. Her needle danced in and out of the fabric, the rhythmic motion soothing her restless mind. Yet, despite the peaceful atmosphere, the hours seemed to stretch on interminably, devoid of purpose or connection. That was when Fanny burst into the room, her eyes scanning the lawn and gardens before calling out, "Miss Caroline!"

A soft, melodious voice responded from the corner, and Fanny hastened to deliver a small note into Caroline's waiting hands. The girl's fingers, still moving with the precision of a skilled craftswoman, barely registered the touch of the paper. Fanny's words hung in the air, however, imbuing Caroline with a sudden spark of vitality. "Joe Scott's son, Harry, brought it," she said, her voice like a whispered secret.

As if awakened from a dream, Caroline lifted her head, her gaze snapping with interest. "Harry Scott? Who sent him?" she asked, her voice husky from disuse. Fanny's words had worked their magic, reviving the dull haze that had shrouded her senses all morning.

The note, hastily read, revealed a warm invitation from Hortense, now back from Wormwood Wells, inviting Caroline to join her for tea. The prospect of escaping the isolation that had gripped her since Robert's departure from the household was too enticing to resist. Caroline's hands moved swiftly, laying aside the half-finished basket and gathering her belongings. She quickly donned her straw bonnet, its simplicity framing her heart-shaped face and dark hair, and wrapped a black silk scarf around her shoulders. The soft rustle of the fabric was a gentle accompaniment to her thoughts, as she prepared to leave the confines of her solitary existence behind.

As she ran down the winding green lane, the scent of blooming wildflowers filled her senses, transporting her to a world of vibrant colors and sweet fragrances. Though Robert was not at the cottage, the knowledge that he had recently been there was a comforting thought. Entering the familiar rooms, surrounded by the echoes of his presence, was a balm to her weary soul.
