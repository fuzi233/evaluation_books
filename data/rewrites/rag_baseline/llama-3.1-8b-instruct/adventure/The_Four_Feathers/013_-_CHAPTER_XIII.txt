As the afternoon sun cast a warm glow over the bustling streets of Hill Street, Ethne stood poised at the open window, her senses heightened in anticipation. The air was alive with the sweet scent of blooming flowers and the distant jingle of carriage bells, transporting her to a world of elegance and refinement. Her companion, Mrs. Adair, sat at the tea table, her eyes fixed on the clock as the hands ticked away the minutes, marking the passing of time.

A sudden jolt of excitement coursed through Ethne as a cab rattled down the street, its rhythmic clatter echoing through the room. "That's a cab," Mrs. Adair said, her voice tinged with a hint of curiosity. Ethne leaned forward, her gaze scanning the street below, her heart quickening with every step the cab took further away from their doorstep. "It's not stopping here," she whispered, her words barely audible over the din of the city.

Mrs. Adair's eyes met Ethne's, searching for answers. There was something beneath the surface, a tension that had been building, and she couldn't help but notice the faint tremble in Ethne's hands as she grasped the windowsill. The cab disappeared from view, leaving behind only the echoes of its departure. "Colonel Durrance is late," Mrs. Adair said, her voice laced with concern.

Ethne's response was immediate, her words spilling out in a rush. "He might have thought that I had come because I looked upon him as rather helpless, and I don't wish him to think that. He has his servant with him." Her eyes darted back to the window, her mind racing with thoughts of the telegram she had received, the one from Lieutenant Calder, bearing news of Colonel Durrance's affliction. Blindness, a fate that had been thrust upon him, a weight that hung over him like a shadow.

Mrs. Adair's gaze lingered on Ethne, sensing the turmoil brewing beneath the surface. "You remember the telegram I showed to you?" she asked, her voice low and gentle. Ethne nodded, her eyes clouding over with memories. "Yes. I want you to promise never to mention it. I don't want him to know that I ever received it." The words hung in the air, a silent plea, a reminder of the secrets that lay hidden beneath the façade of their lives.
