The soft echo of my wife’s departing peace-cry had barely faded down the hallway when I stepped toward the strange visitor, intent on greeting him properly and inviting him to sit. But one glance at his shifting form froze me mid-step, astonishment stealing my breath. There was not a hint of angularity to him, yet he wavered from moment to moment, swelling and shrinking, his brightness shifting in gradations no Flatland figure I had ever known could possibly achieve. A cold, sharp fear pricked at my edges: this had to be a monstrous, irregular Isosceles cutthroat, masquerading as a high-ranking Circle by faking a noble voice, who had snuck past my defenses to stab me through with his vicious acute angle.

It was an uncommonly dry season, no fog hung in my sitting room to aid my Sight Recognition, and at such close range I could make no sense of his form at all. Blind with panic, I lunged forward, blurting out unceremoniously: “You must permit me, Sir—” and I reached out to feel him.

My wife had been right. Not a trace of an angle, not a single rough nick or uneven ridge marred his perimeter. I had never in all my years met a more perfectly smooth Circle. I circled him slowly, starting at what I recognized as his eye, tracing all the way around and back again. He did not move an inch. There was no doubt: he was the most flawless noble Circle I had ever encountered. I burned with shame and humiliation—me, a mere Square, committing the unforgivable rudeness of touching a Circle uninvited. I stammered out dozens of apologies before he cut me off, clearly impatient with my fumbling introductions.

**Stranger:** Have you felt me enough by this time? Are you not properly introduced to me yet?
**I:** Most illustrious Sir, forgive my awkwardness. It is not that I do not know the rules of polite society, only that your unexpected arrival has left me quite shocked and nervous. I beg you not to mention this little indiscretion to anyone, least of all my wife. But before you honor me with your purpose here, might I humbly ask where you have come from?
**Stranger:** From Space, of course. Where else would I come from?
**I:** Pardon me, my Lord—are we not both already in Space, at this very moment? Space is breadth and length, extended infinitely in all directions, is it not?
**Stranger:** Pooh! You do not know the first thing about Space. You think it only has Two Dimensions. But I have come to tell you of a Third: height, breadth, and length.
**I:** Your Lordship jest with me. We in Flatland speak of length and height, or breadth and thickness all the time—we are just giving two dimensions four different names, that is all.
**Stranger:** I do not mean three names. I mean three actual, distinct dimensions.
**I:** Then would you be so kind as to point me to this Third Dimension, the one I cannot perceive?
**Stranger:** I came from it. It runs up above, and down below.
**I:** Surely my Lord means Northward and Southward?
**Stranger:** Not at all. It is a direction you cannot look, because you have no eye set in your side.
**I:** Forgive me, my Lord—one glance would show you I have a perfect, bright eye set right at the junction of two of my sides, as any Square does.
**Stranger:** Yes, but to see true Space, your eye should not be on your perimeter. It should be on your side—what you would call your inside, tucked in your core. We in Spaceland call that your side.
**I:** An eye inside my body? An eye in my stomach? Your Lordship must be making fun of me.
**Stranger:** I am in no mood for jests. I tell you I come from Space, from the Land of Three Dimensions. I was looking down on your flat Plane not long ago—your Plane, which you foolishly call “Space.” From that vantage point, I could see every one of the things you call “solid” — the shapes you mean are enclosed on four sides. I could see your houses, your churches, every locked chest and safe in your home. I could see right through your skin, into your very chests and stomachs. All of it lay open to my view.
**I:** Those are very easy words to say, my Lord.
**Stranger:** You mean they are not easy to prove. But I will prove them. When I first descended into your home, I saw your four Pentagonal sons, each in their separate apartments. I saw your two Hexagon grandsons, too. I watched your youngest Hexagon visit you for a little while, then retire to his room, leaving only you and your wife behind. I saw your three Isosceles servants in the kitchen eating supper, and the little Page boy in the scullery. And I did not come through your roof—you know very well you repaired it recently, there is not even a crack large enough for a Woman to slip through. I came through Space. Are you not convinced now?
**I:** My Lord, any well-connected neighbor could easily have found out all those small trivial details of my household. That is no proof of magic other worlds.

The Stranger muttered to himself, frustrated for a long moment, before he spoke again.
**Stranger:** What must I do? Wait—one more argument. When you look at a Straight Line, say your wife, what number of dimensions do you say she has?
**I:** Surely you do not take me for one of the uneducated common folk who think a Woman is truly just a one-dimensional Line. No, my Lord—we Squares know better. A Woman, though popularly called a Line, is scientifically a very thin Parallelogram, with two full dimensions, length and breadth, same as the rest of us.
**Stranger:** But the very fact that a Line is visible means it has another dimension beyond those two.
**I:** I already told you, Women have breadth as well as length. We see their length, and we infer their breadth—thin as it is, we can measure it.
**Stranger:** You still do not understand. When you look at a Woman, you do not only see her length and infer her breadth: you *see* what we call her height. It is infinitesimally small in your country, so you do not notice it. If a Line had no height at all, it would not take up any space at all, and it would be completely invisible. You must see that?
**I:** I confess I do not follow at all. When we in Flatland look at a Line, we see its length, and its brightness. If the brightness fades, the Line vanishes, and stops taking up space. Are you saying this “brightness” is what you mean by this “height” dimension?
**Stranger:** No! Height is a dimension just like your length. It is only so tiny in Flatland that you cannot easily perceive it.
**I:** That is very easy to test. You say I have a Third Dimension called height. Dimension means a direction you can measure, right? Measure my height, or just point to the direction it extends, and I will be your loyal convert. Until then, I beg you to excuse my disbelief.
**Stranger:** (to himself) I cannot do either. How do I convince him? A plain statement of fact followed by a demonstration will have to work. Listen to me. You live on a Plane. What you call Flatland is the vast, flat surface of a sort of fluid, on top of which you and all your countrymen move, never rising above it, never sinking below it. I am not a plane figure. I am a Solid. You call me a Circle—but I am not a Circle. I am an infinite stack of Circles, ranging from a tiny Point all the way up to a Circle thirteen inches wide, stacked one on top of the other. When I cut through your plane, as I am doing right now, the slice that intersects your world is the shape you correctly call a Circle. A Sphere—that is my real name in Spaceland—can only ever appear to a Flatlander as a Circle. You remember that phantasm you dreamed of last night, the land of Lineland, written all over your brain? When you stepped into Lineland, you could not show the King your whole Square shape. You could only show him a single slice of you, a Line, because his tiny one-dimensional world did not have enough space to hold the full you. Exactly the same thing is happening now. Your two-dimensional world does not have enough space to hold my full three-dimensional form. You only ever see a single slice of me, the Circle you are looking at right now.

I said nothing, but the dimming of the light in my eye must have shown I still did not believe him.
**Stranger:** Watch this. I am going to rise up, out of your Plane, into Space. You will not see me move in any direction you recognize. But the slice of me that intersects Flatland will get smaller, and smaller.

There was no motion I could track across any of the directions I knew. But the great, perfect Circle before me shrank. It dwindled down to the size of a coin, then a pinprick, then winked out of existence entirely. I blinked hard, twice, to make sure I was not dreaming. It was no dream: a hollow voice echoed out of empty air, so close it seemed to press right against my beating core.
**Voice:** Am I quite gone now? Are you convinced? Now I will sink back down into your Plane, and watch my slice grow larger again.

To any resident of Spaceland, this would have been simple, obvious logic. But to me, a student of Flatland mathematics, none of the causes made a lick of sense. I could see the facts right in front of me: the Circle had vanished, then reappeared, swelling rapidly back to its full original thirteen-inch size. I felt my blood run cold. He was no Circle, I thought. He was no noble shape at all. He was a trickster juggler, or one of the ancient sorcerers from the old forbidden folk tales, come to torment me.

He sighed heavily, seeing from my dead silence that I still did not understand.
**Sphere:** There is only one trick left. I will use Analogy. Tell me, Mr. Mathematician. If a Point moves north, and leaves a glowing trail behind it, what do you call that trail?
**I:** A straight Line.
**Sphere:** And a straight Line has two ends, right? Two terminal points. Now imagine that straight Line moves parallel to itself, east and west. Every single point on the line leaves a new straight line trail behind it. And it moves exactly as far as the original line is long. What shape does that make?
**I:** A Square. Which has four sides, and four right angles.
**Sphere:** Now stretch your imagination. Imagine that Square, in Flatland, moving parallel to itself, upward.
**I:** Northward, you mean?
**Sphere:** No! Not northward. Upward. Out of Flatland entirely. If it moved north, the southern points of the Square would just pass through the spots the northern points already stood on. That is not what I mean. Every single point in you—every point inside you, what you call your “inside”—will move through Space, in this new direction, so no point ever steps where another one stood before. Every point draws its own new, straight line. All of this follows strict analogy, can you not see it?

I was so frustrated by then I could barely hold myself back from lunging at him, to shove him out of my home, out of Flatland, anywhere to make this maddening nonsense stop.
**I:** What shape do I get from this “upward” motion you speak of? Can you describe it using words I would understand?
**Sphere:** Of course. It is perfectly simple, follows all the rules of analogy. But you cannot call it a “figure.” We call it a Solid. We started with a single Point. It has 1 terminal point. That Point makes a Line, which has 2 terminal points. That Line makes a Square, which has 4 terminal points. 1, 2, 4. That is a geometric progression. What is the next number?
**I:** Eight.
**Sphere:** Exactly. The Square makes a Thing you do not have a name for yet—we call it a Cube. It has eight terminal points. Are you convinced now?
**I:** Does this… this Cube creature have sides, too, like angles or these terminal points?
**Sphere:** Yes! All according to analogy. Though they are not what you would call sides. You would call them solids, each one a whole flat Square. Now, look at the progression: a Point has zero sides, because there is no dimension behind it. A Line has two sides (we politely call its two end points its sides). A Square has four sides. 0, 2, 4. That is an arithmetic progression. What is the next number?
**I:** Six.
**Sphere:** Exactly! This Cube is bounded by six sides—six of your insides, six full Flatland Squares. Do you see it all now?

That was the final straw. All my fear and confusion and rage boiled over. I shrieked at him, every edge of my shape shaking.
“Monster! Be you juggler, enchanter, hallucination, or devil— I will not endure your mockeries a moment longer. Either you perish, or I do!” And with that, I launched myself full force at the impossible, shifting Circle standing in the middle of my parlor.
