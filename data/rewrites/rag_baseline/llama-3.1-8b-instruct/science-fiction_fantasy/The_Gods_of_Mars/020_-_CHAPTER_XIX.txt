In the damp, cavernous heart of Zat Arras' fortress, I stood frozen, the metallic tang of desperation clinging to my skin like a shroud. The Jed of Zodanga's cold smile stretched across his face as he inquired about the pleasure of my unexpected visit. His words dripped with the syrupy sweetness of malice, and I responded with a silence as unyielding as the stone walls that imprisoned me. My gaze locked onto his, a piercing stare that mirrored the contempt I harbored for the treacherous Zodangan leader.

As the tension in the room grew, Zat Arras dismissed my guards, leaving only his two companions and myself to bear witness to the unfolding drama. His voice, like a winter breeze, cut through the air, each word carefully chosen to slice at my resolve. "John Carter," he began, his tone glacial, "by the laws of Helium, by the customs of our people, and by the verdict of an impartial court, you are sentenced to death. Yet, I hold the power to spare your life, to grant you freedom, or to condemn you to the darkness below. The choice is mine alone."

A faint scent of ozone wafted through the air as Zat Arras paused, his eyes glinting with a malevolent light. "If I were to release you, free to roam the streets of Helium for a year, I daresay the people would not press for your execution. But there is a condition, John Carter. Tardos Mors will never set foot in Helium again. Neither will Mors Kajak, nor Dejah Thoris. Helium must choose a new Jeddak within the year, and I, Zat Arras, will be the one to claim the throne. All you need do is pledge your allegiance to my cause, and your life will be spared."

I knew Zat Arras well enough to sense the cruelty lurking beneath his words. If I were to refuse, he would stop at nothing to claim the throne, and Dejah Thoris would be lost to the horrors of the arena of Issus. My mind racing, I weighed the options, weighing the value of my life against the danger that lay ahead. In that moment, I knew what I had to do. The proud daughter of a thousand Jeddaks would never betray her people, and neither would I.
