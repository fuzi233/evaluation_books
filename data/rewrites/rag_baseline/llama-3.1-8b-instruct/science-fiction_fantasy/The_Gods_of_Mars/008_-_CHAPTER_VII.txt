A Fair Goddess

The air hung heavy with the stench of salt and smoke as the black pirate and I locked gazes, our faces inches apart. For an eternal moment, we stood frozen, each waiting for the other to make the first move. Then, a cruel smile crept across the pirate's lips, like a crack in a fragile vase. An ebony hand emerged from above the edge of the deck, a revolver glinting in the faint moonlight. The cold, hollow eye of the barrel found its mark, and I felt a shiver run down my spine.

In a flash, my free hand shot out, grasping for the pirate's throat, just within reach. The ebony finger tightened on the trigger, and the pirate's voice was reduced to a strangled hiss as my fingers clamped down on his windpipe. The hammer clicked futilely against an empty chamber. Before he could recover, I yanked him over the edge of the deck, forcing him to drop his firearm and cling to the rail with both hands.

My grip on his throat held firm, preventing any outcry. We struggled in silence, he to break free, I to drag him to his death. His face turned a sickly shade of purple, his eyes bulging from their sockets like ripe fruit about to burst. He knew he was running out of time, and with a Herculean effort, he flung himself backward onto the deck, releasing his hold on the rail to claw at my fingers in a desperate bid to free himself.

That was all the time I needed. With a swift, decisive motion, I swept him clear of the deck. His plummeting body threatened to rip me from the anchor chain, sending us both tumbling into the dark waters below. But I held fast, my fingers locked around his throat, crushing the life from him. A single scream from his lips would summon his comrades from above, and I couldn't let that happen.

As his struggles grew weaker, I continued to choke him, my grip unyielding. Finally, his contortions ceased, and I released my hold, watching as he vanished into the darkness below. I climbed back to the ship's rail, my eyes scanning the deck for any signs of danger. The moon had dipped below the horizon, leaving only the faint glow of the distant satellite to illuminate the scene.
