After the destruction of the Ten-thousand, and with fresh terror seeping into our bones from the depths of the Night Land, it became clear that no rescue could be made. The Youths who now walked the Road Where The Silent Ones Walk were beyond reach—beyond help.

Yet one might wonder: why did we not signal them? Why did we not call out with the Home-Call—the great voice that rose from the Machine atop the sealed base of the Mighty Pyramid? We could not. To send that sound was to announce to the Monsters of the world that life still stirred beyond the walls of the Redoubt. And though we hoped the Evil Forces knew nothing of the Youths’ journey, we could never be sure. For such powers, if they held knowledge, or ignorance, neither could be trusted.

Still, we sensed something strange in the air—an Influence, quiet but undeniable. The Instruments registered it faintly, like a whisper on the edge of hearing. As I have said before, we believed this force came from the House of Silence, far off in the Night Land, perched upon a low hill north of the Great Road. Many among the Monstruwacans feared it reached for the Youths—but we had no proof. So we watched. We waited.

Then, as the hours passed, the Youths drew near to the bend in the Road where it turned sharply northward—closer now to the grim House. And suddenly, the Influence grew stronger. I felt it in my soul, certain it originated from that place. But certainty was not yet ours. Still, I spoke of it to the Master Monstruwacan. He trusted me, and he too sensed a hidden power emanating from the House.

There was talk—brief, desperate talk—of sending the Home-Call across the night, warning the Youths, begging them to return. But the Master refused. Such a cry would risk their lives. It was not right to summon them back unless we were certain they would perish without intervention. For the Home-Call was not merely a voice—it was a thunderclap, echoing over the world, loud enough to tell every Monster in existence that men still walked outside the Redoubt. In ancient times, when flying-ships sailed the skies, the Call had been a beacon of hope. Now, it was a death-knell.

A day and a night passed. Throughout, multitudes pressed to the embrasures, staring into the dark, watching the Youths. They knew of the Influence. They felt the dread gathering. There was much talk—some wise, some foolish, all full of fear. Yet no one dared go forth. No one stepped beyond the wall. And truthfully, this is not surprising. I have written it before, and thought it often.

The land itself seemed awake—restless, watchful. Low roars echoed across the wastes, sounds that sent chills through the spine. If I had not mentioned this earlier, it is my failing. But the task of recording all I have seen is immense. Bear with me, reader, for I strive to speak plainly, even if my words falter.

During those twenty-four hours, we learned the Youths had not slept. They had eaten only once. Those who watched through the Great Spy-Glass confirmed it. They moved forward with relentless speed, driven by some unseen force, racing toward the North along the Dismal Road. Soon, they would collapse—or kill themselves in their desperation.

This confirmed our worst fears: they were under a spell. A curse from the House of Silence. And then, a Monstruwacan arrived at the Master’s side, breathless, reporting a sudden surge in the Influence. At the same moment, I looked through the Great Spy-Glass—and saw them break from the road, running faster than ever, drawn directly toward the House.

The Master hesitated not. He sent the Home-Call into the night—a roar that shook the very foundations of the Pyramid. Then, through the natural eye, he sent a message: *They are being led to destruction by a force from within the House of Silence. Fight for your souls. If you cannot resist, end your lives quickly. Do not enter that place.*

Silence fell over the Pyramid. The bellowing of the Home-Call brought stillness, for all knew what it meant. Millions heard it. Millions prayed. From the heart of the Mighty Pyramid, a counter-force surged—not physical, but spiritual—a wave of will, of love, of sorrow, rising from the prayers of countless souls.

I felt it in my spirit. A vast, trembling energy flooded the aether, a storm of supplication so powerful it stunned me. The night itself seemed to hum with a sacred noise. And through the Great Spy-Glass, I saw the Youths stop. They gathered, confused, as if waking from a dream, realizing they had wandered into a nightmare.

Then came a roar from the embrasures—five hundred thousand eyes watching, and more on the View-Tables. The people shouted, a tide of triumph rising. But it was premature. The counter-force broke. The Evil Force pulled again. The Youths turned. They ran once more.

And in that moment, something new appeared before them—waves of mist, like cold white fire, glowing with an icy light that cast no warmth, no illumination. It formed a barrier between them and the House.

We knew then: a Power of Goodness had intervened. One of the sweet spirits who guard us from the darkness. All saw it—some clearly, others dimly. But none had the Night-Hearing, save a few. I was one.

Soon, a Monstruwacan reported the Influence had vanished from the Instruments. The force from the House was severed. We knew then—something mighty fought for the Youths’ salvation.

The Pyramid held its breath. A hush, thick with awe and dread. Hope flickered.

But the Youths wavered. Then—once more—the Master sent the Home-Call. A final plea: *For the sake of your souls, for the love of your mothers, come home while you can. This power protects you. Return to sanity.*

I thought I saw some turn toward the Pyramid. Perhaps they heard. But then—Aschoff, the leader, turned. A man of strength from the Nine-Hundredth-City. He leapt into the cold fire.

The mist vanished. The barrier dissolved. Aschoff ran forward. The others followed.

They reached the low hill. They climbed swiftly. Two hundred and fifty young men—pure of heart, innocent except for the waywardness of youth.

They came to the great open doorway—*“hath been open since the Beginning.”* Through it poured the cold, unchanging light and the silence so deep it could be *felt*, a silence that spread across the land like a shroud. The windows gave no view, only emptiness.

Aschoff entered. The others followed.

They were never seen again.

Their parents watched from the walls. Their faces reflected the horror of the night. The people remained silent. Some whispered they would return. But all knew—deep in their hearts—that these youths had entered the mouth of destruction.

For me—whose spirit holds the Night-Hearing—there came a terrible dread. Not sound, but a whisper of agony, a scream from the void. But no voice came. Nothing. Only silence—so complete, so absolute, that it drowned all other horrors.

For the first time, I understood how dreadful true silence could be. Once, I would have welcomed the distant thunder of the Great Laughter, or the whine from the Silver-fire Holes, or the bay of the Hounds. But now, that silence—*that* silence—was worse than any roar. It was the silence of annihilation.

And so it was clear: no aid could reach the People of the Lesser Redoubt. We did not know where it stood. We could not go.

And thus, those poor thousands must face their fate alone. A thought that pierced the soul.

But I—my heart burned with another grief. For I had long held a secret hope: that one day, I might walk into the Night Land, find the Lesser Pyramid, and stand before Naani—my lost love—my soul’s twin—and say: *I am That One.* And she, who had been mine in a past eternity, would know me instantly. She would run to me, and we would be together again, as we had been before time began.

Now I knew that dream was dead. And the thought that she might suffer—perhaps even now, in the grip of some monstrous thing—was unbearable. I nearly seized the Diskos and ran into the dark, not to save her, but to die trying.

I called to her often. With my mind, I sent the Master-Word—our secret signal—so she would know it was truly me, not a trick of evil. I warned her: *Never leave the Redoubt. Wait for the Master-Word. No friend would lure you into the night.*

I spoke to her across the darkness, sending thoughts through the aether. But always, there was no reply. Only silence. Or sometimes—a faint tremor, a weak pulse of the Master-Word—just enough to make my heart leap, then sink.

I listened constantly. My body weakened. My spirit strained. I cursed myself for lacking control. But I could not stop.

Day after day, my heart grew heavier. Life seemed trivial against the loss I carried.

And then—voices. Voices from the night, claiming to be Naani. I tested them with the Master-Word. None answered correctly. I did not mock them. I let them fade. But I kept my distance. For to speak with such voices is to invite madness.

And so, the Monsters of the Night toyed with me—luring, tormenting. Perhaps they sought to destroy me. Or perhaps they simply wished to suffer me.

My love consumed me. I grew thin. The Master Monstruwacan—like a father to me—spoke gently, tried to reason with me. I loved him more for it. But my health did not return. My heart was dying, torn apart by love denied.

It was strange that my own parents did not speak to me. But I had no parents. Not for many years. I should have said this sooner. My fault.

One night, I woke from a troubled sleep. And I heard it—Naani’s voice. Calling my old name. Crying out in anguish. I sat up, heart pounding. I sent the Master-Word into the night.

And then—around me—the aether thrummed. The Master-Word beat back—faint, fading, almost gone. I called again. *Naani!* I begged her to tell me what wrong was done to her.

But no voice came. Only silence. And then, a single, weak pulse.

I was broken. I stood, hands raised, and vowed to the blackness: *I will not stay here. While she suffers, I will not live in safety. I will find her. I will die trying.*

I sent the vow through my mind. *Be strong, Naani. I will come.*

But the night answered only with silence.

I dressed quickly. I went to the Tower of Observation. I needed to speak with the Master. My heart burned with purpose.

I told him everything. That I would no longer wait. That I would go into the Night Land—to find Naani, or die.

He listened. His face fell. He pleaded. He said no one could survive such a journey. That I would be lost before the week ended.

But I said nothing. Only that this was my duty. My promise. My fate.

At last, he saw I would not change. He warned me: I was too thin, too weak. I should wait, regain my strength.

But I said: *I am ready. I am lean, yes—but purer. The dross has burned away. Fear does not touch me. This is love. And love makes the soul fearless.*

And so I entered the Room of Preparation. The door closed behind me.

For three days and nights, I underwent the ritual—the cleansing of the spirit. The truths of the Night Land were revealed: horrors no child should hear, mutilations, soul-crushing abasements. These were locked away, guarded by the Master of Preparation.

When I heard them, I wondered how anyone could ever leave the Redoubt. How any student would dare step into the dark.

But I knew the answer: because youth craves adventure. Because the spirit rebels against safety. And though I grieve for violence between men, I do not mourn this hunger. It is part of us. It is eternal.

On the fourth day, my armor was brought. Grey, heavy, forged for the cold. Beneath it, a close-fitting suit to shield me from the night’s chill. I took the scrip—food and drink, specially prepared, stitched with the Mark of Honour. Women had sewn it. Love had shaped it.

And so I stood—ready. Lean, pure, untouched by fear.

For I had prepared not just my body, but my soul. And in that preparation, I found strength.

And now—my journey begins.
