As the last wisps of afternoon sunlight danced through the stained glass windows of the club, casting a kaleidoscope of colors upon the worn wooden floor, Captain Willoughby settled into his favorite armchair, a haze of smoke swirling around him like a loyal companion. His reputation as a bore preceded him, and his audience, comprised of weary souls seeking refuge from the city's din, often fled at the mere sight of him. Yet, he persisted, undeterred by their dismissive glances, lost in a world of self-importance, where every anecdote and aphorism was a gem waiting to be unearthed. His words dripped with an air of profundity, a delicate balance of trite and the mundane, served with a side of saccharine sweetness. Those who had the misfortune of listening too long found themselves trapped in a web of tedium, their willpower slowly unraveling like the threads of a worn tapestry.

On this particular autumn afternoon, the club's members had fled, leaving Willoughby to his usual solitary corner, a testament to his unwavering dedication to the art of monologue. The waiter's arrival, bearing a card, brought a spark of excitement to his eyes, a fleeting glimmer of hope that he might finally find an audience worthy of his genius. The card read "Colonel Durrance," a name that sent a shiver down his spine. Why would the illustrious colonel deign to visit him, especially after their last encounter? It was as if Durrance had somehow divined his plans to journey to Kingsbridge.

"Colonel Durrance?" Willoughby said aloud, his voice tinged with a mix of curiosity and trepidation. He scratched his chin thoughtfully, the scratching sound echoing through the suddenly silent room. "Show him in," he instructed the waiter, his eyes narrowing as he wondered what Durrance's visit might portend.

As the door swung open, Durrance strode into the room, his presence commanding attention. Willoughby's instincts told him to expect questions, to be prepared to employ his considerable diplomatic skills to deflect any inquiries regarding his acquaintance with the enigmatic Miss Ethne Eustace. But to his surprise, Durrance seemed uninterested in prying into his affairs. Instead, he offered a warm smile and a courteous greeting, his eyes twinkling with amusement as he accepted Willoughby's offer of a cigar.
