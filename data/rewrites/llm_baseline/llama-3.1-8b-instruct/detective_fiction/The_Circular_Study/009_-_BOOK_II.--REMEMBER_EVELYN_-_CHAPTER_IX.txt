With the space cleared, he motioned for Miss Butterworth to re-enter the study, her eyes never leaving the spot where the valet had stood.

When they were alone once more, Mr. Gryce began, his voice measured and deliberate. "One thing has become clear from our experiment: Bartow's involvement in the crime is ruled out." He paused, surveying the room as if searching for some hidden clue. "Now we can focus on the young people. Have you heard anything from them?"

Miss Butterworth's gaze dropped, a faint frown etched on her face. "No, nothing."

Mr. Gryce's expression remained neutral, but his eyes betrayed a glimmer of curiosity. "Nor from the elderly gentleman who arrived later?" he asked, his voice a gentle prod.

Miss Butterworth's shoulders rose in a slight shrug. "No, nothing from him either."

A fleeting look of disappointment crossed her face, and Mr. Gryce hastened to reassure her. "We mustn't jump to conclusions. The note we found may hold the key to unraveling this tangled web." He pulled out the crumpled paper from his pocket and smoothed it out on the desk. The words "I return your daughter to you. She is here. Neither she nor you will ever see me again.
