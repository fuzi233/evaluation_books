The scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the halls of Briarmains as Louis Moore lay in the guest bed, his brother's life slipping away in the darkness. Mr. Yorke stood over him, his eyes fixed on the crimson stain spreading across the young man's chest. The sound of the wind rustling through the trees outside seemed to fade into the background as the Yorkshire gentleman's gaze lingered on the fallen form. For a moment, he forgot the chill of the autumn night, and the damp earthy smell of the forest floor.

As the first light of dawn crept into the room, Mrs. Yorke emerged from the shadows, her face a picture of calm resolve. She surveyed the scene before her, her eyes taking in the still figure of the young man and the concern etched on Mr. Yorke's face. She nodded to herself, her mind already turning to the practicalities of caring for the injured stranger. The household would need to be put on notice – the cook would have to prepare a special diet, the maids would have to be instructed to tend to his every need.

Within the hour, the kitchen was abuzz with activity as Mrs. Yorke oversaw the preparation of a hearty breakfast. The aroma of porridge and toasted bread filled the air, and the sound of sizzling meat carried from the pan. She moved with a quiet efficiency, her movements a testament to her years of managing the household. As she worked, she glanced frequently at the young man, her expression softening ever so slightly as she took in the sight of his pale face.

When Louis Moore's brother arrived, Mrs. Yorke's demeanor changed. She welcomed him warmly, her eyes shining with a warmth that belied her tough exterior. She allowed him to sit by his brother's side, to hold his hand and press his forehead with gentle fingers. As the day wore on, she rose herself to fetch a tray of hot food, her movements a testament to her care and attention to detail. The brothers ate together, their faces lit by the warm glow of the fire, and Mrs. Yorke watched over them with a maternal eye.
