The kitchen was heavy with the scent of yesterday's despair, the air thick with unshed tears. Catherine moved listlessly about the Hoog Straet, her eyes red-rimmed from the previous day's weeping. After a quiet dinner, she donned her cloak and headed to the convent, determined to soften Gerard's heart and lay the groundwork for a reconciliation.

As she arrived, she struggled to rouse the porter from his slumber, finally learning that Gerard had departed the night before and wouldn't be returning anytime soon. Catherine's sighs carried her to Margaret's quarters, where she found the young woman sitting idle, her boy clasped tightly in her arms as if he were her sole solace. Catherine implored Margaret to join her at the Hoog Straet, suggesting that perhaps she could bring Eli around.

"What's the purpose?" Margaret replied, her voice laced with resignation. "Don't you think we're the ones responsible for all this suffering?"

"Nay," Catherine countered gently, "we're not so heartless, and Eli has always been fond of you. Maybe your presence can help to heal the rift."

Margaret's expression softened, and she agreed to accompany Catherine. Upon their arrival, they discovered Eli and a carpenter replacing the signs bearing Cornelis and Sybrandt's names with one bearing Margaret Brandt's. Catherine felt a pang, as if a knife had been plunged into her heart. "The harm done to one person becomes another's sustenance," she muttered.

"You're a kind soul," Eli said, his voice tinged with coldness. "But strength often prevails."

The next day, Giles dropped by, and Catherine recounted the tale of her family's woes, hoping to elicit his sympathy. However, Giles's prejudices ran contrary to her words, and he dismissed the notion that the black sheep had gotten off lightly. "They deserved to be hanged at Margaret's doorstep, and more," he declared, before turning his attention back to his favourite, Eli. "I'll show him what it means to have a brother at court with a heart that beats for friendship and a mind that charts the right course."

Margaret's gaze met Giles's, and she whispered softly, "Bless you, Giles." Little Kate chimed in, "You've always been a true friend to him, dear Giles, but I fear I don't know what you can do for him now."
