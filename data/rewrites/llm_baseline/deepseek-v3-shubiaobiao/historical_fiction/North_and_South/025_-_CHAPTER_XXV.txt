*"Revenge may have her own;
Roused discipline aloud proclaims their cause,
And injured navies urge their broken laws."*

Margaret found herself comparing the two proposals she had received—one from Henry Lennox, the other from John Thornton. The first had been a quiet, almost reluctant slip from friendship into something warmer, swiftly regretted by both. But Mr. Thornton’s declaration had struck her like a thunderclap. There had been no gradual shift between them, no softening of opposition into affection. Their exchanges had been nothing but friction, their opinions at odds. He had dismissed her thoughts when they clashed with his own, as if her defiance were a petty irritation to be brushed aside. And yet, here he stood, laying bare a love so fierce it left her trembling.

At first, she had wondered if pity alone had driven him—some misguided chivalry after her reckless act at the riot. But no. The truth had settled upon her before he even left the room: he loved her. Not out of obligation, not as a fleeting impulse, but with a stubborn, unyielding force. The realization unnerved her. She recoiled from it, as if his devotion were a weight she could not bear.

She paced the room, restless. Why did his persistence unsettle her so? She wished she had been sharper, colder in her refusal. But the words had failed her in the moment. Now they crowded her mind, too late to be spoken. The memory of his face—intense, unflinching—lingered like a shadow.

To distract herself, she threw open the window, then the door, desperate for movement, for company. But the house lay silent, heavy with midday stillness. She could not stay here, alone with her thoughts.

*Bessy Higgins.* The name surfaced like a lifeline. Margaret seized it.

She found Bessy stretched on the settle by the fire, though the air was thick with summer heat. The girl’s face was pale, her breathing labored. Without a word, Margaret adjusted the pillows, lifting her gently.

"I didn’t think I’d see thee again," Bessy murmured.

Margaret flushed. "I couldn’t come yesterday. My mother was ill."

Bessy’s eyes flickered with understanding. "I sent for thee selfish-like. The noise, the shouting—it near tore me apart. I just wanted to hear thy voice, reading something peaceful afore I go."

Margaret obliged, though Bessy stirred restlessly, her mind too troubled to settle. Finally, she burst out: "Stop. It’s no use. I can’t quiet my thoughts." She turned her face away, then whispered, "Thou heard of the riot at Marlborough Mills?"

Margaret stiffened. "Your father wasn’t there?"

"Nay. But it’s broken him all the same." Bessy’s voice was thick with exhaustion. "He’s a Union man, see? They had a plan—peaceful protest, no violence. But Boucher ruined it all. Lost his head, started the fight. And now..." She swallowed. "Father says he’d hand Boucher to the masters himself, just to prove the Union ain’t about lawlessness."

Margaret’s hands clenched. "Boucher didn’t throw that stone," she said sharply.

Bessy’s gaze sharpened. "Thou was there?"

Margaret did not answer.

Bessy sighed, drifting back into weariness. "Read to me," she pleaded. "Something far away. Make me forget."

Margaret read until Bessy’s breath evened into sleep. Then she slipped away, her heart heavy.

At home, her mother was in rare spirits, praising the water-bed that had finally granted her rest. But the lightness faded when Frederick’s name arose.

"I must see him," Mrs. Hale whispered, her voice fraying with desperation. "If I live—if I die—I must see my boy once more." Her fingers clutched Margaret’s. "Promise me. Bring him home."

Margaret could not refuse. She wrote the letter at once, sealing it before doubt could take root.

But when her father found her, his face was grave. "The risk is great," he admitted. "The navy does not forget mutiny."

A chill crept over Margaret. Had she summoned Frederick to his doom?

Her father squeezed her hand. "You did right. But the consequences... those are beyond us now."

And with that cold comfort, they walked home together, the weight of the future pressing upon them.
