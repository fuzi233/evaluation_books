As the morning sun cast its golden glow upon the sleepy household, Lieutenant Sutch sprang to life, his excitement palpable in every movement. He rustled through the rooms, stirring the servants into a flurry of activity as he packed and repacked his belongings, searching for the perfect arrangement. The air was alive with the scent of freshly brewed coffee and the soft clinking of dishes, a symphony that usually lulled the household into a tranquil morning routine. Not today. Today, the atmosphere was electric, charged with the anticipation of a new adventure. For thirty years, Lieutenant Sutch had been relegated to a life of inactivity, existing on the periphery of the world, a relic of a bygone era. But now, fate had bestowed upon him a chance to reclaim his place in the world, to prove himself once more. His mind whirred with the possibilities, each one more tantalizing than the last.

Over breakfast, he shared his plans with his companion, Durrance. "I shall be ready this afternoon," he declared, his eyes shining with enthusiasm. "I shall catch the night mail to the Continent. We might go up to London together; for London is on your way to Wiesbaden." Durrance's response was measured, his voice laced with a hint of curiosity. "No, I have just one more visit to pay in England. I did not think of it until I was in bed last night. You put it into my head." Sutch's interest was piqued. "Oh, and whom do you propose to visit?" he asked, his tone betraying a hint of wariness.

"General Feversham," replied Durrance, his voice steady. Sutch's expression turned guarded, his eyes narrowing as he pondered the implications. "Why in the world do you wish to see him?" he asked, his words laced with a mixture of concern and skepticism. Durrance's response was straightforward.
