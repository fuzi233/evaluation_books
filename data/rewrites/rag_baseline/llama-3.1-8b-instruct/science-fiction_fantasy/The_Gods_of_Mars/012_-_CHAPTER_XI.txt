When Hell Broke Loose

As the crimson sun rose over the bleak horizon, casting an eerie glow across the barren expanse of Omean, Xodar and I began our clandestine preparations for escape. Our makeshift table, hewn from the cold, unforgiving stone of our cell, became an impromptu workspace. I grasped the crude instruments at hand – a rusty buckle from my harness and the jagged edge of the luminous gem I had pilfered from Sator Throg – and set to work sketching a detailed map of the south polar regions onto the stone floor. The lines and markings danced across the surface as I calculated the approximate direction and distance to Helium, our destination.

Next, Xodar drew a meticulous map of Omean, highlighting the positions of Shador and the entrance to the outer world. As I poured over these charts, the geography of our confinement became seared into my mind like the branding iron that marked the slaves of Omean. Xodar, meanwhile, shared with me the rhythms and rituals of the guards who patrolled Shador, their slow, deliberate pace allowing for brief periods of unguarded time. I calculated that for five minutes at a stretch, one side of the prison was left vulnerable, a fleeting window of opportunity we could exploit.

"This information, though valuable," Xodar cautioned, "is mere theory until we're free."

I smiled, my confidence buoyed by the prospect of our impending escape. "Leave that to me," I said, my words dripping with conviction. "We'll make our move the night a small craft is moored near Shador's shores."

"How will you know?" Xodar asked, his brow furrowed in skepticism.

I gestured toward the barred window, and with a fluid motion, I leapt onto the sill, taking in the panorama beyond. A cluster of small vessels and two massive battleships lay anchored within a hundred yards of Shador. "Tonight," I whispered, my heart racing with anticipation.

But before I could share my decision with Xodar, the door creaked open, and a guard stepped inside, his back to me. I froze, aware that discovery would mean certain capture and imprisonment. The narrow partition separating our cell from the next offered a precarious perch, and I seized the opportunity, scrambling up the iron grating until I could secure a foothold on the windowsill.
