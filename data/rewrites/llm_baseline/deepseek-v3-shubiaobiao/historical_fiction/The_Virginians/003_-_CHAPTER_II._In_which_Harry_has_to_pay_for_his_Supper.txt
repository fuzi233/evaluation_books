Harry Warrington stood before the ancestral doors of Castlewood, his heart pounding with a mix of anticipation and unease. The great house loomed before him, its stones steeped in family history—his history. Yet when he rang the bell, no fanfare greeted him, no eager steward rushed to embrace a long-lost son of the line. Instead, a disinterested servant peered out, barely masking his impatience as Harry announced himself.

*"The family is away,"* the man muttered, already turning back to his interrupted card game. Only after persistent pleading was Harry permitted a fleeting glimpse of the portrait gallery—his grandfather’s stern visage in periwig and breastplate, his grandmother’s youthful beauty immortalized in paint. But the housekeeper shooed him out like an intruder, muttering of preparations for the Earl’s imminent arrival.

Disheartened, Harry left his name scrawled on a scrap of paper—*Henry Esmond Warrington, of Virginia*—and retreated to the village inn, where the landlord barely glanced up at his title. The contrast stung. In Virginia, any stranger bearing the Warrington name would have been welcomed with open arms. Here, he was just another face, another mouth to feed.

As evening fell, Harry lingered by the bridge, watching the rooks circle the turrets of Castlewood. Lost in thought, he barely registered the thunder of hooves until two riders nearly trampled him. A curse, a flash of powdered hair, and they were gone—his cousins, though he did not know it yet. The grand coach that followed, emblazoned with the Esmond crest, rolled past without pause. Behind its windows, pale faces peered out, indifferent.

That night, as Harry brooded in his inn chamber, the door burst open. A flushed, liquor-scented figure staggered in—*William Esmond*, his cousin, slurring apologies and insults in equal measure.

*"Beg pardon—thought you were a damned bailiff!"* William hiccuped, swaying. *"Aunt Bernstein insists you come. Won’t take no for an answer—never does."*

Harry’s pride flared. *"I won’t be summoned like a servant."*

William only laughed, calling for more punch before the inn’s staff dragged him out, still bellowing challenges.

---

The family debated Harry’s fate over wine and silver.

*"Must we really invite him?"* Lady Castlewood sighed. *"He’s a stranger, and from the colonies at that."*

*"But he’s family,"* protested Lady Maria, though her interest was less in kinship than in novelty.

Only the Baroness Bernstein, sharp-eyed and imperious, cut through the pretense. *"You shame yourselves,"* she snapped. *"A grandson of Henry Esmond, turned away like a beggar?"*

Yet beneath her outrage lay amusement. She knew their greed—how they fawned over her wealth, how even the rebellious Lord Castlewood weighed his words carefully when her purse was in question.

---

At first light, she strode through dew-laden gardens, her mind made up. When Harry arrived, summoned by her note, she studied him—his proud bearing, his grandfather’s fire in his eyes.

*"You’ll stay here,"* she declared, looping her arm through his. *"This is your home, whether they like it or not."*

Harry hesitated. *"After yesterday—"*

*"Yesterday is done,"* she interrupted. *"Today, you take your place."*

And with that, the game began anew.
