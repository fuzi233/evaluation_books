It wasn't until the following Friday that Sally managed to drag herself away from the bedside vigil and start for Detroit. By Saturday morning, she had arrived, driven straight to the Hotel Statler, and successfully located Gerald. After phoning his room to summon him, she marched into the dining-room and ordered breakfast, needing sustenance as much as conversation.

Sitting there, staring at the tray, Sally felt a heavy, low-spirited slump. The nursing duties of Mr. Faucitt had exhausted her physically, and the train ride hadn't offered much rest. But the real ache in her chest wasn't fatigue; it was the sting of Gerald's tone over the phone. He had sounded listless, as if her return after weeks apart was merely a routine administrative update rather than a joyous reunion. She felt hurt, perplexed, and slightly foolish.

A second cup of coffee helped, though. Sally reasoned that men, especially men like Gerald, were notoriously foggy-eyed in the morning. Once he'd hit the showers, he'd likely transform into a cheerful, energetic creature. For now, though, the coffee was essential.

Just as she poured the liquid, a stout young man stumbled into view through the open door. Sally recognized him instantly from a fleeting glimpse earlier—she had thought him vaguely resembling her brother Fillmore. As he approached, searching the room with wide, anxious eyes, Sally's heart skipped a beat. It *was* Fillmore.

"Puzzling," Sally muttered to herself. "Where is a man of affairs supposed to be? New York, presumably?" Yet there he stood, looking disheveled and bewildered.

"Fill!" she called out.

He froze in the doorway, scanning the room as if expecting an ambush. Then, seeing her, he lumbered over, his movements stiff and nervous.

"Why, Sally?" he stammered, his face flushing with what looked like guilt. "I thought you were still in Europe."

"I got back a week ago," Sally replied, trying to ignore the tremor in his voice. "But I've been stuck nursing poor old Mr. Faucitt. He's been terribly ill. Anyway, I'm here to see Mr. Foster's play, *The Primrose Way*. Is it a hit?"

"It hasn't opened yet," Fillmore said, sounding deflated.

"Don't be absurd, Fill. It opened last Monday."

"No, it didn't!" Fillmore protested, his voice rising. "Haven't you read the papers? They've closed all the theaters because of this infernal Spanish influenza. Nothing's played this week. It makes the whole company edgy. I've had the darndest time."

"And what does *that* have to do with you?" Sally asked, genuinely confused.

Fillmore cleared his throat, looking around nervously before settling back onto his chair. "Well, er—I didn't tell you this. I'm sort of mixed up in the show. Cracknell—he was at college with me—suggested I come down to check it out. Maybe he wants me to put some money in?"

"Silly goose," Sally teased, hoping to lighten the mood. "I thought he owned the planet."

"Yes, he has plenty, but fellows like to let a buddy in on a good thing."

"A good thing?"

"The play's fantastic."

"That's exactly what Mr. Faucitt said," Sally countered, shifting gears. "But Mabel Hobson..."

Mentioning Mabel Hobson caused Fillmore's face to contort with genuine horror. "Oh, she's dreadful, Sally! She can't act, and she walks around like a bulldozer. The other day, there was a huge fuss about a paper-knife prop..."

"A paper-knife?" Sally blinked. "How could a paper-knife possibly cause a fuss?"

"One of the props, you know," Fillmore explained, his voice trailing off into pained silence. "It got mislaid. I'm certain it wasn't my fault..."

"How could it be yours?" Sally asked, truly baffled. Love seemed to have dissolved Fillmore's usual confidence, leaving him a bundle of apologies.

"Well, you know how angry women are. Blame the first person they see... This paper-knife..." He sighed, unable to finish the thought.

Suddenly, Fillmore's face lit up. "Mr. Faucitt said Elsa Doland was brilliant. But—the girl you really need to see is Miss Winch. Gladys Winch. She plays the maid. She barely speaks, mostly just says, 'Did you ring, madam?' But the way she says it! Sally, that girl is a genius! The greatest character actress in a dozen years. Mark my words, soon her name will be blazing up Broadway in electric lights. Personality? Ask me! Charm? She wrote the lyrics and music for the show! Looks? Well, she's not conventionally pretty, but she has something else..."

"All right, all right!" Sally interrupted, smiling despite herself. "I know all about Gladys, Fill. But please, inform me how you dared to get engaged without consulting me first?"

Fillmore turned a deep shade of crimson. "Oh, do you know?"

"Yes," Sally said dryly. "Mr. Faucitt told me."

"Well..." Fillmore mumbled.

"Well?"

"Well, I'm only human," he argued weakly.

"I call that a very humble admission. You've gone quiet, Fill."

He had indeed improved since their last meeting. It was as if someone had punctured his ego balloon and let the pompous air escape. If this transformation was due to Miss Winch, as Mr. Faucitt hinted, Sally approved. The romance seemed wholesome.

"I'll introduce you both someday," Fillmore promised.

"I'd like to meet her very much."

"I've got to see Bunbury now. I thought he might be here."

"Who's Bunbury?"

"The producer. Probably eating in his room. Better go up."

"You're busy, aren't you? Little marvel! Lucky they've got you watching them."

Fillmore retreated, and Sally settled back to wait for Gerald, her earlier hurt replaced by a sense of shared chaos. A few minutes later, Gerald arrived.

"Oh, Jerry darling," Sally said warmly as he approached. "I'm so sorry I'm late. I've just heard about everything."

Gerald sat down heavily. His appearance matched his voice: a nervous, dull shadow wrapped around him like a wet garment.

"It's just my luck," he groaned. "Something like this could only happen to me. Those damned fools! Where's the logic in shutting theaters, even with influenza around? They let people jam together all day in stores. If that doesn't kill them, why would a theater? Besides, it's all nonsense. I don't believe in Spanish influenza. People catch colds, panic, and think they're dying. It's a fake scare."

"I don't think it's just a scare," Sally said gently. "Mr. Faucitt had it badly. That's why I couldn't come sooner."

Gerald ignored her, digging a spoon somberly into his grapefruit. "We've been dragging our heels day after day, bored to tears. The company is falling apart. They're sick of rehearsing when they don't know if they'll ever open. They were fired up a week ago, and now they're sagging. It will ruin the play. My first chance! Just chucked away."

Sally listened with a growing sense of desolation. She tried to be fair, acknowledging his terrible disappointment, but she hated self-pity in a man. Her own vanity stung; clearly, her arrival hadn't acted as a magic cure. She remembered what Mr. Faucitt had said about Gerald and felt a pang of disloyalty. She had never noticed before how self-centered he was, but now it was thrusting itself in her face.

"That Hobson woman is starting to make trouble," Gerald continued, poking aggressively at scrambled eggs. "She shouldn't have the part. Elsa Doland could do it a thousand times better. I wrote a few extra lines for Elsa recently, and Hobson flew into a rage. You don't know what a star is until you've seen a promoted Follies prop trying to be one. It took me an hour to talk her down before she threatened to quit."

"Why not let her quit?" Sally asked.

"For heaven's sake, talk sense!" Gerald snapped, his temper flaring. "Do you think Cracknell would keep the show running if Hobson wasn't in it? He'd cancel it immediately, and where would I be? You don't realize this is a big break for me. I'd look a complete idiot if I threw it away."

"I see," Sally said briefly. She had never felt so wretched. Travel abroad, she decided, was a mistake. It isolated you from reality. Analyzing her feelings, she realized Gerald was trying to have it both ways: demanding her sympathy while erecting a wall against it. He wanted her pity but refused to accept it, leaving her feeling shut out and useless.

"By the way," Gerald said abruptly, "I have to keep Hobson jollied along constantly, so please don't go around saying we're engaged."

Sally's jaw dropped. "If finding out I'm engaged to you is a handicap for you..."

"Don't be silly!" Gerald retorted, switching instantly to pathos. "Good God! It's tough! Here I am, worried to death, and you..."
