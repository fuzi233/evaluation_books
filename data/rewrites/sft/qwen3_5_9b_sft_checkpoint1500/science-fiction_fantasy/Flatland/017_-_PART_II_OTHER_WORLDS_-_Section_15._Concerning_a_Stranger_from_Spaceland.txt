The transition from dream to reality marked the final dawn of the 1999th year. Outside, the rhythmic tapping of rain against the walls had signaled the end of the day hours long ago. Inside, I sat beside my wife, lost in contemplation of the past year and the vast horizons opening before us—the next century, the approaching millennium.

*Note: When I use the term "sit," I do not refer to the physical postures known to inhabitants of higher dimensions, who lean back on legs they cannot conceive of. For beings like us, lacking limbs, "sitting" and "standing" are merely internal states of will. To an observer, these shifts manifest as subtle variations in surface luster, corresponding to the intensity of one's intent. However, the mechanics of these perceptions require a discourse far beyond the scope of this narrative.*

Our four sons and two grandchildren had withdrawn to their private chambers, leaving only my wife and myself to witness the turning of the age. My thoughts were seized by a lingering memory of my youngest grandson, a hexagon of exceptional brilliance and mathematical perfection. Earlier, during our customary exercises in visual recognition, where we rotated about our centers to test his ability to discern our orientations, his answers had been flawless. In gratitude, I had introduced him to the principles of arithmetic within geometry.

Using nine unit squares arranged to form a larger square with a side length of three, I demonstrated that while our vision cannot perceive the interior volume, the area remains calculable by squaring the side length: $3^2 = 9$. This concept held true for our world. Yet, after a moment of quiet reflection, the boy posed a question that unsettled me: "Grandfather, you taught me to raise numbers to the third power. If $3^2$ creates a square, what must $3^3$ create?"

"It means nothing," I replied sharply, "at least not in the realm of geometry, for we exist in only two dimensions." I then attempted to illustrate how a point traversing a distance forms a line ($3^1$), and how a line moving parallel to itself generates a square ($3^2$).

Before I could conclude the analogy, the boy interrupted with sudden fervor. "Then, if a point makes a line, and a line makes a square, surely a square moving through space must create something of the third dimension! It must be $3^3$!"

"Retire to your quarters," I snapped, feeling a surge of irritation. "If you wished to retain wisdom, you would cease speaking such absurdities."

Disgraced, the boy vanished. Alone again, I struggled to focus on the impending millennium, yet the boy's words echoed in my mind. With but a handful of grains remaining in the hourglass, I turned it northward for the final time of the old cycle. As I did, I muttered aloud, "That child is a fool."

Instantly, the air grew heavy. A presence materialized in the room, accompanied by a chilling breath that seemed to seep directly into my soul.

"He is no fool!" my wife cried out, her voice trembling. "And you blaspheme the commandments by disparaging your own kin!"

I ignored her protests, scanning the empty space around me. Though I saw nothing, I *felt* the weight of the visitor, shivering under the invisible pressure. Startled, I rose. "What troubles you?" she asked. "There is no draft. Look around—there is nothing."

"There is nothing," I admitted, sinking back into my seat, though the words stuck in my throat. "The boy is a fool. $3^3$ has no place in our geometry."

As soon as the words left my lips, a distinct voice answered from the void, echoing with undeniable authority: "The boy speaks truth. And $3^3$ possesses a profound geometrical significance."

Both my wife and I heard the voice, though she failed to grasp its implication. We leaped forward, drawn toward the unseen speaker. Our blood ran cold as the figure materialized.

At first glance, it resembled a woman viewed from the side. Yet, fleeting observation revealed that its edges dissolved into dimness with unnatural speed—a trait incompatible with female physiology, which relies on sharp angles. It looked like a circle, but unlike any circle I knew, it fluctuated in size in a manner defying the laws of Flatland.

My wife, however, lacked the composure to analyze these anomalies. Driven by the instinctive jealousy and haste characteristic of our sex, she immediately concluded that a woman had slipped into our home through some hidden vent. "By what right does this intruder enter?" she demanded. "You swore, my love, that our new dwelling contained no apertures!"

"And there are none," I assured her, attempting to calm her. "But tell me, why do you assume the stranger is female? My Sight Recognition reveals..."

"Oh, I tire of your Sight Recognition!" she retorted, reciting the proverbs of our gender. "'Feeling is truth,' and 'A straight line to the touch outweighs a circle to the eye.'"

Reluctantly, fearing further agitation, I conceded. "Very well, if this must be, demand an audience."

Adopting a demeanor of regal grace, my wife stepped toward the entity. "Allow me, Madam, to feel—and be felt by—" she paused, recoiling violently. "Oh! It is not a woman! There are no angles, no traces of corners whatsoever. Could I have offended a perfect circle?"

"I am, in a specific sense, a circle," the voice replied softly. "Indeed, I am a circle more perfect than any born in Flatland. But to speak with precision, I am many circles woven into one."

The tone shifted to a gentle courtesy. "I carry a message for your husband, but I dare not deliver it in your presence. Would you permit us a brief privacy?"

"My wife refused to entertain the notion of delaying our guest, insisting that her own solitude had long been overdue. With repeated apologies for her momentary lapse, she withdrew to her chamber."

I cast a glance at the hourglass. The final grains had spilled. The sands of the second millennium had run dry, and the light of the third millennium burned anew.
