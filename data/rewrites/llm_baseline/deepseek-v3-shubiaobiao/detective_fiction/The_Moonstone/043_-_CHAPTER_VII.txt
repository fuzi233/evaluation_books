The moment I stepped into the room, Rachel rose from the piano.

The door clicked shut behind me. We stood frozen, the length of the room between us, the air thick with unspoken accusations. She had moved to rise, but now she was still again, as if the act of seeing me had drained her of all other motion. Her eyes fixed on me—wide, searching, full of something I couldn’t name.

Had I startled her? I took a step forward, my voice low. *"Rachel."*

The sound of her name broke whatever spell held her. Color rushed back into her cheeks, her breath quickened. She moved toward me—not with purpose, but as if drawn against her will. Step by step, she closed the distance between us, her gaze never leaving mine. The closer she came, the more I forgot why I was there—forgot the suspicions, the disgrace, everything but the woman before me.

Then she was within reach. I saw her tremble, saw the hesitation flicker in her eyes. I couldn’t stop myself. I pulled her into my arms, my lips finding hers in a desperate, reckless kiss.

For a heartbeat, I thought she kissed me back.

Then—horror. A sharp cry tore from her throat, and she shoved me away with a strength I hadn’t known she possessed. Her eyes burned with fury, her mouth twisted in contempt. She looked at me as if I were a stranger. Worse—a criminal.

*"You coward!"* she spat. *"You miserable, heartless coward!"*

The words struck like a blow. Of all the reproaches she could have chosen, this was the cruelest.

*"There was a time,"* I said, my voice tight, *"when you would have told me I’d offended you in a worthier way than that. I beg your pardon."*

Something in my tone made her flinch. Her gaze flickered away, then back, reluctant. When she spoke again, her voice was quieter, but no less bitter.

*"Perhaps I deserve some excuse. After what you’ve done, was it manly to come here like this? To test my weakness for you? To surprise me into letting you touch me?"* She gave a sharp, mirthless laugh. *"But that’s a woman’s view. I should have known better than to expect honor from you."*

The apology was worse than the insult. It cut deeper.

*"If my honor weren’t in your hands,"* I said, *"I’d walk out that door and never return. You speak of what I’ve done. Tell me—what have I done?"*

Her laugh this time was raw, disbelieving. *"You dare ask me that?"*

*"I’ve kept your disgrace a secret!"* she shot back. *"I’ve suffered for it! And now you stand there and ask me what you’ve done? Was there ever a shred of gratitude in you? You were a gentleman once. You were dear to my mother. You were—"* Her voice broke. She turned away, collapsing into a chair, her face buried in her hands.

I waited, struggling for control. Her contempt stung, but so did the sight of her distress. Pride warred with something deeper, something that refused to let me walk away.

*"If you won’t speak first,"* I said, *"then I must. I came here with something to say. Will you hear me?"*

She didn’t move.

So I told her everything—the discovery at the Shivering Sand, Rosanna’s suicide, the nightgown, the suspicions that had led me here. She didn’t interrupt. She didn’t even look at me.

When I finished, the silence was suffocating.

Then, calmly, I asked the question that would decide everything.

She shot to her feet, her eyes blazing. *"Are you mad?"*

*"What game are you playing?"* she demanded. *"Do you think I don’t see through you? You’ve come here to buy my silence, haven’t you? Your father’s death made you rich. Is that it? You think you can compensate me for the Diamond now?"* Her voice turned venomous. *"Or are you ashamed of what you did and trying to hide behind lies about Rosanna?"*

I couldn’t hold back any longer.

*"You’ve wronged me!"* I snapped. *"You suspect me of stealing the Diamond. I have a right to know why!"*

Her laugh was sharp, brittle. *"Suspect you?"* She stepped closer, her voice dropping to a whisper. *"I saw you take it. With my own eyes."*

The words hit like a bullet.

I stood frozen, my mind reeling. *She saw me.* How? When? None of it made sense—yet her certainty was absolute.

She drew back, watching me crumble. *"I spared you then,"* she said. *"I would have spared you now if you hadn’t forced this out of me."* She turned toward the door, then hesitated. *"Why did you come here? Why humiliate us both?"*

I moved without thinking, catching her wrist. *"Rachel,"* I said hoarsely, *"you once loved me."*

She shuddered but didn’t pull away. Her hand trembled in mine.

*"Let go,"* she whispered.

But I didn’t. And in that moment, despite everything, I still had power over her.

Slowly, I led her back to the chair. *"Listen to me,"* I said. *"You saw me take the Diamond. Before God, I swear I didn’t know I’d taken it until this moment. Do you believe me?"*

She didn’t answer. Her head bowed, her fingers tightening around mine even as she whispered again, *"Let go."*

I didn’t. Instead, I pressed on, desperate for the truth.

*"Tell me everything,"* I urged. *"From the moment we said goodnight to the moment you saw me take the Diamond."*

She lifted her head, tears streaking her cheeks. *"Why go back to it?"*

*"Because we’ve both been deceived,"* I said. *"There’s something here we’re missing. Help me see it."*

And so, haltingly, she did.

She told me of that night—how she hadn’t slept, how she’d risen to fetch a book, how she’d seen a light under the sitting-room door. She thought it was her mother at first. Then she saw *me.*

*"You were in your nightclothes,"* she said, her voice hollow. *"You had a candle. You walked to the cabinet, opened the drawer, and took the Diamond. I saw it in your hand."*

Every word was a knife.

I released her hand and stood, pacing like a caged animal. There had to be an explanation. *Had to.* But what?

She watched me, her expression hardening again. *"Well?"* she said. *"You made me hope. What do you say now?"*

I had nothing.

And then, like a fool, I accused her. *"If you’d spoken sooner—"*

She exploded.

*"You think I should have called you a thief to your face?"* she shouted. *"After loving you? After defending you? You vile, wretched man!"*

I reached for my hat. It was over.

But she slammed the door before I could leave.

*"No,"* she said, her voice shaking. *"You’ll hear me out. Then you can crawl away like the liar you are."*

And so I stayed.

What choice did I have?
