"John Carter, John Carter!" she sobbed, burying her beloved head against my shoulder. "Even now, I can scarcely believe the witness of my own eyes. When the girl, Thuvia, told me that you had returned to Barsoom, I listened, yet I could not comprehend it. Such happiness seemed impossible for one who had suffered so profoundly in silent loneliness for all these long years. Only when I finally realized the truth—and then learned of the awful place where I was held captive—did I begin to doubt that even you could reach me here."

"As the days passed, and moon after moon slipped by without bringing even the faintest rumor of you, I resigned myself to my fate. And now that you have come, I can hardly believe it. For an hour, I have heard the sounds of conflict within the palace. I knew not what they meant, but I have hoped against hope that they might be the men of Helium, headed by my Prince."

"And tell me," she whispered urgently, "what of Carthoris, our son?"

"He was with me less than an hour ago, Dejah Thoris," I replied. "It must have been he and his men whom you have heard battling within the precincts of the temple."

"Where is Issus?" I asked suddenly, my heart leaping at the mention of the false goddess's name.

Dejah Thoris shrugged her shoulders, a gesture heavy with dread. "She sent me under guard to this room just before the fighting began within the temple halls. She said she would send for me later. She seemed very angry and somewhat fearful. Never have I seen her act in such an uncertain, almost terrified manner. Now I know it must be because she has learned that John Carter, Prince of Helium, is approaching to demand an accounting for the imprisonment of his Princess."

The sounds of conflict—the clash of steel, the shouting, the hurrying of many feet—drummed against the walls from various parts of the temple. I knew I was needed there, but I dared not leave Dejah Thoris, nor could I bear to take her into the immediate turmoil and danger of the battle. At last, a plan formed in my mind. I remembered the deep pits from which I had just emerged. Why not secrete her there until I could return and fetch her away safely, forever from this awful place?

I explained my plan to her. For a moment, she clung to me more tightly.

"I cannot bear to be parted from you now, even for a moment, John Carter," she said, her voice trembling. "I shudder at the thought of being alone again, where that terrible creature might discover me. You do not know her. None can imagine her ferocious cruelty who has not witnessed her daily acts for over half a year. It has taken me nearly all this time to realize even the horrors I have seen with my own eyes."

"I shall not leave you, then, my Princess," I promised.

She was silent for a moment, then she drew my face to hers and kissed me tenderly.

"Go, John Carter," she commanded softly. "Our son is there, and the soldiers of Helium are fighting for the Princess of Helium. Where they are, you should be. I must not think of myself now, but of them and of my husband's duty. I may not stand in the way of that. Hide me in the pits, and go."

Reluctantly, I led her to the door through which I had entered the chamber from below. There, I pressed her dear form to mine, and then, though it tore my heart to do it and filled me only with the blackest shadows of terrible foreboding, I guided her across the threshold, kissed her once again, and closed the door upon her.

Without hesitation, I hurried from the chamber toward the center of the greatest tumult. Scarce had I traversed six chambers before I arrived at the theater of a fierce struggle. The Black Warriors were massed at the entrance to a great chamber, attempting to block the progress of a body of Red Men moving toward the inner sacred precincts of the temple. Coming from within, I found myself behind the Blacks. Without waiting to calculate their numbers or the foolhardiness of my venture, I charged swiftly across the chamber and fell upon them from the rear with my keen long-sword.

As I struck the first blow, I cried aloud, "For Helium!"

Then I rained cut after cut upon the surprised warriors. Meanwhile, the Reds outside took heart at the sound of my voice. With shouts of "John Carter! John Carter!" they redoubled their efforts so effectively that before the Blacks could recover from their temporary demoralization, their ranks were broken, and the Red Men burst into the chamber.

The fight within that room, had it had but a competent chronicler, would go down in the annals of Barsoom as a historic memorial to the grim ferocity of her warlike people. Five hundred men fought there that day—the Blacks against the Reds. No man asked for quarter, nor did anyone give it. As though by common assent, they fought, as though to determine once and for all their right to live, in accordance with the law of the survival of the fittest. I think we all knew that upon the outcome of this battle would hinge forever the relative positions of these two races upon Barsoom. It was a battle between the old and the new, but not for once did I question the outcome. With Carthoris at my side, I fought for the Red Men of Barsoom and for their total emancipation from the throttling bondage of a hideous superstition.

Back and forth across the room we surged, until the floor was ankle-deep in blood, and dead men lay so thickly there that half the time we stood upon their bodies as we fought. As we swung toward the great windows overlooking the gardens of Issus, a sight met my gaze that sent a wave of exultation over me.

"Look!" I cried. "Men of the First Born, look!"

For an instant, the fighting ceased, and with one accord, every eye turned in the direction I had indicated. The sight they beheld was one no Man of the First Born had ever imagined possible. Across the gardens, from side to side, stood a wavering line of Black Warriors. Beyond them, forcing them ever backward, was a great horde of Green Warriors astride their mighty Thoats.

As we watched, one, fiercer and more grimly terrible than his fellows, rode forward from the rear. He shouted some fierce command to his terrible legion. It was Tars Tarkas, Jeddak of Thark. As he couched his great forty-foot metal-shod lance, we saw his warriors do likewise. Then we interpreted his command.

Twenty yards now separated the Green Men from the Black line. Another word from the great Thark, and with a wild, terrifying battle-cry, the Green Warriors charged. For a moment, the Black line held—but only for a moment. Then the fearsome beasts bearing equally terrible riders passed completely through it. After them came *utan* upon *utan* of Red Men. The Green horde broke to surround the temple. The Red Men charged for the interior, and then we turned to continue our interrupted battle; but our foes had vanished.

My first thought was of Dejah Thoris. Calling to Carthoris that I had found his mother, I started on a run toward the chamber where I had left her, with my boy close beside me. After us came those of our little force who had survived the bloody conflict.

The moment I entered the room, I saw that someone had been there since I had left. A silk garment lay upon the floor. It had not been there before. There were also a dagger and several metal ornaments strewn about as though torn from their wearers in a struggle. But worst of all, the door leading to the pits where I had hidden my Princess was ajar.

With a bound, I was before it, and, thrusting it open, I rushed within. Dejah Thoris had vanished.

I called her name aloud again and again, but there was no response. I think in that instant I hovered upon the verge of insanity. I do not recall what I said or did, but I know that for an instant I was seized with the rage of a maniac.

"Issus!" I screamed. "Issus! Where is Issus? Search the temple for her, but let no man harm her but John Carter. Carthoris, where are the apartments of Issus?"

"This way," cried the boy, and without waiting to know that I had heard him, he dashed off at breakneck speed, further into the bowels of the temple. As fast as he went, however, I was still beside him, urging him on to greater speed.

At last, we came to a great carved door, and through this Carthoris dashed, a foot ahead of me. Within, we came upon such a scene as I had witnessed within the temple once before—the throne of Issus, with the reclining slaves, and about it the ranks of soldiery. We did not even give the men a chance to draw their weapons; so quickly were we upon them. With a single cut, I struck down two in the front rank. And then, by the mere weight and momentum of my body, I rushed completely through the two remaining ranks and sprang upon the dais beside the carved Sorapus throne.
