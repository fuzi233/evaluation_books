"Examine your own interior and record what you find!" cried Herr Cant, and the cuckoos of the earth seemed to answer his summons. Peer deeply into the Rhine, where its waters are profoundest, and into the Thames, where they flow thickest; then paint the silt beneath. Cast a bucket into a cistern of self-delusion, and whatever rises must, surely, be eternal verity, must it not? Yet, first among truths, no child of Adam ever truly reads his own heart save through the habit formed and the illumination gained by studying countless others. Even then, armed with sagacity borrowed from reflection, he can only decipher the script of his soul, never reading it with fluent ease.

Halfway to Sevenbergen, Gerard cast his gaze inward and questioned his spirit regarding his journey there. The heart responded without a moment's pause: "We depart solely out of curiosity—to learn why she spurned us, to demonstrate that her rejection has shattered none of us, and to prove that we remain entirely content with our future honors and the benefice awaiting us. We desire neither her nor any ally of her fickle sex."

Soon, Gerard located Peter Brandt's cottage. A maiden sat in the doorway, working diligently with her needle, while a burly figure leaned upon a longbow, conversing with her. A sharp, inexplicable ache seized Gerard at the sight. Yet, the man proved to be past fifty, an ancient soldier whom Gerard recalled seeing display admirable force and skill at the shooting butts. In another heartbeat, the youth stood before them. Margaret raised her eyes, dropped her work, and emitted a faint cry, her countenance alternating between pale and flushed. These tokens of emotion vanished swiftly, replaced by a chill, stony indifference far colder than if she had never betrayed any agitation.

"What! Is it you, Master Gerard?" she exclaimed. "What on earth brings you here, I wonder?"

"I chanced upon you and wished to pay respects and inquire after your father."

"My father is well. He will arrive shortly."

"Then I suppose I may wait for his return."

"As you wish. Good Martin, go into the village and tell my father a friend awaits him."

"And not a friend of yours?"

"A friend of my father is a friend of mine."

"That remains doubtful. It was unlike a friend to promise to wait, only to vanish the instant my back was turned. Cruel Margaret, you know not how I scoured the town for you; how, lacking you, nothing brought joy to me."

"These are idle words. If you had truly desired my father's or my company, you would have returned. I had prepared a bed for you at my cousin's house; he would have honored you greatly, and perhaps, who knows, I might have done likewise. That day, I was in the right humor. Do not expect me to be so again, nor should any young man dare hope for it."

"Margaret, I returned the moment the Countess released me, but you were nowhere to be seen."

"Nonsense! You did not return, or you would have seen Hans Cloterman at our table. We sent him to escort you on."

"I saw no one there, save a drunken man who had just fallen."

"At our table? Describe his attire."

"I paid little heed; he wore garments of sombre hue."

At these words, Margaret's complexion slowly warmed. Soon, however, adopting an air of incredulous severity, she posed numerous shrewd inquiries, to which Gerard replied with unwavering loyalty. Eventually, the clouds dissipated, and they deduced the origin of their misunderstanding. Followed by a sudden flood of tenderness, intensified by the wrongs they had inflicted upon one another, came mutual confessions. Neither had known peace since their parting, and neither could ever have found happiness apart from this fortunate reunion.

Gerard discovered a Latin Vulgate manuscript lying open on the table and lunged for it like a hawk. Manuscripts were his supreme delight; yet before his fingers could grasp the pages, two pale hands descended flat upon the leaf, and a crimson face emerged above them.

"No, remove those hands, Margaret, that I may see where you read, and I shall read alongside you at home; thus our souls shall meet within the sacred page. Will you not allow it?" She refused. "Then I must kiss them away." He pressed kisses upon her hands so frequently that, overcome by sheer shame, she was forced to withdraw them. And behold! The holy book lay open at the verse, "An apple of gold in a setting of silver."

"There!" she declared. "I had been searching for this line for ages, and found it only moments ago—and yet, I am caught!" With a touch of inconsistency, she indicated the passage to Gerard with her fair finger.

"Yes," he murmured, "but today, it is all concealed within that large cap."

"It is a becoming cap, I am told by some."

"Perhaps; yet what it conceals is beautiful."

"It is not; it is hideous."

"Well, it appeared beautiful in Rotterdam."

"Yes, everything was beautiful that day"—she sighed softly.

Then Peter entered, welcoming Gerard warmly and offering him supper. Margaret vanished, leaving Gerard to enjoy a pleasant, scholarly conversation with Peter. Soon, Margaret reappeared, her hair arranged in a silver net, casting a glance that was half-coquettish, half-playful. She moved about them with grace, spreading the meal, and radiated a brightness born of genuine joy and contentment. In the cooling evening, Gerard enticed her outside; she protested, yet complied. He urged her along the path toward Tergou; she demurred, yet followed. They strolled hand in hand, pausing occasionally. When he finally needed to depart, they pledged themselves never again to quarrel or misunderstand one another, sealing their vow with a long, affectionate kiss. Gerard returned home on wings.

From that day forward, Gerard devoted most of his evenings to Margaret. Their attachment deepened with every passing hour, until the time they shared became the substance of their existence, rendering the remainder of life merely endured. At first, this bond proceeded smoothly. Obstacles existed, yet to the eyes of hope, youth, and love, they appeared distant and trivial. The volatile feelings and passions of so many others, destined to thwart this union, offered no warning smoke of their volcanic nature or power. The course of true love ran placid and smooth, drawing these two young hearts into its current forever.
