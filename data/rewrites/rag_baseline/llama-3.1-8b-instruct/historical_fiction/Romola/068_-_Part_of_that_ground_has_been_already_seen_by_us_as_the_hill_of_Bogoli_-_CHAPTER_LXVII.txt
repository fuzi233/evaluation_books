As the last wisps of daylight surrendered to the gathering twilight, Baldassarre descended onto the banks of the Arno, the chill grey mist clinging to his worn cloak like a damp shroud. The desolate stretch of riverbank, hemmed in by warehouses and granaries, was a sanctuary he had grown accustomed to visiting in the stillness of the night. Here, amidst the long grass and rushes, he would often find solace in the gentle lapping of the water against the channelled slope. It was a fleeting respite from the hardships that had become his life – the endless search for scraps to eat, the whispered prayers for a crust of bread or a raw carrot to sustain him.

Baldassarre's gaze drifted across the darkening river, his thoughts lost in the memories of a life once filled with purpose. The monks of San Miniato had cared for him during his convalescence, but the wound he carried went far deeper than any physical affliction. His knife was gone, and with it, his chance at redemption. The thought of begging on the church-steps, a silent plea for mercy, filled him with a revulsion he couldn't shake. Yet, it was a prospect he was willing to endure, driven by the glimmer of a hope that refused to die.

In the depths of his mind, a flicker of expectation continued to burn, a flame that refused to be extinguished. It was a spark that had been kindled by the memory of a woman, a love that had been cruelly torn from him by the pestilence that ravaged Florence. Her absence had left a void he couldn't fill, a chasm that seemed to grow wider with each passing day. And so, he waited, his heart beating in anticipation of a deliverance he couldn't quite define, a promise hidden in the darkness like a fish waiting to be caught in the shallows.

As the stars began to twinkle above, the Compagnacci moved unseen, their intentions whispered from mouth to mouth like a deadly rumor. Two groups of armed men, their numbers swelling with each step, converged upon the city, their destination the Via de' Bardi. They knew the house they sought, a place of opulence and plenty, where the master lay unsuspecting in his bed.
