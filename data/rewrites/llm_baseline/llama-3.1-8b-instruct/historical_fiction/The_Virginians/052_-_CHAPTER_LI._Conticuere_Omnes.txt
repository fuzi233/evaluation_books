As we step across the street, you'll find the General's party gathered at Lord Wrotham's charming estate, which his lordship has kindly lent to his dear friend. It's a reunion of sorts, bringing together the same little family group we met at Oakhurst and Tunbridge Wells. James Wolfe has promised to join them for dinner, but his attention is currently captivated by Miss Lowther, and he'd far rather steal a glance from her enchanting eyes than indulge in the exquisite cuisine prepared by Lord Wrotham's skilled cook. You might consider taking Mr. Wolfe's place; after all, he's unlikely to arrive. I'll observe from the sidelines and report on the conversation.

Observe how radiant the women appear tonight! We recall the time Harry Warrington was taken by the bailiffs, and how Mrs. Lambert, upon hearing the news, hastened to her husband, imploring him to help their son. "Never mind his past rebellious nature," she urged, "or the return of your gifts – any person would feel hurt, especially someone as spirited as Harry. Let's put aside our financial struggles and the boys' education expenses; we must find a way to extricate him from this predicament." She reminded Martin of his previous success in helping Charles Watkins, and how he repaid every last penny. "You brought joy to a whole family, and even secured a promotion – no one can deny your good fortune," she exclaimed.

Martin, ever willing to lend a helping hand, was easily swayed by his wife's words and decided to seek out James Wolfe, offering to post bail for Harry. He bid farewell to his family, exchanging a knowing glance with Theo, who seemed to sense his mission (perhaps their mother had whispered the plan in her ear). As he departed, Mrs. Lambert followed close behind, her emotions finally unbridled.

In private, she threw her arms around Martin's neck, showering him with kisses and tears, calling upon the heavens to bless him. This tender display caught the attention of old Mrs. Quiggett, the household's diligent housekeeper, who looked on in astonishment. "We've had a disagreement, and we're making amends!" Martin reassured her, attempting to compose himself as they walked away.
