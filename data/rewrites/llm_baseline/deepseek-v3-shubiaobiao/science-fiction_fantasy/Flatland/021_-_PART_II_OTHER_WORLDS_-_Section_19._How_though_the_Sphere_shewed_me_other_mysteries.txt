My brother’s imprisonment struck me with grief, and in desperation, I tried to leap down into the Council Chamber—to plead for him or at least bid him farewell. But I was powerless, bound to the will of my Guide, who spoke grimly: *"Do not trouble yourself with him. You will have time enough to mourn later. Follow me."*

Once more, we rose into the vastness of Space.

*"Until now,"* the Sphere declared, *"I have shown you only Plane Figures and their interiors. Now, you must learn of Solids."* He produced a stack of square cards, placing one upon another—not North or South, but *on*, in a direction I had never known. Layer by layer, he built a shape: equal in height, length, and breadth. *"This,"* he said, *"is a Cube."*

Yet to my eyes, it was no Solid, only a jagged Plane—an abomination, as if some criminal’s insides had been torn open.

*"You see a Plane,"* the Sphere explained, *"because you lack the understanding of light, shadow, and perspective. Just as a Hexagon in Flatland would seem a mere Line to one who cannot recognize shapes, this is truly a Solid. Feel it, and you will know."*

He guided my touch, and at last, I understood. Six faces, eight corners—a being of three dimensions, birthed from the motion of a Square through Space. I marveled that something as humble as I could, in theory, be the progenitor of such a magnificent form.

Still, the mysteries of light and depth eluded me. Patiently, the Sphere adjusted objects, shifted angles, and let me explore with touch until, at last, the truth crystallized. I could distinguish a Circle from a Sphere, a Plane from a Solid.

This moment was the pinnacle of my journey—a paradise of revelation. What follows is the story of my fall.

The Sphere would have continued, instructing me in Cylinders, Pyramids, and Dodecahedrons, but I interrupted. Not from weariness—no, my thirst had only grown.

*"My Lord,"* I ventured, *"you who I can no longer call the Perfection of Beauty, grant me one request: show me your *interior*."*

**Sphere:** *"My what?"*

**I:** *"Your insides—your stomach, your veins."*

**Sphere (irritated):** *"What madness is this? And why say I am no longer perfect?"*

**I:** *"Because you have taught me to seek One greater still! As you surpass Flatland, uniting Circles into a Sphere, surely there exists One above you—a being who unites Spheres into a higher form! And just as we now gaze down upon Flatland, seeing all things laid bare, so must there be a realm where *your* insides are visible to a still loftier eye!"*

The Sphere dismissed me with impatience. *"Enough! We have work to do—you must learn to preach the Gospel of Three Dimensions to your blind kin."*

But I pressed on. *"If you could lift me into the Third Dimension to see Flatland’s secrets, why not take me further—into the *Fourth*? There, we might gaze down upon Spaceland, piercing every Solid, uncovering the earth’s treasures, beholding the very guts of Spheres!"*

**Sphere:** *"There is no such land. The idea is absurd."*

**I:** *"Not to me! If a Line implies a Plane, and a Plane implies a Solid, does not a Solid imply a *higher* Dimension? Consider the progression: 2, 4, 8, 16—each dimension doubling the bounds of the last! And if a Cube moves into the Fourth, would it not produce a being with *sixteen* corners?"*

The Sphere fell silent, then grudgingly admitted that some in Spaceland spoke of visitations—beings appearing and vanishing without cause. *"But none call it a Fourth Dimension."*

**I (triumphant):** *"Then let me ascend! If this realm is Thought itself, so be it! Let me see the Cube unfold into a greater shape, and from there, rise further—through the Fifth, the Sixth, beyond!"*

I raved, intoxicated by truth, deaf to the Sphere’s warnings. Then—

A crash. A rush. A fall.

Down, down, down—back into Flatland.

The last thing I saw was that wretched, infinite Plane—my world, my prison—before darkness swallowed me.

When I awoke, I was a Square again, lying in my study, the familiar voice of my Wife calling me home.

The higher realms were lost to me. But the hunger remained.
