The scent of freshly baked biscuits wafted through the damp air of our cell, carried by the faint breeze that seeped through the narrow window slits high above. Xodar and I sat huddled together, our eyes scanning the rough-hewn stone floor as we pored over the makeshift maps etched into the dust. I had fashioned a crude diagram of the south polar regions using a buckle from my harness and the sharp edge of the gleaming gemstone I'd salvaged from Sator Throg. Xodar's skilled hands had transcribed the layout of Omean, carefully marking the location of Shador and the hidden entrance to the outer world.

As we studied our handiwork, I mentally calculated the distance between our prison and the distant city of Helium, my mind whirling with the possibilities of our escape. Xodar, sensing my distraction, spoke up, his voice barely above a whisper. "The guards' routine is predictable, John Carter. During their designated rest periods, only one man patrols the perimeter of Shador, pacing a slow circuit that takes nearly ten minutes to complete."

I nodded, committing the detail to memory. "This means that for almost five minutes at a time, a section of the prison remains unguarded." My eyes met Xodar's, and I could see the understanding dawning on his face.

Our planning session was interrupted by the arrival of a guard, who stepped into our cell with a gruff demand. "Where is the white man?" His gaze swept the room, but he didn't notice me perched precariously on the windowsill, my heart pounding in anticipation. With a swift movement, I sprang onto the partition wall, my metal-tipped boots scraping against the stone as I landed softly on the opposite side. The guard's deep voice boomed out, but I was already gone, disappearing into the shadows of the adjacent cell.

Xodar's concerned face peered up at me as I reappeared beside him. "Do you see now how we'll escape?" I whispered, a smile spreading across my face. But Xodar's expression remained skeptical. "I see how you can navigate these walls, John Carter," he said, "but I'm still uncertain about how I'll follow your example."
