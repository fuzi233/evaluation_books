Consciousness returned not as a flood, but as a series of sharp, rhythmic interruptions. Pain was the primary datum, followed immediately by a profound, disorienting gap—a memory loss that felt less like forgetting and more like a structural failure of the self. I attempted to rise, a mechanical command issued to paralyzed limbs, but a strange, invisible force held me down. It was a gravity specific to my own weakness, a tether that bound me to the ground until I understood, later, that I had been utterly drained.

My back pressed against the rough texture of the earth. Near me, a sound emerged: a wet, ragged panting. Turning my head with agonizing slowness, I saw her. The Maid lay close, still naked, her chest heaving. In her hands, she gripped a long pole—the very staff I had severed when the Humped Men descended upon us. She pushed with it, a desperate, frantic motion, driving the raft away from the shore.

Memory snapped back into focus. We were adrift. She had saved me while I was unconscious, dragging my lifeless weight onto the vessel. How she managed this feat, I never learned; she claimed ignorance, attributing her success solely to a surge of love that granted her impossible strength. Later, she confessed the truth: she had regained consciousness on the beach, feeling my head heavy upon her bosom, realizing I was already gone. Her heart nearly shattered at the sight of my blood staining the sand, but when she lifted me, I breathed. Hope, fragile and bright, ignited in her eyes.

She scavenged water from the river, pouring it over my face, but I remained in the dark. Suddenly, a subtle intuition seized her—a warning of approaching danger. She chose between saving me alone or dying with me. She dragged me across the uneven terrain to the raft, fetched the pole, and shoved off. As she prepared to row, she noticed her torn clothes still clutched in the grasp of a surviving Humped Man. With frantic speed, she snatched them back, leaped aboard, and began to push.

From the treeline, two figures emerged. Silent, intent, they tracked her movement. She worked with utter desperation, pulling the raft outward until they reached the water's edge. They hesitated, staring stupidly, unable or unwilling to swim, perhaps sensing a dread inherent in the deep. Then, they began to howl. That sound reached me as I finally stirred on the raft, distant and fading as she rowed harder. By the time I fully comprehended the situation, I was drifting toward the small island, a harbor of sorts. She piloted me there, thinking only of my survival, ignoring her own peril. I was a corpse carried by love, helpless and blind, saved only by the care of my beloved.

For a long time, clarity eluded me. There was only pain, weariness, and a haze of half-waking states where reality felt fluid and strange. Yet, beneath the fog, a constant presence remained: Love. A gentle watchfulness that kept the black mists of weakness at bay and replaced unknown despondencies with a quiet hope.

Then, the haze lifted. I woke to find myself lying on something soft, enveloped in a sweet quietness. The Maid knelt beside me, her face illuminated by a love and gladness so intense it felt like drinking pure health. She kissed my mouth, her tears tracing sweet paths down my cheeks. I kissed her back, filled with contentment. She cradled my head, offering me liquid sustenance. After I drank, she kissed me again, lightly, like a breeze. Sleep claimed me once more, peaceful and secure.

This cycle repeated three times. On the third awakening, I felt a spark of strength returning. I moved my hand, testing the limits of my recovery. She sensed my need, holding my hand tightly, allowing me to drift back into sleep while gazing into my eyes with affectionate vigilance.

On the fourth waking, I whispered, "I love you." She broke into sudden weeping, clutching my hand to her breast. On the fifth, I became aware of my surroundings: I lay naked within a cloak, my body wrapped in bandages woven from her own torn garments. Threads from her dress had been twisted into cordage, and needles fashioned from the thorns of island bushes. Her fingers had bled repeatedly, breaking thorns and starting anew, a hundred times over, to create this makeshift armor for me.

Seeing me examine her hands, she blushed, recalling how I had last seen her. She kissed me softly to hide her shame. I wished to kneel in reverence, to show the depth of my devotion, a sentiment shared by anyone who has truly loved.

By the fifth day, I was nearing ease, wondrously happy. She spoke cheerfully, yet insisted I remain quiet due to my fragility. On the sixth day, I answered her questions, declaring my love aloud. She confirmed her own recovery, though her emaciated frame and weary eyes betrayed the toll taken by her vigil. I fed her tablets, kissed them, and then commanded her to prepare broth. Once done, I beckoned her to my side. Lying beside me, I placed her head in the crook of my arm, instructing her to sleep without fear of disturbing me. She initially worried about her weight, but I pressed her closer, demonstrating that my strength was returning. She nestled into me, exhausted, and fell into a deep slumber.

She slept for twelve hours, barely moving except for occasional soft moans and shifting her face closer to mine. In her absence, I felt no loneliness, only a profound contentment. Looking down at her sleeping form in the hollow of my arm, I saw a holiness radiating from her face, uplifting my spirit into a quiet glory of love.

At the third, sixth, and ninth hours, I drank broth, using my recovering right arm to handle the Diskos. I treated the weapon as a companion, setting my hand upon it with familiarity. The Diskos possessed a strange unity with its user, a bond forged in the fires of necessity.

At the twelfth hour, she awoke suddenly, startled by my laughter. Seeing my quiet joy, she was relieved, though reproached herself for losing track of time. I mocked her sternly, forbidding her to dwell on the lapse, urging her instead to share in our happiness. She threatened me playfully with a small fist, fearing I might reopen my wounds, but I remained unharmed.

Curiosity struck me; I asked if she had brothers, noting her natural playfulness. Realizing my thoughtlessness instantly, I took her hand to assure her of my kindness. She nodded quietly, kissed my palm, and slipped away to avoid weeping. I called gently, and she returned, smiling despite covering fresh tears while preparing more broth. Before eating, I pulled her into my arms. She submitted gladly, careful not to press too heavily on my injuries.

We ate together, talking happily. Soon, I slept again, requiring her proximity even while she stayed awake. This arrangement brought us complete satisfaction.

The seventh day dawned wonderfully. Waking to find her asleep against my side, nestled like a child, she roused instantly, having watched over me throughout the night. We ate and drank, her care ensuring my comfort. Now permitted to take whole tablets followed by water, I felt a surge of gratitude. Hunger pangs drove me to consume them rapidly, needing to restore my blood quickly to protect her and continue our journey toward the Mighty Pyramid. The tablets satisfied me better than broth, fueling my rapid recovery.

We kissed the tablets, drank from the same cup, existing in a state that was both childish innocence and mature partnership. She shifted my bandages, washed me, and kept me reclined, though I scarcely minded the restriction, feeling too weak to resent it. Her company was a constant source of sweetness; she joked, sang, and laughed, her joy infectious, accelerating my healing.

Later, she left briefly for her toilette. I urged her to hurry, promising to wait. She returned shortly, her hair a lovely cloud around her shoulders, her bare feet emerging from a bath in a secluded pool. She teased me for my impatience, admitting she dressed partially in front of me simply because she knew I delighted in watching her arrange her abundant hair. There was a shy, quaint stirring in her heart, but her hunger to be with me outweighed it. She loved being observed, finding pleasure in my gaze as much as I found it in hers.

The raft drifted with a deceptive ease, balanced perfectly by the rhythm of our strokes. I held the rear paddle; she held the front. We moved with a steady, synchronized push, covering ground faster than a hurried walk across the solid earth. By the twelfth hour, we stopped to eat and drink, resuming our labor with a motion so fluid it felt less like rowing and more like rocking gently on our heels. Hours bled into one another, marked only by the soft cadence of our conversation and her frequent glances backward, lips parted in a silent invitation that she always shook her head to deny. She refused to abandon her post, unwilling to trade the rhythm of the water for the uncertainty of walking alone.

At the eighteenth hour, we drew our blades inward. She spread her cloak beneath us, creating a makeshift bed, and we ate, drank, and fell asleep instantly, sinking into a deep, unbroken rest. Eight hours later, we awakened simultaneously, disoriented for a fleeting moment before realizing we were untouched by dreams or danger. A shared laugh erupted between us—a pure joy in simply existing awake together. We washed briefly in the sea water, refreshed, and resumed our journey along the coast, moving peacefully and contentedly for the remainder of the day.

This voyage lasted four full days, measured in twenty-four-hour cycles. We made no haste, preserving our strength. Nothing occurred to disturb us except a singular sighting: a massive creature emerging clumsily from the sea onto the shore, browsing on vegetation. From our distance, its nature remained ambiguous, yet it elicited no terror, only relief that it lay far behind us. To us, it seemed a natural occurrence, devoid of the monstrous odor that usually plagues the imagination. Such creatures defined that country; perhaps the Early World resembled it too. Extreme conditions breed extremes, a principle evident in the convergence of ancient laws and current realities. Whether driven solely by circumstance or a deeper spiritual force remained unknowable, yet the idea held no offense to reason. Even if humanity had once been a fish, or existed in a brutish state akin to the Humpt Men, the core essence—the Spirit of Man—remained constant. Development alters the vessel, refining the flesh and awakening latent spiritual capacities, but it does not change the fundamental architecture of the human soul. We move swiftly between states, capable of regression or ascent, yet we remain, ultimately, human.

Beyond this philosophical drift, two other phenomena marked our passage. First, the great beast; second, a towering volcanic peak rising directly from the ocean. The sea boiled around its base, punctuated by dozens of great jets roaring upward, accompanied by strange grunts emanating from the depths. These signs testified to the raw, waking energy of the Deep Country. We lingered in awe, staring back at the Hill of Fire and Force long after passing it. Beyond this, the geography shifted as the Great Sea fractured into smaller, interconnected channels, guiding us forward.

She showed me the specific spots where I had slept during my initial arrival, her curiosity piqued by the geography of my solitude. As the fourth day concluded, we reached the shore at a flat expanse sloping upward toward the mouth of the First Gorge. It was the tenth hour, mirroring the start of our journey. The water trip had been serene, a sanctuary of safety, but we knew the peril ahead awaited us. Our resolve hardened; if we conquered the gorge and reached the Mighty Home safely, the reward would be a lifetime of shared loveliness, a glorious repayment for our stress.

We beached the raft, wondering if anyone would ever see it again. She severed a small fragment of wood, a token for future remembrance. Gathering our gear, she helped me re-don my armor. The scrip and pouch settled against my back, the diskos gripped in my hand. She adjusted her belt, ensuring her knife was accessible, her bundle now reduced to essentials. Then, kneeling, she kissed the raft. Memories pooled around her; this was a final severance from the first phase of her life. Her heart stirred with a complex mixture of sorrow and gratitude. I knelt gently, taking her hands, and led her away from the floating timber, toward the dark maw of the Great Gorge.

To our right stood a grim, colossal mountain, crowned by the four fire-hills we had seen earlier. Below them ascended great ridges of ash, deposited over eons. She gazed upon this scene with endless wonder, a sight that defied comprehension for any human eye. We climbed into the high entrance of the gorge, stepping briefly into the gloom before pausing to turn one last time toward the Country of Seas. It was a solemn moment, knowing we might be the last of the old humans to witness that land, hidden forever in the world's eternal night. I wondered if the Humpt Men would eventually evolve to match the spiritual sweetness inherent in humanity, a thought that felt both odd and inevitable. We listened to the distant mutter of the fire-mountains and the ambient noise of life on that deep continent, standing mere paces from the silence of the gorge that would lead us into the Eternal Strangeness of the Night Land. She clung tightly to my arm as we looked back at the red glow of the dying world, where we had nearly died. Tears streamed silently down her face—not purely sorrow, but a poignant blend of grief and happiness, mourning the island where she nursed me back to health, while anticipating the stories she would tell her children of a place they would never see.

We rounded the mighty corner of the gorge and stepped forward into the gloom, stumbling slightly. For sixteen hours, we walked steadily, disturbed only by the encroaching darkness. It had been twenty-six hours since our last sleep, a foolish endurance that threatened to exhaust me before we reached the Night Land. Her voice urged caution. Eventually, we found a safe spot to rest. While eating and drinking, we calculated our progress, deciding strictly to limit our daily travel within the gorge to sixteen hours, followed by eight hours of uninterrupted sleep. We adhered to this discipline for five days until we emerged from the gorge.

Once in the lighted sections, morale lifted, though we were frequently stifled by the horrid gases rising from the fissures. My strength grew consistent with every step. She never allowed herself to be carried, moving instead with surprising lightness and agility, acclimated to the constant wandering. Periodically, I paused to show her the exact locations of my previous slumbers. She insisted on standing precisely where I had lain alone during my desperate searches, her tenderness palpable, her speech failing under the weight of her emotions.

Her questions became frequent: when would we reach the Night Land? How far was it? Excitement built within her, mirrored closely by my own. I pondered the Mighty Redoubt and the strange, monstrous landscape awaiting us. Most profoundly, I was shaken to my core by the urgency of getting her to safety, fearing that despite our progress, disaster might strike. This anxiety made racing impossible; we could not exceed our established hours. Instead, we relied on wisdom, resting faithfully after the sixteenth hour, trusting that patience was our strongest weapon.

The distance to the Pyramid was measured not in miles, but in the shrinking radius of my despair. My home, that mountain of sweet refuge, loomed vast against the eternal night, a silhouette of impossible scale. Yet, the weight of the girl in my arms—the only warmth in a universe turning cold—anchored me to the ground. Despair had a grip on my chest, iron-tight, rendering the magnitude of the sanctuary irrelevant. All that mattered was the safe harbor of the Circle, and she.

I moved forward, a single vector cutting through the static. A hollow in the terrain glowed with the heat of a fire-hole. From that darkness, something crawled upward. It emerged as a giant, hair matted, limbs thick with the texture of old roots. Its face turned to me, then shifted into motion. It approached, hands thrust forward, eager and terrible. In the flickering light of the hole, I saw the claws: monstrous, brutish tools designed to rend flesh like paper.

My instinct screamed to flee, but the cost of retreat was higher than the cost of death. I dropped to the earth, accepting the inevitable collision. Rage, cold and sharp, surged through me. I leaped, a furious arc aimed at the giant's throat. My blade struck, but the creature sidestepped with the fluid grace of a predator. Before I could recover, its massive arm swept out from the shadow, snatching my helmet from my head and hurling me backward. I rolled across the dirt, bruised and shaken, but alive.

I rose instantly. The Diskos roared in my hands, a wheel of blinding energy. I swung, connecting high on the giant's torso. The weapon bit deep, glutting itself in the monster's flesh. The giant fell, a toppled tower of muscle and bone, legs standing rigid in the firelight while a fountain of blood erupted into the night.

No pause. I sprinted to the girl, pulling her tight against my chest, and vaulted over the corpse. Behind me, the land erupted in a chorus of spiritual cries—a million voices rising in recognition, casting their love and awe onto my path. For a brief, electric moment, the isolation broke.

Then, the landscape resumed its hunger. Strange shapes detached themselves from the rocks and bushes. I struck them down with the Diskos, a blur of motion, unaware of their nature. Soon, the entire country seemed to crawl with foul life. Every step was a battle; every breath tasted of impending doom. The end of our lives was no longer a possibility; it was a certainty closing in. The air filled with grim roars, the low thrum of evil, and once, the distinct thunder of giants running.

To the southeast, the baying of the Night-Hounds grew louder, a rhythmic countdown to extinction. I knew then that my strength was insufficient. Just as the hope drained from my lungs, a strange blue flash tore through the upper atmosphere, striking the earth below. Again, and again, perhaps a dozen times. Accompanying the flashes was a crackling sound, sharper than thunder, piercing the ancient silence. These were the signals. The humans were fighting. They were coming for me.

The waking of the land was a violent awakening. The monsters roared, the great forces stirred, and a dreadful laughter echoed from the lost east, a sound that belonged to a nightmare. The hounds bayed constantly now, a pack of hellhounds closing the gap. I was alone, save for the dying girl in my arms, looking vainly for the Hundred Thousand prepared to aid me. There was nothing but shifting shadows and the movement of monstrous life. The hounds were minutes away. Death was inches away.

I stopped running and began to sprint. The Pyramid was close; the glow of the protective Circle was visible, broken only by the encroaching gloom. A desperate hope ignited: I could reach the safety of the Circle with her.

The baying intensified, doubling in horror. To lose her here, so near the sanctuary, was unbearable. The great mountain of my home seemed to rise right before me, yet still two miles distant. I cried out, a sound swallowed by the wind, asking why no help came. The hounds were half a mile away, scenting me.

Suddenly, the anguish of the millions washed over me. Their emotional noise was clear in my spirit. They saw my desperation, my futile calls. A great, sweet spiritual force surrounded me, born of their shared understanding and love. They understood I was at the edge of hope. The hounds were upon me.

Then, the earth shook. The beat of the Earth-Current returned, a frantic rhythm signaling the humans' desperate intervention. To my left, a vast pack of hounds charged, heads low, bodies the size of horses, appearing and vanishing in the strobe-light of their approach. We were dead in a minute unless they acted.

I planted my feet. Running was useless. I stared from the beasts to the Pyramid, and back to the beasts. Two hundred fathoms away lay the pack; hundreds of them. As I fixed my gaze on the Pyramid, a monstrous flame burst from its sealed base, rushing downward. It slammed into the land where the hounds ran, blotting out the night in a brilliance so intense I saw nothing else but the shining, dreadful glory of the fire. The blast wave hit me, a heat that threatened to wither me where I stood.

I realized then: the humans had unleashed the Earth-Current. Thunder rolled across the land, tearing the air and splitting the earth. The roaring of the monsters was drowned out by the cataclysm. Rocks hurled themselves in every direction with deafening crashes. It was a mercy I survived the debris field.

In an instant, the humans severed the current, reclaiming control. Silence returned, absolute and dark, save for the lingering smoke and the distant hum of destruction. The daze lifted quickly. I scanned the horizon, checking for threats. For a time, there was nothing but the grim laughter from the dead east.

I ran, staring upward at the Pyramid. Its light was fading, growing dimmer with every passing second. Initially, I blamed my dazzled eyes, but the dimming was real. The great expenditure of the Earth-Current to save us had depleted the reserve. If the current failed completely, the entire population of the Refuge would perish. The Masters knew this; they could not restore the flow without risking total annihilation. This realization chilled me, fueling a fresh desperation.

I expected the Hundred Thousand to arrive, but they did not. The land groaned anew, emitting new, peculiar noises. Living things crept between me and the light of the Circle. The final stretch would require a bitter fight. I gripped the Diskos, swinging it free, and pressed on.

My spirit sensed a warning. I looked up, hoping the Master Monstruwacan would signal danger via the Set Speech. Instead, there was only an upward stillness, a dimming of the Pyramid's lights. Later, I learned the observation towers and the pyramid's machinery had all failed for nearly an hour due to the strain of the rescue attempt. Death had come close to claiming the millions.

Yet, my spirit was warned by the turmoil of the masses and the vague mental call of the Master. I moved warier, scanning every angle. Suddenly, a pale circle hovered overhead, steady and quiet, drifting over us. It was one of the Powers of Holiness, a shield between our souls and the dread power seeking our destruction. Fear receded; I trusted the holy force and continued, running with caution.

Within four hundred paces of the Circle, the light was already dimming. Was the guard still effective? Anxiety tightened my chest. I ran swift, wary, terrified.

From a patch of darkness, three beast-men rose, growling. The first was too close for the Diskos. I drove the haft into its skull, then leaped aside, swinging the weapon with a madness that was also chilling. The girl was secure in my arm, a babe in my grasp. I met the remaining two as they charged, cutting with quick, light strokes. Anger focused my heart into a place of cold, deadly intent. My judgment became brutal and precise. They fell like mice. Unharmed, I waited.

Immediately, a shout of wonder and welcome erupted from within the Circle. Men in grey armor stood inside, but they did not advance.

I understood then why the Hundred Thousand had held back. Beyond the Circle, monstrous black mounds swayed with a force of strange life, inducing a primal horror. Any human venturing outside would be destroyed in the spirit, lost utterly. Sacrifice was useless; the dead offered no aid.

Shouts came from the Hundred Thousand, urging me to haste. I ran with all my strength. I looked up at the Circle of Holiness, drifting steadily above us. We were saved.

One hundred paces remained. Brutal things awaited. From the shadows, a herd of squat, brutish men surged forward, grabbing at me, trying to tear the girl from my arm. Success seemed imminent; I could not free myself while guarding her and wielding the Diskos simultaneously.

I kicked with my metal boots, driving them back, wrenching free in a chaotic spin. I leaped backward, and the herd pursued, a tide of horrid brutes chasing me toward the light.

Consciousness returned not as a sudden dawn, but as a slow, rhythmic rising from the deep water of the Country of Silence. I was lying on a bed, a sensation so foreign and yet so necessary that I understood, vaguely, that I would never need it again, nor ascend from the silence below. From the abyss beneath the world, the Underground Organs began to throb—a deep, resonant thunder that sounded like music played beyond the reach of death. It rolled outward, a chanting of multitudes singing across far mountains, shifting between the low, mournful breath of a distant wind and the clear, ancient melody of the Song of Honour. I knew, in a dreamlike state, that millions in those depths were celebrating this miracle of joy. Yet the vision remained faint, half-hidden behind closed eyelids that refused to open fully, as if I were drifting eternally on strange, unreal waters.

Then came the scent of reality: sweet, lovely odours drifting from the great Fields where flowers grew perpetually along the lift's pathways. The lift itself was ascending, carrying me through miles of stone. Perhaps I stirred slightly, for the voice of the Master Doctor reached me, low and gentle, bidding me rest. All was well with the Maid. Afterward, I drifted into a haze, existing in a suspended state of half-life and half-sleep, wondering without anxiety whether I was truly dead.

Days followed, quiet and empty of thought. The Master Doctor would lean over me constantly, his gaze sharp and probing. Finally, after intervals that seemed endless, another figure bent over me. It was the dear, lovely face of Mine Own. Her eyes spoke love directly into my soul, though her expression remained calm and hushed. In that moment, I began to live again in my body. My hands fumbled weakly; she caught them, holding them tight, and life flowed from her to me. She was silent, gentle, and in that stillness, contentment took root, leading naturally to a peaceful slumber.

When the day finally came for me to rise, the attendants carried me to one of the Quiet Gardens of the Pyramid. They placed me there and stepped back, leaving me seemingly alone. Then, someone emerged from behind a bush, looking at me with a momentary hesitation, a shy uncertainty. Yet the love shining in her eyes rendered that shyness insignificant. Truly, it was Mine Own. I had never seen Naani dressed as a maid before, and she appeared more dainty, more exquisite, than I could have imagined. Suddenly, I attempted to rise to meet her, overcome by a natural foolishness, but she ran quickly to stop me. She sat beside me, resting my head against her breast, offering her lips freely. In that single instant, she was both maid and mother to me.

She commanded me to remain still, and we sat in an utter, dumb happiness until the attendants returned. The Master of the Doctors was among them, and I saw a genuine satisfaction in his face. From that day forward, Mine Own visited me daily. My recovery accelerated with astonishing speed, for Love healed me. Soon, I was permitted to descend to the Fields, but only via private paths, for the Multitudes might otherwise swarm to follow me; I needed solitude. Mine Own accompanied me. The Master Monstruwacan and the Master of the Doctors had agreed, appointing an Officer of Marriage to wed us quietly and simply. I was too weak for a public ceremony, which would occur later, when the Millions bestowed upon us a Guard of Honour stretching eight miles high from the summit to the base of the Mighty Pyramid. That grand ceremonial was reserved for the future, a tribute the Peoples could not deny giving to their hero.

For now, Mine Own was my constant companion, my wife, my strength, restoring me to perfect health. We were in the Love Days—the most beautiful season, provided the Love was true. We wandered the mighty Fields at will, walking the Love Paths near the villages. These villages were vast, truly countries in themselves. We hid our names to avoid the natural curiosity and kindness of strangers, needing to remain utterly together and quiet. We chose sleeping spots where the floral beauty was most wondrous, carrying provisions, though we ate whenever we reached a village.

Mine Own kept her promise a hundredfold, preparing great, hearty meals and teasing me relentlessly about my gluttony, kissing me to ensure I never found words in my defense. She was everything my heart and spirit desired, accompanying me with Love and entering my spirit with pure Joy. Once, we descended briefly into the Country of Silence, though we stayed only a short time before my memories returned. In later wanderings, we explored that realm often, accompanied by Memory, the holiness of great thoughts, and a Love that holds all things.

As we left the Country of Silence, I told Mine Own of a terrible realization: when she had been suspended by the Horrid Force of the House, I had suffered dreadful pain, fearing I would never wake to discover her kissing me while I slept. Mine Own blushed with lovely shame, unaware that I had been conscious of her sweetness during her supposed death. She accepted my agony, knowing she had died before the Earth-Force's vapour freed her spirit. The Doctors explained that she had been stunned, her spirit frozen, her entire being suspended. Only the great life-force of the Earth-Current had awakened her spirit, allowing her body to resume flowing blood and life. The Doctors had searched the old records extensively, finding perhaps one similar instance in antiquity, but nothing comparable throughout the ages.

While wandering and resting in the Fields, I shared stories with her, learning that she too had acquired strange knowledge during her illness, though not much, for she had been utterly helpless, forced from her bed while I lay still. The Master Doctor had ordered this separation to prevent my certain death, hoping to awaken my spirit. I marveled at the depth of my love, knowing she had held my hands bravely and gently despite having scarce power to stand. I offered a small prayer of praise for Mine Own.

Thus, I arrive at my conclusion, with one final account. This occurred shortly after our second, public marriage. One day, My Wife—Mine Own—took me with sweet cunning to the Hall of Honour. Upon arrival, I saw the Peoples standing in silence, waiting expectantly. She led me to the center of the hall. Suddenly, I understood her purpose. Standing in the Place of Honour was a statue of a man in broken armour, carrying a maid forever. I fell silent. How can one comprehend the magnitude of this honour? Such accolades were granted only to the Great Dead. I was but a young man, far removed from greatness, except that I loved with all my heart and spirit, rendering death a trivial thing compared to Love.

Love makes the heart sweet and brave, granting understanding in humility and wonder. Mine Own wept with joy and honest pride beside me. A profound silence of sympathetic love filled the great Hall. Those present allowed us to depart in quiet dignity. Walking away with Mine Own, close beside me, I realized I had gained Honour, yet learned that Honour is merely the ash of Life unless tempered by Love. To possess Love is to possess all, for True Love mothers Honour and Faithfulness; these three construct the House of Joy.
