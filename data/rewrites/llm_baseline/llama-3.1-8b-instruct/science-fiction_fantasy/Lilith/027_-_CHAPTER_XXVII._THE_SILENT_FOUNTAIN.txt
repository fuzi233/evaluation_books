As I trailed behind the leopardess, her spotted coat blending seamlessly into the evening shadows, I caught a fleeting glimpse of her as she vanished through the gates of the palace. Upon entering the grand entrance-hall, I found the princess standing amidst a tapestry of crimson hues, her robes discarded carelessly on the stone floor. The drying blood on her skin told a different tale than the one she would soon weave.

Her eyes flashed with a mix of irritation and fear as she noticed my presence, and she swiftly turned her gaze away, attempting to feign a smile. "I've had a bit of an accident," she began, her voice laced with a hint of desperation. "Heard that the cat-woman had returned to the city, I decided to intervene. But she'd brought one of her abominable creatures with her, and it attacked me before I could defend myself."

A shiver coursed through her frame, and I felt an inexplicable pang of sympathy for her, despite knowing she was a master of deception. Her wounds were genuine, and her expression betrayed a glimmer of the same haunted look I'd seen in the cave. My conscience pricked at me for not intervening sooner, and I suspect my expression reflected my regret.

"Foolish child!" she exclaimed, forcing another smile onto her lips. "Don't cry, surely! Wait for me here; I'll be back momentarily. I need you to fetch me some remedies for my scratches." Her words dripped with a calculated sweetness, but I sensed an undercurrent of unease beneath.

My trepidation propelled me to follow her, and as I watched, she slipped into the dark recesses of the palace. I held my breath, fearful of losing sight of her. The air grew thick with an otherworldly hum, like the gentle buzzing of a thousand insects. Gradually, the assembly within the black hall began to take shape, illuminated by an ethereal glow that danced across the space.

I recognized fragments of scenes I'd witnessed before – the princess had been present in each of them. The dancers in the forest-hall, their skulls gleaming in the moonlight; the battle in the Evil Wood, where she'd urged her companions forward with a fierce cry; even the solitary figure on the heath, struggling to contain the swirling mist. Yet, I remained close behind her, watching as she stood motionless, her head bowed in contemplation.
