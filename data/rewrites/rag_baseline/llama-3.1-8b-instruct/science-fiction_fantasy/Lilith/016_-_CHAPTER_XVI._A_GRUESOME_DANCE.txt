A GRUESOME DANCE

I rose to resume my journey, the desert's unforgiving vastness stretching before me like an endless canvas. My thoughts wandered to the distant memory of a verdant oasis, a haven where the sweet songs of a harp-like brook would soothe the parched earth and lift the spirits. Yet, as I trudged onward, the desolate terrain seemed to mock my desires, its arid expanse swallowing all hope of respite. The prophecy etched within me whispered a haunting truth: it is not the vista that lies ahead, but the depth within that foretells our fate.

As the sun dipped toward the horizon, casting a golden glow across the wasteland, I spotted a rugged rocky ascent rising before me. My initial yearning to climb vanished, replaced by a deep fatigue that begged for rest. The soft, emerald-green moss beneath my feet proved an irresistible invitation, and I collapsed onto its gentle surface. Weary limbs relaxed, my mind began to quiet, and the soothing melodies of the nearby brooklets enveloped me. The harmony of water and stone was a symphony that stirred my soul, and I felt the weariness ebb away, like the gentle lapping of the brook against its banks.

As I lay there, my gaze roamed the rocky slope above, tracing the ancient history etched into its face. The tale of a long-forgotten cataract, which once thundered through the channels now dry and still, unfolded before me. My heart swelled with the memory of that primal power, as the waters danced and merged in a final, resounding crash. But the lulling song of the brooklets soon carried me into a dreamless slumber, my spirit merging with the rhythms of the wilderness.

I awoke before dawn, and with renewed purpose, climbed to the crest of the rocky outcropping. Alas, the desert stretched before me, a seemingly endless expanse of fine, powdery sand that had swallowed the very course of the river I had followed. The powdery dunes stretched to the horizon, a desolate sea devoid of life. Yet, as I gazed back, I spotted the bifurcation of the river's course, its twin branches now nothing more than a distant memory.
