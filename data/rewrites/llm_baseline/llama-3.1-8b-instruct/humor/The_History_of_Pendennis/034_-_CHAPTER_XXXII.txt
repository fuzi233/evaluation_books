His modest expenditures, though enjoyable in their simplicity, now seemed woefully inadequate to sustain his lifestyle. The club's membership fees had already devoured a substantial portion of his resources, and the acquisition of his humble furnishings had further depleted his coffers. With only a final five-pound note remaining, Pen felt the cold, hard reality of his financial predicament bearing down upon him.

Warrington, blissfully oblivious to his companion's pecuniary struggles, continued to sip his ale, his expression a picture of serenity. Though he had known Pen since their university days, when both had indulged in reckless extravagance, he remained unaware of the true extent of his friend's financial woes. Pen's opulent tastes, his penchant for luxury, and his inability to resist the charms of even the most modest of expenses had all been well-concealed from his friend's prying gaze.

As Pen surveyed the meager remains of his fortune, a sense of melancholy washed over him. His mother, a woman of limited means, had always struggled to provide for his every whim, while his uncle, a man of refined taste and breeding, had instilled in him a deep appreciation for the finer things in life. Yet, despite this upbringing, Pen's nature was one of generosity and kindness, often bordering on the reckless. His habit of lavishing praise upon those who served him – the waiter, the cabman, the vendor at the market – was a testament to this innate character trait.
