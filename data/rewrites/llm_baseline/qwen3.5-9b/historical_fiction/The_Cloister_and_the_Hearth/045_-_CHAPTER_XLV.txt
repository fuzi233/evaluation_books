The small gathering at the hosier's dwelling remained seated around the table, still digesting the weight of recent events, when the mother entered. Her gaze swept sharply across the circle before her, then she placed a folded letter flat upon the wood. Without hesitation, she began to recite its contents from memory, tracing the invisible lines with her finger—a futile gesture intended to convince herself, and perhaps the room, that she could truly read the words she was speaking. Suddenly, she lifted her head, fixing a piercing stare upon Cornelis and Sybrandt. Their eyes immediately lowered. In that instant, the tempest that had been simmering beneath the surface erupted fully upon them.

Catherine seemed to inflate with rage, ruffling her feathers like an agitated hen. From her lips poured a torrent of wisdom and nonsense alike, a mixture of grand invectives and petty insults that no man, however eloquent, could ever muster in a single stream, let alone a woman. A fair, albeit condensed, sample of her speech follows, delivered in one relentless breath:

"I have long harbored suspicions that you kindled the fire between Gerard and your father, setting that old rogue, Ghysbrecht, to stoke it. Now, here are Gerard's own written words confirming your treachery. You have driven your own flesh and blood into a distant land, robbing the mother who bore you of her darling—the pride of her eye, the joy of her heart. Yet, you remain consistent from end to end. When we were all boys together, my other children were a comfort; you were a curse—mischievous and cunning. You required a woman half a day merely to mend your torn garments. Why? Because labor wears cloth, but play tears it apart. Prudence arrives with the beard, but it never came to you. You were always the last to retire and the last to rise; for honesty sleeps early, and industry wakes at dawn. Where there are two 'lie-in-beds' in a household, there are inevitably a pair of ne'er-do-wells.

Often I have sat watching your conduct, wondering whence you sprang. You bear no resemblance to your father, nor are you akin to me—not even as a wasp resembles an ant. Surely, you were altered in the cradle, or perhaps a cuckoo dropped you upon my floor, for you possess neither our hands nor our hearts. Of all my offspring, none but you have ever mocked those whom God afflicted. Frequently, with my back turned, I heard you sneer at Giles for lacking size, and at my lily Kate for not being as sturdy as a Flemish mare. If you can rob a church, you certainly can do worse! For in the eyes of Him who created both Kate and Giles, and in mine who suffered for them—as I suffered for you—you are paltry, unfeeling, treasonous curs!

No, I will not hush, my daughter; they have filled the cup to overflowing. It requires a great deal to turn a mother's heart against the sons she has nurtured upon her knees. Many times I have closed my eyes to avoid seeing too clearly, biting my tongue lest your father discover what I knew; he would have cast you out instantly. But now, the cup is full. And where did you acquire all this wealth? For the past month, you have rolled in riches. You earned not a penny of it. May I never hear from another mouth how you obtained it! It is since that night you stayed out so late, returning with a swollen head, Cornelis. Sloth and greed are a terrible mismatch, my masters. Those who love money must either sweat or steal. Well, if you plundered any poor soul of it, it was surely some woman, I swear it; for a man would drive you off with his bare hand.

It matters not; it serves one purpose. It reveals how you intend to manage our affairs once they become yours. I have observed you, my lads, for some time. You have squandered a groat between you today. Meanwhile, I spend scarcely a groat per week and sustain you all, good and bad. Very well, I cease waiting for the shoes that might follow your coffins. This shop and this house shall never belong to you. Gerard is our heir—poor Gerard, whom you have exiled and done your utmost to destroy. Never address me as 'mother' again! Yet, you have made him tenfold dearer to me. My poor lost boy! Soon I shall see him again, hold him in my arms, and place him upon my knees. Yes, you may stare! You are too clever, yet not clever enough. You severed the stalk, but you left the seed—the seed that shall outgrow you and outlive you. Margaret Brandt is swift, and the child within her is Gerard's. What belongs to Gerard belongs to me. I have prayed to the saints that it might be a son, and it shall be—it must be.

Kate, when I discovered the truth, my very bowels yearned over her unborn child as if it were my own. He is our heir. He will outlive us. You will not; for a corrupt heart within a carcass is like a worm in a nut, quickly reducing the body to dust. So, Kate, retrieve Gerard's bib and tucker from the drawer you know of. One of these days, we shall carry them to Sevenbergen. We will borrow Peter Buyskens' cart and go to comfort Gerard's wife under her burden. She is his wife. Who is Ghysbrecht Van Swieten? Can he stand between a couple and the altar, and sever those united by God and the priest? She is my daughter, and I am as proud of her as I am of you, Kate. As for you, stay out of my sight for a while, for you are like the black dog in my eyes."

Cornelis and Sybrandt caught the implied dismissal and slipped away, wracked with remorse, impenitence, and hatred. They avoided her gaze whenever possible, and for many days thereafter, she said not a word—good, bad, or indifferent—to either of them. Thus, she freed her spirit.
