The mist-shrouded streets of Milton seemed to awaken with the first light of dawn, the gas lamps still flickering like fireflies as the inhabitants stirred within their homes. In the Thornton household, the morning bustle was already underway, the sound of clinking china and murmurs of conversation drifting from the dining room. Mrs. Thornton, resplendent in a silk gown of deep crimson, stood poised in the doorway, her eyes fixed intently on her son, John, as he poured steaming coffee into delicate china cups. The air was alive with the scent of freshly baked bread and the faint tang of coal smoke carried on the chill breeze.

"I suppose we must," Mrs. Thornton said finally, her voice firm, yet tinged with a hint of reluctance. She had not made many social calls of late, preferring the comfort of her own home to the exertions of visiting. Yet, her son's persistent urging had worn her down, and she had agreed to pay a call on the Hales, a family they had recently met through their connections in the town. The thought of hiring a cab, however, had given her pause, and she had questioned John about the expense, her practical mind weighing the cost against the benefit.

John, ever the pragmatist, had countered her doubts with a logic that was both persuasive and reassuring. "If Mr. Mason and his wife were friendless in a strange place, like the Hales," he had argued, "I would want you to call on them, wouldn't you?" His mother had hesitated, her pride warring with her desire to be a dutiful hostess. "I am going to-morrow," she had said finally, "so there is no need for you to order horses."

John, however, had been insistent, his determination a testament to the strong bond between them. "The last time you were out in a cab, you came home with a headache from the jolting," he reminded her, his eyes crinkling at the corners as he smiled. "But I'll make sure to order horses for today, just in case." His mother had acquiesced, her expression softening as she gazed at her son, her heart swelling with a mixture of love and admiration.
