As Gerard and Denys entered the public room of the inn, the landlord hastily removed his cap in greeting, his eyes darting aimlessly about the space. Gerard chuckled good-naturedly, remarking, "We're certainly not in Germany anymore." The mistress of the house, a warm-hearted woman in her forties, curtsied and smiled brightly, inviting them to sit. She dusted the chairs with her apron, though they didn't need it, and Gerard thanked her graciously. As they settled in, the landlord hovered nearby, his hat constantly slipping from his grasp, a habit born of absent-mindedness.

"What's the plan, Denys?" Gerard asked, raising an eyebrow. "Supper for two, or shall we wait for the rest of our party?" Denys shrugged, unfazed. "Why not? We can enjoy our meal without delay." Gerard laughed, "Ah, Burgundy forever!" and Denys grinned, "Courage, camarade, let's dine."

The atmosphere in the inn was relaxed, with wimple and kirtle the prevailing fashion among the women, while the men wore doublets and hose, though not many, and those who did were not particularly skilled in their use. The mistress moved about with ease, chatting with the travelers as if they were old friends. She called out to the maid, who arrived with a steaming tray of food, and then stepped back, allowing the younger woman, her daughter, to take her place at the table.

The daughter, Marion, was a vision of loveliness, with a bright smile and a gentle touch. She listened intently as Gerard shared stories of his journey, her eyes sparkling with kindness. She lingered for nearly half an hour, and when she departed, Gerard declared, "This is indeed an inn, reminiscent of home." Denys beamed with pride, quoting the French proverb, "Qui fait François, fait courtois" – those who make the French, make courteous ones.

As they finished their meal, a lively young woman entered the room, her features sharp and her laughter infectious. The travelers exchanged banter with her, each trying to outdo the others in clever remarks. Marion, however, proved to be the undisputed champion, her quick wit and sharp tongue leaving them all in stitches.
