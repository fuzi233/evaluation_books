As the Thursday evening succumbed to the darkness, an uneventful night unfolded beneath the estate's watchful gaze. However, with the dawn of Friday, whispers of two peculiar occurrences began to circulate. Firstly, the baker's man reported a chance encounter with Rosanna Spearman, veiled and walking towards Frizinghall via the winding path across the moor. This revelation raised eyebrows, given Rosanna's distinctive shoulder, yet the baker's man remained adamant in his account. Alas, it transpired that Rosanna had spent the entire Thursday afternoon confined to her room, nursing her ailments.

Meanwhile, the postman delivered a second item of news that shook the household: Mr. Candy, the unfortunate doctor, had succumbed to a chill after his ill-fated journey on the birthday night. As he lay bedridden, battling a high fever, his mind wandered in a state of delirium, spewing forth nonsensical words with the same ease he exhibited in his lucid moments. The family sympathized with his plight, though Mr. Franklin's concern for Miss Rachel seemed to stem more from the uncertainty surrounding the Moonstone than genuine worry for the doctor's well-being. His conversation with my lady at breakfast hinted at a notion that, unless the mystery was resolved promptly, Miss Rachel might require the most skilled medical attention available.

As breakfast drew to a close, a telegram arrived from Mr. Blake, responding to his son's inquiry. It revealed that he had secured the services of an expert detective, Sergeant Cuff, who was due to arrive on the morning train. The mention of this renowned investigator sent Mr. Franklin into a flurry of excitement, recalling tales he had heard from his father's lawyer during his time in London. "I begin to hope we're nearing the end of our troubles," he said, his eyes shining with anticipation. "If half the stories I've heard are true, Sergeant Cuff is unmatched in unraveling enigmas."

As the hours ticked by, the household grew increasingly restless, awaiting the arrival of this celebrated detective. Superintendent Seegrave retreated to his quarters to compile a report, while I longed to accompany the Sergeant to the station. Alas, my lady's carriage and horses were reserved for more pressing matters, and the pony-chaise was needed later for Mr. Godfrey, who was reluctantly departing to attend to a Ladies' Charity.
