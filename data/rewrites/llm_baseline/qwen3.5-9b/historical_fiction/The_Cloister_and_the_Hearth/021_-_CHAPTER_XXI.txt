Speech is often the vessel through which human thought flows, yet there exist emotions so primal and overwhelming that they escape the tongue entirely, erupting instead as raw, eloquent cries. In such moments, man sheds his civilized veneer, revealing himself as nothing more than a creature of instinct; for when beasts are driven to the brink, they too emit sounds of anguish or triumph, though they lack the power of articulate speech. There was something terrifyingly feral in the roar of victory that erupted from the thicket as Dierich's men emerged upon their quarry, mirrored instantly by the piercing shriek of terror that drove Gerard and the mule forward. The pursuers' hands grasped at empty air mere feet behind them as the fugitives fled for their lives. For a fleeting second, disoriented like lions whose springs go awry, Dierich and his party hesitated, allowing Gerard and the beast to gain a ten-yard advantage. Then, weapons raised, they surged forward once more. Capture seemed inevitable; this was not the first time these groups had tested their mettle against one another. On the open plain earlier that day, the pursuers had steadily closed the gap on the trio, and now, finally, the contest would be decided solely by speed. Within moments, they covered a hundred yards, yet that crucial ten-yard buffer remained intact.

This sudden acceleration baffled Dierich Brower. The explanation lay in the dynamics of movement: when three flee together, their pace is dictated by the slowest member. From Peter's house to the forest's edge, Gerard had been forced to match Margaret's slower stride. But now, unburdened by her presence, he ran at his own natural velocity, aided by a mule capable of leaving everyone else in the dust. Furthermore, the benefits of youth and a life of chastity began to manifest. Light grew perceptibly between the hunted and the hunters. Desperate to close the distance, Dierich made a final, frantic surge, gaining two yards, only for Gerard to silently reclaim the ground within seconds. Frustration boiled over; curses rang out from the pursuing ranks. Martin heard them, and a spark of hope ignited in his eyes. "Hold fast, Gerard! Courage, brave lad! Look how they scatter!" Indeed, the formation was breaking. Dierich now led by one man, while another lagged dangerously in the rear.

They ascended a long, gentle rise where endurance proved paramount. Here, the vigor of youth, sheer grit, and the strength derived from sober living outweighed experience. Before reaching the summit, the weight of Dierich's forty years pressed upon him as heavily as forty leaden bullets. "We've lost the prize," he panted, his voice ragged. "Seize him dead if you cannot take him alive!" With those words, he abandoned the chase, trailing at a walk. One by one, Jorian Ketel and the others fell back until only a single figure remained relentless—a hound of war that refused to yield, though slowly losing ground with every step. That man was Eric Wouverman.

Pressing hard behind him, the group approached a steeper, shorter rise within the woods. "Grab the reins!" Martin shouted. Gerard complied, and the mule's powerful hooves carried them upward with a speed surpassing even his previous running pace. Witnessing this maneuver, Eric's resolve crumbled. Now nearly eighty yards behind Gerard and significantly ahead of his nearest companion, he halted abruptly. Obeying Dierich's silent command, he lowered his crossbow, aimed with deliberate precision, and, just as the trio vanished over the hill's crest, released the bolt. A cry of horror tore through the air. The next instant, as if struck by lightning, the entire party collapsed onto the earth—man and beast alike, brought low by the unseen arrow.
