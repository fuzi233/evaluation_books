PAULINA.


Several days passed, and it became apparent that Paulina was not likely to form any meaningful connections within the confines of Bretton House. Her demeanor was not one of overt misbehavior, nor was she prone to acts of defiance; instead, she exuded an aura of listlessness, an unrelenting melancholy that seemed to weigh upon her. Her countenance bore the unmistakable signs of homesickness, an affliction that often besets individuals severed from the familiarities of their native culture. As I, Lucy Snowe, observed her sitting in a corner, her head buried in her lap, the room seemed to take on a sense of desolation, as if the very presence of this young girl was a poignant reminder of the distance between her and the world beyond Bretton.

On moonlit evenings, when I chanced upon her kneeling upright in bed, her face bathed in the soft luminescence of the night lamp, I was struck by the fervor with which she prayed. Though her words were whispered, often unspoken, the phrases that escaped her lips betrayed a singular preoccupation – a fixation on a single, all-consuming idea: her father. It was a trait that I had come to associate with the affliction of monomania, a condition that I believed to be both a blessing and a curse. 

Her prayers, though inaudible to me, seemed to revolve around a single, plaintive phrase: "Papa; my dear papa!" This fixation on her father was a telling characteristic, one that hinted at a deeper psychological dynamic at play.

But Paulina's emotional landscape was about to undergo a profound shift. On a particular afternoon, Mrs. Bretton, attempting to rouse her from her customary corner, coaxed her onto the window-seat and tasked her with observing the passing pedestrians and counting the number of ladies who walked down the street within a set timeframe. Paulina sat listlessly, her gaze drifting aimlessly, until – my attention drawn to her – I witnessed a transformation of striking intensity. The fixed, somber expression gave way to a sudden radiance, her features illuminating with an unbridled enthusiasm. "It is," she exclaimed, as if awakened to a profound realization.
