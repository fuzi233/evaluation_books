The Ark drifted without helm, without oar, without engine—guided only by the will of the High Gods. It moved through the endless sea as if carried by unseen hands, its course determined not by man but by fate itself. Inside, darkness reigned, thick with the scent of age and decay, broken only by groans and the creaking of timbers under the weight of the storm. I searched in vain for the fire-box to relight the lamp that had fallen, so we were left to wander in silence, blind to what lay ahead.

The waves rose and fell like giants playing a cruel game. I clung to Nais beside the narrow bed, holding her fast as the vessel heaved beneath us. We spoke little, but in our shared sorrow and quiet endurance, there was a bond deeper than words. When Atlantis sank into the depths, it did not vanish quietly. Its final breaths stirred the ocean—great whirlpools erupted from the drowned fires, spewing forth smoke and fury as a last offering to the Sea Gods. Then came the wind—a hurricane of such power that it swept across the face of the world, leaving nothing untouched, no ruin unshattered. For seven days and seven nights, the tempest raged, measured only by the hour-reckoner hanging inside the Ark. At last, the wind ceased, and only a slow, oily swell remained, rolling gently beneath us.

It was then I dared to climb the stairs and open the hatch above. A rush of fresh air flooded down, clearing the stench of damp and death. The Ark rode high on the calm waters, and I descended to bring Nais up to meet the sun. Together, we stood at the edge, bathed in his golden rays, and offered our thanks to the Lord of Light for sparing us another day. Then we lay down where we stood, weary beyond measure, seeking rest in the warmth of the sky.

Yet sleep would not come. I stared out over the vast expanse of water, searching for any sign of land. But there was none. The horizon stretched endlessly, unbroken by island or shore. Above, the heavens remained still and clear. The deep was scattered with remnants of Atlantis—trees torn from their roots, beams split and floating, a shattered boat of hides, and occasionally, the bloated forms of men and beasts riding the swells beside us. These fragments were proof enough: what had happened was real, not a nightmare.

Eventually, exhaustion claimed me. I slipped into unconsciousness, clutching Nais’s hand. When I awoke, she was already awake, watching me with gentle eyes. We were both rested now, renewed in spirit. Rest brings peace, and peace brings acceptance. We no longer resisted our fate. With quiet resolve, we returned below to eat and drink, to strengthen ourselves for whatever lay ahead.

Now, at last, I could see the Ark clearly. Though it had never before touched water, it showed no flaw—no leak, no crack. Its floor was dry, its structure sound. The wood bore grain, yet nowhere was there a seam or joint. The timbers had been shaped and grown together by a craft long lost, fused into one living whole. Then some ancient treatment had hardened it, making it as strong as iron and impervious to time and tide.

Inside, the Ark held treasures of survival. At one end, great tanks stored grain; at the other, fresh water. Elsewhere, jars held medicines, essences of animals, samples of life preserved for future use. On every wall, carved in rough but vivid relief, were images of the creatures that once roamed Atlantis—some familiar, others strange, long vanished with the people who once knew them. And there were weapons, tools, and instruments of craft—everything needed for a new beginning.

For weeks, we drifted across the ocean, ruled entirely by the currents and the gods. We had no control over direction, no way to speed or steer. The High Gods had chosen us—the last two survivors—and they alone dictated our path. We accepted this with humility, trusting in Their wisdom.

Of the land we eventually reached, where we settled and raised children, I shall speak in due time. But first, I must tell how these writings came to be.

In the center of the Ark, beneath a heavy coffer, lay scrolls containing all the Mysteries—knowledge gathered over centuries. I read them, and my soul trembled. These were powers beyond imagining—forces that had once shaped Atlantis, and brought about her fall. Among them was the Secret of Life and Death, the same truth Phorenice had discovered and for which she died. Others defied description, too terrible to record.

I could not bear the burden of such knowledge. To keep it felt like a sin. So, one night as the Ark rolled beneath the stars, I took hot irons and burned the wax-coated sheets, erasing every word. If I sinned, the High Gods would judge me. If such secrets must endure, They would reveal them again to worthy hands. But I destroyed them—there, upon the waves.

Later, when we reached dry ground, I rewrote these pages—not the forbidden truths, but the story of how Atlantis fell, and how we survived.
