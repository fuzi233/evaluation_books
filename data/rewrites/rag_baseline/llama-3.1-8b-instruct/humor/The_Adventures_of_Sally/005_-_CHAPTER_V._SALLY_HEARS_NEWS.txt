The streets of New York gleamed like polished wood, a warm light dancing across the wet pavement. Sally's taxi driver navigated the morning chaos with a practiced ease, weaving between pedestrians and streetcars. The city's rhythms seemed to pulse in harmony with her own, a symphony of steel and stone that filled her with a sense of belonging. For a moment, she forgot the weariness that had settled in her bones, the ache of being away from the familiar comforts of her boarding house.

As the taxi pulled up to the curb, Sally felt a flutter in her chest. She paid the driver, gathered her belongings, and stepped out onto the sidewalk. The cool morning air was alive with the scent of freshly brewed coffee and baking bread. It was a smell she associated with Mrs. Meecher's boarding house, a place that had become a sanctuary for her over the past three years. She had promised herself that she would find a new apartment, one that would be hers alone, but for now, it seemed like a luxury she couldn't afford.

The familiar facade of the boarding house loomed before her, its windows glowing like lanterns in the morning light. Sally felt a pang of nostalgia wash over her, a longing for the simple pleasures of her old life. She pushed open the door, and a bell above it let out a tired clang. Mrs. Meecher's voice called out from the depths of the house, a warm, raspy sound that was both a greeting and a warning.

"Sally, dear, you're back! Come on up, I've got your room ready."

As she climbed the stairs, the creaking of the wooden steps beneath her feet seemed to echo the rhythm of her heartbeat. She reached the top landing, and Mrs. Meecher's door swung open, a welcoming smile spreading across her face.

"Welcome back, dear! I'm so glad you're home. Come in, come in, and sit down. I've got some news to share with you."

Sally dropped her bag onto the floor, feeling a sense of relief wash over her. She had been looking forward to this reunion, to reconnecting with the people and places that had become her family. But as she followed Mrs. Meecher into the parlor, she sensed a hint of unease in the air, a subtle undercurrent that threatened to disrupt the warmth of their reunion.

"What's wrong?" Sally asked, her voice barely above a whisper.
