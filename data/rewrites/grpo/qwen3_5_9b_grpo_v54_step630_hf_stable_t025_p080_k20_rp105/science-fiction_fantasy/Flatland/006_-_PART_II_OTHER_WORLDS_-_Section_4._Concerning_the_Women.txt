If the sharp, wedge-shaped Triangles of the Soldier class strike fear into the hearts of the populace, it requires little deduction to realize that our Women are forces of a magnitude far exceeding even their military counterparts. Where a Soldier is merely a wedge, a Woman is a needle—a creature defined entirely by its extremities, possessing lethal acuteness at both ends. To this inherent danger, she adds the terrifying ability to render herself practically invisible at will. Thus, in Flatland, the Female is not a being to be trifled with; she is a walking catastrophe.

Perhaps my younger readers from Spaceland will pause here, asking how such invisibility is possible. The answer lies in simple geometry, requiring no complex magic. Consider a needle resting on a table. Viewed from the side, it presents a long, dangerous line. Viewed from the end, however, it collapses into a mere point, vanishing from sight. So too is it with our Women. When a woman turns her side toward us, she appears as a straight line, a clear threat. When she faces us directly, showing her eye or mouth—organs that in Flatland are identical—we see only a brilliant, lustrous point, effectively invisible. Conversely, when she turns her back, presenting only a dull, sub-lustrous extremity, she becomes nearly indistinguishable from the background, her rear serving as a natural "Invisible Cap."

The peril posed by these entities is absolute. In Spaceland, one might survive a collision with a working man or suffer a wound from a soldier; in Flatland, running into a Woman is synonymous with immediate and total destruction. And when she is invisible, or visible only as a faint glimmer, the margin for error shrinks to zero. Even the most cautious citizen risks annihilation.

Consequently, the laws of Flatland are rife with statutes designed to mitigate this existential threat, particularly in the southern states where gravity is stronger and involuntary motion is more common. The code generally mandates three primary rules: First, every dwelling must possess a dedicated eastern entrance for females, ensuring they enter with "becoming and respectful manner," distinct from the western door used by men. Second, no woman may traverse a public space without maintaining a constant, audible "Peace-cry," under penalty of death. Third, any female suffering from conditions causing involuntary motion—such as St. Vitus's Dance or severe sneezing—is to be instantly destroyed.

Some states impose further restrictions, forbidding women from standing still in public or mandating that they be trailed by a male relative. Yet, the wisest circles have concluded that excessive restriction breeds resentment. When confined to their homes or hampered abroad, women's tempers flare, often resulting in domestic massacres that decimate entire villages within hours. Therefore, the three core laws remain the standard for regulated societies.

Ultimately, our greatest safeguard is not legislation, but the biological limitations of the women themselves. While a single backward thrust can kill instantly, the woman's own frailty prevents her from sustaining the struggle; she must disengage immediately or be shattered. Furthermore, the power of fashion acts as a subtle defense. In civilized states, it is considered a mark of breeding for a woman to sway her back rhythmically from right to left. This undulation, envied by wives of lower ranks who can only achieve a monotonous pendulum swing, ensures that in households of status, the "back-motion" is as ubiquitous as time itself, granting husbands and sons immunity from invisible attacks.

Do not mistake this for a lack of affection. Unfortunately, the Frail Sex is governed by the passion of the moment, devoid of reflection, judgment, or foresight due to their lack of angles and consequent lack of brain-power. In their fury, they recognize no distinctions. I have witnessed a woman exterminate her entire household, only to ask, half an hour later, where her husband and children had gone once her rage subsided.

Thus, a woman must never be irritated while she retains the ability to turn around. Once confined to apartments designed to deny her this mobility, she becomes impotent for mischief, unable to remember threats or promises made in the heat of the moment.

Domestic life in Flatland proceeds smoothly enough, save for the lower military classes, where husbands' lack of tact and reliance on their own sharp angles rather than diplomacy leads to disaster. These reckless males often neglect the proper construction of women's quarters or provoke their wives with irrevocable insults, resulting in massacre. Paradoxically, this destructiveness is viewed by many as a providential mechanism for controlling population and stifling revolution.

Even in the most harmonious, circular families, life lacks the deep peace found in Spaceland. Safety has been purchased at the cost of comfort. It is an ancient instinct among the women of the higher classes to keep their eyes and mouths perpetually directed toward their husbands and male guests. To turn one's back upon a husband in a distinguished household is a portent of lost status. While this ensures safety, it creates a cacophony of relentless discourse. In the homes of the working class, where a wife may turn her back to hum her Peace-cry, there are moments of silence. But in the upper echelons, the bright, penetrating eye and the voluble mouth are ever-present. The skill required to dodge a sting is useless against the torrent of speech. As the wife has nothing meaningful to say and no conscience to restrain her, many cynical husbands confess they would rather face the silent, deadly sting than endure the safe, sonorous noise of a woman's other end.

To the citizens of Spaceland, the plight of our women may seem tragic. Indeed, it is. While a male Isosceles might hope for evolution and a sharper angle, a Woman has no such future. "Once a Woman, always a Woman" is the immutable decree of nature; evolution itself seems to suspend its progress in her favor. Yet, we find a grim comfort in this prearrangement: since they have no hopes for the future, they possess no memory to recall past humiliations and no forethought to anticipate future suffering. They are trapped in the eternal present, a necessity of their existence and the foundation of our society.
