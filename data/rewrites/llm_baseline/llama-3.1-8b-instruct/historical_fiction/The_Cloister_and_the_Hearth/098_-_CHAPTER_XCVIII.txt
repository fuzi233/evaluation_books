The scent of fresh bread wafted through the air as Gerard stepped into the Dominican convent, its stone walls a testament to the passing of time. He had come to seek solace among the gentle brothers, to surrender his worn body to their care, just as he had once surrendered his spirit to the Lord. The prior, Brother Ambrose, governed the convent in the interim, his wise hands guiding the community with compassion and kindness. Gerard's eyes, sunken from the weight of his years, searched for the familiar face of his dear friend, whose presence had been a beacon of comfort in his darkest hours.

As a novice, Gerard had first entered these hallowed halls, but the truth was, his body had begun to falter, his steps slowing with each passing year. His home, once a sanctuary of love and laughter, now felt like a prison, weighed down by the memories of her, the love of his life. Ambrose, perceptive as ever, recognized the depth of Gerard's sorrow and the desperation in his eyes. He welcomed Gerard with open arms, offering a warm cell above the gateway, where the scent of blooming flowers and baking bread would envelop him, and he could find solace in the simple rhythms of monastic life.

But their tranquility was short-lived, for the new prior soon arrived, his arrival like a gust of wind that swept away the calm. His name was Jerome, and he brought with him a stern resolve to root out the perceived vices and tighten the reins of discipline within the convent. At first, his focus was on rectifying the community's shortcomings, but when he learned that Gerard, the vicar of Gouda, had taken refuge among them, his demeanor turned icy. "Let him first relinquish his position," Jerome commanded, his voice like a crack of thunder. "Or else, he must depart. I will not harbor fat clergy within these walls." The words stung Gerard, and he rose to his feet, his eyes locking onto those of the prior, a spark of defiance flickering in their depths. "Clement!" he called out, his voice barely above a whisper.
