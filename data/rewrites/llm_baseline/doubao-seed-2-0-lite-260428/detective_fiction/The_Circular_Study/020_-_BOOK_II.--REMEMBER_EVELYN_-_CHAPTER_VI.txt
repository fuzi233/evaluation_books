A thick, thrumming silence hung over the room in the wake of that final, wrenching appeal, every person gathered there still reeling from the extraordinary story they had just heard. Small, breathless whispers broke out across the crowd as they exchanged disbelieving half-sentences, while Thomas Adams turned to his wife, his face softening with equal parts tender love and raw, unspent anxiety. The inspector passed the crumpled, annotated memorandum he had received from Mr. Gryce back across the table to the veteran detective. The sheet was covered in scrawled questions, almost every one marked in the margin with the same sharp, handwritten note: *Answered*.

The full list of riddles the police had been chasing since the murder read:
1.  Why would a woman calm enough to pause mid-interview to fix her hair (the comb found discarded on the floor of Adams’s bedroom was proof of this) spiral so fast into ungovernable frenzy that she killed a man she held no known prior grudge against? *Answered*.
2.  What did Felix Adams mean by the note he wrote right before that fatal interview: “I return you your daughter. Neither you nor she will ever see me again. Remember Evelyn!” *Answered*.
3.  Why did Felix use the first-person “I” in that note? What standing did he hold with his brother’s new bride that gave him the right to speak of her that way, after she had married Thomas? *Answered*.
4.  After that note was written, why did Felix, when attacked, fight to swallow the very paper he had written it on, dying with it still clamped tight between his teeth? *Answered*.
5.  If he had been killed in a blind, unplanned burst of rage, as the young woman had claimed, why did his face show nothing but quiet sorrow and deliberate resolve, no trace of the furious shock a man would feel when struck a mortal blow by an attacker he never saw coming? *Answered*.
6.  If he had the strength to grab that note and drag it to his mouth, why did he not use that same strength to flip on the bright white light that would have called for help? Instead he left the crimson lamp glowing, a signal they had decoded to mean: *“Nothing more required just now. Keep away.”* *Answered*.
7.  What was the purpose of the huge steel plate hidden between the two casings of the study doorway, and why did it stay locked motionless in its socket at the exact climax of Felix’s life? *Answered*.
8.  Why did old Poindexter show up on the scene barely minutes after the murder, muttering the overheard line “It is Amos' son, not Amos!”? Had he not known who he was supposed to meet at the house? Was the identity of the victim more of a shock to him than the sight of a dead man with a cross on his chest and a dagger in his heart? *Answered*.
9.  Remember the conclusions we drew from Bartow’s silent pantomime: Mr. Felix Adams was killed by a left-handed thrust. Watch for proof the young woman is left-handed, and do not forget we still need an explanation for why she held one arm stretched rigid behind her for so long in the immediate aftermath of the murder. *Not Answered*.
10. What did it mean that the parrot that screamed “Remember Evelyn!” on repeat would sometimes alter its cry to wail “Poor Eva! Lovely Eva! Who would strike Eva?” The full truth of the tragedy had to prove that Felix Adams had known his brother’s bride for a very long time, far better than anyone had guessed. *Answered*.
11. If Bartow the deaf-mute servant was only a witness to the crime, and no accomplice, why did he show unmistakeable, wild joy the second he saw Felix dead? The scraps of Felix’s handwriting the police had not yet found would later reveal the servant was consumed by a bitter, unspoken jealousy, and he’d been overjoyed to see the master he hated struck down by the woman Felix had obsessed over. *Answered*.
12. This schedule was confirmed after repeated tests with Bartow and the strange multi-colored lamp in Felix’s study: White light = Water wanted, Green light = Bring overcoat and hat, Blue light = Put back books on shelves, Violet light = Prepare study for the night, Yellow light = Wait for next signal, Red light = No help needed, stay away. A red light was glowing in the study at the exact moment of the murder. *Answered*.

Only two small threads had not been fully untangled, the most critical being point 9, the riddle of the left-handed stab. Mr. Gryce conferred for a quick, quiet moment with the inspector, then approached Thomas Adams and asked if he had the strength to reenact exactly how he had seen his wife deliver the fatal blow. The shocked young man stared back, too stunned to speak at first. He knew rumors had spread that the deaf-mute servant had mimed the crime for the police, but no one had told him the specific details of that pantomime—information the detectives had deliberately held back from all witnesses.

He did not answer right away, but sat lost in thought for a long minute, then lifted his left hand, grabbed a heavy paperweight off the inspector’s desk, and lunged across his own body, driving the makeshift weapon toward an imaginary figure standing at his right side. He looked up at the faces of the men around him, his expression tight with unspoken terror, searching for any sign of leniency.

“You just demonstrated that your wife struck the blow left-handed,” Mr. Gryce said. “But I have watched her every second since she arrived here, and I have not seen a single sign that she is left-handed.”

“She is not,” Thomas said, his shoulders slumping in relief. “But she could not use her right hand, because I had clamped my own hand over hers the second she lunged for the dagger. Our hands were locked together, knotted so tight I still bear the bruise, until the horror of what she had done crashed over her and she fainted, collapsing to the floor sobbing, ‘Tear down the cross and lay it on your brother's breast. I would at least see him die the death of a Christian.’”

A wave of unmistakeable relief passed over both men. The riddle of her strange, rigid pose, holding one arm stretched out behind her for minutes after the stabbing, was explained perfectly: she had still been trapped in that iron-clasped grip with her husband. Mr. Gryce picked up the blue pencil the inspector had laid down, and with a quiet, satisfied smile, scrawled a thick, decisive *Answered* straight across paragraph number 9. The last major puzzle piece of the circular study murder had clicked into place.
