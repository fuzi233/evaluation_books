Madame Beck navigated the world with a steadfast resolve, unwavering in her dedication to maintaining an aura of composure. Her demeanor towards her children reflected this unyielding nature, devoid of warmth or tender affection. Though solicitous about their physical well-being, she rarely indulged in the simple pleasures of motherhood – the soft caresses, gentle words, or warm embraces that often come so naturally to parents.

I recall observing her in the garden, lost in thought as she watched the bees flit about in a nearby flowerbed with Trinette, their governess. Her expression conveyed a sense of measured concern, a deliberate consideration for the future of those under her care. The youngest child, a fragile yet endearing infant, would occasionally break free from its nurse and hasten towards Madame, laughing and panting with excitement. In response, Madame would extend a cautionary hand, gently restraining the child's forward momentum: "Take care, my child," she would say, her tone detached, before calmly retrieving the infant and returning it to Trinette.

Her behavior towards the eldest child, Désirée, revealed a different aspect of her character. This young girl was a force to be reckoned with, her mischievous nature often leaving those around her perplexed and exasperated. Madame would frequently remark, "What a pest this Désirée is! What poison has been distilled within this child!" Her remarks were echoed in both the kitchen and the schoolroom, where Désirée's antics had earned her a reputation as a master provocateur. She possessed an uncanny ability to manipulate those around her, frequently goading her governess and the servants to the point of exasperation.

Désirée's mischievous exploits knew no bounds – she would often sneak into the attic, rummage through drawers and boxes, and willfully destroy valuable items. She would also target the buffet in the dining room, smashing delicate china or glassware, or raid the pantry in the storage room, drinking sweet wine and breaking jars and bottles. Whenever Madame discovered evidence of these transgressions, her reaction remained remarkably serene: "Désirée requires close supervision." And so, she maintained a watchful eye on the errant child, keeping her in constant sight.
