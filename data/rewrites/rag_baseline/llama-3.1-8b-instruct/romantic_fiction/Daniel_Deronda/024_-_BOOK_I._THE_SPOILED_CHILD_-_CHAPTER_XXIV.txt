In the hushed, golden light of late summer, the Gascoigne rectory stood as a testament to the unyielding rhythms of the English countryside. Gwendolen, her dark hair pulled back in a tight braid, walked beside her mother through the dew-kissed gardens, the crunch of gravel beneath their feet a steady heartbeat that underscored the quiet desperation that had taken hold of their household. They had stopped earlier at Sawyer's Cottage, its bare rooms and dusty furnishings a stark reminder of the life they were about to leave behind. The thought of it weighed heavily on Gwendolen's heart, and she felt a familiar surge of defiance rise up within her.

"How will you endure it, Mother?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper, as they continued on their way. "You and the girls, all of you crammed into that tiny space, with the yellow and green wallpaper closing in on you like a prison? And without me?"

Her mother's response was gentle, but Gwendolen knew better than to expect any real comfort from her. Instead, she pressed on, her words tumbling forth in a torrent of frustration. "If I hadn't promised to earn my own keep, I'd rather be there than become some governess, stuck in a palace with a bunch of spoiled children. It's like a bad dream, and I still can't believe Uncle Gascoigne would allow you to take such a step."

As they approached the rectory, Gwendolen's mother offered a measured reply, her words a soothing balm to the raw emotions that had been building inside her daughter. "Don't be unreasonable, dear child. Your uncle has done what he thinks best. And think of all the luxuries you'll enjoy at the palace – the fine clothes, the lavish feasts, the music and the art. You've always loved those things, haven't you?"

Gwendolen's retort was swift and sharp, her words a reflection of the turbulent world she navigated within her own mind. "That's just it – I used to love those things, but now they seem hollow, like a shell that's been emptied of its meaning. And what about all the other people who aren't so fortunate? Don't they deserve a bit of consideration too?"

As they entered the rectory, Gwendolen's uncle and aunt greeted her with a warmth that surprised her. For a moment, she forgot her anger and her fear, and allowed herself to be drawn into their circle
