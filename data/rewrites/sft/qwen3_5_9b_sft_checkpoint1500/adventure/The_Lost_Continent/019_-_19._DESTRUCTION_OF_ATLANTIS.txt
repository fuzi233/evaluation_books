An aged priest, his legs shaking beneath robes too heavy for his frame, approached and laid a trembling hand upon my shoulder.

"Well?" I demanded, cutting him off with sharp impatience. I had no appetite for interruptions at this precise hour.

"News has been conveyed to the Three, O King, regarding the peril that threatens us."

"They shall know then that I stand here, brother, to engage in the finest combat of my life," I replied, my voice rising. "When it concludes, I shall depart to the Gods. I will stand behind the stars to greet them when they arrive, for I regret deeply that they are too elderly and frail to witness a glorious slaughter firsthand."

"I bear commands from them, O King, which I dread you will receive poorly," the priest stammered. "You are strictly forbidden from finding your death in this fray. The Three require you for a purpose far greater than battle."

I whirled upon him, anger flaring. "I shall accept no such decree, my brother. I refuse to believe it was ever issued. You must have misunderstood. If I am a man, a priest, a soldier, and a king, then honor dictates that no enemy shall pass this gate while I draw breath. Go back and spit this message in their teeth!"

The old man smiled, a glint of envy in his eyes—he, too, had once been a warrior of keen spirit. "I told them you would not readily accept such a message. I asked for a sign, and they bore with me, granting me one. I am to deliver this jewel to you, O King."

"How did they come by it?" I asked, staring at the object in his hand. "It is a bracelet from the arm of Nais."

"They must have stripped her of it. I knew not it belonged to Nais. The instruction I received stated that the owner of the jewel waits within the Ark of the Mysteries, awaiting you. The further use the Three intend for you concerns her greatly."

Even hearing this, I must confess, my resolve wavered sorely. I felt the shame less heavily in recounting it now, for every true soldier understands the torment of such a dilemma. The prospect of the fight was intoxicating; I would have stayed to die gloriously. Yet, in the end, my love for Nais triumphed. I offered the necessary salute, acknowledging the order and pledging obedience.

Turning to the cluster of priests remaining to defend the pass, I bid them farewell. "You will suffer what I shall miss, my brothers," I said softly. "I envy you that struggle. Yet, though I am King of Atlantis, I am but one of the Seven, and thus a servant of the Three. I must obey their will, spoken in the tongue of the Highest Gods. You will stand behind the stars before I join you, and I pray you commend me to those you encounter there. It is not my desire to be absent from your ranks."

Their smiles were kind, and they saluted me with courtesy and steel. We parted ways. I did not witness the battle, but I sensed its ferocity from the silence that fell before Phorenice's hordes erupted onto the mountain's crest. That last remnant of Atlantis' lesser priests fought hard, dying with honor.

Bitter feelings gnawed at me as I ascended the pass, threading through groves and between temples and colleges perched on the upper slopes of the Sacred Mountain until I reached the boundary line—a threshold forbidden to all but the privileged in calmer times. Conventions had expired; my temper burned hot. I might have strung onward with little scruple had I not been stopped.

Inside the temple marking the boundary, old Zaemon awaited. With grave solemnity and adherence to ancient rites, he sought dispensation from the High Gods for my trespass, refusing to yield the path until his ceremony concluded.

Below, Phorenice's tower and bridge were already deployed. The clash and screams confirmed that the handful of priests on the last gate's ramparts were trading their lives for the highest possible reward in death. Though trained fighters, they were old and weak; the odds were insurmountable. Soon, the stronghold would fall to the storm, leaving the roof of the Sacred Mountain exposed to the invader. If anything else needed doing, it had to be done swiftly, before peace dissolved entirely. Lengthy ceremonial seemed a luxury the moment demanded.

Yet, Zaemon was intent on delay. He led me to the Ark of the Mysteries, chiding my impatience, and waited reverently for me to kiss it before calling aloud. Another old man emerged from the aperture atop the Ark, climbing down the side battens. He was hoary, frail, and emaciated. Together with Zaemon, they were the sole survivors raised to the highest degree of the Clan, guardians of secrets and powers unknown to mortals.

"Look!" cried Zaemon, his shrill voice echoing as he swept a trembling finger across the shattered landscape below. I followed his gaze, and a chill crawled through my veins. All was laid bare. Our Lord the Sun burned high in a cloudless blue sky, day shimmering in His heat. Below, everything appeared peaceful and still, save for the smoking mountains and the sea, which boiled with a strange, violent disorder.

But it was the sea that haunted me. Far out on the distant coast, it surged against the rocks in monstrous rolls. Up the great estuary, where Atlantis stood, it gushed in successive, towering waves that never returned. The lower lands on both sides were already erased beneath tumultuous waters; harbor walls were submerged, and the flood crept upward into the lower wards of the great city.

"You have seen?" asked Zaemon.

"I have seen."

"You understand?"

"In part."

"Then let me tell you all. This is the beginning, and the swift end follows. The Most High Gods, who sit behind the stars, have a limit to Their patience, and it has been crossed. Atlantis, the great continent beyond, and all within it, are doomed to unutterable destruction. Long ago, it was foreseen that this wiping-out would occur through the sins of men, leading to the construction of the Ark of the Mysteries under divine direction. No mortal tool can scratch its surface; no wave or rock can wreck it. Within lies recorded on ancient sheets all worldly learning not shared with the common folk, stores of grain sufficient for two persons for four years, sweet water, seeds, weapons, and whatever else was deemed fit."

He paused, his eyes wide with terror and awe. "From this vast realm, the High Gods have decreed that two shall not perish. Two shall be chosen—a man and a woman—who are fit to carry away the ancient learning, dispose of it as they see best, and rear a new race to honor the Sun and the Gods elsewhere. The woman is already within the Ark, seated in her appointed place. Though she is my daughter, the burden of her selection rests with you. As for the man, the choice falls upon yourself."

Shock numbed me. "I do not care to be a survivor."

"You are not asked for your wishes," the old man insisted. "You are given an order from the High Gods, who know you to be Their faithful servant."

Habit, strong as iron, took hold. I offered the requisite salute and declared, "I hear and obey."

"There remains the elevation to the sublime degree of the Three," Zaemon continued, his voice steady despite the chaos. "If your learning is so slight that you cannot grasp the keys to many powers and the highest mysteries when revealed, the fault cannot be remedied now."

Time was running short. The fight raged below at the gate, a miracle that the few priests had held their ground so long. But the ocean rolled inland in an ever-growing flood, and the mountains smoked, belching fresh volleys of rock as the weight increased upon their foundations. Soon, the besiegers would see the doom threatening them. Madness would seize them, sweeping all obstructions aside with frantic fury.

Yet, Zaemon and his companion showed no haste. Lacking a temple, sacrifice, or incense, they used the sky as their roof and the Gods as their witnesses. Still, they performed the rituals of my initiation with all the grandeur and majesty accumulated over the ages, curtailing not a single sentence. Outside, a mountain burst with a bellowing roar as incoming waters met its fires, but the old men recited their sentences gravely. Phorenice's troops breached the last defenses, pouring a frenzied stream through the groves and temples, but the priests never faltered in their chant.

It was said this ceremony was the greatest and most impressive of our holy rites, and I found it so. Even the tremendous circumstances hemming them in could not make these frail elders forget the deference due to the highest order. For myself, I was less entranced. Standing bareheaded in the heat, I struggled to concentrate, torn by a thousand foreign emotions. The cataclysm unfolding around me demanded attention, and my mind drifted constantly to Nais. Yet, I maintained a decent facade, responding to the required chants and striving to memorize the keys to power and learning hidden within the phrases.

It became evident that if the ceremony did not conclude soon, it would be cut short abruptly. Phorenice's legions swarmed onto the mountain's crest, now aware of the dreadful ruin descending upon their homeland.
