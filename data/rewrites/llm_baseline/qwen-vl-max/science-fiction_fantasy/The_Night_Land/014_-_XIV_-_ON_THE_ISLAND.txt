I came to myself slowly, weak and disoriented, pain thrumming through my body like a distant echo. I tried to rise, but something held me down—perhaps the weight of my wounds, perhaps the exhaustion that had dragged me under. My head throbbed, and the world swam in shadows.

I lay on my back, breath shallow, when a soft sound reached me—a faint, ragged panting. Turning my head with great effort, I saw her. The Maid. She was near, still bare, her form trembling as she pushed the raft forward with a long pole—the very one I’d cut during the attack of the Humpt Men. In that moment, memory flooded back: the violence, the darkness, the suddenness of it all. I was on the raft. She had saved me.

I made a small sound, a whisper of life, but she didn’t hear. Her gaze was fixed on the shore, eyes sharp with fear. Behind her, from the trees, came the howling of the Humpt Men—familiar, terrible voices. I understood then: she had pulled me from the wreckage, carried me to safety while I was unconscious. How? I never knew. Neither did she. Only love gave her the strength to act, to risk everything for me.

Later, she told me what had happened. When she regained consciousness, she found me lying across her chest, motionless. My head was heavy, my blood staining the earth around us. She thought I was dead. Heartbreaking, she lifted me gently, cradled me, and wept. But then—life. A faint pulse. Hope surged within her. She ran to the raft, filled my headpiece with river water, and splashed it over my face. Nothing. Still no response.

Then, a sudden instinct—a warning from the depths of her spirit. Danger was coming. She would not lose me. She struggled, dragged me across the rocky ground, brought me to the raft, climbed aboard, and pushed us into the water. As she grabbed the pole, she saw her torn clothes still clutched in the hand of a fallen Humpt Man. Without hesitation, she snatched them and ran. She leaped onto the raft just as two survivors burst from the woods, chasing her silently, hungrily. But they stopped at the edge of the water. They could not swim—or feared the unknown terrors beneath. They stood, stunned, then let out their howls of rage and frustration.

That was the sound I heard when I woke—her desperate efforts pulling us away from death, the echoes of our enemies fading into the distance. I wanted to help, to rise, to fight beside her. But weakness overwhelmed me again, and I slipped back into unconsciousness. And she—my beloved, my naked guardian—guided us safely to the little island, her only thought: to save me.

When I finally awoke again, clarity returned slowly. Pain lingered, but so did warmth. I was laid upon something soft, wrapped in cloth. Peace settled around me like a gentle fog. And there she was—kneeling beside me, her eyes full of love and relief. She leaned down and kissed me, tears falling softly onto my face. I kissed her back, every movement a promise, a return to life.

She cradled my head, fed me a broth made from tablets and water, measured by my timepiece. Then she kissed me once more—so tenderly, it felt like a breeze brushing my lips. I drifted into sleep, safe in her care.

This scene repeated three times. On the third awakening, I moved my hand slightly. She noticed, took it in hers, and held it tightly. I slept again, content, watching her with half-lidded eyes.

On the fourth morning, I whispered, “I love you.” She wept, clutching my hand to her chest, as if holding me could keep me alive forever.

By the fifth day, I was healing. My body was bandaged—her own torn garments stitched together with threads from her clothes, needles fashioned from thorns. She had worked tirelessly, mending, cleaning, caring. I watched her, and she blushed, kissing me to hide her shyness. I wished I were strong enough to kneel before her, to honor her sacrifice. But even in weakness, I felt whole because of her.

Each day, she brought me broth, washed me, changed the bandages, and dried them carefully. We were nearly out of supplies, but she made do. On the sixth day, I spoke freely. I told her how much I loved her—not just with words, but with every breath. She said she was well, though I saw the toll it had taken—she was thinner, her eyes shadowed, yet radiant with joy.

I asked her to bring me the tablets. She did. I kissed them, then ate. I asked for broth, and she prepared it. Then I requested the Diskos be placed near me. I called her to my side, and she lay beside me, her head resting in my arm. I told her to sleep. She hesitated—afraid she might weigh too heavily—but I held her gently, showing her I was stronger now. She nestled close, and slept deeply for twelve hours, her breathing calm, her face peaceful.

I watched her, entranced. She was beautiful—delicate, pure. Her presence filled me with quiet awe. I drank broth at intervals, my right arm free to reach for the Diskos. I touched it often, as if it were an old friend. It felt alive, connected to me. Perhaps it was. Or perhaps it was simply my heart, uplifted by love, that made it feel so.

At noon, she woke suddenly, startled. She looked at me—then smiled, relieved. I laughed quietly, and she scolded herself for losing track of time. I teased her, forbidding her to speak another word about it. She playfully punched my nose, and I laughed so hard she feared I’d reopen my wounds. But I was fine. Just happy.

I asked her if she had brothers—why she played so freely. Instantly, I realized how foolish the question was. I took her hand, silent, and she nodded, then kissed my palm. She stepped away, and I knew she was hiding tears. I called to her softly. She returned, smiling, but I saw the dampness in her eyes. Still, she made new broth, and I insisted she come to me first.

We ate together, laughing, talking. I fell asleep, but kept her near. That night, I dreamed of her—of us—safe, together.

The seventh day dawned bright and sweet. I woke to find her sleeping beside me, her face pressed against my shoulder. She stirred at once—watchful, ever vigilant. We ate, bathed, and joked. I was allowed full tablets now, and I devoured them, feeling strength return. Each tablet helped rebuild my blood, restore my vitality. I needed this power—to protect her, to reach the Mighty Pyramid.

We shared food, kissed each other’s tablets, drank from the same cup. We were children, lovers, partners. She changed my bandages, washed me, and kept me low-lying. I didn’t mind. I was too weak, and she was too precious to resist.

Afterward, she went to bathe in a pool beyond the bushes. I asked her to hurry. She promised, and returned soon—her hair flowing like a cloud, her feet bare and glistening. She teased me for being impatient, but I knew she stayed longer because she enjoyed my gaze.

I pulled her to me, scolding her for not caring for her feet. I made her place them toward me, and then—slyly—I tied her toes together with a strand of her hair. She gasped, then laughed. I had captured her. She tried to pull away, but kept the hair intact, sitting bound beside me, pretending not to notice.

Then she styled her hair, and I untied it, teasing her. She kissed me and asked how she’d ever manage if I kept doing this. I plucked another lock of her hair, and she cut one from mine. We braided them together—our hair joined—and she tucked it into her bosom. I wanted to do the same, but was too weak. So she cut another pair, and we kissed each other’s locks, then braided them again. I placed mine beneath the bandage over my heart. She pouted—because the second braid had been kissed, the first hadn’t. I refused to swap. We quarreled, then kissed, forgiving each other.

She made me lie still, threatened to punish me if I touched her hair again. I pretended to be humble. She held my hands with one small fist, barely able to grasp me. Then, with her free hand, she covered my eyes. While I was helpless, she kissed me—daring, sweet—then released me, demure.

We sat in silence. Then I reached out, my hand trembling, pale from lack of blood. She knew what I meant. She clenched both fists and placed them in my palm. They were small, fragile. But I felt whole. I was happy.

I teased her again, saying her hands were too tiny to do anything. She wrapped her arms around my neck, kissed me fiercely, then stepped back, afraid of unbalancing me.

I asked her to sit beside me. I told her a story—an old tale from the past. Of a man who met the One Maid Upon All The Earth. They married, she died, and he was consumed by grief. Then, in a dream, he awoke into a future age—only to learn she lived there too. He searched for her, found her, and though she was changed, she remained lovely. He revered her—his wife from a forgotten time—love so deep it became a constant ache of sweetness and sorrow.

I couldn’t finish. She began to weep, dropped to her knees, and pressed my hand to her breast. She placed her hand over my lips, and whispered through her tears. Her face glowed with motherhood, her eyes lit with memory—something ancient, painful, yet sacred.

And then I remembered. The babe. The child who lived on after she died. I told her. There was silence. Then she leaned into me, and I drew her into my arms. She tried to speak of the vision, but words failed her. I stayed silent too. Some truths are beyond speech.

She kissed me, and returned to her tasks.

It was a perfect day. I had strength enough to talk, to laugh, to be truly present. She was rested, her heart at ease. We joked, teased, and shared memories. I asked her an old riddle—about a horse. She answered instantly, as if remembering from beyond time. "He was a little hoarse," she said.

We both paused. We had never seen a horse. Yet we knew. Not with our eyes, but with our souls. We looked at each other, wondering what such a creature was—yet feeling its shape in the dim corners of our minds.

We wandered through ages, dreams, and echoes of a world long gone. Then, I pulled us back. From the abyss, into the present. And she smiled again.

I told her everything I knew of the Mighty Pyramid—its towers, its secrets, its purpose. She listened, spellbound. It was like hearing tales from another star. She asked questions, endlessly curious. And I answered, my voice steady, my heart full.

For hours, we talked. She believed. She hoped. She dreamed.

And in that moment, on the quiet island, surrounded by silence and love, I knew—no matter what lay ahead, we would face it together.
