As Major Pendennis made his way back to the George Inn, the evening mist swirling around him like a damp veil, he encountered an unexpected visitor waiting at the entrance – his trusted valet, Mr. Morgan. With a discreet nod, the valet ushered the Major into the warm glow of the coffee-room, where a lone figure slumbered amidst the remnants of dinner. The Major's gaze fell upon the sleeping form of Mr. Foker, the son of Lady Hagnes, his eyes closed as if in surrender to the allure of a copious meal.

Upon entering, Major Pendennis was met with the faint scent of cigar smoke and the faintest hint of absinthe. The room's sole occupant, Mr. Foker, stirred, his eyes fluttering open as he blinked away the haze of sleep. As their gazes met, he rose from his slumped position, stretching his limbs in a languid motion, and invited the Major to peruse the evening's newspaper. The Major, grateful for the distraction, accepted the offer and engaged in conversation with Mr. Foker, whose affable nature quickly put him at ease.

As they sipped their drinks, the Major inquired about the young man's identity, and Mr. Foker, with a hint of mischief, revealed his connection to the esteemed Lady Agnes Foker. The Major, pleased to discover a shared acquaintance, complimented Mr. Foker on his family resemblance, noting the distinct Rosherville features that seemed to run in the Foker bloodline.

Their conversation flowed effortlessly, with Mr. Foker regaling the Major with tales of his adventures in Baymouth, where he was currently preparing for the bar exams. The Major listened intently, his expression a mix of amusement and concern, as Mr. Foker spoke candidly about his friend Arthur Pendennis, his nephew and ward. The young man's words were laced with a mix of admiration and criticism, as he described Arthur's endearing qualities and his propensity for being 'flat' – a term Mr. Foker employed to describe Arthur's apparent lack of interest in romantic entanglements.

The Major's curiosity was piqued, and he pressed Mr. Foker for further insight into Arthur's character. With a wink, the young man launched into a lively account of their evening at the theater, where Arthur had been smitten by the charms of Miss Fotheringay, whose real name was Costigan.
