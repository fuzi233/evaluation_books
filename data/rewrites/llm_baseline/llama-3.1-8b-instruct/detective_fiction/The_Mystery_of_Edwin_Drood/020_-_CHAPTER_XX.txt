A single thought dominated her scattered thoughts: she must escape from this unsettling man. Yet, where could she turn, and how could she flee? The fear she harbored for him had only been shared with Helena, and speaking out about it now might inadvertently unleash the very calamity he had hinted at.

The weight of responsibility bore down on her, as the memory of his words and demeanor fueled her growing anxiety. Her mind had been a maelstrom of conflicting emotions and half-formed suspicions for the past six months. A nagging, unspoken doubt would periodically surface, only to be pushed back down into the depths of her psyche. Jasper's obsessive investigation into his nephew's disappearance, and his own self-absorption in the mystery, had created an atmosphere of tension and speculation in the community. No one suspected Jasper of foul play, and Rosa found herself wondering if her own darker thoughts were a product of her earlier aversion to him. Wasn't that, in itself, a sign of the baselessness of her suspicions?

Rosa wrestled with the questions that had plagued her for weeks. What possible motive could Jasper have for harming his nephew? The thought of attributing such a heinous act to his desire for her was almost too distressing to contemplate. She shied away from the notion, as if the mere consideration of it was a crime in itself. Replaying their conversation by the sundial in the garden, she couldn't shake the feeling that Jasper's words were calculated to manipulate her. He had consistently portrayed the disappearance as a murder, aligning with his public persona since the discovery of the watch and shirt-pin. Wouldn't a man fearful of being caught try to deflect attention from the crime? His declaration that he might have taken his nephew away, had their bond been weaker, struck her as a calculated move to sow seeds of doubt. By presenting his six months of efforts as a justifiable act of vengeance, Jasper aimed to win her trust. Could such a violent passion be genuine, or was it merely a ruse?
