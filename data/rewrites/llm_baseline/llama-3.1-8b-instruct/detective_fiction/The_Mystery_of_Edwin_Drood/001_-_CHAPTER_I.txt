The morning light crept through the grimy window, casting a faint glow on the squalid room. A lone figure, clad in worn clothing, slowly sat up, rubbing his temples in an attempt to shake off the haze of sleep. He lay on a rickety bed, its wooden slats splintered and worn, with a makeshift bedframe that seemed to have given way beneath his weight. Across the bed, three others slept fitfully – a Chinese man, a Lascar, and a gaunt woman. She was the only one awake, blowing softly into a crude pipe, attempting to coax a spark from its depths. As she shaded the flame with her bony hand, it cast an eerie light on the scene.

"Another?" she rasped, her voice like the rustling of dry leaves.

He looked around, his hand pressed against his forehead, trying to clear the cobwebs from his mind. "You've given me more than enough already," he muttered, his words barely audible.

The woman continued to blow on the pipe, her eyes fixed on him. "Five since you arrived last night," she said, her tone laced with a whining complaint. "Business has been slow, dearie. Fewer ships dock, fewer sailors to sell to. My poor head aches something fierce. You'll pay the going rate, won't you, dearie?"

As she spoke, she handed him the pipe, and he hesitated before taking it, his hand shaking slightly. He looked at the woman, her face illuminated by the flickering flame, and saw a disturbing resemblance to the Chinese man beside her. The same sharp cheekbones, the same dark eyes, the same ashen complexion. The Lascar stirred, his limbs twitching in a restless sleep.

The woman's gaze drifted to him, her eyes glinting with a mixture of calculation and desperation. "Remember, dearie, the market price is high right now. Three shillings and sixpence for a thimbleful. Nobody but me knows the secret to mixing it, and I'm the only one who can make it just right. You'll pay up, won't you, dearie?" Her voice trailed off, lost in the haze of smoke and opium, as she sank back onto the bed, her eyes closing in a mixture of exhaustion and longing.
