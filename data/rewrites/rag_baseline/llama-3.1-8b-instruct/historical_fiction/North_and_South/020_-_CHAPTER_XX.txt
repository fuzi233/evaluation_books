As the mist-shrouded dawn broke over Milton, casting an ethereal glow over the crumbling facades of the town, Margaret Hale stepped out into the chill morning air, her footsteps echoing off the cobblestones. The weight of her encounter with Higgins's family still lingered, a heavy burden she carried within her, refusing to be shaken off. Her mother, sensing the turmoil brewing inside her, awaited Margaret's return with a mixture of trepidation and expectation, for she knew that her daughter's walks often brought back tales of the downtrodden and the struggling, stories that stirred something deep within Margaret's compassionate heart.

"And can your factory friend come on Thursday to see you dressed?" her mother asked, as Margaret entered the parlor, her eyes red-rimmed from the dampness outside.

"She was so ill, I never thought of asking her," Margaret replied, her voice tinged with a sense of responsibility, as if the fate of the entire town rested on her shoulders.

"Dear, everybody is ill now, I think," Mrs. Hale said, her voice laced with a hint of jealousy, a sentiment born of her own confinement within these four walls. "But it must be very sad to be ill in one of those little back streets." The old habits of thought, forged in the quiet lanes of Helstone, resurfaced, and her kindly nature took hold. "It's bad enough here. What could you do for her, Margaret? Mr. Thornton has sent me some of his old port wine since you went out. Would a bottle of that do her good, think you?"

Margaret hesitated, her mind torn between the desire to ease Bessy's suffering and the awareness that such gestures might only prolong the strike's futility. "No, mamma, I don't believe they are very poor—at least, they don't speak as if they were; and, at any rate, Bessy's illness is consumption—she won't want wine. Perhaps, I might take her a little preserve, made of our dear Helstone fruit. No! there's another family to whom I should like to give—Oh, mamma, mamma! how am I to dress up in my finery, and go off and away to smart parties, after the sorrow I've seen today?"
