As Gerard knelt beside the still form of his beloved wife, a wave of numbness washed over him, a numbness born not from grief, but from ritual. The last rites, once a familiar comfort, now felt mechanical, a hollow echo of the countless times he'd performed them for others. His mind reeled, struggling to grasp the magnitude of his loss, as if the weight of her passing had somehow shattered something within him.

In a daze, he rose to his feet, his eyes fixed on the wooden box that held her, his fingers tracing the intricate carvings that adorned its surface. The cart, hired for the evening, waited outside, its single horse patiently bearing the burden of her coffin. Gerard's thoughts were consumed by the desire to lay her to rest in the churchyard, surrounded by the familiar stones and the silence of the night.

As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting the world in a somber hue, Gerard climbed into the cart, his arms wrapped tightly around the coffin. The man who had hired the cart walked alongside, his footsteps quiet on the deserted road, speaking only in hushed tones to avoid disturbing the mournful silence. When they stopped for the night, Gerard refused to be comforted, clinging to the cold wood as if it were a lifeline.

The landlady, moved by his anguish, attempted to coax him into some rest, but Gerard's grief had become a living, breathing entity, a creature that would not be tamed. As the night wore on, the darkness outside seemed to deepen, mirroring the void within him. It was not until the sound of voices, a chorus of sympathetic wails, pierced the stillness that Gerard's reverie was shattered. The cart had arrived at the manse, and the community had gathered to pay their respects.

Their faces, etched with concern and sorrow, swam before him like a sea of unending tears. Gerard's voice, raw and cracked, struggled to find its way out, and when it finally emerged, it was a mere whisper, a desperate plea to the people he had come to love. "My children...my children..." The words caught in his throat, and all he could manage was a sob, a sound that shook his very being.
