As the afternoon sun cast its warm glow through the windows of the Esmond estate, George Warrington sat quietly in the parlor, puffing on his pipe. His thoughts drifted to the stories of his grandfather, Colonel Esmond, who had spent his later years in America, chronicling the tales of his life. Those memoirs held a special place in George's heart, for they revealed the life of Madame de Bernstein, a woman whose spirit and beauty had captivated him even in her later years.

As he sat lost in contemplation, the sound of his aunt's cane tapping against the floor signaled her arrival from a social gathering. She entered the room, her eyes sparkling behind a veil of rouge and diamonds. George couldn't help but think back to the vibrant days of her youth, when her presence had been a beacon of light and joy. He remembered the tales of her early life, of the firework displays at Williamsburg on the King's birthday, where the explosion of color and light had left an indelible mark on his imagination.

His aunt, Mrs. Beatrice Esmond, took a seat beside him, her eyes scanning the room as she spoke, "It seems, George, that there have been some... misunderstandings between you and my sister since your return." Her words hung in the air, leaving George to ponder what exactly she meant. He chose not to pry, letting the silence between them speak for itself.

Mrs. Esmond's gaze turned to the small desk in the corner, where a letter lay beneath the inkwell. She instructed her attendant to retrieve it, and George recognized the handwriting as that of his mother, Madam Esmond. The letter had arrived weeks ago, carried by George himself, but he had yet to share its contents with anyone.

With a hint of curiosity, Mrs. Esmond handed the letter to George, her eyes sparkling with a mix of warmth and concern. As he began to read, the words on the page brought to life the story of his family's struggles and joys.
