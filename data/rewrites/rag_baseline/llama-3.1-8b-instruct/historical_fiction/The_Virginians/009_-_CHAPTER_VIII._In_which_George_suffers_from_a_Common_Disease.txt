The morning sun cast a pale glow through the windows of Castlewood, illuminating the dust motes dancing in the air. George Esmond's thoughts lingered in the darkness of his bedchamber, weighed down by the fatigue of a restless night. His mother, Madam Esmond, had prepared for a grand celebration, but the weight of her efforts hung heavy in the stillness. Outside, the sounds of the household began to stir – the clinking of dishes, the murmur of hushed conversations, and the soft rustling of silk dresses.

As George dressed, his mind wandered to the impending departure of his friend, Mr. Washington. He had heard the praise of Mr. Washington from his mother, and the way she spoke of him made him feel both envious and uncertain. He had met Mr. Washington only briefly, yet the man's presence seemed to carry an air of gravity, a weight that George couldn't quite grasp. As he powdered his hair and adjusted his attire, George felt a pang of anxiety, wondering what lay ahead for him and his friend.

The sound of footsteps outside his door broke the spell, and George opened it to find his mother standing in the hallway, her eyes fixed on him with a mixture of concern and expectation. She was resplendent in a magnificent dress, one that seemed to shimmer in the morning light. Her gaze fell upon George, and a faint smile played on her lips. "George, dear, you look lovely," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "Your father would be proud."

George's heart swelled with a mix of emotions as he gazed at his mother. He knew that his father's legacy was a complex one, a web of expectations and obligations that weighed heavily on their family. He also sensed that his mother's words were laced with a hint of sadness, a reminder of the life they lived and the choices they had made.

As they made their way downstairs, George caught sight of Mr. Washington, his tall figure standing by the window, gazing out at the garden. There was something about the man that commanded respect, a quiet authority that seemed to emanate from within. George felt a sense of trepidation as he approached him, unsure of how to navigate the complexities of adulthood and the expectations that came with it.
