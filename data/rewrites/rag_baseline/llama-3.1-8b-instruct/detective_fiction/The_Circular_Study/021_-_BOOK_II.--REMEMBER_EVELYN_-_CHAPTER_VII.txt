In the flickering candlelight of the study, Lady Violet Lebas's slender fingers danced across the pages of a yellowed book, her eyes scanning the lines with an intensity that belied the tranquility of the summer evening outside. The soft patter of rain against the panes was a soothing melody, a reminder that even in the midst of turmoil, the world outside remained stubbornly indifferent. Her thoughts, however, were consumed by the tumultuous events of the past few days, the echoes of which still lingered in the air like the scent of damp wool and beeswax that clung to her clothes.

Just then, the door creaked open, and Mr. Gryce stepped inside, his eyes scanning the room as if searching for something – or someone. His gaze landed on Lady Violet, and a hint of a smile played on his lips. He approached her, his long strides eating up the distance between them.

"Ah, Lady Violet, I see you're still pondering the events of the past few days," he said, his voice low and measured. "I've been pondering them myself, trying to make sense of it all."

Lady Violet looked up, her eyes narrowing slightly as she took in the concern etched on Mr. Gryce's face. "I'm sure you've been doing your best to untangle the threads, Mr. Gryce," she said, her voice husky from disuse. "But I fear I've been of little help in your efforts."

Mr. Gryce chuckled, his eyes crinkling at the corners. "Not at all, my dear lady. Your insight has been invaluable. I've been speaking with Miss Butterworth, and I believe we've finally unraveled the mystery of the Adams affair."

Lady Violet's gaze sharpened, her interest piqued. "And what did you learn?" she asked, her fingers tightening around the book.

"It seems Mrs. Adams's story has been vindicated," Mr. Gryce replied, his expression serious. "The evidence points to self-defense, and it's likely she'll be cleared of any wrongdoing."

Lady Violet's face relaxed, a look of relief washing over her features. "I'm glad to hear it," she said, her voice barely above a whisper.
