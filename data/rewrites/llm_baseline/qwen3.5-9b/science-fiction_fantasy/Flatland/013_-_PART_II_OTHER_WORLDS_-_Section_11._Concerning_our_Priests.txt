It is finally time to leave behind these preliminary sketches of Flatland's geography and physics to address the true purpose of this journey: my initiation into the fourth dimension. Everything written thus far serves only as a prologue to this revelation. Consequently, I must omit countless fascinating details regarding our mode of locomotion without limbs, our methods of construction without hands, the meteorological patterns of our zones, the biology of our flora, and the mechanics of our script. These omissions are not due to oversight, but rather a respect for your limited attention span. However, before unveiling the secrets of Space, there is one final pillar of our society that demands discussion: the Controllers of Destiny, the Objects of Universal Adoration—our Circles, or Priests.

Do not mistake the title "Priest" for its common usage in your world. In Flatland, a Circle is the supreme administrator of every conceivable domain: commerce, engineering, education, statecraft, morality, and theology. They are the invisible causes behind every visible effect; they direct all action while performing none themselves. While the uneducated masses assume a Circle is a perfect geometric curve, the enlightened know the truth: a Circle is merely a polygon with an immense number of infinitesimal sides. As the count of sides grows, the shape approaches perfection. A figure with three hundred sides is nearly indistinguishable from a true circle to the human eye.

Yet, there is a profound taboo associated with this form. Among the elite, tactile recognition is forbidden. To physically trace the perimeter of a superior being is considered the height of insolence. This cultural aversion to touch allows the upper classes to maintain the mystique of their status. By avoiding contact, they preserve the illusion of their infinite smoothness. Mathematically, if a standard Circle averages three feet in circumference, a figure with three hundred sides possesses edges barely one-tenth of an inch long. Those with six or seven hundred sides have edges smaller than the head of a pin from Spaceland. Tradition dictates that the High Priest currently reigning possesses ten thousand sides—a number so vast it effectively renders him a true sphere in the eyes of the law.

Unlike the rigid caste system governing triangles and squares, where social mobility is strictly dictated by generational inheritance (gaining exactly one side per birth), the path to becoming a Circle is fluid and perilous. Nature imposes two conflicting laws on this evolution: as a lineage ascends socially, its rate of development accelerates, but its fertility plummets. In the homes of polygons nearing circularity, sons are rarely born; twins are unheard of. Conversely, the offspring of a near-perfect polygon may inherit hundreds of additional sides instantly.

To bridge the gap between a complex polygon and a Circle, art intervenes through a brutal medical practice. Physicians have discovered that the fragile edges of a high-class infant can be surgically fractured and reset with surgical precision. This procedure, known as Neo-Therapy, carries a staggering mortality rate. Roughly nine out of ten children perish during the operation. Nevertheless, driven by an obsessive desire for nobility, parents of borderline status rush their infants to the Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium within their first month of life. The entire ordeal lasts just one year. Most often, the child returns to the family crypt, another nameless entry on the crowded cemetery stones. But occasionally, against all odds, a procession emerges carrying a survivor—not a polygon, but a Circle. This singular miracle validates the sacrifice, prompting countless other families to gamble their children's lives on the slim chance of achieving divine geometry.
