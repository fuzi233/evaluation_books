As I descended into the afternoon's golden light, the scent of baking bread wafted from the kitchen, mingling with the damp earthy smell of the river. Eli was within, his hands moving deftly as he prepared the evening meal. His eldest and youngest sons had ventured out into the world, searching for signs of Gerard's return, while Catherine and her daughter, Kate, had walked a short distance down the road, hoping to catch a glimpse of him. I remained in the sitting-room, a space both humble and dignified, its walls adorned with the simple beauty of Dutch tiles that shone like polished gemstones. The room was a testament to the ingenuity of the Hollander people, who had pioneered the art of cleanliness, and their love of order.

The small, lozenge-shaped windows allowed slivers of sunlight to dance across the floor, casting intricate patterns on the tiles. A massive oak table stood at the room's center, its surface worn smooth by generations of use. On one side, a copper pail held holy water, a reminder of the family's deep faith. Nearby, a sturdy dwarf, Giles, clung to the table's edge, his eyes gleaming with a fierce intensity as he prepared to launch himself into another acrobatic feat. His unusual form, the result of a haphazard creation, allowed him to defy gravity, swinging from the table's rim with an ease that belied his awkward appearance.

Kate entered the room, her eyes fixed on Giles in dismay. "Oh, Giles! How can you?" she exclaimed, her voice tinged with concern. "Mother is nearby. You'll dent the table." Giles snarled, his face twisted in a scowl, but Kate's calm demeanor remained unshaken. "Am I?" she inquired dryly. "That's news to me." Giles's growl only grew louder, but Kate merely raised an eyebrow, her expression a mask of innocence. This unexpected exchange sparked a burst of humor, and Kate sat down, her eyes welling up with tears. Her mother entered the room just then, her sharp gaze sweeping the space, taking in Giles's position beneath the table and his sheepish expression. "What's amiss now?" she asked, her voice firm but laced with a hint of amusement.
