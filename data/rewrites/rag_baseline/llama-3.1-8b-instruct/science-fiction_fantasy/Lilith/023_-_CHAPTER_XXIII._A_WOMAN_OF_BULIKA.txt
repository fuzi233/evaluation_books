A Woman of Bulika

As the evening sun cast its golden glow upon the dusty streets of Bulika, I ducked into a narrow alley, seeking refuge from the blistering heat. The scent of sizzling spices wafted through the air, mingling with the acrid tang of fresh bread baking in the nearby ovens. My stomach growled in anticipation, reminding me of the meager meal I had yet to find. In the mouth of the alley, I stopped, and gazed out at the moonlight casting a silver glow over the cobblestones.

Just then, a woman emerged from the shadows, her dark hair tied back in a neat knot, her eyes darting nervously about the alley. A few seconds passed, and a majestic leopard, its coat marked with the intricate patterns of a prized breed, darted across the archway. The woman pressed close to me, her breath catching in her throat.

"If the beast comes near," I said, wrapping my arm around her shoulders, "I'll take care of it, and you must run."

She nodded, her eyes welling up with tears. "Thank you," she whispered.

"Have you seen this leopard before?" I asked, my hand instinctively reaching for the pouch of dried herbs at my waist.

"Yes, several times," she replied, her voice trembling. "It's the princess's pet. You're a stranger, or you'd know better."

"I'm a traveler," I said, smiling wryly. "But tell me, is it common for the leopard to roam free?"

"It's supposed to be caged, its mouth muzzled, and its paws bound with crocodile leather. But it often escapes, and seeks out the blood of any child it can find. Luckily, there aren't many mothers in Bulika to worry about."

She broke into sobs, and I guided her gently to a nearby bench, where we sat together in the moonlight. As she composed herself, I asked her about the city's customs.

"We don't believe in working," she said, her voice still shaking. "Our people are rich because our ancestors saved for us, and we've never spent a coin. When we need money, we sell a few of our precious gems."

"But what about the poor?" I asked, intrigued by the complexity of their social hierarchy.
