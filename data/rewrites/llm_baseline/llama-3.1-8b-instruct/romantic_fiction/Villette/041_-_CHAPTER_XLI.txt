As the Feast of the Assumption cast its serene silence over the convent, I found myself alone in the deserted first classe, surrounded by the tools and half-finished projects of the joiners' workshop. The morning's mass had been attended, and the boarders and teachers had set off on a leisurely walk into the countryside, their destination a farm-house where they would partake of a midday repast. I lingered behind, reluctant to let slip the last two days of my time here, as precious as a lifeline clinging to a fragile raft.

The afternoon sun cast a warm glow through the windows, illuminating the dust motes dancing in the air, but I felt listless, unable to muster the energy to leave my post and take a stroll in the garden. My thoughts were consumed by the uncertainty that had been gnawing at me since the previous night's encounter with Freedom and Renovation. Their boasts of strength and liberation now seemed hollow, and I couldn't shake the feeling that I had been too hasty in my judgment.

Just as I was lost in thought, the sound of footsteps echoed through the empty corridors, growing louder with each passing moment. I glanced up to see a lone workman making his way towards me, his shoes clicking on the stone floor in a rhythmic cadence. In Villette, artisans and servants always worked in pairs, and I wondered what could have brought this single individual to our doorstep. As he entered, I caught sight of him, and my heart skipped a beat – it was M. Paul, dressed in a surout and velvet-trimmed coat, exuding an air of purpose and cheerfulness.

My initial reaction was one of trepidation, but as our eyes met, I felt a sense of calm wash over me. His kind and benevolent expression put me at ease, and I couldn't help but notice the warmth in his eyes. Could it be that he had come to bid me farewell, to reassure me before his departure? The thought sent a pang of longing through me, and I knew I had to push aside my doubts and insecurities. This might be my last chance to speak with him, and I wouldn't let Jealousy's dark whispers spoil the moment. I smiled, and as our gazes locked, I felt a deep connection, a sense of understanding that transcended words.
