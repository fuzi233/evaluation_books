The fourth night, I slipped into what felt like sleep—only to wake into true awakening. My eyes opened upon absolute darkness, yet I knew with certainty: I lay in the house of death. Every moment since my arrival had been a dream, and now, at last, I was *alive*.

*"Finally,"* my heart whispered, and it surged with joy.

I turned—and there stood Lona, waiting for me. I had never lost her, only the sight of her. She needed no mourning. Though the room was black, *she* was not. Light shimmered from her eyes, radiant as the Mother’s own, and her face glowed with the same unearthly luminescence. Her death-garment, now luminous with resurrection, gleamed like fresh snow under the sun. The girl who had fallen asleep had awakened a woman, brimming with the essence of life itself.

I pulled her into my arms. *This* was reality.

*"I woke first,"* she murmured, smiling as if the thought still astonished her.

*"You did,"* I said. *"And woke me with you."*

*"I only watched you,"* she replied, *"and waited."*

A candle floated toward us through the dark, and moments later, Adam, Eve, and Mara joined us. Their greeting was serene, as though such awakenings were ordinary.

*"I trust the darkness treated you kindly?"* Eve asked.

*"Not entirely,"* I admitted. *"But the waking makes it worth every shadow."*

*"This is only the beginning,"* she said. *"You are barely roused."*

Adam’s voice was warm. *"He is clothed in Death—which is but Life’s brightest garment."* He embraced Lona, clasped my shoulder, studied the slumbering princess, and gave the leopardess an affectionate pat. *"I expect we’ll meet you both again soon."*

*"Must we die once more?"* I asked.

*"No,"* he said, echoing Eve’s smile. *"You’ve died into life. Now you need only *live*—fully, fiercely. The more you live, the stronger you become."*

*"The will alone summons the strength,"* Eve said. *"Pure life knows no weariness."*

Mara kissed my brow. *"Did I not promise all would be well? Tell others this: *What will be well, is already well.*"* She sighed—not in sorrow, but as though she knew few would believe it. *"Now you know me,"* she finished, her smile luminous.

*"I do,"* I said. *"You are the voice that cried in the wilderness before the dawn. The shepherd who drives the lost sheep home before night falls."*

*"One day, my work will end,"* she said softly. *"Then I shall rejoice with the great Shepherd who sent me."*

Adam’s voice cut through like dawn. *"All night long, the morning lingers near."*

A sound like beating wings shuddered through the air.

*"The Shadow stirs,"* Adam said. *"He claims one here as his own—but what is ours can never be his again."*

I turned toward my parents’ resting places—only to find their couches empty, claimed instead by the Little Ones. A pang of that old, desolate dream struck me, and I flinched.

Lona touched my arm. *"What troubles you?"*

*"Their absence,"* I whispered.

*"They left long ago,"* Adam said. *"They kissed you as they departed and whispered, *‘Come soon.’*"*

*"How could you, lost in that hollow house of yours?"* He turned to Lona. *"Your parents, child, will come for *you.*"*

The time had come. Lona pressed a kiss to her murderous mother’s brow, then embraced her father.

*"That kiss will call her home,"* Adam said.

*"Who were her parents?"* Lona asked.

*"My father is hers as well,"* Adam answered.

We knelt then, thanking them—for death, for life. Their breath swept over us like a blessing.

A sound like thunderous wings filled the air.

*"The sun comes,"* Adam said.

And I heard it—the roar of a star, the crescendo of creation itself. The three exchanged a glance, and their smiles became a three-petaled flower of light, drifting upward in silent praise.

Then—they *changed.*

Adam and Eve stood before me as angels of resurrection, Mara as the Magdalene at the empty tomb. Adam’s face burned like lightning; Eve’s napkin scattered brilliance like sparks. A wind rose, pulsing like a dying beast’s breath.

*"You hear *his* wings now,"* Adam said—not the sun’s, but the Shadow’s. *"He is restless, for he carries his own torment within."*

*"Is there nothing deeper in him?"* I asked.

*"A shadow cannot exist without substance,"* Adam said. *"Nor substance without light behind it."*

Then—a sound. Distant, golden, triumphant.

*"The cock crows!"* Adam cried. *"After eons of silence, he *sings!*"*

And I heard it—the defiance of death, the herald of dawn. The chaos yearning for kingdom.

A great flapping echoed.

*"The black bat flees,"* Mara said.

*"Amen, golden cock!"* Adam shouted, and his voice shook the house.

At that word, the Little Ones sprang upright, blinking. *"Crow! Crow again!"* they cried, as if they’d seen the bird in dreams. They turned to their sleeping companions, kissed them tenderly, then bounded from their beds—only to freeze at the sight of the princess, still slumbering.

Odu ran to the leopardess, stroking her head. *"Wake up!"* he pleaded. When she didn’t stir, his face crumpled. *"She’s cold!"*

*"She waits for the princess,"* Mara said gently.

Odu touched the princess’s cheek, then recoiled. *"She’s cold too! Why won’t they wake?"*

Adam knelt beside him. *"Her waking isn’t ripe. She must forget enough to remember. Then—she’ll rise."*

*"But the cock has *crowed!*"* Odu protested, shaking his friends. *"Peter! Crispy! Wake up!*" When they didn’t stir, tears spilled down his face.

*"Let them sleep,"* Adam said. *"Would you leave the princess alone when she wakes? They are happy. So is the leopardess."*

Odu wiped his eyes—a gesture both new and ancient.

Eve led us out. No farewells were spoken. The horse and elephants waited, but we walked, too buoyant to ride. They followed, and the world stretched before us, alive with dawn.
