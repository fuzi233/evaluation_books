As I stood amidst the desolate silence, news arrived like a cold gust of wind that Margaret Van Eyck had departed this life, leaving behind a legacy that would forever alter the tapestry of our lives. By her will, penned a year prior, she had bequeathed her entire estate to her beloved daughter, Margaret Brandt, stipulating that she provide for Reicht Heynes until her marriage. This bequest included a grand, furnished house, replete with precious paintings and sketches that would have been a treasure trove in the modern era. Among these masterpieces, one stood out – a poignant depiction of "A Betrothal," crafted by the skilled hands of Margaret Van Eyck, with Jan contributing the remaining elements. The artwork's beauty was matched only by its significance, as it captured the solemnity of the ceremony, the gravity of the man, and the demure composure of the woman.

Margaret Brandt dispatched a message to Reicht, instructing him to remain in the house until she felt prepared to return, so that he might cherish the memories of the face and voice that once welcomed him there, and take especial care of the treasured diptych. Her words were a testament to the bond she shared with her mother, and the enduring legacy she sought to preserve.

Meanwhile, Luke Peterson's return brought with it a mixture of joy and chaos. Upon learning that Gerard had taken his vows, Luke's enthusiasm knew no bounds. He hastened to Margaret, proclaiming, "Mistress, heed me! If he cannot marry you, I can!" But Margaret's response was laced with a deep-seated sorrow. "You?" she asked, her voice tinged with a hint of resignation. "Why, I have seen him." The weight of her words hung in the air like the scent of old parchment, as Luke struggled to comprehend the complexities of her heart.

Undeterred, Luke proposed seeking the counsel of his cousin, a learned lawyer, to determine the validity of their marriage. But Margaret rejected this suggestion, choosing instead to rely on the whispers of her own heart. "I shall ask my own heart, not a lawyer," she declared, her voice firm but laced with a hint of vulnerability. "So that is your regard for me; to go making me the town talk, oh, fie!"
