Mother and Daughter

Lona's distaste for the people and, particularly, the women, grew with each passing moment. She longed to depart the place as swiftly as possible, fearing the longer they lingered, the more perilous their situation became. Conversely, I felt a deep conviction that abandoning our mission would be a grave mistake, one that would not only thwart our chances of success but also undermine the resolve of the Little Ones. The prophecy hung precariously in the balance, and the prospect of facing the princess, who would surely not hesitate to attack us, weighed heavily on my mind. If we were to encounter her, Lona's presence was our greatest asset – her beauty might captivate the princess, rendering her less hostile. Alternatively, if the princess resorted to violence, I would stand firm between them, willing to defend Lona at all costs. I knew that the princess was doomed, and that our presence might precipitate her downfall.

Undeterred by the uncertainty surrounding the princess's connection to the prophecy, I outlined our plan to Lona, and to my surprise, she agreed to accompany me to the palace. Our mission was not without precedent – the princess had witnessed the approaching army of the Little Ones from the palace tower, and her subsequent terror was palpable. She had failed to vanquish them, and now, with the prophecy looming large, she was consumed by desperation.

As we made our way to the palace, the city slumbered, oblivious to the turmoil brewing within its walls. We rode our majestic horses, while the Little Ones' smaller steeds and elephants accompanied us. The air was heavy with anticipation, and I couldn't help but wonder what lay ahead.

Upon arriving at the palace, we discovered the gate ajar, and we passed through the tranquil paved grove to reach the entrance. In the vestibule, we found the spotted leopardess, seemingly lifeless or asleep, in her cage. The Little Ones paused, awaiting our cue, as we prepared to face whatever lay within.

As we entered the palace, the darkness enveloped us, punctuated only by the faint scent of incense wafting through the halls. The air was thick with the weight of history and mystery, and I couldn't shake the feeling that we were walking into a realm both ancient and forgotten. Our footsteps echoed through the corridors, a reminder that we were not alone in this foreboding place.
