The wells of Obak lay shrouded in an eerie silence, an unforgiving expanse of shifting sand that stretched towards the horizon like an endless canvas of beige and gold. The sun beat down relentlessly, its fiery touch igniting the air with an electric heat that seared the skin and parched the throat. Yet, amidst this desolate backdrop, a lone figure emerged each morning, driven by the unyielding rhythms of his routine. Abou Fatma, a man shrouded in mystery, would venture forth from his hidden lair, accompanied by two majestic camels of the pure Bisharin breed. As the sun climbed higher, he'd lead them to the well-mouth, where he'd water them with a practiced hand, and sit in contemplative silence for the better part of three hours.

The air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and the acrid tang of creosote, a smell that clung to the nostrils like a wet shroud. Abou Fatma breathed it in deeply, his eyes scanning the horizon for any sign of movement. Five mornings had passed since his secret sojourn began, and the stillness had been almost palpable, a weight that pressed upon his shoulders like a physical force. But on this sixth morning, as he prepared to depart, a sudden commotion shattered the tranquility. A donkey and its driver materialized on the crest of the dunes, their figures stark against the brilliant blue of the sky.

Abou Fatma's gaze locked onto the donkey, his eyes narrowing as he took in its grey coat. He rose to his feet, his movements economical and deliberate, and turned to regard the driver. The man's jellab was pulled forward, shielding his face from the sun, but his legs, a deep ebony, stood out starkly against the golden sand. A negro, and one who seemed out of place in this desolate landscape. Abou Fatma's expression remained impassive, his eyes never leaving the stranger as he waited for him to approach.

The negro laid down his spear, shield, and shorter spear, and sat beside Abou Fatma with an air of quiet confidence. "Salam aleikum," he said, his voice low and smooth, like the gentle lapping of the Nile against its banks. Abou Fatma returned the greeting, his voice a low rumble, and waited.

"Aleikum es salam," he replied, his eyes never leaving the stranger's face.
