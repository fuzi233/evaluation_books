From our vantage point just below the crest of the Sacred Mountain, the city sprawled beneath us like a detailed map etched in stone and shadow. Far below, the harbor and its vast estuary shimmered, while the distant hills smoked and fumed in their eternal haze. Dominating the center of the urban sprawl stood the great stone circle of our Lord the Sun, grim and bare against the sky. Closer still rose the massive royal pyramid, its golden facets drinking in the sun's rays to reflect a dazzling brilliance. Before this towering monument, in the central square, sat the throne of granite—a structure so dwarfed by the distance it resembled nothing more than a molehill. It was there, within that silent seat, that my love had slept for nine long years.

Old Zaemon followed my gaze, his expression heavy. "Ay," he sighed, "I know exactly where your heart lies. When Deucalion first arrived from Yucatan, he was a man of iron. The king we have chosen—the finest among us—possesses a certain fragility."

"It can be forged into greater strength," I replied, my voice steady despite the turmoil in my chest. "Give me Nais, living and warm, ready to fight, and I shall be a warrior far superior to the cold viceroy you describe."

"I have already pledged my word; you shall have her. Yet, this victory will exact a toll from your new kingdom, perhaps even costing us the life of Phorenice and her brood. However, I doubt it. She is too cunning. Once we initiate our plan, she will likely deduce our intent and her role in it, fleeing before harm can strike. The High Gods, who ordained these trials for our refinement, have granted her a peculiar insight: she knows how to trigger these earth tremors."

"I have witnessed her manipulate them," I admitted. "Pray, tell me your scheme."

"The details will unfold in due course. For now, I bid you rest. Tonight, your task is to descend into the city, claim your woman—that is my daughter—as soon as she is freed, and bring her back to us. You will require the strength of a titan for this feat." He stepped back, scrutinizing me from head to toe. "Few would mistake you for the tidy, clean-chinned Deucalion anymore, my brother. Your current appearance serves as excellent camouflage for tonight's descent when we awaken the city with our seismic terror. As you stand now, you are sufficiently hairy, shaggy, naked, and filthy to pass as a wild savage newly arrived from Europe. Be wary, however, that no refined citizen mistakes your powerful frame for servitude and attempts to enslave you."

"I pity the fool who tries," I retorted.

Zaemon laughed, a rare sound that cut through the tension. "Indeed, I pity him as well." But the laughter vanished instantly, replaced by solemn gravity. Playfulness was a stranger to Zaemon. "Go to your quarters, my King. Leave me to enter the Ark of Mysteries and prepare alongside another of the Three for the tasks ahead. Handling the tremendous powers we must unleash tonight is no light burden. Danger awaits both of us. If we do not meet again until we stand behind the stars to render account to the Gods, then farewell, Deucalion."

That day, I slept as a soldier sleeps—taking full, uninterrupted rest, allowing no anxious thought to fracture my dreams. Weaklings allow their sleep to be haunted; warriors do not. When the darkness fully descended, I roused myself and gathered those tasked with managing the ropes. The moon offered no illumination that night; instead, the air was choked with a profound, suffocating blackness. Thus, I remained invisible as they lowered me toward the earth.

I reached the tumbled rocks at the base of the cliff and proceeded with extreme caution. Phorenice's soldiers huddled around their campfires nearby, their postures suggesting a premonition of impending doom. I had no desire to heighten their alertness. Creeping swiftly through the deepest shadows, I finally arrived at the termination of the passage I had previously used to slip beneath the city walls.

The lamp hung in place, guiding my path through the winding tunnels. The air here tasted even more foul than before, thick with noxious vapors, leading me to conclude that Zaemon had already initiated the internal mechanisms destined to convulse the city. Yet, finding an exit proved difficult. This was not due to crowds blocking the way, but rather because the architecture itself had shifted. In ages past, the Priestly Clan dictated all architectural plans, ensuring no structures clashed with their secret sanctuaries. Now, however, the priests held little sway, and architects, prone to error, had sealed countless passages and hiding spots, rendering them useless.

Forced to navigate the labyrinth wherever the paths led rather than where I wished, I emerged on the far side of the city, nearly adjacent to where the outer wall met the harbor. I exited unseen, scrupulously erasing all traces of my departure. Beneath my feet, the earth felt less like solid ground and more like the deck of a ship rolling gently on calm waters. While no visible tremor yet disturbed the populace—who continued to sleep undisturbed—I could sense that the solid deadness of the world had lifted; any moment now, the ground might erupt into devastating waves of motion.

*Gods!* Was I arriving too late to witness the unsealing of my beloved? Would she be exposed to the prying, cruel eyes of the public as terrified citizens flooded the open spaces? In my imagination, I saw rough, brutal hands thrusting upon her helpless form, and my very soul seemed to dry up at the thought.

Driven by a frantic urgency, I raced down the narrow, twisting streets, fixated solely on reaching the square before the royal pyramid before disaster struck. With exquisite cruelty, I had been compelled to inter her alive beneath the granite throne. If my strength and speed allowed, I would retrieve her with the same fierce determination.

Few interrupted my furious sprint. Here and there, a beggar lurking in the gutters shouted, "Thief! Throw a coin or I'll pursue!" But if anyone chased, I was already gone. My speed outpaced all pursuers. Soon, as the earth's swing beneath my feet intensified and the building facades began to groan, grind, and spit showers of dust, panicked screams erupted from doorways. Yet, none stopped to hinder the ragged, shaggy savage trampling through the mud, his naked feet kicking up dirt.

Eventually, I burst into the great square. It was filling with a chaotic tide of people fleeing the narrow streets, a scene of darkness, noise, dust, and sickness. Beneath us, the ground rippled like a restless sea, the undulations growing increasingly terrifying. Houses collapsed unseen in the gloom, adding to the cacophony.

However, the great pyramid was designed by ancient masters to withstand such shocks. Its stones were dovetailed with marvelous precision and reinforced by massive metal clamps. It was boasted that half its foundation could be excavated, and it would still stand firm under heaven, enduring longer than the hills themselves.

Flickering torches revealed the great stone doors wide open. Frightened inhabitants scrambled out only to vanish into the gloom. But the pyramid's fate mattered little to me now—not whether Phorenice was trapped inside or escaped to wreak further havoc. I crouched beside the granite throne in the center of the square, hearing its stones grate together like snapping bones as it rocked violently.

In that night of dust and darkness, visibility was nil, yet I suspect the Gods, in recompense for the agony of my long-loved love, bestowed upon me a special clarity of sight. Watching intently, I saw the great carved rock forming the capstone shift slightly, then again, then again—a tiny jerk for each pulse of the earth, yet a significant displacement. Moreover, the stone consistently moved in one direction.

There was method to Zaemon's desperate gambit, something I had missed in my blind panic. Seizing the opportunity, I climbed the steps on the side where the capstone was sliding and clung there, burning with anticipation.

The earth-swings grew more violent, though the increments were imperceptible. The din of collapsing buildings, the shrieks of the wounded, and the cries of the terrified rose higher into the night. Dust thickened the air until one could barely breathe, blotting out the rare stars as the night turned pitch black. Clinging to an angle of the granite throne, crouched on the second-to-top step, I struggled to maintain my footing against the violence.

Yet, the huge capstone, carved with the snake and the outstretched hand, still held my love locked in her living tomb. I wanted to bite the cold granite in frustration at the barrier preventing me from reaching her. The crowd thronging the square was mad with terror, their numbers making my situation more desperate by the minute. "Phorenice, Goddess, aid us now!" some cried. When the prayer brought no relief, they fell to reciting the old confessions of faith they had learned in childhood, turning in their hour of dread to the gods they had spurned for decades. It was a curious commentary on their true beliefs.

Loudly, the crash of falling masonry increased. Even from the royal pyramid itself, though obscured by darkness, came sounds of grinding stone and cracking metal, signaling that even its supreme majesty was reaching its breaking point.
