That evening, I sat in my modest lodgings, lost in thought, when a knock at the door broke the silence. It was Mr. Bruff, the lawyer, who stood before me with an air of quiet introspection. His usual confidence and warmth were noticeably absent, replaced by a somber demeanor that struck me as unusual.

As we exchanged a brief, wordless handshake, I couldn't help but wonder what had brought him to my doorstep so late. "Have you returned to Hampstead?" I asked, attempting to break the tension.

Mr. Bruff shook his head, his eyes clouding over. "No, I've just arrived from Hampstead. I know you've finally uncovered the truth, Mr. Franklin, but if I had foreseen the cost of your discovery, I would have preferred keeping you in the dark."

He paused, collecting his thoughts before continuing. "I took Rachel back to Portland Place, and it was impossible for her to travel alone. I must admit, I'm not entirely absolving you of responsibility for the distress she endured, given our previous encounter. However, I implore you to spare her further pain. She's young, resilient, and with time and rest, she'll recover. I ask that you refrain from seeking her out again without my permission."

I nodded solemnly, offering him my assurance. "After all she's been through, and after my own ordeal, you may rely on my discretion."

Mr. Bruff's expression eased slightly, and he set his hat aside, drawing his chair closer to mine. "That's settled," he said, his voice tinged with relief.
