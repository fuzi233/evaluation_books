As Oleron stepped out into the cool, damp air, the wooden boards beneath his feet creaked in protest. He had left the candle burning, the flame dancing like a solitary spirit on the table. A faint memory began to resurface, like a mist clearing on a summer morning – the soft click of the door, the deliberate slowness of his exit, the door closing softly behind him. But it was not until he reached the bottom of the stairs that the past truly asserted itself, like a sudden storm brewing on the horizon.

A flurry of movement erupted from the depths of the disused cellar, and a sleek form darted out into the night, vanishing into the darkness. Oleron's heart skipped a beat as he let out a childish sob, the sound lost in the wind. He shook his head, chiding himself for his foolishness. It was only a cat.

Beyond the gate, the flickering light of a nearby sign beckoned him towards the promise of warmth and comfort. "To Let" boards hung crookedly, like a gaunt skeleton's fingers, but Oleron's gaze was drawn upwards, to the glint of light emanating from behind one of the red blinds. He hesitated, his fingers drumming against his thigh as he pondered his next move.

The public house loomed ahead, its door swinging open like a welcoming mouth. Oleron pushed inside, his eyes adjusting slowly to the dim light within. The air was thick with the smell of smoke and spirits, and the murmur of hushed conversations hung in the air like a fog. He spotted a small bar, tucked away in a corner, and made his way towards it, the wooden floor creaking beneath his feet.

The bartender, a woman with a kind face and a wild shock of white hair, looked up as he approached. "Brandy," Oleron muttered, his voice barely audible over the din of the crowd. She raised an eyebrow, but poured him a generous measure without comment. Oleron took a sip, feeling the burn spread through his chest like a slow fire.

As he sat there, nursing his drink, Oleron's thoughts turned to the task ahead. He would need to gather his belongings, to pack up the remnants of his life and leave them behind. But for now, he simply sat, lost in the warm, golden glow of the public house, his worries temporarily forgotten.
