("Beloved, thy footsteps tread the world in shadow—
Stepping, like silver dust upon a night—
Touching the frost with a sacred glow...
Thy Voice a hymn beyond the mountain's height,
Which terrifies my soul with joy untold.")

On one night, near the close of the sixteenth hour, as I prepared for rest, the air itself vibrated with a strange, electric hum, a phenomenon that occurred often in those days. Yet this vibration carried a peculiar, haunting power. Within my mind, the voice of Naani rang clear and distinct, surrounding me completely.

Though I recognized it as hers, I did not answer at once. Instead, I projected the steady rhythm of the Master-Word into the darkness. Immediately, the response came back—a solid, pulsing beat echoing through the night. I sent my thoughts to Naani, asking why she sought communication via the Instrument during a time when all were asleep and the Monstruwacans were guarding the watch. Their vigil began at the eleventh hour, meaning it was already five hours into their waking cycle; she should have been resting, secluded within the Tower of Observation under her father's care. I assumed her voice sounded so clearly in my mind because it traveled through the Instrument.

To this inquiry, she offered no verbal reply. Instead, a specific impression landed directly in my spirit, causing me to tremble. She spoke words that began:

"Beloved, thy footsteps tread the world in shadow—"

As the syllables formed around me, a phantom memory surged forth. I recalled crafting these exact words for Mirdath the Beautiful in the distant Eternity of our Age, when she had perished and left me alone in a desolate universe. A wave of emotion, weak yet overwhelming, seized me. In an instant, I reached out with my mental faculties, demanding an explanation from Naani for the turmoil she had wrought in my heart.

Once more, she refused a direct answer. Instead, she repeated the words across the abyss of the dark world. Suddenly, the truth struck me: it was not Naani speaking, but Mirdath the Beautiful, emerging from the eternal night. I called out, "Mirdath! Mirdath!" projecting my cry into the void. Lo! the distant, faint voice responded again, repeating the lines. Although the timbre belonged to Mirdath, it was simultaneously the voice of Naani. My heart knew the reality of this union: I had been rebirthed into this living time alongside that One Spirit with whom my essence had been joined through the ages. I called to Naani with every ounce of my strength, but there was no answer, no sign of hearing, despite hours of pleading.

Thus, I fell into a state of utter exhaustion, unable to find peace or slumber. Eventually, I slept.

Upon awakening, my first recollection was of the miraculous event that had transpired in the realm of dreams. No mortal mind in this world could have known those words unless it was the spirit of Mirdath, my Beloved, hovering just above my shoulder in that lost epoch, as I had once uttered them to her from a broken heart. The voice had been Mirdath's, and Mirdath's voice had been Naani's. What could any man say of this but what resided in his own heart?

Immediately, I summoned Naani, calling her once, then twice. Soon, the air around me thrummed with the solemn beat of the Master-Word. I sent the word outward as a token of assurance, and instantly, Naani's voice returned, slightly weakened—as it always was when she lacked the Instrument, relying instead on her brain-elements.

I answered her, questioning her urgently regarding her midnight utterances. She denied everything, clarifying that she had no memory of speaking and had slept soundly through the entire period, dreaming only a singular, bizarre dream.

For a brief moment, I was confused, meditating on what to believe. However, I suddenly realized that Naani's distant voice continued to thrill the aether, and she intended to recount the dream that had haunted her mind.

She described the vision: a towering, dark figure, massive in stature and clad in alien garments. He had been confined to a small, solitary chamber, filled with profound sorrow. Naani had approached him. The man attempted to write to soothe his grief, and though the language was strange and unknown to her waking mind, she could read the characters. She could not recall the content of his writing except for one short line, which contained the word *Mirdath* written above it. She noted the strangeness of dreaming this name, suspecting I might have planted the idea with my initial calls.

With a tremor in my spirit, I asked Naani to reveal exactly what she remembered of the stranger's writing. In a moment, her distant voice echoed around me:

"Beloved, thy footsteps tread the world in shadow—"

No further words followed, yet this fragment was sufficient. With a strange, mad triumph in my soul, I completed the phrase in my mind, sending it back to her. It struck her spirit with the violence of a blow, jarring her from her stupor. For a moment, she stood dumb before the sheer weight of this new certainty. Then, her spirit awoke fully, and she nearly wept from the fright and the sudden, blinding wonder of the revelation.

Instantly, the air around me resonated with her voice, which now sounded like Mirdath's and Naani's intertwined. I heard the tears of her spirit purify the bewildered, growing gladness in her transmission. She asked me, as one who had suddenly opened the Gates of Memory, if she might truly be Mirdath. I, utterly weak and shaken by this splendor of fulfillment, could offer no instant answer. She asked again, using my old love-name, with a surety in her far voice. Still, I was strangely mute, the blood pounding peculiarly in my ears, before speech finally returned to me swiftly.

This became the manner of our spiritual meeting, spanning all the everlasting night.

Consider the picture: Naani standing in the world of that far eternity, her spirit conversing with mine, gazing back through the partially open gates of her memory into the past of our current life and Age. She saw far more than was granted to me in that Age; she witnessed other instances, other reunions, some confusing and possessing only half-meant meanings to me. Yet, regarding our present Age and life, we spoke as if it were merely yesterday, a time hallowed and sacred.

Naturally, the certainty of this bond stirred me fiercely to complete action. All my heart and spirit cried out to be with that One who was Mirdath, now speaking through Naani.

Yet, how could this union be won? None of the learned men of the Mighty Pyramid knew the location of the Lesser Redoubt, nor could the Records and Histories of the World provide such knowledge. There was only a general conjecture among the Students and Monstruwacans that it lay somewhere between the North-West and North-East sectors. No man possessed surety, nor could anyone conceive of the distance separating us from that Refuge.

Counting all this, there existed the incredible danger and peril of the Night Land, coupled with the hunger and desolation of the Outer Lands, sometimes termed the Unknown Lands.

I spoke extensively with Naani concerning the matter of their position. Neither she nor her father, the Master Monstruwacan of that Refuge, knew our location. They only knew, according to the Records, that the Builder of the Lesser Redoubt had emerged from the Southern Worlds in the Beginning.

Furthermore, Naani's father set the ancient Compass to bear. As he explained through the Instrument, the immense power of the Earth-Current must be influencing the device, perhaps pulling the pointer away from its steadiness. Indeed, the needle swung in an arc holding between North and South, within the Western quadrant. This behavior had persisted for them eternally, rendering it a helpless guide. Save for the possibility that the Earth-Current accompanying us exerted a subtle pull toward our location, we had constructed a calculation placing the Lesser Redoubt to the North, and ourselves to the South. Yet, this was all built upon the shifting sands of guesswork; nothing worthy of risking life and soul.

Out of curiosity—and though a million times it had been done in past ages—we set the Compass before us, retrieving it from the Great Museum. As ever in that Age, it spun wildly if we merely stirred the needle, refusing to settle anywhere with surety. The flow of the Earth-Current issuing from the "Crack" beneath the Pyramid possessed the power to deflect it from the North, setting it wandering. This may seem strange to the present Age, yet to them, it was the true nature of things; it was harder to believe that it had ever pointed steadfastly, proving a guide of reliability.

Be it known, we knew the positions of the Lands by tradition, inherited from the ancient time when, in the Half-Gloom, they had built the Pyramid. They had mastered the use of that ancient Compass and, with sight of the Sun, named the Directions. Though we of that far-future day had forgotten the origins of those directional names, we used them simply because our fathers had, a million years prior. Likewise, we adhered to the names of the days, nights, weeks, months, and years, despite the absence of any visible markings other than the everlasting night. Yet, this seemed entirely natural to that people.
