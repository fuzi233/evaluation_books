As the days passed since Wilbur Whateley's passing, an unsettling stillness settled over the countryside. The formalities following his death were carried out with a sense of bewilderment by the local authorities, who were keen to keep the more unusual aspects of the case from the prying eyes of the press and the public. Teams were dispatched to Dunwich and Aylesbury to investigate the properties and notify any potential heirs, but they found the area in a state of heightened anxiety. The ground beneath the hills seemed to be rumbling ominously, and the once-clear air was now thick with an unnatural stench. The abandoned farmhouse, once boarded up and eerily silent, now emitted a low, lapping sound that sent shivers down the spines of those who dared to approach.

Earl Sawyer, who had cared for Wilbur's animals during his absence, was now wracked with a debilitating nervous disorder. The officials, anxious to avoid confronting the malevolent aura surrounding the farmhouse, chose to limit their investigation to a single visit. They compiled a lengthy report, which they submitted to the courthouse in Aylesbury, and the contentious issue of Wilbur's inheritance remained a topic of dispute among the numerous Whateleys scattered throughout the Miskatonic valley.

A mysterious manuscript, penned in an unfamiliar script, lay scattered across the desk in Wilbur's study. The officials deemed it a diary due to its varying handwriting and ink usage, but even the most skilled linguists at Miskatonic University struggled to decipher its cryptic content. The university also received Wilbur's collection of obscure books, which added to the enigma surrounding the young man's life and death. The gold, which had always been used to settle Wilbur's debts, remained unaccounted for.

On the ninth of September, a chill ran through the night air as the hill noises reached a fever pitch. Dogs barked incessantly, and early risers detected a pungent odor hanging heavy over the landscape. At dawn, Luther Brown, a young farmhand, returned to George Corey's farm in a state of panic, accompanied by the frantic cows that had followed him back from the meadow. As he stumbled into the kitchen, Luther tried to recount his terrifying experience to Mrs. Corey.
