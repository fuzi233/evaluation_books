In the days following Harry's ill-fated meeting with Lady Maria, the castle's tranquil atmosphere was filled with the soft murmur of conversation. George, eager to keep his aunt informed, dispatched a discreet letter detailing the events that had transpired. Shortly after, Lord Castlewood arrived at the estate, his visits to his aunt being few and far between, but always welcomed. As he settled into his chair, he began to recount the intricate dance of alliances and rivalries that had unfolded.

"My dear aunt," he said, his voice low and measured, "the game has been played, and it's over. There's no need for pretenses or duplicity. I'll speak freely, as one should when the stakes are laid bare." His words hung in the air like the gentle rustle of leaves on an autumn breeze.

Madame de Bernstein listened intently, her expression a mask of calm interest. "Your candor, dear nephew, does you great credit. Your affection for your sister shines brightly, even in defeat."

Lord Castlewood smiled, his eyes crinkling at the corners. "I'm speaking without rancor, aunt. We've lost this round, and I see no point in harboring resentment. Besides, you, who have emerged victorious, have no cause to bear malice."

The Baroness nodded graciously, her eyes sparkling with amusement. "I'm in excellent spirits, thank you for asking, Eugene. Tell me, was that visit from Maria to Harry at the debtors' prison a clever move on your part?"

Eugene's smile deepened, and he leaned forward, his voice taking on a conspiratorial tone. "I merely suggested that Maria show compassion to her affianced lover in his hour of need. Her actions were motivated by a desire to do what was right, rather than any ulterior motive."

Madame de Bernstein raised an eyebrow. "And yet, you were the one who guided her in this decision, weren't you?"

Eugene shrugged, a hint of a smile playing on his lips. "Perhaps I offered some advice, but the outcome was never certain. We would have triumphed, nonetheless, had not the elder brother appeared at the most inopportune moment. I've been trying to console Maria by pointing out the silver lining – that we lost, but not before gaining a glimpse of George Warrington's character. He seems a sensible man, not excessively self-interested, unlike some elder brothers I could mention."
