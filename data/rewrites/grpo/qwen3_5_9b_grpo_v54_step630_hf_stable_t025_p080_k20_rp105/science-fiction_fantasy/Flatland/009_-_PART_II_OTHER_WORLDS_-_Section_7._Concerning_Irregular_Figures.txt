Throughout the preceding pages, I have operated under an assumption—what ought to have been declared as a foundational axiom from the outset—that every sentient being in Flatland possesses a Regular Figure, meaning a construction of perfect geometric symmetry. To be precise: a Woman is not merely a line, but a perfectly straight one; an Artisan or Soldier must possess two sides of exact equality; a Tradesman, three; and a Lawyer, of which I am a modest member, four. In general, for any Polygon to exist within our society, all its sides must be equal in length.

While the absolute magnitude of these sides naturally correlates with the individual's age—a newborn female measuring roughly an inch, whereas a mature woman may stretch to a foot—their equality is the critical variable. For males of every rank, the sum of an adult's side lengths typically approximates two feet. Yet, physical scale is secondary; the entire edifice of Flatlandian social life rests upon the immutable law that Nature mandates equal sides.

Were our sides unequal, our angles would inevitably diverge. Under such conditions, the mere estimation of a single angle would fail to reveal an individual's true form; one would be compelled to verify every angle through the tactile ordeal of Feeling. Life, however, is too brief for such exhaustive scrutiny. The sciences and arts of Sight Recognition would instantly collapse; the practice of Feeling would cease to function as a reliable tool; social interaction would descend into peril or impossibility; trust and foresight would vanish. No citizen could safely arrange the simplest of social encounters. In short, civilization would regress into barbarism.

Am I rushing my readers toward these inevitable conclusions? Surely, a moment's reflection, aided by a single analogy from daily existence, must convince anyone that our entire social system is predicated on Regularity. Consider meeting two or three Tradesmen in the street. You identify them instantly by their characteristic angles and slightly dimmed sides, inviting them into your home for lunch. You act with absolute confidence because everyone knows the precise area occupied by an adult Triangle. Now, imagine that your Tradesman drags behind his regular vertex a chaotic parallelogram, twelve or thirteen inches in diagonal. What would you do with such a monster wedged firmly in your doorway?

I fear I insult the intelligence of my readers by detailing scenarios obvious to any inhabitant of Spaceland. Clearly, measuring a single angle would no longer suffice in such a catastrophe; one's entire existence would be consumed by feeling or surveying the perimeters of acquaintances. Already, avoiding collisions in a crowd taxes the wisdom of even a well-educated Square. But if no one could calculate the Regularity of a single figure among them, chaos would reign supreme. The slightest panic would inflict grievous injuries, or, if Women or Soldiers were present, perhaps considerable loss of life.

Thus, expediency aligns with Nature, stamping its approval upon the Regularity of form, and the Law hastens to support this consensus. In Flatland, "Irregularity of Figure" signifies the same as, or indeed exceeds, the combination of moral obliquity and criminality known to you in higher dimensions, and is treated accordingly. It is true that some propagators of paradoxes argue there is no necessary link between geometric and moral irregularity. They claim the Irregular is born to be shunned by parents, ridiculed by kin, neglected by servants, and scorned by society, barred from all positions of trust. They describe how police watch every movement until adulthood, when the individual is either destroyed if exceeding the margin of deviation or imprisoned in a Government Office as a seventh-class clerk, forbidden marriage, forced into drudgery for a pittance, and subjected to constant surveillance during vacations. They ask, what wonder that human nature becomes embittered and perverted by such treatment?

Yet, this plausible reasoning fails to convince me, nor the wisest of our statesmen, that our ancestors erred in establishing the axiom that tolerating Irregularity is incompatible with the safety of the State. Undoubtedly, the life of an Irregular is harsh; but the interests of the Greater Number demand it remain so. If a man with a triangular front and a polygonal back were permitted to exist and propagate an increasingly irregular lineage, what fate would befall the arts of life? Must our houses, doors, and churches be altered to accommodate such monstrosities? Must ticket collectors measure every man's perimeter before granting entry to a theater or lecture hall? Should an Irregular be exempted from the militia? And if not, how is he to be prevented from bringing desolation to his comrades? Furthermore, what irresistible temptations to fraud must beset such a creature? How easily could he enter a shop with his polygonal front forward, ordering goods from a trusting tradesman to any extent? Let advocates of false philanthropy plead as they will for the abolition of Irregular Penal Laws; I, for my part, have never encountered an Irregular who was not also what Nature evidently intended him to be: a hypocrite, a misanthrope, and, within the limits of his power, a perpetrator of all manner of mischief.

This does not mean I advocate the extreme measures seen in some States, where an infant deviating by half a degree from correct angularity is summarily destroyed at birth. Some of our greatest geniuses suffered deviations as severe as, or greater than, forty-five minutes in their earliest days; their loss would have been an irreparable injury to the State. Moreover, the art of healing has achieved glorious triumphs through compressions, extensions, trepannings, colligations, and other surgical or dietary operations that have partially or wholly cured Irregularity. Therefore, advocating a *Via Media*, I propose laying down no fixed or absolute line of demarcation. However, at the stage when the frame is just setting and the Medical Board reports that recovery is improbable, I suggest that the Irregular offspring be painlessly and mercifully consumed.
