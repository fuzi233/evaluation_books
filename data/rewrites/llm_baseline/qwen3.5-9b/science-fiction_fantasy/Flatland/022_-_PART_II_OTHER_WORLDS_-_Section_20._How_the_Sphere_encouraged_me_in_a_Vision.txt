With less than a minute to spare for reflection, a sudden instinct compelled me to conceal my recent experiences from my wife. I did not fear she would betray my secret, but I knew that to any woman in Flatland, such a narrative would remain utterly unintelligible. Thus, I hastily concocted a lie to reassure her, claiming I had accidentally fallen through the cellar's trapdoor and remained stunned until rescued. In our land, where the southward gravitational pull is so feeble, even this improbable tale seemed extraordinary to a female mind; yet my wife possessed a wisdom surpassing the average of her sex. Perceiving my unusual agitation, she offered no argument but insisted I was unwell and required rest.

Grateful for the opportunity to retreat to my chamber, I withdrew to ponder the events alone. Before my eyes finally closed, a heavy drowsiness seized me. Struggling to recall the Third Dimension—specifically the construction of a cube via the motion of a square—I found the image hazy. However, I clung to the phrase *"Upward, and yet not Northward"* as my guiding key, convinced that holding fast to these words would unlock the mystery. Chanting them mechanically, like a protective charm, I drifted into a deep, refreshing sleep.

In my dreams, I found myself once again beside the Sphere. His radiant hue signaled that his earlier wrath had transformed into perfect placability. Together, we glided toward a brilliant yet infinitesimally small point, drawing my master's gaze. As we neared, a faint hum emanated from it—a sound akin to a Spaceland bluebottle, though far less resonant. Even within the absolute stillness of the vacuum through which we soared, the noise reached our ears only after we slowed our descent to within twenty human diagonals of the source.

"Look yonder," my Guide instructed. "In Flatland you have lived; in Lineland you have seen a vision; with me, you have soared to the heights of Spaceland. Now, to complete your education, I will descend with you to the lowest depths of existence: the realm of Pointland, the Abyss of No Dimensions."

We gazed upon a wretched figure. "That Point is a being like ourselves, trapped in a non-dimensional gulf," the Sphere explained. "He is his own world, his own universe; he cannot conceive of anything outside himself. To him, length, breadth, and height are unknown concepts, for he has never experienced them. He possesses no notion of the number two, nor does he understand plurality. He is his own One and All, effectively *Nothing*."

The Sphere paused, observing the creature's profound self-contentment. "From this, learn a vital lesson: to be self-satisfied is to be vile and ignorant; to aspire is infinitely superior to blind, impotent happiness. Now, listen."

Silence fell, broken only by a tiny, low, monotonous tinkling rising from the buzzing entity—a sound reminiscent of a Spaceland phonograph. From this source came the words: *"Infinite beatitude of existence! It is; and there is none else beside It."*

"What does this puny creature mean by 'It'?" I asked.

"He means himself," the Sphere replied. "Have you not observed that infants and childish minds, unable to distinguish themselves from the world, often speak in the third person? But hush!"

"It fills all space," the Point continued in its soliloquy. "And whatever It fills, It is. Whatever It thinks, It utters; whatever It utters, It hears. It is the Thinker, the Utterer, the Hearer, the Thought, the Word, and the Audition. It is the One, and yet the All-in-All. Ah, the happiness! Ah, the happiness of Being!"

"Cannot you startle this creature from its complacency?" I urged. "Reveal to it its true nature, just as you revealed mine. Show it the narrow limits of Pointland and lead it upward to something greater."

"That is no easy task," my Master admitted. "Try yourself."

Raising my voice to its utmost limit, I addressed the Point: "Silence, contemptible creature! You claim to be the All-in-All, yet you are merely Nothing. Your so-called universe is but a speck in a line, and a line is but a shadow compared to—"

"Hush, hush! You have spoken enough," the Sphere interrupted sharply. "Listen now, and observe the effect of your speech on the King of Pointland."

The monarch's radiance increased, shining brighter than ever, proving his complacency remained undisturbed. Hardly had I ceased speaking when he resumed his chant:

*"Ah, the joy! Ah, the joy of Thought! What can It not achieve by thinking! Its own thought returning to Itself, suggesting Its own disparagement, thereby enhancing Its happiness! Sweet rebellion stirred up to result in triumph! Ah, the divine creative power of the All-in-One! Ah, the joy, the joy of Being!"*

"You see," my Teacher observed, "how little your words accomplished. As far as the Monarch comprehends them, he accepts them as his own—for he cannot conceive of anything other than himself—and takes pride in the variety of 'Its Thought' as proof of his creative power. Let us leave this God of Pointland to the ignorant fruition of his omnipresence and omniscience; nothing you or I can say can rescue him from his self-satisfaction."

As we floated gently back toward Flatland, I heard my companion's mild voice offering the moral of the vision, urging me to aspire and to inspire others to do the same. He confessed that he had initially been angered by my ambition to soar beyond the Third Dimension; however, having gained fresh insight, he was no longer too proud to admit his error to a pupil. With renewed humility, he began to initiate me into mysteries even higher than those I had witnessed, demonstrating how to construct Extra-Solids through the motion of Solids, and Double Extra-Solids through the motion of Extra-Solids—all strictly following analogy, executed by methods so simple they were accessible even to the female sex.
