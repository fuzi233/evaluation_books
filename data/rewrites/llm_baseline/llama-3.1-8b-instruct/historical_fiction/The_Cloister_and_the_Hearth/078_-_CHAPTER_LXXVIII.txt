As Ghysbrecht Van Swieten walked home from the town hall, the fading sunlight casting long shadows across the cobblestones, he felt a sense of unease settle over him like a shroud. His conscience, long accustomed to being silenced by the demands of his avaricious nature, stirred fitfully, its protests growing louder with each passing year. The weight of his treachery hung heavy upon him, a burden he dared not share with anyone, lest it lead to his downfall.

The memory of his deception, committed in a moment of weakness, still rankled within him. He had taken Gerard's letter to Margaret, tampered with the seal, and presented it as his own, all in the name of securing his family's fortune. The birth of her child had only served to compound his guilt, highlighting the cruel fate he had orchestrated for her. Should his actions be discovered, he would bring shame not only to himself, but to the family of his friends, who had long been kind to him.

As he approached his house, a woman stood waiting in the vestibule, a small child cradled in her arms. Her veil hid her face, and Ghysbrecht, assuming she was there to beg or plead for something, greeted her with a haughty tone. But as she lifted her veil, revealing the determined face of Margaret Brandt, he was taken aback. Her eyes, once bright with hope, now burned with a fierce intensity, her voice trembling with emotion.

"Where is my Gerard?" she demanded, her words piercing the air like a knife. "Is he alive?"

Ghysbrecht stuttered out a response, hoping to placate her, but Margaret's grip on his shoulder tightened, her fingers digging deep into his flesh. "You know the truth," she accused, her voice rising. "Why does my son not return, nor send a word? Tell me, or I will shout it to the whole town!"

As he protested his innocence, trying to maintain a façade of dignity, Ghysbrecht felt his color drain from his face. He knew the game was up, that Margaret's determination would not be swayed. And yet, he hesitated, caught between his avarice and his conscience, unsure which to yield to. The memory of his friends, who had once stood by him, now taunted him, reminding him of his betrayal.
