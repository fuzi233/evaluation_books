The events since her awakening in the boat had left an indelible mark on her, much like the way a flame seeps into the wax, softening its rigid form.

She remembered the feeling of disconnection, of being untethered and aimless, consumed by a sense of discontent that had threatened to engulf her. In those moments, death had seemed a plausible escape, a release from the burden of existence. Yet, as she lay there, the cry of another person in distress had pierced her heart, and she had responded with a surge of energy, driven to share in the lives of those around her, to offer what comfort she could to those in need.

This experience had been a transformative baptism, one that had awakened a deep sense of purpose within her. In Florence, her relationships had been complicated by the expectations of society, the constraints of marriage and duty, and the demands of her faith. When those expectations had failed her, she had recoiled, feeling stunned and disconnected from the world around her. But now, she saw things differently. If the world was imperfect, and the cross was an illusion, then the suffering she could alleviate was a tangible reality. As long as she had the strength, she would extend a helping hand to those who faltered.

Her time there was at an end, but the connections she had formed would remain, a testament to the power of human compassion. As she looked back, she realized that her actions had been guided by a flawed understanding of what was right and wrong. She had judged others harshly, while failing to examine her own motivations and choices.

Had she been truly committed to her ideals, or had she been driven by a desire to escape the complexities of her life?
