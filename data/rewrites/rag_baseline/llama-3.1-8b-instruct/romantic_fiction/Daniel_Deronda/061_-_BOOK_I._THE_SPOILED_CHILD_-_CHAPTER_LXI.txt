The morning light filtering through the leaded glass of the Meyrick townhouse cast intricate patterns on the polished floorboards as Hans Meyrick slipped inside, his latch-key clicking softly in the lock. The air was heavy with the scent of fresh flowers and baking bread, carried on the warm breeze that wafted through the open windows. He made his way to the parlor, the newspaper rustling in his hand like a small, crisp bird. Mirah, seated at the piano, her fingers poised above the keys, looked up with a start as Hans burst in, the newspaper crackling loudly as he shook it out. The music screeched to a halt, and Mab's voice rose in protest.

"O-o-o, Hans! Why do you make such a racket?" she complained, her eyes wide with annoyance.

Hans grinned, unfazed, and plopped down beside Mirah on the music stool. "Wonderful news, my dears!" he exclaimed, waving the newspaper aloft. His mother, Mrs. Meyrick, looked up from her seat by the window, her expression expectant.

"What is it, Hans?" she asked, her voice tinged with a hint of worry. "Anything about Italy? The Austrians giving up Venice?"

"Not exactly," Hans replied, a mischievous glint in his eye. "Something from Italy, though." He paused, savoring the moment, aware that his family's collective curiosity was piqued.

Mrs. Meyrick's brow furrowed in concern. "Not bad news, I hope?" she ventured, her thoughts immediately turning to Daniel Deronda, a friend of the family who had been a frequent visitor to their home.

Hans chuckled, his tone laced with amusement. "Not bad for anyone we care about, I think," he said, his words dripping with irony. "Rather...fortunate, considering the circumstances."

Mab rolled her eyes good-naturedly. "If you must talk about yourself, Hans, do it behind your back," she teased, nudging him playfully.

Hans laughed, his eyes sparkling with mirth. "Oh, all right. But I'm just saying, I'm rather surprised to still be alive, considering my questionable judgment." He winked at Mirah, who looked up at him with a faint, troubled smile.
