Gerard awoke to find Denys gazing upon him with evident unease. "It is you still asleep!" the friend exclaimed. "'Tis already high noon."

"It was a blessed slumber," Gerard replied softly. "Methinks Heaven itself bestowed it upon me. It seemed to draw a veil between my spirit and that dreadful nightmare. To consider that we sit here, breathing and whole... how terrifying the vision must have been!"

"Indeed, lad," Denys answered wisely. "Once such horrors are behind us, one must view them merely as dreams or fleeting shadows. Eat your breakfast, and soon enough, the memory will fade. Furthermore, I promised to escort you into the town by midday to present yourself before His Worship."

Gerard dipped a piece of coarse rye bread into a cup of red wine and consumed it to break his fast. He then accompanied Denys across the battlefield where the recent skirmish had occurred. Returning from the scene, he walked with a shudder still clinging to his frame. As they set out again toward the town, he kept glancing anxiously through the hedgerows, expecting ambushes that reason dictated were impossible, until they finally arrived at the gates of the little settlement.

Denys led him straight to "The White Hart."

"No need to fear highwaymen here," Denys assured him confidently. "I have known this hostler for years. He is a respected burgess, likely serving as bailiff. In fact, this is where I intended to lodge last night. But we lost precious time, and darkness overtook us—until—"

"And there stood a woman at the door," Gerard interrupted, his voice trembling slightly. "She proved far wiser than a mere Jeanneton, telling us that all danger had passed."

"Why, what saved our lives if not that woman?" Denys asked earnestly. "Yes, she risked her own safety to aid us."

"That is true, Denys," Gerard admitted. "Though I hold little regard for women generally, I am compelled to seek out this poor girl, to thank her and reward her, even if I must part with every penny in my purse to do so. Would you not agree?"

"By God's grace, yes," Denys responded without hesitation.

"But where might we find her?"

"Perhaps the alderman can inform us. We must visit him first."

The alderman received them with a countenance of such singular and inexplicable gravity that they paused in surprise. Yet, after a brief moment of reflection, his face softened into a grim smile. He immediately began to question Gerard, taking careful note of every answer given. Once finished, he declared that they must remain in the town until the thieves were tried, warning them that failure to appear as witnesses would result in heavy fines and imprisonment.

They stared at him, bewildered by the severity of the command.

"Nevertheless," the magistrate continued, "it will not be long; the culprits were caught red-handed." He added casually, "And you should know, under any circumstances, you could not leave this place for the coming week."

Denys fixed him with a stare of confusion, while Gerard smiled inwardly at the old gentleman's naivety. Could a provincial town in Burgundy truly possess such allure as to detain a man bound for Rome and longing for Margaret?

Now, however, Gerard turned his attention to the matter nearest both their hearts.

"Your Worship," he said respectfully, "we are unable to locate our benefactress within the town."

"Nay, but pray tell, who is this benefactress?" the alderman inquired.

"Who? Why, the virtuous maiden who approached you last night," Gerard explained, his heart swelling with gratitude. "She saved our lives at great peril to herself. Oh, sir, our hearts burn within us to offer thanks and blessings; where may we find her?"
