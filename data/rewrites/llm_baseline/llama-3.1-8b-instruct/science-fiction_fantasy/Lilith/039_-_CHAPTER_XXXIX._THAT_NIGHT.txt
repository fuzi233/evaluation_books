The night wore on, casting a heavy veil over the household. The children, still shaken by the events of the previous evening, found it impossible to shake off the feeling of unease that clung to them like a damp shroud. As they lay abed, they couldn't help but think of the peculiar sensations they'd experienced: the wind that had shaken the house, the water that had flooded the garret, and the swarm of cats that had invaded their room.

Their mother, Mara, had listened intently to their tales, her face a mask of concern. But as she helped them prepare for bed, she seemed preoccupied, her thoughts elsewhere. I watched her from across the room, my eyes drawn to the white leopardess, who cowered in a corner, her eyes fixed on some unseen terror. The lamp cast eerie shadows on the walls, making it seem as though the room itself was alive and watching.

As the night deepened, the air grew thick with an unspoken expectation. The princess, still reclining on the settle, appeared lost in thought, her face a picture of serenity. But beneath the surface, I sensed a current of tension, a sense that something momentous was about to unfold.

Mara's return marked a shift in the atmosphere. She drew the settle closer to the center of the room, creating a sense of containment, as if the night itself was being corralled. The small fire crackled softly, casting a warm glow over the space. The veiled woman sat across from me, her presence a reminder of the mysteries that lay just beyond our understanding.

Time seemed to stretch and contract, each minute ticking by with agonizing slowness. The cloudy forms that danced in the shadows seemed to grow more defined, as if taking shape before our very eyes. And then, without warning, a silvery creature emerged from the darkness, its slow, sinuous movements mesmerizing. It slid across the clay floor, drawn inexorably to the fire, where it vanished into the flames.

The room held its breath, suspended in a state of anticipation. Midnight drew near, and yet nothing seemed to happen. The silence was oppressive, a physical presence that pressed down upon us all. I felt a subtle vibration, as if the very foundations of the house were shifting, but I couldn't pinpoint its source. A sense of foreboding settled over me, a feeling that we were standing at the threshold of some great reckoning.
