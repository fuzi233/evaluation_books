As Gerard stirred, Denys's anxious gaze met his. "Ah, you're awake," he said, relief etched on his face. "High noon already." Gerard rubbed the sleep from his eyes, still disoriented from a vivid dream that lingered like a shroud. "A blessed sleep, indeed," he murmured. "It's as if heaven itself intervened, separating me from that terrible night."

Denys nodded sympathetically. "That's the wisest approach, lad – treat it as a distant memory, a fleeting shadow. Come, let's break our fast, and you'll forget all about it." Gerard poured himself a cup of red wine and dipped a slice of rye bread into its depths, savoring the familiar comfort of the simple meal. With Denys by his side, they strolled across the battle-scarred landscape, the silence between them a testament to the lingering unease. Eventually, they made their way back to the road, Gerard's eyes scanning the hedges for any sign of danger, his heart still racing with the remnants of his nightmare.

As they entered the small town, Denys led him to "The White Hart", a bustling inn that exuded warmth and hospitality. "No need to worry about cutthroats here," Denys assured him. "I've known the landlord for years; he's a respected burgess and the local bailiff. We'll be safe under his roof." Gerard's eyes wandered to the fire crackling in the hearth, the savory aromas wafting from the kitchen enticing his senses.

As they settled into their seats, Gerard turned to Denys. "You spoke of a woman at the door last night, didn't you? She claimed the men we encountered were harmless." Denys nodded, his expression serious. "Aye, and she risked her life to save ours. We owe her a debt of gratitude, and I'd like to repay her, even if it means sharing my last penny." Gerard's determination echoed Denys's sentiments, and together they vowed to find the mysterious benefactress.

Their quest led them to the town hall, where the alderman greeted them with an enigmatic smile. After a moment's consideration, he began to ask Gerard a series of questions, transcribing his responses with a quill. The alderman's demeanor remained inscrutable, but his words soon revealed a surprise: Gerard and Denys were to remain in the town until the perpetrators were brought to justice
