Clement sighed. Doubt crept into his heart—had he guided this tempestuous soul wisely? Yet his training had taught him patience; in moments of uncertainty, to wait, to listen, to trust that clarity would come. So he held his silence and prayed for wisdom. He moved only to withdraw his foot gently, but the penitent clutched it with a desperate cry, pressing her forehead against it as tears fell. Shame and remorse burned in her, flushing her throat crimson as she trembled before him.

“Daughter,” he said softly, “take heart. Torment yourself no more over Gerard, who is gone. I am Brother Clement, sent to you not for judgment, but to guide your soul toward peace.”

She sobbed. “Yes—yes—”

“Leave this pilgrimage. Return to Rome. True repentance is not found in distant trials, but in the humble battles of daily life—patience with those near us, kindness to those who rely on us. It is easy to kneel before strangers; harder to walk gently among our own.”

“I will never raise a hand against her again.”

“I speak not only of servants, but of all whose lives touch yours—kin, friends, even those who try you. Let this be your penance: each night, and each dawn after prayer, remember your sin and God’s mercy. Let humility soften your heart. The world bows to the rich; seek instead the quiet poor, those who suffer without complaint. Serve them, and you serve Heaven.”

“And must I marry?”

“You must. Within the year, wed a man who fears God. You are clay, daughter—shaped by the hands beside you. Bound to folly, you will falter; joined to grace, you may yet rise, a pious princess… or even a saint.”

Her eyes widened. “*Me?*”

“You.”

He bid her rise and begin her new path. She lifted herself to her knees, unmasking to meet his gaze—a look of mingled sorrow and resolve—before bowing her head. “I will obey you as I would an angel. Yet my heart aches, for I fear I will never see you again. Let me at least dry your feet.”

“It is needless.”

“It is no penance. Please—do not deny me this.”

He relented. As she tended to him, her voice trembled. “I will have Masses said for *her* soul. May I?”

The request startled him. A faint warmth touched his cheeks. “A kind thought,” he murmured. “Do as you will. Such deeds return to the doer like blessings. I am *your* confessor, not hers. Your soul is my charge now.” He leaned closer, urgency in his whisper. “Daughter, I see angels and demons warring for you this very hour. Fight—*fight*—on the side of light. Do you remember all I’ve said?”

“Every word,” she breathed. “They are carved into my heart.”

“One last counsel: pray much to Christ, and little to His saints.”

“I will.”

“Then we part here. You to your duty, I to mine.”

Her voice broke. “Farewell.”

“Farewell?” He straightened, sudden fervor brightening his gaze. “Christians do not say ‘forever.’ We part now, as earth parts from the sun—only to meet again in radiance. You and I divide for this life. And what is life? A breath in eternity. We are but lines in the great story of the Church, grains in the sand of time. Farewell—for the brief span called a lifetime! We part in sorrow; we will meet in joy. We part as mortals; we will rise as spirits. We part in shadow; we will stand together in the endless light, where Christ reigns, and all souls are made pure. There, in the blink of an eye, beyond time, we will meet again—you, and she, and I—amid saints and angels, in the presence of God Himself. *Forever. Forever. Forever.*”

And so they parted—he with eyes uplifted, aflame with holy fire; she, stepping back, casting one last lingering glance at the figure who had stirred her soul. She left subdued, yet strangely at peace.

Clement journeyed to Basel, where he taught, preached, and immersed himself in study and devotion. His life became a testament to discipline—his mind fixed on scripture, his flesh subdued by fasting. Those around him began to whisper: here walked a man touched by grace, a descendant of the martyrs.

But in quiet moments, his thoughts strayed to the princess, and to the souls he had left behind—wondering, hoping, praying that his words had taken root. For in the end, all paths led upward. All separations were fleeting.

And all endings were but pauses in the eternal.
