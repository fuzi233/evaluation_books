Friday morning dawned over the estate, bringing with it two pieces of news that sent ripples of curiosity through the household. The first tidbit came from the baker's man, who claimed to have spotted Rosanna Spearman strolling towards Frizinghall on the previous afternoon, shrouded in a thick veil. It was peculiar that anyone could mistake the distinctive shoulder of the reclusive young woman, but the baker's man swore he had seen her. However, this story was soon disputed by the fact that Rosanna had been confined to her room all day, her health precarious as ever.

As I poured the tea at breakfast, the postman arrived with another piece of news that left us all agog. It transpired that Dr. Candy, the affable physician, had fallen prey to a chill on the rainy birthday night and was now suffering from a fever. Despite his unfortunate condition, his words remained as glib as ever, much to the dismay of those around him. Mr. Franklin's concern for Miss Rachel seemed to stem more from the uncertainty surrounding the Moonstone than from any genuine worry for the doctor's well-being.

Just as we were finishing our meal, a telegram arrived from Mr. Blake, the elder, in response to his son's inquiry. It informed us that he had secured the services of a renowned detective, Sergeant Cuff, who was due to arrive by the morning train. Mr. Franklin's reaction was telling, as he seemed to recall some intriguing anecdotes about the Sergeant from his father's lawyer during his visit to London. "I daresay we're on the cusp of resolving our anxieties," he said, his eyes sparkling with optimism. "If half the tales I've heard are true, Sergeant Cuff is unmatched in unraveling mysteries."

As the anticipation grew, Superintendent Seegrave retreated to his room to prepare the report that would surely be expected from him. I longed to accompany him to the station to collect the Sergeant, but our carriage was reserved for Mr. Godfrey, who had graciously postponed his departure until the last train to hear the detective's assessment of the case. The evening before, he would be attending a Ladies' Charity event, where he would be required on Saturday morning.
