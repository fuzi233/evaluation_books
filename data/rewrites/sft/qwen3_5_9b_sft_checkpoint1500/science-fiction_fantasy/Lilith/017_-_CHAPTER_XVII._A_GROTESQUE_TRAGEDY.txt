I hadn't taken ten steps before I spotted something odd ahead, a shape that halted my progress. Moving closer, I discovered it to be a carriage of antiquated design, though ruined, it still stood erect on its heavy wheels. Flanking the central pole, where they belonged, lay the skeletons of two horses; from their bleached, grim skulls, shriveled reins trailed down to the hands of a skeleton coachman perched upon his tattered hammer-cloth. Both doors hung open, having long since detached from their frames. Inside, two skeletons sat slumped in opposite corners, leaning back against the rotting wood.

As I watched, they suddenly jolted awake. With a rattling crunch of shifting vertebrae, each leaped from its respective door. One collapsed immediately, landing in a heap. The other remained standing for a fleeting second, its skeletal frame trembling dangerously under the stress of movement; then, with great labored effort—its joints stiff and unyielding—it dragged itself to the opposite side of the vehicle, clutching the back for support. Its slender leg bones appeared insufficient to bear its weight, and it nearly toppled again in the attempt.

The prone figure eventually managed to rise, propelling itself upward into a seated position by a sudden exertion of will. For a brief moment, it swiveled its yellowed cranium from side to side, observing its surroundings. Ignoring its neighbor, it scrambled to its feet using the spokes of the rear wheel for leverage. Standing half-erect, its back turned to the other, it gripped one of its own knee-joints with both hands. With similar difficulty and numerous contortions, the kneeling companion followed suit and approached.

"Have you injured yourself, my lord?" it asked, its voice sounding distant, poorly articulated, as if blown away by some ethereal gale.

"Yes, I have," the other replied in a matching but coarser tone. "You offered no assistance, and this accursed knee is dislocated!"

"I did my utmost, my lord."

"No doubt, my lady, for it was terrible! I feared I'd never find my footing again!--But, bless my soul, madam! Are you missing your bones?"

She glanced down at herself.

"I have nothing else to be missing," she returned, "and YOU, at least, cannot complain! But what on earth is happening? Am I dreaming?"

"YOU may be dreaming, madam—I cannot say; but this knee of mine forbids me the comforting illusion.—Ha! And I, I perceive, have nothing to walk in but bones! Not so unbecoming for a man, perhaps! I trust to goodness they aren't MY bones! Every ache feels different, and this loose knee is the worst! The bed must have been damp—and I was certainly drunk enough not to notice!"

"Probably, my lord of Cokayne!"

"What! What!--You make me suspect I'm also dreaming amidst these pains! How DO you know the title my boisterous henchmen bestowed upon me? I don't recall you!--Regardless, you have no right to take liberties! My name is—I am Lord----tut, tut! What do you call me when I'm—when you're sober? I can't—at the moment,--Why, WHAT IS my name?--I must have been VERY intoxicated when I retired! I frequently am!"

"You visit so rarely at my home that I do not know, my lord; but I may accept your word for THAT!"

"I hope so!"

"--if for no other reason!" "Hoity-toity! I've never lied in my life!"

"You never spoke anything but falsehoods to me."

"On my honor!--Why, I haven't seen this woman before!"

"You knew me well enough to deceive, my lord!"

"It seems I am beginning to dream that I have met you before, but, upon my oath, there is nothing familiar about you! Stripped of your garments, how is anyone to determine who you might not be?--One thing I CAN swear—that I have never seen you so undressed before!--By heavens, I have no memory of you!"

"I am relieved to hear it: my memories of you are the less distasteful!--Good morning, my lord!"

She turned away, hobbling with a clattering rhythm, pausing after a few steps.

"You are as cold-hearted as—as--any other woman, madam!--Where in this infernal place shall I locate my valet?--What was the cursed name I used to call the fool?"

He twisted his bare head this way and that on its creaking axis, still clutching his knee with both hands.

"I shall serve as your valet for once, my lord," said the lady, turning back to him. "--What requires doing? It is difficult to ascertain!"

"Tie my leg on, naturally, you fool! Can't you see it is almost severed? Heigho, my dancing days!"

She scanned the area with her empty sockets, locating a tuft of fibrous grass, which she used to bind together the adjacent segments that formed the knee. Upon completion, he took one or two carefully tentative steps.

"You used to stomp quite differently, my lord!" she remarked as she rose from her knees.

"Eh? what!--Now that I gaze upon you again, it strikes me I used to despise you!--Eh?"

"Naturally, my lord! You detested many individuals!--your wife, of course, included!"

"Ah, I begin, I be-gin---- But--I must have been absent for some time!--I truly forget!--There! That damned, wretched scrap of grass is snapping!--We used to manage PRETTY well together--eh?"

"Not that I recall, my lord. The sole joyful intervals I shared with you occurred during the first week of our union."

"Was that the manner of affairs? Ha! ha!--Well, it is concluded now, thank goodness!"

"I wish I could believe it! Why were we seated together in that carriage? It awakens dread!"

"I believe we were divorced, my lady!"

"Hardly sufficient: we remain united!"

"A sorrowful reality, yet amenable to correction: the forest appears to possess some breadth!"

"I doubt! I doubt!"

"I regret I cannot conceive of a compliment to offer you--without resorting to deceit, that is. Judging by your stature and pallor, you have endured hardship since I last beheld you! I cannot possibly be QUITE so exposed as your ladyship!--I beg your pardon, madam! I trust you will accept that I jest in a dream! It matters not, however; whether dreaming or awake, everything is identical—all mere semblance! One cannot verify anything, and that equates to knowing there is nothing! Life may instruct any fool in this!"

"It has instructed me in the folly I showed in loving you!"

"You were not the solitary fool to commit such an act! Women possessed a penchant for falling in love with me:--I had forgotten that you were one of them!" "I did love you, my lord--a little--at one time!"

"Ah, there lay your error, my lady! You ought to have loved me deeply, loved me devotedly, loved me savagely--loved me eternally! Then I would have grown weary of you sooner, and not harbored such intense hatred thereafter!--But let past grievances remain in the past!--WHERE are we? Location is the inquiry! To exist or not to exist, is NOT the query!"

"We reside in another realm, I presume!"

"Granted!--yet in which or what nature of other realm? This cannot be Hell!"

"It must be: there exists marriage herein! You and I are condemned to one another."

"Then I am unlike Othello, condemned to a virtuous spouse!--Oh, I recall my Shakespeare, madam!"

She plucked a fractured branch that had fallen into a thicket, steadying herself with it, and continued her slow retreat, tossing her small skull.

"Hand that stick to me," cried her erstwhile husband; "I need it more than you."

She offered him no response.

"Do you intend to compel me to beg for it?"

"Not at all, my lord. I intend to retain it," she replied, hastening her departure.

"Give it to me immediately; I mean to possess it! I require it."

"Alas, I believe I require it myself!" returned the lady, walking slightly faster, accompanied by a sharper cracking of her joints and the clinking of her bones.

He attempted to follow, but nearly fell: the binding around his knee had ruptured, and with an oath he halted, seizing his leg anew.

"Come and retie it properly!" he would have thundered, but instead he emitted a high-pitched whistle.

She paused and regarded him.

"Come and retie it instantly!" he repeated.

She advanced a step or two further away.

"I swear I will not touch you!" he cried.

"Continue swearing, my lord! There is no one here to witness your oaths. However, pray, do not lose your temper, lest you shatter yourself into fragments, and where to find sufficient cordage to bind all your erratic joints, exceeds my capacity to determine."

She returned, kneeling once more beside him—placing the disputed stick beyond his grasp and within hers first.

The instant she completed the rebinding, he lunged, appearing to intend seizing her by the hair; but his rigid fingers slipped over the smooth crown of her skull.
