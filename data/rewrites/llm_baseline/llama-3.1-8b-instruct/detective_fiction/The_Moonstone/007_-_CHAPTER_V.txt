As the sun beat down on the sand, I struggled to rise from my seat once more, but Mr. Franklin stayed my attempt with a gentle hand. "There's one advantage to being stranded here," he said with a hint of a smile, "we're quite alone. Stay put, Betteredge; I need a word with you." I watched him, searching for glimpses of the boy I remembered, but the man before me was a stranger. His complexion had paled, and a curly brown beard now framed his face, a far cry from the rosy-cheeked youth I recalled. His eyes, though, remained bright and straightforward, a spark of recognition that bridged the years.

"Welcome back to the old place, Mr. Franklin," I said, trying to hide my confusion. "The sooner you arrived, the better."

"I've come early for a reason, Betteredge," he replied, his eyes scanning the surrounding area. "I think I've been followed in London for the past few days, and I took the morning train to shake off a suspicious character. I want to make sure we're not being watched."

His words sent a shiver down my spine, conjuring memories of the three jugglers who'd appeared at our doorstep earlier that day. Penelope's words echoed in my mind: "They mean mischief for Mr. Franklin Blake." I turned to ask him about the stranger, but he forestalled me.

"Tell me about the jugglers, Betteredge," he said, his brow furrowed in concern. "Perhaps they're connected to the man I'm worried about."

"How did you know about the jugglers?" I asked, my curiosity getting the better of me.

"I spoke with Penelope at the house," he replied. "She told me about the visitors. And she said you have a talented daughter, Betteredge – with a small ear and a small foot, just like her mother."
