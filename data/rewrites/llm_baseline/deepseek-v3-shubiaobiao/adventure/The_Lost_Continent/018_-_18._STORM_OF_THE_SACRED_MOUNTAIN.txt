The clamor of battle reached us even in the quiet of Nais’ sickroom. Her eyes fluttered open, weak but alert. “What is that sound?” she murmured.

I knelt beside her, smoothing the damp hair from her brow. “Atlantis makes war upon itself,” I said. “Phorenice’s army storms the Sacred Mountain.”

A faint smile touched her lips. “And you—you are King now?”

“By the will of the Priests, yes. Though the crown feels lighter than the duty.”

She reached for my hand, her fingers cool and fragile. “You should not have risked so much for me.”

“Atlantis will endure,” I said. “But you, Nais—you are irreplaceable.”

A blush warmed her pallor. “And you, my lord, have grown handsomer without the beard.”

I laughed, but the thunder of the assault outside pressed urgency upon me. “Rest now. I must see to our defenses.”

Her grip tightened briefly before she let me go. “Come back to me.”

***

The mountain’s defenses were crumbling. Phorenice’s forces, driven by her fury, had breached gate after gate, their bodies piling high in the narrow pass. Our Priests hurled boulders down, crushing whole companies, but for every wave of attackers slain, another surged forward.

I found Tatho leading them, his face grim beneath his helmet.

“Deucalion!” he called up, mocking. “A ghost from the past.”

“A ghost who still breathes,” I shot back. “Call off this madness, Tatho. The Mountain’s secrets are not meant for her hands.”

He shook his head. “You know I serve Phorenice above all. Even old friendship won’t stay me.”

“Then you march these men to their deaths for nothing.”

“They die for her glory,” he said, and raised his sword.

The rocks we loosed crushed another charge, but the cost was brutal. Our own numbers dwindled with every arrow or fire-tube dart that found its mark. The defense was unsustainable.

A desperate plan took shape. If the pass itself could be destroyed…

I ordered the retreat, pulling our surviving Priests back to the highest gate. Behind us, we shattered the ancient pathway—balustrades, statues, the very stones of the road—sending it all crashing into the abyss. The last slender ledge trembled beneath my feet as I crossed, then collapsed into the void.

The way to the Mountain was severed.

For now.

***

Phorenice, ever ingenious, was not so easily thwarted. By dusk, her engineers swarmed below, assembling some monstrous device. Timber and metal gleamed in the fading light as they pieced together a bridge or a siege tower—something to span the chasm we had carved.

Zaemon remained locked within the Ark, communing with powers beyond my grasp. I could only watch and wait, the weight of kingship heavy upon me.

And through it all, one thought anchored me: Nais, waiting in the quiet of her chamber, her trust like a beacon in the storm.

The battle was far from over. But neither was hope.
