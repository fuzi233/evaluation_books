As the afternoon sun cast its warm glow through the windows, Dr. Donaldson made his way to the Hale residence, his footsteps echoing through the quiet streets. It was his first visit since the family's routine had been disrupted by the illness that had taken hold of Mrs. Hale. Margaret, ever vigilant, stood poised at the threshold of her mother's bedroom, her ears straining to catch the faintest whisper of conversation within. Her own habits of intimacy with her mother had long been a comfort, but now she found herself shut out, relegated to the role of anxious observer.

As she paced the narrow corridor, the soft creaking of the floorboards beneath her feet seemed to mock her, a reminder of the secrets kept hidden behind the closed door. Every so often, she stopped to listen, convinced she detected the faint sound of a moan, and her hands clenched into fists as she struggled to contain her growing unease. Time seemed to stretch out before her like a canvas, each tick of the clock marking the slow passage of moments that felt like hours.

Just as she thought she heard the rustle of fabric and the muffled murmur of voices, the sounds ceased, and the silence that followed was oppressive, heavy with foreboding. When the door finally swung open, Margaret emerged, her eyes flashing with determination as she addressed the doctor.

"My father is away, attending to his pupil," she explained, her voice steady despite the turmoil brewing within her. "Please, come into his study downstairs." As she led the way, she couldn't help but notice the wary glance cast her way by Dixon, the family's loyal but long-serving servant. Margaret's gaze met hers, and for an instant, a flicker of amusement danced across her features, a fleeting respite from the weight of her concerns.

With a deep breath, she composed herself, adopting the demeanor of the elder sibling, a role she had long assumed in her mother's absence. As they descended the stairs, the familiar contours of the house seemed to recede, leaving only the pressing question that hung between them like a challenge: what news did the doctor bring?

In the study, Margaret's composure faltered, and her voice cracked as she demanded the truth. "What is the matter with Mother?" she asked, her words tumbling out in a rush.
