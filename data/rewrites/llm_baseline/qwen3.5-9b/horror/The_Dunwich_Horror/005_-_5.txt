The winter that followed brought an occurrence scarcely less bizarre than Wilbur's initial journey beyond the Dunwich borders. His correspondence with the Widener Library at Harvard, the Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris, the British Museum, the University of Buenos Aires, and the Library of Miskatotic University in Arkham had yielded nothing but silence regarding the loan of the tome he craved. Desperate, he eventually departed in person—shabby, filthy, bearded, and speaking with a rough, uncouth accent—to seek the copy held at Miskatonic, the institution closest to him geographically.

Standing nearly eight feet tall and dragging a cheap, new valise purchased from Osborn's general store, this dark, goatish gargoyle materialized one day in Arkham seeking the forbidden volume secured behind locks and keys within the college library: the abominable *Necronomicon* of the mad Arab Alhazred, rendered in Latin by Olaus Wormius and printed in Spain during the seventeenth century. Having never witnessed a city before, Wilbur possessed no other thought than to locate the university grounds. Indeed, he marched past the great white-fanged watchdog, which barked with unnatural fury and malice while tugging frantically against its heavy chain, paying the beast no mind whatsoever.

Accompanying him was the priceless yet flawed manuscript of Dr. Dee's English translation, bequeathed by his grandfather. Upon gaining access to the Latin original, Wilbur immediately commenced a collation of the two texts, intent on locating a specific passage missing from the seven hundred and fifty-first page of his damaged volume. He could not help but mention this task to the librarian—the learned Henry Armitage (A.M., Miskatonic; Ph.D., Princeton; Litt.D., Johns Hopkins), who had previously visited the farm and now politely interrogated him. Wilbur admitted he sought a particular formula or incantation containing the terrifying name *Yog-Sothoth*. It baffled him to encounter discrepancies, duplications, and ambiguities that rendered the identification process exceedingly difficult.

As he copied the chosen formula, Dr. Armitage involuntarily glanced over his shoulder at the open pages. The left-hand page of the Latin edition displayed monstrous threats against the peace and sanity of the world. Nor should it be assumed that humanity holds the title of Earth's oldest or final master, nor that the common mass of life and substance exists in isolation. The Old Ones exist; they always have, and they ever shall. They do not traverse the spaces we comprehend, but dwell *between* them. They move serenely and primally, undimensioned and invisible to our eyes. *Yog-Sothoth* knows the gate. *Yog-Sothoth* is the gate. *Yog-Sothoth* is the key and guardian of the gate. Past, present, and future are unified within *Yog-Sothoth*. He knows where the Old Ones breached the barrier in ages gone by, and where they will breach it anew. He knows the paths they have trodden across Earth's fields, where they tread still, and why no mortal eye can behold them in their fullness. Men may sometimes perceive their approach by scent, though no one can discern their appearance except in the distorted features of those whom they have impregnated with mankind; and among these offspring are countless varieties, ranging from shapes resembling man's truest image to forms devoid of sight or substance entirely. They wander unseen and foul in desolate places where their Words have been spoken and their Rites howled forth at their appointed seasons. The wind gibbers with their voices, and the earth mutters with their consciousness. They bow down forests and crush cities, yet neither forest nor city can witness the hand that strikes. Kadath in the cold wastes has known them, and what does any man truly know of Kadath? The ice deserts of the South and the sunken islands of the Ocean hold stones engraved with their seals, but who has beheld the deep-frozen city or the sealed tower draped long ago in seaweed and barnacles? Great Cthulhu is their cousin, yet he can spy them only dimly. *Iä Shub-Niggurath!* Thus shall you know them. Their hand rests upon your throat, yet you see them not; and their dwelling place is identical to your guarded threshold. *Yog-Sothoth* is the key to the gate through which the spheres converge. Man rules now where they once ruled; soon they shall rule where man currently reigns. After summer comes winter, and after winter, summer. They wait patiently and potently, for here they shall reign again.

Dr. Armitage, connecting the text he read with his knowledge of Dunwich and its brooding presences, alongside accounts of Wilbur Whateley and his dim, hideous aura stretching from a dubious birth to a cloud of probable patricide, felt a surge of terror as tangible as the damp chill of a tomb. The bent, goatish giant before him appeared to be the spawn of another planet or dimension; something only partially human, linked to black gulfs of essence and entity stretching like titanic phantasms beyond all spheres of force, matter, space, and time.

Presently, Wilbur raised his head and began speaking in that strange, resonant manner, hinting at vocal organs unlike those of ordinary mankind.

"Mr. Armitage," he said, "I calculate I must take that book home. There are things within it I must attempt under special conditions I cannot achieve here, and it would be a mortal sin to allow bureaucratic rules to hinder me. Permit me to take it along, sir, and I swear no one will notice the difference. I don't need to assure you I will care for it well. It wasn't my doing that placed this Dee copy in its current state..."

He paused, sensing the firm denial on the librarian's face, and his own goatish features shifted into a crafty expression. Armitage, half-ready to suggest copying the necessary sections, suddenly recoiled at the potential consequences. The responsibility of granting such a being access to such blasphemous outer realms was too great. Whateley perceived the situation and attempted to respond lightly. "Well, all right, if you feel that way about it. Perhaps Harvard won't be so fussy as you are." Without further comment, he rose and strode out of the building, stooping low at every doorway.

Armitage heard the savage yelping of the great watchdog and studied Whateley's gorilla-like lope as he crossed the small section of campus visible from the window. Reflecting on the wild tales he had heard and recalling the old Sunday stories in the *Advertiser*, he remembered the lore gathered from Dunwich rustics and villagers during his single visit there. Unseen entities, not of Earth—or at least not of three-dimensional Earth—rushed fetid and horrific through New England's glens, brooking obscenely atop the mountain peaks. Of this, he had long been certain. Now, however, he sensed the close proximity of some terrible fragment of the intruding horror and glimpsed a hellish advance within the black dominion of the ancient, once-passive nightmare.

With a shudder of disgust, Armitage locked away the *Necronomicon*, yet the room remained permeated by an unholy, unidentifiable stench. "As a foulness shall ye know them," he quoted. Yes—the odor was identical to that which had sickened him at the Whateley farmhouse less than three years prior. Thinking of Wilbur, goatish and ominous, he once again mocked the village rumors concerning his parentage. "Inbreeding?" Armitage muttered half-aloud to himself. "Great God, what simpletons! Show them Arthur Machen's *The Great God Pan* and they'll deem it a common Dunwich scandal! But what creature—what cursed, shapeless influence on or off this three-dimensional Earth—was Wilbur Whateley's father? Born on Candlemas, nine months after May Eve of 1912, when whispers of strange earth noises reached clear to Arkham... what walked upon those mountains that May night? What Roodmas horror fastened itself upon the world in half-human flesh and blood?"

During the ensuing weeks, Dr. Armitage dedicated himself to gathering all available data on Wilbur Whateley and the formless presences surrounding Dunwich. He contacted Dr. Houghton of Aylesbury, who had attended Old Whateley in his final illness, finding much to ponder in the grandfather's last words as recounted by the physician. A visit to Dunwich Village yielded little new information; however, a close examination of the *Necronomicon*, specifically in the sections Wilbur had sought so avidly, seemed to provide new and terrible clues regarding the nature, methods, and desires of the strange evil vaguely threatening this planet. Conversations with several students of archaic lore in Boston and letters sent to numerous others elsewhere instilled in him a growing amazement that slowly progressed through varying degrees of alarm until reaching a state of acute spiritual dread.

As summer advanced, he felt dimly that something must be done regarding the lurking terrors of the upper Miskatonic valley and the monstrous entity known to the human world as Wilbur Whateley.
