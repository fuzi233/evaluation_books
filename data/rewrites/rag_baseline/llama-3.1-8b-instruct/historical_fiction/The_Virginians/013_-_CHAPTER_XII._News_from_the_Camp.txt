The silence within Castlewood's halls felt oppressive, a heavy blanket draped over the space where laughter and music once lingered. Harry Warrington wandered through the rooms, his footsteps echoing off the walls as he searched for a distraction from the ache in his chest. His brother's absence weighed heavily on him, a constant reminder of the life he'd left behind. He paused in front of George's empty chamber, the air thick with memories of the conversations they'd shared, the stories they'd swapped, and the dreams they'd forged together.

Madam Warrington's voice carried from the adjacent room, a steady beat of conversation that seemed to mock Harry's melancholy. She spoke of George with a warmth that bordered on obsession, as if her son's return was a certainty rather than a distant hope. Her words were laced with a quiet desperation, a testament to the fear that gripped her heart. Harry's face remained impassive, a mask that hid the turmoil brewing beneath.

As he made his way to the dining hall, the scent of roasting meat wafted through the air, a fleeting comfort that couldn't pierce the veil of sorrow. He took his seat at the head of the table, his eyes scanning the familiar faces of his family and the servants. Madam Warrington raised her glass in a toast, her voice steady as she proclaimed, "To the King!" The words fell flat, a hollow echo in the silence that followed.

Harry's gaze drifted to the letter on the table before him, the words of his brother's latest missive etched on the page like a map of longing. He read the words aloud, his voice a monotone recitation of George's experiences in the army. The family listened intently, their faces a study in contrasts – Madam Warrington's stoic calm, Mrs. Mountain's tear-stained cheeks, and the servants' somber expressions. As Harry finished reading, the room fell silent once more, the only sound the ticking of the clock in the hallway.
