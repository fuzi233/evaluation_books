The soft glow of candles illuminated the ornate chandeliers within the Academy of Music, casting a warm ambiance over the assembled crowd on that January evening of the early seventies. Christine Nilsson's voice soared above the whispers and rustling of silks as she took to the stage, her performance of Faust a testament to her remarkable talent. Though whispers of a new Opera House were circulating among the city's elite, the world of high society remained enamored with the Academy's intimate setting and exceptional acoustics.

As the curtain rose on the garden scene, Newland Archer slipped into his seat at the back of the club box, his arrival coinciding with the prima donna's poignant rendition of "He loves me—she loves me not." Her voice wove a spell, notes as delicate as the petals of a flower dancing in the air. The audience was entranced, their faces bathed in the golden light of the opera house.

Newland's delayed entrance was a testament to his refined sensibilities. He had savored the anticipation of this moment, much like a connoisseur savoring a fine wine. His pleasure was not merely in the experience itself, but in the expectation, the promise of something exquisite to come. As he settled into his seat, the music swelled, and Christine Nilsson's voice soared, her words a gentle caress on the ears of the enthralled audience.

The conventions of his world governed Newland's actions, from the precise manner in which he parted his hair to the carefully selected flower adorning his lapel. Yet, in this moment, it was not these trifles that held his attention, but the pure, unadulterated beauty of the music. The prima donna's voice danced across the opera house, a masterful blend of drama and passion that left the audience breathless. As she reached the crescendo, her final phrase echoing through the hall, Newland felt the world slow its pace, and for a fleeting instant, time stood still.
