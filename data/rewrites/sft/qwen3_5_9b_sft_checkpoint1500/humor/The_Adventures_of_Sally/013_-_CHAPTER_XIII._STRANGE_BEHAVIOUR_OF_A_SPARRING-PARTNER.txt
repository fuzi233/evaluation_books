Sally sat in her apartment on the morning of her return to New York, vibrating with a tension that mirrored a swimmer hesitating at the edge of a freezing pool. She ached, yes, but the ache was a sharp, stimulating fire rather than a dull, smothering melancholy. She had finally stopped wavering on the brink and plunged into the mid-stream of life, ready for whatever wreckage awaited her.

Her immediate destination was the office of Fillmore Nicholas Theatrical Enterprises Ltd., specifically to locate Ginger. She had developed an urgent, desperate need to see him. His stolid, unflappable friendliness offered the kind of emotional ballast she desperately required after the terrifying isolation she had felt at the docks. The customs sheds, with their echoing emptiness, had drained her courage; she needed to feel grounded again.

Checking her watch, she noted the surprising lateness of the hour. She could easily catch Ginger at the office and drag him out for lunch. Putting on her hat, she stepped out into the city.

The restless tide of change in New York had already washed over the outer office, replacing the friendly, pimpled clerk she had known previously with a new recruit. This stripling was also generously pimpled, but his demeanor was a study in grim suspicion. He peered at Sally with narrowed eyes, as if expecting her to steal the office blotting-pad, and demanded her business with an acerbity that suggested he was trying to vent his frustrations on her.

"I want Mr. Kemp," Sally said firmly.

The boy scratched his cheek with a ruler, his expression so austere that no one would have suspected he had been juggling paper weights on his chin moments before. "What name?" he asked coldly.

"Nicholas," Sally replied. "I am Mr. Nicholas' sister."

On a previous visit, this declaration had resulted in chaos, but today it worked like magic. It struck the boy like a bullet. He dropped the ruler, convulsively startled, then scrambled to recover it. The shock of realizing he was dealing with the boss's sister instantly deflated his peevishness. His attitude shifted from hostile to politely subservient, as if the sun had burst through a London fog.

"Will you take a seat, lady?" he asked, offering a smile so winning it felt artificial, and dusting her sleeve with his coat. "Mr. Nicholas is out, miss. He's back in New York, but he's gone out."

"I don't want Mr. Nicholas," Sally corrected. "I want Mr. Kemp."

"Sorrow at my inability to help shone from every feature on the boy's face. 'Don't know of anyone of that name around here,' he apologized. 'Does he look like anything?'

"He has very red hair," Sally supplied.

"Never seen him in here," the boy confirmed. The truth chilled Sally. Blaming herself for leaving England, she realized Ginger must have made a catastrophic mess of things. Surely Fillmore wouldn't have dismissed a protegé she had personally protected unless it was truly hopeless.

"Where is Mr. Nicholas?" she asked, needing answers.

"Really out, miss," the boy said, regaining his social chitchat mode. "He went off to White Plains in his automobile half an hour ago. I guess he's gone up to take a look at Bugs Butler."

"Bugs Butler?" Sally frowned. "Who is Bugs Butler?"

The boy's demeanor darkened. "Who *is* he!" he exclaimed, abandoning his polite facade. "That's what everyone's talking about. Who has Bugs Butler ever licked? A lot of stiffs on the coast, that's all. Except Cyclone Mullins, and it took fifteen rounds to get a decision over him. The boss wants to give him a shot at the champ, but I'd bet my two bucks in my vest pocket that he can't last six rounds with Lew Lucas."

Sally felt a glimmer of understanding. "Oh, Bugs—Mr. Butler is one of the boxers in this fight my brother is managing?"

"That's right," the boy nodded eagerly. "Lew Lucas is the lightweight champ. He's a bird! Used to live next door to me. I've seen his mother a hundred times. Is any stiff like Bugs going to beat a guy like that?"

"It doesn't seem likely," Sally admitted.

"You spoke it!" the boy cheered, batting at a fly. "Can he make a hundred and thirty-five pounds ringside without looking weak?"

"It sounds awfully difficult."

"They say he's clever," the boy scoffed. "But the poor fish can't punch a hole in a nut-sundae."

Sally stood up, absorbing the chatter. "How shall I find my brother when I get to White Plains?"

"Oh, anyone'll show you the way to the training camp. Hurry, there's a train you can make now."

Inside the training camp, a large hall resembling a cross between a barn and a gymnasium, Fillmore Nicholas was just beginning to breathe a sigh of relief. A crowd of plutocrats and journalists surrounded the roped-off ring, eager to witness the final preparations of Bugs Butler before his upcoming bout. Fillmore had been cornered by Lester Burrowes, the manager, only minutes prior, and was finally free to slip away to the hotel for food—a luxury he sorely needed after skipping lunch in New York.

As he approached the heavy door, he froze. Standing outside was Sally.

"Hallo, Fillmore!" she called softly.

The effect was instantaneous and catastrophic. Fillmore leaped three inches into the air, his composure shattered like glass. He struggled for breath, his lips dry, staring at her with wide, panicked eyes. How could she be here? She had left England only weeks ago, intending to stay at Monk's Crofton. Yet here she was, in the heart of a boxing camp, having bypassed all social protocol.

"Sally!" he croaked, a mixture of awe and terror. "What... what..."

"Did I startle you? I'm sorry."

"What are you doing here? Why aren't you at Monk's Crofton?" Sally glanced nervously at the ring, where a figure in a bathrobe was stretching. "I decided I wanted to get back to America. Circumstances arose which made it pleasanter to leave."

"Do you mean to say..." Fillmore stammered, "that Carmyle proposed to you and you turned him down?"

Sally flushed crimson. "I don't think it's particularly nice to talk about that sort of thing, but... yes."

Desolation washed over Fillmore. The perfect arrangement—the wealthy, quiet, aristocratic Bruce Carmyle marrying Sally, securing a future for both families—it all collapsed because of her caprice. "But why?" he pleaded.

"Oh, Fill!" Sally sighed, sensing the rasp of his grief. "I should have thought the reason was obvious."

"You mean you don't like him?"

"I don't know whether I do or not. I certainly don't like him enough to marry him."

Before Fillmore could process the betrayal, Mr. Lester Burrowes arrived, clutching his tweed jacket and a brown bowler hat. "Say, Mr. Nicholas, you ain't going'? Bugs is just getting ready to spar."

"My sister," Fillmore stammered, pointing to Sally. "Miss Nicholas."

"Pleased to meecher," Burrowes grinned, ignoring Fillmore's distress. "Say, you've never seen this boy of mine when he was feeling right. He's a wizard! A Hindoo!"

Before Fillmore could escape, Sally intervened. "Can he make a hundred and thirty-five ringside without being weak?" she asked.

Burrowes froze, his mouth opening in a dumbfounded O. The question, simple yet technical, stunned him. He had never expected Sally to understand boxing mechanics. He gaped at her, forgetting Fillmore entirely.

"It took him fifteen rounds to get a referee's decision over Cyclone Mullins," Sally recited seriously. "And K-leg Binns..."

Burrowes rallied, protesting the details, and eventually produced a photograph of Bugs Butler. "Take a slant at that and tell me if he don't look the goods."

A young man in a bathrobe emerged, seeing Sally, and quickened his pace. Burrowes introduced them. "Bugs, this is Miss Nicholas, come to see you work out."

Sally examined the real-life version of the photo. He was a singularly repellent young man, with a cruel curve to his lips and a cold, arrogant eye. He smirked at her, a dangerous and sinister gesture. However, she forced a smile. "I hope you are going to win, Mr. Butler."

"You betcher," he asserted, his chest swelling beneath the robe.

As the manager ushered in his sparring partner, a tall, red-haired young man named Reddy, Sally watched with a sinking heart. When Reddy pulled off his sweater, revealing a head of tousled red hair, Sally gasped. It was Ginger Kemp.

Ginger started awake from his stupor, embarrassment stamped on his agreeable features. Sally plucked at Burrowes' elbow. "Who—is—that?" she whispered.

"Just one of Bugs' sparring partners," Burrowes muttered, hurrying to position the fighters.
