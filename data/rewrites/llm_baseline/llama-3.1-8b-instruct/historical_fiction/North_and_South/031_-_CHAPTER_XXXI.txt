As the grey October mist swirled around the town, shrouding its streets in a damp, dismal veil, Margaret Hale moved listlessly through the rooms of their Milton home. Her eyes, red-rimmed from lack of sleep and overwhelmed by sorrow, struggled to stay dry as she helped Dixon with the morning's tasks. The weight of responsibility rested squarely on her shoulders – her father and brother, lost in their grief, required her steady presence and practical support.

In a desperate bid to maintain a semblance of normalcy, Margaret focused on the mundane routines of the household. The fire crackled, casting flickering shadows on the walls as she busied herself preparing breakfast. Yet, every glance around the room only served to heighten the contrast between the tranquil scene and her own turbulent emotions. As she stood by the sofa, attempting to compose herself, Dixon's gentle touch on her shoulder brought her back to the present.

"Come, Miss Hale," Dixon whispered, her voice laced with an uncharacteristic warmth. "You mustn't let your sorrow consume you. We need your strength now more than ever. Who will manage the funeral arrangements? Who will attend? Where will we hold it? And Master Frederick is beside himself with grief. Your father, God bless him, is lost without your mother. We can't afford to lose you too."

Margaret's heart swelled with a mix of gratitude and despair. Perhaps Dixon's words were meant to offer comfort, but they only served to underscore the magnitude of their loss. Yet, the servant's unusual display of tenderness struck a chord within her. Summoning a faint smile, Margaret patted Dixon's hand and rose to announce breakfast to her father and brother.

Mr. Hale entered the room as if in a daze, his eyes clouded by a haze of sorrow. Frederick followed, his face flushed with forced cheer, and flung his arms around Margaret, bursting into tears. Throughout the meal, Margaret struggled to steer the conversation towards safer topics, lest the weight of their collective grief become too much to bear.

After breakfast, Margaret decided to tackle the funeral arrangements with her father. However, he merely nodded listlessly, agreeing to her proposals without offering any real guidance. As she left the room to confer with Dixon, Mr. Hale called her back, his voice barely above a whisper.
