As the chill of winter began to seep into the air, Mrs. Jennings's thoughts turned to her own home in the quiet streets near Portman Square, a place she had called her own since her husband's passing. A successful merchant, he had left her with a comfortable legacy, which she now cherished as she spent her winters surrounded by the comforts of her London townhouse. As January approached, she found herself thinking longingly of the familiar routines and rituals that filled her days: the scent of roasting meats wafting from the kitchen, the warmth of the fire crackling in the hearth, and the sound of traditional folk music drifting from the parlour.

It was during one of these nostalgic reveries that Mrs. Jennings suddenly turned her thoughts to the Dashwood sisters, whose company she had grown fond of during her visits to their country estate. Without warning, she extended an invitation to Elinor and Marianne to join her in London for the winter season, her words tumbling forth with a warmth that belied the reserve she often wore in polite society. Elinor, perceiving the eagerness beneath her sister's hesitant expression, responded promptly on behalf of both, citing their mother's need for their care during the cold months ahead.

Mrs. Jennings received the refusal with a mixture of surprise and good humour, reiterating her invitation with a promise to make their stay as comfortable as possible. She envisioned the three of them sharing meals and laughter around the dinner table, just as she had done with her own daughters, Charlotte and Fanny, when they were young. Her eyes sparkled as she pictured the prospect of hosting the sisters, and she couldn't help but tease them gently about the potential benefits of being under her wing – the connections she might make for them among the city's eligible bachelors, the introductions she could arrange to further their social standing.

Sir John Middleton, who had been watching the exchange with interest, chimed in with his own thoughts on the matter. He suggested that Marianne, in particular, would find the trip to town a welcome respite from the quiet of the countryside, and that Elinor's presence would only add to her sister's enjoyment. Mrs. Jennings laughed good-naturedly at the idea of the two sisters growing tired of her company and finding amusement in each other's company behind her back.
