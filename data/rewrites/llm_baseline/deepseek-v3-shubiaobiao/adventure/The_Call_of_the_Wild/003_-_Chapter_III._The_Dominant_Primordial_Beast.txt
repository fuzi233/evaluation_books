The wild spirit within Buck grew stronger with every mile, forged by the brutal demands of the trail. Yet he wore this newfound ferocity like a hidden blade—controlled, calculating. Survival had sharpened his instincts; he avoided conflict where he could, biding his time. But between him and Spitz, the lead dog, there simmered a hatred too deep for words.

Spitz, sensing a rival, bared his teeth at every chance. He goaded Buck, hungry for the fight that would end with one of them dead. It nearly came to blows one bitter night on the shores of Lake Le Barge, where the wind carved through them like a knife. Exhausted, they camped on the frozen lake itself, backs to a sheer cliff.

Buck had burrowed a warm nest beneath an overhang—until he returned from his meager meal to find Spitz curled in his spot. A snarl ripped from his throat. No more retreating. He lunged, the force of his attack shocking even himself. Spitz reeled, unprepared for the fury of a dog he'd dismissed as soft.

François whooped as the two clashed, but before the fight could be decided, chaos erupted. Starving huskies—dozens of them—swarmed the camp, drawn by the scent of food. Gaunt, desperate, they were more wolf than dog. The team scattered under their assault. Buck found himself flanked by three at once, teeth tearing into his shoulders. Blood slicked the snow.

Pandemonium ruled. Dave and Sol-leks fought back-to-back; Joe snapped like a demon, crushing bone. Buck wrenched free from one attacker, only to feel Spitz’s fangs sink into his throat—treacherous, cowardly. A club swung, yelps filled the air, and the invaders fled—but not before half their supplies were gone, the sled’s harnesses chewed to ruin.

The team limped away, battered. Dolly’s throat was torn; Joe lost an eye. Yet the trail spared no one. They pushed on through the Thirty Mile River’s treacherous ice, where every step risked drowning. Perrault, unshaken, led them through the frozen jaws of death.

Buck’s paws, unhardened by generations of wilderness, left him hobbling. François carved his own moccasins into boots for him, and Buck repaid the kindness with stubborn charm—refusing to move one morning until they were laced on.

Then madness struck. Dolly, quiet until then, howled like a wolf and charged Buck in a frenzy. He fled, her frothing jaws a breath behind, until François’s axe ended her. Spitz seized the moment, ripping into Buck’s weakened body—until the lash sent him sprawling.

**"Spitz is a devil,"** Perrault muttered. **"One day, he’ll kill Buck."**

François grinned. **"No. Buck is two devils. Watch—when he snaps, he’ll eat Spitz whole."**

The rebellion had begun. Buck undermined Spitz at every turn—protecting shirkers, stealing his authority. The team unraveled, defiance spreading like a sickness. Even gentle Billee grew bold.

Then came the chase. A snowshoe hare sent the dogs into a frenzy, Buck at the lead, the ancient thrill of the hunt singing in his blood. This was life at its peak—the raw joy of pursuit, of teeth meeting warm flesh. But Spitz, ever cunning, cut the hare off at a bend. His killing strike drew a shriek, and the pack howled in savage triumph.

The message was clear: only one of them would lead. The final fight loomed, inevitable as the frozen dawn.
