Throughout the preceding chapters, I have operated under a premise that ought to have been declared as the first law of our existence: every soul in Flatland is born a Regular Figure. By this, I mean that a Woman is not merely a line, but a perfectly straight one; an Artisan or Soldier possesses two sides of exact equality; a Tradesman boasts three; and a Lawyer, to whom I humbly belong, commands four. In every Polygon, the sides must stand in strict concordance. While the magnitude of these sides fluctuates with age—a newborn female measuring scarcely an inch, whereas a mature woman may stretch to a foot—their equality remains immutable. For the males of our various orders, the sum of an adult's side lengths approximates two feet. Yet, it is not the scale of our dimensions that defines our society, but the mathematical perfection of their symmetry.

Nature herself decrees that all figures possess equal sides, for without this divine mandate, our angles would fracture into chaos. Imagine a world where a single glance cannot reveal a man's station; instead, one would be compelled to trace every angle through the painful art of Feeling. Life, already fleeting, would succumb to such tedious scrutiny. The entire edifice of Sight Recognition would crumble; the skill of Feeling would vanish; social intercourse would descend into perilous uncertainty. Trust, foresight, and the simplest arrangements of daily life would evaporate, plunging civilization back into a state of barbaric suspicion. Do I rush too quickly to these inevitable truths? A mere moment's reflection, coupled with a single observation from our streets, must convince any rational mind that our social fabric is woven entirely from the thread of Regularity.

Consider the Tradesmen you encounter on the thoroughfare. You identify them instantly by their obtuse angles and slightly dimmed sides, inviting them into your home for luncheon with absolute assurance. This confidence relies on the fact that every adult Triangle occupies a predictable, measured space. Now, envision a nightmare scenario: suppose one of these men dragged behind his respectable vertex a jagged parallelogram, twelve or thirteen inches in diagonal. What would you do with such a geometric monster wedged firmly in your doorway? The measurement of a single angle would no longer suffice; your entire existence would be consumed by the frantic surveying of perimeters. Even now, navigating a crowded street taxes the intellect of the most educated Square. But if no citizen could calculate the Regularity of those around them, chaos would reign supreme. Panic would turn lethal, especially if Women or Soldiers were present, resulting in catastrophic loss of life. Thus, expediency aligns with Nature, both sealing their approval upon the sanctity of Regular Conformation. The Law, ever vigilant, supports this natural order.

In Flatland, "Irregularity of Figure" carries the weight of moral obliquity and criminal intent. Though some radical voices argue that geometric irregularity bears no necessary connection to ethical failings, their arguments ring hollow. They claim the Irregular is cast aside from birth—scorned by kin, ignored by servants, and distrusted by society. Watched jealously by the police until adulthood, he faces a grim choice: destruction if his deviation exceeds the legal margin, or imprisonment in a government office as a seventh-class clerk, barred from marriage, condemned to a drudgery of miserable pay, and subjected to constant surveillance even during his holidays. One can easily understand how such an environment breeds bitterness and perversion in the human heart. Yet, this plausible reasoning fails to sway the wisdom of our statesmen, who rightly hold that tolerating Irregularity is incompatible with the safety of the State.

Undoubtedly, the life of an Irregular is harsh, but the welfare of the Many demands such severity. If we permitted a man with a triangular front and a polygonal back to propagate a lineage of increasing deformity, what fate awaits our cities? Would our houses, doors, and churches be dismantled to accommodate such grotesque anomalies? Must our ticket collectors measure every perimeter before granting entry to theaters or lecture halls? Should an Irregular be exempt from military service? If not, how do we prevent him from sowing desolation among his comrades? Furthermore, consider the irresistible lure of fraud. How effortlessly could such a creature enter a shop with his polygonal facade forward, ordering goods ad infinitum from a trusting merchant? Despite the pleas of false philanthropists to abolish these penal laws, I have never encountered an Irregular who did not embody Nature's intended design for such beings: a hypocrite, a misanthrope, and a perpetrator of mischief within the bounds of his capability.

This does not justify the extreme measures taken in some nations, where infants deviating by half a degree are summarily destroyed. Indeed, some of our greatest geniuses suffered deviations exceeding forty-five minutes in their infancy; their premature deaths would have been an incalculable loss to the State. Moreover, the medical arts have secured glorious victories through compressions, extensions, trepanning, and dietary regimens that have partially or wholly corrected these defects. Therefore, advocating a *Via Media*, I propose no fixed line of absolute exclusion. However, at the precise moment when the frame begins to set and the Medical Board deems recovery improbable, I suggest that the Irregular offspring be painlessly and mercifully consumed, sparing the State further corruption and the child a life of suffering.
