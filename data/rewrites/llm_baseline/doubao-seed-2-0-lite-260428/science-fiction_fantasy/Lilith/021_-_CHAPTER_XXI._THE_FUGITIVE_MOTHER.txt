The fraying silver of this otherworld’s moon lit my path as I hurried, until a tattered bank of storm cloud swallowed it whole. Out of the sudden ash-gray dark, a pale shape burst forth: a woman in a shift bleached bone-white by constant exposure to the strange night, a swaddled infant clamped tight to her chest, hunched low as she ran. Her path ran parallel to my own, and she never spotted me, every line of her body thrumming with the raw, driven speed of one hunted past the edge of endurance, terror sharp enough to taste hanging thick around her.
“Something’s chasing her,” I breathed to myself. “One of the prowling horrors of this accursed night is at her heels.”
I knew calling out would only send her spiraling into worse panic. Instead, I stepped square into the wake of her footprints, planting myself to block whatever came after. For a heartbeat I watched her recede into the dim, then a soft, lightning-fast rush sounded behind me—before I could twist around, something bounded clean over my head, a hard heavy paw striking my forehead with a crack that sent me sprawling.
I was on my feet before my gown could even settle, blood trickling warm down my brow, but all I caught of my attacker was a flash of vanishing white slipping through the mist. I gave chase, the sticky blood crusting on my skin, but I had barely cleared half a dozen paces when a shriek tore through the night so raw and ragged it made the very air quiver. I ran faster, my gut tight with the certainty I was already too late.
A minute later, a low white shape came lurching toward me through moonlight dusted with fine vapour. For a second I thought it was a second hunter beast, moving slow, half crawling, in lurching, floundering leaps like a creature broken and in agony. I stepped off the path to wait, hand curling around the hilt of the small knife I carried.
When it drew near, I saw it was moving on three legs, its left forepaw held high, cradled away from the grass. Its pelt was shining bone-white patterned with dark oval spots, and a strange low rushing hum hung around it, like the sound of spring water spilling over long grass. As it hobbled past, I saw dark warm blood streaming from its lifted paw.
“Someone faster and braver than me wounded it,” I thought. But unaccountable pity welled up in my chest so sharp it ached—even if I’d held a double-bladed axe in both hands, I knew I could not have struck the suffering thing. It kept leaping in broken, hobbling bursts until it vanished into the dark, its blood still flowing in a thin soft torrent that seeped back through the grass right at my feet.
“At that rate, it’ll bleed out long before it finds another target,” I reasoned, and kept moving. The woman might still need help, and I wanted to find whoever had stepped in to defend her.
I spotted her not far off, seated on the grass, the child curled safe in her lap.
“Can I help you at all?” I called out soft and low.
She jolted so hard at the sound of my voice she nearly tipped upright to run. I dropped immediately to the ground, hands up where she could see them even through the dim. “You’ve nothing to fear from me. I was chasing the beast, and was so glad to see you’d found a protector faster than me! It just passed me by with its paw pouring blood—by now it’s nearly dead, for certain.”
“There’s little hope of that,” she said, her voice shaking so hard the words stuttered. “Do you not know who that beast belongs to?”
I’d had odd, creeping suspicions, but I told her I knew nothing of the brute, and asked where her mysterious champion had gone.
“What champion?” she said, confused. “I saw no one else at all.”
“Then how did you hurt the monster so badly?”
“I bashed its paw in with a sharp flint—hit it as hard as I could manage. Didn’t you hear it scream?”
“I thought that scream was yours!” I said, stunned. “You are the bravest woman I’ve ever met.”
“That was the leopardess,” she said. “I’ve never heard a beast make a sound like that, it was exactly the scream of a woman being tortured. My throat was frozen tight with fear, I couldn’t have made a sound even to save my baby. When I saw that horrid mouth inches from my darling’s soft little neck, I grabbed the first heavy stone I could reach and smashed her lame foot right in.”
“Tell me about the creature,” I said. “I’m a stranger to this land, I don’t know its ways.”
“You’ll learn fast enough if you’re heading for Bulika,” she warned. “I can never go back there, not ever.”
“I am heading to Bulika,” I told her. “I’m going to see the princess.”
“Steer clear. You’d do best to turn back now—though wait, perhaps you’re—no. They say the princess is a good, kind woman.”
I heard a small shift of fabric, and the moon was so swaddled in thick storm clouds by then I could barely make out the outline of her shape. I thought she was about to run from me again.
“I mean you no harm at all,” I said quickly. “What oath can I swear to make you believe me?”
“I can tell from the way you speak you’re not from Bulika’s people,” she said. “I’ll trust you. I’m not one of them either, else I’d never be able to trust anyone—they never trust a single soul. I wish I could see your face, but I like your voice. There, my little one is fast asleep. That foul beast never touched her. It was my baby she was hunting, right from the start. She would have torn me to pieces the second she got her jaws on the child, for daring to run off with it.” She stroked the baby’s soft hair as she spoke. “Some say the princess keeps two white leopardesses. I only ever seen this spotted one. Everyone in Bulika knows her. The second the princess hears word of a new baby born, she sends the beast straight to drink its blood. The child either dies right after, or grows up to wander the hills, empty and witless. I tried to run away with my baby before, but the princess was off on a journey, so I thought I could wait a little longer until I was strong enough for the road. But she must have taken the beast with her on her trip, and came riding back the very hour I left. She caught my trail fast. I heard that snuffling sniff-snuff right behind me, and I ran. Oh, how I ran. But my baby will not die. There is not a single mark on her skin.”
“Where are you taking her?” I asked.
“Somewhere no one from Bulika will ever find us,” she said.
“Why is the princess so cruel?”
“There’s an old prophecy that a child will be the death of her. That’s why they say she will never take a husband, no matter the offer.”
“But what will become of her whole kingdom, if she kills every last newborn baby?”
“She does not care a whit for her country. She sends her witches out to the villages, to teach the women spells to make their babies fade, to feed them horrid poisons that make their wombs barren. Some say she’s struck a bargain with the Shadows, to wipe the whole human race out entirely. Every night we all lie awake shivering, listening to the questing beasts pad past our huts. She can smell a new baby from miles away, and she’ll lie down at the door of the hut for days, waiting for a crack to slip in. There are old rhymes that are supposed to chase her away, but they don’t always work.”
She surged to her feet all at once, panicking. “I’m sitting here chattering, and that beast might have dragged itself home already! Its mistress will send the second one out to hunt us down before dawn!”
“I don’t think it will ever make it back to the palace,” I said, standing beside her. “Let me carry the baby for you, to make the run easier.”
She said nothing, only clamped the child tighter to her chest when I reached out to offer.
“I still can’t make sense of how that brute was bleeding so much,” I said, falling into step beside her.
“Take my advice,” she said, not looking at me. “Don’t go anywhere near the palace. At night you can hear sounds coming from it, like a crowd of dead people trying to scream, but their mouths are sewn shut so no sound can get out but a terrible muted gurgle.”
Then she bade me goodbye, sharp and abrupt, turning away without another word. It was clear she did not want my company any longer, so I stood still, watching her pale shape melt into the dark, until the soft sound of her footsteps over the damp grass faded away entirely, leaving nothing but the hum of the night wind, and the distant, unblinking glow of Bulika’s palace far off on the horizon.
