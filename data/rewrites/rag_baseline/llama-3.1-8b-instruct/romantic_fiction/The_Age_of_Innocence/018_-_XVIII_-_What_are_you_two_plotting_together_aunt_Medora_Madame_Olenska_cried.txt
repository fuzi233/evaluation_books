Madame Olenska stood before the mirror, the soft light of the drawing room dancing across her features. Her gaze lingered on the reflection, and for a moment, her expression was a map of the turmoil within. She turned, and the sound of her silk dress rustling against the velvet of the sofa was the only noise that broke the stillness. Her eyes locked onto Archer's, a fleeting glint of amusement flashing in their depths before she let out a soft sigh.

She gestured to the bouquet on the table, the flowers' delicate petals a stark contrast to the tension that had built in her presence. "Who would think to send me flowers now?" she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "It's as if they think me a foolish girl, eager to be wooed." The words hung in the air, heavy with the weight of unspoken emotions.

Without a word, Archer rose and took the flowers, his movements economical and deliberate. He handed them to Nastasia, who hovered in the doorway, her eyes darting between her mistress and the young man. "Take these to the Winsetts," Madame Olenska instructed, her voice firm but controlled. "Their wife is ill; perhaps the flowers will bring her some small comfort."

As the maid departed, Madame Olenska turned back to Archer, her eyes narrowing slightly. "My aunt Medora says I'm accustomed to grand gestures, to the thrill of the unknown. But what does she know of me, really?" Her gaze drifted back to the mirror, and for a moment, Archer thought he saw a glimmer of vulnerability behind the mask of poise.

He took a step closer, his voice low and measured. "Perhaps your aunt sees more than you think." Madame Olenska's eyes snapped back to his, a hint of challenge in their depths. "Or perhaps she simply sees what she wants to see," she countered, her smile a subtle, enigmatic curve.
