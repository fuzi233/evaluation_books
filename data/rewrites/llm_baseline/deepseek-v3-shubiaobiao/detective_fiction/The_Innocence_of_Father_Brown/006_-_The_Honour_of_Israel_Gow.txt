A storm loomed over the glen, the sky bruised with streaks of olive and silver as Father Brown, wrapped in his grey plaid, reached the end of the valley. Before him stood the grotesque silhouette of Glengyle Castle—a jagged, slate-roofed monstrosity that seemed less a nobleman’s home than the lair of some forgotten witch. The pines surrounding it swayed like a black tide, their whispers carrying the weight of old sins.

Scotland’s aristocracy had always bred madness, but the Glengyles had refined it into an art. The last earl, a specter in his own home, had vanished without leaving his castle. The only witness to his existence was Israel Gow, a red-haired, hollow-eyed servant who spoke to no one and buried his master with the solemn efficiency of a man who had done it before.

Father Brown had come at the request of Flambeau, who was investigating the earl’s peculiar demise alongside Inspector Craven. The castle’s hollow halls held stranger relics than bones: heaps of loose diamonds, mounds of snuff scattered like sand, tiny gears and springs as if a clockmaker had gone mad, and candles without candlesticks—forced into the necks of whisky bottles like votive offerings.

“A psychological museum,” Flambeau called it.

Father Brown studied the oddities with quiet amusement before offering theories—each more absurd than the last. Perhaps the earl fancied himself a Bourbon king, reliving the ancien régime. Or perhaps he was a thief, using snuff to blind pursuers and diamonds to cut glass.

But the truth lay deeper.

Among the relics were defaced Catholic texts, their holy names meticulously scraped away—halos erased, the very word *God* obliterated. Father Brown’s face darkened.

“We must open the grave.”

The storm howled as they climbed the hill, the wind screaming through the pines like lost gods. The coffin, when they pried it open, held bones—but no head.

“Three headless men stand by this grave,” murmured Father Brown.

Back at the castle, sleep offered no answers, only respite. By dawn, the priest was in the garden, watching the silent Gow dig potatoes—except for one spot he avoided.

Under that untouched earth, Flambeau’s spade struck something hard.

It rolled free—grinning up at them.

The Earl of Glengyle had been found at last.
