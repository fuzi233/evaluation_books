I still have not traced Lona through the mist-shrouded borderlands, but Mara walks at my side more often than not now. She has unspooled dozens of quiet, secret truths for me already, and every breath we take together unfurls a new lesson I had no name for before.

Late at night, when the edges of my study blur soft and indistinct, I catch myself wondering: could that last supposed waking— the moment I stumbled back through my old front door to find my books stacked on their shelves, the kettle still humming half-forgotten on the hob—have been nothing but another layer of the dream? Am I still curled in that cold stone chamber of slumbering un-life, my eyes sealed shut, not yet ripened enough to shed the last of my dream-flesh and rise into the true world beyond? Or did I fail to surrender fully to that long, slow sleep, clinging to a thread of stubborn waking awareness that hauled me back far too soon, before the work of that place was done?

If that waking was itself only a dream, it must surely be a dream pointing toward a still finer, truer waking waiting just over the horizon. I cannot be the plaything of some hollow, false vision. No dream that thrums with that much warmth and beauty could hold nothing but a lie at its core.

When the dark gnaws at my certainty, I cry out: “Could God Himself, the forger of stars and worlds, craft something as soft and perfect as the things I dreamed?”
And Hope answers back, unshaken: “Then where did that dream come from in the first place?”
“From the dark hollow of my own mind, surfacing into the light of my awareness,” I protest. “My brain was its mother, and the fever singing in my blood its father.”
Hope laughs soft then. “Say rather your brain was a violin, strung with threads of the world unseen, and that fever in your blood was the bow that drew the song out of it. But who carved the violin? Who guided that bow across every string so perfectly to pull that exact melody free? Who set every songbird perched on its bough in the great Tree of Life, and startled each one to sing its own note in its own time? Where did that wild, unchained fantasia come from? And where did the life that danced to its tune originate? Did you, all alone in the dark of your own unconscious, whisper ‘Let beauty exist. Let truth shine bright’—and did it happen, just like that?”

Humans dream, and we hunger after the things we see in those dreams. The Other, the one that breathes life into all worlds, broods, wills, and makes those half-glimpsed things real. If you dream a dream that is only yours, small and self-spun, you become its toy, caught in its endless loops. But if the dream was given to you by that Other? That same power is more than capable of making it come true.

I have never gone looking for that cursed mirror again. The hand that reached through it to hurl me back to this side gave me a warning I will not ignore. I will never try to leave through that door again. “All the days of my appointed time will I wait till my change come.”

Sometimes when I glance at the spines of my old books lined on the shelf, they ripple, as if an invisible wind is blowing straight through their solid paper and leather, as if a crack is opening between worlds right there, ready to spill something new and unnameable into my study. Other times when I walk the roads outside, the whole sky and earth, the grass under my boots and the boughs of the oak trees, shake for a heartbeat, like they are about to dissolve entirely and show me the face of the reality hiding beneath them. Then the tremor passes, and they settle back, looking exactly like the familiar, ordinary world I have always known.

At odd moments, I catch faint whisperings curling around my ears, like people who loved me are standing just out of sight, murmuring about me. But the second I try to make out their words, the whispers cut off, and all around me is dead quiet. I cannot tell if these things are blooming in my own brain, or slipping through the thin veil from the other side. I do not chase them. I do not fight them. They come, and I let them go.

Strange, half-remembered memories drift through me in broad daylight, too, too fuzzy to pin down, like faces seen through a fogged window from a past I cannot quite place. I do not sleep to dream anymore, not the way I used to. For all I know, the more awake I think I am, the deeper I am sunk into the grandest dream of all. But when I finally wake for good, into that greater life that holds this small one like a mother holds her child against her chest, I will know. There will be no more doubt.

I wait. Asleep, or awake, I wait.

Novalis says, “Our life is no dream, but it should and will perhaps become one.”
