The air inside St. Luke's Church was heavy with the scent of old wood and damp stone. Outside, a gentle spring breeze carried the faintest whisper of the city's awakening. Newland Archer stood on the chancel step, his eyes fixed on the door as he waited for the sound of carriage wheels on the cobblestones. His best man, Van der Luyden, hovered beside him, fidgeting with the folds of his gloves.

As the sexton's signal echoed through the church, Archer's thoughts turned to the ritual they were about to perform. A rite of passage, a carefully choreographed dance of tradition and expectation. He had played this role before, and yet it still felt like a mechanical exercise, a series of gestures and responses rehearsed to perfection.

The bridesmaids' bouquets of white lilac and lilies-of-the-valley had arrived on time, along with the gold and sapphire sleeve-links for the ushers and the best man's cat's-eye scarf-pin. Archer had written his thank-you notes, each one a carefully crafted exercise in polite evasion. His luggage was stored at Mrs. Manson Mingott's, where the wedding breakfast would take place, and a private compartment had been reserved on the train that would carry the newlyweds away.

As the moments ticked by, Archer's mind began to wander. He thought of all the weddings he had attended, all the faces he had seen in these very pews. The same women, the same men, the same rituals repeated ad infinitum. And yet, despite the familiarity, he couldn't shake the feeling that this time was different. This time, he was the one standing at the threshold, poised between two worlds.

Handel's March swelled through the church, its pompous grandeur a stark contrast to the quiet intimacy of the moment. Archer's thoughts drifted to the future, to the unknown destination waiting for him and his bride. Would they find happiness there? Or would they simply repeat the same patterns, the same routines, the same disappointments?

As the minutes passed, Archer's attention snapped back to the present. The door of the church creaked open, and a flutter of movement caught his eye. The bride was arriving. With a practiced smile, he turned to face her, his heart beating with a mixture of excitement and trepidation. The ritual was about to begin, and he was ready to play his part.
