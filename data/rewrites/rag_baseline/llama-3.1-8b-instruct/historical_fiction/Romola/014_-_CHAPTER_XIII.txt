The seventh of September sun beat down upon the narrow cobblestone streets of Florence, casting a warm glow over the bustling crowds that thronged the piazzas. More than two months had passed since Romola and Tito had shared their love with each other, their hearts still aglow with the sweetness of their confession. As Tito entered Nello's humble shop, the scent of sandalwood and orange blossoms wafted through the air, mingling with the rich aroma of freshly brewed coffee.

Nello, the barber, lay reclining on his bench, his cap pulled low over his eyes, one leg tucked beneath him and the other stretched out, as if surrendering to a languid slumber. A nearby candle cast flickering shadows on the walls, illuminating the scattered pages of a manuscript lying on the floor. Sandro, a young apprentice, sat in the corner, intently focused on the intricate patterns of his game of _mora_, his fingers moving with deliberate slowness as he pondered the arithmetic challenges set before him.

As Tito approached, he gently plucked the lute from its stand and began to strum a lilting melody, the music seeming to awaken Nello from his torpor. The barber sprang to life, his eyes snapping open as he reached for his comb, his movements economical and practiced.

"Ah, Tito mio, forgive me for my tardiness," Nello exclaimed, his voice husky from sleep. "I fear I have indulged too freely in the poetic draughts of Lorenzo's Carnival Song." He smiled wryly, running a hand through his unruly hair. "A symptom of my creative excess, I fear – the wine of my understanding threatens to spill at the spigot of authorship, leaving me an empty vessel, dry and stale."

As Nello began to tease out the knots in Tito's curls, he inquired, "What is it that brings you to my humble shop today, my young friend? Your gentle touch on the lute suggests a request, a desire to capture the essence of beauty, to distill the fleeting moments of life into something lasting." His fingers moved with deft precision, expertly working out the tangles in Tito's hair, the scent of orange blossoms rising from his hands like a benediction.
