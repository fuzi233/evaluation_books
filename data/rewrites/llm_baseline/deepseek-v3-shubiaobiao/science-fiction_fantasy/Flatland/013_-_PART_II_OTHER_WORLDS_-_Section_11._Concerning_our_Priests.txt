It is time I turned from these scattered observations about Flatland to the heart of this account—my awakening to the secrets of Space. *That* is my purpose; all that precedes is but prologue. And so, though I might elaborate on countless facets of our existence—how we move without feet, how we build without hands, how rain traverses our zones without obstruction, the nature of our hills and mines, our script upon linear tablets—I must set them aside. Not from neglect, but from respect for the Reader’s time.

Yet before I proceed, I owe a few words on the very foundation of our society: the Circles, or as we name them, the Priests. Do not mistake the title for what it signifies in your world. Among us, Priests are no mere clergy. They are the architects of all affairs—masters of commerce, generalship, science, law, and morality. They decree, and others labor; they govern, and all obey. To them, we pay near reverence.

Now, though common speech grants the name "Circle" to all of their order, the learned know the truth: no Circle is truly circular. They are Polygons, but of such finely divided sides that the angles elude even the most delicate touch. A figure of three hundred sides has edges no longer than a hundredth of a foot; at six hundred, they are scarcely wider than the point of a Spaceland pin. By tradition, we grant the Chief Circle ten thousand sides—a courtesy, nothing more.

Unlike the lower Orders, whose sides increase but one per generation, the Circles are bound by no such slow arithmetic. Nature imposes two opposing laws upon them: as their lineage ascends, their evolution quickens, yet their fertility wanes. A five-hundred-sided Polygon may sire but one son, yet that son might boast fifty or a hundred sides beyond his father.

And then there is Art’s hand in their refinement. Our physicians have learned to fracture the fragile sides of a highborn infant and reset them with such precision that—though the risk is grave—a child may leap centuries of lineage in a single generation. Few survive this ordeal. For every ten subjected to the Circular Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium, nine join the silent ranks of the Cemetery. But when the tenth emerges, no longer a Polygon but (by grace) a Circle, the triumph drives multitudes to stake their heirs upon the same desperate chance.

Ambition, it seems, spares no sacrifice.
