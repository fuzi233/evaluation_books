Outside, the sea swelled and subsided with a steady, almost musical rhythm, as if the very fabric of the universe was tuned to a single, mournful note.

We had been adrift for what felt like an eternity, our bodies numbed by the relentless rocking of the Ark. I tried to recall the events that had led us here, but my mind was a jumble of fragmented images and half-remembered sounds. The crash of the storm, the screams of the others, the feeling of weightlessness as the world seemed to spin out of control... it was all a blur.

As I drifted in and out of consciousness, I became aware of Nais's gentle touch on my hand. She was awake, her eyes fixed on me with a soft, compassionate gaze. I forced myself to sit up, my movements slow and labored. The air was stale and musty, heavy with the scent of damp wood and decay. The Ark creaked and groaned around us, its ancient timbers protesting the constant motion. I stood up, my legs trembling beneath me, and made my way to the porthole. Outside, the sea stretched out before us like an endless, gray canvas, unbroken by even a hint of land or horizon.

We had lost everything, it seemed – our home, our people, our way of life. The thought was crushing, a weight that threatened to break me. But as I looked out at the sea, I felt a glimmer of determination spark within me. We would survive, Nais and I. We would ride out this storm, this desolate expanse of water, and find a way to rebuild, to start anew.
