As Mrs. Dashwood's letter was dispatched, she found solace in sharing the news with her son-in-law and his wife, declaring that she had secured a new home and would soon be leaving their presence. The couple listened in stunned silence. Mrs. John Dashwood remained mute, but her husband expressed a polite hope that she wouldn't relocate too far from Norland. Mrs. Dashwood smiled knowingly, her eyes sparkling as she revealed her plans to move to Devonshire. At the mention of the distant county, Edward turned towards her, his expression a mix of surprise and concern. "Devonshire?" he asked, his voice laced with a hint of trepidation. "So far from here? And to what part specifically?" Mrs. Dashwood's face lit up as she explained the location, situated just north of Exeter, within four miles' reach.

"It's a humble cottage," she continued, "but I'm confident that I'll be able to host many dear friends there. A few extra rooms can be added if needed, and if my friends find it inconvenient to travel such a distance, I assure you, I shall make every effort to accommodate them." With a warm smile, she extended an invitation to Mr. and Mrs. John Dashwood to visit her at Barton, and to Edward, a particularly affectionate one. Though her recent conversation with her daughter-in-law had solidified her decision to leave Norland, it hadn't altered her intention to stay close to her beloved Edward. In fact, she had made it a point to issue an open invitation to him, subtly indicating that she disregarded her daughter-in-law's disapproval of their relationship.

Mr. John Dashwood apologized profusely for the inconvenience caused by his mother's decision to settle in Devonshire, rendering it impossible for him to assist with the removal of her belongings. He genuinely felt remorseful, knowing that the effort he had planned to exert in fulfilling his father's promise was now unattainable. The majority of Mrs. Dashwood's possessions – linens, fine china, books, and a beautiful pianoforte inherited from Marianne – were transported by boat. Mrs. John Dashwood watched the departing packages with a tinge of sadness; she couldn't help but feel a sense of injustice that Mrs. Dashwood's modest income would allow her to possess such a grand piano.
