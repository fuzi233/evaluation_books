As I emerged from my chamber, invigorated by a restful night's sleep, I couldn't shake the feeling of revitalized purpose. My fatigue, it seemed, had been merely a temporary reprieve, and my mind now buzzed with renewed vitality. I finished dressing with a sense of urgency, as if the day's events depended on my punctuality.

A knock at the door interrupted my reverie, and I called out, expecting the chambermaid to enter. Instead, a burly man strode in, his features rugged and weathered. "Give me your keys, maître," he demanded brusquely, his accent a thick, guttural dialect.

I hesitated, taken aback by his abruptness. "Why?" I asked, trying to stall for time.

"Gif!" he growled, snatching the keys from my hand, "All right! You'll get your trunk soon enough." His manner was curt, almost dismissive, but I sensed a hint of apology beneath the surface.

It transpired that he was a customs official, tasked with collecting luggage from departing guests. I breathed a sigh of relief, grateful that the encounter had been brief. With my bearings still foggy, I set out to find the breakfast room, unsure what to expect.

As I descended the grand staircase, I took in the opulent surroundings – the high ceilings, the painted walls, the wide windows that flooded the hallway with light. The veined marble beneath my feet seemed a stark contrast to the humble chamber I had occupied. My thoughts turned philosophical, pondering the sagacity of the inn's staff in assigning accommodations to their guests. How did they discern our social standing and financial circumstances with such ease?
