Silence seeped into the parlor like a chill draft. Newland Archer stood before the peeling stucco house, the wisteria's tendrils snaking around the balcony like a withered fist. The Countess Olenska's words echoed in his mind: "after five." He checked his pocket watch, the ticking a reminder of the passing hours. The afternoon sunlight cast long shadows across the pavement, illuminating the disheveled street's modest inhabitants – dressmakers, bird-stuffers, and writers who scribbled away in dingy rooms.

Archer's thoughts turned to the Wellands, their stifling social round a weight he couldn't shake. The memory of May's sparkling eyes the night before still lingered, but the prospect of twelve dozen hand-embroidered items weighed heavily on his mind. He recalled the afternoon's visitations, the polite smiles and forced conversation, and felt a familiar sense of disconnection.

As he stepped inside, a swarthy foreign-looking maid greeted him with a warm smile, her prominent bosom a striking feature beneath her colorful neckerchief. Archer's gaze lingered, and she led him through the narrow hall into a dimly lit room. The fire crackled, casting flickering shadows on the walls. The air was heavy with the scent of old books and something else – a hint of mystery.

The maid vanished, leaving Archer to ponder the Countess's enigmatic summons. He wondered if she was more complex than her innocent facade suggested. Time ticked by, and the silence grew thicker, until he felt like a stranger in this quiet, foreign world.
