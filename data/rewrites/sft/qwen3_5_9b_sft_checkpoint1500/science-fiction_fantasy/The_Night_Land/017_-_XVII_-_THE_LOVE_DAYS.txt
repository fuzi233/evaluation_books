When consciousness returned to me, I realized I was lying on that same bed, having ascended the Lift from the silent void. It seemed impossible that I would ever need rest again, nor return to the realm of Silence from whence I had risen. A vague, strange sensation filled me as the deep, subterranean organs of the world began to throb, sending a thunderous resonance upward. It sounded like a distant, ethereal music playing beyond the threshold of death—a rolling chant sung by multitudes across far-off mountains, sometimes like a low wind blowing from the Deep, other times resolving into the ancient, majestic melody of the Song of Honour. I understood, though in a dreamlike state, that the millions living beneath those depths were celebrating this miracle of joy. Yet, everything remained faint, half-hidden behind the heavy lids that refused to fully open. I felt as though I were floating on strange, unreal waters. Around me, however, drifted sweet and lovely odors, tangible and real, emanating from the vast fields where flowers grew eternally along the passages of the ascending Lift.

Perhaps I stirred slightly, for then the voice of the Master Doctor came, low and gentle, bidding me rest. He assured me that the Maid was safe. Soon after, I drifted into a haze, existing in a blur of days where I half-lived and half-slept, wondering without care if I were truly alive or dead. Then came a period of profound quietude, where I thought of nothing. The Master Doctor often leaned over me, scrutinizing my face with intense focus. Finally, after what seemed like endless stretches of time, another figure bent over me. It was the face I loved most—the beloved, lovely countenance of Mine Own. Her eyes spoke volumes of love into my soul, yet she remained calm and hushed. Slowly, life began to return to my body. I made a clumsy movement with my hands, and she gently took them, holding them close. From her, warmth and vitality flowed back into me. She was ever wordless and tender, and as contentment took root within me, a natural slumber claimed me.

A day arrived when I was permitted to rise. Those who cared for me carried me to one of the Quiet Gardens within the Pyramid, setting me down and seemingly leaving me alone. Then, a figure emerged from behind a bush, looking at me for a moment with a shy hesitation. However, the radiant love shining in her eyes rendered that shyness insignificant. Truly, I knew it was Mine Own, my Maid, yet I had never before seen her dressed with such grace and prettiness. Looking at her, I realized she was far more dainty and beautiful than I could have ever imagined. Suddenly, I attempted to rise and approach her, driven by some natural impulse, but she quickly ran to me, stopping my foolish movement. She sat beside me, resting my head against her bosom. She did not refuse me her lips, offering instead a dual comfort as both a devoted maid and a nurturing mother in that single, perfect moment.

Afterward, she urged me to remain perfectly still. We sat together in an utter, speechless happiness until the attendants returned. With them came the Master of the Doctors, whose face bore a trace of satisfaction. From that day forth, I saw Mine Own daily. I recovered with astonishing speed, for Love itself healed me. Soon, I was allowed to descend to the Fields, though via private routes, for the multitudes might otherwise have swarmed around me, needing solitude to heal. The Maid accompanied me. Both the Master Monstruwacan and the Master of the Doctors had agreed to this arrangement, arranging for an Officer of Marriage to unite us quietly and simply. I was still too weak for the grand Public Wedding, which would come later—when, indeed, the millions bestowed upon us a Guard of Honour stretching eight miles high from the summit to the base of the Mighty Pyramid. That ceremony would await its proper time, a tribute the people could not deny themselves in honoring me.

Surely, the Maid stayed with me always, becoming my wife and the source of my enduring strength. As I regained perfect health, we wandered freely through the immense Fields, walking paths dedicated to lovers, situated near the scattered villages. These villages were truly colossal, akin to countries in size. We hid our names to avoid being besieged by the natural curiosity and kindness of others; we needed to be utterly together and quiet. We sought out spots for our slumbers where the floral beauty was most wondrous, carrying provisions with us, though we also ate whenever we reached a village. Mine Own kept her promises a hundredfold, preparing me hearty, generous meals. She teased me relentlessly about my gluttony as I ate, kissing me away just as I might have found words to defend myself. Truly, she was everything my heart and spirit desired; she companionship me with love and brought joy into my spirit.

Once, we ventured downward into the Country of Silence, though we did not stay long, for my memories began to return. In future times, we wandered there often, accompanied by memory, holiness, and great thoughts, bound by a love that holds all. As we left that land, I told Mine Own how, when her life had been suspended by the horrid force of the House, I had suffered dreadful pain, fearing I would never wake to discover her kissing me while I slept. She blushed with lovely modesty, unaware that I had been conscious of her sweet affection during her supposed death. She had thought entirely of my agony when she believed herself lost to the vapors of the Earth-Force.

She came to me with a dear understanding. The doctors explained that she had been stunned and frozen in spirit, her entire being and life suspended. Only the great life-force of the Earth-Current had awakened her spirit, allowing her body to live again and her blood to flow properly. The physicians had spoken and searched deeply in the ancient records of their work, finding mention of one such occurrence in olden times, but nothing comparable throughout a mighty age of years. While wandering and resting in the Fields, I often recounted matters to Mine Own. I knew she had learned some odd things before I recovered, though not excessively, for she, too, had been in terrible straits. She had risen from her bed while I lay so still, an order given by the Master Doctor who feared I would truly die without some means to awaken my spirit. In truth, you must consider the depth of my love as I know she held my hands so bravely and gently, barely able to support herself. I offered a little holy praise for Mine Own.

Thus, I come to this conclusion, with one final tale to share. This happened a while later, after we had undergone the second marriage—the solemn Public Ceremony. One day, my wife, Mine Own, took me with a sweet cunning to the Hall of Honour. Upon arrival, I saw many of the peoples standing in silence within that vast hall, waiting for something significant. My wife walked forward with me to the center of the hall. Suddenly, I understood why she had led me there with such sweetness. Standing in the midst of the Hall, in the Place of Honour, stood a statue of a man in broken armor, forever carrying a maid.

I fell silent. How can this age comprehend the honor this meant? Such honors were reserved solely for the Great Dead. I was but a young man, far removed from greatness, save for the fact that I loved with all my heart and spirit, making death seem small compared to love. You must understand how love makes the heart sweet and brave, granting humility, wonder, and a natural pride that someone would think to honor me so. Mine Own wept with joy and honest pride at her husband's side. An utter silence of dear sympathy filled the great Hall. Those present allowed us to depart in quietness, a lovely testament to mutual understanding.

I walked lovingly and thoughtfully with my wife, staying near her. I had gained honour, yet I learned a vital lesson: honour is but the ash of life unless it is fueled by love. And I had love. To possess love is to possess everything, for true love mothers honour and faithfulness, and these three elements build the House of Joy.
