As twilight descended upon the castle, casting long shadows across the manicured lawn, the silence within its stone walls seemed to reverberate with the echoes of the day's events. I sat beside the fireplace in our drawing room, the flickering flames dancing across the tapestries that adorned the walls, their gold frames glinting in the soft candlelight. Our conversation revolved around the young stranger who had arrived earlier that evening, her mysterious carriage abandoned in the forest. My father, ever the patriot, had insisted on serving the national beverage, tea, alongside our coffee and chocolate, a nod to tradition that warmed the hearts of our guests.

Madame Perrodon and Mademoiselle De Lafontaine sat with us, their faces aglow with curiosity as they recounted their initial impressions of our guest. "She is, without a doubt, the loveliest creature I've ever had the pleasure of meeting," Madame Perrodon said, her voice barely above a whisper. "Her youth and gentleness are captivating." Mademoiselle nodded in agreement, her eyes sparkling with excitement as she added, "And that voice! It's as melodious as a songbird's."

As we spoke, my mind wandered back to the carriage, now safely ensconced in the courtyard. I recalled the sound of its wheels and hooves fading into the darkness, the eerie feeling that lingered long after it disappeared from view. A shiver ran down my spine as I thought of the woman in the carriage, the one who hadn't gotten out. Mademoiselle's description of her – a hideous black woman with a colorful turban, her eyes gleaming with malice – sent a chill through me. "Did you notice anything peculiar about the servants?" she asked, her brow furrowed in concern. My father nodded, his expression grim. "They were a rough-looking bunch, indeed. But I suppose they're clever rogues, managing to get everything in order despite their appearances."

As the night wore on, our conversation ebbed and flowed, like the river that wound its way through the forest outside. The fire crackled and spat, casting a warm glow over the room, and I couldn't help but feel a sense of comfort in the familiar rhythms of our evening routine.
