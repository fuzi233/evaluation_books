The Last Silence.

As I walked through the deserted alleys of Florence, the weight of uncertainty settled upon me like a shroud. The words of Savonarola echoed in my mind, repeated by the lips of many: "Pray for me, for God has withdrawn from me the spirit of prophecy." I recalled the day he signed his confession, and how it shook Romola with new doubts about his true intentions. Those doubts only deepened as she heard the whispers of his words, spoken in the dead of night, in the stillness of her chamber.

The nineteenth of May had brought the Papal Commissaries to Florence, their arrival announced by the cries of the people, demanding the death of the Frate. The streets resounded with the chant: "It is the Frate's deception that has brought on all our misfortunes; let him be burned, and all things right will be done, and our evils will cease." The city's mood had turned, and with it, the tide of public opinion.

As I navigated the narrow streets, I caught glimpses of the city's inhabitants, their faces twisted in a mix of fear and hatred. The air was heavy with the scent of woodsmoke and baking bread, a reminder of the city's daily routines, yet the atmosphere was tense, like a held breath. I quickened my pace, my heart heavy with foreboding.

The next day, the trial resumed, and with it, the torture of Savonarola's fragile frame. He fell to his knees, his body wracked with convulsions, as he retracted his confession, proclaiming that he had spoken falsely, and that he would suffer for the truth. The words spilled out in a torrent: "The things that I have spoken, I had them from God." But the torture continued, and when he was asked to explain, he spoke of deceit, of saying what he thought would bring him favor.

The assessors of Florence were present, and soon, the words of retraction spread like wildfire. Romola's uncertainty grew, her mind racing with possibilities. She thought of the moment when Savonarola might speak freely, unencumbered by fear or falsehood. When the dread of death loomed large, and he stood before the people, they would not be able to silence him. She vowed to be there, to bear witness to his final words.
