As the train rumbled out of Detroit, Sally settled into a more optimistic mood, her spirits buoyed by the whirlwind of events that had transpired since her departure. She couldn't help but feel a twinge of guilt for her earlier doubts and fears, knowing that men like Gerald were driven to succeed, not just for personal satisfaction, but for the success of those around them, including herself.

The past few days had been a blur of excitement and validation, with "The Primrose Way" receiving a resounding welcome from the amiable Detroit audiences. The theatre had finally opened on Tuesday, and the crowd had devoured the play with gusto, hungrily seeking entertainment after their period of enforced abstinence. The critics, too, had joined in the chorus of praise, hailing Gerald as a rising star and Elsa Doland as a talented newcomer. Even Fillmore had received a nod of recognition, though the only discordant note was the critic's careless misspelling of Gladys Winch's name as Wunch.

Fillmore, however, took the slight in stride, his natural optimism and good humor allowing him to brush off the setback. Business had been thriving throughout the week, with Elsa's performances growing stronger with each show. The arrival of a heartfelt telegram from Mr. Cracknell, requesting to buy the piece back, was a pleasant surprise, and the visit from Ike Schumann, the renowned theatre owner, had been the icing on the cake. Schumann's seal of approval was a coveted stamp of quality, and Fillmore beamed with pride as he shared the news with Sally.

Sally smiled knowingly, and Fillmore, lost in thought, suddenly snapped back to attention. "Eh?" he asked, his brow furrowed.

"I said yes, do," Sally replied, her voice low and teasing.

Fillmore's expression turned puzzled. "Do what?"

"Do buy a fur coat," Sally said, her eyes sparkling with amusement. "You've been thinking about it, haven't you?"

Fillmore's face flushed, but he couldn't deny the truth. He had indeed considered it, if only briefly. After all, a man had to stay warm. Sally pressed on, her tone light and playful. "With an astrakhan collar, perhaps?"

Fillmore's annoyance was palpable, but he tried to laugh it off. "As a matter of fact, I was thinking about something else entirely."
