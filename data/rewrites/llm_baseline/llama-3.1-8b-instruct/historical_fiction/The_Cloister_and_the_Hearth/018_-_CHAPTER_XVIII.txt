The scent of damp earth and ozone hung heavy over the village as Ghysbrecht Van Swieten lay awake, his mind racing with the thought of Peter's disappearance. A storm had rolled in during the night, and its intensity had kept him from joining the search party. But now, as the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, he couldn't shake the feeling of unease that had settled in his stomach like a stone. He threw off his covers and dressed quickly, making his way downstairs to saddle his mule.

As he rode to the "Three Kings" at Sevenbergen, the storm's remnants still lingered, leaving behind a trail of wet leaves and the sweet smell of blooming wildflowers. Inside the tavern, the fire crackled and spat, casting a warm glow over the sleepy faces of his men, who were sprawled out on chairs, tables, and the floor. Ghysbrecht's voice boomed through the room, shaking them awake. "Rise and shine, my friends! We've got work to do!"

The tale of their failed search spilled out, a mix of frustration and admiration for their zeal. "Foolishness!" Ghysbrecht exclaimed, his face reddening with anger. "You should have searched every inch of that house – under the girl's bed, in the pantry, and even the walls themselves!" Dierich Brower rubbed the sleep from his eyes, yawned, and muttered, "If you find him, I'll ride a horse to the moon."

Within minutes, the group was back at Peter's house, this time with a renewed sense of purpose. Ghysbrecht handed his mule over to Jorian Ketel and led the charge into the house, his men close behind. The air inside was stale and musty, but Ghysbrecht's keen nose picked up on something else – the scent of freshly baked bread wafting from the kitchen. His heart skipped a beat as he followed the aroma upstairs, where one of the men suddenly shouted, pointing out the window. There, in the distance, Peter and Margaret walked hand in hand with Martin, the sun casting a golden glow over the scene.

Ghysbrecht's face lit up with excitement as he rushed back down the stairs and leapt onto his mule. With a loud yell, he spurred the animal forward, and the group took off in hot pursuit, the scent of fresh bread forgotten in the rush of the chase.
