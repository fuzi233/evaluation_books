The rain hammered against the ceiling of our dwelling, a relentless percussion that marked the final breath of the 1999th year. Inside, the atmosphere was thick with the weight of transition, as I sat beside my wife, contemplating the vast sweep of the past millennium and the terrifying prospect of the new one. Our children and grandchildren had already withdrawn to their respective chambers, leaving us alone to witness the turn of the age.

My mind drifted back to the afternoon's lesson, a memory that refused to fade despite the encroaching darkness. My youngest grandson, a hexagon of startling brilliance and mathematical purity, had recently challenged the very foundations of our understanding. During our standard exercises in Sight Recognition, where we rotated upon our centers to test his spatial acuity, he had asked a question that sent a chill through my logical faculties. Holding together nine squares to demonstrate that $3^2$ equals nine, I had explained that area exists only because lines move through space. When he pressed further, asking what $3^3$ might represent geometrically, I had dismissed it as nonsense, insisting that our world possessed only two dimensions. Yet, even as he retreated to his room in shame, the echo of his query lingered: *If a point becomes a line, and a line becomes a square, surely a square moving through a third dimension must create something greater.*

Now, as the sand in the hourglass dwindled to its final grains, I turned the glass northward to mark the end of the old era. In doing so, I muttered aloud, "The boy is a fool."

Instantly, the air grew heavy. A presence coalesced in the center of the room, radiating a coldness that seeped through my skin. "He is no such thing!" my wife cried, her voice trembling with indignation. "And you are breaking the Commandments by dishonoring your own kin!"

I ignored her pleas, scanning the empty space until I felt the phantom pressure of the unseen entity. "What is the matter?" she demanded, sensing my agitation. "There is no draft. Look around; there is nothing."

"There is nothing," I admitted reluctantly, forcing myself to sit again. "The boy is a fool. $3^3$ has no meaning in our Geometry."

"The boy is not a fool," a voice replied, clear and resonant, cutting through the silence. "$3^3$ possesses an obvious geometrical meaning."

Both of us heard the words, though my wife grasped none of their significance. We lunged forward, drawn by the sound. Horror seized us when we finally perceived the figure standing before us. At first glance, it resembled a woman viewed from the side, but a closer inspection revealed that its extremities dissolved into a blur too rapid for any creature of our sex. It looked like a circle, yet it shifted in size and shape with a fluidity impossible for any regular polygon we knew. While I struggled to analyze these anomalies, my wife, driven by the instinctive suspicion of her gender, immediately concluded that a woman had slipped through a hidden vent.

"How did this person enter?" she shrieked, accusing me of negligence regarding our ventilation system.

"There are no vents," I countered, attempting to apply Sight Recognition. "By my perception, this figure..."

"I have no patience with your Sight Recognition," she interrupted, reciting the proverbs of our frail sex. "'Feeling is believing,' and 'A straight line to the touch is worth a circle to the sight.'"

Reluctantly, fearing to provoke her further, I gestured for an introduction. My wife stepped forward, adopting her most gracious demeanor. "Permit me, Madam, to feel and be felt by—"

She recoiled violently. "Oh! It is not a woman! There are no angles, not a single trace of one. Could I have offended a perfect Circle?"

"I am, in a certain sense, a Circle," the voice responded calmly. "And a more perfect Circle than any found in Flatland. But to speak more accurately, I am many circles in one."

The tone softened. "I carry a message for your husband, which I cannot deliver in your presence. If you would allow us to retire for a brief moment—"

My wife refused to entertain the notion that our august visitor required privacy. Asserting that her own duties demanded immediate attention, she apologized profusely for her earlier indiscretion and hurried away to her chamber.

As she departed, I glanced at the hourglass. The last grain of sand had fallen. The third Millennium had begun.
