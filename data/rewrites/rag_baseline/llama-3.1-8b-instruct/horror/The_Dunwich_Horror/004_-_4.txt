As I stepped out of the musty farmhouse, the humid air clung to me like a damp shroud. The night sounds of Cold Spring Glen were a familiar lullaby – the hooting whippoorwills, the distant rumble of the hills, and the creaking trees swaying in the wind. It was a symphony of desolation, a reminder that this was a place where the fabric of reality was thin. The Whateley farm stood as a testament to the strange and the unknown, its secrets hidden behind the crumbling facade of a once-thriving homestead.

Wilbur Whateley, the enigmatic heir, stood beside me, his eyes sunken, his voice low and measured. We spoke little, our conversations reduced to the bare essentials, like the sparse notes of a forgotten melody. His grandfather, Old Whateley, lay dying, his chest heaving with each labored breath. The doctor, Dr. Houghton, hovered nearby, his expression a mixture of concern and trepidation.

As the night wore on, the whippoorwills' chorus grew louder, their cries timed to the rhythm of the dying man's gasps. It was an unsettling cadence, one that seemed to reverberate deep within the earth itself. The air vibrated with an otherworldly energy, as if the very fabric of reality was being pulled taut.

Old Whateley's eyes fluttered open, and he fixed Wilbur with a gaze that was both fierce and pleading. "More space, Willy," he whispered, his voice barely audible. "You grow, and that grows faster. It will be ready soon." His words trailed off, lost in the cacophony of night sounds.

I watched as Wilbur's face contorted in a mixture of confusion and determination. He nodded, his eyes locked on his grandfather's, as if willing him to continue. The old man's words hung in the air, a cryptic warning that only Wilbur seemed to understand. The doctor, sensing the gravity of the situation, took a step back, his eyes darting towards the vacant space above us.

The whippoorwills' cries reached a fever pitch, their rhythm synchronized with the dying man's labored breathing. It was a macabre dance, one that seemed to draw us all into its vortex.
