Tracing a circular path around the fortress, I returned to the open gates, crossed the deep, ravine-like moat, and emerged onto a vast courtyard paved with stone. Towering trees, resembling giant poplars, stood at regular intervals along the perimeter. In the center rose one tree significantly taller than the rest; its upper branches spread outward, giving it the faint silhouette of a palm. Through the massive trunks, I caught glimpses of the palace—a structure utterly alien to my experience, yet evoking an unmistakable Indian aesthetic. It was long and low, crowned by lofty corner towers and dominated by a colossal central dome that soared halfway to the treetops.

The main entrance lay in the center of the facade: a low archway shaped like a flattened ellipse. No guards were visible; the doors stood wide open, inviting entry without challenge. Stepping inside, I found myself in a grand hall shaped like a long ellipse. To one side stood a cage housing a magnificent leopardess. She lay coiled, her head resting on her paws, restrained by a heavy steel collar, her mouth muzzled and her paws bound. Her coat was snow-white, marked with dark ovals, and her eyes were startlingly wide, featuring canoe-shaped pupils and brilliant green irises. She seemed to watch me intently, yet not a muscle twitched—not an eyelid, not a toe, not a whisker. Her tail extended behind her rigid as iron. I could scarcely determine if she breathed at all.

From this vestibule, two low passages branched off. I chose one, expecting it to lead directly to the throne room, but it quickly forked into a labyrinth of narrow, irregular corridors. Just as I squeezed through a gap barely wide enough for two people, a page collided with me. He recoiled in terror, only to scan me with growing audacity, puffing out his chest.

"To see the princess?" he demanded.

"A likely thing!" he scoffed. "I haven't seen Her Highness this morning myself!"

Seizing him by the back of the neck, I shook him gently but firmly. "Take me to her immediately, or I will drag you with me until I find her. Let her know exactly how her servants receive her guests."

He exchanged a wary glance with me, then began pulling blindly, leading me not toward the royal chambers, but into a bustling kitchen. Servants moved sluggishly, barely awake. I expected them to swarm me and throw me out, but instead, they froze, their eyes widening in horror. They weren't looking at me, though. Their gaze was fixed on something behind me, their expressions turning ghastly as they stared.

Turning slowly, I saw the white leopardess standing there, radiating a menace that could have terrified stouter hearts. One servant, realizing an immediate attack wasn't imminent, finally composed himself. I released the boy's neck.

"Take me to the princess," I commanded.

"She hasn't left her room yet, my lord," he whispered nervously. "Tell her I am here, waiting for an audience."

"Will you give me your name?" I pressed.

"Tell her that one who knows the White Leech wishes to see her."

"She'll kill me if I deliver such a message. I dare not."

"You refuse?"

He cast a fearful look at the beast and scurried away. The kitchen staff remained transfixed, too paralyzed by fear of the leopardess to move. Turning back to the graceful creature, who now stood with her muzzle lowered to my heel—white as milk, a warm splendor in the gloom—I stooped and patted her. She looked up; the mere movement of her head caused the remaining servants to scatter in panic. Rising on her hind legs, she placed her paws on my shoulders. I wrapped my arms around her. She pricked her ears, broke free, and vanished in an instant.

The page I had dismissed earlier reappeared. "Please, come this way, my lord," he urged.

My heart hammered, bracing itself for the inevitable confrontation. Following him through endless twisting passages, I was finally ushered into a room so vast and dark that the walls seemed to dissolve into nothingness. A single patch of light illuminated a small section of the floor, while everything else remained absolute black. Looking up, I saw an oval aperture high in the ceiling, framed by the joints of black marble blocks. The light on the floor revealed tightly fitted slabs of the same material. Later, I learned the entire elliptical wall was constructed of this light-absorbing black marble, while the roof formed the long half of an ellipsoid, with the opening positioned directly over one focus of the floor's ellipse. Faint reddish lines seemed to flicker briefly before vanishing entirely.

Suddenly, a radiant form materialized in the center of the darkness, casting a brilliance in every direction. Draped in soft white robes, her hair cascaded down like a black cataract against the marble. Her eyes burned with a luminous void, and her arms and feet gleamed like warm ivory. She greeted me with the innocent smile of a young girl—in face, figure, and motion, she seemed poised right on the threshold of womanhood.

*Alas,* I thought, *my calculations of danger were flawed. Is this the woman I rescued—the one who struck me, scorned me, and abandoned me?*

I stood frozen in the shadows, watching her gaze search the darkness for me. Then, she vanished.

"She refuses to acknowledge me!" I concluded despairingly.

But the next instant, her eyes flashed from the dark, locking directly onto mine. She had seen me and come to meet me!

"You have found me at last!" she declared, placing a hand on my shoulder. "I knew you would!"

My body quivered with conflicting sensations, a turmoil I lacked the power to analyze. I felt simultaneously drawn to her and repelled by her; each feeling seemed to validate the other.

"You shiver!" she observed. "This place is cold for you! Come."

Silent, struck dumb by her overwhelming beauty and sweetness, I allowed her to take my hand. She led me to the spot of light, where she lingered for a fleeting moment.

"You have grown brown since I last saw you," she noted.

"This is nearly the first roof I've been under since you left me," I replied.

"Whose was the other?" she asked.

"I do not know the woman's name."

"I would gladly learn it! The instinct of hospitality is weak among my people!"

Taking my hand again, she guided me through the darkness to a curtain of black fabric. Beyond it lay a staircase of white stone, which she ascended, leading me to a breathtaking chamber.

"How you must miss the hot, flowing river!" she remarked. "But there is a bath in the corner, free of white leeches! At the foot of your couch lies a garment. When you descend, I shall be in the room to your left, at the base of the stairs."

As she departed, I accused myself of presumption. How could I treat this lovely woman as an entity of evil when she treated me with the warmth of a sister? Where had the marvelous transformation occurred? She had rejected me with a blow; now she embraced me almost tenderly. Had she known my doubts? Could she explain everything? Could I trust her explanation? Regarding her hospitality, I had surely earned it—and deserved to accept it—at least until I rendered a final judgment on her character. Could such beauty and such wickedness coexist in one soul? If so, how? Unable to resolve the paradox, I deferred the question.

Clear as crystal, the water in the great white bath, sunk into the marble floor, sparkled invitingly. Since leaving home, I had seen little water save for the hot streams in the Veiled Woman's cottage and the pools in the wounded leopardess's trail. This looked celestial. I plunged in.

Instantly, my brain filled with a strange, delicate odor—one I did not wholly enjoy. Doubt resurfaced: had she medicated the water? Enchanted it? Was she manipulating me unlawfully? Furthermore, how could there be water here in the palace while the city outside was parched? Remembering the crushed paw of the leopardess, I sprang from the bath. What had I been bathing in?

Again I saw the fleeing mother, again heard the howl, again witnessed the limping beast. Yet, what mattered the source? Wasn't the water sweet? Was it not simply the pitcher-plant secreting life from its heart for the weary traveler? Water came from heaven; what significance did the well or the spring hold? Still, I did not return to the bath.

Donning the white woolen robe, embroidered with intricate patterns at the neck and hem, I descended the stairs to the chamber my hostess indicated. It was perfectly round, crafted entirely of alabaster, devoid of windows. Light permeated the space everywhere, creating a soft, pearly shimmer rather than a harsh shine. Vague, shadowy forms drifted across the walls and low dome, resembling loose rainclouds over a grey-blue sky.

The princess awaited me, clad in a robe woven with argent rings, discs, rectangles, and lozenges—effectively a suit of silver mail. It hung unbroken from her neck, concealing her feet, though its long, open sleeves left her arms bare. In the center of the room stood an ivory table laden with cakes and fruit, alongside an ivory jug of milk, a crystal vessel containing pale rose-colored wine, and a white loaf.
