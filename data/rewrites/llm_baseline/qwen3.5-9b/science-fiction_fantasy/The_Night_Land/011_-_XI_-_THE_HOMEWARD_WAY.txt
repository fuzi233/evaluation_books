It seemed a world of light compared to the suffocating, lonely gloom that lay at the bottom of the ancient sea. I realized I had emerged onto land situated to the right of my original entry point during my outward journey. Before me stretched a landscape dotted with countless fire-holes, a sight that warmed my heart, though prudence dictated caution; life often thrived around these flames.

I looked down at the Maid, who gazed up at me. She moved closer, appearing remarkably lovely and sweet, yet her face bore the pallor of extreme exhaustion. Guilt gnawed at me; I had pushed her too hard. In truth, I felt forged of iron, while she remained a delicate, precious girl. She refused my self-reproach, simply standing close and gazing at me with eyes of profound beauty. I wrapped my arms around her and kissed her, then scanned the horizon to chart our next move.

From my vantage point, the blue shimmer I had spotted at the entrance of the Upward Gorge was visible, though distant. It was this celestial glow that had guided us across the seabed earlier. While faint, it served as a vital beacon, confirming we were moving toward the far side of the ocean floor rather than wandering in blind circles. After a moment's contemplation, I determined the Gorge lay to my left, but not directly so. To my left, far in the distance, burned the red glow of the Giants' Hole. My path required a careful balance: avoiding the Giants entirely while steering clear of the mysterious blue light ahead, which I deeply mistrusted.

With the direction established, I placed an arm around the Maid, who stood quietly beside me. Pointing outward to the left, I explained that the Gorge lay somewhere in that direction, hidden from view but identifiable by the landmarks I remembered from my initial descent.

The Maid, observant despite her fatigue, began to orient herself within the vast landscape. "How do you intend to proceed?" she asked.

"Directly," I replied, "but carefully. We must avoid approaching either the blue light or the Giant's fiery pit."

She gestured for me to examine the terrain ahead. There, amidst scattered fire-holes tinged with a sickly green hue, lay a dangerous zone. "That is a tract of toxic gas," she warned, referencing data from the Lesser Redoubt. "Wherever this green glow appears, the air is poisonous." This hazardous corridor extended far to the northwest, coinciding precisely with the location of the great blue light. When I asked its name, she replied only, "The Shine."

My internal guide, Mine Own, urgently advised against venturing deeper into The Shine. "The Fixed Giants dwell within its borders," she insisted, hidden by the brilliance except when rolling mists revealed them. These entities reminded me of the Great Watchers guarding the Mighty Pyramid—monstrous forces of terrible evil. A memory surfaced instantly: the grotesque face I had witnessed amidst the bright smoke upon my first arrival. That was undoubtedly one of the Fixed Giants.

"How wide does this gas zone stretch?" I queried. Mine Own indicated the extent, and I realized a dilemma: keeping clear of The Shine might make reaching the Gorge impossible. Then, Naani posed a crucial question: How had I crossed the land previously?

"I walked by sheer luck—or perhaps guidance," I admitted. "I instinctively stayed on the far side of the gas trail, leading me safely to the Olden Seabed."

Naani proposed a solution: descend back into the seabed, travel southwest beneath the surface to bypass the gas zone entirely, resurface, and then proceed cautiously toward the Gorge, mindful of the Giants patrolling the Great Red Fire-Hole. It was a sound strategy, exactly what I would have devised myself. However, seventeen hours had passed since our last rest, and the Maid was visibly drained.

"It is wise to rest soon," I suggested, urging us to regain our strength.

Agreeing quickly, we selected a nearby fire-hole to the north. It proved farther than anticipated, requiring nearly an hour to reach. As we approached, it revealed itself as a massive, glowing chasm rising from the rocky hollow. At the edge, I signaled silence, retrieved the Diskos from my hip, and knelt, beckoning Mine Own to join me.

Descending into the hollow, we observed the great fire burning deep within the earth. Surprisingly, no monsters lurked immediately around the flame, offering a fleeting sense of relief. Yet, the instinct to stay away lingered; fire attracted life, and danger often gathered there. For a long time, we listened intently to the night, hearing nothing but the wind.

Despite my warnings, the Maid was freezing. Her shivering compelled me to override my caution. "We must go down," she begged. Reluctantly, we descended into the heat.

Strangely, my spirit whispered a warning just as we arrived, contradicting my physical desire for warmth. Experience taught me that danger near fire-holes was always present, yet when separated from them, the threat felt abstract, while the cold felt undeniably real. Now, however, the reality of the hazards surged back.

Scanning the rocky floor, I found no large beasts, only three snakes and two scorpions hiding in crevices. They retreated slightly but held their ground. Clearly, sleeping here was ill-advised; the heat would draw creatures to us in our sleep. I decided to remain vigilant but chose not to alarm the Maid, hoping to persuade her gently later.

Soon, the Maid grew warm enough to retrieve food and drink from my pack. We ate and drank in companionable silence. As she consumed her second tablet, I sensed the lingering sting of my previous discipline, yet she accepted it completely, belonging utterly to me.

While eating, I kept watch, scanning the shadows. Suddenly, the Maid noticed my tension and glanced over her shoulder. Indeed, a snake slithered among the rocks. Panic flared in her eyes, and we scrambled to find shelter. We located a small cave nestled in an upright rock wall, roughly a hundred feet from the fire. It was tall, dry, and empty—a perfect sanctuary.

Inside, the firelight filtered softly, creating a cozy haven. Yet, fear of the Land hung heavy in the air. Mine Own trusted me implicitly, believing I could protect her from any monster. This faith brought me immense comfort, tempered only by my own terror for her safety.

We slept that night, sharing a cloak. Though the fire offered little warmth, the cave was less biting than the open darkness. Twenty hours had elapsed since our last slumber, leaving us exhausted, though our minds remained alert to danger.

Seven hours into our rest, a sudden noise shattered the silence. A piercing scream tore through the night—the terrified cries of humans. My heart sank; these were the screams of the poor souls trapped in this nightmare land. Duty called me to rush to their aid, but I could not abandon the Maid.

Immediately, the night erupted in roars and shouts. Massive, husky voices echoed everywhere, accompanied by the thunderous tread of giants. The Maid trembled violently, clutching me as she recalled the horrors of her long wanderings. Even my courage faltered; these were the monstrous voices that haunted every step of my journey.

Then, a new sound pierced the chaos: the brutal slaughter of a young woman. My heart sickened for the Maid, while a surge of righteous fury threatened to consume me. The screaming ceased abruptly, replaced by more distant cries and the heavy footsteps of pursuers.

A cluster of ragged, naked humans ran past our hollow, gasping and weeping, driven like wild animals by unseen predators. Their plight stirred a fierce longing in me to intervene, but I restrained myself; leaving the Maid exposed was unthinkable. Just as I hesitated, four colossal figures burst from the darkness. Three were dull-skinned and bristling with hair, but the fourth was a horrifying, pale-white monstrosity blotched with lividity—a true man-beast filled with corruption. They vanished into the night, followed by the sound of a dying scream.

These creatures possessed no courage; they hunted rather than fought. I reflected on the history of the Lesser Redoubt peoples, starved and weakened over generations, lacking the spirit to resist such overwhelming odds.

Suddenly, I heard the Maid sobbing silently from the back of the cave. I reached to comfort her, but froze. A naked figure sprinted frantically along the hollow's edge, looking back over her shoulder. She dove under a low rock ledge, moving with desperate stealth.

A squat, bull-like brute emerged from the shadows, sniffing the air like a predator. He spotted her instantly and lunged silently.

Without hesitation, I leaped twenty feet down into the hollow. Anger fueled my jump, and I landed hard, unharmed. But it was too late. The brute seized the maiden, and she let out one final, dreadful scream before falling dead in his grasp.

Wrath blinded me. Blood boiled in my veins as I charged the monster. The Diskos spun in my hands, singing with rage. The brute turned, expecting an easy kill, but I struck. My blow missed as he dropped to his hands, dodging. He grabbed my legs, intending to tear me apart. I slashed wildly, severing one of his monstrous claws. He hurled me across the hollow; I hit the ground, my armor clanging, the Diskos ringing like a bell.
