As Gerard and Denys trudged through the freshly fallen snow, the tracks of countless wolves marked the road ahead, a testament to the creatures' unrelenting hunger. Gerard gazed down at the ground, his eyes tracing the faint impressions in the pristine white expanse.

"We did well to heed the old man's warning," Denys said, his voice low and thoughtful.

"Aye, we did," Gerard agreed, his brow furrowed in contemplation. "I recall hearing them prowling beneath our window last night, their mournful cries echoing through the market square. But none of the burghers showed mercy, tossing out scraps instead of succoring the famished creatures."

Gerard's eyes sparkled with amusement, though his expression remained abstract. The pair continued in silence, their footsteps the only sound breaking the stillness.

"What weighs so heavily on your mind?" Denys asked, his tone gentle.

"Your kindness," Gerard replied, his voice tinged with sincerity.

Denys's countenance darkened, his eyes flashing with indignation. Though he possessed a good-natured disposition, he was prone to outbursts of temper when provoked. "A civil question deserves a civil answer," he said dryly.

Gerard's smile faltered, and he spoke hastily. "I meant no offense, truly."

"Then why feign concern for my character, when you know I possess little virtue?" Denys demanded, his voice laced with sarcasm.

Gerard's gaze met Denys's, and he chose his words carefully. "If another had spoken thus, I would have retorted, 'You lie.' But to you, I say, 'You see only the virtues of women, but not those of men.' And I defy your unreasonable anger once more, saying that I was indeed pondering your kindness of heart. Had I wedded the 'Tete d'Or' instead of you, I would have been content with a life of quiet ease, leaving you to your solitary pursuits."

Denys's scowl softened, replaced by a hint of contrition. "Ah, lad, I see you're there. In truth, I wasn't thinking of your virtues, but of our friendship and the bond we share. You'd have been better off at the 'Tete d'Or,' basking in the warmth of her affection, while I'd have remained alone.
