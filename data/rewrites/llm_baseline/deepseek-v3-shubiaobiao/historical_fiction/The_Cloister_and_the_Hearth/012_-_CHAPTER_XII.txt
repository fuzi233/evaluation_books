Ghysbrecht Van Swieten clutched the key to Gerard’s cell in his fist, his patience wearing thin. *Let hunger teach him obedience*, he thought, waiting until the stroke of ten before ascending the stone steps, a loaf of bread and pitcher of water in hand, his servant trailing behind with a wary grip on his sword.

At the door, he paused, pressing his ear against the wood. Silence. A smirk curled his lips. *Good. Despair has dulled his defiance.* He turned the key and swung the door wide—only to freeze, his breath hitching in his throat.

The cell was empty.

Ghysbrecht’s body locked in shock, his mind refusing to accept the sight before him. The servant peered over his shoulder, eyes widening at the dangling rope, the displaced iron bar. “By all the saints—”

But Ghysbrecht did not hear him. His gaze snapped to the chest in the corner, and with a strangled cry, he lunged forward, clawing at the lid, his fingers trembling as they scraped over the empty interior.

“Gone!” His voice cracked like brittle parchment. “Gone—*all of it!*”

The servant recoiled. “Master? What’s lost?”

Ghysbrecht’s face twisted. “The—the town records,” he stammered, but the lie was thin. His mind raced, fury and terror warring within him. *The boy knew. He knew the secret.*

“A hundred crowns to the man who finds them!” he spat. “Every last scrap!”

The servant’s eyes gleamed. “Then the reward is as good as mine. Gerard must have taken them.”

Ghysbrecht seized him by the collar. “Then *move*, fool! Before he spreads what he’s seen!”

——

Less than an hour later, Dierich Brower stood in the Eliassoen home, flanked by armed men. Catherine wrung her hands. “What has my boy done now?”

Dierich’s smile was slick as oil. “A youthful prank, nothing more. But the burgomaster’s records are missing, and Gerard must answer for it.”

Kate, sharp-eyed, stepped forward. “Gerard is gone from Tergou,” she said coolly. “He left last night.”

Dierich’s mask slipped. “Where?”

“Far from here. He swore never to return.”

A lie. She *knew* it was a lie. But if Gerard had fled to *her*—Margaret—then every moment wasted put him in greater danger.

As Dierich stormed out, Kate caught Giles’s sleeve. “They mean him harm,” she whispered. “You must warn him. *Now.*”

Giles grumbled but relented. He would go—on foot, if he must.

Kate pressed a message into his hands, made him repeat it until the words were etched in his mind. Then she knelt, her prayers following him into the night.
