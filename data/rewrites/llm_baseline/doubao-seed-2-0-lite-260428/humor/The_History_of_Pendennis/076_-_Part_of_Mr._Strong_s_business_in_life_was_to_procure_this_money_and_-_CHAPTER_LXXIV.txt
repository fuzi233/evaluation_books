The train zipped Arthur off to Tunbridge far faster than he’d bargained for, though he had more than enough time on the 20-minute journey to marathon every bad life choice he’d ever made, like scrolling a folder labeled *Total Disaster Moments* at 2 a.m.
“Great, this is it,” he moped to himself. “All that romance, ambition, big fancy dreams? Down the drain. When I told my mom no to marrying Laura, I turned down an actual literal angel. And then when I caved to my uncle’s begging and chased Blanche, the parliamentary seat, and all that fat cash? Turns out the cash vanished, and I’m left holding a wife-to-be and zero of the nice stuff I agreed to settle for instead of a real heart. My old uncle doesn’t even get why I refuse to take that dodgy fortune, he’s madder than a wet hen that I’m being such a prude. I’m never pleased, I never make anyone else happy either. What’s Blanche even gonna get out of marrying a guy this much of a flop? Even if I wrote a bestseller, I’d be the first person to make fun of my own book. If I became a big lawyer, I’d have to bully witnesses and lie for a living, that’s not exactly a life to brag about.”
He glanced around the carriage. Across from him, a priest hadn’t lifted his nose from his prayer book the whole trip, except when they went through the Reigate tunnel where he couldn’t see a thing. Next to the priest, a grumpy old man was giving him the most unhinged over-the-top hater stare over his newspaper, clearly daydreaming about burning all clergymen at the stake. “Those guys don’t have any doubts,” Pen thought. “They’re just marching along totally sure they’re right. Lucky sods.”
“Care to borrow the paper, mate?” the stout man holding the hateful anti-clergy journal offered. Pen thanked him, took it, and proceeded to not read a single full sentence, too busy spiraling in his own head.
At last he pulled himself out of his moping, decided he was gonna be the world’s most honest, devoted boyfriend to Blanche, start fresh, stop being a sad sack, be a good husband and a good dad someday, raise kids with an honest name no fancy nonsense required. By the time the train pulled into Tunbridge, he was practically bouncing, carpet bag in hand, ready to face his fortune.
A hack cab zoomed him straight to Lady Clavering’s house, and he rehearsed his big noble speech the whole ride: *Blanche, I don’t get your last letter, but I’m here, I’m ready to marry you right this second, no fortune required, I’ll love you forever, the lot of it*.
He skidded up to the front door, where a servant he’d never seen before stared at him like he was a random vagrant who’d wandered in off the street, and didn’t even reach for his carpet bag. “Her Ladyship’s not at home, sir,” he said.
“I’m Mr. Pendennis,” Arthur said. “Where’s Lightfoot the usual butler?”
“Lightfoot got sacked,” the guy said. “My orders are-”
“I can hear Miss Amory singing in the drawing room,” Pen interrupted, shoving past him. “Take my bag to a spare room, yeah?”
He waltzed right into the drawing room, where Blanche was warbling a super dramatic sad love ballad at the top of her lungs. Little Frank Clavering was half-asleep on the sofa not caring a jot about the music, and right next to Blanche at the piano, gawking at her like she’d invented ice cream, was Harry Foker.
Everyone jumped. The music stopped mid-note. Frank woke up, looked at all three of them, went “Oh! This is a right old mess!” and sniggered so hard he bolted out of the room before anyone could say a word.
Right on the piano, next to Blanche’s elbow, was the exact same purple leather jewelry box Pen had seen Foker carrying outside a jeweler’s three days prior. It was open, displaying the gaudiest snake bracelet you ever saw, with a ruby the size of a marble for a head and diamonds all down the tail.
“Uh… hey Pendennis,” Foker said, turning so red his ears matched the ruby on the bracelet. Blanche squawked, shoved her handkerchief over the bracelet like it was a dirty magazine a parent just walked in on, and tried to play polite. “How is dearest Laura?” she stammered.
Pen couldn’t even be mad. He burst out cackling so loud the chandelier rattled.
“Oh this is the secret, is it?” he wheezed. “Don’t hide, Foker you daft loyal thing. I’d never get between you and Blanche, not when you’re this smitten, not when you’ve got 15 thousand a year to spoil her rotten.”
“It’s not the money!” Blanche declared, striking a dramatic noble pose. “It’s not rank, it’s not gold! It’s his constancy, his big faithful loving heart that I care about! I once thought I loved *you*, Arthur, I truly did, but you were always so cold and heartless, you trifled with my poor maiden heart and threw my affection back in my face! I explained all of it to Harry, he knows the whole story of how cruel you were to me.”
“Every last bit of it,” Foker confirmed, nodding so hard his hat almost fell off.
Pen just shrugged. “Fair enough. I’m not gonna argue. I came down here ready to make you an honest offer, but clearly you’re far happier here. I hope you two have a great time, Foker, you deserve every bit of happiness for being this loyal. Shake my hand, mate, yeah? You gonna tell everyone I’m a total cad?”
“I think you’re a-” Foker started to splutter, before Blanche cut him off.
“Henry! No fighting!” she cried, staring up at the chandelier like it was sending her angelic blessings. “We must forgive him! Arthur is like a brother to me! We grew up together! Arthur, I forgive you with all my heart, for making me realize what real love is! Shake hands, you two, be friends!”
Foker was so flattered he was sniffling. “Oh my gosh you’re an actual angel. I’d kiss the ground if she told me to, I love her that much. C’mon Pendennis, shake, it’s fine, if she forgives you I forgive you too. I love anyone she says is okay.”
Blanche let him kiss her hand, all seraphic and soft, hiding her eyes behind her handkerchief like she was weeping happy tears.
Then Frank Clavering wandered back in. “Oi, Pendennis, your cabbie’s been waiting forever, he drank all the beer the kitchen gave him and he’s threatening to leave without you if you don’t pay him.”
“I’ll go right now,” Pen said, ready to bolt for the hills.
“Wait, one private word,” Blanche said, shooing Foker and Frank out to the hall to smoke a cigar.
When they were alone, she dropped the whole angel act immediately. “You’re gonna run right back and marry Laura, aren’t you? You never loved me. You’re such an idiot, Arthur. You gave up that parliamentary seat, all that power, all that fancy status, over a stupid silly scruple? Why wouldn’t you take the money that’s rightfully mine? I know all about my dad, by the way. I found out ages ago. It’s no big deal. I’m gonna marry Harry, I’ll wait until after the wedding to tell him about my dad, no one’s gonna spill the secret, right? You won’t tell him, will you? I need excitement, I need big feelings, and Harry gives them to me. You’re too boring, you’re over all your ambitions already, you’d never make me feel special in society.”
Pen stared at her, suddenly realizing every single emotion she’d ever shown him was fake. Her passion, her anger, her sadness, all of it was just a cheap little performance she pulled out whenever it suited her, flaring bright for five minutes before fizzling out completely.
He waved goodbye, walked out to the cab, and paid the driver, snickering to himself. Man, he really should have bought that return ticket before he got on the train. This whole trip was the dumbest wild goose chase he’d ever gone on.
