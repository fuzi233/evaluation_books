The moon cast an ethereal glow over the clearing as the Little Ones huddled together, their faces etched with worry and fear. I stood amidst them, surveying the scene with a practiced eye. Our queen lay motionless, her beauty now twisted into a grim visage that seemed to hold a dark and ancient power. The children's sobs filled the air, a mournful melody that wove itself into the fabric of the night.

As I took in the scene, a murmur of recognition rippled through the group. Their companions, riding in on Lona's horse, drew near, their faces pale and drawn. The Little Ones parted to let them pass, their cries growing louder as they surrounded their fallen queen. I yielded my burden to them, feeling a pang of sorrow as they received it with such tender desperation. Despite their attempts to rouse her, she remained still, her eyes closed as if in a deep sleep.

The children's words tumbled forth in a jumbled mix of fear and confusion. Some spoke of a man who had come down the hill from the palace, his presence spreading like a dark stain across the landscape. Others described a shadow that had engulfed them, its blackness seeping into their very souls. I listened intently, my mind racing with the implications of their stories.

Odu, ever the voice of reason, broke through the chaos. "I have seen that woman before," he whispered, his eyes fixed on the queen. "She fought the white leopardess, the night they woke us with their roaring." His companion shook his head, but Odu persisted. "Look at her eyes! They're like nothing I've ever seen before. She's a wild beast, just like the leopardess."

As I watched, a shiver ran down my spine. There was indeed something feral about the queen's gaze, something that spoke of a deep and ancient power. I placed a reassuring hand on Odu's shoulder. "She's the princess, boy. Wickedness has taken its toll on her, that's all."

Her eyes snapped open, and for an instant, I thought I saw a glimmer of recognition. Then, her face contorted in a mixture of anger and sorrow. "It was very wrong of me to run away!" Odu exclaimed, his voice barely above a whisper.

I leaned in, my voice low and gentle. "What made you run, Odu? I expected to find you where I left you."
