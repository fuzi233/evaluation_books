In the wake of their calamitous shipwreck, my wife's thoughts turned to the practicalities of their precarious situation. As she recounted, her initial day on the island was consumed by a mix of anxiety and observation, monitoring my every move and responding to my signals. Rising before dawn, she took heart in seeing my reassuring gesture that our little family was safe, and quickly replied with her own message. While our sons slept, she devoted herself to strategizing our next steps.

"The current circumstances are untenable," she mused, echoing my own sentiments. "This exposed rocky outcropping, exposed to the scorching sun, is an unsuitable place to call home. Our makeshift tent offers little respite from the heat, and we cannot sustain ourselves for much longer." She proposed that we, like my sons and I, should strive to contribute meaningfully to our survival. "Why not seek out a more hospitable location, a shaded clearing where we might establish a more comfortable base? I'm convinced that among those verdant woods and groves, we'll find a suitable haven, and I intend to locate it."

Upon waking, our youngest son, Jack, was already busy working with his knife, carefully preparing a piece of the jackal's hide. He had envisioned a means to create a makeshift dog collar, using the animal's skin as a durable material. He meticulously cleaned and scraped the skin, then inserted nails through the skin's edges, securing them in place. With a deft touch, he wrapped sailcloth around the raw skin, ensuring the nail heads were hidden from view.

As Jack requested my assistance, stitching the canvas and skin together, I was struck by his dedication to the task. Initially, I hesitated to intervene, allowing him to take pride in his handiwork. However, witnessing his persistence and good-natured attitude, I eventually decided to lend a hand, finishing the collar and a matching belt he had fashioned for himself. I offered words of encouragement, suggesting that he think of a method to prevent the skin from shrinking in the heat.

Ernest, ever the pragmatist, chimed in with a reasonable suggestion, which Jack promptly implemented. By nailing the skin to a wooden board and exposing it to the sun, he allowed it to dry and harden. Our plan to venture deeper into the island was met with enthusiasm by both boys.
