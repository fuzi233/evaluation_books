In the dimly lit study, the air was heavy with the scent of stale smoke and worn leather. Lady Evelyn Fanshawe sat perched on the edge of her chair, her slender fingers drumming a staccato beat on the armrest as she gazed out the window at the rain-soaked gardens below. The rhythmic patter of the droplets on the leaded panes was a melancholy serenade, one that echoed the somber mood that had settled over the household since the tragic events of the previous night.

Her thoughts were interrupted by the soft creak of the door as Mr. Gryce entered, his eyes scanning the room with a practiced intensity. Beside him, the mute butler, Jenkins, stood frozen, his gaze fixed on the spot where his master lay recumbent on the bearskin rug. It was as if time itself had been arrested, leaving only the echoes of violence and death to reverberate through the space.

Gryce's voice cut through the silence, his words a stern command that sent a shiver down Jenkins' spine. "Speak, man! Who killed your master?" The butler's response was a jarring cacophony of guttural sounds, a language that defied comprehension. With a jerky motion, he touched his ears and lips, as if pleading for understanding, before violently shaking his head in negation.

The shock of this revelation was palpable, and Styles, who had been watching the scene unfold, took a step back, his face pale. "Deaf and dumb," he muttered, as if the words themselves were a curse. The butler's eyes, however, betrayed a different story – a spark of malevolent glee that danced in their depths like a malignant fire.

As the minutes ticked by, Jenkins reenacted the events of the previous night, his movements a macabre dance that left the assembled group transfixed. His performance was a twisted parody of grief, a mockery of the emotions that should have been his own. When he finished, he stood waiting, his eyes glinting with an otherworldly light that seemed to mock the very concept of mortality.

Styles' patience was wearing thin. "Can't you speak?" he demanded, his voice rising in exasperation. "Can't you hear?" The butler's response was a sly, wicked smile, one that seemed to say he knew secrets that would never be revealed.
