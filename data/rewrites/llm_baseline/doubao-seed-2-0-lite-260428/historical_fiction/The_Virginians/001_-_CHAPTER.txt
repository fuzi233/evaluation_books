We open our tale with young Harry Warrington crossing the Atlantic for the first time, bound for the old Esmond family estates in England, little guessing the steep, unexpected price he will pay for his very first supper on foreign soil. We shall first introduce you to the sprawling, sun-dappled Tidewater plantation of Castlewood in Virginia, where the ancient, storied Esmond line has taken deep root across two generations, before Harry stumbles on a long-lost kinsman he never knew existed, touching off the small, sharp family frictions that no great landed clan ever escapes.

The two Warrington boys, George and Harry, will soon leave their Virginia home to see the wider world, just as the drums of the French and Indian War begin to beat across the colonies. George will first fall victim to the universal young man’s ailment of head-over-heels, all-consuming infatuation, before the pair are welcomed into the warm, generous hospitality of neighbours and strangers alike on a sweltering mid-summer Virginia afternoon. Soon two men both named George — our hero George Warrington, and the young up-and-coming colonial colonel George Washington — will gird their swords to march against the French, and dispatches back from the front will lead Harry on a foolish, fruitless quest across hundreds of miles of wilderness, until he at last takes ship for England.

He will find himself a welcome guest at the ancient English Castlewood manor on a soft, quiet 1750s Sunday, where his faithful valet Gumbo will astonish the stuffy local gentry with his unexpected skill at the ancient English sport of archery. The scent of half-forgotten secrets will hang thick in the halls of the old house, and a long-buried old family romance will come spilling out, tangled up both in unlooked-for good fortune and the giddy, heedless pull of reckless young love. The path of ease and indulgence is all too easy for a young colonial heir to slip down, but he will soon find kind Samaritans to catch him when he stumbles, and a stretch of gentle convalescence in a quiet country inn, followed by bright, giddy holidays that carry him from the sleepy village of Oakhurst all the way to the fashionable spa at Tunbridge Wells, where he will make a host of new acquaintances that carry him far, far away from the quiet plantation life he once knew.

Chance will pile on top of chance as he gambles his way through the London pleasure districts, treading all the familiar crooked paths of the 18th century metropolis, whiling away days in idle, unhonoured ease before he pens a long, fond letter back home to his kin in Virginia. Soon he will find himself caught between a fiery political agitator and a surly, quarrelsome bear of a man, a gilded family coach will be ordered to carry the party cross-country, and sharp-tongued old Hester will deliver a long, bitter soliloquy over the state of the household. Mr. George Warrington will host a lavish tea party and a grand ball for his new London acquaintances, but the festivities will quickly tangle the pair up in messy romantic and financial entrapments that all but spell ruin. A string of duels will be fought over petty slights and contested honour, the blustering parson Sampson will face down his own personal Philistines, and Harry will step in to save the day more than once, paying off a decades-old family debt even as he racks up crushing new ones of his own, falling into the heedless, reckless life of a rake that carries him to dizzying social heights before the fate everyone expected finally arrives to bring him low.

Harry will stumble across two uncles he never knew he had, find himself tangled in the chains of a loveless engagement, welcome troubled visitors to his London lodgings, and start at an apparition that appears out of the London fog one night to prove an unexpected friend in need. We shall lay out pages of the finest, most earnest moralising over the follies of young men, then fall quiet as a grave at the climax of a long-awaited revelation, every eye fixed and every mouth stilled, before we settle into the glittering, gilded Court end of London society. Harry will spend many quiet nights smoking his pipe by his fireside, the two brothers will have long, earnest talks that mend old rifts, and one young woman left pining will find herself abandoned like Ariadne on her empty island. Harry will time and again find himself pushed out of favour, left to watch from the sidelines as others take the limelight, before the pair attend a raucous night at the play, where a performance of *Macbeth*, a midnight supper, and a very messy misunderstanding leaves everyone in a fine kettle of fish. The young pretender to the crown will march his troops up the hill and down again, and swords will be drawn as the two men head off to war, following the tragic muse Melpomene through battlefields that Harry will survive to tell the tale, coming home at last to the warm welcome of a soldier long returned.

He will soon set off to court the lady of his heart, only for a private domestic tragedy to play out, followed by two new romances just beginning. Harry will head west across the Atlantic to return to his Virginia home, where a sweet, sheltered young innocent catches his eye, and Cupid will weave his mischief through half the households of the Tidewater, handing out favours and breaking promises in equal measure. We draw on the old Warrington family manuscript to tell you how our lady of the manor climbed to the very top of the social ladder, and how the whole clan gathered to keep Christmas at Castlewood in the frosty winter of 1759. News will come sailing across the ocean of Britain’s great victory over France in Canada, the course of true love will run all its expected bumpy paths, before Mr. Warrington makes a wild, impulsive leap into a waiting Landau coach that somehow sees every last passenger clamber out safe and sound. The pair of star-crossed lovers separated by family secrets will play out their own Pyramus and Thisbe farce, mixing bright comedy and sharp tragedy in equal measure, before a bright young mixed-race girl known to all as Pocahontas changes the whole shape of the family’s fate. Hard times will fall on the household, the last of old Miley’s precious gold moidore will be spent through a run of bad luck, and the family will find troubles and small, quiet consolations to carry them through. Harry will meet the same fate that all men must, but at last he will find his safe harbour, to settle at home under his own vine and fig tree, long after the old royalist anthem *God Save the King* has faded to be replaced by the sprightly new tune of Yankee Doodle marching through the towns of a new nation. He will find himself a colonel with no regiment to lead, fight a battle and run away with honour, satisfied that he has fought enough fights for one lifetime, at last to rest in peace — and this is the full tale of *The Virginians*.
