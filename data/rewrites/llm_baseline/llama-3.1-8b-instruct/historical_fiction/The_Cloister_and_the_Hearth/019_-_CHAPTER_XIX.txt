As the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, Gerard hastily roused Martin from his slumber. The old soldier's eyes widened in surprise, for he had assumed Gerard had slipped away unnoticed through the window the previous night. Gerard, sensing the urgency of their situation, sought Martin's counsel on how to evade the pursuit that had been dogging their footsteps. Martin, with his years of experience, advised that there was only one safe route to take: a winding bridle-road through the nearby forest, which would lead them to a nearby hostelry where they could acquire a swift horse and continue their journey out of Holland.

Without hesitation, the pair set off, Martin armed with his trusty bow and quiver of arrows, while Gerard grasped the sturdy oak staff provided by Peter. Margaret, donning a practical cloak and hat, bid farewell to her husband and father, her eyes filled with worry as she watched them disappear into the forest. Peter, usually a stoic man, couldn't help but offer a warm blessing as he accompanied them to the forest's edge.

As they entered the forest, the dew-kissed grass beneath their feet glistened like a canvas of diamonds, and the sweet scent of wildflowers wafted through the air. Gerard felt invigorated by the prospect of escaping their pursuers, but Margaret's anxious glances kept him alert to their surroundings. Suddenly, she let out a terrified cry, and, driven by instinct, sprinted towards the forest, her voice echoing through the trees.

Ghysbrecht and his men, hot on their heels, emerged from the mist, their faces set with determination. Martin and Gerard knew resistance was futile, and they followed Margaret's example, dashing deeper into the forest. The pursuers gained ground, but Martin's words of encouragement – "Just make it to the wood!" – kept their spirits high, as if the very trees themselves were reaching out to shield them with their branches.

However, an unexpected threat materialized as Ghysbrecht, mounted on his mule, charged towards them, intent on cutting them off. His goal was to isolate Gerard, the man he saw as a threat to his authority. Margaret, desperate to protect Gerard, clung to him, but he shook her off, his eyes blazing with a fierce determination.
