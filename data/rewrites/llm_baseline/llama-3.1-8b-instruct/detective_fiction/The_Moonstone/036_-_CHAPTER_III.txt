As I sat beside Mr. Murthwaite in the dining room, the warmth of the fire and the rich aromas wafting from the roasting meats enveloped us like a comforting blanket. The evening's festivities had begun to wind down, and the gentlemen, now free from the polite constraints of the ladies' presence, turned their attention to the evening's main course – a sumptuous roasted haunch of venison. The conversation, as was to be expected, veered towards the topic of politics, a subject that always seemed to stir the passions of our English friends.

Personally, I have never been one for the cutthroat world of politics, finding it as dull as a poorly seasoned stew. Mr. Murthwaite, however, was a different matter altogether. As he sipped his wine, his eyes seemed to glaze over, and I could sense his mind wandering towards the realms of Morpheus. I decided to test his mettle, and gently steer the conversation towards a more intriguing topic – the mysterious Moonstone.

"Mr. Murthwaite," I began, "I believe you were acquainted with the late Lady Verinder, and that you took a keen interest in the strange events surrounding the Moonstone's disappearance?" My words landed like a sprinkle of paprika on a well-seasoned dish, awakening the traveler from his reverie.

He looked at me with a hint of surprise, and then his eyes narrowed as he sought to place me. "Ah, yes... Mr. Bruff, I believe? A solicitor, if I'm not mistaken?"

I nodded, and introduced myself, reminding him of my connection to the Herncastle family and my role in the Moonstone's storied past. Mr. Murthwaite shifted in his seat, positioning himself to face me directly, and turned away from the rest of the company. His eyes locked onto mine, and I could sense a spark of interest ignite within him.

"Tell me, Mr. Bruff," he said, his voice low and measured, "have you heard anything of late about the Indians involved in this affair?"

I leaned forward, intrigued by the turn the conversation had taken. "I had an unexpected visitor in my office yesterday, Mr. Murthwaite. One of the Indians involved in the conspiracy."

Mr. Murthwaite's expression changed from curiosity to surprise, and then to a look of intense interest. "Go on," he urged, his voice barely above a whisper.
