Silence fell over the rocky terrain like a shroud, a stillness that only the occasional faint breeze broke. I stood beside Coppinger, surveying the landscape of twisted rocks and dusty scrub. Our footsteps, light as they were, seemed to echo through the desolate expanse. The air was heavy with the scent of salt and damp earth, a reminder of the recent rain that had left its mark on this unforgiving land. We had risen with the sun, the only sound the creaking of our joints as we moved. 

Coppinger, ever the enthusiast, began to demonstrate his latest theory on the benefits of morning exercise. His lumpy figure wobbled as he moved through a series of awkward stretches, a sight that would have been comical in another context. I followed his lead, my own body protesting the sudden exertion. Our conversation was limited to the essentials – water, food, and the pressing need to press on. The fonda at Santa Brigida lay several miles distant, and we had yet to replenish our supplies.

As we walked, the landscape shifted, the terrain becoming increasingly treacherous. The barranca below us was a dry riverbed, its surface cracked and barren. A small trickle of water remained, and we made our way down to refresh ourselves. The toothbrush, a luxury we had carefully preserved, felt like a small indulgence in this harsh environment. Our conversation turned to the matter at hand – the search for the lost university. Coppinger's eyes sparkled with excitement as he outlined his plans for further exploration.

I couldn't help but feel a sense of resignation wash over me. We had already uncovered two mummies, their brittle skin releasing dust with every touch. Coppinger, however, remained undeterred, convinced that we were on the brink of a major discovery. The flashlight and camera, his trusty companions, seemed to hold a strange allure for him. As we ascended the cliff, the sun beating down upon us, I couldn't help but wonder how much longer we would continue this pursuit. The thrill of the unknown had begun to wear thin, replaced by the dull ache of fatigue and the creeping realization that we might be chasing a dream.
