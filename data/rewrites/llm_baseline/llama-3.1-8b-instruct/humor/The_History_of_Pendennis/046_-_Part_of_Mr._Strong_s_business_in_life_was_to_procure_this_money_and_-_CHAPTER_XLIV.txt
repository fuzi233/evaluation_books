Colonel Altamont emerged from his chamber in the upper story at Shepherd's Inn, the morning light streaming in through the window casting a warm glow on his weathered features. He descended to the sitting room, where the Chevalier Strong sat engrossed in the newspaper, a cigar dangling from his lips. The air was thick with the scent of freshly brewed coffee and the faint tang of cigar smoke. Strong, a man who took pleasure in making his surroundings comfortable, had already partaken of a hearty breakfast, courtesy of Mr. Grady's culinary skills. His easy chair, adorned with a plush cushion, seemed tailored to his frame, and he leaned back, a look of contentment on his face.

Altamont's arrival was met with a warm smile from Strong, who set aside his paper and regarded his fellow lodger with a mixture of amusement and curiosity. Despite their differences, the two men had maintained a cordial relationship, Strong's dislike of Altamont tempered by a sense of obligation and Altamont's good-natured demeanor. The night before, Strong had seen Altamont retire to bed with a full glass in hand, only to find the bottle empty upon waking. Undeterred, Strong had helped himself to a glass of water, savoring the quiet moment before turning in.

As Altamont entered, he let out a hearty bellow for Grady, requesting a pint of pale ale. Strong chuckled, knowing the Colonel's fondness for the brew, and poured the ale into a pewter flagon, which Altamont then drained with relish. Wiping his mouth with the back of his hand, Altamont declared himself thoroughly revitalized by the drink. "There's nothing quite like that beer," he said, "especially when the coppers are hot. I recall many a time I've quaffed a dozen pints of Bass in Calcutta, and...and..."

Strong laughed, interrupting the Colonel's reminiscence. "And at Lucknow, I suppose?" he added, his eyes twinkling with mirth. Altamont nodded, launching into a tale of his exploits the previous evening. "I'm afraid I'm a bit of a madman when I've had a few drinks," he confessed, running a hand through his graying hair.
