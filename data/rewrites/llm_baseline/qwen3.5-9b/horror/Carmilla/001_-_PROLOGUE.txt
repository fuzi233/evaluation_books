Tacked to the manuscript that follows is a lengthy note penned by Dr. Hesselius. In it, he refers readers to his own treatise on the peculiar phenomenon illuminated by these pages—a subject he addresses therein with his customary erudition, sharp insight, and striking conciseness. That essay stands alone within the forthcoming collection of this extraordinary scholar's works. Since my purpose here is merely to engage the general public, I shall allow the intelligent woman who recounts this tale to speak without interruption. After much deliberation, I have resolved to omit any summary of the Doctor's complex reasoning or excerpts from his remarks on a matter he deems "to involve, not improbably, some of the most profound mysteries of our dual nature and its intermediate states."

When I first uncovered this document, I felt compelled to revive the correspondence begun decades ago between Dr. Hesselius and his remarkably astute and meticulous informant. Alas, to my deep regret, I discovered that she had passed away during the intervening years. Had she lived, she might have contributed little more than what she has already communicated in the following pages, rendered with such scrupulous attention to detail that, as far as I can judge, leaves nothing to be desired.
