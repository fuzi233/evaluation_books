"The House of Death"

The Mother of Sorrows rose, muffling her face against the chill, and summoned the Little Ones. They had slept as though suspended in amber, unmoving through the long vigil, yet the moment her voice cut the silence, they sprang to their feet—fresh, vibrant, as if newly forged. With cheerful steps, they trailed her down the stairs, where the Princess lay, her tears still carving rivulets down her pale cheeks. The children's joyous expressions hardened instantly into solemn gravity. They cast glances from the weeping Princess to the storm outside, then back to her face.

"The sky is collapsing!" declared one.

"The white fluid is spilling from the Princess!" cried another, his voice trembling with awe.

"Are these rivers?" asked Odu, staring intently at the thin streams cascading down her hollowed cheeks.

"Yes," answered Mara, "the most magnificent of all rivers."

"I thought rivers were vast and furious, rushing like a legion of Little Ones, screaming with noise!" he countered, turning his gaze to me—the only adult present who had spoken of such things.

"Behold the rivers of the heavens!" Mara commanded. "Watch how they descend to awaken the waters beneath the earth. Soon, rivers will flow everywhere, joyful and thunderous, like thousands upon thousands of happy children. Oh, how they will bring you delight, Little Ones! You have never witnessed them, nor known the purity of water!"

"That signifies the gladness of the ground because the Princess has become virtuous," Odu observed. "Observe the gladness of the sky!"

"Are the rivers the gladness of the Princess?" queried Luva. "They are not her blood, for they are not red!"

"They are the essence within the essence," Mara corrected gently.

Odu placed a single finger to his eye, inspected it, and shook his head in confusion.

"The Princess will not bite anymore!" Luva announced confidently.

"No; she shall never repeat such folly," Mara replied. "--But now we must transport her closer to home."

"Is that a nest?" Sozo pointed upward.

"Yes; a very large nest. However, we must relocate her to another location first."

"What is that place?"

"It is the largest room in this entire world. --Though I suspect it is scheduled for demolition; it will soon be overcrowded with little nests. Go and retrieve your burdens."

"Please, are there any cats in it?"

"Not one. The nests are saturated with exquisite dreams; there is no room for a cat to intrude."

"We shall be ready momentarily," Odu stated, sprinting out, followed by all but Luva.

Lilith had awakened, listening with a sorrowful smile etched upon her features.

"But her rivers are flowing so swiftly!" Luva exclaimed, standing beside her, transfixed by the sight of her face. "Her robe is... I cannot describe it. The clumsy ones will not approve!"

"They will not mind," Mara assured her. "Those rivers are so pure they cleanse the entire world."

I had succumbed to sleep by the fire earlier, but I had been awake and attentive for some time, and now I rose.

"It is time to ascend, Mr. Vane," our hostess said.

"Tell me, please," I asked, "is there no route to bypass the channels and the den of monsters?"

"There exists a direct path across the riverbed, which I will demonstrate," she answered; "however, you must traverse the monsters once more."

"I fear for the children," I admitted.

"Fear will not approach them," she rejoined firmly.

We departed the cottage. The beasts awaited at the threshold. Odu was already perched atop the neck of one of the two carriers designated for the Princess. I mounted Lona's horse; Mara lifted her burden and handed her into my arms. Upon her return with the Princess, a cry of triumph erupted from the children: she was no longer veiled! Gazing at her, mesmerized by her ethereal beauty, the boys forgot to accept the Princess from her grasp; instead, the elephants embraced Lilith tenderly with their trunks—one encircling her torso, the other her knees—and, with Mara's assistance, laid her gently between them.

"Why does the Princess wish to depart?" a small boy questioned. "She would remain virtuous if she stayed here!"

"She wishes to go, and she resists going; we are aiding her," Mara explained. "She cannot remain virtuous here."

"To where exactly are you assisting her?" the boy persisted.

"To a place where she receives greater aid—assistance to open her hand, which has been sealed for a millennium."

"For so long? Then she has mastered living without it; why reopen it now?"

"Because it is clamped upon something that belongs to no one else."

"Please, Lady Mara, may we have some of your dry bread before we leave?" Luva requested.

Mara smiled, producing four loaves and a massive jug of water.

"We shall dine as we travel," they declared. Yet they consumed the water with evident delight.

"I believe," remarked one of them, "this must be elephant-juice! It renders me incredibly strong!"

We set forth, the Lady of Sorrow walking alongside us, radiating a brilliance surpassing the sun, with the white leopardess trailing in her wake. I initially assumed she intended merely to escort us to the path across the channels, but I quickly realized she accompanied us the entire journey. I attempted to dismount so she might ride, but she refused permission.

"I bear no burden," she stated. "The children and I shall walk together."

It was the most resplendent morning imaginable; the sun burned with maximum intensity, and the wind sang its sweetest melody, yet these comforts failed to soothe the desert, for it possessed no water.

We traversed the channels without incident, the children frolicking around Mara throughout the trek, failing to reach the summit of the ridge overlooking the bad burrow until the sun was already beginning its descent. Realizing the moon might arrive late and fearing for the children's safety, I ordered the Little Ones to mount their elephants.

The Lady of Sorrow now led the way beside me; the elephants followed—the two bearing the Princess in the center; the leopardess brought up the rear. Just as we neared the terrifying precipice, the moon ascended, illuminating the shallow basin awaiting us, undisturbed. Mara stepped into it; not a ripple disturbed the surface, nor did the soil beneath my horse shift. But the instant the elephants carrying the Princess touched the ground, the seemingly solid earth began to heave and churn, awakening the entire dread brood of the hellish nest. Monsters surged upward on all sides, every neck extended, every beak and claw outstretched, every mouth gaping wide. Long-billed heads, grotesquely jawed visages, and countless knotted tentacles lunged toward Lilith. She lay paralyzed in agony of fear, daring not move a finger. Whether the hideous entities even perceived the children remains doubtful; certainly, not one touched a child, nor did any loathsome limb penetrate the living rampart of her bodyguard to seize her.

"Little Ones," I shouted, "keep your elephants tightly clustered around the Princess. Be courageous; they will not harm you."

"What will not harm us? We do not know what to be brave against!" they replied, unaware of the monstrosities surrounding them.

"Never mind," I returned; "simply stay close."

They were armored in their ignorance! Inability to perceive was their salvation. Whatever they could not possibly comprehend posed no threat.

Yet the horrific forms I witnessed that night! Mara advanced several paces ahead of me when a solitary, disembodied head bounced along the path between us. The leopardess charged from behind the elephants, intending to seize it, but with frightful contortions of visage and a loathsome howl, the entity executed a rapid rotary twist, leapt from her grasp, and burrowed itself into the earth. The death in my arms assuaging my terror, I regarded the spectacle with equanimity, though surely nowhere else in existence could such a cursed assembly be beheld!

Mara continued ahead, and the leopardess now walked closely behind her, shivering frequently, for the air was biting cold. Suddenly, the ground to my left began to heave, and a low wave of earth slithered toward us. Rising higher as it approached, a dreadful head emerged, adorned with fleshy tubes for hair. Opening a great oval maw, it snapped at me. The leopardess sprang but fell baffled, unable to intercept it.

Barely beneath our feet, the head of an enormous snake shot upward, its eyes glowing with a lamp-like, wallowing glare. Again the leopardess rushed to attack, finding nothing. Against a third monster she darted with equal fury, met with identical failure, and then sullenly ceased to heed the phantom horde. But I understood the peril and hastened the crossing—all the more so because the moon behaved strangely. Even as it rose, it appeared ready to collapse, abandoning the ascent as hopeless; indeed, I watched it sink back nearly its own breadth. The arc it traced was exceedingly low, and now it descended rapidly.
