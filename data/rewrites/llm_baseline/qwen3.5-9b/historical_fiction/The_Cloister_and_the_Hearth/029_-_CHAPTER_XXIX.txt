Gerard stood stunned by this abrupt revelation, his complexion shifting rapidly between pale fear and flushed anger. He ground his teeth in fury, recalling how even the most spirited man resembles a wild boar: he may flee a superior force simply because he lacks the stupidity to stand his ground, yet if pursued relentlessly and driven to desperation, he will turn upon his pursuers with terrifying ferocity. Resolving to fight, Gerard squared his shoulders for battle; but instantly, his resolve crumbled, and he whispered with anguish, "But my axe lies in the Rhine."

Together, they conferred. Prudence urged them to bypass the village entirely, while hunger demanded they procure food. Though hunger's voice was loud, Prudence argued with greater conviction. They agreed to cut across the open fields instead. Halting beside a haystack, they secured two bundles and lay concealed within a dry ditch, hidden from view though stinging by nettles. Taking turns, they ventured forth and returned with turnips, gnawing on these meager provisions at intervals throughout their flight until sunset arrived.

Soon, they crawled out, shivering violently into the driving rain and encroaching darkness, emerging onto the road beyond the village. It was a night of dismal gloom, black as pitch, with winds howling fiercely. They were invisible, unheard, and unable to see or hear anything; indeed, they might have slipped past their enemies like ghosts, unnoticed amidst the natural terrors of the stormy night. In this chaos, where they seemed to claw their way through solid black marble, they nearly forgot the presence of their foes. When the moon finally rose, they had traveled leagues away from Düsseldorf, yet they continued trudging onward.

Eventually, a massive structure loomed before them. "Take courage!" cried Denys. "I believe I recognize this convent. Yes, it is so! We are now within the diocese of Jülich; Cologne holds no sway here." With those words, they crossed the threshold and found themselves safe within the sanctuary walls.
