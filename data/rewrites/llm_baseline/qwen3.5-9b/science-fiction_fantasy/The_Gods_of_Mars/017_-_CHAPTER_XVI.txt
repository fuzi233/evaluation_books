As Carthoris, Xodar, Tars Tarkas, and I stood transfixed before the magnificent vessel that held such profound significance for us all, we watched as a second, then a third ship crested the distant hills, gliding gracefully to join their sisters. Soon, a squadron of single-seat air scouts launched from the upper decks of the nearest battleship, diving swiftly toward the ground surrounding us. Within moments, we were encircled by armed sailors. Just as an officer stepped forward to address us, his gaze landed on Carthoris.

With an exclamation of startled joy, he rushed forward, seizing the boy's shoulders. "Carthoris, my Prince!" he cried. "Kaor! Kaor! Hor Vastus greets the son of Dejah Thoris, Princess of Helium, and her husband, John Carter. Where have you been, O my Prince? All Helium has been plunged in sorrow. Terrible calamities have befallen your great-grandfather's mighty nation since the fatal day you left our midst."

"Grieve not, my good Hor Vastus," Carthoris replied warmly. "I do not return alone to cheer my mother's heart and the hearts of my beloved people, but I also bring back the one whom all Barsoom loved best—her greatest warrior and her savior, John Carter, Prince of Helium!"

Hor Vastus turned in the direction Carthoris indicated. When his eyes fell upon me, he seemed poised to collapse from sheer astonishment. "John Carter!" he gasped. A troubled look instantly clouded his features. "My Prince," he stammered, "where hast thou..." and then he halted, unable to voice the terrifying truth he suspected. The loyal soldier dared not force from my lips the confession that I had returned from the bosom of the Iss, the River of Mystery, escaping the shores of the Lost Sea of Korus and the Valley Dor.

"Ah, my Prince," he continued smoothly, as if no interruption had occurred, "your return is sufficient. Let Hor Vastus' sword have the honor of falling first at your feet."

With these words, the nobleman unbuckled his scabbard and cast his sword onto the ground before me. If you understand the customs and character of the Red Martians, you would grasp the depth of meaning behind this simple gesture. It was an absolute pledge: *My sword, my body, my life, and my soul are yours to command. Until death and beyond, I look solely to you for authority. Whether you are right or wrong, your word is my only truth. Whoever raises a hand against you answers to my blade.* It is the ultimate oath of fealty, rarely paid to a Jeddak unless his character and chivalry have inspired such fervent devotion. Never before had I witnessed such a high tribute bestowed upon a mere mortal.

There was only one appropriate response. I stooped, picked up the sword, kissed the hilt, and then stepped to Hor Vastus, buckling the weapon onto his hip with my own hands.

"Hor Vastus," I said, placing a hand on his shoulder, "you know the promptings of your own heart best. I doubt I shall need your sword often, but accept from John Carter, upon his sacred honor, the assurance that I will never call upon you to draw this blade except in the cause of truth, justice, and righteousness."

"That I knew, my Prince," he replied, "before I ever threw my beloved blade at your feet."

As we spoke, more flyers darted between the ground and the battleship. Presently, a larger transport boat, capable of carrying a dozen passengers, lowered itself gently nearby. As it touched down, an officer leaped from the deck to the ground and advanced to Hor Vastus, saluting sharply.

"Kantos Kan desires that this party we have rescued be brought immediately to the deck of the *Xavarian*," the officer reported.

Approaching the craft, I scanned for my companions and realized with a jolt that Thuvia was missing. Inquiry revealed that no one had seen her since Carthoris had sent her *thoat* galloping wildly toward the hills, hoping to carry her to safety. Immediately, Hor Vastus dispatched a dozen air scouts in various directions to search for her. Given the terrain, she could not have strayed far.

We boarded the transport and ascended to the *Xavarian*. The first to greet me was Kantos Kan himself. My old friend had risen to the highest rank in the Helium Navy, yet to me, he remained the same brave comrade who had endured the privations of a Warhoon dungeon, the horrors of the Great Games, and the perils of our search for Dejah Thoris in Zodanga. Once an unknown wanderer and he a simple *padwar*, today he commanded Helium's aerial terrors, while I was a Prince of the House of Tardos Mors and Jeddak of Helium.

He did not ask where I had been. Like Hor Vastus, he dreaded the truth and refused to wrench a statement from me. He knew it would surface eventually, but for now, knowing I was safe was enough. He greeted Carthoris and Tars Tarkas with boundless delight, though he spared them no questions regarding their absence. He barely kept his hands off the boy.

"You do not know, John Carter," he told me quietly, "how deeply we of Helium love this son of yours. It is as though all the great love we bore his noble father and his poor mother had been centered entirely in him. When it became known he was lost, ten million people wept."

"What do you mean, Kantos Kan?" I whispered, sensing a sinister undertone in the phrase 'poor mother.'

He drew me aside. "For a year, since Carthoris disappeared, Dejah Thoris has grieved endlessly for her lost boy. The blow of years past, when you failed to return from the atmosphere plant, was somewhat mitigated by the duties of motherhood, for your son broke his white shell that very night."

"That she suffered terribly then, all Helium knew," I noted. "But with the boy gone, there was nothing left. Expedition after expedition returned with the same hopeless tale of no clues. Our beloved Princess drooped lower and lower until everyone felt she would soon join her loved ones in the Valley Dor."

"As a last resort," Kantos continued, "Mors Kajak, her father, and Tardos Mors, her grandfather, took command of two mighty expeditions. A month ago, they sailed north to scour every inch of the northern hemisphere. Two weeks have passed with no word, but rumors spread of a terrible disaster claiming all lives."

"About this time," Kantos paused, his voice heavy, "Zat Arras renewed his suit for her hand. He has pursued her relentlessly since your disappearance. She hated him and feared him, but with both her father and grandfather gone, Zat Arras wielded immense power, for he remains Jed of Zodanga—a position Tardos Mors appointed him to after you refused the honor."

"He had a secret audience with her six days ago. What transpired is unknown, but the next day Dejah Thoris vanished, taking a dozen of her household guards and body servants with her, including Sola, the green woman—Tars Tarkas' daughter, you recall. They left no word of intent, but it is always thus with those who embark on a voluntary pilgrimage from which none returns. We can only assume Dejah Thoris has sought the icy bosom of the Iss, and her devoted servants chose to accompany her."

"Zat Arras was at Helium when she disappeared. He commands this fleet searching for her. We have found no trace, and I fear the quest is futile."

While we conversed, Hor Vastus' scouts returned empty-handed. Depressed by the news of Dejah Thoris's disappearance, my anxiety grew further regarding the fate of the girl I believed to be the daughter of a proud Barsoomian house, whom I intended to return to her people. I was about to request a further search when a flyer from the flagship arrived with an officer bearing a message from Zat Arras to Kantos Kan.

Kantos read the dispatch and turned to me. "Zat Arras commands me to bring our 'prisoners' before him. There is naught else to do. Though he is supreme in Helium, it would be far more in keeping with chivalry and good taste were he to come here and greet the savior of Barsoom with the honors due him."

"You know full well, my friend," I smiled grimly, "that Zat Arras has ample reason to hate me. Nothing would please him more than to humiliate and then kill me. Now that he has such an excellent excuse, let us go and see if he has the courage to take advantage of it."

Summoning Carthoris, Tars Tarkas, and Xodar, we entered the small flyer with Kantos Kan and Zat Arras's officer, ascending quickly to the deck of Zat Arras's flagship. As we approached the Jed of Zodanga, no sign of greeting or recognition crossed his face—not even to Carthoris. His demeanor was cold, haughty, and uncompromising.

"Kaor, Zat Arras," I greeted him, but he ignored me.

"Why were these prisoners not disarmed?" he demanded of Kantos Kan.
