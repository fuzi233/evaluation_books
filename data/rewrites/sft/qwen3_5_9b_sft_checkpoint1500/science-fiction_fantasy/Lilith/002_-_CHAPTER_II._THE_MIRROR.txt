Silence settled over the house like a heavy blanket, unbroken for nearly a week. By the time the stillness finally gave way to motion, I had already spent countless hours wrestling with the manuscript fragment, trying to pry loose the invisible force that bound it. My efforts were futile; the object remained stubbornly locked, defying every attempt at release.

Yet, a different purpose had been brewing within me for some time. The atmosphere inside the study closet had grown oppressive, thick with a silence that unsettled me regarding the state of the books within. One afternoon, this vague intention hardened into a firm resolve. As I rose from my chair to begin the dusting, I noticed a disturbance near the door.

It was not the old librarian himself, though he moved with a familiarity that suggested presence. Instead, I perceived a shifting shadow, taking form as a slight, stooping figure clad in a shabby dress-coat that dragged near his heels. As he walked, the coat's tails parted slightly, revealing pale, thin legs encased in black stockings and oversized feet shod in wide, slipper-like shoes.

A sudden, magnetic pull drew me after him. Whether I was chasing a phantom or a man, certainty did not matter; the impulse to follow was absolute. He slipped past me into the hall and crossed toward the base of the grand staircase. I trailed close behind, watching him ascend to the first floor, passing the chambers where the family usually gathered. He pressed onward through a broad corridor, turning down a narrower flight of steps leading to the second floor.

Following him upward, I arrived at a landing that felt entirely alien to me. It was a realm I barely knew, a labyrinth of forgotten corners. I had never known siblings to drag me through such explorations, and having been taken away as a mere child, the house had remained a closed book to me until my return a month prior.

Beyond the landing, he led me through winding passages to the foot of a steep, wooden spiral stair. With every step I climbed, the wood groaned under my weight, yet the figure ahead made no sound. Halfway up, the vision faded. At the summit, the shadowy silhouette vanished completely, leaving me alone in a space filled with lingering glooms, yet devoid of the specific entity I had pursued.

I found myself standing in the main garret, a vast, cavernous space dominated by massive timber beams and soaring rafters. Great distances stretched out around me, punctuated only by occasional doors and long corridors where faint light filtered through cobwebbed windows and small, dusky skylights. Awe mixed with a thrill of discovery washed over me; this untamed expanse was truly mine, a territory waiting to be claimed.

In the center of this desolation stood a crude enclosure built of rough, unpainted planks. Its door stood slightly ajar. Assuming the old librarian might have hidden there, I pushed it open and stepped inside.

The interior was bathed in a peculiar, deserted light—a pallid illumination that seemed to belong to a world abandoned long ago. The air carried a disconsolate quality, as if the room mourned its own existence. Dust motes swirled lazily in the shafts of sunlight piercing the gloom, illuminating a tall, narrow mirror standing against the far wall. It was an antique piece, framed in dark ebony. Perched atop the frame sat a carved black eagle with wings spread wide, clutching a golden chain in its beak, from which dangled a heavy black sphere.

For a moment, I merely observed the surface, studying its craftsmanship. Then, abruptly, the illusion shattered. The glass did not reflect the chamber behind me, nor did it show my own face. For a fleeting instant, I felt the solid walls dissolve into mist, questioning whether I had ever truly looked *into* a mirror at all.

Instead, I saw a landscape transformed. Before me lay a rugged, heather-covered wilderness. In the middle distance, jagged hills of strange, brooding geometry rose against a sky that seemed too low. Along the horizon, the peaks of a distant mountain range cut sharp lines. Closest to me stretched a tract of moorland, flat and utterly melancholy.

Struggling with poor eyesight, I took a tentative step forward to inspect a grey stone in the foreground. As I leaned in, I saw it: a large, ancient raven hopped toward me with a solemn, unhurried grace. Its plumage was a deep, purplish-black, softened here and there with patches of silver-grey. It appeared to be searching for insects, oblivious to the intrusion.

Unsurprised by the impossibility of life within a painting, I advanced further, eager to get a clearer view. But the ground beneath me betrayed me. I stumbled—perhaps over the invisible edge of the mirror's frame—and fell forward.

Suddenly, the glass ceased to exist. I was no longer standing in a dusty garret. I was lying on the cold, heaving earth of that houseless heath, the wind whipping at my clothes, the ancient raven looming directly above me, its beak inches from my face.
