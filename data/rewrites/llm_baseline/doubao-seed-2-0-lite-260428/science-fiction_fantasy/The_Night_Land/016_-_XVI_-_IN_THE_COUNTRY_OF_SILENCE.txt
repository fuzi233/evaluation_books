I drew at last within sight of the Mighty Pyramid, its bulk rearing vast out of the unending night like a forged mountain of life and safety. It would have stunned me afresh with its impossible scale, had not despair and bone-deep weariness clamped so tight to my heart I could not spare a thought for wonder—every fibre of my being burned for only one thing: to bear my Own Maid across the threshold of that great refuge. It still stood miles away, vast and glowing faint against the black sky.

I drove forward across the broken waste at a brutal, unrelenting pace. As I passed a hollow where a smoldering fire-hole cast wavering red light, something dragged itself up from the depression: a great shaggy, fur-covered man, who lifted his clawed, monstrous hands and stumbled eagerly toward me. His nails jutted hard and sharp as butchery blades, curving far past his fingertips, suited to tear through flesh and armor alike.

I set my burden flat to the earth in the same breath I drew my weapon. I cared nothing for my own life; I would not be delayed by any creature, not when my Maid’s life hung by a thread. I launched myself at the giant, cold, unbridled fury thrumming in my veins, and struck hard—but he twisted aside in a heartbeat, dodging the blow. His monstrous arm snaked out of the shadow cast by the fire-hole’s dancing flame, closed around my headpiece, and wrenched it from my skull with such brutal force he flung me a dozen feet backward onto the stony earth. I was unharmed save for bruises and the ringing shock of the blow, and I was on my feet in an instant, the Diskos roaring and blazing in my grip as I swung it. It bit through the giant’s torso as if he were made of smoke, glutted itself on his flesh even as his shoulders twisted in death. His upper half crumpled to the ground, leaving his legs and lower trunk standing upright in the fire-light, blood spouting upward in a dark fountain against the night.

I did not pause. I leaped to my Maid, swept her back into my arms, and ran on past the corpse, which collapsed behind me with a wet, horrid thud. The air thrumming around me hummed with the shared excitement of the Millions who lived in the Pyramid’s upper levels, their telepathic voices rising in a wave of love and delight—they had witnessed the fight, and poured their collective strength toward me across the dark miles.

I had not traveled a full mile more when two vague, shadowy shapes oozed out from behind a jumble of standing rocks. I cut them down with the Diskos without slowing, and never learned what they had been. After that, it felt as if I smote forever. Strange, slimy, fanged things poured out of the thorn thickets and crevices in the stone, as if the entire night land crawled with foul, ravenous life. I fought them in a daze of grim, mounting despair, pushing ever faster, certain the end of both our lives was close and I would not win my Maid to safety.

The land roared with the baying of giants, high and terrible, and lower, crawling sounds that curdled the blood in my veins. Once I heard the heavy, pounding footfall of a pack of giants running in the dark, and the whole of that endless night reeked of unnameable evil. I do not know what spared me from being torn apart a hundred times over, save that the brutal trials of my long journey had burned every last trace of softness and weakness from me, leaving no crack through which the malevolent forces of the Night could reach to break my mind.

Then the deep, dreadful baying of the Night-Hounds rose from the southeast, and drew nearer. I knew then no strength of my own could shield my Maid from them.

Out of the high dark where the Last Light hung, a sharp blue flash lanced downward, striking the earth far to the southeast. Then another, then a dozen more, accompanied by a sharp, crackling noise louder than any thunder I had ever known. The humans in the Pyramid were fighting for me, burning their power to clear a path.

It was as if all the life of the Night Land that had stirred before had been only half-asleep. The ground shook beneath my feet as hordes of monsters roared in confusion, great ancient forces roused from their aeons-long slumber, and that hideous, distant cackling Laughter from the hidden cursed country of the lost East boomed across the waste. The baying of the hounds grew louder by the second, a full massive pack now, no more than a mile off to my southeast. I was alone, save for my dying Maid in my arms, and I strained my eyes for the vanguard of the Hundred Thousand Prepared Warriors who had descended from the Pyramid’s levels to come to my aid. There was no sign of them, only shifting shadows, distant monstrous movement, and the hounds drawing closer every moment. Death was at my heels.

I did not slow my stride. I broke into a run, the glowing barrier of the Circle of Protection around the Pyramid clearly visible through the dark, peeking out between the hills. A desperate hope flared in my chest: I might yet carry my Maid across its threshold. The hounds’ baying was half a mile off now, to my left, their terrible hunting cries sharp with the scent of us. I screamed in useless despair, begging for aid, and the Millions’ collective anguish wrapped around me like a warm cloak—they felt my hopelessness, and poured every drop of their shared love and support into my spirit.

In that same moment, the deep thrumming vibration of the Earth-Current rose through the soles of my boots, and I saw the massive pack of hounds burst out of the dark to my left, great beasts the size of war horses, heads low as they ran full tilt for me. I knew we would be torn to shreds in less than sixty seconds if the Pyramid did not act. I stopped running, planted my feet, and glanced from the onrushing horde to the Mighty Pyramid, and back again. The hounds were scarce two hundred fathoms away, hundreds of them, closing fast.

As I stared toward the redoubt, a torrent of roaring, searing flame burst from its sealed lower levels, rushing down onto the path the hounds were running. The night vanished entirely in the blinding, terrible glory of that fire. A blast wave hit me, hot enough to singe my exposed skin even at that distance, and the great thunder of the Earth-Force tearing through the air and rending the stone beneath the hounds drowned out every other sound. The hounds were gone, swallowed by flame and ruptured earth, great boulders hurled in every direction by the force of the discharge. The Humans had loosed the full power of the Earth-Current to save me.

In a heartbeat, they cut the current off, reining the force back under their control. A vast silence fell over the waste, broken only by the crackle of residual flame where the Current had struck. I shook off my dazed stupor in seconds, and ran on, hope blazing hot in my chest that I would yet win us to safety.

My eyes adjusted to the dim dark quickly, and I scanned every direction, wary for any remaining threat. For a long stretch I saw nothing living, and heard no sound save that far-off, horrid Laughter from the dead East. As I ran, I glanced up often at the Mighty Pyramid, and soon realized its usual glow was far dimmer than it had ever been. The vast, unprecedented drain of the Earth-Current they had unleashed to save us had nearly depleted the redoubt’s core power. I knew at once what that meant: they could not risk firing the Current again until it recharged, or they would doom every last one of the Millions living inside. The Masters had no more great weapons to aid me.

The land soon began to fill with the low, hungry noises of roused monsters once more, shapes creeping between me and the dim glow of the Circle of Protection. I hefted the Diskos, tensed for the bitter final fight. A sudden unshakable warning pricked at the back of my spirit: new peril was close. I looked up toward the Pyramid, waiting for the Master Monstruwacan to flash a warning via the agreed telepathic signals, but no message came. The whole of the Pyramid’s machinery had failed, I later learned: every observation instrument, every great lift, every air pump had ceased running for nearly an hour while the Earth-Current’s flow stabilized. The entire civilization of mankind had come within a breath of total annihilation for my sake.

My spirit, wreathed in the shared awareness of the Millions, sensed the danger anyway, and I moved far more warily, scanning the dark every which way. Then I spotted it: a pale, steady glowing circle hanging motionless in the air, moving directly above my Maid and me. It was one of the holy, protective Powers of Light, standing between our souls and some nameless Evil Force that had crept near to destroy us. I did not fear it, placed my full trust in its guard, and ran on.

I drew within four hundred paces of the Circle, certain I would win my Maid to safety at last, when three shaggy beast-men rose up out of the dirt at my feet, growling and lurching for me. The first was so close I had no room to swing the Diskos, so I smashed its skull in with the weapon’s heavy haft. I twisted aside, swung the Diskos free, and cut through the other two in a cold, brutal frenzy, my Maid no heavier than a sleeping child in the crook of my arm. I slew them before they could land a single blow, unharmed.

A great shout of wonder and welcome rose from just inside the Circle, and I looked up to see thousands of men in gray armor lined up along the barrier. None of them stepped out to meet me, and in an instant I understood why. Monstrous, heaving Black Mounds of seething dark energy lined the entire stretch of no-man’s-land outside the Circle, writhing with a life that turned my stomach cold. Any human who dared step beyond the Circle’s threshold would have their soul torn apart instantly, devoured utterly by that Evil power. No aid could reach me, no matter how many of the Hundred Thousand were willing to die trying.

They shouted to me to run faster, and I poured every last scrap of my strength into my stride, watching that pale holy light hold steady above us, leading me on. I was no more than a hundred paces from the Circle’s glow when a horde of squat, brutish tusked men poured out of the shadows all around me, grabbing for me, trying to tear my Maid from my arms. I kicked out with my metal boots, wrenched free of their grasp, and leaped back, the Diskos spinning and roaring in my hands. I carved through them in a whirling blur of steel, holding my Maid high above their short, grasping reach so they could not harm her, even as they rained heavy stone blows down on my armor, denting and cracking it, leaving new wounds bleeding across my back and shoulders. The brutes seemed endless, but I fought ever forward toward the Circle, the shouts of the Hundred Thousand ringing in my ears as the men watched, many of them held back by their comrades from charging out to die uselessly.

I was scarce fifty paces from the barrier when the front rank of the Hundred Thousand hurled their spare Diskos weapons out into the horde around me, thinning the crowd of tusked brutes to clear a path. I summoned the last of my failing strength, charged through the gap, and stumbled over the glowing threshold of the Circle, its usual defensive resistance faint and weak as the drained current seeped back to full power. A thousand hands reached out to help me, then drew back, held off by an unspoken awe—my armor was caked head to foot in monster blood, the Diskos still dripping gore in my grip, and I was a stranger returned from a death no one had expected me to survive.

I stood swaying there, gasping, trying to form words to beg for doctors to save my Maid, who lay limp in my arms. Somewhere out in the dark, giants began to run. The men shouted around me, calling for doctors, warning each other of the approaching threat, pointing out that the holy light above us and the writhing Black Mounds outside the Circle had both vanished. The low, distant murmur of the Millions rose from the miles-high upper levels of the Pyramid, a soft, endless thunder of shared emotion drifting down through the dark.

When I found my voice, I asked the nearest man if any doctor was among the ranks. A Master of the Diskos, the highest ranking commander present, stepped forward, saluted me with his weapon, and made to take my Maid from my arms. I asked again, slow and hoarse, for a healer. He barked an order, and the hundred thousand armored men shifted ranks to form a long, silent lane stretching straight to the Great Gateway of the Pyramid. Two men from the upper cities ran forward, carrying a small Master Doctor slung between them on a stretcher. He gently helped me lay my Maid down on the stone, and the Master of the Diskos gestured for all the men to turn their backs, to give the doctor privacy to examine her.

A great, utter silence fell. The Hundred Thousand did not speak, no sound rose from the Pyramid. Everyone knew, by then, the terrible stake of this moment. The doctor looked up at me, his face quiet and pitying, and I knew at once. My Own Maid was dead. He gently drew a cloth over her face, stood, and signed to his men to support me, and carry her inside. I shook them off, my breath coming in ragged gasps, and when the Master Doctor saw that I still had the strength to stand, even with my heart broken, he waved his men back.

I stooped, and lifted my Own Maid back into my arms for the last short journey. I walked down the lane of the Hundred Thousand, every man I passed holding his Diskos reversed in the highest, most solemn honor the order knew. No one spoke. The world felt empty and still, my great task seemingly for nothing—until I remembered: I had saved her from the unspeakable torments of the outer dark. She had died warm, safe, wrapped in my arms, not screaming and mad in the clutches of the things that lurked in the far East. But the memory stabbed me, sharp as a blade: I would never get to wake to find her kissing me as I slept, the quiet little moment I had dreamed of for so long. A wave of blinding grief crashed over me, and I staggered, for a heartbeat ready to fall. The Master Doctor steadied my elbow for a moment, then stepped back, letting me find my footing on my own.

As I drew near the Great Gateway, the lights of the Pyramid blazed bright once more, the machinery of the lifts and air pumps whirring back to full life as the Earth-Current recharged to its usual strength. The great massive doors rumbled open, operated by their ancient power-driven mechanisms, and the full council of the Pyramid’s Masters stepped out to meet me. The dear old Master Monstruwacan led them, moving as fast as his old legs could carry him, like a father rushing to greet the son he had feared lost. When the men at the gateway told him the Maid was dead in my arms, every one of the Masters paused, and stood silent, reversing their own Diskos in tribute. It was an honor almost no living soul had ever earned.
