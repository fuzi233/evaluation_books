As we drifted downstream from Vienna, the Danube's character began to shift, its waters spreading out into a desolate expanse of willow-bushes that stretched as far as the eye could see. This forsaken landscape was marked on the maps as _Sümpfe_, a sea of marshes that seemed to swallow the river whole. In times of high flood, the water lapped at the edges of this vast, sandy plain, but in the normal seasons, the willows swayed gently in the breeze, their silver leaves glinting in the sunlight like a shimmering expanse of molten metal.

These were not trees, but rather low, humble bushes with rounded tops and supple stems that bent and flexed in the wind. They seemed to be in constant motion, as if the entire plain itself were alive and shifting. The wind whipped up waves of leaves that rippled and curled like the surface of a lake, creating an ever-changing tapestry of green and silver hues. It was as if the willows were dancing to some unheard melody, their branches swaying in time with the wind's gentle caress.

As we navigated the Danube's twisting channels, we felt a sense of liberation, as if the river had finally broken free from the confines of its banks. We paddled through a labyrinthine network of waterways, the current tossing us about like a leaf on a gusty day. The willows seemed to close in around us, their whispering leaves creating a soothing background hum that lulled us into a state of tranquility. We were no longer in the world of human habitation, but in a realm of primordial beauty, where the rhythms of nature held sway.

We had entered this enchanted land on the crest of a rising flood, our Canadian canoe gliding effortlessly across the water. As we approached the land of the willows, the world seemed to shift and change around us, like a series of cinematic images flashing by in rapid succession. The bustling streets of Vienna gave way to a desolate landscape of willows, wind, and water, a place where the very fabric of reality seemed to be in a state of flux.
