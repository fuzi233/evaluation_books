The Sphere's most ardent appeals had evaporated into silence, leaving only the hollow echo of his own frustration. I met him with the blunt, unyielding edge of my right angle, driving forward with a force capable of shattering any ordinary Circle. Yet, he did not break. Instead, he flowed like water around a stone, slipping effortlessly from my grasp without shifting his orientation, dissolving into the ether until the space where he stood became a terrifying blank.

But his voice persisted, cutting through the sudden void. "SPHERE. Why do you stubbornly reject reason?" he demanded. "I sought in you, a man of logic and mathematical prowess, a worthy vessel for the Gospel of the Third Dimension—a message I am granted to preach but once every millennium. Yet now, I am lost. Words fail. Let deeds speak instead."

He paused, his presence coalescing with renewed intensity. "Listen, my friend. From my vantage point in Space, I perceive the interiors of all things you deem sealed. Behold, near that cupboard where you stand, I see several of your 'boxes'—devoid of lids or bases, as all Flatland objects are—overflowing with coin. Beside them lie two ledgers of account. I am descending now. Observe: the doors remain perfectly still, untouched by my passage. I enter the cupboard, retrieve a ledger, and ascend. I rush to the closet and burst the door open. One ledger is gone. With a mocking laugh, the Stranger materialized in the far corner, and simultaneously, the stolen tablet materialized upon the floor. I seized it. There was no ambiguity—it was the missing document.

A groan of sheer horror escaped me, questioning the sanity of my own mind. Yet the Stranger pressed on, his voice dripping with condescending certainty. "Do you not now comprehend that my explanation alone fits the evidence? What you call 'Solid' is merely superficial; what you call 'Space' is nothing more than a vast, infinite plane. I dwell in Space, looking down upon the insides of the very surfaces you worship. You too could escape this plane, provided you possessed the requisite will. A mere upward or downward vector would grant you sight equal to mine."

He gestured expansively. "As I rise, distancing myself from your plane, my vision expands, albeit on a diminished scale. Ascending now, I behold your neighbor, the Hexagon, and his kin within their respective chambers. Further still, I see the interior of the Theatre, ten doors distant, as the audience departs. Across the city, I observe a Circle in his study, immersed in his books. I shall soon return to you. But first, consider this final proof: allow me to deliver a single, gentle touch to your stomach. It shall cause no serious injury, and the fleeting pang you feel will pale in comparison to the intellectual enlightenment awaiting you."

Before I could formulate a protest, a searing lance of pain shot through my midsection, accompanied by a demonic laughter that seemed to erupt from within my own soul. Moments later, the acute agony subsided, replaced by a lingering, dull throb. As the Stranger began to re-emerge, growing larger with each passing second, he remarked casually, "See? I have caused you little harm. If this does not persuade you, nothing ever will. What is your verdict?"

My resolve shattered. It was unbearable to submit to the whims of a sorcerer who could manipulate the very organs of my body. Desperation clawed at my throat; I needed to pin him against the wall, to trap him until rescue arrived. Again, I drove my sharpest angle into him, screaming for aid, alerting the entire household to the intrusion. At that precise moment of impact, I believed the Stranger had slipped beneath our plane, struggling to regain his footing. Regardless, he froze, immobile. Hearing what I assumed were footsteps approaching, I pressed my attack with renewed vigor, shouting until my lungs burned.

A convulsive tremor racked the Sphere's form. "This must not happen," I seemed to hear him mutter internally. "Either he submits to reason, or I must employ the ultimate sanction of civilization." His voice boomed outward, urgent and commanding. "Hear me! No outsider must witness this revelation. Recall your wife immediately; she must not enter this chamber. The Gospel of Three Dimensions cannot be thwarted in this manner. The fruit of a thousand years of anticipation must not be cast aside. I hear her approach. Retreat! Back! Depart from me, or you shall accompany me—to destinations unknown—into the Land of Three Dimensions!"

"Fool! Madman! Irregular!" I shrieked, my voice cracking with rage. "Never shall I let you go! You shall pay the price for your deceptions!"

"Ha! Have we reached this precipice?" the Stranger roared, his form swelling ominously. "Then accept your destiny: you shall leave your plane. Once... twice... thrice... It is done!"
