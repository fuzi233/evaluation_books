Her slender fingers rested against the cold stone, as if seeking solace in its solidity, and her large, expressive eyes, a deep, rich purple, sparkled with a mixture of awe and pity as they locked onto the gaunt figure of the hermit.

"Aha!" he exclaimed, his voice like a rusty gate creaking in the wind. "You've finally arrived, flesh and blood, just as you did with Anthony. But I'm not fooled. I've seen your tricks before." He snatched up a small, ornate crucifix, its surface worn smooth by the touch of countless hands, and held it out to her, the flickering candle flame casting eerie shadows on the walls. "Exorcizo te," he intoned, his voice trembling with a mix of fear and conviction.

Margaret's response was immediate and pitiful. She held out her hands, palms upturned, as if imploring him to spare her. "No, please, don't harm me! I'm Margaret, your friend from Gouda. I've come to bring you news of your appointment as vicar." She took a step forward, her movements hesitant, as if unsure of her reception.

The hermit's face contorted in a mixture of anger and doubt. "Liar!" he spat, his voice rising in a crescendo. "Margaret was beautiful, but you're...you're something else entirely. You shrank away from the name of the Lord, you cunning hypocrite. In Nomine Dei exorcizo vos."

Margaret's eyes widened in terror, and she gasped, her voice barely above a whisper. "Jesu, have mercy! Don't curse me! I swear, I'll leave immediately. I didn't mean to intrude. Oh, Gerard, is this how we meet after all these years?" She dropped to her knees, her body wracked with sobs, her face twisted in a mask of superstitious fear and wounded affection.
