I strained every fraction of my mass, ramming my sharpest, hardest right-angle vertex straight into the Stranger with enough force to crush an ordinary mid-ranking Circle into crumpled, unrecognizable thread. It was useless. He did not slip left, did not jolt right along the flat familiar plane of Flatland — he simply slid out of the world entirely, the pressure of his body melting away from my contact until nothing remained but empty, still air where he had stood. Yet his voice did not vanish.
"Why will you refuse to listen to reason?" the Sphere’s tone hummed with uncharacteristic frustration. "I had hoped a thoughtful, accomplished mathematician such as yourself would prove the perfect apostle for the Gospel of Three Dimensions, the teaching I am only permitted to share once every thousand years. But words have failed. Deeds, not arguments, shall prove the truth. Listen: from my vantage in Space, I can peer straight through the outer perimeter of every shape you consider sealed and private. Yonder, beside you, is the cupboard you locked half an hour ago, key tucked safe in the inner seam of your frame. It holds two account tablets, and stacks of what you call boxes — all flat, no tops or bottoms, as every object in Flatland must be. I am going to reach through the gap between our worlds and fetch you one of those tablets. The doors will not move. The lock will not click. Now I am inside the cupboard. Now I take the tablet. Now I ascend back to you."
I lunged for the cupboard, tearing the door open so hard the hinge squealed. One of the two tablets was gone, no trace of forced entry on the lock. A soft, mocking laugh echoed from the far corner of my room, and the missing tablet clattered to rest at my feet. I snatched it up, ran my edges over every scratch and mark on its surface. There was no doubt. It was mine.
I groaned, half convinced I had lost my geometric wits entirely, but the Stranger pressed on. "Surely no other explanation fits what you have just seen. What you call 'solid' in Flatland is no more than a thin outer surface to me. What you name 'Space' is nothing but the single flat plane you have spent your entire life confined to. I exist in the axis above and below that plane, so I can see the inside of every closed box, every home, every living body, no wall or perimeter can block my view. If you could summon the will to make a tiny motion along that third, unthought-of axis, you could see everything I see. The higher I climb away from your plane, the more of your world I can take in, scaled smaller the further I get. Look: I ascend now, and I can see your neighbor the Hexagon moving through his home with his family. Now I see the inside of the theater ten doors away, where the audience is just spilling out after the evening lecture. On the far edge of the district, I see a senior Circle scholar hunched over his books in his private study. I will descend back to you now, for one final, unarguable proof: I am going to give you the lightest possible touch to the inside of your torso. It will not cause you permanent harm, and the small pain cannot possibly compare to the revelation that waits for you."
Before I could utter a single cry of protest, a sharp, stabbing pain lanced through my interior — no blade had touched my outer edges at all, no cut had been made in my perimeter, and yet I felt the sting deep inside me. A wild, unearthly, demoniacal laugh seemed to ring out from within my own frame, then the sharp agony faded to no more than a faint dull throb. The Sphere slowly swelled in size as he settled back fully onto Flatland’s plane. "There," he said. "I have not hurt you badly, have I? If you are not convinced now, I do not know what will ever convince you."
My resolve hardened like set iron. It was unthinkable to live the rest of my existence at the mercy of some sorcerous intruder who could steal my property, poke at my insides, and vanish out of sight whenever he pleased. If I could just pin him hard against the wall long enough for the rest of my household to come running, we could hold him for the constables.
I slammed my sharpest right-angle into him a second time, screaming at the top of my lungs for aid. At the moment of my attack, the Sphere had been half-sunk below Flatland’s plane, and he struggled to pull himself back into our world, frozen motionless under my pressure. I thought I heard the footsteps of my family approaching the door, and I redoubled my force, shouting even louder for help. A violent convulsive shudder ran through the Stranger.
"This cannot fail," I thought I heard him mutter to himself. "Either he will listen to reason, or I must resort to the last resource of civilization." He raised his voice, shouting at me over my calls for help, "Listen! No uninitiated Flatlander can witness what you have seen, it will shatter their minds. Send your wife away the second she steps through that door. The Gospel of Three Dimensions cannot be frustrated like this. A thousand years of waiting cannot be thrown away for nothing. I hear her hand on the latch! Step back from me right now, or I will drag you out of this plane — to a Land of Three Dimensions you cannot even imagine."
"Fool! Madman! Irregular abomination!" I howled back. "I will never release you. You will pay the penalty for these impostures for the rest of your days."
"Ha! Then it has come to this," the Sphere thundered, and his form blazed bright for a split second. "Meet your fate. Out of your plane you go. Once — twice — thrice — 'tis done!"
