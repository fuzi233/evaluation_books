As the tempest raged outside, Ghysbrecht Van Swieten's restlessness simmered like a pot left unattended over a low flame. His aversion to thunderstorms had always been a well-guarded secret, a vulnerability he dare not reveal to his companions. But tonight, as the wind howled and the lightning danced across the sky, he couldn't shake the feeling of being a ship lost at sea, tossed about by the whims of fate. He tossed aside his blankets and rose from bed, his joints creaking with the weight of his years. The storm would pass, and when it did, he would saddle his mule and ride to the "Three Kings" at Sevenbergen, where his men waited, no doubt sharing tales of their fruitless search for Peter.

As he crept downstairs, the wooden floorboards groaned beneath his feet, a reminder of the countless nights he'd spent pacing these very same halls. His eyes adjusted slowly to the darkness, and he lit a lantern, casting flickering shadows on the walls. The air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and smoke, a potent mixture that usually invigorated him, but tonight only served to heighten his unease. He saddled his mule with a practiced hand, his mind racing with visions of what might have transpired while he slept. Had Peter found shelter? Had he discovered some hidden weakness in the town's defenses?

The storm finally began to subside, leaving behind an oppressive stillness that only served to heighten Ghysbrecht's anxiety. He mounted his mule and set off into the night, the silence broken only by the soft thud of hooves on the cobblestones. The "Three Kings" loomed ahead, its wooden sign creaking in the gentle breeze like a beckoning finger. He dismounted, his joints protesting the sudden movement, and pushed open the door, revealing a scene that would have been comical in more tranquil times: his men sprawled across chairs, tables, and the floor, fast asleep.

Ghysbrecht's face twisted into a scowl as he roused them with a series of sharp kicks and curses. They stumbled to their feet, bleary-eyed and disoriented, and Ghysbrecht listened intently as they recounted their tale of woe, their words laced with the sweet scent of self-congratulation.
