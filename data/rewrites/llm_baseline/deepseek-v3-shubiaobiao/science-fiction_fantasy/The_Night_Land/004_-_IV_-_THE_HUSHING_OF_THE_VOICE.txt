*("Dearest, thine own feet tread the world at night—
Treading, as moon-flakes step across a dark—
Kissing the very dew to holier light…
Thy Voice a song past mountains, which to hark
Frightens my soul with an utter lost delight.")*

On the night it happened, as I prepared for sleep near the end of the sixteenth hour, the aether around me shuddered—a sensation familiar in those days, yet this time it carried an unfamiliar weight. Then, unmistakably, Naani’s voice resonated within me, clear as if she stood beside me.

I did not answer at once. Instead, I sent the Master-Word pulsing into the void, testing the truth of what I heard. Moments later, the answering vibration returned, steady and sure. Still, I hesitated. Why would Naani speak to me now, when the Monstruwacans of her Redoubt kept their watch and all others slept?

Her reply came not in explanation, but in words that struck me like a blade:

My breath left me. These were words I had once whispered to Mirdath the Beautiful, long ages past, when grief had hollowed me after her death. How could Naani know them? Unless—

I called out to her, demanding sense of this, but she only repeated the verse, her voice now unmistakably *both* Naani’s and Mirdath’s, twined together. A madness of hope seized me. Had time folded upon itself? Was Naani the rebirth of the one I had loved before the world grew cold?

I shouted into the dark, *"Mirdath!"*—and the voice answered, echoing the words again. Then, silence. For hours I called, but no reply came. Exhaustion dragged me down at last into fitful sleep.

When I woke, the memory burned brighter than any dream. None alive could know those words save Mirdath herself, and now they had come to me through Naani. I reached for her at once, my mind casting the Master-Word into the aether. Her response came faintly, weakened by distance.

*"I did not speak to you in the night,"* she insisted. *"I slept."*

Yet she had dreamed—a dream of a sorrowful man, broad-shouldered and dark, writing words she could not recall, save one line: *"Dearest, thine own feet tread the world at night—"*

I finished the verse for her.

A gasp trembled through the aether. Then—recognition. A flood of memories not her own, yet *known*. The gates of another life swung open. *"Am I truly Mirdath?"* she whispered.

And when she spoke my old love-name, certainty crashed over me. Across the abyss of time and darkness, we had found each other again.

---

But joy was fleeting. Soon after, disaster struck the Lesser Redoubt. The Earth-Current, their last defense, faltered. Naani’s voice grew fainter with each passing day as their power waned. Monsters pressed closer. Madness took some; they fled the gates and were devoured. Despair gnawed at her people, and though I strained to hear her, her words dissolved into silence.

Then—a miracle.

In the eighteenth hour, as I slept, the Master Monstruwacan woke me. The Instruments had trembled with a ghostly pulse: the Master-Word, followed by a declaration—*"We are coming!"*

Naani heard it too. *"Who calls?"* she begged, her voice thready with hope.

Through the great spy-glass, we saw them—figures crossing the Electric Circle, moving *away* from the Pyramid. Five hundred youths from the Upper Cities, bound for the Night Land. They had overpowered the Sleep-Time Watch, forced the Eye-Gate, and vanished into the dark.

Fools. Or heroes.

The Master Watchman raged, but the deed was done. New laws were etched in steel, but no law could bring them back.

And I? I knew then what I must do.

If Naani’s voice was to be hushed forever, I would not let it fade alone in the dark.
