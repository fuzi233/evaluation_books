The afternoon sun cast a warm glow over Barton Cottage as Mrs. Dashwood welcomed her nephew, Edward Ferrars, with open arms. It was only natural that he would visit, given the close bond they shared. Her initial surprise gave way to genuine warmth and affection as she lavished him with attention. Edward's reserved demeanor began to thaw under her gentle persuasion, and soon he was his old self again. The family's collective perception was that he was still a little subdued, though, and Mrs. Dashwood attributed this to her daughter-in-law's parsimonious ways.

As they settled around the dinner table, Mrs. Dashwood turned to Edward with interest. "Tell me, dear Edward, what are your mother's plans for you at present? Will you continue to pursue a life in the public eye, despite your reservations?" she asked, her voice filled with a hint of amusement.

Edward smiled wryly. "I'm afraid my mother has finally come to accept that I have neither the talent nor the inclination for a career in politics."

"But how will you establish your reputation, then?" pressed Mrs. Dashwood. "Fame is a must for your family's expectations, and without any desire for expense, social connections, or a profession, it seems a daunting task."

"I won't bother," Edward replied, his eyes crinkling at the corners. "I have no ambition to be renowned, and I'm content with the hope that I'll never be forced into it. Thank goodness, I lack the necessary charisma and eloquence."

"You're a practical soul, Edward," observed Mrs. Dashwood. "Your desires are modest, like those of most people."

"Not at all," Edward countered. "I wish for happiness, just as everyone else does – but on my own terms. Grandeur means little to me, but a comfortable life, free from want, is essential."

Marianne spoke up, her eyes sparkling with conviction. "Happiness is not dependent on wealth or status, surely?"

Elinor nodded in agreement. "Wealth has its value, but beyond a certain threshold, it offers little true satisfaction, at least not for oneself."

Marianne playfully scolded her sister. "Elinor, you're being too practical! Money can bring joy, but only when there's nothing else to provide it."
