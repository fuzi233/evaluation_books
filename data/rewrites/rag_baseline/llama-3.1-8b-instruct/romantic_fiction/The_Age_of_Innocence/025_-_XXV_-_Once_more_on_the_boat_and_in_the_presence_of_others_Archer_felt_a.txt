The sunlight filtering through the train windows cast a warm glow on Archer's face as he stared blankly ahead. The rhythmic clickety-clack of the wheels on the tracks seemed to lull him into a state of reverie. It was as if the day's events had left him suspended in a dream, disconnected from the world around him. His thoughts still lingered on the woman he'd parted with, the memory of her smile and laughter echoing softly in his mind.

As the train rumbled on, Archer's gaze began to focus on the faces of the other passengers. They seemed distant, indistinct, like figures in a watercolor painting. He felt a pang of melancholy, a sense of disconnection from the people around him. The air in the train car was thick with the smell of stale air and worn leather, a scent that transported him to another time, another place.

His eyes landed on a face that sparked recognition. A young man, standing out among the crowd, his features etched with a mix of exhaustion and curiosity. Archer's mind struggled to place him, to connect the dots between their previous encounters. And then, like a key turning in a lock, it clicked – the young man was the French tutor he'd seen leaving the Parker House the day before.

The young man approached Archer, his eyes fixed on his face with a mixture of hope and apprehension. "Monsieur, surely we met in London?" he asked, his English tinged with a faint accent.

Archer's grip on his own arm tightened as he extended his hand in greeting. "Ah, yes – in London! I'm glad to see you made it here safely." He studied the young man's face, taking in the lines of fatigue and the furrowed brow. "What brings you to America?"

The young man's smile was hesitant, his eyes darting about the train car before settling on Archer's face. "I'm afraid I'm only here for a short time, Monsieur. I return the day after tomorrow."

Archer's expression softened, his interest piqued. "I see. Well, since we've run into each other, might I invite you to lunch? Down town, perhaps, at a small restaurant I know?"
