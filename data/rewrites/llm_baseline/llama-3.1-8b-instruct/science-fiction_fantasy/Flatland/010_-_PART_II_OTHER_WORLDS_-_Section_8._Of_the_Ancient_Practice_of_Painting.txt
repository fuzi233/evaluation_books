In the annals of Flatland, there existed a time when life was vibrant and multifaceted, much like the intricate patterns woven into the tapestries that adorned our ancestors' dwellings. Yet, this effulgent era, spanning several centuries, has long since faded into memory, leaving behind only whispers of a bygone age. Our forebears, blessed with a profound understanding of the fundamental hues and a nascent art of painting, had managed to infuse their existence with a kaleidoscope of colours.

It is said that a pioneering figure, a visionary Pentagons whose name has been lost to the sands of time, had first grasped the essence of chromatic expression. This trailblazer, known to us as Chromatistes, began by embellishing his own abode, then his loyal servants, and eventually, his family members, gradually extending his artistic touch to include himself. The benefits of this endeavour were twofold: aesthetically, it brought unparalleled beauty to their surroundings, while practically, it facilitated effortless identification and interaction among the community.

As the news of Chromatistes' discovery spread like wildfire, an entire generation was swept up in the fervour of colour. Within a fortnight, nearly every Square and Triangle in the vicinity had emulated the trend, while a minority of the more traditional Pentagons remained resolute in their resistance. However, as the months passed, even the higher echelons of society succumbed to the allure of chromatic self-expression. It was only a matter of years before the phenomenon had engulfed the entirety of Flatland, leaving only the Women and the Priests untouched.

Nature itself seemed to intervene, posing an insurmountable barrier against the extension of this practice to these two classes. Many-sidedness, it was argued, was an inherent characteristic of distinction, implying a corresponding diversity of colours. Yet, this reasoning was deemed inapplicable to the Priests, who, if they chose to assert their claim to being Circles, possessed only one side, and thus, by definition, no sides at all. As for the Women, with their singular aspect, they were, in fact, devoid of sides, their circumference serving as a testament to their unique nature. And so, the Priests and Women stood firm, unmoved by the siren call of chromatic splendour, remaining steadfast in their commitment to a colourless existence.
