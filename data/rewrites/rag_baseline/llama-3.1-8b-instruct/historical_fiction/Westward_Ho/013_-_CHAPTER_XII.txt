The symbiotic relationship between the storied Bideford Bridge and the surrounding town has long fascinated scholars of Devonshire's cultural heritage. For centuries, the bridge has been an integral part of the community, its significance extending far beyond its role as a mere architectural landmark. Dubbed the "omphalos" of Bideford, the bridge has been imbued with a series of mystical attributes, earning it the reputation as an "inspired bridge," a "soul-saving bridge," an "alms-giving bridge," an "educational bridge," and, notably, a "dinner-giving bridge." These multifaceted qualities have been attributed to a series of enigmatic events that have shaped the bridge's history.

According to local lore, the bridge's construction was overseen by a series of unseen hands, which laboriously transported stones downstream each night to their current location. The choice of site was deemed crucial, as the area was notorious for its shifting sands, making it essential to establish a stable foundation. In recognition of this feat, Bishop Grandison of Exeter granted indulgences and benedictions to those who contributed to the bridge's completion, acknowledging the mutual benefits it would bring to both the souls and bodies of the townspeople.

While the bridge's structural integrity has been subject to scrutiny, its ability to withstand even the slightest disturbance remains a topic of debate. Some have noted that, despite its foundation being laid upon a bed of wool, the bridge still exhibits a remarkable tendency to shake at the mere approach of a horse. Conversely, others have marveled at the feat of William Alford, who, as a testament to the bridge's strength, successfully carried four bushels of saltwater measure across its entire length on his back, a challenge undertaken for the sake of a wager.

Beyond its engineering prowess, the bridge has also demonstrated a more endearing quality – a sense of hospitality. Each year, it hosts lavish dinners, which have become a highlight of the social calendar in Bideford. It was during one such occasion, held in 1583, that a heated discussion arose between Mr. St. Leger of Annery and a group of bridge trustees regarding the merits of salmon caught above versus those caught below the bridge. In an effort to settle the matter, Mr. St. Leger proposed a wager, offering five pounds to anyone who could produce a salmon from the pool below Annery that rivaled the flakiness of one they had recently enjoyed at Appledore.
