As the warm summer breeze wafted through the crowded streets, Margaret hastened up the stairs, eager to change into her simple yet elegant attire, donning her bonnet and shawl to pay a visit to the ailing Betsy Higgins. Her footsteps echoed through the narrow passageways, a testament to the quiet significance of her newfound connection with the inhabitants of this humble neighborhood. For Margaret had come to understand that it was not grand gestures, but small acts of kindness and genuine concern that forged lasting bonds between people.

Upon entering the Higgins' abode, Margaret was struck by the contrast between the rough-hewn stone floor and the unpolished stones beneath the furniture. Despite the sweltering heat, a large fire crackled in the hearth, casting a golden glow over the space, which only served to heighten the oppressive atmosphere. Unbeknownst to Margaret, Mary Higgins had intentionally lit the fire as a gesture of hospitality, a nod to the warmth and comfort she sought to extend to her guest. Betsy, reclining on a plush squab beneath the window, looked up at Margaret with a faint smile, her eyes shining with a mix of exhaustion and longing.

As Margaret settled beside her, Betsy reached out a frail hand, tracing the intricate patterns on Margaret's dress with a childlike wonder. "I've often wondered," she said, her voice barely above a whisper, "why the people in the Bible spoke so highly of fine clothing. But to see you dressed in such finery... it's almost as if you're from another world." Her gaze drifted to Margaret's face, drinking in the sight of her refined features. "Where did you acquire this lovely gown?"

"In London," Margaret replied, a hint of amusement dancing in her eyes. "Though my true home lies in a forest, far removed from the city's din."

Betsy's eyes sparkled with curiosity. "Tell me about it," she implored, her voice laced with a deep longing. As she leaned back, her eyelids drooping, Margaret felt an unexpected sense of ease wash over her. For the first time since leaving Helstone, she found herself pouring out her heart to this young girl, recounting tales of the forest's ancient trees, their leaves rustling softly in the breeze, and the gentle song of the hidden brook that babbled through the underbrush.
