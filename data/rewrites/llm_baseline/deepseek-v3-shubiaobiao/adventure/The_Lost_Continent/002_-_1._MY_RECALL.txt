The grand reception had ended. The proclamation was read, Empress Phorenice’s name exalted, and the new Viceroy installed with all the ancient pomp and ceremony befitting the occasion. My rule was formally relinquished. Tatho now sat upon the serpent throne, the jeweled chain of office draped around his neck. As drums thundered and trumpets blared, he rose for his first procession through the gilded council chamber as Viceroy of Yucatan.

I followed, arms folded, head bowed, trailing him past glittering rows of soldiers and courtiers. The vaulted ceiling trembled with cries of *"Long live Tatho!"* and *"Glory to the Empress!"*—obligatory cheers, dutifully given. The new ruler acknowledged them with stately nods.

One by one, he approached the lesser thrones of the provincial governors—East, North, and South—receiving their homage as tradition demanded. And I, the deposed, walked humbly behind him.

Twenty years ago, these same honors had been mine. Now fate had shifted. Yet though protocol forbade open acknowledgment, I felt the weight of silent respect from soldiers, nobles, and chieftains alike. Their glances spoke what their lips could not.

The ceremony unfolded with rigid precision. Three times we circled the hall beneath the pyramid’s shadow, and not a single voice rose in accusation against my rule. No petitions for reform were offered. The people’s silence was their tribute.

Finally, we reached the private chambers that had been mine mere hours before. The massive stone doors groaned open at Tatho’s command, and we stepped inside, leaving the court behind.

Alone at last, the new Viceroy turned and bowed low.

*"Deucalion,"* he said, *"believe me—I never sought this office. It was forced upon me. Had I refused, my head would have rolled, and another, less friendly to you, would have taken my place. The Empress tolerates no defiance."*

*"My brother in all but blood,"* I replied, *"there is no man in Atlantis or her colonies to whom I would rather entrust my people. For twenty years, I have governed Yucatan—first under the old King, then under this new Empress. I know this land like my own soul. I have walked its cities, hunted its forests, built its roads and rivers. I have ruled through famine and feast, war and peace. And now I pass them to you, Tatho, with a father’s plea—care for them well."*

*"I will follow your example,"* he vowed. *"But tell me, were you truly blindsided by this recall?"*

*"A formality,"* I scoffed.

Tatho glanced around, then lowered his voice. *"I think not. Troubled times loom, and Phorenice wants her strongest tools close at hand."*

*"Speak plainly,"* I urged. *"These walls are thick, and Yucatan has no taste for spies."*

He exhaled. *"Very well. The throne trembles. Phorenice seeks loyal men to steady it."*

*"Because at home, your name is legend. Mothers name their sons ‘Deucalion.’ You are held up as the model of what a ruler should be."*

*"I never sought fame,"* I said, frowning. *"I served duty, not vanity."*

*"The Gods are less spoken of now,"* Tatho mused. *"Men worship newer powers."*

His expression darkened. *"That is a longer tale. She was a swineherd’s daughter, though none dare say it now. Zaemon, governor of her province, took her in. She learned quickly—too quickly. By fifteen, she commanded his household, then his armies. When rebellion stirred, she crushed it, absorbing the defeated into her ranks. She has a way of making loyalty inevitable."*

*"They underestimated her. Sent armies. She destroyed them. The last was mine—eight-to-one odds, and still she won. I tried to die fighting. Instead, she offered me service."*

*"Then I will see for myself,"* I said. *"But know this—I return to Atlantis as a servant of duty, not desire."*

Tatho smiled faintly. *"We shall see how long that lasts."*
