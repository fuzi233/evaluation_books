As the sun rose over the rolling hills of Donegal, casting a warm glow over the sleepy town of Ramelton, Ethne Lennon prepared for the day's festivities. In the kitchen, she busied herself with the final preparations for the evening's grand ball, the scent of roasting meats and baking pastries wafting through the air. The sound of sizzling bacon and the gentle clinking of dishes filled the room, mingling with the soft hum of conversation from the servants as they went about their tasks.

Just then, the door burst open and Ethne's cousin, Feversham, strode into the kitchen, his eyes scanning the room with a hungry gaze. "Ah, Ethne, my dear, you're cooking up a storm today!" he exclaimed, his eyes lighting upon the array of dishes spread out before her. "What wonders do you have in store for us tonight?"

Ethne smiled, her cheeks flushing with pleasure at the sight of her cousin's enthusiasm. "Just a little something I've been experimenting with," she said, her hands moving deftly as she adjusted the seasoning on a tray of freshly baked scones. "A traditional recipe from our family's old cookbooks, passed down through generations. I think you'll find it quite delightful."

As she spoke, she handed Feversham a steaming plate of fresh-baked soda bread, still warm from the oven. He took a bite, his eyes widening in appreciation. "By Jove, Ethne, this is incredible! You've outdone yourself." The two cousins spent the next few moments in happy conversation, discussing the finer points of Irish cuisine and the intricacies of traditional cooking techniques.

As they chatted, Ethne's thoughts wandered back to the letter she had received that morning, informing her of Feversham's regiment being ordered to Egypt. She felt a pang of guilt for having kept him from the opportunity, and a sense of responsibility for the potential risks he might have faced. But as she glanced over at her cousin, she knew that she had made the right decision – for now, at least. "We should be off, Feversham," she said finally, smiling brightly. "The guests will be arriving soon, and we have a lot to do before the ball begins."
