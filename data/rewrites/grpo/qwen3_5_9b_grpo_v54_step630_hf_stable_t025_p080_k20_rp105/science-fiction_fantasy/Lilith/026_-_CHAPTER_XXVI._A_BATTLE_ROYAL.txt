I collapsed onto the bed, my mind racing through the tale she had spun. In her haste, she had betrayed herself with a single careless word, unraveling the mystery of my discovery in the forest! The leopardess had leaped away; the princess lay defeated on the riverbank; the flowing water had shattered her spell. Her own confession laid bare the peril facing the Little Ones, and I realized with a jolt that I had just saved the life of their most terrifying foe.

I had barely reached this conclusion when sleep dragged me under. The exquisite wine may not have been entirely harmless.

When I awoke, it was night. A lamp hanging from the ceiling cast a soft, ethereal glow across the chamber. A profound, delicious lethargy wrapped around me. I felt as though I were floating, untethered from land, drifting on the bosom of a twilight sea. Existence itself was pure pleasure. There was no pain. Surely, I was dying.

No pain—ah, what a sudden, sharp sting of mortal agony that was! What a sickening blow! It pierced straight through my heart! Again! That was pure sharpness—and so nauseating! I tried to raise my hand to press against my chest, but something held it down.

The pain was fading, yet my entire body felt paralyzed. Some malevolent force had seized me—something hateful! I wanted to struggle, but my limbs refused to obey. My will screamed in protest, futile and trapped. I surrendered, lying still. Then, I felt a soft hand on my face, guiding my head back into the pillow, and a heavy weight settled across my torso.

My breathing deepened; the weight lifted from my chest. I opened my eyes.

The princess stood above me on the bed, gazing out into the room with the vacant expression of a dreamer. Her great eyes were clear and calm. Her lips bore a look of satisfied passion; she wiped a streak of red from her mouth.

She caught my gaze, leaned down, and struck me across the eyes with the handkerchief in her grasp. It felt like the edge of a blade slicing through my vision, and for a moment or two, I was blind.

I heard a dull, heavy thud, like a large, soft-footed beast landing after a short leap. I opened my eyes and saw the great sweep of a long tail as it vanished through the half-open doorway. I lunged after it.

The creature was gone. I shot down the stairs and into the alabaster hall. The moon hung high, bathing the place in a pale, sun-bleached radiance. The princess was nowhere to be seen. I had to find her; in her presence, I might survive, but outside it, I was helpless! I was a tame animal for her to feed upon, a human spring for a thirst demon! She showed me favor only to use me more easily! My waking eyes did not fear her, but they would close, and she would return! Without seeing her, I felt her presence everywhere, for she could be anywhere—even waiting for me in some hidden cavern of sleep! Only with my eyes fixed on her could I feel safe from her!

Beyond the alabaster hall, it was pitch-black. I had to grope my way forward, hands and feet feeling for purchase. Finally, I brushed aside a curtain and entered a black hall. There, I found a great, silent assembly. How it was visible, I could not say or imagine, for the walls, floor, and ceiling were shrouded in an infinite blackness, darker than the deepest moonless night. Yet my eyes managed to separate, however vaguely, a few individuals from the mass. They interpenetrated and divided, surrounded by the darkness. It seemed my eyes would never fully recover. I pressed my eyeballs and stared again, but the vision remained indistinct. Blackness mingled with form; silence and undefined motion possessed the vast space. It was a dim, confused dance, filled with recurring glimpses of shapes not unknown to me. Now a woman appeared, with glorious eyes staring out of a skull; now an armed figure mounted on a skeleton horse; now one, then another of the hideous burrowing phantasms. I could trace no order or relation in the mingling currents and eddies. If I seemed to catch the rhythm of a dance, it was only to see it break into chaos. With the shifting colors of the seemingly solid shapes, a multitude of shadows mingled, appearing independent of their originals, each moving according to its own free shadow-will. I searched everywhere for the princess, but throughout the wildly changing kaleidoscope, I saw her not, nor found any sign of her presence. Where was she? What might she not be doing? No one paid me the slightest notice as I wandered hither and thither seeking her. Losing hope, I turned away to look elsewhere. Finding the wall, I kept my hand against it, for even then I could not see it clearly, and groped along until I reached a curtained opening into a vestibule.

Dimly lit by moonlight, the leopardess's cage served as the arena for what appeared to be a desperate, silent struggle. Two vastly different forms, human and bestial, with a tangled confusion of mingling bodies and limbs, writhed and wrestled in closest embrace. It had lasted only an instant when I saw the leopardess emerge from the cage, walking quietly toward the open door. As I hurried after her, I glanced back: there was the leopardess in the cage, crouching motionless as when I first saw her.

The moon, halfway up the sky, shone round and clear. The bodiless shadow I had seen the night before walked through the trees toward the gate, and after him went the leopardess, swinging her tail. I followed a little way behind, as silently as they, and neither of them once looked back. Through the open gate, we descended to the city, lying quiet beneath the moonshine. The face of the moon was very still, and its stillness looked like that of expectation.

The Shadow took his way straight to the stair at the top of which I had lain the night before. Without pausing, he ascended, and the leopardess followed. I quickened my pace, but a moment later, I heard a cry of horror. Then came the fall of something soft and heavy between me and the stair, and at my feet lay a body, frightfully blackened and crushed, yet still recognizable as that of the woman who had led me home and shut me out. Standing petrified, I watched as the spotted leopardess bounded down the stairs with a baby clamped in her mouth. I darted forward to seize her before she could turn at the foot, but that very instant, from behind me, the white leopardess, like a great bar of glowing silver, shot through the moonlight and grabbed her by the neck. She dropped the child; I caught it up and stood to watch the battle between them.

What a sight it was! Now one, now the other uppermost, both too intent for any noise beyond a low growl, a whimpered cry, or a snarl of hate—followed by a quicker scrambling of claws as each, worrying and pushing and dragging, struggled for footing on the pavement! The spotted leopardess was larger than the white one, and I was anxious for my friend; but soon I saw that, though neither stronger nor more active, the white leopardess possessed greater endurance. Not once did she lose her hold on the neck of her opponent. From the spotted throat finally issued a howl of agony, changing, by swift-crowded gradations, into the long-drawn crescendo of a woman's uttermost wail. The white one relaxed her jaws; the spotted one drew herself away and rose on her hind legs.

Erect in the moonlight stood the princess, a confused rush of shadows careering over her whiteness—the spots of the leopard crowding, hurrying, fleeing to the refuge of her eyes, where merging they vanished. The last few, outsped and belated, mingled with the cloud of her streamy hair, leaving her radiant as the moon when a legion of little vapors has flown, wind-hunted, off her silvery disc—save that, adown the white column of her throat, a thread of blood still trickled from every wound of her adversary's terrible teeth. She turned away, took a few steps with the gait of a Hecate, fell, covered afresh with her spots, and fled at a long, stretching gallop.

The white leopardess turned also, sprang upon me, pulled my arms asunder, caught the baby as it fell, and flew with it along the street toward the gate.
