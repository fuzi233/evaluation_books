As the morning sun cast its warm rays upon the cottage, Elinor noticed the subdued spirits of her dear friend, Willoughby. His visit, though welcome, offered only a fleeting respite from the uncertainty that had been plaguing her heart. His evident unhappiness was a concern she couldn't shake off, and she yearned to know whether he still harbored the affection she once believed he held for her. However, the inconsistencies in his demeanor made it difficult for her to discern his true feelings.

The next morning, Willoughby joined Elinor and Marianne in the breakfast room before the rest of the household stirred. Marianne, ever the optimist, bid them a hasty farewell, leaving the two to converse in private. But just as she reached the stairs, she caught sight of Willoughby emerging from the parlor.

"I'm off to tend to my horses," he announced, "since you're not quite ready for breakfast. I'll return shortly." With that, he departed, leaving Elinor and Marianne to wonder at his sudden absence.

Upon his return, Willoughby regaled them with tales of his walk to the village, where he had chanced upon various aspects of the valley that had captivated him. The village itself, situated atop a gentle rise, offered a panoramic view of the surrounding countryside, which had left him enchanted. This topic, being a subject dear to Marianne's heart, soon found her engaging Willoughby in a discussion about the beauty of the landscape. She posed questions, seeking to know which features had particularly caught his eye, but Willoughby interjected, "Marianne, I fear I'm not equipped to speak to the finer points of the picturesque. I'm apt to describe hills as steep when they're merely rugged, and landscapes as wild when they're simply untamed. I'd rather you were satisfied with my simple admiration, for I fear my words would only serve to confuse and bore you."

Marianne smiled wryly, acknowledging the truth in his words. "It's a trait I've observed in many," she said. "They speak of the beauty of nature with grandiose language, only to reveal their lack of genuine feeling beneath."
