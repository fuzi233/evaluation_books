The scent of damp earth lingered in the garden, a reminder of the previous day's rain. Harry strolled beside Maria, his footsteps echoing off the stone walls. They walked in silence, the only sound the soft rustle of leaves beneath their feet. As they approached the Lambert estate, Harry's thoughts turned to the family who had taken him in after his fall. He recalled the warm hospitality, the kindness of Colonel Lambert and his wife. A desire to repay their generosity stirred within him.

"I want to make them a present," Harry said, his voice barely above a whisper. He patted the pocket where his winnings lay. The crisp notes seemed to mock him, a reminder of his recent success at Morris's expense. "I must make them a gift."

Maria suggested they visit the toy shop to purchase dolls for Hester and Theo, but Harry dismissed the idea. "Dolls for grown women? I think not." He remembered the sisters' remarkable talents – their language skills, their love of books, and their music. Theo composed and sang her own songs, a talent Harry found captivating. The image of her playing the harpsichord lingered in his mind, the melancholy notes echoing through the garden.

As they walked, Maria mentioned the women in their lives who had criticized her for her interest in Harry's well-being. Harry defended her, assuring her that she was dear to him, though he knew he often neglected her. The memory of his recent successes at cards still danced in his head, and he felt an overwhelming urge to share them with Maria. He recounted his wins, his laughter and boasts carrying across the garden.

The sun began to set, casting long shadows across the lawn. Harry's enthusiasm waned, replaced by a sense of unease. He recalled the warnings from Maria about the dangers of gaming, the tragic fate of his family members who had succumbed to its allure. His fingers drummed against his leg, the rhythm of his anxiety matching the cadence of the crickets in the nearby bushes.

"You must beware, Harry," Maria cautioned, her voice low and serious. "Gaming has destroyed our family. Your uncle, your father, even your aunt... they've all fallen prey to it."

Harry's response was immediate, his defenses rising. "I'm not afraid of Lord March or Mr. Morris. I'll prove myself, Maria. I'll show them what I'm capable of." His words trailed off, lost in the fading light, as he continued his restless pacing
