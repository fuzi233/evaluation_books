The echo of my wife's Peace-cry faded into the silence of the evening, and I turned back toward the stranger who stood before me. I intended to approach him closely, perhaps offering him a seat, but the sight of him left me paralyzed by a shock so profound it rendered speech impossible. To my eyes, accustomed to the rigid geometry of Flatland, he appeared utterly devoid of angles. Yet, despite this apparent smoothness, his form seemed to fluctuate constantly, shifting in size and radiating a brilliance that defied the laws of any figure known to my world. A terrifying thought seized me: could this be a monstrous irregularity, a disguised Isosceles triangle feigning the guise of a Circle, intent on stabbing me with a hidden, acute angle?

The air in the room was unnaturally clear; the fog that usually obscured distant shapes was absent due to the unusually dry season. In such conditions, visual recognition became treacherous, especially at close range where perspective could deceive. Overcome by a paralyzing dread, I lunged forward, abandoning all decorum. "Sir!" I cried, rushing to touch him. "You must permit me..."

My hand found only smooth, unbroken curvature. My wife had spoken the truth. There was not a trace of an angle, not the slightest roughness or inequality. Never in my life had I encountered a circle so perfectly realized. He remained still as I circled him, starting from what I took to be his eye and returning again. Throughout, he maintained a flawless circular symmetry, leaving no room for doubt regarding his twodimensional nature.

From this state of awe, we moved into a dialogue, which I recall as faithfully as I can, suppressing much of my stammering apologies. I was consumed with shame and humiliation, a Square daring to physically inspect a Circle—a breach of etiquette I dared not repeat to my wife. The conversation began with the Stranger's evident impatience at the length of my initial hesitation.

**Stranger:** "Have you felt me sufficiently by now? Are you not introduced?"

**Me:** "Most illustrious Sir, forgive my clumsiness. It stems not from ignorance of social customs, but from a mixture of surprise and nervousness caused by this unexpected visit. I beg you to keep this indiscretion secret, particularly from my wife. However, before we proceed further, might you deign to satisfy the curiosity of a man who wishes to know whence this visitor comes?"

**Stranger:** "From Space, from Space, Sir: whence else?"

**Me:** "Pardon me, my Lord, but are you not already in Space, both you and your humble servant, at this very moment?"

**Stranger:** "Pooh! What do you know of Space? Define it."

**Me:** "Space, my Lord, is height and breadth indefinitely prolonged."

**Stranger:** "Exactly: you see, you do not even know what Space is. You believe it consists of only two dimensions. I have come to announce to you a third—height, breadth, and length."

**Me:** "Your Lordship is pleased to be merry. We also speak of length and height, or breadth and thickness, thus denoting two dimensions by four different names."

**Stranger:** "But I mean not merely three names, but *Three* Dimensions."

**Me:** "Would your Lordship indicate or explain to me in what direction lies this Third Dimension, unknown to me?"

**Stranger:** "I came from it. It is up above and down below."

**Me:** "My Lord seems to imply that it is Northward and Southward."

**Stranger:** "I mean nothing of the kind. I mean a direction in which you cannot look, because you lack an eye on your side."

**Me:** "Pardon me, my Lord; a moment's inspection will convince your Lordship that I possess a perfect luminary at the junction of two of my sides."

**Stranger:** "Yes: but to see into Space, you ought to have an eye, not on your perimeter, but on your side—that is, on what you would likely call your inside; but we in Spaceland would simply call it your side."

**Me:** "An eye in my inside! An eye in my stomach! Your Lordship jests."

**Stranger:** "I am in no jesting humor. I tell you I come from Space—or, since you refuse to understand what Space means, from the Land of Three Dimensions whence I but recently gazed down upon your Plane, which you foolishly call Space. From that advantageous vantage point, I perceived everything you refer to as SOLID (meaning 'enclosed on four sides'): your houses, your churches, your very chests and safes. Yes, even your insides and stomachs lay open and exposed to my view."

**Me:** "Such assertions are easily made, my Lord."

**Stranger:** "But not easily proved, you mean. But I intend to prove mine."

"When I descended here, I observed your four sons, the Pentagons, each in their respective apartments, and your two grandsons, the Hexagons. I watched your youngest Hexagon linger briefly before retiring to his chamber, leaving you and your wife alone. I saw your Isosceles servants, numbering three, dining in the kitchen, and the little Page working in the scullery. Then I arrived here. How do you suppose I entered?"

**Me:** "Through the roof, I suppose."

**Stranger:** "Not so. As you know very well, your roof has been recently repaired and possesses no aperture large enough for even a woman to penetrate. I tell you I come from Space. Are you not convinced by what I have told you of your children and household?"

**Me:** "Your Lordship must be aware that facts concerning the belongings of your humble servant could be easily ascertained by anyone in the neighborhood possessing your Lordship's ample resources for gathering information."

*(To himself)* **Stranger:** "What must I do? Stay; one more argument suggests itself to me. When you see a Straight Line—for example, your wife—how many dimensions do you attribute to her?"

**Me:** "Your Lordship treats me as if I were one of the vulgar, those ignorant of mathematics who suppose a Woman is truly a Straight Line of only One Dimension. No, no, my Lord; we Squares are better advised. We are as well aware as your Lordship that a Woman, though popularly termed a Straight Line, is scientifically a very thin Parallelogram, possessing Two Dimensions, namely, length and breadth (or thickness)."

**Stranger:** "But the very fact that a Line is visible implies that it possesses yet another dimension."

**Me:** "My Lord, I have just acknowledged that a Woman is both broad and long. We perceive her length and infer her breadth; which, though slight, is measurable."

**Stranger:** "You do not understand me. I mean that when you see a Woman, you ought—not merely infer her breadth—but *see* her length, and to SEE what we call her HEIGHT. Although that last dimension is infinitesimal in your country. If a Line were mere length without 'height', it would cease to occupy space and become invisible. Surely you recognize this?"

**Me:** "I must indeed confess that I do not in the least understand your Lordship. When we in Flatland see a Line, we see length and BRIGHTNESS. If the brightness disappears, the Line is extinguished, and, as you say, ceases to occupy space. Am I to suppose that your Lordship gives the title of a Dimension to brightness, and that what we call 'bright' you call 'high'?"

**Stranger:** "No, indeed. By 'height' I mean a dimension like your length: only, with you, 'height' is not so easily perceptible, being extremely minute."

**Me:** "My Lord, your assertion is easily put to the test. You claim I have a Third Dimension, which you call 'height'. Now, Dimension implies direction and measurement. Do but measure my 'height', or merely indicate to me the direction in which my 'height' extends, and I will become your convert. Otherwise, your Lordship's own understanding must hold me excused."

*(To himself)* **Stranger:** "I can do neither. How shall I convince him? Surely a plain statement of facts followed by ocular demonstration ought to suffice. —Now, Sir; listen to me."

"You live on a Plane. What you style Flatland is the vast, level surface of what I may call a fluid, upon which you and your countrymen move, unable to rise above it or fall below it.

"I am not a plane figure, but a Solid. You call me a Circle; but in reality, I am not a Circle, but an infinite number of circles, varying in size from a Point to a circle of thirteen inches in diameter, stacked one upon the other. When I cut through your plane as I am doing now, I create a section in your plane which you, very correctly, call a Circle. For even a Sphere—which is my proper name in my own country—if he manifests himself to an inhabitant of Flatland—must necessarily manifest himself as a Circle."
