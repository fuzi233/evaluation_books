As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the quaint streets of the town, Major Pendennis strolled towards the George Inn, lost in thought. Upon entering, he was greeted by the familiar figure of his faithful valet, Mr. Morgan, who intercepted him at the door with an air of quiet deference. "I think, sir, you might wish to join a gentleman in the coffee-room," Mr. Morgan said, his tone suggesting that this was a matter of some import.

The Major's brow furrowed in anticipation, and he quickened his pace towards the coffee-room. As he pushed open the door, a figure slumped over a table came into view. It was Mr. Foker, the son of Lady Agnes Foker, fast asleep amidst the remnants of dinner and the faint scent of cigar smoke. A half-empty decanter and the remnants of dessert lay scattered before him, a testament to a night spent indulging in revelry rather than the play.

The Major's eyes narrowed as he considered how best to approach the young man. However, Mr. Foker proved to be one step ahead, as he stirred from his slumber and rubbed the sleep from his eyes. "Like to peruse the evening paper, sir?" he asked, extending a hand towards the Globe newspaper, which lay abandoned on the table.

The Major's face broke into a warm smile as he accepted the offer. "I am deeply in your debt, Mr. Foker. I believe I have the pleasure of speaking to the son of Lady Agnes Foker, a lady whose acquaintance I have had the pleasure of making. You bear a striking resemblance to your mother, sir."

Mr. Foker chuckled, his eyes sparkling with amusement. "Ah, I say, I beg your pardon. I took you for a gentleman of commerce." He paused, collecting his thoughts before continuing. "To whom do I have the pleasure of speaking?"

"To a relative of a friend and schoolfellow of yours, Mr. Foker," the Major replied, his voice dripping with warmth. "Arthur Pendennis, my nephew, has often spoken of you in the highest regard. I am Major Pendennis, his uncle and guardian. May I join you at your table? I have had the pleasure of sitting at your grandfather's table on several occasions."
