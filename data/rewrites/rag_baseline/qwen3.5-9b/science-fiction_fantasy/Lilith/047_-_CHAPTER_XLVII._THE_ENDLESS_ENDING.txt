"The Endless Ending" remains elusive, though Mara's presence anchors me firmly in the present. She has imparted wisdom beyond measure, continuing to shape my understanding with every passing hour. Yet, a gnawing uncertainty persists: Was that final awakening merely another layer of the dream? Am I still trapped within the chamber of death, suspended between slumber and consciousness, unripe for the transition? Or did I fail to surrender fully to sleep, emerging prematurely from a cycle not yet complete? If that awakening was itself a fabrication, then it must herald a superior state of being—a promise of a truer dawn where I am no longer a pawn of illusion. Even a false vision must harbor a kernel of higher truth at its core.

In moments of profound doubt, I cry out, "Could the Divine Architect conceive of such exquisite realities as those I envisioned?" Hope responds, "From the depths of your obscured self, into the luminous expanse of your awareness." I counter, "But how did it first descend into your darkness?" Hope replies, "Your mind was the vessel; the fever coursing through your veins provided the impetus." Then comes the refinement: "Rather, your intellect was the instrument, the burning passion the bowstring drawing forth the melody. But who crafted the instrument? Who directed the bow across the strings? Consider further—who placed the songbirds upon the branches of the Tree of Life, startling them into song in their appointed order? Where did the fantasia originate? What animated the dance? Did you, in the abyss of your unconscious, decree, 'Let beauty exist; let truth appear,' and instantly beauty manifested while truth remained mere semblance?"

Man dreams and desires; God contemplates, wills, and brings to life. When a mortal crafts his own dream, he becomes its plaything; when another bestows it, that other holds the power to fulfill it. I have ceased seeking the mirror entirely. The hand returned me; I shall not exit through that portal again. "For all the days of my allotted time, I shall wait until my transformation arrives." Occasionally, surveying my collection of texts, they seem to tremble as if caught in a phantom breeze, threatening to dissolve into another realm. Similarly, when wandering outdoors, the sky and earth, the foliage and turf, momentarily quake as though ready to vanish, only to settle once more into their familiar, static forms. At times, faint whispers drift around me, as if beloved voices discuss me behind closed doors, yet the moment I attempt to discern the words, silence descends absolute. I cannot determine if these phenomena arise within my mind or infiltrate from without. I neither pursue nor suppress them; they arrive, and I release them. Vague, indistinct memories—refusing categorization—peer out at me through the foggy panes of the past under the harsh glare of daylight, though I no longer dream. Perhaps, ironically, when I am most lucid, I am merely dreaming a deeper dream! But when I finally awaken into that existence which, like a mother cradling her child, encompasses this current life within its bosom, I shall possess undeniable certainty. I wait. Whether in slumber or wakefulness, I wait.
