I wiped the moisture from my eyes and caught sight of the raven perched on the rim of a massive stone basin. The pale, cold light of dawn gleamed off his glossy feathers as he looked down upon me with calm detachment. I lay submerged in the water of the great fountain my father had built in the center of the lawn, propped up on my elbows, lifting my face just enough to meet his gaze. Above us, high in the sky, the thick, steel-like stalk of the fountain's mechanism shot upward, erupting in a torrent that soared a hundred feet before blooming into a crown of white foam.

Stinging from the chill of the bird's earlier words—"You told me nothing!"—I retorted, "I instructed you to do nothing anyone you distrusted asked you to do!"

"Tut! How was a mortal expected to remember that?"

"You will not forget the consequences of forgetting it," Mr. Raven replied. He leaned over the marble edge of the basin and extended a clawed hand toward me. I grasped it, and instantly, I was standing beside him on the dry grass, dripping and shivering.

"You must change your clothes immediately," he commanded. "A wetting matters little regarding your origins—though currently, such an accident is rare; here, it brings mere inconvenience."

He resumed his form as a raven, striding with dignified steps toward the house, whose doors stood wide open.

"I haven't much to change," I laughed, tossing aside my robe after climbing the tree. "It has been ages since I molted a feather!"

The raven chuckled. "In the house, no one seems awake."

I retreated to my chamber, donned a dressing gown, and descended to the library. As I crossed the threshold, the librarian emerged from a closet. Without a word, I collapsed onto a nearby couch. Mr. Raven pulled a chair close and sat beside me. For a moment, neither of us spoke.

Finally, I broke the silence. "What does it all mean?"

"A pertinent inquiry," he answered. "No one truly understands anything; a person can only grasp what a thing signifies. Whether they do depends entirely on how they utilize it."

"I have utilized nothing yet!"

"Not significantly, but you possess the awareness of that fact, which counts for something. Most individuals spend a lifetime realizing they have learned nothing and accomplished less. At least, you have harbored the desire to be useful."

"I wished to assist the children—the precious Little Ones, specifically."

"I am aware of your intent—and you chose the wrong path."

"I didn't know the correct path."

"That is accurate, yet you are culpable for failing to discover it."

"I am prepared to accept whatever truth you reveal, provided I comprehend its meaning."

"If you had accepted our invitation, you would have known the right way. When a man refuses to act within his own sphere, he must travel far to locate his work."

"Indeed, I have traveled far and arrived nowhere, for I still have not found my work! I abandoned the children to learn how to serve them, only to realize the peril they faced."

"When you were with them, you were exactly where you could aid them. By leaving, you sought your work elsewhere. It requires wisdom to know when to depart; folly lies in rushing back too soon."

"Do you imply, sir, that I could have aided the Little Ones by remaining with them?"

"Could you instruct them by abandoning them?"

"No. But how was I to begin teaching? Furthermore, they were far ahead of me!"

"That is true. Yet, you were not a ruler against which to measure them! If they possessed your knowledge—or rather, what you might have acquired—they would be ahead of you, utterly out of sight. However, you observed they were not growing, or growing so sluggishly that the concept of growth had not yet formed in their minds. They were even fearful of growing! You had never witnessed children simply *remain* children."

"But surely I lacked the power to force them to grow!"

"You could have eliminated certain obstacles preventing their growth!"

"What obstacles? I am unaware of them. I suspected perhaps it was a lack of water!"

"Certainly it was! They had no tears to shed!"

"I would have gladly prevented them from needing to weep!"

"No doubt you would—that is the hallmark of all foolish philanthropy! Why, Mr. Vane, were it not for the necessity of weeping, your world would never have been deemed worthy of salvation! You admitted you considered it might be thirst they felt; why did you not dig them a few wells?"

"That never occurred to me!"

"Not even when the sounds of subterranean waters reached your ears?"

"I believe it happened once. But I feared the giants for their sake. That was precisely why I endured so much abuse from those beasts myself!"

"In truth, you nearly conditioned these noble little creatures to dread the stupid Bags! While they fed, comforted, and worshipped you, you simultaneously allowed yourself to become a slave to bestial men. You offered the darlings a seeming coward in place of a hero! A graver injustice you could scarcely inflict. They surrendered their hearts; you owed them your soul! By now, you should have compelled the Bags to become woodcutters and water-carriers for the Little Ones!"

"I fear your assessment holds merit, Mr. Raven! Yet, I genuinely believed that imparting further knowledge might harm them, rendering them less innocent, less lovely."

"They afforded you no cause to harbor such fears!"

"Is not a modicum of knowledge a perilous thing?"

"That is one of the cherished falsehoods of your world! Is humanity's greatest knowledge merely 'a little'? Or because it is vast, does that render it dangerous? The notion that knowledge is inherently grand makes any degree of understanding seem more hazardous than any amount of ignorance. Knowing everything would not constitute greatness."

"At least, I acted out of love for them, not cowardice!"

"Granted. Nevertheless, you ought to have served the Little Ones, not the giants! You should have provided the Little Ones with water; then, they would swiftly have taught the giants their true station. Meanwhile, you could have ordered the giants to fell two-thirds of their coarse fruit trees to make space for the delicate ones! You squandered your opportunity with the Lovers, Mr. Vane. You speculated upon them instead of assisting them!"
