As the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, Lieutenant Sutch stirred from his slumber, his mind racing with excitement. He sprang from bed and began to rouse the household, his footsteps echoing through the silent halls as he hastened to prepare for the day ahead. His servants scurried to attend to his needs, their usual calm demeanor replaced by a sense of urgency as they struggled to keep pace with the lieutenant's frenetic energy. The kitchen, too, was soon abuzz with activity, the aroma of freshly baked bread wafting through the air as Sutch's cook, Mrs. Jenkins, set about preparing a hearty breakfast.

As the meal was served, Sutch turned to his friend Durrance, who was sipping his tea in quiet contemplation. "I shall be ready to depart by this afternoon," Sutch announced, his voice filled with anticipation. "I'll catch the night mail to the Continent, and we can stop in London on our way to Wiesbaden." Durrance, however, declined the offer, revealing that he had a prior engagement in Surrey – a visit to General Feversham, the father of Harry Feversham, whose recent redemption had left the young man with a newfound sense of purpose.

Sutch's interest was piqued. "Whom do you plan to visit, exactly?" he asked, his curiosity getting the better of him. Durrance explained that he wished to inform General Feversham of Harry's courageous actions, hoping to ease the old man's concerns and alleviate his guilt. Sutch listened intently, his expression unreadable, as Durrance spoke of the weight of responsibility that rested on the general's shoulders. Though Sutch had always harbored a deep-seated resentment towards General Feversham, he couldn't deny the old man's dedication to his family's honor, even if it sometimes led him astray.

As they finished their meal, Sutch's thoughts turned to the culinary delights that lay ahead. He envisioned the rich flavors and aromas of the European continent, the spices and herbs that would dance on his palate as he traveled through the rolling hills and bustling cities. His stomach growled in anticipation, and he excused himself to attend to his packing, his mind whirling with visions of the feasts to come.
