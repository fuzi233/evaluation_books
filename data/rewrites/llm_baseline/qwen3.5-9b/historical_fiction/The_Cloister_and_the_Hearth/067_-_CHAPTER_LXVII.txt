It would indeed have been strange if, possessing such scanty clues, those men could have deciphered the message written upon the riverbank. Yet, right there on that very spot, an event had just transpired—one that stands alone in human history, likely destined to remain unmatched until the final hour. This tale shall be recounted briefly, woven together from the voice of the narrator and the memory of a participant within the drama itself.

Gerard, having penned his short farewell to Pietro and Andrea, slipped away toward the river as twilight deepened. Driven by a grim determination common among those resolved upon their own undoing, he took careful measure. He stuffed his pockets with every piece of silver and copper he owned, intending to weigh his descent more surely. Thus equipped, he hastened to a secluded stretch of the stream he had previously noted as rarely traversed. Some, particularly women, glance around to ensure a witness is nearby; but this desperate soul scanned the shadows solely to confirm solitude. To his irritation, however, he spotted a solitary figure leaning against the corner of an alleyway. Pretending to wander aimlessly, he moved away, only to return moments later. There stood the same figure once again, emerging from a side street and lingering with deliberate slowness.

"Does he watch me?" Gerard wondered, his heart pounding. "Does he know my purpose? Impossible!" He strode briskly onward, navigating two streets before making a wide circuit, yet when he returned, the man had vanished. But as Gerard cast a wary gaze around to verify the emptiness of the path, there he was—just a few paces behind, seemingly adjusting his shoe. Realizing he was being shadowed, Gerard suddenly noticed in the pale moonlight a steel gauntlet resting in the sentinel's hand. Instantly, the truth struck him: this was an assassin. Strangely, it never crossed his mind that his own life was the intended target. After all, he knew of no enemies in the world.

Turning directly toward the bravo, Gerard approached with frantic urgency. "Good friend," he cried out breathlessly, "buy my arm! Strike a single blow! Behold, here is all I possess;" forcing his coins into the assassin's grasp. "Oh, pray thee! Pray thee! Do one act of mercy and release me from this hateful existence!" As he spoke, he unfastened his doublet, exposing his bare chest. The man stared silently at him. "Why do you hesitate?" Gerard shrieked, his voice cracking. "Do you lack compassion? Is it too great a pain to raise your arm and deliver the blow? Or is it because I am poor and cannot offer gold? Wretched creature, you can only strike a man from behind; you dare not meet his gaze! Very well, then, merely turn your head and keep silent!" With a snarl of utter contempt, Gerard fled from him and threw himself into the water.

"Margaret!"

At the heavy splash of his body hitting the stream, the bravo seemed to shake off a sudden stupor. He sprinted to the bank, issuing a strange cry, before plunging in after the man seeking death. What ensued next shall be related by the assassin himself.
