I collapsed onto the bed, my mind racing through the tale she had told me. In her moment of vulnerability, a single careless word had unlocked a mystery—revealing how I had found her in the forest: the leopardess leapt over, the princess lay helpless on the riverbank, and the flowing water had shattered her self-imposed spell. Her own words now painted the danger that threatened the Little Ones—and I had unwittingly saved their greatest enemy. The realization struck me just as sleep claimed me. Perhaps the wine had not been entirely harmless.

When I awoke, night had fallen. A soft lamp hung from the ceiling, casting a gentle glow across the room. A deep, dreamlike calm washed over me. I felt suspended, adrift on a twilight sea far from shore. Life itself was pleasure. There was no pain—until there was. A sharp, searing sting pierced my heart. Another followed—more intense, more cruel. I couldn’t move my hand to touch the wound; something held it down. The pain faded, but my body remained numb. Something vile pressed upon me—something loathsome. I tried to fight, but my will was trapped, powerless. I gave in, lying still.

Then I felt a delicate hand on my face, gently pressing my head into the pillow. A heavy weight rested across my chest. I breathed easier as it lifted. My eyes fluttered open.

The princess stood above me, one foot on the bed, gazing into the room with a distant, dreamy expression. Her eyes were clear, her lips tinged with satisfaction. She wiped a smear of red from her mouth. Our eyes met. She leaned forward and struck me across the eyes with a handkerchief—like dragging a blade across glass. For a moment, darkness swallowed my vision.

A dull thud echoed—the sound of a large, soft-footed creature landing after a short leap. I blinked, and saw the long sweep of a tail vanishing through the half-open door. I lunged after it.

It was gone. I raced down the stairs, bursting into the alabaster hall. The moon hung high, bathing the space in pale silver light—like the inside of a sun-bleached moon. But the princess was nowhere.

I needed her presence. Only then could I defend myself. Without her, I was helpless—a captive offering, a living well for a demon’s thirst. She had shown kindness only to exploit me. My waking eyes didn’t fear her—but when they closed, she would come.

I searched blindly through the darkened corridors, hands outstretched. At last, I touched a curtain. I pushed it aside and stepped into the black hall.

There, an eerie gathering awaited. Though the walls, floor, and ceiling were lost in impenetrable darkness—deeper than any starless night—I could vaguely make out figures moving within the void. Shapes flickered in and out of existence: a woman with radiant eyes peering from a skull, a warrior mounted on a skeletal steed, grotesque burrowing phantoms rising from the shadows. No pattern emerged—only chaos. Forms merged, split, danced, dissolved. Shadows moved independently, guided by their own silent wills.

I scanned the crowd, searching for her. But she was not there—not in this shifting, surreal nightmare. Where was she? What was she doing?

No one noticed me as I wandered. Despair crept in. I turned away, tracing the wall with my fingers. Groping forward, I reached a curtained entrance leading to the vestibule.

Moonlight filtered through, illuminating the leopardess’s cage. Inside, two forms writhed in silent combat—one human, one beast—locked in a desperate embrace. Before I could react, the leopardess stepped out of the cage, calm and composed, walking toward the open door.

I followed, glancing back—only to see the leopardess still crouched motionless in the cage, exactly as I’d first seen her.

Above, the moon rose full and bright. The shadow I’d seen the night before walked through the trees toward the gate. Behind him came the leopardess, her tail swaying like a banner.

I trailed them silently, unseen. They passed through the gate and descended into the city, quiet under the moon’s gaze. The moon seemed still—expectant.

The shadow moved straight to the stair where I had lain the night before. He climbed without pause. The leopardess followed.

I quickened my pace. Then—a cry of horror. A soft, heavy object fell between me and the steps. At my feet lay a body—blackened, crushed, unmistakably the woman who had brought me home and locked me out.

Frozen, I watched as the spotted leopardess bounded down the stairs, a baby clutched in her jaws. I lunged forward, ready to snatch it from her.

But from behind me, a flash of silver cut through the moonlight. The white leopardess—like a bar of molten moonlight—shot forward and seized the other by the neck.

The baby dropped. I caught it. I stood, transfixed, as the two cats clashed.

What a battle! One moment the spotted leopardess dominated, the next the white one twisted free. Neither roared—only growled, whimpered, snarled—each movement followed by frantic scrambling of claws, teeth gnashing, bodies wrestling for purchase on the stone.

The spotted one was larger, but the white proved more enduring. She never released her grip. Slowly, the spotted leopardess began to fail. From her throat rose a howl—deepening, twisting—until it became the agonized wail of a woman at the edge of death.

The white leopardess loosened her jaws. The spotted one pulled back, rising on her hind legs.

And there, bathed in moonlight, stood the princess. Shadows surged across her form—spots fleeing, converging, dissolving into her eyes. The last few streaked into her hair, vanishing like mist before dawn. She shone, pure and radiant, save for the thin trail of blood running down her throat—each drop marking the fury of her foe’s fangs.

She turned, took a few steps with the stride of a goddess, then fell—her skin once again speckled—and fled into the night at a gallop.

The white leopardess turned too. She leapt toward me, tore my arms apart, caught the falling child, and vanished into the street, racing toward the gate with the baby cradled in her jaws.
