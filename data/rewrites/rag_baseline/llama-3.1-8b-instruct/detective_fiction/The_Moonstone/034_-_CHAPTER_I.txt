As I sit here, reflecting on the intricate tapestry of my tale, I find myself entwined with the lives of those who have shaped the narrative of the Moonstone. The threads of fate, so delicate and intertwined, weave together the stories of love, loss, and legacy. It begins with a visit to the bedside of my dear friend, the late Sir John Verinder. A man of many charms, Sir John harbored a weakness, one that would prove pivotal in the unfolding drama: his reluctance to confront the responsibility of making his will. His devoted wife, Lady Verinder, and I worked tirelessly to persuade him to address this pressing matter. It wasn't until his illness took hold that he finally saw the wisdom in our words.

Upon entering the room, I was greeted by the soft murmur of his snores. As I approached, he stirred, his eyes fluttering open to acknowledge my presence. "Ah, Mr. Bruff," he said, his voice laced with a hint of weariness. "I shan't be long about this. And then I'll rest once more." He watched intently as I gathered my tools: pens, ink, and paper. "Are you prepared?" he asked, his gaze lingering on the instruments before returning to mine. I nodded, dipped my quill, and waited for his instructions.

With a faint smile, he spoke, "I leave everything to my wife. That's all." His eyes drifted shut, and he settled into a peaceful slumber. I felt compelled to intervene, to clarify the terms of his will. "If I may be so bold, sir, are you certain you wish to leave your entire estate – lands and monies alike – to Lady Verinder, without condition or restriction?"

"Yes," he replied, his voice firm, "only, I suppose you could put it more succinctly. Why can't you simplify it, and let me rest?" His property, a vast and varied legacy, lay at his discretion. While, in most cases, I would have counseled caution, I knew Lady Verinder to be a paragon of virtue and prudence, capable of managing the trust her husband had placed in her. Within the span of ten minutes, the will was drafted, signed, and sealed, and Sir John, weary but content, surrendered to his slumber.
