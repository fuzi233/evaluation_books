As Romola emerged from the hospital, she found herself taking a detour along the winding streets of Florence, drawn towards the gentle curve of the Arno River. More than an hour had passed since she'd left the hospital, and she now approached the Ponte Vecchio from a different angle, her thoughts preoccupied with the prospect of seeking out Baldassarre at San Stefano. Though she dreaded the encounter, a nagging sense of responsibility lingered within her, as if abandoning him would mean forsaking a connection that felt almost familial.

The sun beat down upon her, casting long shadows across the cobblestones as she navigated the crowded streets. As she reached the juncture of the roads where the Por' Santa Maria would be on her right-hand and the Ponte Vecchio on her left, she found herself swept up in a sea of worshippers. People fell to their knees, and Romola followed suit, joining in the fervent devotion as the Great Cross of the Duomo passed by, leading the procession. She was later than she'd anticipated, and now she must wait until the Cross had disappeared before continuing on her way.

As she rose from her knees, the transition from kneeling to standing brought a sudden awareness of her fatigue. A shopkeeper beside her offered words of kindness, saying, "Madonna Romola, you must be weary from standing. Gian Fantoni would be honored to offer you a place in his home. His door is just here; let me open it for you." Romola was accustomed to being greeted with such warmth by the people of Florence, whose faces she'd grown familiar with from countless visits to the Duomo. For her, the concept of home had shifted from the Via de' Bardi, where she often sat in solitude, to the city itself, where every turn revealed a friendly smile or a look of recognition.

Grateful for the offer, Romola stepped through the open doorway and followed the shopkeeper up to an upstairs window, where a stout woman with three children in the simple garb of the Piagnoni welcomed her with reverence. From this vantage point, Romola watched as the procession moved slowly across the Ponte Vecchio, its participants' footsteps echoing off the stone buildings. The air was heavy with the scent of incense and the soft rustle of woolen robes.
