The fog crept through the narrow alleys of Flatland like a living entity, shrouding the city in an impenetrable veil. Yet, within the confines of the Leeborough estate, a different world unfolded. I, Edwin A. Abbott, sat in my study, surrounded by the trappings of a life spent devoted to the pursuit of knowledge. The fire crackled, casting flickering shadows on the walls as I pondered the weight of my discovery. The Revelation of the Three Dimensions had come to me in a dream, and I felt an insatiable urge to share it with the world.

But the world, it seemed, was not yet ready to receive such a revelation. The recent Proclamation of the Council still lingered in the air, a constant reminder of the dangers of spreading ideas deemed seditious by the ruling Circles. I knew that my Sons, though well-respected in their fields, might view my theories with suspicion, their loyalty to the established order too strong to allow them to question the fundamental nature of our reality.

My mind turned to my Grandson, a bright and inquisitive young mind, unencumbered by the prejudices of the older generation. I recalled his earlier conversation with the Sphere, and how he had shown a spark of understanding, a glimmer of recognition that there was more to the world than the two dimensions we knew. I resolved to approach him first, to gauge his receptivity to the idea of a third dimension, and perhaps, to convert him into a willing apostle for the cause.

As I waited for my Grandson to arrive, I took a moment to compose myself, collecting my thoughts and rehearsing the demonstration that would change everything. The simplicity of the proof eluded me, however, and I found myself questioning the very fabric of my own understanding. The notion of "Upward, not Northward" seemed less certain, less self-evident, than I had initially thought. My Wife's entry into the room broke the spell, and I decided to postpone our discussion, hoping to clarify my thoughts before embarking on this perilous journey.
