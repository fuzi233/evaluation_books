As I lay in bed, sipping the early morning tea and watching Jeeves flit about the room, preparing my attire for the day, I often pondered what I would do if he were ever to decide to leave me. It's not a concern I dwell on now that I'm in New York, but in London, the thought sent shivers down my spine. I recall the numerous attempts made by unsavory characters to lure him away from me. Young Reggie Foljambe, for instance, was known to have offered him a handsome sum, far exceeding what I paid him, and Alistair Bingham-Reeves, whose valet had a reputation for pressing trousers askew, would gaze at Jeeves with an unnerving intensity whenever he visited me.

The thing that unsettled me most, however, was Jeeves's unwavering competence. Even in the simple act of inserting studs into a shirt, he exuded an air of quiet assurance that was both reassuring and intimidating. I relied on him implicitly in times of crisis, and he never failed to deliver. Moreover, he consistently went above and beyond to assist my friends in need, often putting their interests ahead of his own. A case in point was dear old Bicky and his uncle, the Duke of Chiswick.

It was a few months ago, when I returned to our flat late one evening, that Jeeves informed me of Bicky's visit. "Mr. Bickersteth called to see you this evening, sir, while you were out," he said, handing me my final drink. "He appeared a trifle agitated." I raised an eyebrow, intrigued by the prospect of discussing something other than my own concerns with Jeeves. We had been somewhat distant lately, and it had been challenging to find topics that didn't touch on my decision to grow a moustache – a choice that had, much to my dismay, earned Jeeves's disapproval. I had come to realize that, while his fashion sense was impeccable, he shouldn't have a say in my facial hair. After all, no one could accuse me of being unreasonable, and I had given in to his wishes on numerous occasions when it came to my wardrobe choices. However, a valet's claim on one's upper lip was a different matter altogether.
