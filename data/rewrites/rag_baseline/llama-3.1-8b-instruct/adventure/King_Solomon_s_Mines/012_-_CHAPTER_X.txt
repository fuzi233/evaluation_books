The crimson sun sank low over the Kukuanan hills, casting a fiery glow over the parched landscape. In the midst of this desolate beauty, a sense of foreboding settled over our small encampment. The air was heavy with the weight of secrets and the whispers of the wind carried the faint scent of smoke and ash. It was on this eve, when the moon hung low in the sky, that the witch-hunt would begin. A time when the king's cruelty knew no bounds, and the cries of the innocent mingled with the rustling of the leaves.

As we approached the humble abode of Infadoos, I motioned for him to join us, his weathered face a map of the trials he had endured. "Come, Infadoos," I said, "let us speak with you." His eyes, worn from the hardships of a lifetime, searched ours, and with a nod, he agreed to share his thoughts.

"The land cries out in anguish beneath Twala's rule," he began, his voice a low, mournful sigh. "His cruelties have become the stuff of legend, a testament to the darkness that dwells within his heart. Tonight, many will be accused of sorcery, their lives forfeit to the whims of the king. No man's fate is secure, for if the monarch desires something, nothing can stand in his way. Cattle, wives, or even a man's very life – all are subject to his capricious nature."

Infadoos' words hung in the air, heavy with the weight of truth. "And yet," I pressed on, "why do the people not rise up against him?" The old soldier's gaze drifted towards the distant mountains, his eyes clouding over with memories of a bygone era.

"Twala is the king, and in his place, Scragga would only bring greater suffering. His heart is blacker still than his father's, and the yoke he would impose would be heavier still. If Imotu had not fallen, or if Ignosi had lived, perhaps things might have been different. But those days are lost to us now."

A voice, low and measured, broke the silence. Umbopa stepped forward, his eyes aglow with a fire that seemed to burn brighter with every passing moment. "How do you know that Ignosi is dead?" he asked, his words dripping with a quiet conviction.
