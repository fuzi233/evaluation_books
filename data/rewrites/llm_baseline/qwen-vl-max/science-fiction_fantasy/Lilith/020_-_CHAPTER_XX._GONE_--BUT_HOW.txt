I stood up, dazed and trembling, my heart a hollow drum in my chest. She was gone—vanished like mist under a rising sun. The silence that followed her departure was heavier than any storm. The woman I had dragged from death’s edge, the one I had nurtured back into life, had fled. And now, the world felt stripped bare, as if all meaning had been torn away.

I could not stay here. Not alone. Was it my fault? Had I wronged her in some way I couldn’t see? I had saved her—not for myself, but because she deserved to live. Yet she turned from me with hatred in her eyes. How could she despise me? I had touched her only with care, with reverence. Like a father cradling a child lost to the world, I had held her through fever and pain. And yet… she rejected me.

Was it pride? Or something deeper?

My thoughts spiraled as I moved forward, following the trail of her steps across the soft grass. I’d just taken a quick plunge into the river—cold water, sharp and bracing—before setting out after her. The leeches had drained me, the blow from her hand had weakened me further, but now, strange as it seemed, strength returned. My limbs felt lighter, more alive. I could keep pace with her without effort.

“Is this the end?” I whispered, the words hanging in the air like a prayer.

Her face flashed before me—the anger, the coldness. I understood her rage at being forced back into life. But beyond that… what had I done? Could modesty itself recoil from kindness? Could a woman who knew every act I’d performed still feel shame toward me? I had never crossed a line. I had never once betrayed trust. And yet, she loathed me.

Had all my labor—my desperate hope—been for nothing? Was I to be repaid only with scorn?

“No,” I said aloud, voice firming. “Beauty must have a heart. Even if buried deep beneath ice and shadow, it must exist. The deeper it lies, the more fiercely it will awaken when stirred. To awaken that heart would be greater than giving her mere life. It would give her purpose. It would give her soul.”

She climbed a gentle hill ahead of me, walking with steady grace, as though she knew exactly where she was going. Then I noticed: she was pulling ahead. I summoned what remained of my strength—and it surged within me. My veins hummed with renewed energy. My body felt almost weightless. I moved like wind over water, catching up swiftly.

She didn’t look back. Not once.

She walked like a goddess of ancient myth—swift, serene, untouched by haste. I closed the distance until I was just three yards behind. Then she turned.

Not abruptly, but with a fluid elegance that made the motion seem eternal. She stopped. Her face was calm. No sign of fatigue. No breathlessness. Only paleness—pure, luminous, like moonlight on snow. Her eyes… they were vast, absorbing the sky, illuminating everything around them. It was nearly noon, yet the world felt cloaked in night, as if an invisible dew had fallen, making stars appear enormous and near.

"Why do you follow me?" she asked, voice quiet, yet edged with command—as if she had never seen me before.

"I have lived," I said, "only for the hope of seeing your eyes again."

"You will not be spared," she replied, coldly. "Stop where you stand."

"Not until I know you are safe," I insisted.

"Then take the consequences," she said, and continued forward.

But as she turned, she cast me a glance—and I froze.

It wasn't anger. Not even scorn. It was something worse: beauty so absolute, so terrifying in its purity, that it struck like a blade. I felt myself crumbling under its weight.

Despair flared, then snapped into resolve. I ran.

I caught up to her.

"Have pity on me!" I begged.

She ignored me.

I followed—like a child chasing a mother who pretends to leave him behind.

"I will be your slave!" I cried, reaching out, placing my hand gently on her arm.

She recoiled as if bitten by a serpent.

I cowered beneath the fire of her gaze—but could not turn away.

"Pity me," I pleaded again.

She resumed walking.

And so I followed.

The sun rose, crested the sky, and began its descent. Hours passed. I did not stop. She did not pause. Not once. Not even to breathe. The day bled into twilight, and darkness swallowed the land.

Still I followed.

We had walked through thick, yielding grass—soft as velvet underfoot—until suddenly, she stopped. Then she collapsed onto the ground.

There was still enough light to see that she was exhausted. Utterly spent.

I stood behind her, staring down.

Did I love her? I knew she was not good. Not in the way the world defines goodness. But I could not leave her.

I knelt beside her.

"Begone!" she hissed. "Do not dare touch me!"

Her arms lay limp at her sides—still, rigid, as if paralyzed.

Then—suddenly—they wrapped around my neck, tight as iron chains. She pulled me down, her lips pressing against my cheek.

A sharp pain pierced me—deep, electric. It pulsed through my veins. I could not move. Not a muscle.

Slowly, the pain faded.

A warmth spread through me—a dreamlike numbness, sweet and heavy. Then… nothing.

Darkness.

When I came to, the moon hung low, pale and distant, casting no light—only a faint glow in the black sky. My cheek stung. I touched it. Wet. Blood.

My neck ached. There, too, a damp spot.

I sighed, weary beyond measure.

I turned my head slowly.

And saw it.

The moonlight—once dim—now gathered around her. She stood bathed in a shimmering halo, glowing like a spirit from another realm.

I rose, unsteady, swaying toward her.

"Down!" she commanded, voice sharp as steel. "Follow me a step—if you dare!"

"I will," I murmured, voice cracking with effort.

"Set foot inside my city," she warned, "and my people will stone you. They do not welcome beggars."

I did not hear her.

Weak, half-conscious, I did not know I was moving—yet the distance between us shrank.

She stepped back. Raised her left hand. Clenched it.

And struck me across the forehead.

It was like being hit by a hammer forged from iron.

I fell.

Then I rose—cold, soaked, but clear-headed, strong.

No wound. No pain.

But why was I wet?

I looked around.

The moon had not risen higher. Time had barely passed.

She stood several yards away, her back to me.

She was doing something. I couldn’t tell what.

Then—she glowed.

And I realized: she had stripped off her clothes.

She stood naked in the moonlight—white as bone, radiant as starlight.

One moment she stood.

Then she fell forward.

A streak of white shot across the field—fast, low, silent.

At the same instant, the moon blazed to life, full and bright.

And I saw.

It was a creature—long-bodied, swift, bounding across the grass in great, curved leaps. Dark spots trailed along its back, like shadows clinging to it—perhaps from trees or foliage it had passed beneath.

"God of mercy," I breathed. "Is that thing heading for the city?"

From afar, I heard it—a sudden eruption of screams, of terror, spreading through the darkened town. The pale beast leapt from house to house, tearing, destroying, devouring.

I watched, frozen.

Then—behind me—another shape shot past.

Faster than the first.

Pure white. Larger. More powerful.

Its path led straight to the place where the lady had fallen.

My mouth went dry. I ran.

But by the time I reached the spot, there was no one.

Only her clothes—lying in the moonlight, abandoned.

I stared after the second beast.

It moved with impossible speed—leaping long distances, skimming the earth like a ghost. It followed the same path as the first, vanishing into the horizon.

But where was she?

Had the first beast attacked her silently? I hadn’t heard a scream.

Could it have carried her away?

Impossible. A creature bearing such a burden could not run so fast. And I would have seen it.

Yet—no trace.

I searched the area—thoroughly, desperately. Nothing.

Then I turned, and set out after the beasts.

Not knowing what I would find.

But knowing I could not stop.
