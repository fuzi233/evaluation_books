CHAPTER III. THE RAVEN

As I turned to scan the surroundings, the world dissolved into a haze of uncertainty, much like the indistinct boundary between fog and field. My gaze drifted over the desolate landscape, but nothing seemed familiar. It was as if I had stumbled into a realm where the very fabric of reality had begun to unravel. Every step felt like a gamble, every sense heightened to the point of paranoia. I extended my arms, feeling the chill of the autumn air, and began to circle around, searching for some tangible connection to the world I thought I knew.

My attention was drawn to a lone figure perched on a nearby branch, its sleek black form a stark contrast to the barren surroundings. The raven watched me with an air of quiet curiosity, its gaze piercing through the veil of confusion that shrouded me. For a moment, I considered the absurdity of seeking guidance from such a creature, and turned away, overwhelmed by the depth of my bewilderment and the creeping sense of fear that threatened to consume me.

Yet, I found myself turning back to the raven, drawn by an inexplicable sense of trust. Its voice, though roughened by a tendency to croak, was not unpleasant, and its words, though cryptic, held a hint of wisdom.

"I did not come through any door," I said, trying to shake off the feeling of disorientation.

The raven cocked its head to one side, its eyes glinting with a knowing light. "I saw you come through it—with my own ancient eyes," it replied, its tone neither dismissive nor disrespectful.

I shook my head, frustration mounting within me. "I never saw any door."

The raven's expression turned thoughtful. "All the doors you've seen so far were doors in. Here, you stumbled upon a door out. The strange thing to you will be that the more doors you exit, the deeper you'll find yourself."

I felt a shiver run down my spine as I tried to grasp the raven's enigmatic words. "Tell me, where am I?"

The raven's response was characteristically laconic. "Impossible. You know nothing about being in a place. The only way to understand where you are is to start making yourself at home."

"How can I do that when everything feels so alien?" I asked, desperation creeping into my voice.
