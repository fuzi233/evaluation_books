In the velvety hush of a late autumn morning, the misty veil of dawn clung to the streets of Milton, shrouding the sleepy town in a gentle, ethereal gloom. Edith Hale stirred, her thoughts already preoccupied with the weighty matters that lay ahead. She moved with deliberate stealth, careful not to startle Sholto, who was engrossed in his morning routine, as if the very fate of the household hung in the balance. Two o'clock loomed, and still the drawing-room remained sealed off, its occupants locked in a somber discussion that seemed to hold the power to shape the future. The air was heavy with anticipation, the only sound the soft ticking of the grandfather clock, a metronome marking the passing of time.

As the minutes ticked by, a solitary footstep echoed down the stairs, and Edith peered out from the doorway, her eyes questioning. "Well, Henry?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Henry's response was curt, a mere acknowledgement of the unspoken understanding that hung between them. "Come in to lunch," Edith urged, her tone a mixture of entreaty and resignation.

But Henry declined, his words a testament to the weight of responsibility that bore down upon him. "I've lost too much time already." His tone was laced with a deep-seated frustration, a sense of being trapped in a labyrinth from which there was no escape.

Edith's face fell, her hopes dashed against the rocks of reality. "Then it's not all settled?" she asked, her voice tinged with despair.

"Not at all," Henry replied, his words a stark reminder of the unbridgeable chasm that yawned between them. "It never will be settled, Edith, so give up thinking about it." His words cut through her like a knife, leaving her reeling.

"But it would be so nice for us all," she protested, her voice a desperate plea. "I should always feel comfortable about the children, if I had Margaret settled down near to me." Her words hung in the air, a poignant reminder of the fragility of their lives, the delicate balance of happiness and heartache that hung precariously in the balance.
