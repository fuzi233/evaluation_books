The golden light of morning bathed Florence as Romola made her way once more to San Marco. The night before, Fra Salvestro had granted her request—a rare audience with Fra Girolamo in the chapter-house. The friar, ever cautious against scandal, permitted such meetings only under strict conditions, ensuring they bore no shadow of secrecy.

The streets hummed with tension. Every corner, every loggia, buzzed with heated debate. The fate of the conspirators hung in the balance, and Florence was divided. Pamphlets circulated—some demanding swift justice, others pleading for law and mercy. Romola’s fingers tightened around the papers she had snatched from Bratti’s stall.

*"Justice sells quicker,"* the peddler had grinned, *"so I charge extra for it."*

She hurried past San Giovanni, avoiding familiar faces, her mind fixed on one purpose.

---

The cloisters of San Marco were quiet, save for murmured exchanges between lay visitors and friars. Fra Salvestro stood chatting idly, his curiosity piqued by gossip. Romola ignored them, her gaze drawn instead to the frescoes lining the walls—those same sorrowful figures that had witnessed her grief for Dino. The memory flickered, but she smothered it. There was no room for the past now.

She unfolded the handbills, scanning the arguments. The government had new evidence—another plot, they claimed. Her stomach twisted. *Tito’s doing?* The suspicion burned, but she forced it aside. There was no time.

The door opened. Savonarola entered.

She knelt, as custom dictated, then rose to face him. The months since she had last seen him had carved deeper lines into his face. His eyes, once alight with fervor, now held something harder—something distant.

*"Father,"* she began, voice steady despite the tremor in her hands, *"you know why I’ve come."*

He inclined his head. *"Speak, my daughter."*

*"It is Bernardo del Nero’s life I plead for."* The words rushed out. *"He is no traitor. He is a man of honor, condemned without the appeal you fought to grant all Florentines. Will you stay silent now?"*

Savonarola’s expression did not soften. *"The State must act for its survival. These men conspired not just in the past—they plot still."*

*"Plot?"* Romola’s breath caught. She unfolded the second handbill, scanning the fresh accusations. *"New evidence—conveniently now?"* Her voice sharpened. *"Or is this a pretext? Lorenzo Tornabuoni, your ally, was spared. Yet my godfather, who has never feigned loyalty, is to die?"*

A flicker of irritation crossed his face. *"I do not desire his death."*

*"Then speak against it!"* she cried. *"You wield influence enough. Or do you fear to weaken your own cause?"*

The air between them thickened. Savonarola’s jaw tightened. *"The cause of God’s kingdom demands sacrifice. Even of good men, if their deaths preserve Florence from greater evil."*

*"Greater evil?"* Romola’s voice broke. *"What kingdom is this, built on blood and hypocrisy? You once preached mercy. Now you hide behind politics."*

His eyes flashed. *"You judge what you do not understand. The wicked are not always the greatest obstacles—sometimes it is the virtuous who stand in God’s way."*

*"Then you admit it!"* she shot back. *"You want him dead *because* he is good!"*

For a heartbeat, silence. Then Savonarola’s voice dropped, low and strained. *"Be grateful your soul has never known such choices. The path I walk is darker than you fathom."*

*"No,"* Romola whispered, stepping back. *"God’s kingdom is wider than your party. And if this is its price, then I want no part in it."*

Their gazes locked—hers blazing with defiance, his burning with certainty. No reconciliation lay here.

Without another word, she turned and left.

The door closed behind her, sealing the rift between them.
