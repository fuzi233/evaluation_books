Tars Tarkas and I had no time to speak of what had brought us to this grim valley. Around us lay the twisted corpses of our attackers, but the eerie cry from the cliffs above had summoned reinforcements—a tide of nightmarish creatures surging toward us from every direction.

“To the cliffs!” Tars Tarkas roared. “Our only chance is to find shelter—a ledge, a crevice, anything!”

We sprinted across the crimson meadow, my strides shortened to match his. Behind us, the horde closed in, their shrieks and snarls filling the air. Three hundred yards of open ground lay between us and the sheer rock face, and as we ran, I scanned the cliffs for any sign of refuge.

The barrier of stone rose like a fortress, unbroken save for the lone figure still wailing his unnatural call. No cracks, no handholds—just smooth, impassable rock. To the right, the cliffs vanished into a dense forest, its emerald canopy brushing against the stone. To the left, the wall stretched unyielding into the horizon, merging with distant peaks. The river burst from the cliff’s base, but its path offered no escape.

Then the sun crested the cliffs.

Light struck the stone, and in an instant, the dull surface blazed to life—veins of gold, clusters of rubies, pockets of diamond glittering like a scattered trove. But what seized my attention were the dark gaps high above, barely visible beneath the forest’s edge. Caves.

“There!” I pointed.

Tars Tarkas grimaced. The trees were our only path upward, but Martians were no climbers. Still, we had no choice.

We veered toward the nearest trunk, the creatures at our heels. The fastest among them—a monstrous plant-man—lunged, his barbed limbs stretching for my throat. I spun, blade flashing, but the creature twisted, its whip-like tail slamming me to the ground. Before I could rise, it was on me, its many mouths seeking my flesh. I seized two writhing tendrils, muscles straining as I fought to keep its fangs from my neck.

Then I saw them.

Behind the plant-man, a wave of white apes surged forward—fifteen-foot-tall horrors, their too-human faces twisted in fury. I knew them too well. They were death incarnate.

The horde crashed over me. Fangs pierced my skin; cold, sucking lips fastened on my veins. I writhed free, my sword carving a bloody path, but for every beast I felled, three more took its place.

Tars Tarkas dropped from the branches, his great frame landing beside me. Back to back, we fought—two warriors against an army. Step by step, we retreated until our backs pressed against the tree’s massive trunk.

Then Tars Tarkas spotted it—a narrow opening at the base.

“Go!” he commanded.

I refused. “Not without you.”

He snarled, but there was no time to argue. As the creatures swarmed, I covered his escape, my blade a whirlwind of steel. When he was through, I leapt after him, just as the plant-men dragged me down. Tars Tarkas’ hands locked around my ankles, hauling me inside as I drove my sword through the last foe’s heart.

Breathless and bleeding, we listened as the beasts howled outside. The tree’s hollow interior offered scant refuge, but as my eyes adjusted, I saw it—a shaft leading upward, rungs carved into the wood.

We climbed.

Five hundred feet later, we emerged onto a trembling branch, its end dipping toward a narrow ledge and a waiting cave. One by one, we crossed, Tars Tarkas anchoring me as I scrambled onto the stone. Then, with a leather strap, I pulled him to safety.

Below, the valley sprawled—a sea of scarlet and emerald, watched over by the golden cliffs. For a fleeting moment, I thought I saw the glint of a spire amidst the trees, but it was gone before I could be sure.

The cave beckoned.

Inside, the tunnel stretched into darkness, its walls smooth, its path straight. We advanced, hands clasped to avoid separation, until a door barred our way. A press of my palm against its surface found the hidden mechanism, and it swung open.

Light spilled from within.

We stepped inside—and the door slammed shut behind us, locking with a click.

From the shadows, laughter rang out, cold and mocking.
