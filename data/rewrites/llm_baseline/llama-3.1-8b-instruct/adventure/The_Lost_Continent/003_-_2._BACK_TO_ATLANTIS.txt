As I lay in the darkness, the words of Tatho still echoing within my mind, I couldn't shake the feeling that I had made a grave mistake in becoming so deeply entrenched in my duties as governor of Yucatan. The isolation had grown suffocating, and I began to feel the weight of neglecting the world beyond our shores. The whispers of the mariners who traversed the seas hinted at a world of intrigue and secrecy, with only the Empress's benevolence deciding which tidbits of information would be shared with the outside world. Yet, I had been consumed by my work, and the constraints of my office had become my sole focus.

But the discipline of the Priestly Clan left me no choice but to conform to its dictates. With my removal from office, all authority and records now rested with my successor, and though I might remain as a guest in the pyramid that had once been mine, it was clear that I should depart as soon as possible and report to Atlantis. Tatho, however, proved a loyal ally, appearing beside me before dawn to offer his assistance.

I requested a simple thing: a ship to take me home. Tatho seemed taken aback by my modesty. "You may certainly have one," he said, "but my vessels are in dire need of repair. If you take them, your journey will be slow and arduous. Why not commandeer your own navy, which lies idle in harbor? Those ships are yours, Deucalion – or, at the very least, they belong to the State."

I bristled at his suggestion, my words firm but measured. "Those ships are not mine, nor do they belong to the State. They are the property of private merchants or the government, and I have no claim to them." Tatho's eyes narrowed, his gaze piercing. "You are Yucatan, Deucalion – or, rather, you were, and have been for nigh on twenty years."

His words struck a chord, and I recognized the implication behind them. I responded coolly, "The ships are not mine to command. I am a servant of the colony, not a naval commander."
