Two hours later, I had descended completely from the Gorge and stood amidst that foreign country. Though I felt a fresh, troubling bewilderment, I rejoiced in the startling light and splendour of this sudden land. Before reaching its floor, I had stood at the Gorge's mouth and gazed out over the vast expanse. There, I counted twenty-seven great volcanoes, excluding two monstrous ranges of fire-hills burning to my right, nor did I count the hundred thousand smaller fires scattered about. Truly, it appeared a realm of fire and water. A small fire-hill stood within a sea mere miles from where I stood, with perhaps twenty others spreading behind it. I counted three small seas and one mighty ocean stretching endlessly into the red light of the hills, vanishing entirely from sight without a visible end.

Islands rose from these waters, crowned with volcanoes; in other parts, the fire-hills thrust straight upward from the sea. Over the nearest waters lay a plentitude of steam, suggesting the sea boiled at intervals in various places. Within the red atmosphere of the place, I heard a muttering thunder, low and constant, shaking the air now from one distance, now from another. I judged these to be the voices of the fire-hills, speaking with the fire that lived within them.

Conceive how utterly new this all was to me. In that country, there existed a constant Voice of the Energy of Life—the World-Noise of our Age returned with a keen, undeniable apparentness, perhaps even keener than it is now. Let me describe more closely what was ready to my gaze. First, a huge, blackened mountain attracted me to the left of the Gorge's mouth, rising upward into the night, perhaps fifteen or twenty miles high. From its side grew a mighty peaked volcano, estimated at five miles high, situated on the far side. Above it, a second peak soared nine or ten great miles higher into the blackness. Two other mighty fire-hills burned and glowed on the left crest of that black mountain, upward at such a monstrous angle that they seemed to create strange, smouldering suns within the night. Vast mountains of ash and burned stuff rose from the earth below these peaks, cast forth by the perched volcanoes and pouring downward through Eternity to build grey, sombre monuments to the dreadful glory of Time. To my right lay endless sea and the red blazing of the hills; to my left, mighty forests, with monstrous fire-hills rising beyond the great woods. This was the first impression upon my brain and senses.

After descending from the Gorge, I paused, unsure which way led to my search. Looking about for a long while, I eventually climbed back up and cursed my folly for not mapping my route before coming down. Upon returning to the Gorge, I saw there was but one way: the seas lay to my right, but to the left, where shores met the seas, a clear path seemed to exist for a space. Perhaps, after traveling so far, I would find a further way forward. Thus, I descended again into the Country of the Seas, the red-shining land of water and fire.

By the time I reached the floor, twenty-four hours had passed since my last sleep. I needed a nook to slumber. I found a neat place where three great trees grew around a little basin of rock, dry and warm. After eating three tablets and drinking some water—my belly yearning as ever—I made my bed in the rocky basin and lay down, thinking briefly of Naani before falling asleep.

Suddenly, I woke to find myself swimming in hot water. A mercy, I thought, that I was not drowned. I rose to my feet; the basin was full of hot, steaming, pungent water. It poured in from a smoothed slit on the far side, gurgling and bubbling, driven upward by a deep, boiling well. Glad that it did not boil as it emerged, I noted the cycle: the water poured forth at seasons for an eternity, then receded through fissures in the basin bottom, repeating slightly beyond the length of every hour. Indeed, the basin emptied slowly as I watched.

Much wetted, I stripped off my armour, dipping out my effects first, and came down to naked flesh. I found a spot where the rock was hot and spread my garments. While they dried, I entered the hot pool for a pleasant bath, feeling no great fear of danger, as I believed I had left all such threats within the Night Lands. Yet, I held the Diskos upon the pool's edge, lacking proper assurance. As it proved, many monstrous beasts inhabited that country, but I never felt the nearness or horrid power of any Evil Force. These, I conceived, were congregated about the Mighty Pyramid, attracted by the great spiritual essence of so wondrous a multitude of humans gathered in one spot, much like sharks following a ship laden with cattle. How the Evil Powers gained entrance to this State of Life, I knew not certainly, having offered certain thoughts on the matter earlier, though I realized such reasoning was likely vanity.

Presently, my clothing was dry. Having emerged from the bath, which nearly flowed back into the earth, I dressed, donned my armour, and found my mind more lightsome, yet ready for another sleep. I did so, spending six more hours beside the pool, waking occasionally from the gurgle and bubble of the filling water. When the six hours ended, I awoke fully fitted in senses and feeling to proceed again through the red-lighted country, doing so after eating and drinking.

All that day I traveled at a great pace, with nameless woods to my left and sea-shores to my right. Often, trees grew into the water, forcing me to walk among them. A wonderful mystery lived there, unknown to me in all my life prior to entering this country—a glad, wild mystery absent from the solemn beauty of the Underground Fields groves. The scent of the woods was sweet to my spirit. Throughout my journey, the shore remained to my right, while great forests surrounded me to the left. Life thrived in those darksome woods; living eyes peered out at me occasionally before retreating into the dark. I did not know whether to fear them or ignore them, yet naught came near to harm me.

Three times that day I encountered little fire-hills burning red, sending out fire and noise that I heard through the forest before reaching them. Around each was a deadness and desolation where the fire had killed the big trees, yet the quick life of little plants grew more nigh, born and living between the fire-bursts. That same day, I passed thirty-seven boiling springs. Whether they truly boiled, I knew not, only that they sent out great steam often and made strong roaring noises, sounding like wild beasts roaring strangely from afar.

At the eighteenth hour, I sat down as I had at the sixth and twelfth, eating two tablets and drinking water that fizzed richly and quickly. Afterwards, I lay down to slumber, greatly wearied. I chose a place against a great rock, ensuring no creature could approach from behind. I fell easily into sleep, fixing it upon me that I slumber only with the body, for I knew, by the shining eyes in the darksome woods, that strange creatures abode in the mighty forests. Ere sleep overtook me, I thought of Naani, whose spirit seemed to hover near, striving pitifully to speak with me. I placed a blessing upon her and determined to hasten my journey, making desperate speed if possible, to reach the strange, unknown place in the dead world where the lesser Refuge stood. Then, I was asleep in a moment.

Lo! Suddenly, I awoke. Lovely was the brightness of that country, revealing my danger instantly, suspending me in no fearful doubt as the grey darkness and strange shadows of the Night Land did. On the instant I raised myself to my elbow, I saw certain things crouching within the borders of the trees, no more than a score paces off. I perceived in a moment that my spirit had given me knowledge and woken me. Staring while I gripped the Diskos, I saw six squat men, humpt at the neck and shoulder, crouching in a row, partially hidden by shadows. They watched me, their eyes shining like beast eyes. A strange terror came upon me. Yet, I had the Diskos and my armour. Though my heart shook a little, my spirit was assured to conquer.

I rose to my feet, the Diskos ready. Behold! I could not see the Humped Men; they had vanished from that place. Yet, I never saw them go, keeping my gaze steadfast upon where they hid. As you understand, I was near ready to believe there had been nothing within the border of the wood, yet truly I knew the men with the humps had been there.

Looking presently, I found I had slept five hours. I ate two tablets while standing there, watchful, then drank some water, ready to go forward again. I was grown very anxious to leave that place, having no knowledge but that the strangely humped men were but a little way off among the trees, possibly calling an army of others to my destruction.

Ready and geared, I set off at a great stride, holding the Diskos handy and looking this way and that, moving onward with speed. Truly, I had grown so lean and hard that it seemed I possessed the power to outpace those men or any of their kind. All that day, through thirty great hours, I went forward at that stride, watching constantly. At every sixth hour, I ate two tablets and drank a little water, continuing onward.
