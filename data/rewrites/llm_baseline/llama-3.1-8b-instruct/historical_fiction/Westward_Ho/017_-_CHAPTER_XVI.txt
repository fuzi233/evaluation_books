The warm glow of sunset cast its golden light upon the tranquil waters of the Thames, illuminating the majestic vessel that stood proudly at anchor in Deptford Creek. The Pelican, a ship of storied renown, seemed to slumber, her hull weathered to a soft patina, her sails furled against the gentle breeze. Nailed to her mainmast, the verses of poets and scholars told the tale of her glorious voyages and the fearless captain who had commanded her around the world.

As I strolled along the riverbank, I chanced upon a lively gathering of gentlemen, their faces aglow with merriment and conversation. They had come from the city, their fine clothes and ornate badges proclaiming their status and station. The Pelican Inn, a nearby hostelry, seemed to be the epicenter of their revelry, its windows aglow with the warm light of candles and lanterns, its staff hurrying to and fro, bearing trays laden with steaming delicacies.

A grand feast was in progress, and the lord mayor, Sir Edward Osborne, sat at the head of the table, surrounded by an assembly of distinguished guests. Among them, the Earl of Cumberland and Walter Raleigh engaged in hushed conversation, their words punctuated by the occasional quote from the learned Doctor Dee. Raleigh, ever the enthusiast, leaned heavily on the scholar, Richard Hakluyt, who sat quietly to his left, his eyes fixed intently on the reverend elder, Anthony Jenkinson, as the great traveller regaled the assembly with tales of his journeys to the distant steppes of Siberia and the icy expanse of the Arctic Sea.

Nearby, Christopher Carlile, a seasoned captain and veteran of Drake's West Indian campaign, held court with a small group of listeners, his words dripping with the authority of one who had faced danger and come out unscathed. As I watched, a young scholar, likely a student from Oxford, approached the table, his eyes shining with curiosity, eager to learn from the giants of exploration gathered before him. The air was alive with the thrill of discovery, the promise of new lands and untold riches hanging precariously in the balance, as the assembled company debated the secrets of the New World and the mysteries that lay beyond.
