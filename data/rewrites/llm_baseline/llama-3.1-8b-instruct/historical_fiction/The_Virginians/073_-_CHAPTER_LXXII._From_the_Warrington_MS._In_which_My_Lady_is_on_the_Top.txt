As I sit by the fire, gazing across at the chair where my beloved wife has spent countless hours by my side, I often ponder the choices we made when we married, nearly half a lifetime ago. My son Miles, now an accomplished fox hunter, looks back on his daring leaps with a mix of awe and trepidation, wondering how he managed to navigate such perilous terrain. Yet, despite his reckless enthusiasm for the hunt, he remains one of the most ardent sportsmen in our two counties. His mother, too, has known her share of worry, for Miles has broken his collarbone and suffered numerous tumbles, but she has always seen his passion as a source of joy, just as I have cherished our marriage as the greatest blessing of my life.

As I pose a question to my wise and witty companion, I know exactly how she will respond. Her intuition is uncanny, and I've grown accustomed to wearing a particular expression, which seems to prompt her to mirror my thoughts. If I ask, "Do you think the moon resembles cream cheese tonight?" she'll smile and say, "Indeed, it does look remarkably like it – you have a knack for clever similes." When I share my opinions on politics or literature, she offers her astute insights, often drawing from her vast knowledge of history and culture. Our daughters, too, have learned to anticipate her responses, and they frequently tease each other about mimicking their aunt's mannerisms.

Miles, on the other hand, is blissfully unaware of the world beyond the sporting news in the Norwich paper. As long as the scent of the hunt is strong, he cares little for the state of the nation. I've tried to share my love of poetry and music with him, but he remains uninterested, unable to grasp even the simplest Latin verse. My notebook, filled with sketches and musings, lies open on the table beside me, yet Miles rarely bothers to glance at it. Occasionally, however, he'll leave his mark, as when he drew a crude picture of me tumbling over my mare Sultana's head, captioned "The Squire on Horseback, or Fish out of Water." The girls erupted into giggles, and even my wife couldn't help but chuckle, but I took it in stride, knowing that a good jest is a gift from the heart. Despite his lack of wit, I adore Miles for his kind spirit and infectious enthusiasm, and I'm grateful for the love we share as a family
