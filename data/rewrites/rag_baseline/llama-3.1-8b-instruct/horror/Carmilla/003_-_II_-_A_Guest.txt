The sun's descent into the forest cast a warm, golden glow across the landscape, illuminating the wispy tendrils of the lime trees that swayed gently in the evening breeze. My father and I strolled along the winding path, the soft crunch of gravel beneath our feet the only sound breaking the stillness of the summer air. As we walked, he paused, his eyes fixed on some point beyond the trees, and I sensed a hesitation in his step. "General Spielsdorf won't be visiting us as soon as I'd hoped," he said finally, his voice low and somber.

I felt a pang of disappointment, for the General's visit had been the highlight of my summer, and I had looked forward to meeting his niece, Mademoiselle Rheinfeldt. We had expected her arrival the following day, and I had envisioned the days that would follow, filled with laughter and conversation, as we explored the forest together. But now, my father's words hung in the air like a shadow, casting a pall over my excitement.

"When will he come?" I asked, trying to keep my tone neutral, though my heart was racing with curiosity.

"Not until autumn," he replied, his eyes still fixed on some distant point. "Two months, I dare say."

I felt a surge of frustration, and my mind began to whirl with questions. Why had the General delayed his visit? And why had he spoken of Mademoiselle Rheinfeldt in such glowing terms, only to reveal now that she was dead? I turned to my father, my voice barely above a whisper. "Why did you tell me she was dead?"

He hesitated, as if searching for the right words, before handing me a letter, its edges worn and creased. "This is the General's letter. I'm afraid he's in great affliction; the letter seems to have been written in a state of distraction."

As I took the letter, I felt a shiver run down my spine. The words danced across the page, a jumbled mix of grief and anger, of regret and recrimination. I read it twice, once silently, and again aloud to my father, trying to make sense of the General's words. They spoke of a daughter lost, of a tragedy that had struck without warning, and of a monster that had brought about her downfall. The General's language was wild and erratic, but one thing was clear: he was consumed by a desire for vengeance.
