As the sweltering summer sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the tranquil streets of Helstone, Margaret Hale emerged from her bedroom, her dark hair still disheveled from a long day of exploring the countryside. Edith, resplendent in her finery, stood by the dresser, carefully arranging a delicate garniture of pomegranate blossoms amidst the intricate folds of Margaret's gown. The flowers, a vibrant scarlet hue that seemed to dance in the fading light, clashed jarringly with the dead-straw color of the dress itself.

"I'm afraid these blue flowers would have been a far more suitable choice, Dixon," Edith said, her voice laced with a hint of petulance. "But I suppose it's too late now."

Dixon, still perturbed by Edith's disregard for her expertise, shot back, "It's not a dead-straw color, ma'am – it's a straw color. And blue always pairs well with straw." But Edith had already begun to arrange the flowers, her hands moving with a deft precision that belied her words.

As she stepped back to admire her handiwork, her gaze landed on Margaret, who stood in the doorway, her cheeks flushed with the heat. "Oh, Margaret! Here you are," Edith exclaimed, her tone a mixture of relief and annoyance. "I've been looking everywhere for you. But what's this? Your cheeks are as white as alabaster. You look like you've seen a ghost."

Margaret's voice was barely above a whisper as she replied, "I'm fine, Edith. I just need a moment to collect myself." Her eyes drifted towards the window, where the sun's final rays were casting a golden glow over the landscape.

Edith's expression softened, and she reached out to gently adjust the flowers in Margaret's hair. "There, darling, you look stunning. But we can't have you missing dinner, can we? Henry's made a bit of a mess, it seems. He's invited that Mr. Thornton from Milton to join us, and it will be simply delightful to have him meet Mr. Colthurst. Just think of the conversation – Mr. Colthurst will be able to question Mr. Thornton about his experiences in Milton, and it will make for a most fascinating evening."
