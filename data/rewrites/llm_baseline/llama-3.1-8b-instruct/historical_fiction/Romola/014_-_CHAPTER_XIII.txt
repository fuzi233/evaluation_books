As the languid afternoon sun cast its warm glow on the cobblestone streets of Florence, the seventh of September unfolded like a tapestry rich with promise. More than two months had passed since Romola and Tito had shared their love with each other, and the city seemed to have slowed its pace in anticipation of the coming harvest season.

Tito, with the gentle tread of a summer breeze, entered Nello's shop, where the barber lay sprawled on his bench, his cap pulled low over his eyes. One leg was tucked beneath him, and the other had slipped toward the ground, as if it had carried with it a manuscript of verse, now lying crushed amidst the scattered pages. In a corner, Sandro sat engrossed in a game of _mora_, his left hand moving in deliberate, arithmetic strokes, his eyes fixed intently on the intricate dance of numbers.

Tito's fingers danced across the strings of his lute, plucking a soft melody that seemed to match the rhythm of the city's slumbering heartbeat. His voice rose in a gentle, lilting song:

"Quanto è bella la gioventù,
Che si fugge tuttavia!
Chi vuol esser lieto sia,
Di doman non c'è certezza."

The words of Lorenzo de' Medici's Carnival Song floated through the air, a poignant reminder that life's beauty is fleeting, and joy is never guaranteed.

I see I've overslept, despite the potent potion of poetry that was meant to guide me into the realm of dreams.
