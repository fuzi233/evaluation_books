The door to Mr. Tartar's chambers swung open with a soft creak, revealing a space so meticulously ordered it seemed almost otherworldly. Every surface was polished to a high sheen, as if the very light itself had been distilled into these walls. The floors gleamed like freshly fallen snow, and not a single speck of dust dared to sully the gleaming expanse. In this sanctuary of tidiness, every item had its assigned place, from the delicate maps and charts to the rows of books that lined the shelves like sentinels guarding ancient secrets. Even the brushes, neatly stored in their designated compartments, seemed to whisper tales of Mr. Tartar's voyages to far-flung lands.

As one's gaze wandered through this domain, the eye was drawn to the carefully arranged service of plate on the sideboard – a testament to Mr. Tartar's fastidious nature. A single misplaced spoon would have been instantly apparent, a tiny infraction in this otherwise flawless landscape. Similarly, the toilet implements on the dressing table were arrayed with military precision, each one a miniature marvel of organization. Even the curiosities collected from Mr. Tartar's travels were lovingly displayed, each one a tiny window into the wonders he had encountered.

In the garden, a delicate awning stretched overhead, its folds precisely trimmed and tied, evoking the memory of a ship's sail billowing in the breeze. The flowers, too, seemed to thrive in this atmosphere of order, their petals unfolding like tiny flags bearing witness to the beauty of Mr. Tartar's handiwork. As he showed Rosa around, his eyes twinkling with mirth, it was clear that he reveled in the simple pleasures of his domain. He was a man who delighted in the intricate dance of creation and maintenance, where every element worked in harmony to create a symphony of beauty.

"Helena! Helena Landless! Are you there?" Mr. Tartar called out, his voice carrying across the tranquil space.

A second face appeared in the doorway, and Rosa's eyes widened in surprise. "Who speaks to me? Not Rosa?"

"Yes, my darling!" Mr. Tartar replied, his smile warm and inviting. "How did you come here, dearest?"

"I— I don't quite know," Helena stammered, her brow furrowed in confusion.
