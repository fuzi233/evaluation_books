It would have been a grotesque irony if the men who stood upon the riverbank, armed with such barren data, could have deciphered the handwriting etched into the mud beneath their feet. For upon that specific spot, an event had just transpired—a singular collision of fate so profound it stands without parallel in the annals of human history, destined perhaps to remain an unassimilated mystery until the final dissolution of time. Yet, the truth of this moment shall be recounted in stark brevity, woven from the threads of my own observation and the testimony of a participant in the tragedy.

Gerard, having penned his final, trembling adieu to Pietro and Andrea, descended to the river under the cloak of nightfall. His movements were dictated by a dogged, grim resolution, a psychological architecture common only to those who have resolved upon self-annihilation. With methodical precision, he stuffed his pockets with every ounce of silver and copper he possessed, weighting his soul with metal to ensure the certainty of his grave. Thus prepared, he hastened to a secluded bend of the stream, a place he had previously scouted for its isolation. While many, particularly the timid or the fearful, scan their surroundings to confirm the presence of a witness, this resolute wretch scanned the darkness solely to verify the absence of one.

To his mounting agitation, however, he detected a solitary silhouette leaning against the shadowed corner of an alleyway. Instinctively, he feigned a careless departure, strolling away with deliberate nonchalance, only to halt and return to the precise point of convergence. Indeed, the same figure emerged from a side street, drifting like a specter into the periphery. *Does he watch me?* Gerard wondered, a spike of paranoia piercing his despair. *Does he know the purpose of my pilgrimage?* He dismissed the notion as impossible, yet his heart hammered against his ribs. He moved briskly down the lane, navigated two streets, executed a wide detour, and circled back. The figure had vanished. But when Gerard swept his gaze across the moonlit expanse, certain once more, there he was—just a few yards behind, ostensibly adjusting the lacing of his boot.

Gerard realized immediately that he was being hunted. In the pale luminescence of the moon, he discerned a steel gauntlet resting in the sentinel's hand. The realization crystallized with icy clarity: this was an assassin. Strangely, the concept never crossed his mind that the life targeted was his own. He was unaware, blinded by circumstance, that he harbored an enemy in the world. Instead of flight, he turned and approached the bravo with a desperate, eager desperation.

"My good friend," Gerard cried out, his voice cracking with a plea born of madness, "sell me thine arm! A single stroke! Behold, here is all I possess;" and he thrust his handful of coin into the assassin's grasp. "Oh, pray thee! Pray thee! Perform one act of mercy, and liberate me from this hateful existence!" As he spoke, he undid his doublet, exposing his bare bosom to the cool air and the blade.

The man stared, unmoved by the offering or the vulnerability displayed. "Why do you hesitate?" Gerard shrieked, his composure shattering into fragments. "Do you lack compassion? Is the effort of lifting your arm too great? Is it because I am poor, unable to offer further gold? Wretched fool, you can only strike a man from behind; you dare not meet his gaze. Very well, then! Turn your head, shut your eyes, and hold your tongue!"

With a snarl of utter contempt, Gerard spun away from the stoic killer and hurled himself into the churning waters. "Margaret!"

At the heavy splash of his body breaking the surface tension, the bravo seemed to rouse from a trance-like stupor. He sprinted to the bank, driven by a strange, inexplicable compulsion, and plunged into the freezing current after the self-destroyer. What ensued next shall be chronicled by the assassin himself.
