In the depths of a household where emotions were often suppressed, a different kind of turmoil brewed, hidden from prying eyes by the very walls that sheltered it. It was a struggle that defied understanding, one that left behind only faint whispers of its existence, like the fragments of a torn letter that betrayed its author's handwriting.

Those scraps of paper, discovered long after Felix Cadwalader's mysterious demise, were tucked away beneath the floorboards of Bartow's room, their origins and how they came to be there a perpetual enigma. Yet, they hinted at a tragedy that lay beyond the comprehension of mortal men.

"Madness!" the writer's hand scrawled across the page, as if in desperation. "I've seen Eva Poindexter once more, and my very soul is torn asunder by the conflicting desires that rage within me. Eva, oh Eva, the girl I thought of as nothing more than a prize to be won. The girl I've given to my brother. She's too radiant for him; she's too lovely for any man who's ever known the touch of a gentle breeze or the warmth of a loving glance. Is it love I feel? Can I, Felix, with a heart forged of stone, harbor a secret longing for the tender heart of another? Never! I'm at the mercy of my own demons or the cruel whims of fate. Yet, when I think of her, my world brightens, and the heavens themselves seem to smile upon me. Those half-raised eyes, those lips that part with such innocence, that gentle demeanor, and the unyielding strength that commands respect and awakens love – all these haunt me still.

I must flee. I must never lay eyes on her again. Thomas's resolve remains unwavering, but mine falters like a ship battered by a storm. If Thomas had not spent his formative years in the city of light, perhaps he too would have fallen prey to the same weakness. But I am the only one beset by such frailty. Curses upon my—

"No, I won't flee. Not yet. One more glimpse of her face, just to convince myself that my feelings are nothing more than a fleeting fancy. Perhaps I was taken aback by her beauty, mistaking it for something ordinary. If my passion stems from my own weakness rather than her exceptional nature, then I should acknowledge my foolishness before it becomes my undoing.
