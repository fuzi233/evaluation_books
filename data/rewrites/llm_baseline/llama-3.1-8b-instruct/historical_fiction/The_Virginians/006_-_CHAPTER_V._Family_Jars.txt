In the warm, golden light of the afternoon, Madam Warrington sat in her parlor, listening intently as her nephew Harry shared tales of his life back in England. Her eyes sparkled with amusement and curiosity, a testament to her sharp wit and vast knowledge of the world. As she listened, she formed her own opinion about the people and events Harry described, and though it may not have been entirely favorable, what could one expect from a society where humans were inherently imperfect? The refined, city-bred lady winced at the thought of her sister's rustic existence, a life so far removed from the sophistication of the city. Yet, little Madam Warrington was content with her lot, finding joy in the simple pleasures of managing her estate, overseeing the household staff, and tending to the needs of her community.

Her days were filled with a multitude of tasks, from inspecting the kitchen gardens to supervising the maids as they spun wool by the fire. She rode her pony through the fields, checking on the progress of the tobacco and corn crops, and ensuring that the overseers and slave hands worked efficiently. If a slave fell ill, she would venture out in any weather to nurse them back to health, armed with her trusty book of remedies and her well-stocked medicine chest. The neighboring families whispered about her reputation as a skilled healer, and trembled at the thought of being on the receiving end of her potent decoctions and medicinal concoctions.

In the past, when Virginia was still a young colony, there were few towns and many small, rural settlements where the gentry resided. Rachel Esmond, the mistress of Castlewood, ruled over her domain like a benevolent queen, surrounded by her vassals and the men who worked her lands. Many of these gentlemen were proud, idle, and fond of hunting and other gentlemanly pursuits, living in grand style but often in a state of financial disarray. Their households were large, with numerous servants whose livery was often tattered, and their doors were always open to travelers. Madam Warrington was a shrewd businesswoman, managing her estate with care and thrift, earning the admiration of her peers.
