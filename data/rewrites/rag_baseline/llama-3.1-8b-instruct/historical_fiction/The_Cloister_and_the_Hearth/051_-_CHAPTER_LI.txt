As I walked alongside Margaret on that peculiar day, I couldn't help but sense the bittersweet undertones that danced in the air. A true friend had finally arrived, yet the news that accompanied her was as bitter as the autumnal winds that rustled the leaves outside. It was then that Margaret learned Gerard was truly alone in that distant land, a fact that had been foregone for some time. Denys, ever the balm, endeavored to lift her spirits with his effervescent personality and clever jests, earning her affectionate petting and gentle reprimands for his occasional use of coarse language. As he sat at her table, she lovingly scolded him, "Good lack, Denys, to hear such unbecoming words come forth from your refined lips: forbear, or I shall be cross with you: be civil." And so, Denys struggled to reconcile his innate politeness with his rougher tendencies, a battle that left him in a state of comedic turmoil.

Just as Margaret's life was becoming increasingly intertwined with Denys', she received a heartfelt letter from Margaret Van Eyck, a woman she had once considered a benefactress. In the letter, Margaret Van Eyck expressed her regret for losing favor and begged for Margaret's discretion regarding her whereabouts. She confessed to struggling to make ends meet and implored Margaret not to reveal her location to Gerard's people, whom she despised for driving him away and failing to show her compassion afterward. The weight of these words hung heavy in the air as Margaret's thoughts swirled with a mix of emotions.

Margaret Van Eyck's words reached the ears of Reicht, who, in turn, shared them with Catherine. The news sent shockwaves through their community, leaving Catherine pondering the depth of Margaret's resentment. "Ay, and is she turned that bitter against us?" Catherine wondered aloud. "She stole our son from us, and now she hates us for not running into her arms. Natheless, it is a blessing she is alive and no farther away than Rotterdam."
