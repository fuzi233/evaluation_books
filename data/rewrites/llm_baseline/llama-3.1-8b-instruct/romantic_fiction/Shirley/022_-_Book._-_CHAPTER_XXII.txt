As the sun rose over the horizon, casting a warm glow over the rolling hills, Thomas Moore rode out once again, his horse's hooves pounding the dusty road in pursuit of the riot's ringleaders. Though he'd defended his mill against the mob, Moore knew the true challenge lay in tracking down those who'd incited the violence – the cunning strangers from the cities, men driven by desperation and fueled by false promises. He let the common folk, misled by their circumstances, pass without notice, recognizing that they were but pawns in a larger game.

Moore's quarry, however, was a different story altogether. These men, with nothing to lose and everything to gain, were a different breed altogether. They were the ones he hunted with relish, his keen mind and sharp instincts guiding him through the winding roads and sleepy villages. His horse, sensing its rider's intensity, responded with a fierce energy, as if it too reveled in the thrill of the chase.

As Moore rode, the scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, reminding him of the warmth and comfort of home. He almost forgot the stench of the dye-houses, where he spent most of his days, as he breathed in the clean country air. His magistrates, timid and slow, quaked in their boots at the mere mention of his name, knowing that Moore was a force to be reckoned with. He delighted in leaving them in a state of trepidation, their fear of assassination a palpable thing that stayed their hands.

Moore knew the risks involved in his line of work, but he didn't let fear get the better of him. His was a temperament that thrived on challenge, and the prospect of danger only served to sharpen his focus. As he rode through the moonlit moors, the wind whipping his hair into a frenzy, he felt alive, his senses heightened, and his spirit soaring. He knew he was doing what was right, and that knowledge gave him a sense of purpose that nothing else could match.

Four of the ringleaders remained at large, and Moore was determined to bring them to justice. Two had already fallen near Stilbro', and the others were said to be hiding near Birmingham. He wouldn't rest until they were brought to bay. Meanwhile, his mill was being repaired, the carpenters and glaziers working tirelessly to restore it to its former glory.
