Below the stairs, the street stretched out under a pale, indifferent moon, whipped by a wind that felt sickening and unwelcoming. Yet, within my small retreat, the air remained still. Just as I was settling into exhaustion, my eyes snapped open. Perched atop the highest step was the creature I had glimpsed trailing the Shadow—a being of shimmering, living light.

The instant our gazes met, she recoiled. With a fluid, unnatural grace, she reversed her stance, tail leading, and scrambled backward until she vanished down the flight of stairs. I rushed after her, scanning the dark thoroughfare, but she was gone. Defeated, I returned to my hard cot. Two predators now haunted this city: one marked with spots, the other pure white. Though I held little value in Bulika, the life of a child outweighed my own precarious existence. I resolved to guard the door until dawn.

Soon, the latch clicked—a slow, deliberate movement. Rising silently, I slipped inside. Standing there was not the woman I had aided, but the veiled figure from the desert. She said nothing, guiding me into an empty, stone-floored chamber and gesturing to a rug. Wrapped in its coarse weave, I lay down as she sealed the inner door. Outside, the outer door swung shut, leaving me alone in the silver gloom.

Sleep eluded me. Soon, a stifled sobbing drifted through the walls, growing louder until it erupted into the cry of a child, immediately followed by a horrific shriek. I lunged into the corridor. From a side room emerged the white leopardess, clutching a newborn infant in her jaws, cradling it as if it were her own cub.

I tackled her, forcing her to release the baby. The infant hit the stone floor with a pitiful wail. The veiled woman materialized, stepping over our tangled forms in the narrow passageway. She scooped up the child and vanished into the darkness. Moments later, she returned, lifting me from the beast's grasp and shoving me out the door. The leopardess trailed at my heels.

"She has abandoned me!" I thought bitterly. "Left to settle accounts with this monster at her leisure! But we will fight for it!"

I sprinted down the stairs, terrified she would leap onto my back, yet she moved with quiet obedience. At the bottom, I tried to seize her, but she vaulted effortlessly over my head. Turning, she crouched at my feet. I reached down, stroking her pristine white fur; she licked my bare feet with a rough, dry tongue. I patted her, a surge of tenderness flooding my chest. Even if her affection was a mask, how else could I distinguish true love from falsehood in any world?

We stood together when a heavy *squelch* echoed from the center of the street. Rushing over, I found a pulpy, broken mass—once a woman, now merely a ruined shell, likely hurled from an upper window. Looking up, I saw the Shadow pacing the opposite side of the road, the white leopardess trotting faithfully at his side.

I closed the distance, pleading internally for the beast to understand she was not acting freely. But as I approached, she spun and launched herself at me, snarling with terrifying ferocity. Instinct pulled me back. Instantly, she froze, resuming her position behind the Shadow. I advanced again; she attacked again, her eyes blazing like captured emeralds. On my third attempt, she snapped and bit me. Pain shot through me, and I cried out. In that moment, her gaze shifted, conveying a silent question: *Why force me?*

Angry at my own foolishness, I turned away. I had wasted hours here. Night or day, I would march straight to the palace. High above the city's beating heart, surrounded by layers of defense, it loomed like a fortress rather than a residence. Its walls were crumbling, neglected for centuries, rendering them useless against time itself. Great gates stood open across a rocky chasm, though the water that once filled the moat was long dried. Silence hung so thick it seemed to seep into the stones, making even the moonlight feel uncomfortably awake.

To enter as a thief or to break the oppressive silence—I chose neither. Instead, I wandered the perimeter like an outcast dog until I found a small alcove with a stone bench. Seeking shelter from the biting wind, I collapsed, and despite the chill, sleep claimed me instantly.

Something leaped onto me, a rough tongue lapping at my face. "The white leopardess," I realized. "She comes to drink my blood—and surely I cannot afford to resist!" I lay still, bracing for agony. Instead, a gentle warmth spread through me. Stretched along my back, she pressed close, her body heat seeping into mine. Her breath, devoid of any wild scent, enveloped my face in a soothing haze. Conviction washed over me: her intent was kind. I rolled slightly, draped an arm over her, and surrendered to deep unconsciousness.

When I awoke, I imagined lying in a soft, familiar bed. Was I finally home? The scents of a beloved garden flooded my senses. Rubbing my eyes, I looked out. I was not in a bed, but on bare stone in the heart of a hostile city.

Had I truly slept with a leopardess, or was it a dream? She had just departed, for the lingering warmth of her body still clung to me. Leaving the alcove, I carried a hope both fragile and fierce. One truth remained absolute: I must find the princess. If I possessed any power over her, it stemmed from the debt she owed me—the life I had saved, paid for with my own vitality. That realization gave me the courage to face whatever awaited me.
