My resolve hardened instantly. I would not remain silent; I would march forth to evangelize the entirety of Flatland. The Gospel of the Third Dimension must be preached to all, even to the rigid Women and the disciplined Soldiers. My campaign would commence with my own wife. Yet, before I could formulate the opening lines of my sermon, a clamor erupted from the street—a chorus of voices demanding absolute silence. A single, authoritative voice rose above the rest, the tone unmistakably belonging to a Herald.

Listening intently, I deciphered the words of the Council's latest Resolution: *Any individual who perverts the public mind through delusion, or claims to have received revelations from another world, shall be arrested, imprisoned, or executed.*

The danger was palpable. To proceed openly was suicide. Prudence dictated that I suppress all mention of my Revelation entirely. Instead, I would rely solely on the path of pure Demonstration. After all, the logic seemed so self-evident—so akin to arithmetic—that discarding the mystical element of revelation would lose us nothing. *"Upward, not Northward"* was the key to the entire proof. It had struck me with crystal clarity moments before sleep claimed me, and upon waking, it appeared as undeniable as $1+1=2$. Yet, now, under the shadow of the Council's decree, its obviousness felt strangely elusive.

Just then, my wife entered the room. We exchanged a few pleasantries, the mundane chatter of spouses, but I made a conscious decision not to initiate our discussion there. My pentagonal sons were men of high standing and respected physicians, yet they lacked mathematical depth; they were ill-equipped to grasp my thesis. However, a thought occurred to me: a young, pliable hexagon with a natural aptitude for numbers would be the ideal candidate. Why not test my theory on my grandson? His earlier, offhand remark regarding the meaning of $3^3$ had already earned the Sphere's nod of approval.

Engaging a child in this discourse offered me a layer of protection. As a minor, he knew nothing of the Council's Proclamation. Conversely, I could not guarantee that my sons, driven by fierce patriotism and an almost religious reverence for the Circles, might not view my serious advocacy of the "Third Dimension" as sedition worthy of immediate delivery to the Prefect.

Before attempting to instruct the boy, however, I had to address my wife's inevitable curiosity. She naturally demanded to know why the Circle had summoned me for such a mysterious audience and how I had managed to enter the house undetected. I provided her with an elaborate explanation—one I feared was less consistent with the truth than my readers in Spaceland might wish—but I successfully persuaded her to retreat quietly to her domestic duties, ensuring she extracted no further questions regarding the World of Three Dimensions.

With the coast clear, I summoned my grandson. To confess the truth, I felt the vivid images of my vision beginning to slip away, dissolving like the faint outline of a half-remembered dream. I longed to solidify these concepts and create my first disciple.

When the boy entered, I swiftly barred the door. Sitting beside him with our mathematical tablets—or, as you would term them, Lines—I announced that we would resume yesterday's lesson. I reviewed the fundamentals: how a point moving along a single dimension generates a line, and how a straight line traversing two dimensions creates a square.

Then, feigning a mischievous grin, I turned to him. "Now, you impertinent scamp," I said, "you attempted to convince me that a square could, through motion 'Upward, not Northward,' generate a new figure—an extra square existing within a third dimension. Repeat that, you young rascal."

At that precise moment, the Herald's cry echoed again from the street: *"O yes! O yes!"* announcing the new law. Despite his youth, my grandson possessed an intelligence far exceeding his years, having been raised in total reverence for the Circle's authority. He grasped the peril instantly. He held his breath until the final syllables of the proclamation faded into silence.

Then, bursting into tears, he cried out, "Oh, dear Grandpapa! That was merely a joke! I meant absolutely nothing by it! We didn't know about the new Law back then, and I'm certain I never mentioned the Third Dimension. And I am positive I didn't speak of 'Upward, not Northward'; that sounds like utter nonsense! How can something move upward without moving northward? Even a baby wouldn't be so absurd! How ridiculous! Ha! ha! ha!"

"Not at all ridiculous!" I snapped, my patience fraying. "Here, for instance, take this square." Seizing a movable model lying nearby, I demonstrated. "I move it, you see—not Northward, but... yes, I move it Upward—that is to say, not Northward, but somewhere else—not quite like this, but somehow..."

I trailed off, shaking the square aimlessly, unable to articulate the spatial shift required to break the plane. The gesture was futile, bordering on inane. My grandson dissolved into laughter, louder than before, declaring that I wasn't teaching him mathematics but merely playing a prank. With a triumphant unlock of the door, he fled the room.

Thus concluded my first, and ultimately disastrous, attempt to convert a pupil to the Gospel of Three Dimensions.
