As the harvest sun cast its golden glow over the rolling hills, Grandcourt's resolve only strengthened. In the fortnight since making up his mind to propose to Miss Harleth, he had spared no effort to make his intentions clear. Every day, it seemed, brought a new opportunity to show her his ardor. His cousin, Mrs. Torrington, graciously offered her home at Diplow as a venue for a grand gathering, allowing Mrs. Davilow and Gwendolen to join in the festivities alongside many of the county's most esteemed families. And what a spectacle it was – Grandcourt's distinguished treatment of the dowerless beauty stood in stark contrast to his polite but distant regard for the heiress.

The world, in the form of Mr. Gascoigne and the local gentry, took note of these developments and drew their own conclusions. The rector, meanwhile, felt a sense of duty toward his niece, and he and Mrs. Davilow wondered aloud that the proposal had not yet been forthcoming. Grandcourt, too, seemed surprised by the delay, repeatedly telling himself that today would be the day he would finally ask Gwendolen to accept his hand. Yet, as the hours ticked by, the necessary formalities remained unaccomplished. This unexpected twist only fortified his determination for the morrow.

For the first time, Grandcourt admitted to himself a nagging doubt: what if Gwendolen were to refuse him? Though he had never seriously considered such a possibility, the prospect now loomed before him like a specter. He pushed the thought aside, unwilling to confront the discomfort of rejection. Instead, he focused on the practicalities of his plan. He had arranged for a beautiful horse to be brought for Gwendolen to ride, and the three of them – Grandcourt, Mrs. Davilow, and Gwendolen – would set out for Diplow to enjoy a leisurely lunch together. The mid-harvest weather was perfect, with a gentle breeze rustling the uncut corn and a few wispy clouds drifting lazily across the sky. As they rode, the rolling hills and fields of gold stretched out before them, a testament to the enduring beauty of the countryside.
