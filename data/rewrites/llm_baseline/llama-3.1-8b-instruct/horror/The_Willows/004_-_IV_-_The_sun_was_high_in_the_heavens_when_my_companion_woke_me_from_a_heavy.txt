As the sun climbed high in the sky, my companion's gentle voice pierced through my slumber, announcing that the porridge was ready and that we had just enough time to wash up. The enticing aroma of sizzling bacon wafted into the tent, mingling with the morning mist that clung to the air. "The river's still rising," he said, his tone laced with a hint of concern, "and several islands in the middle of the stream have vanished altogether. Our own island has shrunk significantly."

"Any wood left?" I asked, still drowsy.

"The wood and the island will meet their demise together tomorrow," he chuckled, "but we should be fine until then."

I dove into the icy waters from the tip of the island, which had undergone a remarkable transformation overnight. The current swept me down to the landing place opposite the tent, where I emerged shivering and invigorated. The banks whizzed by like a blur, leaving me with a sense of exhilaration that washed away the lingering fears of the previous night. The sun beat down relentlessly, casting no shadow, and the wind remained steadfast, refusing to yield.

Suddenly, the underlying meaning behind my companion's words crystallized, revealing a change in his intentions. He no longer wished to depart hastily, and had seemingly altered his mind. "Enough to last till tomorrow," he said, implying that we would spend another night on the island. This shift in his stance struck me as peculiar, given his conviction from the night before. What had triggered this change?

At breakfast, the banks crumbled and splashed, sending clouds of spray into our frying pan. My companion chatted incessantly about the difficulties the Vienna-Pesth steamers would face in navigating the flooded channel. Yet, it was his mental state that piqued my interest far more than the river's turbulent state or the challenges of the steamers. His manner had changed – he seemed slightly agitated, slightly reserved, with a hint of trepidation in his voice and gestures. I couldn't quite put my finger on it, but I sensed that he had become uneasy.

He ate sparingly and avoided his usual pipe. The map lay open beside him, and he studied its markings intently. "We'd better leave promptly in an hour," I suggested, attempting to broach the subject that had been weighing on my mind. His response left me perplexed: "Rather! If they'll let us."
