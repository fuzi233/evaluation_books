Silence filled the grand dining hall of Warrington Manor. A lone candle flickered on the table, casting eerie shadows on the polished wood. My Lady Warrington sat at the head, a stern expression etched on her face as she surveyed her husband's tenants and family gathered below. Her faith was a well-oiled machine, a finely tuned instrument that she wielded with precision. Every Sunday evening, the villagers would troop to the chapel, their faces a mask of devotion as they followed her lead. But behind closed doors, they whispered among themselves, their true concerns far removed from spiritual matters.

As the evening wore on, the conversation turned to the finer things in life. Sir Miles raised his tankard in a toast to the King, his eyes glinting with a mixture of warmth and calculation. The beer, a meager offering, seemed to be a token gesture, a reminder of the wealth that lay just beyond the reach of these rustic folk. Harry Warrington watched his uncle with a discerning eye, aware that the surface-level affability belied a deeper complexity. As the evening drew to a close, Sir Miles turned to Harry and spoke of the wines to be found in Norfolk, his words dripping with an air of superiority. Harry listened intently, his gaze drifting to the single joint of mutton on the grand silver dish, a meager offering indeed. The wine, too, seemed to be a token, a symbol of the gulf that separated the Warringtons from their tenants.

As the meal drew to a close, My Lady Warrington rose from her seat, her movements deliberate and calculated. She had a way of administering her nostrums, her medicines, to those around her, and Harry suspected that her potions were as potent as they were insidious. He recalled the countless times she had dispensed advice and counsel, her words laced with an air of authority that brooked no dissent. And yet, as he looked around the table, he saw the same mixture of devotion and desperation that he had seen in the faces of the villagers. They were a people bound together by threads of obligation and duty, their lives a delicate dance of appearances and reality.
