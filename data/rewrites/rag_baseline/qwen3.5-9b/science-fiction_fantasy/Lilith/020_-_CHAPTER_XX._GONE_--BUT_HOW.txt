I rose, stumbling over the uneven terrain, my vision swimming with a daze that threatened to swallow me whole. For a heartbeat, she was absent—a void instantly filled by the crushing weight of isolation, returning like a storm cloud after the rain clears. The woman I had dragged from the precipice of death had vanished, leaving me stranded in a desert of desolation. I dared not linger in this hideous solitude. Had I committed some fatal error? Was the debt I had forced upon her too heavy for her spirit to bear? Compelled by a desperate need to share the burden, I scanned the landscape and spotted her moving swiftly across the grass, retreating from the riverbank. Seizing a brief respite to restore my failing faculties, I launched myself into pursuit. Though the recent assault of the white leech and the woman's blow had drained my vitality, my strength surged back with terrifying rapidity, keeping her firmly in my sights.

"Is this, then, the end?" I muttered, the words tasting like ash as my heart brooded a mournful dirge. Her gaze, burning with anger and hatred, haunted my periphery. I understood her resentment; forcing life upon a dying creature was a cruelty in itself. Yet, how had I compounded the injury? Why did she loathe me? Could the virtue of modesty turn against genuine service? How could the proudest woman, acutely aware of every motion I made, harbor such a profound sense of disgrace? With the reverence due a father tending his orphaned child, I had borne her burdens and nursed her wounds. Had all my labor, all my despairing hope, culminated only in ingratitude?

"No," I corrected myself, my voice steadying. "Beauty possesses a heart! No matter how deeply buried, it exists! The deeper the soil, the stronger the bloom when it finally awakens in its beautiful grave! To rouse that heart is a gift superior to the happiest life; it grants her a nobler existence!"

She ascended a gentle slope ahead, her stride straight and steady, purposeful as one who knows her destination. Suddenly, I noted the widening gap between us. Summoning every ounce of reserve, my strength flooded my veins like fresh blood. My body felt ethereal, buoyant, as if gravity had lost its hold. Like a wind cutting through resistance, I rapidly closed the distance. She never glanced backward. She moved with the fluid grace of a Greek deity rescuing a victim, yet without haste. Within three yards, she halted abruptly, turning with unbroken elegance.

Fatigue showed on her face, though not the pallor of sickness, but a stark, pure whiteness. Her breathing was slow, deep, and rhythmic. Her eyes seemed to expand, filling the heavens and casting light upon the world. It was nearly noon, yet the atmosphere felt like a great night, heavy with an invisible dew that made the stars appear unnaturally large.

"Why do you follow me?" she asked, her tone quiet but stern, as if addressing a stranger for the first time.

"I have lived so long on the mere hope of your eyes," I replied, "that I must desire to see them again!"

"You WILL not be spared!" she declared coldly. "I command you to stop where you stand."

"Not until I see you in a place of safety will I leave you," I countered.

"Then take the consequences," she said, resuming her swift, gliding walk. As she turned, she cast a glance my way, and I stood frozen, as if pierced by a spear. Her scorn had failed; she intended to destroy me with her beauty alone. Despair shattered my resolve, breaking the spell. I ran, overtaking her once more.

"Have pity upon me!" I cried.

She paid me no heed. I followed her like a child pretending to abandon his mother.

"I will be your slave!" I shouted, laying my hand upon her arm.

She recoiled as if bitten by a venomous serpent. I cowered before the blazing intensity of her eyes, unable to avert my own.

"Pity me," I pleaded again.

She continued walking. The entire day passed in this relentless chase. The sun climbed to its zenith, paused, and descended beyond the horizon. She never paused; I never ceased. Night fell, and I remained close; to lose sight of her for a single moment meant eternal separation. We traversed thick, soft grass throughout the daylight hours. Abruptly, she stopped and collapsed onto the earth. There was sufficient light to reveal her utter exhaustion. Standing behind her, I gazed down at her form. Did I love her? I knew she was not virtuous. Did I hate her? I could not bring myself to leave her. Kneeling beside her, I waited.

"Begone! Do not dare touch me," she screamed. Her arms lay limp by her sides, paralyzed. Suddenly, they snapped shut around my neck, rigid as the limbs of a torturer. She pulled my face down to hers, and her lips clung to my cheek. A sharp sting of pain shot through me, pulsing violently. I could not move a single hair's breadth. Gradually, the agony subsided. A slumberous weariness, mixed with a dreamlike pleasure, washed over me, and then darkness claimed me.

When consciousness returned, the moon hung low above the horizon, offering no radiance—merely a bright object set against absolute blackness. My cheek burned; touching it revealed a wet spot. My neck ached; there, too, was moisture. I sighed heavily, overcome with fatigue, and turned my eyes listlessly around. The moonlight had vanished, gathered instead about the lady. She stood encased in a shimmering nimbus. Rising and staggering toward her, I obeyed her imperious command.

"Down!" she cried, treating me like a rebellious dog. "Follow me a step if you dare!"

"I will!" I murmured, exerting an agonized effort.

"Set foot within the gates of my city, and my people will stone you: they do not love beggars!"

Deaf to her warnings, weak as water and half-awake, I did not consciously move, yet the distance between us diminished. She stepped back, raising her left arm. With a clenched fist, she struck me squarely on the forehead. I felt the impact as a blow from an iron hammer and fell. I sprang to my feet, cold and wet, yet clear-headed and strong. Had the blow revived me? It had left neither wound nor pain. But how was I wet? I could not have lain unconscious for long, for the moon held no higher position!

The lady stood some yards away, her back to me. She was engaged in some activity I could not discern. Then, a sudden gleam revealed she had discarded her garments, standing exposed and white in the dazed moonlight. In an instant, she stumbled and fell forward. A streak of white shot away in a swift, drawn line. Simultaneously, the moon recovered its brilliance, flashing fully, and I realized the streak was a long-bodied creature rushing in great, low-curved bounds over the grass. Dark spots streamed down its back like a river, as if it had darted along the edge of a forest, snagging the shadows of the leaves.

"God of mercy!" I cried, "Is the terrible creature speeding toward the night-enfolded city?" I seemed to hear from afar the sudden eruption of terror, the wild cries spreading as the pale savage bounded from house to house, rending and slaying. While I stared after it, fear-stricken, a second large creature, pure white, shot past me from behind like a silent arrow. Its trajectory was direct for the spot where the lady had fallen—and presumably lay. My tongue stuck to the roof of my mouth. I lunged forward to pursue the beast, but the spot I aimed for was already far behind it.

"It was well," I thought, "that I could not cry out: if she had risen, the monster would have been upon her!" When I reached the location, no lady remained; only her discarded garments lay dark in the moonlight. I stood transfixed, watching the second beast. It tore over the ground with even greater swiftness than the first—in long, level, skimming leaps, the very embodiment of wasteless speed. It followed the path of the first, growing smaller until it vanished into the uncertain distance.

But where was the lady? Had the first beast surprised her, creeping silently upon her? I had heard no shriek! There had not been time to devour her! Could it have seized her mid-run, bearing her away to its den? So laden, it could not have maintained such speed! And surely, I would have seen it carrying something! Horrible doubts began to awaken in my mind. After a thorough but fruitless search, I set out to follow the trail of the two beasts.
