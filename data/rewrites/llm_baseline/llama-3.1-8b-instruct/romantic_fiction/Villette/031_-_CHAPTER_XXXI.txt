As the spring sun warmed the air, its gentle caress brought with it an unexpected languor, a lethargy that seeped into my bones like the soft petals of a blooming flower. My strength waned, and even the slightest exertion left me drained, my nights sleepless and my days languid. It was on one such Sunday afternoon, after walking the half-league distance to the Protestant church, that I returned, spent and exhausted, seeking refuge in the quiet solitude of my classroom. The familiar wooden desk beckoned, and I collapsed onto it, grateful for the respite.

As I rested, the soothing melody of bees humming in the nearby berceau enveloped me, their gentle song a lullaby that harmonized with the rustling of leaves in the spring breeze. Through the glass door, I watched Madame Beck and her merry group of friends strolling beneath the blossoming orchard trees, their laughter and conversation carrying on the air like the sweet fragrance of blooming flowers. My gaze drifted to a particular figure, a young woman I had encountered before as a visitor at Madame Beck's, whose name was Mademoiselle Sauveur. Rumors whispered that she was a "filleule," a god-daughter, of M. Emanuel, and that a close bond existed between her and the Professor. Though M. Paul was absent from the gathering, I had observed the young woman's easy rapport with him, the way she would run up to him, her arm slipping through his, and lean upon him with the carefree abandon of a child with a trusted guardian.

A fleeting, unsettling sensation had once stirred within me when I witnessed this scene, a presentiment that I had hastily dismissed, unwilling to probe its depths. Now, as I observed Mademoiselle Sauveur's bright silk robes glinting amidst the verdant foliage, my eyelids grew heavy, and I succumbed to a deep slumber.

Time slipped away, and when I awoke, the sun had dipped below the rooftops, casting a soft, grey light over the garden and the room. The bees had retreated, the flowers had begun to fold their petals, and the party had dispersed, leaving behind an empty, tranquil silence. As I sat up, I felt an unusual sense of ease, my limbs unnumb despite the prolonged rest.
