The sound of the river's relentless flow created an eerie melody, a siren's call that beckoned us closer to the abyss. Gerard's eyes, ablaze with a fierce inner light, locked onto mine, and I felt an unspoken understanding pass between us. We knew that the fate of our lives hung precariously in the balance.

Without a word, Gerard leapt into action, his movements a blur as he grasped the bow and the cord, his hands moving in a practiced dance. The next moment, Denys's body burst forth from the water, his face contorted in a mixture of fear and relief. "Now, grip me not!" Gerard's voice pierced the air, his words a desperate warning that seemed to hang suspended above the churning waters. Denys's response was a guttural gurgle, a sound that sent a shiver down my spine.

Gerard's grip on the bow tightened, his knuckles white with tension as he strove to keep Denys's head above water. I watched, transfixed, as he seized Denys's long hair, twisting it hard between his teeth. The muscles of his neck flexed beneath his skin as he fought to keep his friend afloat. The current buffeted us, threatening to sweep us away, but Gerard's strength held firm. He struck out with a powerful arm, propelling us forward with a rhythmic motion. I followed his lead, my own movements a faltering echo of his.

As we struggled against the torrent, Gerard's words of encouragement washed over me like a balm. "Courage," he whispered, his voice barely audible above the din of the river. "Whatever a duck knows, a Dutchman knows; art safe as in bed." The words were a mantra, a promise that we would emerge from this ordeal unscathed. And then, in an instant, we stumbled into shallow water, our feet scraping against the riverbed.
