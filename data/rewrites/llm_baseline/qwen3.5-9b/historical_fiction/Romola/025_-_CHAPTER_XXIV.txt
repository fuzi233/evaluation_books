When Baldassarre forced his way behind the heavy curtain, his wrists bound tight and a rough rope encircling his neck and torso, he halted abruptly at the threshold. The sight before him stole the breath from his lungs. He had anticipated the usual desolation of a church viewed merely as a building: a cavernous nave stripped of life, save for cold stone emblems, side altars where candles burned with feeble, flickering light, and statues that stared back with pale, rigid indifference. Perhaps a handful of worshippers in the distant choir would offer a monotone chant to the empty air. Instead, he found a sea of humanity.

It was a vast multitude, a tapestry woven from every thread of Florentine society—from magistrates and ladies of noble birth to rough-handed artisans and weary peasants. All were turned upward, their faces bathed in a breathless silence, fixed upon the pulpit situated at the angle between the nave and the choir. There stood a Dominican friar, a man of strong features and dark hair, gripping a crucifix as he preached.

For the first few minutes, Baldassarre took no note of the sermon's content. Though his entry had been silent, several pairs of eyes near the door had snapped toward him with immediate suspicion. The ropes around his limbs were undeniable proof of his status as an escaped prisoner. Yet, in a sanctuary, escapees were granted refuge. Furthermore, his advanced age and the wild misery etched onto his face were calculated to evoke pity rather than fear. As he stood motionless, his gaze drifting aimlessly from the throng to the patterned pavement beneath his feet, the observers lost interest in him, drawn back into the magnetic pull of the speaker.

Among those who had initially scrutinized him were Romola's eyes. Having entered late through a side door, she commanded a perfect view of the main entrance. She had watched Baldassarre with prolonged attention; the grey hairs threading his beard and the unmistakable stamp of profound suffering on his weathered face struck a deep chord within her. The cord tightening his throat confirmed her fears, stirring in her heart the specific sensitivities toward the sorrows of age that her entire life had cultivated. She imagined that their eyes had met in that fleeting moment of recognition, but Baldassarre remained oblivious. He possessed only a startled, fragmented awareness of the scene—a flash of consciousness that did not interrupt the fierce, tumultuous storm of emotion raging inside him since his confrontation with Tito. Memories of the past surged forward like delirious visions, twisted by thirst and anguish. No coherent thought regarding the future could form amidst this internal fire; the closest approximation was a bitter realization of his fading strength and a vague, overarching resolve born of total distrust.

Suddenly, he felt his very bones resonate with a booming cadence, sounding like the thunderous echo of his own inner turmoil. A voice, penetrating his marrow with an accent of triumphant certainty, declared: *"The day of vengeance is at hand!"*

Baldassarre shuddered and looked up. Too distant to discern details, he saw only the silhouette of the preacher, arm outstretched, lifting the crucifix high. Yet, he panted for those threatening words as if they were a promise of salvation. After a brief pause, the preacher slowly lowered his arm, set the crucifix down on the pulpit's edge, and folded his arms across his chest. He scanned the crowd, seemingly determined to lock eyes with every single face.

"All ye in Florence are my witnesses," the voice boomed, "for I spoke not in a corner. You are my witnesses that four years ago, ere signs of war or tribulation appeared, I proclaimed the coming scourge. I raised my voice like a trumpet to the prelates, princes, and people of Italy, declaring: 'The cup of your iniquity is full.' Behold, the thunder of the Lord gathers; it shall fall and shatter the cup. Your iniquity, which you taste as pleasant wine, shall be poured out upon you, becoming molten lead."

He pointed accusingly at the clergy. "And you, O priests, who mock, saying, 'Ha, ha! There is no Presence in the sanctuary; the Shechinah is naught; the Mercy-seat is bare; we may sin behind the veil unpunished!' To you, I declare, the presence of God shall reveal Himself as a consuming fire. Your sacred vestments shall turn to winding sheets of flame; for sweet music there shall be shrieks and hissing; for soft couches, thorns; and for the breath of wantons, pestilence. Trust not in your gold and silver, nor in your high fortresses. Though walls be of iron and citadels of adamant, the Most High shall instill terror into your hearts and weakness into your councils until you are confounded and flee like women. He shall break in pieces mighty men without number, replacing them with others. For God can no longer endure the pollution of His sanctuary; He will thoroughly purge His Church."

"And since Scripture declares that God reveals His purposes only to His servants the prophets, He has chosen me, His unworthy servant, making His purpose present to my soul through the living Word and the deeds of Providence. By the ministry of angels, He has shown it to me in visions. His word possesses me so completely that I am but a forest branch when the wind of Heaven blows through it; I cannot remain silent, even if I should be mocked by scorners. For four years, in obedience to the Divine Will, I have preached three truths delivered to me by the Lord: *In these times God will regenerate His Church*; *Before this regeneration comes the scourge over all Italy*; and *These things will come quickly*."

"But hypocrites, cloaking their hatred of the truth with a display of affection, have told me, 'Come now, Frate, abandon your prophecies; it suffices to teach virtue.' To them I answer: 'Yes, you whisper in your hearts that God dwells afar off, that His word is a parchment penned by the dead, and that He does not act as in days of old—rebuking nations, punishing oppressors, or striking down unholy priests as He struck the sons of Eli. But I cry again in your ears: God is near, not afar! His judgments do not change. He is the God of Armies; the strong men who march to battle are His ministers, just as storm, fire, and pestilence are. He drives them by the breath of His angels, and they descend upon the chosen land that has forsaken the Covenant. And thou, O Italy, art that chosen land. Has not God placed His sanctuary within thee, and hast thou polluted it? Behold, the ministers of His wrath are upon thee—they stand at thy very doors!'"

Savonarola's voice had climbed to a peak of impassioned force before falling suddenly into silence. His hands dropped, clasping calmly before him. This silence proved more potent than his shouting; it acted as a powerful spell upon the congregation. Across the vast cathedral floor, men and women sat frozen, faces upturned like breathing statues, until the voice returned in clear, low tones.

"Yet there is a pause—even as in the days when Jerusalem was destroyed, there was a pause that the children of God might flee. There is stillness before the storm: lo, there is blackness above, yet not a leaf quivers; the winds are stayed that the voice of God's warning may be heard. Hear it now, O Florence, chosen city in the chosen land! Repent and forsake evil; do justice; love mercy; cast out all uncleanness from among you, that the spirit of truth and holiness may fill your souls and breathe through your streets and homes. Then the pestilence shall not enter, and the sword shall pass over you leaving you unhurt."

"For the sword hangs from the sky; it quivers; it is about to fall! *The sword of God upon the earth, swift and sudden!* Did I not tell you years ago that I beheld the vision and heard the voice? And behold, it is fulfilled! Is there not a king with his army at your gates? Does not the earth shake with the tread of horses and the wheels of swift cannons? Is there not a fierce multitude capable of laying bare the land like a sharp razor? I tell you the King of France with his host is the minister of God: God shall guide him as a hand guides a sharp sickle, and the joints of the wicked shall melt before him, mowed down as stubble. He who flees shall not escape, and he who escapes shall not be delivered. And the tyrants who built thrones from the vices of the multitude, and the unbelieving priests who traffic in souls and fill the sanctuary with fornication, shall be hurled from their soft couches into burning hell; and the pagans and those who sinned under the old covenant shall stand aloof and say: 'Lo, these men have brought the stench of a new wickedness into the everlasting fire.'"

Now, cast away every other abomination, and you shall be strong in the strength of the Living God.
