The result was instantaneous and miraculous, leaving even the marksman momentarily stunned. He immediately summoned his companions to assist in capturing the fugitives and spurred his horse toward the hill. However, before he could reach the midway point, the figure of Martin Wittenhaagen appeared, drawn bow taut against the sky. Upon seeing Martin poised for action, Eric Wouverman instinctively dove behind a tree, shrinking into its shadow as much as possible. Martin's mastery with the crossbow was legendary, and the dead dog lying nearby served as a grim testament to his lethal precision.

Wouverman peered cautiously around the rough bark; the arrowhead remained fixed upon him, gleaming in the sunlight. Terrified, he dared not shift his position. Minutes passed in tense silence until his reinforcements arrived in full force. With a mocking laugh, Martin vanished from sight, soon followed by the clatter of hooves as he rode away on his mule. The hunters surged forward to the high ground, which offered a commanding view of a narrow, seemingly endless glade. There, far below, they spotted Gerard and Margaret sprinting desperately across the terrain, appearing no larger than gnats. Behind them thundered Martin, driving the mule at full gallop. The hunters had been bested both in cunning and in speed.

A brief explanation clarifies Martin's strategy. While cause and effect often reveal themselves through coincidence, such alignments can sometimes be deceptive. Just as a startled hare might tumble over a thicket precisely when a shot is fired—raising hopes only to vanish unharmed moments later—so too did the burgomaster's mule stumble into a rabbit hole at the exact moment the harmless crossbow bolt whistled overhead. The beast collapsed, throwing both rider and passenger to the ground. Gerard managed to catch Margaret, but her momentum dragged him down with her, scattering the castaways across the soil.

The mule rose quickly, trembling violently. Martin dismounted, glanced around, and noted that only one pursuer remained. Seizing the opportunity, he urged the young lovers to flee on foot while he held off the remaining enemy. Once he had secured his advantage, he galloped after his charges. After a grueling chase, he finally caught up to them. Instantly, he mounted Gerard and Margaret onto the mule, then ran alongside them until exhaustion forced him to stop. Taking turns riding and running in rotation, he ensured that a fresh pair of legs was always available. Consequently, long before the lovers slowed their pace, every trace of them was hopelessly lost to Dierich and his men. Defeated and crestfallen, the hunters turned back to tend to their leader and their wounded hound.
