As I stood before the carriage, preparing to bid farewell to the Brettons, the weight of my impending departure settled upon me like a shroud. Only three days remained before I would be forced to return to the pensionnat, and I found myself counting the moments until that moment, as if willing the hours to slow their relentless march. Yet, despite my longing to hasten their departure, they slipped away unnoticed, leaving me to confront the reality of my impending exile.

"Lucy will not leave us today," Mrs. Bretton said, her voice a gentle coaxing as we sat down to breakfast together. "She knows we can procure a brief reprieve."

I shook my head, a sense of desperation rising within me. "I would not ask for an extension, even if it were possible," I replied, my voice barely above a whisper. "I long to conclude our parting and settle into my new routine at Rue Fossette. My trunk is packed and ready; I must depart this morning."

However, fate had other plans, and Graham's absence proved to be the determining factor. He had promised to accompany me, but his engagements kept him occupied throughout the day, and he did not return until dusk. The ensuing conversation was a delicate dance of words, with Mrs. Bretton and her son urging me to stay one more night, while I strained against the tide of my own desires, longing to be free from the entanglements of our affectionate goodbye.

As Dr. John handed me from the carriage at Madame Beck's doorstep, the rain-soaked streets seemed to stretch out before me like a canvas waiting to be deciphered. The lamplight cast an eerie glow on the wet pavement, transporting me back to a similar night, not so long ago, when I had first stood before this very threshold, a stranger in a foreign land. I recalled the shapes of the paving stones, the same ones that now seemed to hold a different significance under the flickering light. Had I ever shared that poignant memory with Graham? I had not, nor did I feel inclined to do so; it was a secret kept hidden, locked away in the recesses of my mind.
