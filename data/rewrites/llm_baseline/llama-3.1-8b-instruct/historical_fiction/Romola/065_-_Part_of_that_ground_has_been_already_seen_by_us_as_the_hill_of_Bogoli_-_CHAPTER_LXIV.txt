In one of these cells, a lone figure knelt before a crucifix, lost in contemplation.

The hour was one of quietude, and most of the cells stood empty, their simple furnishings and sparse decor a reflection of the austere lives led by the brothers who called this place home. Yet, behind the door at the far end of the corridor, in the private chamber where the Prior would often sit to receive visitors or attend to his duties, a single figure in the long white Dominican tunic and scapular knelt in prayer. At first glance, it might have been any one of the many brothers who walked the halls of San Marco, their thoughts consumed by the daily routine of prayer, study, and service. But beneath the surface, a maelstrom of conflicting desires and motivations churned within the breast of this particular monk.

Savonarola's hands clasped together, his eyes fixed on the crucifix before him, as he mouthed the familiar words of supplication. Yet, his mind was elsewhere, wrestling with the doubts and fears that had been plaguing him for what felt like an eternity. The voices of his detractors echoed in his mind, their accusations and taunts a constant refrain that threatened to undermine his resolve. And then, there were the whispers of those who claimed to support him – the ones who urged him to take the Trial by Fire, to prove his conviction and demonstrate the divine favor that had always been at the core of his message.
