A woman’s heart is fierce in love and cunning in desperation. Where is she who will not play her part to perfection if it means saving the man she holds dear? Nature arms even the simplest of them with wit and daring when the moment demands it, and Margaret was no exception.

The moment the men left, she flung herself from the bed, her breath coming fast, and threw open the chest where Gerard lay hidden. The lid, the blankets, all had been pressed down by her weight—but she had not thought, not truly, of what that might mean. She pressed her ear to the door, listening until the last footsteps faded down the stairs. Only then did she dare turn back.

The chest was not what it seemed. Gerard himself had fashioned it, clever and quick with his hands. Beneath the false bottom, a space just deep enough to hide a man—or so she had believed. But now, as she leaned over it, whispering his name, there was no answer.

Silence.

Her fingers brushed his shoulder, then clutched at him, shaking. *“Gerard!”*

Nothing.

A terrible stillness met her touch. His body was limp, lifeless. She dragged him from the chest with a strength born of horror, hauling him toward the window, throwing it open as if the night air might breathe life back into him. Moonlight washed over his face—gray as ash, his chest unmoving.

Her hands flew to his heart. No beat. No breath.

She cradled his face, whispering, pleading, as if love alone could undo what had been done. *“Gerard, wake up. Please. I didn’t mean—I didn’t know—”* Her voice cracked. How could he be gone? How could she have been the one to smother him? She had pressed down on that lid, sealing him in darkness.

*“Forgive me,”* she sobbed, clutching him. *“I would have let them take you—I would have let them drag you away before this—”*

Her mind reeled. This couldn’t be real. Death did not come so swiftly, so cruelly. Not like this. Not by her own hands.

She begged him to curse her, to shout at her—anything but this silence. When no answer came, her grief twisted into something raw and keening. A scream tore from her throat, then another, wild and shattered.

Outside, the men froze at the sound. It was not the cry of a woman who had outwitted them. It was the sound of a soul breaking.

And they knew, even before they turned back, that something far worse than capture had happened in that room.
