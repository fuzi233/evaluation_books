As the door swung open, a sudden hush fell over the gathering in Cos's sitting room. The familiar faces of Miss Fotheringay, Lieutenant Sir Derby Oaks, and the little crooked old man in the window-seat all turned towards the newcomer. Major Pendennis, resplendent in his well-blackened boots, moved with the confidence of a seasoned soldier, greeting each person present with a gracious bow.

"Ah, my dear daughter, my esteemed friend Mr. Bows, and my gallant young protégé, Sir Derby Oaks," Costigan boomed, his voice rich with warmth as he swept his hand in a sweeping gesture, introducing each individual to the Major's attention. "In one moment, Major, I'm your humble servant," he added, dashing off to the adjacent chamber to adjust his attire. There, he busied himself with his hair-brush, a treasured family heirloom, and donned a fresh stock, a gift from Emily, his daughter. He carefully selected a matching collar and slipped into the new coat, tailored specifically for the occasion of Miss Fotheringay's performance.

Upon his return, Sir Derby Oaks emerged from the same room, looking every inch the dashing young officer in his snug shell-jacket. The three of them – Sir Derby, Miss Fotheringay, and even poor Pen – couldn't help but admire the garment's fit on the young man's broad shoulders.

Meanwhile, conversation flowed between the actress and the newcomer, touching on the usual topics of the weather before Costigan re-entered, resplendent in his new finery. "I needn't apologize to you, Major," he said, his Irish brogue thick and rich, "for receiving you in my shirt-sleeves."

"A soldier's duty is to instruct his juniors in the art of combat," the Major replied, his tone dripping with gallantry. "I recall hearing that you were quite skilled with a sword, Captain Costigan."

Costigan's eyes lit up at the mention of his name. "Ah, ye've heard of Jack Costigan, Major?" he asked, his curiosity piqued.

The Major nodded, his expression sincere. "Indeed, I have, sir. My nephew spoke highly of you, and I believe I may have even met you once, at Sir Richard Strachan's table at Walcheren. You sang for us, if I'm not mistaken."
