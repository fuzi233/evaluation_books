The dim light of the lantern cast an eerie glow on the faces gathered around the supper table. Mr. Warrington's lodgings were warm and inviting, filled with the scent of roasting meat and freshly baked bread. The evening's conversation flowed like a gentle stream, with laughter and debate mingling together. 

Mrs. Lambert, a woman of sharp wit, engaged Mr. Spencer in a lively discussion about the play they had attended earlier that day. "As for what our gentlemen say, sir," she teased, "you must not believe a word of it. 'Tis a delightful piece, and my husband and Mr. George behaved as ill as possible." Her eyes sparkled with amusement as she glanced at her son, George, who sat quietly, observing the exchange.

The General, a man of straightforward nature, chuckled and agreed with his wife. "We laughed in the wrong place, and when we ought to have cried," he admitted. "That's the truth." His daughter, Theo, looked up from her plate, her eyes shining with mirth. "Not when I was looking your way, papa!" she exclaimed, and the General laughed, asking, "Was there ever such a saucy baggage seen?"

Theo's words hung in the air, and Mr. Spencer, seated across from her, smiled warmly. "You know, sir, I didn't speak till I was bid," Theo continued, her voice modest. "I own I was very much moved by the play, and the beauty and acting of Mrs. Woffington. I was sorry that the poor mother should find her child, and lose him. I am sorry, too, papa, if I oughtn't to have been sorry!"

The conversation turned to the play's most memorable moments, with each guest sharing their thoughts and impressions. The General expressed a desire to see the fight between little Norval and the gigantic Norwegian, and Mr. Spencer suggested it to Mr. Rich, the manager. Harry, seated beside Hetty, spoke of Slack and Broughton at Marybone Gardens, and the group laughed at his witty remark. As the evening wore on, the talk turned to David Garrick, the renowned actor, and his powerful performance in Macbeth. "He's a little man," Mr. Spencer said, "but there are times when he looks a giant."
