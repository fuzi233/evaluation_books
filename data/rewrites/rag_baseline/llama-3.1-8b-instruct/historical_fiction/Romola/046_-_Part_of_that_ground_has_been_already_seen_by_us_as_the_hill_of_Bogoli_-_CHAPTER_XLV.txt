At the Barber's Shop.

Tito paused at the Palazzo Vecchio, awaiting the return of the Ten, who managed external and war affairs, that he might present to them the results of his private mission to Pisa, a preliminary step towards an avowed embassy aimed at securing a pacific understanding with the Emperor Maximilian and the League. His diplomatic skills had been well-documented, and as he recounted the findings of his inquiries and interviews with precision and candor, Bernardo del Nero, a respected member of the Ten, couldn't help but be impressed. Though his initial reservations about Tito had returned, strengthened by the sale of the library, Bernardo refrained from voicing them, aware that Romola's silence on the matter signified a tacit request for discretion. Still, he sensed that the breach of her father's wishes had been a source of deep sorrow for her, and the old man's perceptive eyes detected other signs indicating her marriage was not a happy one.

As he watched Tito, Bernardo thought, "Romola, my dear child, has taken to listening to Fra Girolamo and associating with the Piagnoni, a path I never expected her to follow. Women without children or happiness often turn to extremes – either frivolous pursuits or an excessive devotion to faith, believing they carry the weight of heaven's work. My poor Romola, educated in Latin and Greek, is now as much a woman as if she'd spent her days pricking her fingers with a needle. And this husband of hers, employed everywhere due to his smooth demeanor, I hope Tornabuoni and the others won't find themselves disappointed."

With these convictions firmly in place – that one couldn't judge the morality of political agents – Bernardo chose to keep his distance from Tito. While he had no concrete grievances against the Greek, apart from his being a Greek, and his own personal dislike, he decided to maintain a neutral stance. Tito, aware of Bernardo's coldness, responded with a scrupulous respect, and in Florence, the notion persisted that any service done to Romola's husband was, in effect, an homage to her godfather.
