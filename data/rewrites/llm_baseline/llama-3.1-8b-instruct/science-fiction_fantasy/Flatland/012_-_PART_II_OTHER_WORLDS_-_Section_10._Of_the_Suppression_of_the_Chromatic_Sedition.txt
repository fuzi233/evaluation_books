As the chromatic sedition raged across Flatland for three years, it seemed as though anarchy would ultimately prevail.  A great army of Polygons, eager to fight for their cause, was brutally crushed by the superior might of the Isosceles Triangles. Meanwhile, the Squares and Pentagons maintained a cautious neutrality, their allegiances wavering like the delicate petals of a flower in the breeze. 

But amidst the chaos, a darker undercurrent flowed – one of domestic discord. The wives of noble Circles, driven by desperation and fueled by their own fervor for the Color Bill, turned against their loved ones. In many a grand household, they wearied their husbands with pleas to surrender their opposition to the bill, but when their entreaties fell on deaf ears, they resorted to unspeakable acts of violence. Tragically, no fewer than twenty-three Circles met their demise within the very walls of their own homes, victims of a brutal cycle of carnage that left the nation reeling.

The situation seemed dire indeed, and the Priests appeared to have no choice but to submit or face extinction. Yet, fate had other plans. A peculiar incident, one that would come to be remembered for generations to come, was about to alter the course of history. It began with a lowly Isosceles, barely scraping by on the fringes of society, who stumbled upon a cache of colors belonging to a local tradesman. In a moment of reckless abandon, he daubed himself in the vibrant hues of a Dodecagon, his actions driven by a mix of mischief and desperation.

Donning his new persona, he chanced upon a young maiden, the orphaned daughter of a noble Polygon, in the bustling market square. With a voice dripping with deceit, he spun a web of lies, weaving a tale of love and devotion that ensnared her heart. Luck played its part, as did the hapless innocence of the maiden and her family, who failed to see through the charade. And so, the ill-fated union was sealed, only to end in tragedy when the truth was finally revealed. The maiden took her own life, her fate a catalyst for a profound shift in the collective consciousness of Flatland's women.

As news of the catastrophe spread, the women's minds were set aflame with sympathy and foreboding. They envisioned themselves, their sisters, and their daughters facing similar deceptions, and their resolve hardened against the Color Bill
