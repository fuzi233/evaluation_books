The silence preceding the storm was heavier than the thunder that would eventually break it. In mere moments, the regiments tasked with executing the flanking maneuvers vanished into the gloom, tramping off in disciplined silence. They hugged the lee of the rising terrain with surgical precision, ensuring their advance remained invisible to the piercing, predatory eyes of Twala's scouts.

A half-hour—or perhaps more—of agonizing stillness was permitted to pass before the main body stirred. This delay was calculated to maximize the shock value of the assault. The Greys, the vanguard destined to bear the brunt of the conflict, waited alongside their supporting regiment, the Buffaloes, which formed the army's resilient chest. Both units were pristine, untouched by the weariness of battle. The Greys had spent the morning in reserve, losing only a handful of men during the earlier skirmish where I had led a charge that left me temporarily stunned. The Buffaloes, meanwhile, had anchored the left flank, holding firm until the enemy's initial push faltered against the second line, sparing them the chaos of direct engagement.

Infadoos, a general of seasoned caution who understood that morale was the currency of survival on the eve of desperation, seized the pause to address his men. His speech was not merely tactical; it was a tapestry woven from poetry and promise. He extolled the honor of standing in the forefront, framing their impending sacrifice as a privilege to fight alongside the Great White Warrior from the Stars. He dangled the carrot of vast cattle herds and rapid promotions for those who returned, binding their fate to the success of Ignosi's arms.

I cast my gaze down the endless rows of waving black plumes and stoic, weather-beaten faces. A cold dread settled in my stomach, knowing that within the span of a single hour, nearly all of these magnificent veterans—none under forty years of age—would be reduced to dust. It was a cruel necessity, a calculation born of the great general's reckless wisdom: sacrificing a portion of the force to secure victory for the whole. They were walking toward certain slaughter, fully aware of their doom, yet they did not tremble. Not a single warrior betrayed a flicker of fear. They stood ready to trade their lives regiment by regiment against Twala's host on that narrow strip of green below, until extinction claimed them or the wings found their opening. Their absolute devotion to duty, their terrifying indifference to the bitter fruit of their service, inspired in me a mixture of envy and awe I had never known before.

"Behold your king!" Infadoos concluded, his finger pointing toward Ignosi. "Go forth to fight and fall for him, as is the duty of brave men. Cursed shall be the name of any who shrink from death for their sovereign or turn their backs upon the foe. Behold your king, chiefs, captains, and soldiers! Pay homage now to the Sacred Snake, and follow, that Incubu and I may chart a path to the heart of Twala's host."

A hush fell over the serried phalanxes, broken only by the soft, rhythmic tapping of six thousand spear handles against shields. It started as a whisper, like the distant breath of the sea, then swelled into a roaring tide that echoed off the surrounding mountains, shaking the air with heavy, resonant waves of sound. Finally, the crescendo subsided, dissolving into nothingness, before the royal salute cracked through the silence like a whip.

Ignosi, I realized, must have felt a surge of imperial pride that day; no Roman emperor had ever received such a salutation from gladiators facing certain death. Acknowledging the tribute, he lifted his battle-axe, and the Greys moved out in a formidable triple-line formation, each line comprising roughly a thousand fighting men. Once the rear companies had advanced five hundred yards, Ignosi positioned himself at the head of the Buffaloes, also arrayed in a three-fold line, and gave the command to march. I joined the procession, my heart hammering a frantic rhythm against my ribs, praying with every fiber of my being that I might emerge from this spectacle with my skin intact. I had found myself in strange predicaments before, but never one quite so perilous, where the margin for error was non-existent.

By the time we crested the edge of the plateau, the Greys were halfway down the slope, descending onto the tongue of grassland that jutted into the mountain's bend—a shape reminiscent of a horse's hoof extending into its shoe. The atmosphere in Twala's camp on the plain beyond was electric with anticipation. Regiment after regiment surged forward at a long, swinging trot, eager to seize the root of the tongue of land before the attackers could spill onto the plains of Loo.

This strategic bottleneck, the tongue itself, was a killing field. Even at its widest point near the root, it measured no more than six hundred and fifty paces, narrowing to a mere ninety yards at its tip. As the Greys descended the hillside and reached the widening section, they reformed their triple line and halted dead center. We, the Buffaloes, moved cautiously down the tip to take our position in reserve, a hundred yards behind the Grey lines and slightly elevated. From here, we observed Twala's entire force, clearly reinforced since the morning's attack. Despite their losses, they numbered no fewer than forty thousand, advancing swiftly. Yet, as they approached the gorge's entrance, they stalled. They had discerned the trap: only one regiment could fit through the passage at a time. Guarding the throat of this canyon, seventy yards from the mouth, stood the legendary Greys, the pride of the Kukuana army, prepared to hold the chokepoint against overwhelming odds, just as three Romans once held a bridge against legions.

Twala's army hesitated, recognizing the futility of a frontal assault against those three grim, immovable ranks. Then, a tall figure emerged, adorned with the customary nodding ostrich plumes, accompanied by a retinue of chiefs and orderlies—it was Twala himself. He issued a command, and the first regiment roared forward, charging the Greys. The defenders remained statuesque and silent until the attackers closed to within forty yards, at which point a hail of *tollas*, throwing knives, rained down among the enemy lines.

Instantly, the Greys erupted. With a unified bound and a deafening roar, they surged forward with uplifted spears, meeting the charge in a vortex of steel and flesh. The clash of shields rolled across the plain like thunder, and the landscape seemed to ignite with flashes of light reflecting off the shimmering weapons. The surging masses of humanity clashed to and fro, but the outcome was swift. The attacking lines began to thin, and with a slow, inevitable heave, the Greys swept over them like a great wave cresting a submerged ridge. That regiment was annihilated, but the cost to the Greys was steep; a third of their number lay dead, leaving only two lines standing.

Shouldering one another, they halted in silence, awaiting the next blow. I scanned the carnage and spotted Sir Henry's distinctive yellow beard moving amidst the ranks. He lived!

We advanced to the scene of the slaughter, a landscape littered with four thousand prostrate bodies—dead, dying, and wounded, staining the earth a vivid crimson. Ignosi issued a strict order, relayed instantly through the ranks: no enemy wounded were to be killed. We witnessed the command executed with scrupulous precision, a mercy that would have seemed unthinkable had we paused to reflect on the horror of the moment.

Now, a second regiment, distinguished by white plumes, kilts, and shields, advanced against the two thousand remaining Greys. They waited in that same ominous silence until the foe was within range, then launched themselves with irresistible force. Again, the terrible roll of colliding shields shook the air, and the tragedy replayed itself.

This time, however, the outcome hung in the balance for a prolonged period. The attacking regiment, composed of youthful zealots, fought with ferocious intensity, initially seeming to drive the veterans back through sheer numerical weight. The slaughter was horrific, hundreds falling every minute. Amidst the cacophony of warrior shouts and the groans of the dying, punctuated by the music of clashing spears, a continuous hissing undertone rose—"S'gee, s'gee"—the triumphant cry of each victor as he drove his assegai through his fallen opponent.

Yet, perfect discipline and unyielding valor possess a magic that youth cannot match. One veteran soldier outweighs two young ones, a truth that became starkly apparent. Just as we believed the Greys were finished, preparing to fill their vacant space, I heard Sir Henry's deep voice cutting through the din, catching a glimpse of his battle-axe arcing high above his plumes. The dynamic shifted instantly. The Greys ceased yielding; they stood as an immovable rock against which the furious waves of spearmen crashed repeatedly, only to recoil. Soon, they resumed their advance. Without firearms to obscure the view, we witnessed everything clearly. Within a minute, the onslaught faded.

"Ah, these are *men* indeed; they will conquer again," Ignosi cried, grinding his teeth in excited anticipation beside me. "Look! It is done!"

Like puffs of smoke erupting from a cannon's muzzle, the attacking regiment shattered into fleeing groups, their white headgear streaming wildly in the wind. They left their opponents victorious, though alas, no longer a regiment. Of the gallant triple line that had entered combat forty minutes prior with three thousand souls, barely six hundred blood-spattered survivors remained; the rest lay crushed beneathfoot.
