As I stepped out onto the porch, I collided with Sergeant Cuff on the stairs. The cool morning air carried the scent of freshly baked bread wafting from the kitchen, where Mrs. Yolland was preparing breakfast. Despite the awkward encounter, I found myself drawn to the Sergeant's news. My reserve crumbled, and I asked, "What news from Frizinghall?"

Sergeant Cuff's eyes sparkled with a mixture of satisfaction and seriousness. "I've spoken with the Indians," he said, his voice low and measured. "And I've uncovered a crucial detail about Rosanna's secret purchases in town last Thursday. They're scheduled to be released from custody next Wednesday. I'm convinced, just as Mr. Murthwaite is, that they came to our estate to pilfer the Moonstone. However, their plans were foiled by the events of Wednesday night, and they're now no longer connected to the jewel's disappearance. But I can tell you this, Mr. Betteredge – if we don't recover the Moonstone, the three jugglers will return."

Just then, Mr. Franklin emerged from his morning stroll, his eyes fixed intently on some point in the distance. With a subtle nod, he bypassed us and entered the house, leaving me to continue the conversation with the Sergeant. I decided to press on, intrigued despite myself. "So much for the Indians," I said. "What about Rosanna?"

Sergeant Cuff's expression turned somber. "That enigma remains unsolved," he said. "I've tracked her to a small linen shop in Frizinghall, run by a draper named Maltby. She purchased nothing from any other merchant, nor did she acquire any fine fabrics or trimmings. All she bought was a length of coarse cloth – enough to create a simple nightgown."

I raised an eyebrow. "Whose nightgown?" I asked.

"Undoubtedly hers," replied the Sergeant. "Sometime between midnight and three o'clock on Thursday morning, she likely snuck into Miss Verinder's room to conceal the Moonstone while everyone slept. When returning to her own quarters, her nightgown must have brushed against the wet paint on the door. She couldn't remove the stain, nor could she safely dispose of the nightgown without replacing it. That's why she bought the fabric for a duplicate garment."
