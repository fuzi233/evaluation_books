As Moore stepped out into the crisp morning air, the scent of freshly baked bread wafted from the nearby oven, enticing him to linger. He had planned a grand feast to celebrate his return to Hollow's Cottage, and the aroma of warm bread was the perfect accompaniment to his mood. The memory of Mrs. Horsfall's stubborn resistance still lingered, but he had outmaneuvered her at every turn, much to the delight of the servants and the chinking of his purse.

In the kitchen, Moore found a tray of steaming pastries, carefully arranged by the cook, who had heard tales of his return and was eager to please. He sampled each pastry, savoring the flaky crust and sweet fillings, and declared them fit for a king. The cook beamed with pride, knowing her efforts had been appreciated.

Next, Moore sought out Mrs. Yorke in the parlour, determined to make amends for his hasty departure. He found her surrounded by the remnants of a neglected meal, the remains of which seemed to mirror her troubled spirit. As he approached, she looked up, her eyes clouded with concern, and Moore knew he had to tread carefully.

"Good-bye, dear woman," he said, his voice softening as he drew near. "You've been a mother to me, and I'll never forget it."

Mrs. Yorke's gaze softened, and she rose to her feet, her movements hesitant. Moore offered her his cheek, and she kissed him gently, her lips a whisper against his skin. "What a trouble you've been, indeed," she said, her voice barely above a whisper.

As they parted, the children burst into the room, Jessie's tears drying quickly as she flung herself into Moore's arms. He scooped her up, twirling her around in a joyful dance, while Rose watched with a quiet intensity, her eyes shining with a mixture of sadness and understanding.

In the hallway, Moore handed the little girls off to their governess, who smiled warmly at him before ushering them away. As he stepped back into the chill of the morning air, he felt a sense of peace settle over him, like the warmth of a well-baked loaf. He made his way to the chaise, where the boys waited, their faces set in serious lines, but their eyes sparkling with excitement.
