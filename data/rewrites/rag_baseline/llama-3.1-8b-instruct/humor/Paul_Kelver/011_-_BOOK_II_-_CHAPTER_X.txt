The memory of that morning still clings to me like the faint scent of damp earth after a spring rain. My father's birthday dawned, and with it, the unexpected arrival of the inevitable. We had grown accustomed to the thought of his passing, but the actuality was a jarring note in the delicate harmony of our lives. My mother, wrapped in her worn dressing gown, moved quietly through the small, creaking house, her footsteps muffled by the thin partitions that separated us from the outside world.

"Washburn gave him a draught last night," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the distant cries of the costermongers. "Let's not disturb him." And so we sat around the breakfast table, speaking in hushed tones, our words lost in the chill air that seeped through the windows. Later, my mother rose, and I followed, our footsteps echoing through the stillness as we made our way to my father's chamber.

The blinds were drawn, casting the room in a somber gloom. My mother stood frozen, her ear pressed against the door, as if listening for a response that never came. The sounds of the street drifted in – a vendor's shrill cry, a youth's mournful wail – and my mother slowly turned the handle, pushing the door open a crack. We slipped inside, our hearts heavy with anticipation.

He lay motionless, his eyes closed, his lips parted in a faint smile. I had never seen death before, and its mystery was both fascinating and terrifying. My father's face, once lined with the deep creases of laughter and worry, now seemed smooth, almost youthful. As the reality of his passing began to sink in, a sense of numbness washed over me. I would hear his footsteps behind me in the street for days, his voice calling out to me, his face appearing among the crowds, only to vanish into thin air.

For my mother, the separation was a mere transition. Her faith was unshakeable, and she believed that he waited for her on the other side. She spoke of him with a quiet joy, recalling his quirks and kindnesses, his love for us, and the memories we shared. The days passed slowly, but she found solace in the knowledge that he was not truly gone, but merely waiting for her in a place beyond our mortal realm.
