In the twilight of his years, our dear friend from Flatland, now weakened by the weight of time and the skepticism of his fellow citizens, sat in his humble abode, surrounded by the familiar comforts of his two-dimensional world. Though his once-sharp mind had begun to fade, his gratitude remained unwavering as he pondered the swift reception his memoirs had received in the realm of Spaceland. The echoes of their appreciation still lingered in his heart, a bittersweet reminder of the love and curiosity that had drawn his story to the attention of the higher realms.

As he struggled to recall the vigor of his youth, he requested his trusted editor to speak on his behalf, addressing two concerns that had arisen among the readers of Spaceland. The first objection, though seemingly innocuous, had struck a chord within him, a nagging doubt that threatened to undermine the foundations of his world. A critic had pointed out that, since a line, by its very nature, must possess thickness to be visible, it followed that the inhabitants of Flatland must also possess a dimension of height, albeit one that lay beyond their comprehension.

Our friend's response, though born of conviction, had been met with skepticism by his fellow citizens. Yet, he stood firm, his reasoning guided by the understanding he had gained during his brief sojourn in Spaceland. He acknowledged the truth of the critic's assertion, yet denied the inference drawn from it. For just as the people of Spaceland possessed a fourth, unacknowledged dimension – which he chose to call 'extra-height' – so too did the inhabitants of Flatland possess a third, hidden dimension, which he referred to as 'height'.

However, he emphasized that the recognition of these dimensions was not a matter of perception, but of faith. The nature of dimension itself implied direction, measurement, and comparison; qualities that were absent in the infinitely thin lines of Flatland. The lines, being equally and infinitely thin, offered no basis for the conception of a third dimension. No instrument, no matter how delicate, could measure what was not present, nor could reason grasp what defied comprehension.

As he reflected on this conundrum, he recalled a recent encounter with the Chief Circle, the High Priest of Flatland, who had visited him in the State Prison.
