As the morning sun cast its golden light upon the terracotta rooftops of Florence, Romola set out once more for the city's vibrant heart, San Marco. Her destination was the chapter-house of the convent, where she had secured an appointment with the enigmatic Fra Girolamo through the kind intervention of Fra Salvestro the evening before. Given the friar's penchant for guarding his life from the whispers of calumny, such meetings were rare and always conducted with the utmost discretion. Therefore, the hour chosen was one when the cloisters would be bustling with visitors, their presence a testament to the city's openness.

Romola decided to take a route through the heart of the city, weaving past the loggias, piazzas, and shops that served as gathering places for the citizens. Everywhere she looked, she saw the unmistakable signs of public fervor – people engaged in animated discussions, their voices rising and falling in a cacophony of opinions. The three-day interval since the conspirators' execution had given rise to a heated debate, with proponents of justice and those advocating for the law vying for the upper hand. Handbills plastered the city, some bearing bold headlines that extolled the virtues of retribution or the importance of upholding the law, while others presented more nuanced arguments in smaller text. These latter writings, Romola knew, held the true substance of the debate.

Though she hurried along, her eyes scanned the crowds with a practiced intensity, seeking out the handbills that carried the more subtle arguments. She had managed to secure a few from eager readers and wall-posters, but it wasn't until she turned a corner near San Giovanni that she spotted Bratti, a familiar figure in Florence's streets, hawking his wares to passersby. His stock of handbills seemed to be in high demand, and Romola recognized the opportunity to acquire more. Approaching him with a swift step, she asked, "Bratti, have you two types of handbills available? I'll take them quickly, please."

Bratti's response was measured, as he carefully separated the sheets of paper, each one bearing the words 'Law' and 'Justice' in bold print. "Two sorts, yes," he replied, his tone matter-of-fact.
