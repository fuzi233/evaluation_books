A ripple of suppressed emotion moved through the group; they had located their kin in the distance. The pair mounted on Lona's steed spurred forward to merge with the cluster, met by a wavering cry that instantly dissolved into silence. As we closed the gap, the sound of their weeping washed over us like the breaking of fragile waves. Upon reaching them, I witnessed the catastrophe: etched onto their youthful visages was the hollowed-out stare born of primal terror, a transformation no ordinary sorrow could induce. Several drifted toward me, extending arms to claim the weight I carried. I yielded the burden; the desperate, hopeless tenderness in their reception swelled my heart with pity amidst its own ruin. Their lamentations for the Mother-Queen were futile; their attempts to coax a spark of recognition from her failed; their kisses and caresses as they transported her were unanswered. She remained stone-cold. Two bore her arms, stroking them gently; others wedged themselves beneath her frame; those unable to reach crowded the bearers. They lowered her onto a patch of dense, soft turf, and the Little Ones gathered around, sobbing. Beyond the circle stood the elephants, and I lingered near them, peering over the sea of small heads at my Lona. Those closest recoiled, trembling.

Odu spoke first, his voice a whisper. "I have seen that woman before!" he murmured to his neighbor. "It was she who dueled the white leopardess the night they woke us with their screaming!"

"Silly!" retorted his companion. "That was a wild beast, marked with spots!"

"Look at her eyes!" Odu insisted. "I know she is a malevolent giantess, yet she is a wild creature nonetheless. I know she is the spotted one!"

The other stepped closer; Odu jerked him back sharply. "Do not gaze upon her!" he cried, shrinking yet drawn by a hateful fascination in her vacant sockets. "She would consume you instantly! It was HER shadow! She is the wicked princess!"

"That cannot be! They said she was beautiful!"

"Indeed, it is the princess!" I interjected. "Malice has rendered her hideous!"

She heard, and the expression that crossed her features was terrible.

"It was very wrong of me to flee," Odu muttered thoughtfully.

"What drove you to run?" I asked.

"I expected to find you exactly where I left you!"

He paused, lost in memory. "I do not know what propelled me," admitted another. "I was terrified!"

"A man descended the hill from the palace," stated a third.

"How did he frighten you?"

"I do not know."

"He was not a man," corrected Odu. "He was a shadow; he possessed no thickness!"

"Tell me more of him."

"He descended the slope pitch-black, moving like a malign giant, yet flattened. He was pure obscurity. Terror seized us the moment we beheld him, yet we did not scatter; we stood and watched. He advanced as though intent on crushing us. Yet before he reached us, he began to expand, stretching wider and larger until he obliterated our vision entirely, vanishing from sight, only to descend upon us!"

"What do you mean by that?"

"He filled the space between us completely; we could not see one another. Then he was *inside* us."

"How did you perceive his presence within?"

"He altered me profoundly. I felt ill. I was no longer Odu—not the Odu I knew. I desired to rend Sozo apart—though not truly, but as if I did! He turned and embraced Sozo. 'It was not me, Sozo,' he sobbed. 'Deep down, it was Odu, loving you always! And Odu arose, striking Naughty aside.' He grew nauseous, convinced he must commit suicide to escape the blackness. Then came a horrific laughter that seemed to hear my thoughts, vibrating the very air around me. Afterward, I suppose I fled, unaware I was running until I found myself sprinting as fast as possible, dragging everyone else with me. I wished to stop, but the thought never occurred to me until I burst through the gate into the grass. Only then realized I had escaped a shadow seeking to usurp my identity, a void that hated him from within. It was not my own self acting! And now I know I should not have fled! But I did not comprehend my actions until they were complete! My legs acted independently; they grew fearful, forgetting me, and ran! Naughtily! There! And there!"

Thus concluded Odu, delivering a kick to each of his recalcitrant limbs.

"What befell the shadow?" I inquired.

"I do not know," he replied. "I presume he retreated home into the moonless night."

My mind wandered to locate Lona. Dropping to the grass, I cradled the lifeless form in my lap and whispered into her ear, "Where are you, Lona? I love you!" Her lips offered no response. I kissed them, feeling them barely cool, laid the body down once more, and designated a guard. Rising, I prepared to ensure the safety of Lona's people throughout the coming darkness. Before sunset, I established a watch over the princess outside the camp and stationed sentinels around her; intending to patrol the perimeter myself all night, I ordered the remainder of the army to sleep. They collapsed onto the grass, succumbing to slumber instantly.

When the moon ascended, I glimpsed something white—the leopardess. She swept silently around the sleeping encampment, passing three times between the princess and the Little Ones. Immediately, I commanded the watch to lie down with the others, stretching myself beside the corpse of Lona.
