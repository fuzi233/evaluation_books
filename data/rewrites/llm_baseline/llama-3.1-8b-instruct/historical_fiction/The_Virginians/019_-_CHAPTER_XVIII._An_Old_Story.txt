As Lady Castlewood and her children passed through one door of the grand salon, Lord Castlewood departed by another. Her Ladyship's demure eyes, previously fixed on the delicate violets and jonquils arranged within the tambour frame, slowly lifted to Harry Warrington, who stood beneath the ancestral portrait hung above the roaring fireplace. He had gathered a small bouquet of blush roses and adorned his face, his cheeks, his forehead, and even his youthful ears with the flowers.

"Why did you refuse to accompany our aunt, cousin?" Lady Castlewood asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

"I stayed because you instructed me to," Harry replied, his words laced with a mix of innocence and adoration.

"Ah, child, you take everything I say at face value!" Lady Castlewood laughed, her eyes sparkling with amusement. "Do all the gentlemen of Virginia interpret every word as a command?"

Harry's face flushed, and he took a step closer to the tambour frame. "When we strolled in the garden two evenings ago, you whispered to me, 'Harry, if your aunt requests you to leave, you shall depart, and if you do, you will forget me.'" His voice trembled as he produced a wilted flower from his waistcoat pocket. "This rose, given to me by your own hand, I shall treasure forever. You said those words, didn't you?"

Lady Castlewood's expression softened, and she reached out to gently pat Harry's hand. "All men eventually forget, Harry."

"Not in Virginia, cousin," Harry replied, his passion unwavering. "My heart beats solely for you now. I would sacrifice anything to remain by your side. Your kindness and generosity have awakened within me a desire to distinguish myself, to prove myself worthy of your love."

A sudden commotion broke out as a spaniel darted between Harry's legs, causing him to stumble forward. "Look, cousin, if you commanded me to leap from that window, I would do so without hesitation!"
