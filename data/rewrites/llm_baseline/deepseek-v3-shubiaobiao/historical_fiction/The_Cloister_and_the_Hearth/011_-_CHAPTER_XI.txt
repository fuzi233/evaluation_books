The burgomaster’s venomous glare and Gerard’s unjust imprisonment had seared into the young man’s heart a conviction: Ghysbrecht van Swieten was his mortal foe. Now, as he edged around the tower’s corner, his pulse hammered not with fear of phantoms, but of treachery—some trap laid by that same cruel hand. Yet before he could steel himself further, another hand slipped into his, warm and steady. Margaret stood beside him, her courage a silent vow to face whatever peril lay ahead.

Then they saw it.

High upon the tower’s face, a creature with a head of fire crawled upward, its dark form silhouetted against the unnatural glow. Below, a spectral figure in white stood motionless, pale as death. Gerard’s breath caught.

“The rope!” he whispered hoarsely. “It climbs the rope!”

The fiery thing vanished into the cell—his cell—and the window pulsed red, as if the very stones bled. The white specter remained, waiting.

Margaret’s fingers tightened, then slipped from his grasp. Before he could stop her, she was running toward the apparition, her cry slicing the night. Gerard’s heart lurched. Was the devil luring her to doom? He dropped to his knees, voice trembling as he hurled Latin into the dark:

Then—a whimper. Not from Margaret, but from the specter itself. The ghostly figure cowered, pleading for mercy. Hell, it seemed, had its fears.

---

Kate and Giles arrived moments later, staring dumbfounded at the rope dangling from the window.

“Gerard’s escaped!” Giles crowed, already gripping the line.

Kate clutched his arm. “Fool! This is devil’s work! Where would Gerard get a rope? How hangs it from nothing? It’s glamour, meant to snare you!”

Giles snorted. “The road to hell goes *down*, sister. This rope goes *up*.” With a grin, he launched himself upward, the lantern at his neck turning him into a glowing demon in reverse. Kate watched, breathless, until a sudden movement sent her stumbling back—a woman, rushing at her from the shadows.

Both shrieked.

“You’re—you’re alive?” Kate gasped.

“As are you!” Margaret panted, hand on her heart.

Then came a voice from above, sepulchral and booming:

Giles, now a fiery-headed imp, rained down documents upon them, his laughter wild as the papers fluttered like wounded birds. When he slid to the ground, he thrust the bundle at Gerard, palm outstretched.

“Payment,” he demanded.

Margaret frowned. “This isn’t ours.”

Gerard’s jaw set. “Ghysbrecht stole my freedom. I’ll take what I can.”

---

Kate begged him to come home. Gerard’s reply was ice.

“I have no father. Only a jailer.”

She pressed on, revealing Cornelis and Sybrandt’s treachery, but Gerard’s wound was too fresh. “He gave Ghysbrecht the power to cage me. Let Tergou rot. I’ll find no home here again.”

Margaret touched Kate’s arm. “Give him time.”

The sisters exchanged a look, then an embrace. Kate’s sigh as she turned away was a quiet thing, lost to Gerard’s ears but not to Margaret’s heart.

As they vanished into the night, Giles trotting behind with his stolen hoard, Gerard’s last words hung in the air—a vow, and a farewell.
