I awoke with a heart swelling with triumph, my mind already racing through the glorious campaign awaiting me. The Gospel of the Third Dimension was ready to be preached across Flatland, even to the rigid ranks of the Soldiers and the timid Women. My first convert would be my Wife.

But as I finalized this grand design, a hush fell upon the street outside, broken only by the booming voice of a herald. He read aloud the latest Resolution of the Council: a decree demanding the arrest, imprisonment, or execution of anyone who sought to pervert the minds of the populace with delusions or claimed revelations from another world.

A cold shiver ran through me. This danger could not be ignored. Prudence dictated that I abandon any mention of my Revelation entirely and rely solely on the path of Demonstration. After all, the proof seemed so starkly simple—"Upward, not Northward"—that discarding the mystical element should leave nothing lost. Yet, the phrase, which had felt as clear as arithmetic moments before my sleep, now seemed to lose its sharp edge.

My wife entered just then, and after exchanging a few polite, mundane words, I resolved not to burden her with the truth. My pentagonal sons were men of integrity and standing, renowned physicians, yet they lacked the mathematical rigor required for my purpose. Worse, their patriotism and reverence for the Circles far outweighed their love for me. If they discovered I was preaching sedition regarding the Third Dimension, they would surely hand me over to the Prefect.

The solution lay elsewhere. A young, docile hexagon with a mathematical bent would be the ideal student. Why not begin with my grandson? His earlier, innocent musings on the meaning of $3^3$ had already pleased the Sphere. Teaching a child would keep me safe; he knew nothing of the Council's edict, unlike my sons, whose loyalty to the state might override familial affection.

First, however, I had to quell my wife's curiosity regarding the mysterious interview with the Circle and my sudden appearance. I offered her a vague, plausible explanation—perhaps less consistent with the truth than my readers in Spaceland might wish—but she eventually accepted it, returning quietly to her domestic duties without pressing for details about the Third Dimension.

With her distracted, I summoned my grandson. To confess the truth, I felt the vivid images of my vision slipping away like a half-remembered dream, and I longed to test my ability to create a disciple before the memory faded completely.

When he entered, I locked the door. Sitting beside him with our mathematical tablets—what you would call lines—I proposed we resume yesterday's lesson. I reminded him how a point moving in one dimension creates a line, and how a line moving in two dimensions creates a square. Then, forcing a smile, I turned to him. "And now, you scamp," I said, "you tried to convince me that a square could move 'Upward, not Northward' to create another figure, a sort of extra square in three dimensions. Repeat that, you young rascal."

Outside, the herald's cry of "O yes! O yes!" echoed again, proclaiming the new law. My grandson, though young, possessed an intelligence rare for his age and was raised in absolute reverence for the Circles. He understood the gravity of the moment instantly. He waited until the proclamation faded, then burst into tears.

"Dear Grandpapa," he sobbed, "that was only my fun! I meant nothing by it. We didn't know about the new Law, and I'm sure I never mentioned the Third Dimension. And I certainly didn't say anything about 'Upward, not Northward'! That sounds like nonsense. How can something move upward without moving northward? Even a baby wouldn't be that silly. How absurd! Ha! ha! ha!"

"Not at all silly," I snapped, my patience fraying. "Here, take this square." I grabbed a movable model lying nearby. "Watch closely. I move it—not northward, but... yes, upward. That is to say, not northward, but somewhere else..." My explanation dissolved into confusion as I shook the square aimlessly, unable to visualize the movement in two dimensions.

My grandson erupted in laughter, louder than before. "You aren't teaching me, Grandpapa; you're just joking!" he declared, unlocking the door and fleeing the room. Thus ended my first, disastrous attempt to convert a pupil to the Gospel of Three Dimensions.
