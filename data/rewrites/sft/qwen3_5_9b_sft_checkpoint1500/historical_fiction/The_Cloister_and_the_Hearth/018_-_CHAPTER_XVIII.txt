Ghysbrecht Van Swieten lay awake until the dawn, his mind a tempest of dread far worse than any storm. Though he claimed fear of thunder and lightning kept him from joining the night watch, the truth was a cold knot of suspicion twisted in his gut. The moment the skies cleared, he slipped down the back stairs, mounted his mule, and galloped to the "Three Kings" tavern in Sevenbergen. Inside, his men slumbered in a chaotic mix of postures—some sprawled over chairs, others collapsed upon the tables, a few curled up on the bare floorboards. Ghysbrecht roared like a beast, shaking them from their stupor, and demanded the tale of their failed vigilance, punctuated by their own boasts of unyielding zeal.

"Fool!" the burgomaster barked, his face flushed with indignation. "To send you away without me! My life depends on whether he is still there. Did you look beneath the girl's bed?"

"No," admitted Dierich Brower, rubbing his eyes. "There was simply no space for a man to hide there."

"How can you know that unless you looked?" Ghysbrecht snarled, his voice rising. "You must have peered under the mattress and probed every panel with your knives. Rise! Now I will teach you the art of searching."

Dierich Brower sat up, shaking dust from his tunic. "If you manage to find him, then I am a horse and not a man."

Within moments, Peter's house stood once more encircled by the burghers' party. Ghysbrecht tethered his mule to a post outside, entrusting it briefly to Jorian Ketel before leading the charge inside with Dierich and the rest. They moved through the silent rooms, but the house was utterly deserted. Not a soul remained within its walls—not even Peter. They ascended the stairs, expecting to find the fugitive hiding above, yet silence greeted them. Then, abruptly, one of the men threw up his hand, pointing a trembling finger toward Peter's window, which stood wide open to the morning air.

Beyond the glass, the others saw him. At a moderate distance, walking calmly across the open fields, were the three figures they had hunted: the man himself, accompanied by Margaret and Martin. A cry of triumph tore from Ghysbrecht's throat. With an exultant yell, he leaped from the stairs, scrambled onto his waiting mule, and spurred it forward. Behind him, his men shouted and followed, setting off in hot pursuit of the fleeing trio.
