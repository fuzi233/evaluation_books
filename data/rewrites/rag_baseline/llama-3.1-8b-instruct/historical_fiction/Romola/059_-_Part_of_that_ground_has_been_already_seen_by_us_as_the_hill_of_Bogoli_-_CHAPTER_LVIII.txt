A Final Understanding.

As the days dragged on like molten honey, Romola's anxiety thickened, a palpable fog that clung to every surface of her being. Her thoughts were consumed by the uncertainty surrounding her godfather's fate, and she had come to rely on the measured pace of the Florentine justice system to guide her. Almost two weeks had elapsed since the arrest, and still the outcome hung precariously in the balance. The likelihood of leniency had wavered, buffeted by the conflicting interests of those in power. Some argued that clemency would embolden the Republic's enemies, while others claimed that a harsh sentence would only incite the ire of the condemned men's families.

In this intricate dance of politics and persuasion, the Eight, those esteemed magistrates of Florence, hesitated to take responsibility for the final verdict. They sought the counsel of the Signoria, whose members, in turn, deflected the burden onto the Great Council, citing a law passed by the zealous Savonarola nearly three years prior. According to this legislation, a citizen condemned to death by the fateful six votes – the 'six beans' – could appeal to the Great Council. However, the friends of the accused, driven by a desire for delay, resisted this appeal, insisting that the sentence be passed before the appeal could be lodged.

Their opposition prevailed, and a compromise was reached: the sentence would be decided by a larger assembly, comprising the higher magistracies, the Senate of Eighty, and a select group of citizens. Romola, her anxiety sharpened by the possibility that her godfather's fate might be sealed before the day drew to a close, had managed to secure permission to visit him once more, albeit in the presence of witnesses. She had returned to the Via de' Bardi, accompanied by her cousin Brigida, who had left to await the momentous news at the home of a friend connected to one of the magistracies.

Romola sank into the first available seat in the salon, her agitation and despair rendering her oblivious to her surroundings. The vibrant colors and patterns of the room receded into the background as she waited for Brigida's return, her mind fixated on the uncertainty that had become her constant companion. Time seemed to stretch out like a taut string, and Romola felt the weight of her doubts bearing down upon her. But when the door creaked open, she knew it was not Brigida who stepped across the threshold
