The morning sun cast a warm glow over the tranquil scene as Edith strolled beside her husband, Captain Lennox, on their seaside walk. The rhythmic waves caressed the shore, a soothing melody that seemed to lull the world into a state of serenity. As they walked, Edith's thoughts drifted back to the previous evening's events, her mind still reeling from the news that had arrived via a lawyer's letter – Margaret, their young cousin, was the sole heir to a substantial inheritance.

Edith leaned in close to her husband, her voice barely above a whisper. "You didn't know about this, did you?" she asked, her eyes sparkling with curiosity. Captain Lennox shook his head, his brow furrowed in confusion. Edith's eyes sparkled with amusement as she recalled the look on Margaret's face when she'd learned of her newfound fortune – a mixture of shock and trepidation.

As the days passed, Margaret found herself increasingly reliant on Captain Lennox's guidance, seeking his counsel on matters of finance and estate management. Her aunt, Mrs. Shaw, and the Lennoxes, who had been bound for Cromer, had grown accustomed to thinking of him as Margaret's trusted advisor. Edith couldn't help but notice the way Margaret's reserve began to thaw, ever so slightly, as Captain Lennox patiently explained the intricacies of the law. It was almost as if she were a student, eager to learn, and he, a devoted teacher.

Their evening conversations often turned to the subject of Margaret's future, and Edith's thoughts began to wander to the possibility of a union between Margaret and Mr. Montagu. She raised the topic with her husband one evening, her tone playful, yet laced with a hint of mischief. "Do you think these long talks with Margaret will lead to anything, Henry?" she asked, her eyes sparkling with anticipation. Captain Lennox's response was guarded, his expression a mask of caution. Edith's smile grew wider, her thoughts racing ahead to the possibilities.

As the seasons changed, Margaret's plans for travel took shape. Gone were the dreams of a trip to Spain, replaced by a more subdued destination – Cromer, a seaside town where she could rest and rejuvenate.
