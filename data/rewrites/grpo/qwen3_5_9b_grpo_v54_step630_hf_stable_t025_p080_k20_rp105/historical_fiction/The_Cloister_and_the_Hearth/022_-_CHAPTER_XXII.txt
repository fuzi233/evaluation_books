The shot struck with such sudden, magical force that the marksman himself stood stupefied for a heartbeat. Recovering quickly, he hailed his companions to join in the pursuit and turned up the hill. But before he had covered half the distance, the figure of Martin Wittenhaagen rose from the treeline, a bent bow held ready in his hand. Eric Wouverman, recognizing the lethal skill of that weapon and recalling the dog already lying dead, did not hesitate. He darted behind a thick trunk, shrinking himself against the bark until he was as small as possible.

Wouverman peered cautiously around the rough surface; there, still aimed directly at him, was the gleaming point of the arrow. It shone with a cold, deadly light. Terrified, he dared not stir from his shelter. Minutes passed in tense silence before his companions arrived in great force, their numbers swelling with every step. Then, with a scornful laugh, Martin vanished into the woods, and shortly thereafter, the sound of hooves announced his departure on the mule.

All the hunters surged forward together. From the high ground, they commanded a view of a narrow, almost interminable glade. Far below, they spotted Gerard and Margaret running at a prodigious pace, appearing no larger than gnats against the vast landscape. Behind them, Martin galloped with frantic energy, driving the mule *ventre à terre*.

The hunters were outwitted as much as they were outrun. To understand Martin's conduct, one must look to the chain of coincidences that guided fate. As often happens, such coincidences can be deceitful. Just as a hare might tumble over a briar at the exact moment a gun fires, raising false hopes before scudding away untouched, so too did the burgomaster's mule stumble. At the precise instant the crossbow bolt whizzed harmlessly overhead, the beast stepped into a hidden rabbit-hole. She fell, throwing both riders to the earth. Gerard caught Margaret in his arms, but her weight and momentum dragged him down with her. In a moment, the soil was strewn with the principal characters of this drama.

The docile mule rose immediately, standing trembling amidst the dust. Martin was next to recover, and upon looking around, he saw that only one pursuer remained. Seizing the opportunity, he urged the young lovers to flee on foot while he checked the lone enemy, as previously recorded. He then galloped after his companions. After a long, exhausting chase, when he finally caught up to them, he instantly placed Gerard and Margaret back upon the mule. He ran alongside them until his breath failed, then took his turn to ride, passing the reins to the runner who needed rest. By this rotation, the runner was always fresh, and long before they relaxed their speed, all sound and trace of them was hopelessly lost to Dierich and his men. The hunters returned crestfallen, leaving behind their chief and their winged bloodhound to face the silence of the forest.
