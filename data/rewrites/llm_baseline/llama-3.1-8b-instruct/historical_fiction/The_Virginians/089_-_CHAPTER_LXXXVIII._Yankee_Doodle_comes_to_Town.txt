In the tranquil atmosphere of Virginia, where the warm sunlight danced across the rolling hills, a peculiar phenomenon had long been observed – the transplantation of England's wayward sons to the New World. Like seeds carried on the wind, these restless spirits were sown in the fertile soil of America, where they would either bloom into new lives or wither under the weight of their past mistakes. I, too, had played a part in this grand experiment, facilitating the journey of my cousin Hagan to the colonies, where he found solace in the pulpit and a chance to redeem himself.

As I often confessed to my dear friend Madam Theo, my actions were motivated by a mix of compassion and self-interest. She would remind me that my efforts were guided by the highest principles of morality and honor, but I knew better. I had simply wanted to distance myself from the consequences of Hagan's transgressions. Yet, as I reflect on those times, I realize that my pride and error led me to underestimate the strength of his character. Maria, my cousin, proved to be a beacon of kindness and resilience in the face of adversity, offering comfort to those around her even as she navigated her own struggles. Her unwavering dedication to her faith and her community was a testament to her unshakeable spirit.

Meanwhile, another member of our family had also found himself on the opposite side of the Atlantic, in the bustling city of New York. The Honourable William Esmond, Esquire, had been banished to this distant land, carrying with him a reputation for dishonesty and deceit. As the troubles of the time unfolded, we received news of his exploits in his new home. It seemed that he had become a master of spreading falsehoods and causing trouble wherever he went. My Lord Dunmore shared with me a disturbing account of William's claims regarding our family's estate at Castlewood – that it was only held during his brother's pleasure, and that he was negotiating with his brother, the present Lord Castlewood, to purchase the reversion of the estate. We knew the truth, of course, as we possessed the deed of gift and had it registered in the proper authorities. Still, the intent behind William's words was clear, and my brother Hal and I vowed to confront him about this fabrication as soon as we had the opportunity.
