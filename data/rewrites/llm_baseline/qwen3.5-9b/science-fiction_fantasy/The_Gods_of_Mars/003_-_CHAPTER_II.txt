Tars Tarkas and I found no respite to exchange words as we stood frozen before the great boulder, encircled by the twisted carcasses of our grotesque attackers. From every direction, down the sweeping scarlet valley, a torrent of terrifying forms surged forward, answering the eerie summons of the strange figure perched high above us.

"Come!" cried Tars Tarkas, his voice urgent. "We must race for the cliffs. There lies our only hope of even temporary refuge. Somewhere there, we may find a cave or a narrow ledge where two defenders could hold this motley, unarmed horde at bay forever."

Together, we tore across the red grass. I timed my strides carefully, ensuring I did not leave my slower companion behind. We covered perhaps three hundred yards between the boulder and the sheer rock face, searching desperately for a sanctuary to withstand the pursuing nightmare. They were rapidly closing in.

"Hasten ahead!" Tars Tarkas shouted, urging me to scout the shelter. "Discover it, if possible, while I bring up the rear!"

It was a sound strategy; saving even minutes meant life. Ignoring exhaustion, I threw every ounce of my Earth-born strength into the sprint, clearing the final distance in great, bounding leaps. I reached the base of the cliffs in an instant.

They rose perpendicular from the level plain, devoid of the usual rough scree or debris that typically eases the ascent of such formations. Only scattered boulders, fallen from heights unknown, marred the otherwise pristine turf. My initial scan sent a chill through my heart. Nowhere—not a single foothold—could I discern on the lofty escarpment, save where the weird herald stood shrieking his summons.

To my right, the cliff's base vanished into the dense, vibrant foliage of the forest, which reared its gorgeous canopy a thousand feet against the stern stone. To the left, the rock wall stretched unbroken across the valley's head, disappearing into the silhouette of a mountain range that hemmed the valley in on all sides. Some five thousand feet above me, the cliffs towered, casting a dull yellow gloom in the shadow of the sun. Streaks of dusky red, deep green, and flashes of white quartz speckled the face. Beautiful, certainly, but viewed through the lens of desperation, they looked less like a landscape and more like the impregnable walls of a prison.

Just as I scanned the vast expanse for a crack or crevice, the sun crossed the zenith. Suddenly, the dull surface erupted into a million scintillating lights—burnished gold, flaming red, soft greens, and gleaming whites. It was a spectacle of such gorge that the human eye rarely rests upon. The entire cliff face was veined with solid gold, appearing as a wall of pure metal, interrupted only by outcroppings of ruby, emerald, and diamond boulders—a tantalizing hint of unimaginable wealth buried deep within.

But what seized my attention was a row of black spots high across the golden wall, near the treetops. Dark openings! Caves! Possible avenues of escape. Yet, there was but one way to reach them: through the towering trees on my right.

I knew I could scale them. Tars Tarkas, however, with his immense bulk, would struggle. On Mars, hills rarely exceed four thousand feet, and the gradual slopes offer little need for climbing. Even if they did, the Martians prefer circling routes to direct ascents. There was no choice. We had to try.

The Thark understood the gravity of the situation instantly. Behind us, the relentless horde closed in. It seemed impossible for the Jeddak of Thark to reach the forest before them, yet he moved with a desperate urgency. Green Martians do not flee; they fight to the death. His flight spoke of a motivation deeper than fear—perhaps a newfound love for life, a concept alien to his people who so often court death.

For me, the drive was simple: love. Love for Dejah Thoris.

We reached the forest's edge just as the swiftest of our pursuers—a giant plant-man with claws extended to tear at our throats—leapt forward. He was a hundred yards ahead of the rest.

"Tars Tarkas, climb that great tree brushing the cliff!" I commanded, turning to dispatch the beast.

My calculation was flawed. As I raised my longsword, the creature halted mid-charge. My blade sliced harmlessly through empty air. Before I could react, the plant-man's great tail whipped across the ground with the force of a grizzly bear, knocking me flat.

Instantly, the brute was upon me. Cold, sucking lips fastened to my neck, but I grabbed a writhing tentacle in each hand. Muscular and heavy, the thing was strong, but my agility and the strangling grip I maintained promised victory—if only we had time.

Above us, Tars Tarkas climbed with infinite difficulty. Over his shoulder, I glimpsed the true horror awaiting us: a swarm of Great White Apes. Unlike the green Martians, these creatures walked upright, standing fifteen feet tall. They possessed intermediate arms, close-set eyes, high-set ears, and snouts reminiscent of African gorillas, topped with bristling hair. Their resemblance to humans, coupled with their monstrous size, evoked a primal dread unlike anything else on Barsoom.

With a wave of snarling, screaming rage, they descended upon me. The most hideous sound was the horrid purring of the plant-men. Dozens of fangs and talons sank into my flesh; cold lips sealed onto my arteries. I struggled, freed momentarily, and wielded my sword like a dagger, hacking through the swarm.

At that critical moment, Tars Tarkas dropped from the branches. Back-to-back, we fought the tide of savagery. Again and again, the apes charged; again and again, our blades held. The plant-men's tails lashed with tremendous power, but Tars Tarkas and I, the finest swordsmen Mars had ever known, repelled them.

Yet, numbers overwhelm skill. Step by step, we were driven back until we stood against the giant tree we had chosen for ascent. Charge after charge hammered us until we were forced halfway around the colossal trunk.

Suddenly, Tars Tarkas let out a cry of triumph. "Shelter for one, John Carter!" he exclaimed, pointing to an opening in the tree's base, barely three feet wide. Too small for his bulk, but perfect for me.

"We shall both die outside!" he pleaded. "Take it! You may live to avenge me."

"Then we die together," I replied firmly. "Let me guard the opening while you enter. Then, my smaller frame will allow me to slip in before they stop you."

Fighting furiously, we exchanged broken words. Finally, he yielded.

"It is your way, John Carter, to think last of your own life," he said, a grim smile touching his cruel face. "And to command others, even the greatest Jeddaks." Turning to obey a man less than half his height, he added, "If you fail, know that the cruel Thark, to whom you taught friendship, will come out to die beside you."

"As you will, my friend," I answered. "Quickly now, head first, while I cover your retreat."

He hesitated at the word *retreat*, for a Thark never turns his back on living foes. "Haste, Tars Tarkas!" I urged. "Or we both perish!"

As he forced himself into the tree, the howling pack threw itself at me. My blade flashed, green with plant sap, red with ape blood, cutting through the mass of enemies. Though overwhelmed and dragged down a second time, powerful hands gripped my ankles. In a blur of motion, I was pulled inside the tree's hollow interior.

A brief tug-of-war ensued between Tars Tarkas and a clinging plant-man, but I drove my sword home, piercing the beast's vitals. Panting and wounded, I lay on the floor of the hollow while Tars Tarkas defended the entrance.

For an hour, the horde howled outside, attempting to breach the barrier. Eventually, all but a dozen retreated, leaving us to a siege that surely spelled starvation. As the chaos faded and our eyes adjusted to the semi-darkness, I explored our new home.

The tree was hollow for fifty feet, its floor worn smooth by previous occupants. Looking upward, I saw a faint glow—an opening above. Hope flickered. Above, a rough ladder of horizontal bars spiraled up the shaft, connecting to a narrow passage leading to the outside.

I climbed five hundred feet, reaching the opening onto a large, flat limb. Venturing out was too risky; instead, I returned to tell Tars Tarkas. Working in tandem, we hoisted the ladder higher, securing it against the shaft walls to deny the pursuers access.

When we reached the top, Tars Tarkas waited while I ventured forth. The limb angled toward the cliff, terminating just above a narrow ledge at the mouth of a cave. Balancing precariously on the branch's tip, I saw the scarlet valley five hundred feet below and the golden cliffs five thousand above. This cave was different from the ones visible from the ground; it was lower, accessible via the branch.
