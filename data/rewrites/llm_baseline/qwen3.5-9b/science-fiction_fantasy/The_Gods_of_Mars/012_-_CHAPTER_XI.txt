Dawn broke cold and gray over the prison of Shador, bringing with it the urgent necessity of action. Xodar and I immediately began to formulate our plan of escape. First, I tasked him with sketching upon the stone floor of our cell the most accurate map of the southern polar regions possible using only the crude tools available—a buckler from my harness and the razor-sharp edge of the wondrous gem I had stolen from Sator Throg.

Using this makeshift instrument, I calculated the general bearing of Helium and the distance separating it from the aperture leading to Omean. Next, Xodar drew a detailed chart of Omean itself, clearly marking the location of Shador and the specific breach in the dome that offered access to the outside world. I studied these maps until they were burned permanently into my memory. From Xodar, I also gleaned critical intelligence regarding the habits of the guards patrolling Shador. During their designated sleeping hours, only a single sentinel was active at any given time. He paced a circuitous route around the prison, maintaining a distance of approximately one hundred feet from the structure.

"The sentry's pace is excruciatingly slow," Xodar explained. "It requires nearly ten minutes to complete a single lap. Consequently, for roughly five minutes at a time, each flank of the prison remains entirely unguarded as the guard traverses the opposite side."

"This information," Xodar cautioned, "may prove invaluable *after* we secure our liberty, but it addresses matters secondary to our primary objective."

"We shall secure our liberty," I assured him, a grin spreading across my face. "Leave that to me."

"Then when shall we strike?" he asked.

"On the first night a small vessel is moored near the shores of Shador," I replied.

"But how will you detect a ship's arrival? The windows are far too high for us to see anything."

"Not so, friend Xodar. Witness!"

With a sudden leap, I vaulted onto the iron bars of the window opposite our cell and scanned the exterior landscape. Several small craft and two massive battleships lay anchored mere hundred yards from Shador.

"To-night," I murmured, ready to announce my decision, when without warning, the prison door swung open and a guard strode inside.

Had he spotted me perched on the bars, our chances of survival would have vanished instantly. They would have shackled me, unaware of the incredible agility my Earth-born muscles possessed on Mars. The intruder stood facing the center of the room, his back turned toward me. Above my head, separated by a partition wall, lay the only viable path to freedom.

My sole hope depended on his failure to turn. If he rotated, I was doomed; furthermore, dropping to the floor would likely result in striking him, given his proximity.

"Where is the white man?" the guard barked at Xodar. "Issus demands his presence." He began to pivot, searching the cell.

That was my signal. I scrambled up the iron grating of the window, planting one foot firmly on the sill, then released my grip and launched myself toward the partition top.

"What was that noise?" the guard's deep voice boomed as my metal boot scraped against the stone wall, allowing me to slip silently over. I landed lightly on the floor of the adjacent cell.

"Where is the white slave?" the guard demanded again.

"I know not," Xodar lied smoothly. "He was here moments ago. I am not his keeper—search elsewhere."

The black man grunted unintelligibly and moved to unlock the door of a neighboring cell. Listening intently, I confirmed the latch clicking shut behind him. Seizing the moment, I leaped back over the partition and dropped into my own cell beside the startled Xodar.

"Do you now comprehend our method of escape?" I whispered.

"I see how *you* might escape," Xodar admitted, "but I remain ignorant of how I shall cross these walls. Certainty dictates I cannot bounce over them as you do."

We heard the guard pacing from cell to cell until his patrol concluded, at which point he returned to ours. Upon seeing me, his eyes bulged in sheer astonishment.

"By the shell of my first ancestor!" he roared. "Where have you been?"

"I have been imprisoned since you placed me here yesterday," I retorted calmly. "I was in this very room when you entered. Perhaps you should examine your vision."

He stared at me, a mixture of rage and relief clouding his features.

"Come," he ordered. "Issus commands your presence."

He escorted me outside the prison, leaving Xodar behind. Outside, we joined several other guards and the red Martian youth who inhabited another cell within Shador.

The journey to the Temple of Issus, undertaken the previous day, was repeated. The guards kept the red boy and myself strictly separated, denying us the chance to resume our interrupted conversation. Yet, the image of the youth haunted my thoughts. Where had I encountered him before? Every line of his face, his carriage, his speech, and his gestures radiated a strange familiarity. I was convinced I knew him, yet logically, I had never laid eyes on him prior to this moment.

Upon reaching the gardens of Issus, we were diverted away from the temple proper. The path wound through enchanted parks until we arrived at a colossal wall soaring a hundred feet into the air. Massive gates opened onto a small plain, bordered by the same resplendent forests I had witnessed at the base of the Golden Cliffs.

Crowds of blacks strolled in the same direction as our escorts, intermingled with my former allies: the plant-men and the great white apes. These brutish creatures moved through the throng like pampered pets. Should they obstruct anyone, the blacks shoved them roughly aside or struck them with the flat of their swords, causing the animals to slink away in terror.

Soon, we reached our destination: a vast amphitheater situated at the far edge of the plain, half a mile beyond the garden walls. Through a massive arched gateway, the blacks poured in to claim their seats, while our guards guided us to a smaller entrance near one end of the structure.

Passing through, we entered an enclosure beneath the seating area, where numerous other prisoners were herded together under guard. Some wore irons, but most appeared too terrified by the presence of their captors to consider escape.

During the trek from Shador, I had no opportunity to speak with my fellow-prisoner, but now that we were safely within the barred paddock and the guards' vigilance relaxed, I approached the red Martian youth, feeling a peculiar magnetic pull toward him.

"What is the purpose of this gathering?" I asked him. "Are we to fight for the amusement of the First Born, or is there something darker awaiting us?"

"It is a component of the monthly rites of Issus," he explained. "Here, black men wash the sins from their souls using the blood of outsiders. If a black man falls in combat, it signifies his disloyalty to Issus—an unforgivable sin. However, if he survives the contest, he is deemed acquitted of the charges that necessitated this ritual sentencing.

"The forms of combat vary. Groups may be pitted against equals or double the number of blacks; individually, we may face wild beasts or renowned black warriors."

"And if we emerge victorious?" I pressed. "Does that grant freedom?"

He laughed bitterly.

"Freedom? Nonsense. The only freedom awaits us is death. No one who enters the domains of the First Born ever leaves. If we prove capable fighters, we are granted frequent bouts. If we lack martial prowess—" He shrugged. "Eventually, we perish in the arena."

"You have fought often?" I asked.

"Extremely frequently," he replied. "It is my sole joy. Over the course of nearly a year of Issus's rites, I have claimed the lives of some hundred black devils. My mother would be immensely proud if she knew how faithfully I uphold the traditions of my father's valor."

"Your father must have been a formidable warrior!" I exclaimed. "I have known most of Barsoom's warriors throughout my travels; surely I recognized him. Who was he?"

"My father was..."

"Enough!" a guard's rough voice interrupted. "To the slaughter with you!"

We were roughly hustled up a steep incline toward the chambers far below, which opened directly onto the arena. Like all structures on Barsoom, the amphitheater was constructed within a massive excavation. Only the highest tiers, forming a low wall around the pit, rose above ground level; the arena itself lay deep underground.

Beneath the lowest row of seats sat a series of barred cages aligned with the arena floor. We were herded into these, though unfortunately, my youthful friend was not assigned to the cage alongside me. Directly opposite my confinement stood the throne of Issus. There squatted the horrific deity, surrounded by a hundred slave maidens glittering in jeweled attire. Brilliant fabrics of vibrant hues and exotic patterns formed the cushions upon which they reclined around her.

Flanking the throne on four sides, several feet below its level, stood three dense rows of heavily armed soldiers, shoulder to shoulder. Before them stood the high dignitaries of this mock heaven—gleaming blacks adorned with precious stones, gold circles marking their rank upon their foreheads.
