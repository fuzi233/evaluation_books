The distance to the Pyramid was measured not in miles, but in the shrinking radius of my despair. My home, that mountain of sweet refuge, loomed vast against the eternal night, a silhouette of impossible scale. Yet, the weight of the girl in my arms—the only warmth in a universe turning cold—anchored me to the ground. Despair had a grip on my chest, iron-tight, rendering the magnitude of the sanctuary irrelevant. All that mattered was the safe harbor of the Circle, and she.

I moved forward, a single vector cutting through the static. A hollow in the terrain glowed with the heat of a fire-hole. From that darkness, something crawled upward. It emerged as a giant, hair matted, limbs thick with the texture of old roots. Its face turned to me, then shifted into motion. It approached, hands thrust forward, eager and terrible. In the flickering light of the hole, I saw the claws: monstrous, brutish tools designed to rend flesh like paper.

My instinct screamed to flee, but the cost of retreat was higher than the cost of death. I dropped to the earth, accepting the inevitable collision. Rage, cold and sharp, surged through me. I leaped, a furious arc aimed at the giant's throat. My blade struck, but the creature sidestepped with the fluid grace of a predator. Before I could recover, its massive arm swept out from the shadow, snatching my helmet from my head and hurling me backward. I rolled across the dirt, bruised and shaken, but alive.

I rose instantly. The Diskos roared in my hands, a wheel of blinding energy. I swung, connecting high on the giant's torso. The weapon bit deep, glutting itself in the monster's flesh. The giant fell, a toppled tower of muscle and bone, legs standing rigid in the firelight while a fountain of blood erupted into the night.

No pause. I sprinted to the girl, pulling her tight against my chest, and vaulted over the corpse. Behind me, the land erupted in a chorus of spiritual cries—a million voices rising in recognition, casting their love and awe onto my path. For a brief, electric moment, the isolation broke.

Then, the landscape resumed its hunger. Strange shapes detached themselves from the rocks and bushes. I struck them down with the Diskos, a blur of motion, unaware of their nature. Soon, the entire country seemed to crawl with foul life. Every step was a battle; every breath tasted of impending doom. The end of our lives was no longer a possibility; it was a certainty closing in. The air filled with grim roars, the low thrum of evil, and once, the distinct thunder of giants running.

To the southeast, the baying of the Night-Hounds grew louder, a rhythmic countdown to extinction. I knew then that my strength was insufficient. Just as the hope drained from my lungs, a strange blue flash tore through the upper atmosphere, striking the earth below. Again, and again, perhaps a dozen times. Accompanying the flashes was a crackling sound, sharper than thunder, piercing the ancient silence. These were the signals. The humans were fighting. They were coming for me.

The waking of the land was a violent awakening. The monsters roared, the great forces stirred, and a dreadful laughter echoed from the lost east, a sound that belonged to a nightmare. The hounds bayed constantly now, a pack of hellhounds closing the gap. I was alone, save for the dying girl in my arms, looking vainly for the Hundred Thousand prepared to aid me. There was nothing but shifting shadows and the movement of monstrous life. The hounds were minutes away. Death was inches away.

I stopped running and began to sprint. The Pyramid was close; the glow of the protective Circle was visible, broken only by the encroaching gloom. A desperate hope ignited: I could reach the safety of the Circle with her.

The baying intensified, doubling in horror. To lose her here, so near the sanctuary, was unbearable. The great mountain of my home seemed to rise right before me, yet still two miles distant. I cried out, a sound swallowed by the wind, asking why no help came. The hounds were half a mile away, scenting me.

Suddenly, the anguish of the millions washed over me. Their emotional noise was clear in my spirit. They saw my desperation, my futile calls. A great, sweet spiritual force surrounded me, born of their shared understanding and love. They understood I was at the edge of hope. The hounds were upon me.

Then, the earth shook. The beat of the Earth-Current returned, a frantic rhythm signaling the humans' desperate intervention. To my left, a vast pack of hounds charged, heads low, bodies the size of horses, appearing and vanishing in the strobe-light of their approach. We were dead in a minute unless they acted.

I planted my feet. Running was useless. I stared from the beasts to the Pyramid, and back to the beasts. Two hundred fathoms away lay the pack; hundreds of them. As I fixed my gaze on the Pyramid, a monstrous flame burst from its sealed base, rushing downward. It slammed into the land where the hounds ran, blotting out the night in a brilliance so intense I saw nothing else but the shining, dreadful glory of the fire. The blast wave hit me, a heat that threatened to wither me where I stood.

I realized then: the humans had unleashed the Earth-Current. Thunder rolled across the land, tearing the air and splitting the earth. The roaring of the monsters was drowned out by the cataclysm. Rocks hurled themselves in every direction with deafening crashes. It was a mercy I survived the debris field.

In an instant, the humans severed the current, reclaiming control. Silence returned, absolute and dark, save for the lingering smoke and the distant hum of destruction. The daze lifted quickly. I scanned the horizon, checking for threats. For a time, there was nothing but the grim laughter from the dead east.

I ran, staring upward at the Pyramid. Its light was fading, growing dimmer with every passing second. Initially, I blamed my dazzled eyes, but the dimming was real. The great expenditure of the Earth-Current to save us had depleted the reserve. If the current failed completely, the entire population of the Refuge would perish. The Masters knew this; they could not restore the flow without risking total annihilation. This realization chilled me, fueling a fresh desperation.

I expected the Hundred Thousand to arrive, but they did not. The land groaned anew, emitting new, peculiar noises. Living things crept between me and the light of the Circle. The final stretch would require a bitter fight. I gripped the Diskos, swinging it free, and pressed on.

My spirit sensed a warning. I looked up, hoping the Master Monstruwacan would signal danger via the Set Speech. Instead, there was only an upward stillness, a dimming of the Pyramid's lights. Later, I learned the observation towers and the pyramid's machinery had all failed for nearly an hour due to the strain of the rescue attempt. Death had come close to claiming the millions.

Yet, my spirit was warned by the turmoil of the masses and the vague mental call of the Master. I moved warier, scanning every angle. Suddenly, a pale circle hovered overhead, steady and quiet, drifting over us. It was one of the Powers of Holiness, a shield between our souls and the dread power seeking our destruction. Fear receded; I trusted the holy force and continued, running with caution.

Within four hundred paces of the Circle, the light was already dimming. Was the guard still effective? Anxiety tightened my chest. I ran swift, wary, terrified.

From a patch of darkness, three beast-men rose, growling. The first was too close for the Diskos. I drove the haft into its skull, then leaped aside, swinging the weapon with a madness that was also chilling. The girl was secure in my arm, a babe in my grasp. I met the remaining two as they charged, cutting with quick, light strokes. Anger focused my heart into a place of cold, deadly intent. My judgment became brutal and precise. They fell like mice. Unharmed, I waited.

Immediately, a shout of wonder and welcome erupted from within the Circle. Men in grey armor stood inside, but they did not advance.

I understood then why the Hundred Thousand had held back. Beyond the Circle, monstrous black mounds swayed with a force of strange life, inducing a primal horror. Any human venturing outside would be destroyed in the spirit, lost utterly. Sacrifice was useless; the dead offered no aid.

Shouts came from the Hundred Thousand, urging me to haste. I ran with all my strength. I looked up at the Circle of Holiness, drifting steadily above us. We were saved.

One hundred paces remained. Brutal things awaited. From the shadows, a herd of squat, brutish men surged forward, grabbing at me, trying to tear the girl from my arm. Success seemed imminent; I could not free myself while guarding her and wielding the Diskos simultaneously.

I kicked with my metal boots, driving them back, wrenching free in a chaotic spin. I leaped backward, and the herd pursued, a tide of horrid brutes chasing me toward the light.
