Sally's gaze wandered along the table, her eyes meeting the warm smiles of her guests. She felt a sense of contentment wash over her, a feeling that had been building throughout the evening. The initial awkwardness, caused by her brother Fillmore's ill-fitting evening waistcoat, had given way to laughter and conversation. The residents of Mrs. Meecher's boarding house, a mix of transients and permanent fixtures, were finally at ease.

As she listened in on the discussions at her end of the table, Sally couldn't help but feel a twinge of amusement. Her guests were busily debating what she should do with her inheritance, a topic that had been on everyone's mind since the rumors of her wealth had begun circulating. With the amount at her disposal being a mystery, their suggestions had taken on a grandiose quality.

Augustus Bartlett, a man of finance with a penchant for decisive language, leaned forward in his chair. "If I were you, Sally," he began, his voice low and measured, "I'd invest a substantial portion in a reliable bond issue. We've recently launched a promising opportunity that I think would be wise to consider." His words were laced with the confidence of someone who had made a name for himself in the world of high finance.

Elsa Doland, seated beside him, raised an eyebrow. "I think you should buy a theater, Sally, and showcase some exceptional performances." Her words were met with a mixture of nods and murmurs of agreement.

A quiet young man across the table spoke up, his deep voice a gentle counterpoint to the lively discussion. "I wouldn't risk a single penny on the heavyweight championship. I've heard that Battling Tuke has been compromised and will likely concede to Benny Whistler."

As the conversation continued, Sally noticed that the room had begun to divide into two camps. One group, led by Augustus Bartlett, advocated for a cautious approach, suggesting a combination of bonds and real estate investments. The other, more adventurous faction, was drawn to the prospect of betting on the upcoming boxing match, convinced that they could secure a favorable return on their investment.

Just as it seemed that the debate would continue indefinitely, Sally interjected, her voice calm and authoritative. "I think there's been a misunderstanding. My inheritance is actually much smaller than you all seem to think – a mere twenty-five thousand dollars."

The room fell silent, the sudden realization of the actual sum hitting her guests like a cold wind.
