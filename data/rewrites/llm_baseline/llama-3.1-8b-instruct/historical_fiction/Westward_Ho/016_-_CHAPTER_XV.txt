As the days passed, the villagers of Stow remained abuzz with speculation regarding Rose Salterne's disappearance. The local miller, in particular, faced scathing accusations from the community, with many believing he and his wife had driven the young woman to despair. The miller's defense – that Rose had merely "gone off" with a suitor – was met with skepticism, while the women of Stow whispered among themselves about the possibility of a tragic accident.

The facts of the case were few and far between, however, with only a small footprint discovered beneath Rose's bedroom window and a trail of missing linens. The only lead worth pursuing was the faint scent left behind, which the village keepers attempted to follow using the keen senses of their bloodhounds. The dogs, led by the stalwart hound, followed the scent up the lane and onto the downs above, eventually veering towards Marslandmouth.

It was there that the group arrived at the doorstep of Lucy Passmore, a widow whose own fortunes had taken a turn for the better in recent times. With her late husband's passing, Lucy had found herself free from the shackles of her marriage, and her reputation as a skilled healer had earned her a certain degree of respect within the community. She had even managed to secure a favorable audience with Lady Grenville, who had taken pity on her plight and granted her a pension.

However, Lucy's troubles were far from over. As the bloodhounds closed in on her doorstep, she sprang into action, concocting a tale of woe to deflect suspicion. Claiming her husband had been taken by Jesuit priests, she begged Lady Grenville to intervene on her behalf, warning of the dangers posed by these "Popish skulkers." The ruse worked, and Lucy's fortunes continued to rise, but at what cost? As the villagers departed, the sound of her goats crying out for milk echoed through the glen, while the bloodhounds, undeterred, continued their pursuit down to the edge of the sea.

"There they've gone," the keeper exclaimed, as the dogs paused at the water's edge. "We've lost our quarry, keper. It seems we're barking up the wrong tree." The bloodhounds, however, remained steadfast, their ears perked up and their noses twitching as they gazed out into the distance, as if searching for a hint of their elusive quarry.
