As I descended from the rocky precipice, the darkness enveloped me like a damp shroud. The scent of saltwater and ozone hung heavy in the air, and I felt the fine hairs on my arms prickle with every snap of the wind. I had no clear recollection of how I arrived here, only that I was accompanied by a man I'd come to trust – Betteredge. His presence was a comfort, a reminder that I wasn't alone in this forsaken place. We walked back towards the house, the silence between us punctuated only by the crunch of gravel beneath our feet.

We entered the sitting room, and I felt a sense of relief wash over me as I shed the damp nightgown, leaving it in Betteredge's hands. The room was a sanctuary, a haven from the turmoil that churned inside me. Betteredge mixed a drink, combining the fiery spirit with icy water from the well, and handed it to me. I felt the liquid burn its way down my throat, a sensation both painful and invigorating. Under normal circumstances, it would have left me reeling, but today it seemed to steady my nerves.

As I sipped the drink, Betteredge began to speak, his words dripping with conviction. "There's one thing certain, Mr. Franklin," he said, his eyes locked on mine. "He's a liar, to begin with." But his words didn't strike a chord within me. I knew I was innocent of any wrongdoing, yet the evidence against me was overwhelming. The paint on the nightgown, the name – all these pointed to my guilt.

Betteredge's eyes sparkled with a knowing glint as he urged me to take another sip of the grog. "Facts?" he repeated, his voice dripping with sarcasm. "Take a drop more, Mr. Franklin, and you'll get over the weakness of believing in facts! Foul play, sir!" His words sent a shiver down my spine. I couldn't shake the feeling that something was off, that there was more to this than met the eye.
