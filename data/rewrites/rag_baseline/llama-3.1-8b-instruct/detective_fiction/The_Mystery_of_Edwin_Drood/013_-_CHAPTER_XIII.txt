The dormitories at Miss Twinkleton's establishment had grown quiet, the Christmas recess a somber reminder of the impending end to the term. As the days passed, a subtle relaxation of discipline had crept in – clandestine suppers in the bedrooms, marmalade served on curlpaper plates, and the faint scent of cowslip wine lingering in the air. In the evenings, the housemaids were rewarded with scraps of ribbon and tattered shoes for their silence, while the girls donned their most vibrant costumes, their laughter and music weaving a fragile thread of joy amidst the monotony.

Miss Ferdinand's bold solo on the comb-and-curlpaper had been a highlight, though it ended abruptly when she succumbed to giggles beneath the pillows of two stern-faced executioners. Boxes began to appear in the bedrooms, and the attendants scurried about, packing and unpacking with an intensity that belied the triviality of their tasks. Confidences were exchanged in hushed tones, whispers of a golden youth expected to visit, though some – like Miss Giggles – professed indifference, her expression a mask of disinterest.

As the night wore on, the girls' eyelids grew heavy, and one by one, they surrendered to sleep. The compact to stay awake until the final moment was broken, and the room fell silent, except for the occasional creak of a floorboard or the soft hum of a lullaby.

The next day, at noon, the students gathered in Miss Twinkleton's drawing room, the globes shrouded in brown Holland. Glasses of white wine and plates of pound cake lay on the table, a gesture of hospitality. Miss Twinkleton stood before them, her words a measured cadence: 'Ladies, another year has passed, and we find ourselves at the threshold of a new cycle. Let us cherish the memories of our time together, and hold dear the promise of reunion. As we depart, may our hearts remain connected, bound by the ties of friendship and the anticipation of home.' Her voice trailed off, and the room fell silent, the students lost in their thoughts, their minds a canvas of hopes and fears.
