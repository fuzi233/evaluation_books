In the radiant glow of a June morning, the sun-drenched square pulsed with the collective curiosity of its inhabitants. People from all walks of life had gathered before the ancient house, its walls bearing the faded marks of bygone eras, like a testament to the countless stories etched upon its red brick facade. The narrow entrance-alley, once a threshold to mystery, was now a scene of controlled chaos, as two constables stood guard, holding back the tide of onlookers. Women hovered at the periphery, their whispers carried away by the gentle breeze as they exchanged furtive glances with the drawn red blinds of the old house. Behind closed doors, children played, their laughter and shouts a distant counterpoint to the murmurs of the adults.

A portly figure, his nose a sharp and knowing instrument, held court with a small group of listeners, regaling them with a tale that seemed to grow more fantastical with each retelling. Nearby, a shorter, plumper man, his eyes wide with an unsettling intensity, attempted to steal the show with a story of his own, one that revolved around a key and a mysterious transaction. As he spoke, his words hung in the air, tantalizing and incomplete, yet unable to capture the attention of the crowd.

"...and I recall it was revealed to me, that very afternoon," the long-nosed man declared, his voice weaving a spell of intrigue. "I was going about my daily routine, just as Constable Saunders is doing now, when they emerged. I saw her face, plain as day... white as alabaster, without a single blemish. Don't try to deceive me, I assure you!"

A curious onlooker interrupted, his question a gentle prod, "What was it like, Mr. Barrett?"

The long-nosed man's response was a masterclass in restraint, his words dripping with an air of reverence. "It was akin to the face of the woman our Lord addressed, 'Woman, doth any man accuse thee?'—a face as pale as the driven snow. Don't even think of trying to dispute me on this...
