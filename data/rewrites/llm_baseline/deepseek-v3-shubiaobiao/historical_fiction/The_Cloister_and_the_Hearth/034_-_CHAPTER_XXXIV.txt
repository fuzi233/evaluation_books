A rush of footsteps echoed through the inn as half a dozen rough but welcoming hands stretched out, seizing Denys and Gerard in firm grips.

**"You've saved our lives tonight, lads,"** Denys declared, his voice thick with relief. **"You've saved us."**

The scene before them was one of stark contrasts—torchlight flickering against breastplates, bronzed faces of archers beside the pallid, bound thieves, and the fallen giant sprawled in his own blood, abandoned without ceremony. Gerard moved among the soldiers, clasping each man’s hand with shining eyes. In return, they embraced him, pressing rough kisses to his cheeks. He returned the gesture, then turned to a young archer, his own age, with a weary smile.

**"Good soldier, do me a kindness—keep watch over me. A strange drowsiness weighs me down. Let no one slit my throat while I sleep, for pity’s sake."**

The archer laughed, thinking it a jest, but Gerard had already slumped into a deep slumber. Denys watched, puzzled, but held his tongue—it suited his purpose.

Two soldiers poked at the Abbot’s corpse with their boots, marveling.

Denys scoffed at first, but hesitated. *If Gerard wakes and babbles about divine intervention, I’ll look the fool.* But with the boy unconscious, he grinned.

**"Well, lads, I ran him through, but others swarmed me. Needed my blade back, so—foot to his gut, a good yank, and sent him flying! Died midair, I’d wager. Made quite the entrance, eh?"**

The archers chuckled, but one grumbled, **"Easy to say ‘let’s drink’ when there’s no drink to be had."**

Denys smirked. **"Then follow me. My nose sniffs out wine like a hound on a hare."**

Torch in hand, they descended into a dank cellar, the air thick with mold and saltpetre crusting the walls.

**"Ah! That’s the smell of good vintage,"** Denys declared. Then his eyes narrowed. **"What’s that in the corner? A heap of rags? No—a man!"**

The torchlight revealed a trembling figure—the innkeeper, pale as death.

**"Up, coward!"** barked an archer. **"The thieves are bound, and we’re thirsty for your wine."**

**"Th-they’re caught?"** the man stammered, knees wobbling. **"Wine? At once, sirs!"**

Denys stepped forward, blocking his path. **"Hold. He’s one of them."**

The landlord’s face twisted. **"That lying wench! Curse her!"**

Denys’s eyes gleamed. **"Now why curse her, unless her words struck true?"**

The man faltered, but the archers shrugged. **"Let the burghers judge him. For now, let’s drink."**

Denys wasn’t done. **"Then hold him. A prisoner can pour wine, if his hands aren’t tied."**

The innkeeper, desperate, babbled about unpaid rent, but the soldiers only laughed. As they moved to leave, Denys halted them.

He prodded a heap of debris—bones clattered to the floor.

**"Nothing but kitchen scraps,"** the landlord insisted, sweat beading on his brow.

Denys lifted one, examining it in the torchlight. **"A man’s shinbone."**

The innkeeper’s face turned ashen. **"S-surely oxen—"**

**"Then why shake like a leaf?"** Denys’s voice turned icy. He dug deeper, uncovering more—skulls, finger bones, then, with a choked cry, a golden tress of woman’s hair.

**"By God,"** he whispered, clutching it. **"Had you as many lives as hairs here, I’d take them all."**

Seizing the landlord, he forced him to his knees, grinding his face into the bones. **"Smell your work, butcher!"**

The man shrieked, then whimpered like a beaten cur.

With a bowstring, Denys threaded a skull and hung it around the wretch’s neck. **"Wear your trophy, carrion."**

Kicking him into the kitchen, they found the alderman taking statements. The burgher recoiled at the ghastly sight—the landlord, bloodied and collared with death.

**"Bind him,"** the alderman ordered coldly. **"He’s the blackest of the lot."**

The man wailed, begging for the skull’s removal.

**"It suits you,"** the alderman spat. **"A fitting crest for your foul heart."**

As constables hauled the prisoners away, the clerk, a fussy little man, chirped, **"Now, the hand! Where’s the hand?"**

**"Above, nailed to the door,"** Denys replied grimly.

The clerk scurried off, only to be bowled over by fleeing constables—terrified by the glowing corpse upstairs.

By dawn, the inn stood silent, justice served. Only a lone constable remained, watching over the sleeping Gerard, while Denys kept vigil, his hand resting on the golden lock of hair—a promise of vengeance yet to come.
