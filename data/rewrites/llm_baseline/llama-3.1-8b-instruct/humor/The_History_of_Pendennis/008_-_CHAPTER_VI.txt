As Cicero and Euripides continued to captivate Mr. Pen's imagination, the young man's thoughts strayed further and further away from the quiet pursuits of his tutor, Mr. Smirke. Honest Mr. Smirke, however, found himself enjoying an uneventful period with his pupil, save for the occasional glimpse of Mr. Pen's clandestine excursions to Prior's Lane. It was during these stolen moments that Mr. Pen's ardor for Rebecca reached a fever pitch, and he would often find himself rushing to Chatteris to consult the man who tended to the stables, seeking remedies for his beloved's ailing horse and, on occasion, the hire of another steed.

Under the guise of studying a Greek play, Mr. Pen would frequently sneak away to catch the Competitor down coach, spending a couple of hours in Chatteris before returning on the Rival coach, which departed for London at ten at night. Mr. Smirke's naivety almost led to a breach of confidentiality when Mrs. Pendennis inquired if they had spent the previous evening engrossed in reading, but Mr. Pen's timely warning, administered by a discreetly placed boot-heel, prevented the curate from revealing his pupil's secret.

Their conversations on the tender subject were, of course, numerous. It was a pastime both captivating and melancholic to witness two men enthralled by love, with the need for a trusted confidant and keeper of secrets. When informed, under the most solemn oaths of discretion, of Mr. Pen's condition of mind, Mr. Smirke voiced his trepidation, cautioning that it would be his duty to inform Mr. Pen's mother should the attachment prove unworthy or illicit – a prospect that filled him with dread, lest it lead to a quarrel and a loss of the affection he cherished.

"Unworthy, illicit!" Mr. Pen exclaimed, his indignation rising in response to the curate's query. "She is as chaste as she is lovely; I would surrender my very heart to no other woman. I keep the matter hidden within my family, due to reasons of weighty import that I am not at liberty to disclose. Any man who casts aspersions upon her honor or mine incurs my ire, and I will not tolerate it."
