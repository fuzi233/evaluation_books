As I rose from my slumber, the silence enveloped me like a shroud. The memory of her departure still lingered, leaving an ache in my chest that threatened to consume me whole. I had brought her back from the very brink of death, and now she had vanished, leaving me with nothing but the echoes of our time together. I felt the weight of my actions bearing down upon me, and I knew I couldn't stay still. I had to find her, to make things right.

I spotted her walking swiftly across the lush green expanse, her figure growing smaller with each step. The river sparkled in the fading light, its gentle voice whispering secrets to the trees. I took a deep breath, feeling my strength begin to return, and set out to follow her. The last visit from the white leech and the painful words of the woman still lingered, but I pressed on, driven by a sense of purpose.

"Why must it end like this?" I whispered to myself as I walked. Her angry eyes haunted me, their memory etched into my mind like a scar. I understood her resentment – who wouldn't feel that way after being forced back into the world of the living? But why should she loathe me so? Hadn't I tended to her with the care of a father to his child? Hadn't I given her life, and in doing so, offered her a chance at a new beginning?

As I pondered these questions, I watched her ascend a gentle slope, her movements steady and purposeful. I quickened my pace, my legs carrying me forward with newfound ease. My veins seemed to fill with a fresh, golden light, and I felt my spirit soar. I gained on her, my footsteps quiet on the soft earth.

She didn't turn, didn't glance back, her gaze fixed on some point ahead. I closed the distance between us, my heart pounding in anticipation. As I drew near, she turned, her face a mask of serenity, her eyes shining like stars in the gathering dusk. Her breathing was slow and deep, her skin a radiant, moonlit glow. It was as if she had transcended the mortal realm, and I felt small and insignificant in her presence.

"Why do you follow me?" she asked, her voice low and measured, like a gentle stream.

"I've lived on the promise of your smile," I replied, my words barely above a whisper. "I need to see it again."
