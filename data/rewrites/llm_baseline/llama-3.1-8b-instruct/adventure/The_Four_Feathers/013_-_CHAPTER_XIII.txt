As Ethne stood at the window of her family's townhouse in Hill Street, the warm sunlight streaming through the open glass highlighted the delicate petals of the roses blooming in the flowerboxes outside. Mrs. Adair sat at the tea table, sipping her tea and glancing occasionally at the clock on the mantle. The air was filled with the sweet scent of fresh bread wafting from the kitchen, where their cook was preparing the evening meal. The room was abuzz with the gentle hum of conversation and the soft clinking of fine china.

Just as they were settling into their quiet afternoon routine, a jingle of horse bells pierced the air, followed by the rumble of wheels on cobblestone. Mrs. Adair's head snapped up, and she set her cup down with a slight clink. "That's a cab," she said, her voice barely above a whisper.

Ethne leaned forward, her eyes scanning the street below. "It's not stopping here," she said, her voice tinged with a hint of disappointment. As the sound of the cab faded into the distance, Mrs. Adair glanced at the clock once more. "Colonel Durrance is late," she remarked, her brow furrowed in concern.

Ethne's gaze lingered on the empty street, her expression a mixture of anxiety and longing. Mrs. Adair noticed the tension in her posture and couldn't help but wonder what was troubling her friend. "Why didn't you go to the station to meet him?" she asked gently.

Ethne hesitated before answering. "He might think I've come because I pity him, and I don't want him to think that. He has his servant with him." Her eyes drifted back to the window, where the flowers seemed to glow in the fading light. "You remember the telegram I showed you?" she asked, her voice low and urgent.

Mrs. Adair's face softened as she recalled the message from Lieutenant Calder, announcing Colonel Durrance's blindness. "Yes, of course," she replied. "I promised not to mention it to anyone." But Ethne's next words took her aback. "I want you to promise never to mention it to Colonel Durrance himself. I don't want him to know that I received it."
