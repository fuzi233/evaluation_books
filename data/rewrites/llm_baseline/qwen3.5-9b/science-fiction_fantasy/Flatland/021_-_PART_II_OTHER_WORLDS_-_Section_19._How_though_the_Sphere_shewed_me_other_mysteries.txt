Though the Sphere had unveiled countless mysteries of Spaceland to me, my hunger for understanding remained unsated. Yet, fate intervened abruptly. Witnessing my poor brother being led away to imprisonment, I attempted to leap from the balcony into the Council Chamber, driven by a desperate urge to intercede for him or at least bid him farewell. But my body refused to obey; I possessed no volition of my own. I was entirely dependent on the will of my Guide. In gloomy tones, the Sphere commanded, "Heed not thy brother; perhaps thou shalt have ample time hereafter to condole with him. Follow me."

Once more, we ascended into the void.

"Hitherto," the Sphere declared, "I have shown you nothing but Plane Figures and their interiors. Now, I must introduce you to Solids and reveal the architecture upon which they are built. Observe this multitude of movable square cards. See how I place one upon another—not, as you might assume, northward of the other, but *on* the other. Now a second, now a third. Thus, I construct a Solid by stacking squares parallel to one another. Now the Solid is complete; it is as high as it is long and broad, and we name it a Cube."

"Pardon me, my Lord," I replied, trembling. "But to my eye, the appearance resembles an irregular figure whose interior is laid bare to view. In truth, I perceive no Solid, but merely a Plane—such as we infer in Flatland—but distorted into an irregularity that suggests a monstrous criminal. The sight is painful to my eyes."

"True," said the Sphere. "It appears to you as a Plane because you lack the faculty to interpret light, shade, and perspective. Just as in Flatland a Hexagon appears as a straight line to one devoid of the Art of Sight Recognition, so too does a Solid appear flattened to you. But in reality, it is a Solid, a truth you shall soon grasp through the sense of Feeling."

He guided me toward the Cube. Immediately, I realized this marvel was no mere plane. It was undeniably a Solid, endowed with six flat sides and eight terminal points known as solid angles. I recalled the Sphere's earlier teaching: that just such a creature is formed when a Square moves through Space parallel to itself. A surge of joy washed over me; to think that something as insignificant as I could be considered the progenitor of such an illustrious offspring!

Yet, the concepts of "light," "shade," and "perspective" still eluded full comprehension. Without hesitation, I voiced my confusion. To recount the Sphere's explanation in detail would be tedious to an inhabitant of Space who already possesses this knowledge. Suffice it to say that through his lucid instructions, the shifting positions of objects and light sources, and by allowing me to feel various objects—and even his own sacred person—he finally clarified everything. I could now distinctly differentiate between a Circle and a Sphere, and between a Plane Figure and a Solid.

This moment marked the climax, the paradise, of my strange and eventful history. From this point forward, I must relate the story of my miserable fall—a fall most tragic, yet surely undeserved. Why should the thirst for knowledge be ignited only to be extinguished by disappointment and punishment? My will recoils from the painful task of recalling my humiliation, yet, like a second Prometheus, I shall endure this agony and worse if only I can stir within the hearts of Planar and Solid humanity a spirit of rebellion against the conceit that limits our dimensions to two, three, or any finite number short of infinity.

Away with all personal considerations! Let me proceed to the end, as I began, free from digression or anticipation, following the plain path of dispassionate history. The exact facts, the precise words—burned indelibly into my brain—shall be recorded without a single iota of alteration. Let my readers judge between me and Destiny.

The Sphere would gladly have continued his lessons, indoctrinating me in the conformations of all regular solids: cylinders, cones, pyramids, pentahedrons, hexahedrons, dodecahedrons, and spheres. But I dared to interrupt him. Not that I was weary of knowledge; quite the opposite, I thirsted for deeper, fuller draughts than he offered.

"Pardon me," I said, "O Thou whom I must no longer address as the Perfection of All Beauty. Let me beg thee to vouchsafe thy servant a sight of thine interior."

**SPHERE:** My what?

**ME:** Thy interior: thy stomach, thy intestines.

**SPHERE:** Whence this ill-timed impertinence? And what do you mean by claiming I am no longer the Perfection of All Beauty?

**ME:** My Lord, your own wisdom has taught me to aspire to One even greater, more beautiful, and closer to Perfection than yourself. As you, superior to all Flatland forms, combine many circles into one, so doubtless there exists One above you who combines many spheres into a Supreme Existence, surpassing even the solids of Spaceland. Even as we, now in Space, look down upon Flatland and see the insides of all things, so surely there lies above us a higher, purer region. Whither you surely intend to lead me—O Thou whom I shall always call, across all dimensions, my Priest, Philosopher, and Friend—some yet more spacious space, a more dimensionable dimensionality. From that vantage point, we shall gaze together upon the revealed interiors of solid things, where your own intestines, and those of your kin the Spheres, will lie exposed to the view of the poor wandering exile from Flatland, to whom so much has already been granted.

**SPHERE:** Pooh! Nonsense! Enough of this trifling! Time is short, and much remains to be done before you are fit to proclaim the Gospel of Three Dimensions to your blind, benighted countrymen in Flatland.

**ME:** Nay, gracious Teacher, deny me not what I know is within your power. Grant me but one glimpse of your interior, and I shall be satisfied forever, remaining henceforth your docile pupil, your unemancipable slave, ready to receive all your teachings and to feast upon the words falling from your lips.

**SPHERE:** Well, then, to content and silence you, let me state plainly: I would show you what you wish if I could. Would you have me turn my stomach inside out to oblige you?

**ME:** But my Lord has shown me the intestines of all my countrymen in the Land of Two Dimensions by taking me into the Land of Three. What is more difficult now than to take your servant on a second journey into the blessed region of the Fourth Dimension? There, I shall look down with you upon this land of Three Dimensions, seeing the inside of every three-dimensional house, the secrets of the solid earth, the treasures of the mines in Spaceland, and the intestines of every solid living creature—even of the noble and adorable Spheres.

**SPHERE:** But where is this land of Four Dimensions?

**ME:** I know not; but doubtless my Teacher knows.

**SPHERE:** Not I. There is no such land. The very idea of it is utterly inconceivable.

**ME:** Not inconceivable to me, and therefore still less inconceivable to my Master. Nay, I despair not that, even here in this region of Three Dimensions, your Lordship's art may render the Fourth Dimension visible to me. Just as in the Land of Two Dimensions, my Teacher sought in vain to open the eyes of his blind servant to the invisible presence of a Third Dimension, though I saw it not, so surely there exists a Fourth Dimension, perceived by the inner eye of thought. That it must exist, my Lord has taught me. Can he have forgotten what he imparted?

In One Dimension, did not a moving Point produce a Line with *two* terminal points? In Two Dimensions, did not a moving Line produce a Square with *four* terminal points? In Three Dimensions, did not a moving Square produce—that blessed Being, a Cube—with *eight* terminal points? And in Four Dimensions, shall not a moving Cube—alas for Analogy, and alas for the Progress of Truth if it fails here—produce a still more divine organization with *sixteen* terminal points? Behold the infallible confirmation of the series: 2, 4, 8, 16. Is this not a Geometric Progression? Is this not, quoting my Lord's own words, "strictly according to Analogy"?

Again, was I not taught that as a Line has *two* bounding Points, and a Square has *four* bounding Lines, so a Cube must have *six* bounding Squares? Behold the confirming series: 2, 4, 6. Is this not an Arithmetic Progression? Consequently, does it not necessarily follow that the more divine offspring of the divine Cube in the Land of Four Dimensions must possess *eight* bounding Cubes? Is this not also, as my Lord has taught me to believe, "strictly according to Analogy"?
