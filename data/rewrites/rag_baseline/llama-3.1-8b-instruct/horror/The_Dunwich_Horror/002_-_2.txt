The dimly lit attic room was heavy with the scent of decay and forgotten knowledge. In the corner, a tattered book lay open on a rickety desk, its yellowed pages rustling in the faint breeze that seeped through the cracks in the walls. Lavinia Whateley's fingers danced across the spines of the ancient tomes, tracing the worn leather bindings with a mixture of reverence and trepidation. Her eyes, an unsettling shade of pink, seemed to gleam with a knowing light as she delved deeper into the forbidden lore that filled the dusty shelves.

The wind outside howled like a chorus of restless spirits, echoing through the hills and valleys of the countryside. Lavinia's thoughts, however, were consumed by the memories of her son, Wilbur, born on a stormy night in February, 1913. The recollection of that fateful evening still lingered in her mind like a discordant note, a jarring reminder of the strange and wondrous events that had unfolded in the months that followed.

As she wandered through the musty rooms, the shadows cast by the flickering candles seemed to grow longer and darker, like grasping fingers reaching out to snuff out the last vestiges of her sanity. The old farmhouse, once a symbol of warmth and security, now felt like a prison, a cold and unforgiving space that suffocated her with its secrets and silences.

The wind died down, leaving behind an oppressive stillness that hung in the air like a challenge. Lavinia's breath caught in her throat as she recalled the words of her father, Old Whateley, spoken in a hushed tone on that fateful night: "Some day you'll hear a child of Lavinny's calling its father's name on the top of Sentinel Hill!" The prophecy echoed through her mind like a haunting melody, a refrain that refused to be silenced.

As the darkness deepened, Lavinia's thoughts turned to the few people who had seen her son during those early days – Zechariah Whateley, the sole representative of the undecayed branch of the family, and Mamie Bishop, Earl Sawyer's common-law wife, who had visited out of curiosity rather than affection. Their encounters with Wilbur had been brief, yet they had left an indelible mark on Lavinia's psyche, a testament to the strange and foreboding power that her child possessed.
