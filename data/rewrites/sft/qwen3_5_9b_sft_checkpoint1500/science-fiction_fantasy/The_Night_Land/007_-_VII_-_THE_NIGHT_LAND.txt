As I pressed northward and westward, I navigated with extreme caution for a prolonged period, steering clear of that Great Watcher of the Northwest. As I advanced, I organized every aspect of my existence in my mind, to the limit of my imagination. First, I calculated the pace I must maintain and concluded immediately that moderation was essential; ahead of me lay a vast and mighty journey, and who could possibly speak with authority regarding its end?

Next, I arranged a schedule. I intended to regulate the timing of my movements, meals, and sleep into a wise and regular pattern. This discipline would allow me to traverse a great distance with minimal harm to my physical frame, ensuring I remained strong when necessity demanded it. Consequently, I decreed that I would consume food and drink every six hours, and sleep during the eighteenth hour until the twenty-fourth.

By this method, I ate three times within that span and secured six hours of rest. This regimen appeared excellent to me, and I strove to adhere to it throughout my arduous trek through the Night Land. Yet, as anyone might expect, there were occasions—often numerous—when I was compelled to remain vigilant without respite, deferring my slumber to a later date; the Land teemed with grim and dreadful perils.

As humans do, I broke my rules immediately at the outset. I failed to walk for twenty-one uninterrupted hours, hiding and creeping wherever the terrain suggested proximity to the Watcher. When thoughts of sustenance crossed my mind, they brought nausea, so I postponed eating as I formed plans within my head.

However, when twenty-one hours had elapsed, I became profoundly weary and slightly faint, forced to seek a location for rest. Shortly thereafter, I spotted, in the distance, a small fire-hole, similar to several I had passed in those early days. I approached it; there would be warmth to counter the chilling cold of the Night Land, and perhaps a dry, convenient spot for slumber.

Upon reaching it, I found it a cheerful enclave amidst the overwhelming gloom. The opening was merely a few paces wide, filled with a dull, glowing fire that bubbled slightly and emitted a faint sulfur-smoke. I sat down nearby, placing the Diskos upon the rock beside me.

I remained motionless for a while, exhausted to the point where I lacked the courage to eat or drink. Instead, I turned my gaze back toward the Mighty Pyramid. To be truthful, although I had traveled a considerable distance, I was still so close to it that its magnitude both uplifted and depressed me. It seemed to tower closely overhead, shaken by the sheer scale of the task remaining before me, a traveler who had endured a hard and wearisome path.

Yet, this was only one facet of my condition. It was comforting to feel the nearness of my Mighty Home. I knew that countless millions were standing watch over me as I sat there, yet I made no sign. It is not fitting to offer a constant farewell; rather, one must GO. Nevertheless, it was very strange to be so close yet behave with the aloofness of one distant from all humanity. Still, I ordered my actions accordingly, as it seemed proper. I was happy to know that my dear friend, Master Monstruwacan, must frequently have spied upon me through the Great Spy-Glass, and perhaps he was watching me in that exact moment.

A sense of weakness grew within me as I hesitated to consume my provisions, appearing foolish before my kind friend afar. Thus, I opened my bag and extracted three tablets, chewing them down. This was a potent food, treated to possess minimal bulk. However, they did not fill the belly, so I resolved to drink well, to ensure I felt something substantial within me.

To achieve this, I shook a fine dust from a strong, special tube into a small cup. The air acted upon the dust with chemical precision; it boiled and fizzed, rising to fill the vessel with a simple liquid that was, in effect, water. It was a strange phenomenon to witness, yet ordinary after a time.

In this manner, I carried sufficient food and drink within a small bag to sustain life for a great duration. It was a mode of existence marked by discomfort, lacking satisfaction for the palate and stomach, yet sufficient for the needs of the body and a grateful heart in that dark, hungry realm.

Having eaten, I reviewed the contents of my belongings. Beside the Diskos and the ration bag, there was a pouch containing various items. I examined them and retrieved a small compass gifted to me by Master Monstruwacan. He had instructed me that I might venture far into the Night Land and lose the Mighty Pyramid amidst such a vast country and plentiful darkness. Perhaps, if the ancient principles still lingered within the mechanism—even if oriented toward the Pyramid rather than North—it would guide my feet homeward out of the Everlasting Night, restoring its ancient utility.

This was a cunning device, provided it functioned with the Earth-Current, and a rare creation, forged by Monstruwacan's own skilled hands from an antique model housed in the Great Museum, of which I have spoken previously.

I set the instrument upon the ground, but it possessed no definite orientation. Instead, it spun and wavered constantly. I considered this and recalled that I was situated above the region where the Underground Fields sprawled far beneath the earth, likely but a short distance from the "Crack," though a mighty distance vertically above it.

It delighted me to wonder if Master Monstruwacan witnessed my testing of the compass. The light from the fire-hole was good, and the Great Spy-Glass possessed immense power. Yet, I held no certainty. From extensive observation, I knew there was no guarantee in scouting the Land via the Glass. Often, areas appeared perfectly plain where one expected invisibility, and conversely, dullness prevailed where sight should have been clear. This is obvious to all; the wavering lights of strange fires cannot be relied upon to dictate visibility, creating illumination here and darkness there, changing oddly. Furthermore, smokes and mists rose from the earth in this locale, varying in magnitude. Sometimes they were insignificant, lurking low and powerless to confuse the sight; other times, they were great, obstructing vision.

Presently, I stowed the compass in my pouch and composed myself for sleep. Here, I must record that, after many days of journeying outward from the Mighty Pyramid, I eventually found it drawing the northern part of the needle toward it. This was a comfort and pleasure to my spirit. If I ever returned, as I hoped, it would be a subject of great interest to Master Monstruwacan. Yet, in truth, there were other matters that engaged him more; he was, after all, quite human, as everyone knows.

Moreover, regarding this same compass, I discovered a new phenomenon. After a significantly longer interval—as I shall detail in its proper place, if I recall correctly—I found myself a mighty distance from the Redoubt. Fearing I might indeed lose that Great Home in the World's Darkness, I pulled out the strange marvel of the needle to find comfort in its homeward pointing. I uncovered a new power in the night: the machine pointed no longer directly at the Great Redoubt, but toward the West. Thus, I gained knowledge that some Great Power afar in the Darkness of the World swayed upon it. I experienced a childlike wonder that this might be, in truth, that same Power of the North described in the books and my Memory-Dreams. Indeed, no doubt could exist on this matter, yet who, in that time, could perceive after an eternity that ancient Northward Force swaying that small servant to an olden obedience? It was, as it were, a revelation to me: to know within the brain is one matter, but to have knowledge within the heart is another. I had always known of this Northward Force, but I had not known the true meaning of Knowledge.

Yet, another matter caused doubt at that moment. It occurred to me that perhaps the power of the Lesser Redoubt began to act upon the machine, just as the Earth-Current of the Great Pyramid still held a strong drawing upon the needle. If this were true, I would not surely be standing near the Journey's end, for that lesser power of the Lesser Redoubt could have no impudence to pull unless I had come to a closeness with it.

Yet, in truth, as I now know, it was the North that drew. I seem to make a great fuss about this little matter, but how else shall I show you my inner mind, the lack of knowledge, and the peculiar knowings that contributed to that time, and the people thereof? Which is but to say the same thing twice over.

Thus, I composed myself for sleep. I took a cloak-like material crossing my shoulder and hip, wrapping it about me, and lay down in the darkness of the Night by that strange fire-hole.

I laid the Diskos beside me, within the cloak, for it was indeed my companion and friend in bitter need, giving me pleasure to feel the strange thing near. As I lay there, in those drowsy moments proceeding like breath from the mouth of Sleep, I had a half-knowledge that the aether surged about me. I doubt not that many of the Millions had watched my every action, and were humanly stirred by my commendation of my spirit unto sleep, shaking the aether of the world about me with their unity of sympathy.

Perhaps I had some little knowledge of this as I passed, drowsy, into slumber; surely I slept the better for it. Moreover, I was wondrously tired and worn, so I slept very strongly and heavily
