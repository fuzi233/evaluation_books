The air was alive with anticipation as Mrs. Archer chatted with Mrs. Welland in the quiet elegance of May's drawing-room. It was, as Mrs. Archer smiled to her mother-in-law, a momentous occasion – the first big dinner party for the young couple. Since setting up their household, the Newland Archers had welcomed many friends informally, often hosting gatherings of three or four close friends. May, with her mother's beaming encouragement, had taken to it naturally, while Archer had grown accustomed to their relaxed evenings together.

However, this dinner party was different. With a hired chef, borrowed footmen, Roman punch, and roses from Henderson's, it was a more formal affair, one that required careful planning and attention to detail. Mrs. Archer, her mind focused on the arrangements, wrote out the menus on Tiffany's finest gilt-edged paper, while Mrs. Welland supervised the placement of palms and standard lamps.

As Archer arrived home from his office, the two women were still busily engaged. Mrs. Archer had moved on to designing the name-cards for the table, while Mrs. Welland pondered the best way to rearrange the furniture to create an additional cozy nook near the piano and window. May, they told him, was in the dining-room, inspecting the elaborate centerpiece of Jacqueminot roses and maidenhair ferns, and carefully positioning the delicate Maillard bonbons in openwork silver baskets between the candelabras. A large basket of orchids, sent by Mr. van der Luyden from Skuytercliff, stood proudly on the piano.

Mrs. Archer's thoughts turned to the guest list, her sharp gold pen ticking off each name as she reviewed it. "Henry van der Luyden, Louisa, the Lovell Mingotts, the Reggie Chiverses, Lawrence Lefferts and Gertrude... (yes, I suppose May was right to include them) – the Selfridge Merrys, Sillerton Jackson, Van Newland and his wife... (how time flies! It seems only yesterday he was your best man, Newland)... and Countess Olenska... yes, I think that's everyone."
