As the sun rose over the forest, casting a warm glow across the landscape, I set out on the long journey to the House of Bitterness, carrying the lifeless form of Lona, her daughter, cradled in my arms. My heart heavy with grief and determination, I rode Lona's horse, intent on delivering her to her father, who would surely provide a place for her among the honored dead. I had no doubt that he would accept her, for she had passed beyond the veil of mortality long ago.

Alongside me rode Lilith, the woman I had wronged so gravely. I felt no sense of triumph or satisfaction, only a deep-seated guilt and a sense of responsibility for her suffering. How could I justify keeping her captive, even as I struggled to make amends for my transgressions? And yet, what choice did I have? I was her gaoler, bound to her by ties of duty and obligation.

As we traveled, I offered Lilith food, but she refused, her eyes blazing with a fierce, unyielding hatred. It was as if she had given up hope, resigned herself to her fate. Only when we crossed the hot stream did her gaze finally waver, her eyes closing as if in exhaustion.

That evening, as we camped for the night, a young girl approached Lilith, her eyes shining with curiosity. Before I could intervene, the child had pressed a small offering into Lilith's mouth, and let out a cry of pain. I quickly took the child's finger in my mouth, soothing her distress. The child, Luva, gazed up at me with a look of gratitude, before snatching her hand away and offering another morsel to Lilith. This time, however, she withdrew it hastily, and the fruit fell to the ground.

The next day, we continued our journey, crossing the hot stream once more. The Little Ones, those mischievous spirits, were jubilant, eager to explore their surroundings. We stopped at an ancient ivy-hall, its walls covered in lush vines and heavy with ripening grapes. The children were drawn to the fruit like magnets, devouring it greedily and soon succumbing to sleep. I had hoped to see them dance, but instead, they settled into a peaceful slumber, their tiny bodies swaying gently in the breeze.
