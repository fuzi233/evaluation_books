In the warm glow of the setting sun, Baldassarre stepped into the Piazza del Duomo, a place he had avoided since his hasty departure. The memory of being seen half-naked, with his unkempt hair and a rope around his neck, still lingered vividly in his mind. The cruel words of Tito, "some madman, surely," echoed within him, a painful reminder of his vulnerability. The fear of being unable to prove those words false had taken hold, and with it, a gnawing sense of doubt.

As he navigated the crowded square, the sounds of vendors calling out their wares and the murmur of the crowd created a cacophony that was both familiar and jarring. Baldassarre's eyes scanned the faces around him, searching for a glimpse of the monk who had spoken words that cut deep. His heart yearned to hear the monk preach once more, to be reminded of the power of his words and the conviction that burned within him.

But for now, he chose to remain hidden, observing from afar. The desire for vengeance against Tito simmered beneath the surface, tempered by the knowledge that his own strength was uncertain. It was as if Tito had been aided by a malicious guide, whispering dark secrets into his ear. The thought sent a shiver down Baldassarre's spine, and he quickened his pace, disappearing into the winding streets of Florence.

A short while later, he found himself at the city gate, ready to leave for Arezzo. As he walked, the scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, mingling with the smell of damp earth and stone. He stopped at a small bakery, purchasing a loaf of warm bread and a flask of wine. The baker, a kind-eyed woman, asked him about his travels, and Baldassarre replied that he had been imprisoned by the French on his way from Genoa. Her sympathetic expression and the gentle smile she offered encouraged him to stay a while longer, and he accepted her invitation to rest in the shade of her shop.

As he sat, munching on the crusty bread and sipping the rich wine, he noticed a small, intricately carved wooden spoon lying on the counter. The design etched into its surface caught his eye – a symbol of the local tradition, passed down through generations.
