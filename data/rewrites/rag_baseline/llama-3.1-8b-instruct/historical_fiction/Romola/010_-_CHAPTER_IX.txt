A Man's Ransom.

Tito navigated the crowded piazza, his footsteps weaving through the throngs of people gathered to celebrate the festive season. As he made his way along the Corso degli Adimari, his thoughts strayed to the enigmatic contadina he had encountered earlier, her piercing blue eyes lingering in his memory like a whispered promise. Though he hadn't revealed his true identity, Tito couldn't help but wonder if their paths would cross again, much like the threads of a rich Tuscan brocade intertwining in a intricate pattern.

However, his musings were interrupted by the recollection of the frail friar whose gaze had seemed to hold a profound significance for him. The faint memory stirred within him, much like the scent of woodsmoke carried on the autumn breeze. Where had he seen that worn, ascetic face before? The query danced at the periphery of his thoughts, refusing to settle into focus. Tito's attention drifted back to the present, and he found himself at the threshold of a narrow side-street, the sunlight casting long shadows across the cobblestones.

As he turned into the alleyway, his thoughts turned to the upcoming visit to the Via de' Bardi, where he was eagerly anticipated. He envisioned the scene of his arrival, Romola's radiant smile illuminating the space like a lantern in a quiet courtyard. Her blind father's chair, a constant presence in their shared moments, seemed to hold a sacred significance, a symbol of their bond forged in the fire of their daily interactions. Tito felt a deep affection for Romola, one that bordered on reverence, as if she embodied the essence of a great, timeless goddess, her presence awakening a sense of awe within him.

Though they had never been alone together, Tito's imagination ran wild with possibilities, conjuring scenarios of love and devotion. Yet, he was acutely aware that such dreams were impossible, that Romola's path lay far beyond his own. Like a peasant girl in a sun-drenched Greek village, who had once shyly asked for a kiss, Tito longed for love to find him, to surprise him in its own sweet time. But Romola's love would never arrive in that unguarded manner; it would be a gift, carefully crafted and tenderly bestowed, if it were to come at all. And so, Tito's heart remained fixed on the horizon, yearning for a love that seemed as elusive as the morning dew on a Tuscan hill
