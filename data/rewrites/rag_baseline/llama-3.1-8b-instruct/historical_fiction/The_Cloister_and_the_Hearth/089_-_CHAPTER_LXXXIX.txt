As I stood before the frail figure of the dying hermit, I couldn't help but think of the countless meals we'd shared, the simplicity of his table a testament to his unwavering commitment to the spiritual life. The scent of burning incense still lingered in the air, mingling with the dampness of the rain-soaked earth outside. It was as if the very fabric of our world was unraveling, thread by thread, just as the hermit's life force ebbed away. The flickering candlelight cast eerie shadows on the walls as I listened intently to his labored breathing.

The decision to set out for Tergou, to confront the burgomaster and retrieve the parchment, had been made with a sense of purpose I hadn't felt in months. The memory of Margaret's desperate plea still echoed in my mind, a constant reminder of the stakes involved. I had to push aside the trepidation that threatened to consume me, focus on the task at hand. The streets of Tergou would be treacherous, but I steeled myself for the challenge ahead.

As I walked, the sounds of the city grew louder – vendors hawking their wares, the clang of hammer on anvil, the murmur of haggling merchants. I stopped at the fountain, breaking off a crust of black bread and taking a sip of cool water. The taste was a brief respite from the turmoil brewing inside me. But my reprieve was short-lived, as a female servant rushed up, begging me to follow her to the Stadthouse.

I hesitated for a moment, then fell in step behind her. The heavy wooden door creaked as she pushed it open, revealing a dimly lit hallway that seemed to stretch on forever. We climbed the stairs, the air thick with the scent of sickness and decay. At the top, I found myself facing a figure I had once considered a friend – the burgomaster, his eyes sunken, his skin deathly pale.
