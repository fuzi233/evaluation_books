As the sun began to set over Sevenbergen, casting a warm orange glow over the village, Margaret's anxiety grew. She had nothing prepared for the evening meal, and her husband Gerard was due to arrive any moment, famished from a long day's work. Her eyes welled up with tears as she confided in Martin Wittenhaagen, the grizzled old soldier who had been a loyal friend to their family.

"Martin, I'm at my wit's end," she exclaimed. "There's nothing in the house, and Gerard will be coming soon. He's always so thoughtless, forgetting to eat at home and expecting me to provide for him. And to think I have nothing to put before our servant, who has been so kind to us."

Martin scratched his head, his brow furrowed in concern. "What can I do?" he asked.

"It's Thursday," Margaret reminded him. "You're supposed to go hunting today. Please, Martin, help me out."

Martin hesitated, holding up a parchment with a grand seal. "I'm afraid I may not hunt when the Duke or his friends are out," he said. "It's a condition of my stipend and license to hunt in the Duke's forests. I'm bound by contract." He showed her the document, which granted him permission to hunt three times a week, provided the Duke wasn't out himself. The terms were clear: no hunting on Thursdays unless the Duke and his friends were absent.

Margaret sighed, feeling a mixture of relief and disappointment. "Very well, Martin. But please, be careful. If Gerard goes hungry rather than risk your safety, I'd rather that than see anything happen to you."

Martin nodded, his face set in determination. "Don't worry, mistress. I'll take care. I've hunted these woods for years and know them like the back of my hand. I'll just slip in and out, and no one will be the wiser." He tucked a quiver of arrows into his belt and made his way towards the forest, a mere furlong from their village.

As he entered the woods, the sound of horns echoed through the trees, and Martin's heart quickened with excitement. The game was afoot, and he was ready.
