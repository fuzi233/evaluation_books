As the first wisps of autumnal fog crept over the horizon, shrouding the rugged coastline in a damp, gray veil, the Vengeance, a weathered galleon bearing the somber emblem of England, slipped silently into the tranquil waters off Appledore. The morning air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and decaying leaves, a poignant reminder of the transience of life and the fleeting nature of human endeavor. The crew's subdued demeanor reflected the collective sorrow that had settled upon them following the loss of their esteemed comrade, Salvation Yeo.

As the ship's boats pulled ashore, a small procession formed to escort Amyas Leigh, the blind sailor, to his family's ancestral home. The townspeople, well-acquainted with the tragic events that had befallen their fellow citizen, gathered around him with a mix of reverence and pity. The atmosphere was heavy with the weight of collective grief, as if the very fabric of their community had been rent asunder by the loss of a stalwart friend and loyal servant.

Amyas, ever the diplomat, requested that his friends and neighbors refrain from making a spectacle of his return, and instead, accompany him discreetly to the family tomb. The townsfolk, moved by his request, complied, and a solemn procession formed behind the coffin, which had been borne ashore the previous day. As they made their way towards Northam town, the crowd swelled to an estimated five hundred souls, a testament to the esteem in which Salvation Yeo was held by the community.

Meanwhile, Mrs. Leigh, Amyas's mother, remained at home, her heart heavy with the weight of her son's blindness. She sat in the bay-window, surrounded by the familiar comforts of her domestic sphere, accompanied by Ayacanora, the young girl who had been rescued by Salvation Yeo during his perilous voyages. As the tolling of the church bell faded into the distance, Mrs. Leigh opened her prayer book and began to read the burial service, her voice a soothing balm to her own troubled spirit.

"Ayacanora," she said, her voice barely above a whisper, "do you not feel a pang of sorrow for Master Yeo, who risked his life to save you from the jaws of death?" The child's eyes dropped, her thoughts consumed by memories of Amyas, the blind sailor who had become a surrogate brother to her.
