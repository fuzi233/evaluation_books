As Gerard regained consciousness, he found himself propped against the lifeless bear, its fur matted with his own sweat and the stench of death. Denys, his loyal companion, hovered above him, a mixture of relief and amusement dancing across his rugged features. The air was heavy with the scent of wet earth and ozone, and Gerard's stomach churned with a wave of nausea.

"What a mess she's made," Gerard muttered, his words barely audible over the thrumming of his own heartbeat.

Denys chuckled, his eyes crinkling at the corners. "With her blood, not yours, my friend," he said, his voice low and soothing. "You're a bit green around the gills, if you ask me."

Gerard's gaze drifted to the bear's carcass, and he felt a shudder run down his spine. "I...I think it was the smell," he stammered, his face contorting in distaste.

Denys nodded sympathetically. "Aye, it can be potent stuff. But let's get you cleaned up, shall we?" He carefully lifted Gerard to his feet, supporting him as he swayed precariously.

Gerard's eyes locked onto Denys's, and for a moment, they simply regarded each other. The air was thick with unspoken words, the tension between them a palpable thing. Then, without warning, Gerard's reserve crumbled, and he flung his arms around Denys's neck, holding him tight.

Denys's expression softened, and he wrapped his own arms around Gerard, pulling him close. "Ah, little one," he whispered, his voice husky with emotion. "Was ever aught so like a woman?"

Gerard's face flushed, but he didn't pull away. Instead, he buried his face in Denys's shoulder, his body trembling with a mix of relief and vulnerability. Denys held him close, his own heart pounding in time with Gerard's.

As they stood there, the world around them began to fade into the background. The sounds of the forest grew distant, the scent of the bear's carcass receding into the shadows. All that remained was the warmth of Denys's presence, the beat of their shared heart.

It was Denys who finally broke the spell, his voice low and gentle. "We should get moving, Gerard. We've lost too much time already. And I think I see a figure on the horizon...our papa, perhaps?"
