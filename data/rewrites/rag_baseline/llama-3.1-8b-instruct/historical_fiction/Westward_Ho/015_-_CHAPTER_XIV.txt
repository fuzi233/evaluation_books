As we delve into the complexities of diplomatic relations between the English monarchy and the enigmatic Gubbings, it becomes apparent that a nuanced understanding of their cultural context is essential. The Gubbings, a reclusive and nomadic people inhabiting the rugged terrain of Dartmoor, had long been shrouded in mystery. Theirs was a society seemingly devoid of hierarchical structure, existing outside the bounds of conventional ecclesiastical or civil authority. The Devonian dialect spoken by the Gubbings was, in fact, a unique blend of the vernacular and a language all their own, incomprehensible to outsiders, including the more educated members of society.

Historically, the Gubbings had managed to maintain their independence through their mastery of the unforgiving landscape and their exceptional horsemanship. Their predilection for theft, particularly of livestock from neighboring lands, had led to a widespread perception of them as thieves and outcasts. However, this characterization belied a more complex reality, wherein the Gubbings' communal way of life and shared resources allowed them to thrive in an environment inhospitable to outsiders.

Despite their reputation, the Gubbings were not entirely averse to the idea of civilization. In recent years, there had been reports of their children being baptized, and a growing trend towards assimilation into mainstream society. This development had sparked a mix of emotions among the English populace, ranging from skepticism to admiration. Notably, the Puritan writer Thomas Fuller had observed that the Gubbings' newfound civility should serve as a cautionary tale for those who might seek to abandon their own societal norms in favor of a more primitive way of life.

Against this backdrop, a group of travelers, led by Amyas and including Salvation Yeo, set out from Plymouth, bound for the northern reaches of Devon. Their journey was motivated by a desire to avoid the Gubbings' territory, rather than to engage with them. As they traversed the treacherous terrain, they were acutely aware of the risks involved in navigating this unforgiving landscape. The harsh environment, coupled with the Gubbings' notorious reputation, made for a perilous journey, one that would test the mettle of even the most seasoned traveler.
