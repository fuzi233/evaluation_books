It would have been a marvel indeed if men, armed with such scanty clues, could have deciphered the silent script written upon the riverbank. For upon that very spot, an event had just transpired, one so singular in the annals of humanity that it may well stand alone until the final hour of time. Yet, this tale shall be recounted in but a few words, woven together from the voice of the narrator and the memory of a witness.

Gerard, having penned his brief farewell to Pietro and Andrea, descended to the river's edge as the shadows lengthened into night. His preparations were marked by a grim, unyielding resolve, a trait often found in those determined to extinguish their own light. He stuffed his pockets with every piece of silver and copper he owned, ensuring his weight would anchor him deep beneath the current, and hastened to a secluded bend of the stream he knew was rarely trodden.

Some, particularly women, cast anxious glances around to ensure a guardian stands nearby. But this desperate soul scanned the darkness solely to confirm the absence of any living breath. To his chagrin, however, he spotted a solitary figure leaning against the shadowed corner of an alley. Pretending to wander aimlessly, Gerard moved away, only to return moments later.

Sure enough, the same figure emerged from a side street, lingering with predatory patience. "Does he watch me? Does he know my purpose?" Gerard wondered, though he dismissed the thought as impossible. He strode briskly down the lane, circled through two streets, and returned. The man had vanished, yet when Gerard cast a wary glance around, there he stood mere yards behind, ostensibly adjusting his shoe.

Gerard realized he was being hunted. In the pale moonlight, he discerned a steel gauntlet gripped in the sentinel's hand. Then, the truth crashed upon him: he faced an assassin. Strangely, it never crossed his mind that his own life was the target; he remained unaware that enemies lurked in the world.

Turning to confront the bravo, Gerard approached with frantic desperation. "My good friend," he cried eagerly, "sell me thine arm! A single stroke! Behold, here is all I possess!" He thrust his meager coins into the assassin's grasp. "Oh, pray thee! Pray thee! Perform one act of mercy and liberate me from this hateful existence!" As he spoke, he undid his doublet, exposing his trembling bosom.

The man stared, unmoved.

"Why do you hesitate?" Gerard shrieked, his voice breaking. "Do you lack compassion? Is it so great a burden to raise your arm and deliver the blow? Is it because I am poor and cannot offer gold? Wretched fool, you can only strike a man from behind, refusing to meet his gaze. Very well, then! Turn your head and keep your silence!"

With a snarl of utter contempt, the assassin fled, leaving Gerard to throw himself into the dark water.

"Margaret!"

At the heavy splash of his body hitting the stream, the bravo seemed to rouse from a trance. He sprinted to the bank, issuing a strange cry before plunging in after the man who sought death. What followed shall be told by the assassin himself.
