As the sun cast its gentle warmth over the city, Newland Archer embarked on the first of the customary visits, accompanied by his mother and sister, to pay their respects to Mrs. Welland. This was a ritual as predictable as the changing seasons, and one that Newland had grown accustomed to, though he couldn't help but feel a sense of detachment. The formalities were carried out with precision, as they drove to old Mrs. Manson Mingott's estate, where they would receive her blessing.

Mrs. Mingott's abode was a treasure trove of history, its walls bearing witness to the passing of time. While it may not have been as ancient as some of the other family homes on University Place and Fifth Avenue, it still possessed a unique charm, a blend of elegance and whimsy that was quintessentially New York. The furniture, a mix of Mingott heirlooms and Second Empire upholstery, created an atmosphere of refined opulence, a testament to the matriarch's impeccable taste.

As they entered the sitting room, Mrs. Mingott greeted them from her favorite window perch, her eyes fixed on the horizon, as if anticipating the day when the humble surroundings would give way to grandeur. Her confidence was unwavering, and her patience, a virtue she had honed over the years, allowed her to bide her time, certain that the city would eventually yield to her vision of a more refined and sophisticated landscape.

Mrs. Mingott's physical transformation was as remarkable as her spirit. The weight she had gained in middle age had left her with a commanding presence, a statuesque figure that seemed almost regal. Her skin, a canvas of pink and white, stretched taut over a face that was barely visible beneath the folds of flesh. Two tiny hands, delicate as a bird's wings, rested on the armchair, as if poised to take flight at any moment.

The constraints of age and gravity had forced Mrs. Mingott to adapt, and she had done so with characteristic independence. Her reception rooms were now situated on the ground floor, a deliberate choice that defied convention and sparked whispers among the guests who visited her. As they sat in the sitting room, they caught glimpses of her bedroom, a space that seemed to exist in a world of its own, with its plush upholstery and ornate mirror. It was a setup that seemed almost... Continental, a far cry from the simplicity and propriety that defined the lives of most New Yorkers.
