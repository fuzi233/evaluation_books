As the sun rose over the island, casting a warm glow across the landscape, I set out with Fritz to retrieve the sledge, laden with the handcrafted dishes, bowls, and baskets we had created. Our expedition would require stealth and strategy, so I instructed the other boys to remain with their mother, while Fritz and I ventured forth, seeking to unravel the secrets of Calabash Wood.

Our journey took us through a dense thicket of evergreen oaks, where our sow grazed contentedly on the abundant acorns, seemingly oblivious to the commotion we had caused the previous day. Her tranquility was a stark contrast to the cacophony of sounds emanating from within the wood – the melodic trills of various birds, their songs intertwining in a symphony of sound that filled the air. Fritz's sudden discharge of his firearm shattered the serenity, sending a beautiful blue jay and a pair of parakeets – one resplendent in scarlet plumage, the other shimmering with iridescent greens and golds – tumbling to the forest floor.

The sudden commotion sent us into a state of heightened vigilance, our ears perked up at the sound of a low, muffled drumbeat echoing through the trees. We cautiously approached the source of the noise, concealing ourselves amidst the undergrowth as we drew closer to the edge of a clearing. There, atop a weathered log, stood a magnificent bird, its chestnut-brown plumage intricately patterned with dark browns and greys. Its velvety-black tufts on the shoulders glistened with a subtle sheen, as it strutted and preened, its wings beating in a stately rhythm.

The bird's performance was met with a chorus of admiring gazes from a gathering of its kin, who watched with rapt attention as it spread its tail like a fan, stiffened its wings, and began to strike them in rapid, pulsating beats. The resulting drumbeat grew louder, until it seemed to reverberate through the forest, like the distant rumble of thunder. The whirring of its wings created a vortex around the bird, a whirlwind of motion that held us entranced.
