The house was quiet, her mother having slept soundly throughout the night, her occasional gentle stirrings the only disturbance in the stillness. A faint breeze whispered through the air, carrying the sweet scent of blooming flowers and the distant hum of insects, a gentle melody that soothed Margaret's frazzled nerves.

She settled into her work, sitting by the window in Mrs. Hale's cozy room, the soft light casting a warm glow on her face. The tasks of the day lay ahead – helping her mother dress, visiting Bessy Higgins after dinner – but for now, she let her mind wander, trying to shake off the lingering memories of the Thornton family. Yet, like a persistent summer mist, those thoughts clung to her, refusing to dissipate. Her cheeks flushed, a sudden warmth spreading through her pale complexion, as if the sun had burst forth from behind a veil of clouds, illuminating the horizon.

Dixon's soft knock on the door broke the spell, and he slipped inside, his eyes darting towards Margaret as he announced, "Mr. Thornton, Miss Margaret. He is in the drawing-room." Margaret's needle paused mid-stitch, her hands trembling slightly as she set aside her sewing. "Did he ask for me? Is Papa come in?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

"He asked for you, miss; and Master is out," Dixon replied, his eyes darting towards the door, as if expecting Mr. Thornton to emerge at any moment.

Margaret rose slowly, her movements hesitant, as if weighing the pros and cons of facing the man who had left her so unsettled the previous day. Mr. Thornton stood by the window, his back to the door, lost in thought. But his mind was far from the street scene outside; it was consumed by the memory of Margaret's arms wrapped around his neck, the sensation of her touch still lingering, like a whispered secret. His heart pounded in his chest, threatening to overwhelm him, as he struggled to maintain control.
