The last wisps of twilight clung to the streets of Milton, reluctant to surrender to the encroaching night. In the grand foyer of the Thornton estate, Margaret Hale's departure had left a palpable silence, a stillness that Mr. Thornton felt acutely as he entered. His face aglow with a mixture of emotions, he moved swiftly across the room, his eyes scanning the space as if searching for the one person who had just vanished from it.

"Where is she?" he asked, his voice low and urgent, his gaze locking onto his mother, who stood quietly rearranging the furniture, her hands moving with a deliberate slowness.

"Gone home," she replied, her tone crisp and detached.

"Gone home?" he repeated, his footsteps echoing through the room as he began to pace.

"Yes. She said she was well enough, and Mr. Lowe concurred. I fetched him myself."

Mr. Thornton's expression darkened, his eyes clouding over with concern. "I'm sorry she's gone home," he muttered, his words laced with a hint of frustration. "She shouldn't have walked alone."

His mother's gaze didn't waver, her focus fixed on the task at hand. "She had a cab, everything was taken care of. Let's speak of something else. She's caused quite enough disturbance already."

A flash of anger danced in Mr. Thornton's eyes, but he struggled to contain it, his jaw clenched in a visible effort to remain calm. "I don't know what I would have done without her," he said finally, his voice rough with emotion.

His mother's expression remained inscrutable, but a flicker of unease played across her features. "Are you so helpless, then, that you need a girl to defend you?" she asked, her tone dripping with sarcasm.

Mr. Thornton's face reddened, his chest heaving with unspoken passion. "Not many girls would have taken the blows meant for me," he said, his voice low and husky. "Blows meant with goodwill, too."

"A girl in love will do many things," his mother retorted, her words cutting through the air like a knife.
