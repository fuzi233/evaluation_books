As the sun cast its warm rays over the quiet streets of London, Mrs. John Dashwood paid a visit to Mrs. Jennings and her daughter, driven by her confidence in her husband's discerning eye. She had always trusted John's judgment, and today was no exception. Upon meeting Lady Middleton, she found herself charmed by the woman's polished demeanor, which seemed to mirror her own reserved nature. It was as if they shared a secret understanding, a silent language of propriety and decorum that only those with a similar sensibility could appreciate.

Lady Middleton, in turn, was equally taken with Mrs. Dashwood, drawn to her by a subtle kinship born of a shared detachment from the world around them. As they conversed, they discovered a kindred spirit, bound together by their reserve and lack of sentimental excess. Their conversation flowed smoothly, like a gentle stream meandering through the countryside, never straying far from the beaten path.

Not everyone, however, was as enamored with Mrs. Dashwood. Mrs. Jennings, though friendly, found her to be a bit too formal, a woman of pride and reserve who seemed to hold her cards close to her chest. When they parted ways, Mrs. Jennings couldn't help but feel that Mrs. Dashwood had been a tad too distant, her words measured and calculated.

Elinor, meanwhile, had her own concerns on her mind. She longed to know if Edward was in town, but dared not ask. Fanny, her sister, was even more reticent, holding her tongue until she could share the news of Edward's marriage to Miss Morton or the settlement of Colonel Brandon's expectations. Elinor knew that Fanny's reluctance stemmed from her deep affection for the couple, a bond that she wished to preserve by keeping their names unspoken.

It wasn't long before Elinor's curiosity got the better of her. Lucy Steele arrived at their doorstep, seeking Elinor's sympathy and support. She confided that Edward had arrived in town with the Dashwoods, but had been too afraid to visit them in person, fearing detection. Instead, they exchanged letters, their longing for each other tempered by caution. Elinor was secretly pleased to hear that Edward had visited their home, leaving his card twice on the table, and was all the more pleased that she had missed him.
