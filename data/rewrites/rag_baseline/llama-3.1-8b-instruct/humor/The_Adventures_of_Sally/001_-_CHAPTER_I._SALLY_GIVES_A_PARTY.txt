Sally stood at the edge of the room, her gaze drifting over the faces of her guests. The air was thick with conversation, each voice a distinct note in a discordant melody. Her brother Fillmore's white evening waistcoat seemed a jarring presence, a reminder of the artificiality that underlay their gathering. Yet, as she watched, the tension dissipated, and the guests began to relax, their words flowing more freely.

The discussion turned to her legacy, and Sally's role in its management. Some suggested sinking her modest sum in bonds or real estate, while others proposed a more speculative approach, betting on the outcome of a boxing match. The idea seemed frivolous, but the excitement it generated was palpable.

As the debate continued, Sally's unease grew. She had only twenty-five thousand dollars to her name, a sum that seemed woefully inadequate compared to the grand schemes being proposed. She intervened, her words cutting through the din, and the room fell silent. The guests exchanged uneasy glances, their faces reflecting a mix of surprise and dismissal.

Sally's statement hung in the air, a stark reminder of the disparity between her circumstances and those of her friends. For a moment, the room seemed to shrink, the distance between her and the others growing wider. Then, as quickly as it had begun, the conversation resumed, the guests returning to their discussions, their voices rising and falling in a soothing rhythm.
