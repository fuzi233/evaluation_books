The letter lay on the table, its words heavy with a truth Gwendolen had never imagined would touch her. She picked it up, her fingers trembling slightly as she unfolded the crisp paper.

The words struck her like a cold gust of wind. Her mother’s handwriting, usually so composed, now carried a frantic edge. The news was impossible—unthinkable. Ruin. Total ruin. The life of comfort, of unquestioned privilege, had been swept away in an instant.

Gwendolen’s breath caught. She stood frozen, the letter dangling from her hand, her mind racing. This could not be. She had always been meant for better things—for elegance, for admiration, for a world that bent to her whims. Poverty was for others, not for her.

Yet the words did not change.

She tossed her hat aside and turned to the mirror, seeking—what? Reassurance? The reflection that met her was flawless, the smooth coils of her hair still arranged as if for a ball. But the sight brought no pleasure now. Her blue eyes, usually so vivid, stared back at her, hollow with disbelief.

A bitter laugh escaped her. How absurd, to think that beauty could shield her from this.

She sank onto the sofa, reading the letter again, slower this time, as if hoping the meaning might shift. It did not. Her hands clenched in her lap, nails pressing into her palms. She would not cry. Tears were for those who surrendered.

Instead, anger burned through her—sharp, defiant. If only she had won more at roulette! A few more days, a few more turns of luck, and she might have had enough to save them all. The thought taunted her. She still had a little money left. She still had her jewelry. Was it truly impossible?

But no. The risk was too great. The Langens would ask questions, and she would not—could not—bear their pity. Better to leave now, quietly, before anyone could guess her shame.

She moved mechanically, packing her things with swift precision. The night passed in a blur of decisions—selling the necklace at dawn, inventing an excuse for her sudden departure, booking the earliest train. She would not look back.

By morning, she was ready, dressed in gray traveling silk, her expression schooled into cool indifference. She paused before the mirror once more, studying herself. A faint, defiant smile curled her lips. She would not be broken.

Then, on impulse, she leaned forward and pressed a kiss to the glass.

*How could sorrow touch her?* She would outrun it. She always had.

But fate had other plans.

A small package arrived just as she prepared to leave. Inside, nestled in a scrap of cambric, was the necklace she had sold hours before. A stranger had returned it—*him*. She knew it instantly. Deronda. He had seen her, followed her, dared to meddle in her affairs.

Humiliation burned through her. He had stripped her of dignity, left her exposed. She would not give him the satisfaction of knowing she understood.

With trembling hands, she tucked the necklace away, along with her fury. There was only one thing left to do.

She would leave Leubronn today. And she would never look back.

By evening, she was gone.
