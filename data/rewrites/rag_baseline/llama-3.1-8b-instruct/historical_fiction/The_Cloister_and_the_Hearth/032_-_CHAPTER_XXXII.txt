As I stepped into the refectory, the warm aroma of roasting meats wafted through the air, mingling with the scent of freshly baked bread. The tables, laden with steaming platters and glistening dishes, seemed to stretch on forever, a testament to the bounty of the monastery. Amidst the hum of conversation, a murmur arose about the sudden passing of Brother Werter, the skilled scribe who had devoted himself to transcribing the charter and rule of the monastery. Gerard's ears perked up at the mention of his name, and with a hesitant smile, he offered his services to continue the work.

The monks exchanged a look, their faces a mix of curiosity and skepticism. "Not for payment, my lords," Gerard clarified, "but for love, and as a small token of gratitude for the kindness shown to a weary traveler such as myself." A kindly monk nodded in approval, but his expression turned thoughtful as he glanced at the vellum deed in Gerard's hand. "Ah, but Brother Werter was a master of the pen," he said, "and his work cannot be easily replicated."

Gerard, undaunted, produced a specimen of his own writing, the ink still wet on the parchment. The monk's eyes widened in surprise as he hastily rose from his seat and hastened to the high table, where the prior sat in solemn contemplation. With a reverent bow, the monk presented the parchment to the prior, who examined it intently before nodding his head in approval. The room erupted into a chorus of whispers and murmurs, and soon, a dozen pairs of eyes were fixed on Gerard, their owners marveling at his skill.

As the commotion died down, a savory crustade ryal, a game pie filled with spiced meat and gravy, was placed before Gerard, accompanied by a silver cup filled with a fragrant pimentum. He raised the cup in a toast, his eyes meeting those of the distant prior before turning to his companions, and took a sip. The room fell silent once more, and in the stillness, a low chant began to build, growing louder until the entire table was hailing him as a brother. "Art convent bred, deny it not?" they asked, their voices a gentle rumble.
