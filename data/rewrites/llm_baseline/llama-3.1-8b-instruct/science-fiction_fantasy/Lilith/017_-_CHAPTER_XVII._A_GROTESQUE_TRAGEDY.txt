As I walked along the winding path, the crumbling stones beneath my feet whispered secrets of the past. The air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and decaying leaves. Before me, a decrepit carriage stood, its wooden frame weathered to a silvery grey, its heavy wheels frozen in time. Two skeletal horses, their bony heads still attached to the pole, seemed to gaze out into the distance. The skeleton of a coachman perched on the worn hammer-cloth, his bony fingers grasped the reins, as if waiting for the signal to depart.

I approached cautiously, my footsteps echoing off the surrounding trees. As I drew closer, the skeletons inside the carriage stirred, their joints creaking with a mournful sigh. One of them, a female, struggled to rise, her bony limbs trembling as she grasped the spoke of the rear wheel. Her companion, a male, watched with a mixture of concern and confusion, his own knee twisted at an unnatural angle.

"Are you injured, my lord?" the female skeleton asked, her voice a faint whisper on the wind.

"Yes, I am," the male replied, his tone gruff and distant. "You offered no assistance, and now this wretched knee of mine is out of joint!"

"I did my best, my lord," the female replied, her voice tinged with a hint of annoyance.

"Perhaps it was not good enough," the male snapped. "I fear I shall never regain my footing again. And what of you, madam? Are you quite... assembled?"

The female cast a glance down at her own skeletal form, her gaze lingering on the empty sockets where her eyes once resided. "I have nothing else to be out of, my lord," she said dryly. "But I must admit, I find myself uncertain. Where are we? And why do I feel such a sense of disconnection?"

The male's gaze drifted about, his skull creaking on its axis as he searched for a memory. "I, too, am unclear. But I recall a name – Cokayne. Does that ring a bell, madam?"

The female's expression was skeptical. "You may be mistaken, my lord. I do not recall ever meeting you."

The male's face contorted in a mixture of frustration and embarrassment. "I... I must have been intoxicated when I retired. Or perhaps I am dreaming. This confounded knee of mine is aching something fierce!"
