Midnight found us at Hastor, two hours after departing Helium. Kantos Kan, Xodar, and I stood amidst the bustling preparations, while Carthoris, Tars Tarkas, and Hor Vastus had already departed on another cruiser for Thark. The transports were primed to launch immediately, drifting slowly south, with the battleship fleet poised to overtake them by dawn on the second day.

At Hastor, everything was ready. Kantos Kan's planning was flawless; within ten minutes of our arrival, the first great ship soared from its dock. One by one, at a relentless pace, the massive vessels floated gracefully into the night, forming a long, thin line stretching miles toward the south.

Only after settling into Kantos Kan's cabin did I ask for the date. Until then, the darkness of my confinement in Zat Arras had clouded my sense of time. When Kantos Kan revealed the truth, a pang of dread struck me: three hundred and sixty-five Earth days had passed. It was too late to save Dejah Thoris.

The mission had shifted from rescue to revenge. I suppressed the crushing realization that we were arriving too late to enter the Temple of Issus before the Princess perished. Whether she was already dead or merely dying, the uncertainty gnawed at me. Yet, I bore my grief silently. To burden my friends with my sorrow was unnecessary; they had suffered enough. Instead, I focused on the campaign's potential: exposing the cruel deception practiced upon Barsoom for ages could save thousands from their horrific fate. If we could open the fertile Valley Dor to the red men, or reclaim the lost lands between the Mountains of Otz and the ice barrier, we would honor Dejah Thoris's martyrdom. Here, at the bottom of a dying world, lay the only naturally productive soil, watered by rain and dew, yet guarded by superstition and brute force. Breaking that barrier would be a fitting memorial to her.

By dawn on the second day, the fleet rose with the first light. We exchanged signals with the battleships, noting that radio-aerograms were rarely used in wartime due to the inevitability of interception. Tars Tarkas reported the transports safe. The battleships took an advanced position, and the combined fleets moved slowly over the ice cap, hugging the surface to avoid detection by the approaching Therns.

Ahead of us, a thin line of one-man air scouts patrolled, flanking our formation while a smaller group covered the rear. For hours we progressed toward the entrance to Omean until a scout returned, reporting the cone-like summit of the entrance in sight. Simultaneously, another scout raced from the left flank to the flagship's forward deck.

"A great fleet of battleships, south-south-east, my Prince!" the scout cried, breathless. "Thousands of them, bearing down directly upon us!"

"The Thern spies were not idle in the palace of John Carter," Kantos Kan noted calmly. "Your orders, Prince."

I issued commands instantly. Ten battleships were dispatched to guard the entrance to Omean, sealing the shaft to bottle up the enemy fleet. The remainder formed a great V-shape pointing south-south-east. The transports, shielded by their convoys, were to follow closely. Once the apex of the V pierced the enemy line, the formation would open outward, allowing the battleships to engage fiercely and drive a lane through the enemy ranks for the transports to race through and gain altitude above the Thern temples.

"It had not been my intention to be distracted," I declared, "but we must settle this attack once and for all. Peace is impossible while our fleet lingers near Dor, and our chance of returning to the outer world diminishes with every hour."

Kantos Kan saluted and relayed the orders. In moments, the fleet transformed. The ten guard ships sped toward Omean, while the troopships closed ranks, preparing for the surge. Full speed ahead was given, and the fleet sprang through the air like greyhounds. Suddenly, the enemy fleet came into view—a ragged line stretching endlessly, three ships deep. Our onslaught was as sudden as lightning from a clear sky.

Every phase of my plan executed perfectly. Our ships mowed through the Thern battlecraft, and the V opened to reveal a broad lane. As the Therns rallied, a hundred thousand green warriors poured through their courts, while another hundred and fifty thousand leaned from the transports, raining accurate fire upon the ramparts.

Above the fiendish din of the battle below, the two great fleets engaged in a titanic struggle. The Helium battleships circled within the enemy line, a hallmark of Barsoomian naval warfare. Under Kantos Kan's command, the ships moved in high-speed circles, presenting difficult targets. Broadside after broadside hammered the Therns, who attempted to break the formation but failed, like trying to stop a buzzsaw with a bare hand.

From my deck beside Kantos Kan, I watched enemy ships dive in suicidal spirals, signaling their destruction. We maneuvered our circle of death until we hovered above the gardens where our green allies fought. The order to embark was passed, and we rose slowly to the center of the circle. By then, the Thern fire had ceased; they were weary and content to let us pass.

But our escape was not so easy. Hardly had we turned toward Omean when a great black line topped the northern horizon. It was another fleet of war. Kantos Kan's operator received a radio-aerogram from Zat Arras: *"Surrender, in the name of the Jeddak of Helium, for you cannot escape."*

The Therns, realizing we faced a second threat, renewed hostilities. Before Zat Arras could fire, we were locked in a fierce duel with the Thern fleet. Ship after ship fell beneath the pitiless fire. The situation deteriorated rapidly. I ordered the transports back to the gardens, telling the green allies to wreak vengeance, for by night there would be none left to avenge their wrongs.

Then, I saw the ten battleships guarding Omean returning at full speed, their stern batteries blazing. They were being pursued. Another great fleet was chasing them. My heart sank. The expedition was doomed. No one would return across the ice cap. How I wished to face Zat Arras with my longsword before dying! It was he who had caused our failure.

As I watched the ten ships and their pursuers, I saw the true horror: the fleet chasing them was none other than the fleet of the First Born, which should have been bottled up in Omean. A series of misfortunes had converged. Was the curse of Issus upon me? Was there a malign divinity in that hideous carcass? Refusing to believe it, I threw back my shoulders and ran to the deck below to join the men repelling boarders from a Thern craft.

In the wild lust of hand-to-hand combat, my old dauntless hopefulness returned. As Thern after Thern fell beneath my blade, I felt we might yet win. My presence inspired the men, who fell upon the whites with terrible ferocity. Within moments, we turned the tables, swarming their decks. Seeing their commander leap from the bows in surrender, I felt a surge of satisfaction.

I rejoined Kantos Kan, who had observed the battle below. A new thought struck him, and he ordered the colors of the Prince of Helium to be broken from every point of the flagship. A great cheer erupted, taken up by every vessel in our expedition. Then came Kantos Kan's coup. A signal legible to all was strung aloft: *"Men of Helium for the Prince of Helium against all his enemies."*

Soon, the colors of the Prince broke from Zat Arras's ships one by one. Battles raged between Zodangan soldiers and Helium crews, but eventually, the Prince's colors floated above every ship that had followed Zat Arras—except his flagship.

Zat Arras had brought five thousand ships. The sky was black with the three enormous fleets. It was Helium against the field now, reduced to countless individual duels. Maneuvering was impossible in the crowded, fire-split sky.

Zat Arras's flagship drew close to mine. I could see his thin features clearly. His Zodangan crew poured broadside after broadside into us, and we returned the fire with equal fury. The vessels closed until only a few yards intervened. Grapplers and boarders lined the contiguous rails. We prepared for the death struggle.

Just as the first grappling irons were hurled, I rushed to the deck to be with my men. As the ships collided with a slight shock, I forced my way through the lines and was the first to spring onto Zat Arras's deck. Behind me poured a yelling, cheering throng of Helium's finest fighters. Nothing could withstand them in the fever of battle lust.

Down went the Zodangans before that surging tide of war. As my men cleared the lower decks, I sprang to the forward deck where Zat Arras stood.

"You are my prisoner, Zat Arras," I cried. "Yield and you shall have quarter."

For a moment, I could not tell if he intended to yield or face me with a drawn sword. He hesitated, then threw down his arms and rushed to the opposite side of the deck. Before I could overtake him, he sprang to the rail and hurled himself headforemost into the awful depths below.

And thus came Zat Arras, Jeddak of Zodanga, to his end.

The strange battle continued. The Therns and the blacks had not combined against us; wherever a Thern ship met a First Born ship, it was a royal battle. I saw our salvation in this division. Wherever messages could be passed without interception, I ordered all vessels to withdraw to the west and south of the combatants. I sent an air scout to the green men in the gardens to re-embark and ordered the transports to join us.

My commanders were instructed to draw any enemy toward a ship of their hereditary foe, forcing the two to engage while we withdrew. This stratagem worked to perfection. Just before sunset, I saw the remnants of my once-mighty fleet gathered twenty miles southwest of the still-terrific battle between the blacks and whites.

I transferred Xodar to another battleship and sent him with all the transports and five thousand battleships directly overhead to the Temple of Issus. Carthoris, Kantos Kan, and I took the remaining ships and headed for the entrance to Omean.

Our plan was a combined assault on Issus at dawn. Tars Tarkas with the green warriors and Hor Vastus with the red men, guided by Xodar, were to land in the garden of Issus or the surrounding plains. Carthoris, Kantos Kan, and I would lead our smaller force from the Sea of Omean through the pits beneath the temple, a route Carthoris knew well.

I now learned the cause of my ten ships' retreat from the shaft's mouth. When they arrived, the navy of the First Born was already issuing from it. Fully twenty vessels had emerged, and though they fought desperately to stem the tide rolling from the black pit, the odds were too great, and they were forced to flee.

With great caution, we approached the shaft under cover of darkness. At a distance of several miles, I halted the fleet, and Carthoris went ahead alone on a one-man flier to reconnoiter. Half an hour later, he returned, reporting no sign of patrol boats or enemies. We moved swiftly and noiselessly forward.

At the mouth of the shaft, we stopped for all vessels to reach their appointed stations. Then, with the flagship, I dropped quickly into the black depths, followed one by one by the others. We staked everything on the chance that we could reach the temple by the subterranean way, leaving no guard at the shaft's mouth. We lacked the force to withstand the vast navy of the First Born had they returned, so we relied on boldness, hoping the First Born on guard would mistake us for their own returning fleet.

Such proved to be the case. Four hundred of my fleet of five hundred rested safely upon the bosom of Omean before the first shot was fired. The battle was short and hot, but the outcome was inevitable; the First Born, careless in their fancied security, had left but a handful of ancient hulks to guard their mighty harbor.

At Carthoris's suggestion, we landed our prisoners under guard on a couple of larger islands and towed the ships of the First Born to the shaft, wedging them securely inside the great well. We then activated the buoyancy rays on the remaining vessels, letting them rise to further block the passage to Omean as they contacted the lodged ships.

We felt confident that the returning First Born would take time to reach the surface, giving us ample opportunity to make for the subterranean passages leading to Issus. One of my first acts was to hasten personally with a good-sized force to the island of the submarine, taking it without resistance from the small guard there.

I found the submarine in its pool and placed a strong guard upon it and the island, waiting for Carthoris and the others. Among the prisoners was Yersted, commander of the submarine. He recognized me from the three trips I had taken with him during my captivity among the First Born.

"How does it seem," I asked him, "to have the tables turned? To be prisoner of your erstwhile captive?"

He smiled, a grim expression pregnant with hidden meaning. "It will not be for long, John Carter," he replied. "We have been expecting you and we are prepared."

"So it would appear," I answered. "You were all ready to become my prisoners with scarce a blow struck on either side."

"The fleet must have missed you," he said. "But it will return to Omean, and then that will be a very different matter—for John Carter."

"I do not know that the fleet has missed me as yet," I said, though he did not grasp my meaning and only looked puzzled.

"Many prisoners travel to Issus in your grim craft, Yersted?" I asked.

"Very many," he assented.

"Might you remember one whom men called Dejah Thoris?"
