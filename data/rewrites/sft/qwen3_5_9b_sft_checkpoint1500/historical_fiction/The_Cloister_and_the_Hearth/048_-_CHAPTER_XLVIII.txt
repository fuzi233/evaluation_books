Catherine sat in a quiet, heavy dismay, her mind racing with the terrifying arithmetic of time. Gerard would return within a month, perhaps sooner, yet she had not once looked upon his betrothed. A shadow fell over her soul regarding the girl's fate, an uncertainty that curdled into something sickening whenever she let it linger. "Oh, Kate," she whispered, her voice trembling, "what if she has already gone and ruined everything?" Her daughter, Little Kate, protested gently, insisting that Margaret would never be so wicked. But Catherine shook her head, her eyes clouded with a deep, maternal sorrow. "Ah, my lass, you know not the madness that grips young hearts unmoored from a mother's hand," she murmured, recounting stories of girls who plunged into rivers for lovers they would despise a week later, their emotions as volatile as the weather, jumping from despair to hope with a terrifying speed. To her, men were monsters who built their traps with cruel intent, knowing full well that drowning oneself was no different than sleeping, a final act where the water washed away the sin entirely. She spoke of her own near-fatal mistake years past, when a quarrel with Eli had driven her to the edge of a canal, tempted by the promise of dramatic reconciliation and heavenly reward, only to stop because she had nearly finished sewing a new gown. That moment, she explained, proved that life itself was the true treasure, superior to any fleeting passion or grievance. Little Kate clung to the hope that Margaret loved her father too deeply to abandon him, reminding her mother that he was everything to her. Yet Catherine remained unconvinced, placing her faith not in the wisdom of the aged, but in the innocence of the unborn. "Keep your heart high, dear mother," Kate urged, trying to soothe the tempest within her. She finally turned to a solemn entreaty, begging her mother to cease predicting the sex of Margaret's baby, arguing that nature offered equal chances for boy or girl. This plea, however reasonable, was met with immediate and fierce clamor. "Do you dare threaten me with a daughter!" Catherine cried out, her voice rising in indignation. "While I have you here, I need no more girls! Where is the joy in setting a lass upon my knee when I cannot see Gerard again? I tell you it is settled."
