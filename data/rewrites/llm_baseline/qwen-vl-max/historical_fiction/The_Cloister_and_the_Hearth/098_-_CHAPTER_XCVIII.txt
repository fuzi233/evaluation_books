Two weeks later, a frail and hunched figure approached the Dominican convent on the outskirts of Gouda, seeking an audience with Brother Ambrose, who now governed the monastery in the absence of the prior—deceased recently—and before the arrival of his successor. The man was Gerard, returning to the cloister not as a man of faith renewed, but as one whose life had grown too heavy to bear. He came not to serve, but to rest.

He entered as a novice, though only in name. In truth, he was broken—body and spirit—and knew it well. This place, quiet and sacred, was where he wished to lay down his burdens, near Ambrose, his trusted friend, and among the monks whose company had always been a solace. His home, once full of warmth and purpose, now echoed with memories of her—the woman whose absence left a void no sermon or sacrament could fill. The weight of those memories pressed upon him like a physical pain; the house was no longer a refuge, but a prison of longing.

Ambrose, who understood both Gerard’s worth and the depth of his sorrow, harbored a quiet hope that healing might yet be possible. Perhaps with time, care, and gentle guidance, the vicar could recover and return to his parish. With this intention, he assigned Gerard a modest but comfortable cell above the main gate, where the air was fresh and the light soft. He strictly forbade fasting or any harsh penance, insisting that the body must be preserved if the soul were to find peace.

But within days, the new prior arrived—an austere man, stern in manner and uncompromising in rule. At first, he devoted himself entirely to reforming lax customs and restoring order. Yet when he learned that the vicar of Gouda had taken up residence as a novice, he reacted with sharp disapproval. “It is well,” he declared, “but let him first surrender his vicarage—or leave. I will have no fat parsons under my roof.”

The prior summoned Gerard immediately. The latter obeyed, stepping into the chamber with slow, measured steps. The moment their eyes met, both men froze. Recognition flashed between them—sudden, startling, almost unbearable.

“Clement!” one breathed.

“Jerome!” the other replied.

The world seemed to pause. A past long buried stirred anew.
