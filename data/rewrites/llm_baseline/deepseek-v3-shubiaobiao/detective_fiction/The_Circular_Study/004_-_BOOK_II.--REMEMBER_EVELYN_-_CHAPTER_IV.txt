Mr. Gryce found himself facing a puzzle unlike any he had encountered in years. The victim, Mr. Adams, had been a recluse—no family, no neighbors, no obvious ties beyond his mute servant. Even now, hours after the news of his death had spread, no one had come forward with questions or information. Unusual, given the man’s notoriety in the district. But then, everything about Adams had been unusual.

The absence of witnesses only sharpened Gryce’s interest. When the last of the reporters and officials finally cleared out near midnight, he seized the chance to pursue two lingering questions.

The first led him to rouse a dozing boy slumped in a hallway chair.

"Up, Jake," Gryce said. "I need you to show me exactly where that woman approached you today."

The boy rubbed his eyes, fumbling for his cap. "Can’t I see my mother first? She’ll be worried."

"She knows you’re here. I sent word hours ago. She vouched for you—said you’re honest."

Jake brightened. "She’s good to me. Wouldn’t do to lie to her." He jammed the cap onto his head. "Ready."

Gryce led him into the quiet streets, pausing before a shop window where two stuffed frogs dueled with tiny swords.

"Was she pretty?" Gryce asked.

Jake hesitated. "Scared me, more like. Fine clothes, though—real fine."

"Troubled?"

"Not crying, but… she gripped my arm hard. Dragged me to the drugstore. Would’ve run if she hadn’t paid me first."

Gryce moved on. *Not young. An old flame, then. Dangerous.*

Outside the drugstore, he stopped. "Show me where she stood while you went in."

Jake pointed. "She left before I came out. Headed for the curb, waved—not for a streetcar. Maybe a cab?"

Gryce’s brow smoothed. "Any cabs around?"

Jake shrugged. Gryce handed him off to an assistant and returned to the house.

Inside, he found Styles waiting. "Anyone for me?"

"Electrician. Name’s Hines. Parlor."

"Good. Keep others out."

Upstairs was empty. Gryce entered the parlor, exchanged brisk words with the young electrician, then led him to the study. The body was gone, but a single candle burned near the door.

Gryce pressed a button on the central table. "Blow out the candle."

Darkness swallowed the room—then erupted into violet light.

Hines blinked. "Slide mechanism?"

"Press another." The glow shifted—pink, green, white, a sickly yellow.

"Examine it," Gryce said.

Hines did, finding only amateur craftsmanship. "Works well, though."

Gryce frowned. "Self-taught?"

"With those books, yes."

Disappointed, Gryce gestured to a birdcage suspended high on the wall. "Another toy. Lower it."

Hines found a hidden button. The cage descended.

Inside, a starling ruffled its feathers but stayed silent.

Gryce set the cage down, pressed another button—then froze.

The doorway to the anteroom was sliding shut.

Hines chuckled. "Convenient, if you hate getting up."

Gryce didn’t laugh. The gap narrowed—a foot, an inch—then sealed with a metallic *click*.

Steel.

Hines’s smile faded. "No handle on this side."

They searched for a release. Nothing.

Gryce whistled sharply, then hammered the barrier with an alpenstock.

An hour passed before Styles heard. Five more before they chiseled an opening.

The room had been designed to trap.

And Gryce was beginning to suspect why.
