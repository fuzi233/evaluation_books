To grasp the contours of my past, one must first immerse oneself in the austere simplicity of my early years. At seven, my existence revolved around the meticulous rituals instilled by my parents, now lost to me, their lessons still lingering like the scent of a long-forgotten garden. I was taught to maintain the tidiness of my hair at all hours, to fold my clothes with precision, and to record the events of each day in my small diary. The evening hymn, a soothing melody, would follow, accompanied by the peaceful slumber of childhood. Those tranquil nights were a testament to the harmony of my early years, before the darkness that would later shroud my life.

As I grew older, the hymn gave way to bitter meditations, and my sleep became fitful, haunted by the restless thoughts that plagued my pillow. Yet, two habits persisted – folding my clothes with the same dedication as before and keeping my diary. The former linked me to the happiness of my childhood, a fleeting glimpse of a life untouched by the turmoil that would soon beset me. The latter, initially a tool for discipline, proved unexpectedly valuable in serving the whims of a wealthy patron, Mr. Franklin Blake.

I reside in a small town in Brittany, surrounded by a tight-knit circle of English friends, where the gentle rhythms of a Protestant clergyman and a modest market provide a refuge from the surrounding sea of Catholicism. A letter from England has finally reached me, and I find myself thrust back into the spotlight, my insignificant existence recalled by Mr. Blake. He seeks to revive the scandal of the Moonstone, a wound barely closed, and asks me to recount my experiences during a visit to Aunt Verinder's house in London. In exchange, he offers pecuniary compensation, devoid of the sensitivity one might expect from someone of his standing. The pain of recalling those memories will be compounded by the knowledge that I will be compensated with a new wound, inflicted by the cold calculation of Mr. Blake's cheque.

Without my diary, I fear that I would not have been able to earn my reward honestly. It is a faithful chronicler, capturing every detail of my time at Aunt Verinder's, from the smallest particulars to the most significant events. I have recorded everything as it occurred, thanks to the discipline of my early years.
