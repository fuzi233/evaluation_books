The sun cast a warm glow through the windows of Fieldhead as Phoebe descended to the dining room for breakfast. Her cousins, Isabella and Gertrude, joined her, and to their surprise, she proposed a walk. "Who will join me for a stroll in the gardens?" she asked, her eyes sparkling with enthusiasm. The two girls exchanged hesitant glances before Isabella replied, "I'll go, Gertrude?" Their mother nodded her approval, and the trio donned their bonnets, setting out into the crisp morning air.

Phoebe led them through the dew-kissed grass, pointing out the tender shoots of spring bulbs pushing through the earth. As they walked, the scent of blooming lavender wafted through the air, and Phoebe breathed deeply, her eyes lighting up with pleasure. Though she rarely took an interest in the company of her female cousins, today she seemed determined to engage them in conversation. The topics were mundane – the weather, the latest news from the village – but Phoebe imbued them with a sense of excitement, her words dancing with a quiet joy.

As they turned back towards the house, Phoebe lingered behind her cousins, speaking with the gardener, John, about the preparation for the upcoming harvest festival. Her absence was brief, but long enough for the sun to dip lower in the sky, casting long shadows across the garden. Upon entering the house, Phoebe made her excuses and retired to her chambers, citing a sudden fatigue.

Her cousins inquired about her well-being at luncheon, but Phoebe waved off their concerns, assuring them she was merely feeling a touch under the weather. An hour later, when she failed to reappear, Gertrude sought her out, finding her seated at the foot of her bed, her head in her hands. "Are you unwell?" Gertrude asked gently.

Phoebe looked up, her eyes red-rimmed. "A bit," she replied softly. Her cousins exchanged worried glances, sensing a change in Phoebe's demeanor, a subtlety they couldn't quite grasp. Though she rejoined them for dinner and the evening's activities, a quiet melancholy seemed to settle over her, like a mist that refused to lift.
