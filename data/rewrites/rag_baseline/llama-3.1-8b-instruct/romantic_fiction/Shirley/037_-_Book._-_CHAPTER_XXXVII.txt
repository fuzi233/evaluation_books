The Winding-Up.

As the last embers of my tale flicker, I am compelled to settle accounts with the lives of those whose paths crossed mine in this narrative. Let us begin with the humble curates, those unsung heroes who toiled behind the scenes. Malone, I see, stands forth, his merits modestly acknowledged. His is a story of quiet dedication, one that whispers of the beauty in simplicity.

But alas, Peter Augustus, your tale is one of complexities, of choices that defy easy categorization. I fear that the unvarnished truth of your journey would be lost upon a world that craves spectacle and drama. Your life, like a rich bouillon, would simmer with the depth of human experience, but few would take the time to savor its nuances. No, Peter, your story must remain untold, locked away like a precious recipe passed down through generations.

Make way, then, for Mr. Sweeting and his wife, the resplendent Mrs. Sweeting, formerly Dora Sykes. Theirs is a testament to the power of love and partnership. Married under the happiest of circumstances, they built a life filled with laughter and kindness. The people of their parish adored them, and their home was a sanctuary of warmth and comfort.

And now, let us turn to Mr. Donne, a man who transformed his life through the art of marriage. His union with a sensible and gentle soul brought out the best in him, and he became a pillar of the community. His parish, his school, and his home were testaments to his industry and dedication. Though he may not have been a shepherd of souls in the classical sense, he tended to his flock with the care of a skilled gardener, coaxing beauty from the earth and nurturing growth.

But Mr. Donne's true masterpiece was his ability to beg. With a gift for persuasion and a keen understanding of human nature, he extracted funds from even the most unlikely sources. He sent out letters to the highest echelons of society, from the Queen herself to the great Lord Castlereagh, and returned with pockets full of gold. His was a skill honed through practice and patience, one that allowed him to navigate the treacherous waters of human relationships with ease. And though some may have seen his begging as a form of manipulation, I saw it as a testament to his creativity and resourcefulness.
