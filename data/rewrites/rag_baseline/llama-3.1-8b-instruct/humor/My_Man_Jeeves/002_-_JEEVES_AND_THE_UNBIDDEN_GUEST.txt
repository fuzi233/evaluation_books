The misty morning light crept through the windows of my flat, casting an ethereal glow on the worn Persian rug. I emerged from the bathroom, feeling invigorated from the cold shower, and began to get dressed. The previous evening's assertiveness with Jeeves still lingered, a pleasant memory that lingered like the scent of freshly brewed coffee wafting from the kitchen. My rebellion against his dictates had been a necessary act of self-expression, a reminder that even the most fastidious valet should not dictate every aspect of one's wardrobe. The Country Gentleman hat, a personal triumph, sat atop my dresser, a testament to my growing independence.

As I reached for the studs, a knock at the door interrupted my reverie. I opened it to find Jeeves standing in the hallway, his expression as impassive as ever. "A visitor, sir," he announced, his voice low and measured. "Lady Malvern."

I raised an eyebrow. Who was Lady Malvern, and why was she visiting at such an ungodly hour? "Put out something suitable, Jeeves," I instructed, trying to recall our previous encounters. Ah yes, Lady Malvern – a friend of my Aunt Agatha, a writer of some renown. Her book on social conditions in India had been well-received, I recalled.

I followed Jeeves to the sitting room, where I found myself trying to place the connection between Lady Malvern and my aunt. The image of her sharp features and piercing gaze flashed in my mind, accompanied by the faint recollection of a lively discussion at lunch. I shook off the cobwebs and focused on the present moment. Jeeves handed me a suit, and I began to dress, my thoughts turning to the possibilities of this unexpected visit.

Upon completing my attire, I descended to the sitting room, where Lady Malvern and a Lord Pershore were waiting. I greeted them with a sense of curiosity, intrigued by the prospect of rekindling an acquaintance. As I took my seat, Jeeves discreetly excused himself, leaving us to our conversation. I turned to Lady Malvern, hoping to revive the spark of our previous encounter. "Good morning, Lady Malvern. It's been too long. What brings you to London?"
