Monna Brigida's Conversion.

As we walked alongside the Arno, the golden light of late afternoon cast a serene glow over the rooftops of Florence, illuminating the cobblestone streets that led to the Piazza. Romola, my cousin, had mentioned that another woman was expecting her arrival, and I knew that she referred to Brigida, our family's respected elder. Though I had grown accustomed to Brigida's strict adherence to the Piagnone way, I sensed that she was troubled. As we strolled, we chanced upon a group of young men, their faces set in determined expressions, gathered before the stall of Tessa, a humble contadina. Romola, noticing Tessa's distress, asked me to wait for a moment, but I hastily replied, "No, I'll return to Boni's shop – I'll await your return there."

In truth, I harbored certain...let's call them "doubts" regarding my appearance. The Piagnones, with their stern views on vanity, made me feel uneasy about the adornments I wore. Rouge, false hair, and intricate embroidery, once considered the epitome of elegance, now seemed to carry a stigma. Their influence was palpable, like the scent of incense wafting through the air, altering the way I perceived the world. I couldn't help but think of Romola, still young and beautiful, navigating the complexities of a society that demanded much from women. As a woman in her fifties, I had grown accustomed to relying on the admiration of others to sustain my spirits. But of late, I found myself struggling to reconcile my desire for vanity with the Piagnone ideals.

As I pondered these thoughts, I caught sight of my reflection in a nearby windowpane. My skin, once smooth and fair, now showed the telltale signs of age – the faint lines etched into my cheeks, the wisps of grey hair framing my face. My heart sank, and for a moment, I considered abandoning my usual routine. But then I thought of Berta, my younger sister, who would surely laugh at my attempts to conceal my age. Her words would cut deep, and I knew that every woman who wasn't a Piagnone would view me with a mixture of pity and amusement. At fifty-five, I was still relatively young, and all I needed was a touch of artifice to restore my confidence.
