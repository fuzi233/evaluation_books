As the scent of baking bread wafted through the air, Catherine busied herself in the kitchen, her hands moving with the precision of a seasoned cook. Her thoughts, however, were elsewhere, weighed down by the unspoken words that hung between her and her daughter, Kate. The afternoon sun cast a warm glow through the windows, but Catherine's heart felt heavy, burdened by the secrets and silences that threatened to consume their small household.

She had been meaning to visit Margaret Brandt at Sevenbergen for what felt like an eternity, but every time she considered it, something would go awry – the weather, the chores, or Peter Buyskens' mule would be out of the way. Today, however, was different. Today, she had decided to set aside her doubts and excuses, to follow her heart and pay the visit she had been putting off for so long.

But just as she was about to reveal her plans to Eli, her husband, he spoke up, his voice firm and resolute. "Aye, my dear, I forbid you to go," he said, his eyes narrowing slightly as he watched her reaction. Catherine felt a surge of defiance rise within her, but she bit it back, knowing that Eli's disapproval was rooted in his own fears and misconceptions.

When she was alone with Kate, however, she let her true feelings spill out. "I'm a silly hen, always cackling and making a fuss, never getting anything done," she confessed, her voice trembling with frustration. "But I swear, Kate, if I'd known Gerard was in trouble, I'd have gone to Sevenbergen in a heartbeat, no matter what the cost."

Kate looked up at her mother, her eyes wide with understanding. "Nay, mother, with patience, father will come round," she said softly, trying to reassure her. But Catherine knew that patience was a luxury they couldn't afford, not when Gerard was locked away in the Stadthouse tower, a prisoner of his own mistakes.

As she poured the batter into the oven, the aroma of freshly baked bread filled the air, but Catherine's mind was elsewhere, consumed by the tangled web of secrets and lies that threatened to tear their family apart. She thought back to the day Gerard had first fallen in love with Margaret Brandt, and how she had tried to warn him away, fearing the consequences of their affair. But Gerard had been headstrong, and now he paid the price.
