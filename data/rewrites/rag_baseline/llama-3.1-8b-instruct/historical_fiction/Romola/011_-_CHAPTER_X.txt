Under the Plane-Tree.

It was the third week since Tito had handed over his florins to Cennini, and as he strolled towards the Via de' Bardi, his face reflected the serenity of a summer morning. His dark hair was still tousled from the heat of the previous day, and the slight sweat that glistened on his brow only added to the sheen of his skin, like the gentle patina on a well-polished terracotta pot. He walked with a loose-limbed ease, his eyes scanning the narrow streets as if taking in the vibrant colors and sounds of the city, much like a painter might study the play of light on a canvas. His gaze was untroubled, his expression open and inviting, as if the very essence of the Festa had seeped into his pores. 

As he turned out of the warm sunlight into the cool shade of a narrow street, he removed the black berretta, or simple cap, that crowned his dark curls, revealing a smooth, finely-formed brow that seemed chiseled from the same earthy clay as the ancient stones of the city. His eyes, a deep, rich brown, sparkled with a quiet amusement, as if he were privy to some secret joke known only to himself. His presence drew attention, not because of any affectation or pretension, but because of the effortless charm that seemed to emanate from him like the fragrance of fresh basil wafting from a summer kitchen.

The air was alive with the excitement of the Festa, the throngs of people spilling out of the main streets and into the narrower alleys in search of new sights and experiences. Tito moved with the crowd, his footsteps light and unhurried, as if he were savoring each moment, each sensation. His eyes scanned the faces around him, taking in the vibrant colors and textures of the festive attire, the laughter and chatter that filled the air. He was a part of the scene, yet apart from it, a observer and participant at the same time, like a chef who tastes and adjusts the seasoning of a dish even as he serves it.
