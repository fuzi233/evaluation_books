As the sun began to wane on a Thursday afternoon, casting long shadows across the rustic landscape of Sevenbergen, Margaret's anxiety grew. Her household was woefully unprepared for the impending arrival of Gerard, whose thoughtlessness often left her scrambling to conjure something edible from the bare cupboards. Tears pricked at the corners of her eyes as she sought out Martin Wittenhaagen, the grizzled old soldier, for help. He was the only one who might salvage her evening, but she feared his absence might prove too great a risk.

Martin, however, was not one to shy away from a challenge. He scratched his weathered scalp, considering the options. "What can I do?" he asked, his deep voice laced with a hint of amusement.

Margaret's desperation was palpable. "It's Thursday; you're supposed to hunt today, and I counted on you. We have nothing to put before my servant, who loves me dearly."

Martin's expression turned wry. He rummaged through his pouch, producing a parchment adorned with the grand seal of Philip, Duke of Burgundy. The stipend and license granted to him, an archer in the Duke's service, were clearly outlined: four merks yearly, paid by the almoner, and the right to hunt three arrows once a week – on Thursdays, specifically – in any of the Duke's forests in Holland, provided the Duke himself was not hunting. If so, Martin was to stay clear, lest he face a fine and worse.

Margaret's shoulders sagged in defeat. Martin, sensing her despair, placed a reassuring hand on her arm. "Cheer up, mistress," he said, his voice low and soothing. "For your sake, I'll take a risk. I've done it for others who weren't worth your little finger. It's not a perilous venture. I'll just slip into the skirts of the forest nearby. Odds are, they'll drive a hare or fawn within range of my arrow."

With a nod, Margaret entrusted Martin with her promise: to stay hidden and avoid detection. He accepted, his face set in a determined line. Taking his trusty bow and three arrows, he slipped into the nearby woods, the forest's secrets whispering through the trees. The distant sound of horns carried on the breeze, signaling the start of the hunt. Martin settled behind a sturdy oak, his English yew bow, renowned for its power, strung and ready.
