Only then did he hail his companions to join him in the pursuit, launching himself up the hill. But scarcely had he covered half the ascent when the silhouette of Martin Wittenhaagen emerged, a bent bow drawn taut in his hand. Upon seeing Martin poised thus, Eric Wouverman instinctively darted behind the nearest tree, shrinking his form until he was nothing more than a shadow against the bark. Martin's mastery with the weapon was legendary, and the lifeless dog lying nearby served as a grim testament to his lethal precision.

Wouverman peered cautiously around the rough hide of the tree; there, fixed in the wood, was the arrowhead, gleaming with a malevolent intent. It was aimed directly at him. Terrified, he dared not shift his position. Minutes passed in tense silence as he held his breath, waiting for the inevitable strike. Finally, his companions arrived in overwhelming numbers. With a derisive laugh, Martin vanished from sight, and shortly thereafter, the sound of his mule's hooves echoed as he rode away. The remaining hunters surged forward, only to find themselves outwitted and outrun.

From the commanding height, the glade stretched out—a narrow corridor seemingly without end. Far below, they spotted Gerard and Margaret sprinting across the grass, appearing no larger than gnats. Behind them thundered Martin, driving the mule *ventre à terre*, his legs pumping furiously. The hunters had been bested not merely by speed, but by cunning.

To understand Martin's audacious strategy, one must look beyond mere coincidence, for while chance often mimics design, it rarely orchestrates such precise chaos. Just as a startled hare might tumble over a briar precisely when a shot rings out, raising false hopes before vanishing unharmed, so too did fate intervene here. At the very moment the crossbow bolt whistled harmlessly overhead, the burgomaster's mule stepped squarely into a hidden rabbit-hole. She stumbled, collapsing under the combined weight of her riders. Gerard caught Margaret in his arms, but her momentum dragged him down with her, scattering the principal actors across the soil like fallen leaves.

The docile mule rose immediately, trembling violently, while Martin scrambled up beside her. Seeing that only one pursuer remained, he executed a brilliant tactical pivot: he urged the terrified mule onward, forcing the young lovers to flee on foot, while he personally engaged the lone hunter. Once he had secured his companions, he mounted them both, running alongside the mule until his lungs burned, then swapping places with Gerard. In this relentless rotation, the runners never fatigued. Long before the exhausted hunters could recover, every trace of the fugitives was erased from the landscape. Defeated and crestfallen, Dierich and his men turned back, leaving behind their leader and his winged bloodhound to mourn their failure.
