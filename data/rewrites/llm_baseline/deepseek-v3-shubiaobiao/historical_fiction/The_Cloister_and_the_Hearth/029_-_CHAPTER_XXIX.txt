Gerard staggered at the sudden news, his face flushing pale then red with shock. Then his jaw tightened in anger, for even the gentlest soul, when pressed too hard, will turn and fight like a cornered boar. His fingers curled into fists, ready to strike—but the next instant, his resolve faltered. “My axe is in the Rhine,” he muttered bitterly.

They debated their next move. Prudence whispered *avoid the village*; Hunger growled *find food*. Hunger won.

They cut across the fields, stopping at a haystack to steal two bundles, then burrowed into a dry ditch—hidden, but nettles stung their skin. Taking turns, they ventured out and returned with stolen turnips, gnawing on them in furtive silence as dusk bled into night.

When darkness swallowed the land, they crept out, shivering in the lashing rain. The road beyond the village was a black void, the wind howling like a wounded beast. They moved blindly, unseen and unheard, ghosts slipping past unseen foes. The storm itself seemed to claw at them, the night so thick they might as well have been carving their way through solid stone.

By the time the moon rose, they were leagues from Dusseldorf—yet still they trudged on. Then, looming ahead, a vast building materialized from the gloom.

“Courage!” Denys cried. “I know this place—a convent! We’ve crossed into Juliers. Cologne holds no sway here.”

A moment later, the walls closed around them, and they were safe.
