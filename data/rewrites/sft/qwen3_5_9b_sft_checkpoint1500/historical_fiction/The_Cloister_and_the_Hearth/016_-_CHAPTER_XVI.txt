Martin Wittenhaagen stood rigid at the stairwell's base, his yew bow drawn taut until the string hummed near his ear, a steel knife gripped in his other hand. A jolt of pure astonishment froze him when he saw the men return empty-handed, Gerard nowhere in sight. His aim faltered, the weapon slipping down as his mouth gaped in disbelief. The intruders, however, stared back with equal bewilderment, their faces twisted by the shock of catching him poised for slaughter.

"Why, mates," Dierich barked, breaking the silence, "was the old fellow sizing us up for an arrow?"

"Rubbish!" Martin roared, forcing his stony demeanor back into place. "I was merely testing this new string. See? If you doubt me, I will unloose it right now."

"Hmph!" Dierich grunted, eyeing him with deep suspicion. "There is more in you than meets the eye, friend. Put a log on the fire, and let us warm our skins before we descend further."

A roaring hearth was kindled almost instantly, casting dancing shadows against the stone walls. The men clustered around the flames, stripping off their heavy cloaks and letting the heat singe the ends of their matted hair. It was then, amidst the crackling wood and rising smoke, that the shrill, piercing cries erupted from Margaret's chamber.

Every muscle coiled tight. One of the men snatched up a tallow candle and bolted up the steep steps toward the bedrooms.

Martin surged upward in haste, his heart hammering against his ribs. Confused by the sudden wails and terrified by the stranger's intent to pry, he lunged to block the path.

In a flash, Dierich shot out from behind, seizing Martin's arm with iron grip and shouting for the others to hold him fast. The candle-bearer slipped past the chaos, but the remaining men swarmed Martin like wolves. A brutal, tumbling fight ensued, sending the three men sprawling across the floorboards repeatedly, Martin pinned beneath their combined weight. Finally, they subdued the ancient giant, binding his hands and feet with the very rope they had intended for Gerard.

Martin let out a guttural groan. Through the haze of the struggle, he realized the intruder had already breached the sanctum of Margaret's room. Now, helpless and bound, he could only watch.

"Aye, gnash those teeth, you old rascal," Dierich panted, loosening his grip. "You shall not use them again."

"I am convinced," another man muttered, wiping blood from his brow, "that our lives hung by a thread while this brute remained loose."

"He makes me suspect Gerard is not far behind," someone else chimed in.

"No such fortune," Dierich retorted, shaking out his limbs. "Listen! Jorian Ketel has been loitering in that girl's chamber far too long. Some of you, best go check on him."

The crude laughter that followed the command died abruptly as the sound of frantic footsteps thundered overhead.

"Oh, look who returns at last!" Dierich shouted, his voice echoing through the stairwell. "Well, Jorian, pray tell us what mischief requires attention up above!"
