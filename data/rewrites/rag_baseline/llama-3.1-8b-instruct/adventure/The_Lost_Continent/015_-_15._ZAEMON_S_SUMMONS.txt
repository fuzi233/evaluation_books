Silence seeped through the narrow alleys of Atlantis like a chill. Zaemon huddled in his small, makeshift hut, surrounded by the faint scent of wet earth and decaying leaves. The air vibrated with the distant rumble of underground tremors, a constant reminder of the unforgiving landscape that sprawled beyond the fragile shelter. He clutched his worn leather satchel, its worn straps a testament to countless journeys through the treacherous terrain. The weight of his instrument, a battered lyre, pressed against his back, a reminder of the music he had lost – the melodies, the harmonies, the very essence of a civilization that once thrived in this forsaken land.

Outside, the mountains churned and convulsed, spewing forth noxious fumes and molten rock. Zaemon's eyes had grown accustomed to the eerie glow that danced across the horizon, a perpetual twilight that seemed to sear the very soul. He had learned to navigate this unforgiving world, to read the signs of the land and the creatures that inhabited it. The lizards, with their scaly skin and deadly spines, roamed the forests, their massive forms a constant threat. Zaemon had seen them up close, beheld their power and their fury, and lived to tell the tale.

As he packed his belongings, a faint memory stirred within him – a melody, half-forgotten, that echoed through the desolate landscape. He recalled the sound of laughter, the warmth of a fire, and the gentle touch of a loved one. The memory was fleeting, lost in the mists of time, but it left behind a yearning, a deep ache that resonated through his being. Zaemon's fingers instinctively strayed to the lyre, seeking solace in the familiar curves of its neck and body. He ran his thumb over the strings, coaxing forth a mournful sigh that hung in the stillness like a challenge to the unforgiving land.

In this moment, Zaemon knew he was not alone. The creatures of the forest watched him, their unblinking eyes a reminder that he was but a small, insignificant speck in a world that cared little for his existence. Yet, he felt a spark within him, a flame that flickered with each note he drew from the lyre. It was a flame that refused to be extinguished, a beacon that illuminated the darkness, and guided him through the treacherous landscape of his own heart
