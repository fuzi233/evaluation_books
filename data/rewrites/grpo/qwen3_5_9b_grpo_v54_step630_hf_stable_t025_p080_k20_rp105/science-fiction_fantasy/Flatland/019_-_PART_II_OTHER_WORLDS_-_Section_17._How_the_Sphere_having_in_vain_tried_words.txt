The collision was absolute. I drove my sharpest right angle into the Stranger with a ferocity that should have shattered any ordinary Circle, yet he did not break. Instead, he flowed like smoke through my grasp, slipping unarrestably from my contact. He did not edge left or right; he simply ceased to exist within our plane, vanishing into a terrifying blankness. Yet, his voice remained, echoing in the silence where he had stood.

"Still you refuse to listen to reason?" the Sphere demanded, his tone shifting from disappointment to desperate resolve. "I had hoped to find in you, a man of sense and an accomplished mathematician, a worthy apostle for the Gospel of the Third Dimension—a message I am permitted to preach but once in a millennium. But logic has failed. Words are insufficient. Deeds alone shall proclaim the truth. Listen closely, my friend."

"I have told you," the Sphere continued, his voice resonating with a strange omniscience, "that from my vantage point in Space, I perceive the interior of every closed thing you know. Look at the cupboard near you. I see it filled with boxes—your boxes, which lack tops and bottoms—and stacks of money. I see the ledgers of account. I watched you lock that cupboard an hour ago; I know the key rests in your hand. Yet, I descend from above, and the doors remain untouched. Now, I am inside, retrieving a ledger. Now, I ascend with it."

I rushed to the closet and flung the door open. One of the tablets was indeed gone. In the corner of the room, the Stranger materialized with a mocking laugh, and simultaneously, the missing tablet materialized upon the floorboards. I snatched it up. There could be no doubt; it was the stolen record.

A groan of sheer horror escaped my lips. Was I mad? Had my mind fractured under the weight of this impossible sight?

"See now," the Stranger said, his voice calm amidst my turmoil. "My explanation is the only one that fits the phenomena. What you call solid objects are merely surfaces; what you call space is nothing but a vast, flat plane. I dwell in Space, looking down upon the insides of your world. You too could leave this plane if you possessed the will. A mere upward or downward motion would grant you the vision I possess."

He paused, ascending slightly so I could glimpse the Hexagon neighbor and his family in their apartments, then the interior of the distant theater, and finally a Circle studying in his home. "As I rise further, the scale shrinks, but the view expands. Now, as a final proof, allow me to give you a touch. Just a light pressure upon your stomach. It will cause no serious injury, and the slight pain you feel will be negligible compared to the enlightenment you gain."

Before I could protest, a searing lance of pain shot through my abdomen. A demoniacal laughter seemed to erupt from within my own chest. Moments later, the sharp agony subsided, leaving only a dull, throbbing ache. The Stranger reappeared, growing larger with every passing second. "There, have I not harmed you much? If this does not convince you, nothing will. What say you?"

My resolve snapped. To endure such arbitrary torment at the hands of a magician who could manipulate the very organs of my body was intolerable. I needed to pin him against the wall until help arrived.

Again, I drove my hardest angle into him, screaming for aid and alarming the entire household. At that moment, I believed the Stranger had slipped below our plane, struggling to regain his footing. He remained motionless while I pressed against him with renewed vigor, shouting until my voice cracked.

A convulsive shudder racked the Sphere's form. "This must not happen," I heard him think, the words vibrating in my skull. "Either he must accept reason, or I must employ the last resort of civilization."

Then, in a loud, urgent command, he addressed me: "Listen! No outsider must witness this. Send your Wife back immediately before she enters the apartment. The Gospel of Three Dimensions must not be thwarted here. Not thus must the fruits of a thousand years of waiting be wasted. I hear her footsteps. Back! Back! Away from me, or you shall go with me—where you know not—into the Land of Three Dimensions!"

"Fool! Madman! Irregular!" I screamed, refusing to yield. "Never will I release thee! Thou shalt pay the penalty of thy impostures!"

"Ha! Is it come to this?" the Stranger thundered. "Then meet your fate: you shall leave your plane. Once, twice, thrice! 'Tis done!"
