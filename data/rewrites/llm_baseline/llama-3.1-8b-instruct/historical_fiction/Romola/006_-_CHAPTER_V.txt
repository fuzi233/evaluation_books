In the heart of Oltrarno, the southern bank of Florence, where the Arno River flows gently, lay the Via de' Bardi. This historic street, flanked by the Ponte Vecchio and the Piazza de' Mozzi, boasted a unique charm. To the north, the steep hill of Bogoli rose sharply, a testament to the city's ancient stone-quarrying traditions. The quarry, infamous for its unstable stones, had been a crucial source of the city's paving materials for centuries. As the sun began to set, casting a warm glow over the scene, the river's tranquil waters reflected the intricate façade of the buildings, creating a mesmerizing display of light and shadow.

Among these ancient structures stood the remnants of the once-magnificent homes of the Bardi family. Though their grandeur had been reduced to a fraction of its former self, whispers of their storied past lingered in the air. The Bardi, a proud and ambitious clan, had been woven into the fabric of Florence's history, their name synonymous with bravery and cunning. They were a family of merchants and warriors, adept at navigating the treacherous waters of politics and finance. Their legacy stretched back to the earliest days of the city's great rivalries, when nobles vied for power and the streets ran red with blood. The Bardi had played a pivotal role in these conflicts, their hands stained with the blood of their enemies, yet their fingers also deftly handling the minted coins of their merchant ventures.

By the mid-fourteenth century, the Bardi had risen from humble beginnings as popolani to become Counts of Vernio, their influence extending far beyond the city's borders. Their commercial prowess had earned them a reputation as the Christian equivalent of the great trading powers of Europe, supplying specie for the wars of King Edward III and amassing vast fortunes in the process. However, their grand ambitions were soon to be undone by the weight of their own success. The resulting financial shockwaves sent the Bardi's credit crashing, leaving the family reeling and their reputation in tatters.
