The descent from the Gorge took two hours, a measured erosion of altitude that deposited me onto the surface of a country defined by contradiction. I stood amidst a landscape that was simultaneously fresh and bewildering, yet I felt a surge of rejoicing at the sudden, blinding splendor of the light. Before reaching this precipice, I had paused at the Gorge's maw to survey the vista: twenty-seven great volcanoes dominated the horizon, flanked by two monstrous ranges of fire-hills burning in the distance to my right, alongside a hundred thousand lesser embers scattered like spilled salt.

It was a land of fire and water, a chaotic geography where a small volcanic cone stood isolated within a sea, perhaps a mile distant, with a dozen similar peaks spreading outward. To my right, the waters stretched endlessly toward the red incandescence of the fire-hills, vanishing into a luminous infinity. Islands punctuated the ocean, crowned with their own dormant or active vents, while elsewhere, the fire-hills erupted directly from the water itself. A thick plume of steam hovered over the nearest sea, suggesting a perpetual, localized boil. Beneath the red atmosphere, a low, constant muttering thunder shook the air—a rhythmic vibration shifting from distance to proximity—which I judged to be the voices of the fire-hills communicating with the energy trapped within them.

This was a world saturated with the Voice of the Energy of Life, a resonance that mirrored the World-Noise of my own age but with a keener, undeniable clarity. My gaze fixed on specific landmarks: to the left of the Gorge's exit rose a massive, blackened mountain ascending into the night for fifteen or twenty miles. From its flank protruded a mighty peaked volcano, rising five miles into the darkness, followed by a second peak nine or ten miles higher still. Upon the left crest of this black giant, two additional fire-hills burned with such intensity they appeared as strange, smoldering suns suspended in the void. Below these towering infernos lay vast mountains of ash and scorched debris, cast forth eternally by the perched volcanoes to construct grey, sombre monuments to the dread glory of Time. To my right, the endless sea and its red blaze continued; to my left, mighty forests rose, interspersed with more monstrous fire-hills that pierced the canopy. This was the first impression etched into my brain: a symphony of heat and growth.

Descending fully into the Country of the Seas, I reached a pause. Which path was proper? I circled, confused, eventually climbing back into the Gorge to re-evaluate. Foolish, I chastised myself, for failing to map my route before the descent. Returning to the Gorge's mouth, the solution became obvious. To the right lay only the sea; to the left, where the shoreline met the water, a clear passage opened for a considerable distance. Perhaps, I reasoned, this would lead to a further way forward. Thus, I descended again into the red-shining realm of water and fire.

Since my last sleep, twenty-four hours had elapsed. Exhaustion demanded rest. I located a suitable niche: a small, dry, warm basin of rock sheltered by three great trees. After consuming three tablets and drinking water to quell my hunger, I constructed a bed within the rocky depression. As I lay down, my thoughts drifted briefly to Naani before surrendering to sleep.

Awakening abruptly, I found myself submerged in hot water. Relief washed over me; I had not drowned. Rising to my feet, I discovered the basin filled with steaming, pungent liquid pouring from a smoothed slit on the far side, gurgling and bubbling as if fed by a deep, boiling well. Gladly, it did not erupt violently upon emergence. On dry land, I deduced that the water flowed in periodically, receding through fissures in the basin's floor, a cycle repeating roughly hourly. Watching the basin slowly drain confirmed this rhythm.

Soaked, I stripped my armor, retrieving my effects from the pool's warmth. Finding a heated patch of rock, I laid my garments to dry. Once they were sufficiently arid, I entered the pool for a pleasant bath, feeling no immediate threat. I held the Diskos at the pool's edge, however, lacking total assurance. I recalled that this country teemed with monstrous beasts, yet I sensed no nearness of Evil Forces. Those horrors, I surmised, congregated around the Mighty Pyramid, drawn by the spiritual density of humanity packed so tightly in one spot, much like sharks following a ship carrying cattle. How such powers gained entry to this state of life remained uncertain to me, a subject I had touched upon previously, though my reasoning offered no certainty.

My clothes dried, and I emerged from the pool, which was nearly drained back into the earth. Redressed and armored, my spirits lifted, though fatigue lingered. I rested beside the pool for six more hours, occasionally roused by the gurgle of the refilling water. When the time ended, I awoke refreshed, ate, drank, and prepared to continue my journey through the red-lit country.

All day I marched at a brisk pace, the nameless woods to my left and the sea-shores to my right. Often, trees grew directly into the water, forcing me to traverse the aquatic forest. This was a marvel I had never experienced in the Underground Fields; a glad, wild mystery inhabited the groves here. The scent of the woods was sweet to my spirit. Throughout the day, life pulsed in the darksome woods; living eyes peered out intermittently before retreating into the gloom. Fear flickered, but no harm approached.

Three times that day I encountered small fire-hills burning red, emitting fire and noise audible through the forest before I reached them. Around each site lay death and desolation, where great trees had perished, yet the quick life of small plants flourished in the intervals between eruptions. In that single day, I passed thirty-seven boiling springs. Whether they truly boiled was unclear, but they emitted great steam and strong roaring noises, sounding like wild beasts from afar.

At the eighteenth hour, I halted, consumed two tablets, and drank water that fizzed richly and quickly. Wearied, I chose a sleeping spot against a large rock, ensuring no creature could approach from behind. Sleep came easily, though I resolved to slumber only with my body, aware that strange creatures dwelt in the forests. Before succumbing, I thought of Naani, whose spirit seemed to hover nearby, striving pitifully to communicate. I placed a blessing upon her and hardened my resolve to move with desperate speed toward the Lesser Refuge in the dead world, hoping to reach it sooner. I fell asleep instantly.

Suddenly awake, the brightness of the country revealed my danger immediately, sparing me the paralyzing doubts of the grey Night Land shadows. Within a score of paces, crouching at the tree line, I saw movement. My spirit had granted me knowledge, waking me to the threat. Gripping the Diskos, I stared. Six squat men, humped at the neck and shoulders, crouched in a row, partially obscured by shadow. Their eyes shone with the luminescence of beasts. Terror seized me, yet I possessed the Diskos and my armor; my spirit remained assured of conquest.

I rose, the Diskos ready in my hand. Behold! The Humped Men vanished. I had not seen them depart, despite maintaining a steadfast gaze upon their hiding place. Almost ready to believe they had never existed, I knew with certainty they had been there.

I checked my watch mentally: five hours had passed during my sleep. Standing watchfully, I ate two tablets and drank water. Ready to proceed, anxiety gripped me. I believed the Humped Men were nearby, among the trees, capable of striking in a moment, or perhaps calling an army to my destruction.

Securing my gear, I set off at a great stride, keeping the Diskos handy. I scanned left and right, moving with speed. I had grown lean and hard, convinced I possessed the power to outpace them or anything of their kind. All day, for thirty great hours, I marched at this stride, watching constantly. Every sixth hour, I consumed two tablets and sipped water, continuing onward.

I hoped to lose them, but my faith was fragile. Twice and thrice, I sensed things moving through the woods to my left, maintaining a level with my speed yet remaining hidden. This was a shaking thing for my heart, diminishing my hope. Lacking faith to accompany my hope, I could not sleep until finding a proper, safe place. Having traveled thirty great hours without such a location, I finally perceived water ahead, distinct from the eternal sea to my right.

I initially thought the sea turned inward, but upon arrival, I found a river flowing into the sea, originating from the country to my left. At the river's mouth lay a small island, tempting me as a refuge from the Humped Men playing dog to my rear. Yet, this was an idle thought; my need was to cross the river to continue beside the great sea stretching forever before me.

I lacked swimming ability, and the warm-flowing river surely harbored monsters. Climbing the bank to find a narrowing point, I walked a great distance until a second river entered the first, less than a mile from the sea. Now I faced a dilemma: the sea to one side, the second river to the other, and the first river blocking my path. Perplexed, I sought a solution.
