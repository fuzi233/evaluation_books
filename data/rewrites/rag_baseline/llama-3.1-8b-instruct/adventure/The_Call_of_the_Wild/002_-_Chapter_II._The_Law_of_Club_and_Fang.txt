Chapter II. The Law of Club and Fang

Buck's first day on the Dyea beach was a baptism by fire, a rude awakening from the comforts of civilization to the unforgiving vastness of the wilderness. Every moment was a test of his mettle, a reminder that he was no longer a pampered pet, but a living, breathing creature, subject to the whims of nature and the cruel caprices of the wild. The sounds, the smells, the sights – everything was so different from the tranquil world he had left behind. No gentle sunbeams warmed his fur here, nor was there a comforting presence of human companions to soothe his nerves. In this unforgiving landscape, every second counted, and the margin between life and death was razor-thin.

His introduction to the harsh realities of the wild came with a jolt, as he witnessed the brutal fight between Curly and a snarling husky. The violence was raw, unbridled, and devoid of mercy. No quarter was asked or given; once down, one was done for. Buck watched in horror as the huskies, those wolf-like creatures, surrounded Curly and tore her apart with savage efficiency. The memory of that scene would haunt him for a long time, a grim reminder of the law of the wild: might makes right, and the strong survive at the expense of the weak.

As he struggled to come to terms with this new reality, Buck was faced with another challenge. François, the rugged outdoorsman, fastened a harness around his neck, transforming him into a beast of burden, a pack animal tasked with hauling heavy loads through the forest. At first, Buck resisted, his pride wounded by the indignity of being treated like a draft animal. But he was wise enough to recognize that submission was his only option, at least for now. With a heavy heart, he accepted his new role and set to work, learning to obey the commands of François, Dave, and Spitz, the seasoned leader who would become his arch-nemesis.

Under their tutelage, Buck quickly adapted to his new circumstances, mastering the art of pulling a sled through the dense forest. He learned to respond to the crisp commands of "ho" and "mush," to navigate the treacherous terrain, and to avoid the snapping whip of Dave, who nipped his hindquarters whenever he faltered.
