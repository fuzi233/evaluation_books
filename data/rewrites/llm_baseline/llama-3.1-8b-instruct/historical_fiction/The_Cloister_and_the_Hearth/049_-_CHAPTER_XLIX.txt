Margaret Brandt retreated within the safety of her home, her reserve a shield against the whispers that had begun to circulate through Sevenbergen. Many believed her aloofness to be a sign of pride, but Margaret knew the truth – her condition could no longer be concealed, and the consequences would be dire. The thought gnawed at her, day and night, making her reluctant to venture out, even on the rare occasions when she needed to.

As she sat in her dimly lit chamber, surrounded by the familiar scents of baking bread and simmering stews, Margaret's thoughts turned to the contract that should have secured her future. The betrothal agreement, sealed and witnessed, lay in the possession of Gerard, now far away in Italy. She knew that, according to law and custom, a marriage contract was binding, and that betrothed couples often married without further ceremony, their children considered legitimate.

But Margaret's medieval mind was clouded by doubt. Without the contract, she felt vulnerable to the malicious whispers of the townspeople. They would never believe her when she claimed her marriage lines were secure, even if she produced them. The sight of her pregnancy would only fuel the rumors, and the birth of her child would be a cruel irony – proof of her supposed infidelity. Margaret's intelligence, honed from years of managing her family's affairs, told her that she was at a disadvantage. Women, especially, would be quick to believe the worst.

As she pondered her predicament, Margaret's thoughts turned to the humble art of baking. She rose from her chair and began to knead a batch of dough, the rhythmic motion calming her racing thoughts. The aroma of fresh bread wafting from the oven soothed her frazzled nerves, and for a moment, she forgot her troubles. But the respite was short-lived, as the weight of her situation bore down on her once more.

That evening, Margaret visited her friend Margaret Van Eyck, an elderly artist who lived in seclusion, much like herself. Though she feared the older woman's discretion, Margaret's anxiety was palpable. When she returned home, she couldn't shake the feeling of foreboding. Her condition had been discovered by Catherine, a gossip and a neighbor, and Margaret knew that her fate hung in the balance.
