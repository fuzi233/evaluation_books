The ache within me, a yearning to reconnect with Norah's gentle touch, grew more acute with each passing day. It was a craving that threatened to consume me, to reduce me to a mere shadow of my former self.

In moments of respite, when the weight of responsibility lifted and my humor made holiday, I would scribble tales for my own amusement. Though they may not have left an indelible mark, the act of creation brought me solace, a fleeting respite from the crushing demands of my reality. And so, I persevered, fueled by the promise of a tangible outcome – financial stability. By the time my debts were settled, I had earned a reputation, a fragile construct that trembled with each uncertain step.

My friends cautioned against the folly of chasing my artistic aspirations, warning me that I risked sacrificing a certain fortune for a dubious competence. They spoke of the futility of trying to break free from the mold of my earlier endeavors, of being forever typecast as a humorist. "Once a jest, always a jest," they'd say, echoing the sentiments of those who'd witnessed my early triumphs. As I sat with "Goggles" in the quiet corner of the Club, pouring out our hearts to one another, I listened intently as he shared his vision for Romeo – a poignant, if unremarkable, conception that failed to stir my soul. Yet, I feigned interest, paying the price for his willingness to listen to my own musings on the nature of monumental literature.
