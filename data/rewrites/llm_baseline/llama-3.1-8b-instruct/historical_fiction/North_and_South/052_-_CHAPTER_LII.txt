As the morning sun cast its golden rays upon the tranquil town, Edith moved with deliberate quietude, her footsteps barely audible as she checked on Sholto. Her eyes were fixed intently on the drawing-room door, where the murmur of hushed conversation continued unabated, like the gentle lapping of waves against the shore. Two o'clock arrived, yet still the assembly remained ensconced behind closed doors. A lone figure emerged from the stairway, and Edith peeked out into the hallway, her gaze locking onto Henry's resolute face.

"Well, Henry?" she inquired, her voice barely above a whisper, as if the very fate of the world hung in the balance.

Henry's response was curt, his words dripping with a sense of resignation. "Well," he replied, his tone a testament to the weight of unspoken thoughts.

"Come in to lunch!" Edith urged, her voice a gentle nudge against the tide of his melancholy.

"I'm afraid I've already lingered too long," Henry declined, his eyes drifting towards the closed door as if searching for an escape from the crushing burden that lay within.

Edith's expression turned forlorn, her words a plaintive cry into the void. "Then it's not all settled?"

Henry's shoulders slumped beneath his coat, a gesture of defeat. "Not at all, Edith. It never will be settled, not if the 'it' is what I suspect you're referring to. That will never be, Edith. Give up thinking about it."

Edith's eyes welled up with tears, her heart heavy with the weight of her own desires. "But it would be so nice for us all," she pleaded, her voice cracking with emotion. "I should always feel comfortable about the children, if I had Margaret settled down near me. As it is, I am always afraid of her going off to Cadiz."

Henry's expression softened, a glimmer of compassion flickering to life within his eyes. "I will try, when I marry, to find a young lady who has a knowledge of the management of children. That is all I can do. Miss Hale will not have me. And I shall not ask her."

Edith's face fell, her shoulders sagging beneath the weight of disappointment. "Then what have you been talking about?" she asked, her voice a mixture of curiosity and despair.

"A thousand things you wouldn't understand," Henry replied, his words a gentle rebuke.
