As the threads of fate continued to weave their intricate tapestry, a subtle shift occurred within the fabric of the Deronda household. When Deronda returned from his sojourn at the Abbey, he was met with an unexpected sight: Lapidoth, his father, had settled into the lodgings at Brompton. The catalyst for this development lay in Mirah's decision to share with her father the budding friendship between Deronda and Ezra, carefully omitting the pivotal moment when Deronda had rescued her from the brink of drowning. Instead, she dwelled on the connection forged through her association with the Meyrick family, allowing her father to assume Deronda's acquaintance with her stemmed from those gentle souls.

Though Lapidoth was not overly inquisitive about the specifics of Mirah's past, he was keenly interested in the notion that his children possessed a benefactor of considerable standing. As he navigated this new dynamic, he sought to cultivate a sense of domesticity, engaging with Mirah's musical pursuits, tolerating Mrs. Adam's aversion to smoking, and strolling with her through the square, his German pipe and tobacco at the ready. With calculated amiability, he endeavored to win over his children, even going so far as to refrain from pressing Mirah for financial assistance, which she had vowed to decline as a solemn obligation to her brother.

Ezra, ever the stalwart companion, shared with Deronda the developments in their family's life, his words tinged with a cautious optimism. "I have grown calmer in accepting his presence," Ezra reflected, "and I strive to believe that Mirah's affection, coupled with the stability of a peaceful life, may yet persuade him to resist the allure of destructive paths. I have counseled her to entrust him with no resources, and she has pledged to adhere to this principle."

When Deronda visited the next day, Lapidoth, resplendent in his new attire, greeted him warmly, his gaze lingering on the younger man's youthful visage, which Mirah had failed to convey.
