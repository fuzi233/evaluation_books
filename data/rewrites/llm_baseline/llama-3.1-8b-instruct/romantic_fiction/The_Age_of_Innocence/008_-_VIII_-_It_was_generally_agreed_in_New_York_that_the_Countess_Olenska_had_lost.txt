As the whispers spread throughout New York society, it was whispered that the Countess Olenska had lost her beauty, that radiant bloom of youth which had captivated the city's elite during her childhood. She had first appeared on the social scene in Newland Archer's youth, a bright and lovely little girl of nine or ten, whose porcelain complexion and sparkling eyes seemed to beg for the brushstrokes of a master painter. Her parents, wanderers of the continent, had vanished when she was young, leaving her in the care of her aunt, Medora Manson, a woman who, despite her repeated attempts to settle down, seemed doomed to roam the world in search of new experiences.

Medora, a widow several times over, had brought Ellen with her on each of her returns to New York, only to eventually part ways with her husband or her ward, and embark once more on her peripatetic existence. Her latest arrival, with Ellen by her side, had raised eyebrows among the city's socialites, who couldn't help but feel sorry for the young girl, whose parents had been well-liked despite their questionable taste for travel. The little Ellen, with her rosy cheeks and curly locks, seemed too full of life and joy to be mourning the loss of her parents, especially considering her aunt's unconventional approach to mourning, which saw her donning a veil that was decidedly shorter than those of her sisters-in-law.

As Ellen grew older, her Aunt Medora continued to expose her to a variety of experiences, including an expensive but haphazard education, which included lessons in drawing from a live model and playing the piano alongside professional musicians. The result was a young woman with a precocious talent and an air of confidence that bordered on recklessness. When she married a Polish nobleman of great wealth and reputation, it seemed as though she had reached the pinnacle of success. However, rumors soon began to circulate about the difficulties of her marriage, and eventually, the news of its collapse reached New York, sending Medora back to the city, alone and impoverished, in search of a smaller house.
