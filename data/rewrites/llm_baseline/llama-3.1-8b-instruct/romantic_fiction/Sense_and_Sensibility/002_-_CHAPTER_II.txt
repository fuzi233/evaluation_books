The kitchen of Norland was alive with the savory aromas of roasting meats and baking bread, transporting Mrs. John Dashwood back to a time when life was simpler and happier. As she settled into her new role as mistress of the estate, her mother and sisters-in-law became familiar faces in the household, treated with quiet civility by Mrs. Dashwood and kindness by her husband, who seemed to reserve his warmth for those closest to him – his wife and their young son, Harry.

Despite her own initial reluctance to remain at Norland, Mrs. Dashwood found comfort in the familiar surroundings, where every room and every view reminded her of joyful times past. Her mood, like the seasons, shifted easily between sunshine and shadow, carrying her away by her imagination in either direction. When life was bright, her spirits soared; when sorrow clouded her thoughts, she was lost in reverie.

As the days passed, Mrs. John Dashwood grew increasingly disapproving of her husband's intentions regarding his sisters. The thought of taking three thousand pounds from their young son's inheritance was unbearable to her – it would leave him with a meager fortune, barely enough to sustain him through life. She implored her husband to reconsider, pleading with him to think of their child's future and the potential consequences of such a decision. How could he justify depriving his only child of such a significant sum, especially when the Miss Dashwoods, his half-sisters, had no real claim to his generosity? The notion of half-blood relationships held little weight in her eyes, and she wondered why her husband would sacrifice their son's security for the sake of distant relatives.

"It was my father's dying wish," her husband replied, his voice laced with a hint of conviction. "He asked me to support his widow and daughters."

"Ah, but did he not know what he was saying?" Mrs. John Dashwood countered, her tone tinged with skepticism. "Was he not perhaps in a state of confusion? If he had been in his right mind, he could never have expected you to abandon your child's future for the sake of your half-sisters."

Her husband's expression turned thoughtful, and he paused before responding. "He didn't specify a particular amount, my dear. He simply asked me to make their lives easier, to provide for them in some way. Perhaps it would have been wiser if he had left the decision entirely up to me.
