As we sailed along the coast of Atlantis, Tob's skilled navigation had guided us remarkably close to our desired landfall, but we still had a few days of retracing our route ahead of us before reaching the arm of the sea where the great city lay. The promise of solid ground and the scent of earth and vegetation carried on the breeze proved to be a balm to our weary souls. Many of our crew had succumbed to the ravages of scurvy, a cruel fate that had claimed the lives of so many during our long and arduous journey across the vast expanse of ocean. However, the tantalizing prospect of land brought new life to those who had been bedridden, and they soon regained their strength, eager to lend a hand with the shipwork and, when the time came, defend our vessels against any potential threats.

Since my exile in Yucatan, I had harbored some trepidation regarding how I would be received in Atlantis. Despite Tatho's assurances, I had come to accept that the outcome was in the hands of the gods. The Empress Phorenice's power was formidable, and she might well have me executed upon my arrival. Yet, I had entrusted my fate to the benevolence of the Sun, believing that His will would prevail. Thus, I had devoted myself to studying the deeper mysteries, unencumbered by worries about my own destiny.

As we navigated the waters, our navy encountered another fleet, anchored in a sheltered bay just two days' sail from the Atlantis capital. Their scouts, stationed on the high ground above, spotted our approach and alerted their comrades. By the time we rounded the headland, their ships were ready to intercept us, their crews eager to bar our passage. Our own fleet had been reduced to just five ships, having suffered losses due to storms and scurvy. The opposing navy consisted of three sturdy vessels and three sleek galleys, their hulls polished and black, in stark contrast to our weathered and worn ships. We hoisted the banners of Tatho and Deucalion, proudly declaring our allegiance and purpose, while our adversaries chose to remain anonymous, as if ashamed of their origins.

It was clear that they intended to challenge our passage, a common occurrence in the lawless seas, where a ship's captain felt free to attack another vessel if they met far from shore, especially if the other ship was returning from a trading expedition or plundering raid.
