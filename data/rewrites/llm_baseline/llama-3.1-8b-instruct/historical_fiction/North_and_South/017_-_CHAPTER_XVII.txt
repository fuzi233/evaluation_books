As Margaret stepped out into the chill of the morning, her reluctance was palpable. The narrow streets of Milton seemed to stretch before her like an unforgiving canvas, its every brushstroke a harsh reminder of the difficulties that lay ahead. Yet, with each passing moment, her footsteps lightened, her heart swelling with a sense of purpose. The air, alive with the hum of conversation and the clinking of glasses from the nearby taverns, seemed to lift her spirits, drawing her gaze outward, away from the turmoil within.

As she navigated the crowded thoroughfares, Margaret noticed the men lingering on street corners, their eyes cast downward, their voices low and deliberate. The laughter of the women, though boisterous, carried a tinge of desperation, a cry for freedom in a world that seemed determined to hold them back. The sight of the beer-houses and gin-shops only served to deepen her unease, their patrons' rough exterior and harsh words a stark reminder of the hardships that lay just beneath the surface.

In the face of such adversity, Margaret felt a pang of doubt. Perhaps it would be better to visit Bessy Higgins, to offer what little comfort she could in these trying times. The thought brought her some solace, and she made her way to the Higgins' humble abode.

Nicholas Higgins sat by the fire, his eyes fixed on the flame as he puffed on his pipe. Bessy rocked gently in her chair, her face a picture of weariness. As Margaret entered, Nicholas stood, his movements fluid and unhurried, and gestured for her to take a seat.

"How are you, Bessy?" Margaret asked, her voice soft with concern.

Bessy's response was laced with a hint of melancholy. "I'm rather down in spirits, but I'm better in health. I don't like this strike, Margaret. I'm a simple soul who values peace and quiet above all else."

"This is the third strike I've witnessed," Margaret replied, her tone tinged with a mix of curiosity and ignorance. "I must admit, I don't fully understand the concept. Where I come from, we're more concerned with sowing seeds, harvesting crops, and reaping what we sow."
