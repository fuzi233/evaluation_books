"After all I've done for her," he lamented, his voice trembling, "she throws me down." Sally remained silent, her attention focused on navigating the unpredictable rhythm of their dance.

Their steps carried them past the table where Miss Mabel Hobson, resplendent in old gold and black, watched the scene with a detached air. Mr. Cracknell's gaze followed hers, and a pained expression crossed his face as he stumbled beneath her discerning eye. "If I told you what I'd spent on that woman," he confided in Sally's ear, "you wouldn't believe it. And then she throws me down. All because I said I didn't like her hat. It was a terrible choice, but I was trying to be kind." He paused, his words trailing off as he lost his footing and nearly stepped on Sally's ankle.

She suppressed a startled cry, and Mr. Cracknell, interpreting her reaction as a rebuke, attempted to make amends. "I don't mean you're like that," he said, his voice heavy with emotion. "You have a compassionate heart. That's why I'm sharing this with you. You're a perceptive person, and I value your opinion. I've done everything for that woman – secured her position as hostess here, even starred her in a show once. Those pearls she wears? I gave them to her myself. But she won't speak to me now. Just because I didn't like her hat." His words hung in the air like a challenge, and Sally's eyes met his, a mixture of curiosity and concern reflected in her gaze.

"I come here every night, hoping to catch her eye," Mr. Cracknell continued, his voice cracking with desperation. "But she refuses to acknowledge me. What would you do in my shoes?" Sally's frank response was a gentle shrug, and Mr. Cracknell's eyes clouded with uncertainty. "I thought you'd understand, being a perceptive person," he said, his voice barely above a whisper.
