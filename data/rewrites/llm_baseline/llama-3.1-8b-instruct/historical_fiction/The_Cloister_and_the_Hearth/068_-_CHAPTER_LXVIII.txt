As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a warm orange glow over the small cottage, Margaret Brandt lay in bed, her weary body a testament to the trials she'd faced. The faint scent of fresh-baked bread wafted from the kitchen, mingling with the smell of simmering stew, filling the air with comforting aromas. Her heart swelled with gratitude for Catherine's gentle ministrations, who had been by her side throughout her pregnancy. The older woman's words of encouragement and the soft hum of her knitting needles had been a constant solace during these trying times.

Catherine's own life had been a testament to the cycle of birth and nurturing. She had borne many children, and though her own children had long since left the nest, she still took pride in watching over the young ones in her community. Her eyes sparkled as she spoke of her own daughter, Kate, who had threatened to give her a granddaughter, and now, instead, she held a precious grandson in her arms. The thought brought a warmth to her face as she gazed lovingly at the sleeping infant.

Margaret's eyes, however, were fixed on the past. She longed for the man she loved, Gerard, who was far away. The memory of his letter, though a comfort, only served to deepen her sorrow. She reached out a trembling hand, as if to grasp his presence, but her fingers closed on nothing but air. Tears welled up in her eyes as she whispered his name, her voice barely audible above a whisper.

Catherine busied herself with the evening chores, trying to distract herself from the ache in her own heart. She had grown accustomed to Gerard's absence, but hearing her daughter's voice calling out to him, even in jest, brought a pang of sadness. As she turned to check on Margaret, she noticed a look of rapt attention on her face.

"What's wrong, child?" Catherine asked softly, though she already knew the answer.

Margaret's eyes seemed to hold a secret, and her voice was barely above a whisper. "I heard Gerard's voice, mother. He spoke to me."

Catherine's expression softened, but she shook her head gently. "Don't say that, dear one. You must be tired."
