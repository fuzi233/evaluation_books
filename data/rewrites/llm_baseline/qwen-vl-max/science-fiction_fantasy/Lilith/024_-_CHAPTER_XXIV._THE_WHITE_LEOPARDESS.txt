Beneath the stair, the moonlit street stretched cold and silent, the wind moaning through the alleys like a restless spirit. But within my sheltered nook, not a breath stirred. I was settling into rest when—without warning—my eyes flew open. There, just cresting the top step, was the gleaming head of the creature I’d seen trailing the Shadow: pale as snow, luminous in the night.

She froze the moment our gazes met. Then, tail first, she began to retreat. I leapt up—and she had no room to turn. With a desperate twist, she flipped backward, landing on her feet in an instant, and vanished down the stairs before I could reach her.

I raced after her, scanning the empty street from end to end. Nothing. No sign. Only the wind and the moon. Returning to my hard cot, I clenched my fists. Two predators now haunted Bulika—one spotted, one not. One was a beast; the other, perhaps, something worse.

I wouldn’t risk much for any adult here. But a child? A child’s life might be worth every breath I had left. I vowed to watch that door all night.

Then came the slow creak of the latch. I rose quietly, slipped inside. The woman who stood there was not the one I’d helped. It was the desert figure—the muffled one, face hidden beneath layers of cloth.

Without speaking, she led me into a stone chamber, bare and cold. She pointed to a rug. I wrapped myself in it and lay down. She shut the inner door, then opened and closed the outer one behind her. Moonlight bled through the cracks, the only light in the silence.

I couldn’t sleep. Soon, a low moan began—muffled, ragged. It swelled, then broke into a cry. A newborn’s cry. Then—a shriek, sharp and terrible.

I sprang up and burst into the passage. From another doorway emerged the white leopardess, clutching a baby in her jaws—carrying it as if it were her own cub.

I lunged at her. She dropped the infant with a whimper. It fell onto the stone, wailing.

At the sound, the muffled woman appeared. She stepped over us—over the animal, over me—picked up the child, and disappeared into the shadows.

When she returned, she lifted me off the beast, opened the door, and gently pushed me out. Behind me, the leopardess followed.

“She has failed me,” I thought bitterly. “Given me to the beast to deal with at her leisure. But we’ll see about that.”

I ran down the stairs, half expecting her to leap upon me. But she kept pace, calm, quiet. At the bottom, I turned to grab her—she leapt over my head. When I faced her again, she was crouched at my feet.

I knelt. Reached out. Stroked her flawless white fur. She licked my bare feet with a rough, dry tongue—like a dog, yet more tender.

I patted her, held her close. A surge of warmth flooded my heart. Could she betray me? Perhaps. But if I turned from every gesture of affection—fearing it false—how would I ever know true love when it came?

I stood. She rose beside me.

Then—thud. A heavy squelch echoed in the street. A shapeless mass lay in the middle of the road, pulpy, half-dissolved. I ran to it. Beneath the ruin, I recognized the form of a woman—her body thrown from a window above.

I looked around. There, on the opposite side of the street, walked the Shadow. And behind him—again—came the white leopardess.

I followed. My heart urged compassion: perhaps she wasn’t free. Perhaps she was bound.

As I drew near, she turned—snarling, fangs bared. Instinct made me step back. Instantly, she resumed her place behind the Shadow.

I approached again. Again, she lunged—eyes blazing emerald fire.

A third time. This time, she snapped—bit me.

My breath caught. I gasped.

And in that moment, she turned her head, glanced at me—not with rage, but with something like sorrow. As if to say: *Why did you make me do this?*

I turned away, ashamed. I had wasted too long. I had to act.

Night or no night—I would go to the palace.

From the square, I had seen it: towering above the city, encircled by walls and ditches, more fortress than royal seat. But now, its defenses were crumbling. Gates stood wide. The drawbridge was gone. The chasm below was dry. For centuries, it had been abandoned—forgotten.

Silence pressed in. So deep it felt alive. To enter would be to break a spell. To speak—to scream—might wake horrors beyond imagining.

I wandered the walls like a stray. Then found a recess with a stone bench. Sheltered from the wind, I lay down. Cold seeped into my bones—but exhaustion won. I slept.

I woke to something leaping onto me—warm, heavy, breathing against my face. A rough tongue lapped at my cheek.

*The white leopardess,* I thought. *Come to drink my blood.* Why not? What good would resistance do? It would cost me more to fight than to yield.

So I stayed still.

But no pain came. Instead—warmth. A gentle heat spread from her body, pressing against mine. Her breath, soft and clean, wrapped around my face. Not wild. Not cruel. Tender.

I realized—this was no predator. This was care.

I turned, like a child seeking comfort. Threw my arm over her. And sank into dreamless sleep.

When I stirred, I thought I was home. Warm. Safe. The scent of flowers, of earth, of memory. I rubbed my eyes. Opened them.

Stone. Cold stone. The heart of the cursed city.

I sat up. Had I dreamed her? Or had she truly lain beside me?

Her warmth lingered. Still present.

I rose. A new hope stirred in me—vague, unformed, but strong.

One truth was clear: I must find the princess.

I had saved her life. She had given me a second chance—though at great cost to myself.

That bond—however fragile—gave me courage. I would face her. Whatever she was.
