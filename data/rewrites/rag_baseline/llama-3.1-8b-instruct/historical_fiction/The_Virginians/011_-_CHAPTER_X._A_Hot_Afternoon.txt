A haze of heat clung to the windows of Castlewood, filtering through the panes like a damp shroud. Inside, the air was heavy with the scent of old books and dust. In the boys' quarters, Harry and George Esmond sat on their beds, their faces flushed from the previous evening's festivities. The marriage of their stepfather to their mother still lingered, an unspoken topic that threatened to disrupt the fragile balance of their household.

As they spoke in hushed tones, their words hung in the air like a challenge. "We can't let him marry Mother," Harry said, his voice barely above a whisper. "He's our enemy." George nodded, his eyes flashing with a mixture of anger and determination. "We have to stop him, Harry."

The brothers' conversation turned to the potential consequences of their actions. George's face reddened as he recalled his past encounter with Colonel Washington, a wound that still lingered. "I'll never forgive him for advising Mother to have Ward attack me," George said, his voice trembling with indignation. "And now, he's planning something worse. We must make him pay."

Harry's eyes widened in agreement. He admired his older brother's courage and conviction. Together, they hatched a plan to confront Colonel Washington outside the park gates, where they could express their displeasure without compromising their honor. As the night wore on, their resolve hardened, fueled by a sense of justice and a desire to protect their family's reputation.

In the stillness of the night, the boys' determination seemed almost palpable, a slow-burning fire that would soon spread to the rest of Castlewood. But for now, it remained a secret, locked away in the shadows, waiting to be unleashed upon the world.
