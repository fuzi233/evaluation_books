As Catherine stood at the hearth, stirring the simmering stew that filled the air with the savory scent of slow-cooked beef and herbs, she gazed at Eli with a twinkle in her eye. "Answer me one question, Eli," she said, "and I'll ask no more today. What is wormwood?"

Eli, lost in thought as he polished a wooden spoon, looked up with a hint of surprise at the sudden query. "Ah, it's something that tastes overwhelmingly bitter," he replied, his brow furrowed in concentration.

Catherine chuckled, her eyes sparkling with amusement. "A fair answer, Eli, but not quite what I had in mind." She leaned in, a sly grin spreading across her face. "Wormwood is... to have two idle hands, waiting for the perfect recipe to come together."

Eli's eyes widened as the meaning dawned on him, and he shook his head, a wry smile spreading across his own face. "You've hit the nail on the head, Catherine. Two hands, idle and waiting, are indeed the very essence of wormwood."

Catherine nodded, her expression turning serious. "Exactly. And that's why I think we should start a small bakery in Rotterdam. Just think of it – fresh bread, warm from the oven, and all the pastries our hearts desire."

Eli's face lit up at the prospect, and he began to envision the possibilities. "We could sell our wares at the market, and people would come from all over to taste our creations."

Catherine's eyes sparkled with excitement. "And we could hire a few extra hands to help us with the workload. But I have someone in mind for the job – my dear friend Kate. She's been itching to learn the art of baking, and I think she'd be perfect for the task."
