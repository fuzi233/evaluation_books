Silence enveloped the grand ballroom like a shroud, a reminder of the transience of splendor. Mrs. Beaufort stood poised at the edge of the empty dance floor, her beauty frozen in time. The opera's final notes still lingered in the air, a haunting melody that seemed to underscore the emptiness of her life. The Beauforts' mansion, a testament to her husband's questionable reputation, loomed behind her, its brown-stone façade a façade hiding secrets and scandals.

Mrs. Beaufort's gaze drifted to the gilt chairs stacked in the corner, their reflections gazing back like sentinels guarding the secrets of the night. She thought of her own family's history, of the whispered rumors and half-truths that followed her wherever she went. Her mother, Medora Manson, had always done the right thing from the wrong motives, and now Mrs. Beaufort found herself trapped in a life of appearances, a prisoner of her own beauty and her husband's dubious charm.

Julius Beaufort, the enigmatic millionaire, was a master of presentation, a man who could carry off anything with ease. People said he had been helped to leave England, that he owed his success to the international banking-house where he had worked. But Mrs. Beaufort knew better – or at least, she thought she did. Behind the mask of his affability and wit, she sensed a man consumed by his own demons, a man who had traded his soul for a life of luxury and privilege.

As the last notes of the opera faded away, Mrs. Beaufort's thoughts turned to the evening ahead, to the carefully curated guests and the precision-crafted conversation. She would smile and laugh and play the part of the gracious hostess, all while her mind wandered to the truth – the truth about her husband, about her life, and about the emptiness that lay beneath the surface of her perfect facade.
