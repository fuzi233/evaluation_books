I circled the castle, passing again through its open gates and crossing the moat’s shadowed ravine. Inside the courtyard, towering poplar-like trees stood in rigid formation, their leaves whispering secrets to the wind. At its heart loomed the tallest of them all, its crown splayed like a spectral hand against the sky. Between the trunks, glimpses of the palace teased my curiosity—a structure of strange, foreign grandeur, low and sprawling, with corner towers clawing at the clouds and a massive central dome swelling like a bubble of molten silver.

The entrance yawned before me, an elliptical arch, dark and unguarded. I stepped inside.

The hall stretched before me, an elongated oval of polished stone. And there, to one side, crouched in a cage, was the leopardess—pale as moonlight, her dark spots like scattered ink. Steel bound her neck; her jaws were muzzled, her paws muffled. She did not move, but her eyes—great emerald discs with slitted pupils—tracked me with unnerving stillness. Was she alive? A statue? A phantom?

Narrow passages branched from the hall. I chose one at random, its winding path splitting into many. In the tightest of these, a page collided with me. He recoiled, fear flashing across his face before arrogance replaced it.

“What business have you here?” he sneered.

“To see the princess.”

He laughed. “As if she would receive you! I haven’t even seen her today myself.”

I seized him by the scruff, shaking him like a rat. “Take me to her, or I’ll drag you through every corridor until I find her. Let her judge how her servants treat guests.”

His bravado crumbled. He led me to a cavernous kitchen where servants moved sluggishly, their faces pale as they stared—not at me, but past me. I turned.

The leopardess stood at my back, silent, watching.

The servants recoiled. One, bolder than the rest, stepped forward.

“Take me to the princess,” I demanded.

“She has not yet risen, my lord.”

“Then wake her. Tell her one who knows the white leech seeks an audience.”

Fear flickered in his eyes. “She’ll kill me for such a message.”

I glanced at the leopardess. The man swallowed and fled.

The others remained frozen, their gazes locked on the beast. She paid them no mind, pressing close to me. I stroked her head. She rose onto her hind legs, her paws resting on my shoulders, then vanished like mist.

The servant returned. “Follow me.”

My pulse quickened.

He led me through a labyrinth of corridors, finally ushering me into a vast, lightless chamber. The air was cold, the walls invisible. A single dim glow pooled on the floor, and high above, an oval aperture pierced the darkness. The architecture was alien, deliberate—a place designed to unsettle.

Then, like a star flaring to life, she appeared.

Clad in white, her hair a torrent of black silk, the princess stood before me. Her eyes were fathomless, her skin luminous. She smiled—a girl’s smile, innocent, disarming.

Could this be the same woman who had struck me, scorned me, abandoned me?

She stepped closer, her fingers brushing my shoulder. “You’ve found me at last,” she murmured. “I knew you would.”

Her touch sent a shudder through me—not of fear, but of warring instincts. Attraction and revulsion twisted together.

“You’re cold,” she observed. “Come.”

She led me to a bath carved into the floor, its water clear as crystal. But something about it unnerved me. A scent clung to the air—sweet, yet wrong. Had she enchanted it? Poisoned it? I remembered the leopardess’s wounded paw, the howl in the night.

I did not enter.

Instead, I donned the robe laid out for me—white wool, embroidered with silver—and descended to a round chamber of alabaster, its walls shimmering with ghostly light. The princess awaited me there, clad in a gown of woven moonlight, her arms bare.

A feast lay before us: fruit, milk, wine the color of dawn.

“We do not kill to eat here,” she said. “But I hope this pleases you.”

I drank the wine. It was like swallowing a dream.

She watched me, her gaze heavy with meaning. “Men live to die,” she said. “I live to live on. Age does not wither me—it perfects me. I have walked this world for millennia. And yet, until you, no lover has ever proved worthy.”

Her voice was honey, but her eyes held something sharper.

“You passed my test,” she continued. “You followed not out of pity, but devotion. And so I offer you what no other has had: my power, my beauty, my love.”

She knelt before me, her hands resting on my knees. Then I saw it—the glove on her left hand, crude and out of place. Beneath it, I imagined claws.

A roar shook the chamber. The walls trembled.

The princess flinched. “The wine,” she said hastily. “Too strong for you. Rest now. We will speak later.”

She led me to my room, her smile tight with unease.

As the door closed behind her, I wondered: Was that roar the leopardess? And if so—why had it sounded like a warning?
