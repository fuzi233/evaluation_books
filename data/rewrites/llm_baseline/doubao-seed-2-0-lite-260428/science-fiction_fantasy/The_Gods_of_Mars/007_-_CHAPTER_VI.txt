“What in the name of Helium’s royal guard is that?” I breathed to Thuvia, my hand drifting to the hilt of my longsword where it hung at my hip.

She did not speak. Her white, slender finger lifted, pointing straight up into the ink-dark Martian sky, where the glow of Barsoom’s twin moons—Carthoris and Thuria—painted drifting clouds silver. High above the temple spires, walled courts, and blooming thern gardens, shadowy shapes darted, weaving sharp erratic paths like giant predatory birds closing in on a flock of prey.

A split second later, stabbing pinpricks of orange light burst from the hulls of the fliers. The roar of massed rifle volleys rolled down from on high, echoing off the golden cliffs of Otz, and a wall of answering fire blazed back from the thern ramparts and temple battlements.

“The Black Pirates of Barsoom, O Prince,” Thuvia said, her voice tight with long familiarity with the carnage to come.

Airships of the raiding band swept in great descending circles, dropping lower and lower to rain fire on the thern defenders. Volley after volley tore through Barsoom’s thin crisp air, spat from the fliers’ gun ports, while thern guards returned fire so fast their muzzles glowed steady red. When the pirates dropped low enough, thousands of golden-haired, white-robed thern soldiers poured out of temple archways to crowd the gardens and open courtyards. The second they spilled out into the open, scores of small pirate scout fliers peeled off from the main formation and dove straight for us.

The therns fired back through curved shield attachments bolted to the ends of their rifle barrels, but the grim matte-black craft never wavered. Most were small two or three-man scouts, built for speed and hit-and-run raiding, while a handful of bulkier heavy cruisers hung high overhead, lobbing explosive bombs from keel-mounted launchers that blew craters into the temple domes.

On a signal no ear on the ground could catch, every pirate craft in our immediate vicinity dove recklessly for a landing, right in the thick of the massed thern soldiery. Before the hulls had even settled to the grass, the black warriors were leaping over the gunwales, howling like demons, their curved steel sabers flashing in the moonlight.

I had fought ferocious green Martian Warhoons, I had faced the beasts of the dead sea bottoms, but never had I seen fighting like this. The black pirates threw themselves into the fray with wild feral abandon that outstripped every act of ferocity I had ever witnessed. Beneath the bright glow of the twin moons, every second of the battle burned sharp and clear: pale golden-haired therns locking in desperate hand-to-hand combat with their ebony-skinned foes, their robes stained red with blood that dripped onto crushed pimalia blooms under their feet. One pirate drove his saber through a thern’s heart, the body slumping limp at the base of a towering statue carved from solid glowing ruby. A dozen therns forced a lone raider back against a bench hewn from solid emerald, its surface traced with intricate Barsoomian star charts inlaid with cut diamonds.

Thuvia, Tars Tarkas the great Thark, and I stood huddled at the edge of the fray, untouched by the tide of battle for the moment, as fighters swerved past us close enough that I could make out every detail of the raiders I had only ever heard of in old half-forgotten legends from my first time on Mars. The stories said they hailed from the tiny lesser moon, swooping down on Barsoom once every few long Martian years to wreak atrocities, carry off weapons, supplies, and young girls to sacrifice to their dark gods in cannibal orgy.

Up close, they were striking men, broad-shouldered and easily six and a half feet tall, their features sharp, symmetrical, and impossibly handsome, their large black eyes glinting beneath the moonlight, their dark polished ebony skin somehow adding to, rather than detracting from, their fierce unearthly beauty. Their bodies were built exactly the same as therns, red Martians, and my own Earthly frame—only their hearts, it seemed, were made of something far darker. Their lust for blood was a palpable hungry thing, rolling off them as they hacked their way through the thern ranks.

Their small black scout craft lay scattered all across the garden grounds, completely unguarded, and for a second I wondered why the therns did not simply smash every last one of them to splinters. Thuvia answered the unspoken question a minute later, when the roar of fighting receded a little ways from our spot.

“You see now, O Prince,” she said, “why a million warriors guard the thern borders night and day? I have watched this exact same slaughter play out a score of times in the fifteen years I have been their prisoner. The pirates never press hard enough to wipe the therns out completely. They treat us like playthings, to sate their endless hunger for battle, and collect their regular tribute of arms and captives.”

“But why don’t the therns destroy the pirate fliers?” I demanded. “That would stop these raids cold. The crafts are completely unguarded, for God’s sake.”

“They dared once, thousands of years ago,” Thuvia said, her face going pale at the old memory. “The very next night, a thousand of their giant black battleships circled the Mountains of Otz, and bombarded every temple, garden, and courtyard nonstop for an entire Martian moon, until every thern that survived fled deep into the underground catacombs to hide. The therns know they only live at the sufferance of the First Born, as the pirates call themselves. They will never risk that extermination again.”

It was at that exact moment a new unplanned force exploded into the battle. The massive banths—giant ten-legged Barsoomian lions we had released earlier from their pens—had cowered at first from the roar of gunfire and the screams of fighting men, but the scent of fresh blood had roused their rage. One great tawny shape launched itself from a thicket of flowering shrubbery, landing right in the middle of a knot of struggling warriors, and its roar of bestial fury was the signal for the whole pack to charge.

Panic spread like wildfire. Thern and pirate alike forgot their feud to turn on the common enemy, the great banths tearing through their ranks, bowling men over with the sheer weight of their bodies, slashing through armor and flesh with their giant talons, rending bodies apart with their saber-length fangs. The battle turned into a chaotic roiling mess of fur, steel, and screaming men.

I realized in a flash that this was our chance to escape. The therns were far too busy fighting for their lives against the banths to guard their walls. Even better, those unguarded pirate fliers lay right at our feet. I knew every model of Barsoomian airship that had ever been built, after nine years serving in the navy of Helium, from the tiny one-man scout skiffs to the massive dreadnoughts that could outgun any fleet on the planet. Escape was right there for the taking.

I grabbed Thuvia’s arm, jerked my head at Tars Tarkas to follow, and we slipped through the edge of the fighting to the furthest small two-man pirate flier, untouched a hundred yards from the nearest battling warrior. I vaulted onto the deck, my hand closing around the starter lever, my thumb hovering over the repulsor ray button—that miraculous Martian technology that could lift even the largest battleships against Barsoom’s weak gravity.

The craft swayed. It did not lift an inch.

A chorus of savage shouts rose behind me. A dozen black pirates had broken off from the fray and were sprinting straight for us, their sabers drawn.

I realized my mistake in a heartbeat. This was a two-man scout, its repulsor tanks only charged with enough power to lift two average 150-pound red Martians. Tars Tarkas, the 10-foot tall Thark warrior, weighed more than three ordinary men combined. He was anchoring us to the ground like a hundred-pound stone.

There was no time to argue. I jammed the repulsor lever all the way forward, locked the button down, and leaped off the deck of the flier, drawing my longsword to face the onrushing pirates. The craft lurched hard upward, its engines screaming to lift its two remaining passengers. I heard Thuvia’s voice, thin and high above the roar of the engines, “My Prince— I would rather die with you—” before the flier soared up and vanished over the treetops.

For a minute, I thought the dozen pirates would cut me down in seconds. But my Earth-born strength, honed on Virginia’s frontier, was far greater than any Martian’s. My blade wove a shimmering net of death around me, and for long minutes I held them back, my old Virginian battle fury burning hot in my veins, the old familiar fighting smile tugging at my lips.

The pirates fell back a few paces, breathing hard, staring at me with a mix of fury and reluctant respect. “Thern,” one of them snarled, “you fight like a Dator of the First Born. If it were not for your cursed yellow hair and pale skin, you would be a credit to our people.”

“I am no thern,” I called back, about to explain I hailed from Earth, that we could strike a truce to fight the therns together. That was when a heavy hard object slammed into my shoulder blades so hard I nearly face-planted into the grass. The trailing anchor of a passing 10-man pirate cruiser, drifting slow and low over our heads, had struck me. The heavy metal fluke swung past my shoulder, hitting one of the pirates square in the jaw and knocking him cold.

The cruiser was slowly pulling away, the anchor rising higher and higher. I did not hesitate. I put every ounce of my strength into a single leap that carried me right over the heads of the gaping pirates, and my hand closed tight around the last link of the anchor chain just as it was pulling out of reach.

I dangled there, one-handed, dragged through the tops of the flowering trees, as the pirates below screamed and howled in rage. The cruiser banked west, then turned south, carrying me far past the golden cliff crests, out over the vast gulf of the Valley Dor. Six thousand feet below me, the Lost Sea of Korus shimmered like a sheet of silver beneath the twin moons.

Carefully, I hauled myself up the chain, my muscles burning, until my fingers curled over the edge of the cruiser’s gunwale. I pulled myself up, and found myself staring straight into the fierce ebony face of a First Born pirate, his eyes blazing with triumphant, hungry hate.
