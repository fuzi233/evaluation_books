The air in the storeroom hung heavy with the scent of aged spices and decaying fruit as I slowly regained consciousness. The cries of alarm pierced through my haze, and I sprang to my feet, my senses heightened by the fusillade of shots echoing through the subterranean corridors. I stumbled, my legs unsteady from the prolonged sleep, but managed to steady myself as I took in the scene before me. The storeroom, once a sanctuary, was now a battlefield. Bodies littered the ground, except for Thuvia and Tars Tarkas, who, like me, had slept through the initial onslaught.

A dozen lesser therns stood before us, their rifles trained on us with a mixture of confusion and trepidation. As I rose, they hesitated, their faces twisted in a mix of emotions. I seized the opportunity to assert my authority.

"What sorcery is this?" I thundered, my voice shaking with anger. "Are the loyal subjects of Sator Throg to be slaughtered by their own kin?"

One of the therns stepped forward, his eyes darting nervously between me and the others. "We mean no harm, O Master of the Tenth Cycle," he stammered. The others shifted uncomfortably, as if seeking an escape route.

Thuvia leaned in close, her voice barely audible. "Ask them their purpose here."

I turned to the therns. "What brings you to this place?"

The thern who had spoken earlier swallowed hard before responding. "We were sent by the Father of Therns to apprehend two fugitives. One was a white-haired stranger with dark skin, the other a massive green warrior." He cast a suspicious glance at Tars Tarkas, his eyes lingering on the Thark's imposing figure.

Thuvia nodded toward the Thark. "This is one of them, and if you examine the body by the door, you may recognize the other." Her voice dripped with sarcasm. "It seems Sator Throg and his loyal servants were tasked with completing what the lesser therns couldn't – capturing the fugitives. Now, in your haste and incompetence, you've killed most of our party, and nearly succeeded in killing Sator Throg himself."

The therns exchanged uneasy glances, their faces pale with fear. Thuvia's words hung in the air like a challenge.
