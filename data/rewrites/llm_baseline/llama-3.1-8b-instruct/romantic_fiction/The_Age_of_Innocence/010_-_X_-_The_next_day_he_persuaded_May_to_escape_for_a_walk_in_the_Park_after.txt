The next day, Archer convinced May to join him for a leisurely stroll through the park after luncheon. It was a day tailor-made for a tranquil excursion, with the skeletal branches of the trees along the Mall draped in a canvas of cerulean blue and the snow beneath them sparkling like shards of crystal. The gentle atmosphere seemed to heighten May's radiance, and Archer couldn't help but feel a sense of pride as passersby cast admiring glances in their direction.

"I'm so enchanted by the scent of lilies-of-the-valley in my room each morning," May said, her voice barely above a whisper.

"Yesterday, I sent them a bit late," Archer replied, his eyes never leaving hers.

"But the thought of receiving them every day, and knowing you remembered, makes me appreciate them all the more," May continued. "It's almost like having a personal music teacher, someone who anticipates and caters to one's every whim, just like Gertrude Lefferts's did when she was engaged to Lawrence."

Archer chuckled at her observation, his gaze drifting to her flushed cheeks. He felt a surge of contentment, secure in the knowledge that he could spoil her with small gestures like sending flowers. "I saw some exquisite yellow roses at the market yesterday and sent them to Madame Olenska," he said, watching May's reaction.

"How thoughtful of you! I'm sure she'll adore them." May's eyes sparkled with interest. "It's strange that she didn't mention them when she lunched with us today. She did speak of Mr. Beaufort's orchids and cousin Henry van der Luyden's carnations from Skuytercliff. Apparently, it's considered quite the norm in Europe to send flowers as a gesture of affection."

Archer felt a twinge of annoyance at being overshadowed by Beaufort's more extravagant gift. He regretted not including a note with the roses, and his mind began to wander to his unspoken intentions – namely, visiting Madame Olenska the previous day. He hesitated, unsure whether to broach the subject, fearing it might seem presumptuous.

As they walked, the silence between them grew, punctuated only by the soft crunch of snow beneath their feet. Archer decided to steer the conversation toward more familiar territory, discussing their future plans and the Wellands' expectations.
