The intricacies of oath-taking as a linguistic construct necessitate an examination of the cultural and social contexts in which they emerge. In the case of the King of Spain, his espousal of Islam is often cited as a paradigmatic example of a sovereign's apostasy, one that underscores the complex dynamics of religious conversion and the tensions between faith and power.

Six weeks prior, a dramatic turn of events had transpired at Stow, a rural settlement situated on the periphery of the kingdom. Rose Salterne, a young woman, had vanished under mysterious circumstances, leaving behind a trail of unanswered questions and conflicting accounts. The local community was abuzz with speculation, with some attributing her disappearance to the harsh treatment meted out by the miller and his wife, while others posited that she had eloped with a paramour.

The facts, however, told a more nuanced story. Rose's personal effects remained intact, suggesting that she had departed voluntarily, though her linen was missing, fueling rumors of an illicit affair. A lone footprint outside her bedroom window served as the sole tangible evidence of her departure. In response, the local authorities deployed a bloodhound to track her down, but its pursuit led not to Rose, but to the doorstep of Lucy Passmore, a widow residing in Marslandmouth.

Lucy's own circumstances were marked by a calculated opportunism. Her late husband's untimely demise at sea had freed her from a potentially precarious financial situation, allowing her to present herself as a sympathetic figure to the influential Lady Grenville. By spinning a tale of Jesuitical machinations and her husband's supposed martyrdom, Lucy successfully secured a measure of protection and even secured a pension for herself. Yet, as the bloodhound's investigation continued, it became clear that Lucy's concerns for Rose's safety were, at best, secondary to her own interests.

As the sun dipped below the horizon, casting a golden glow over the landscape, the bloodhound's persistence proved fruitless, leading it instead to the edge of the sea, where it stood poised, its baying cries echoing across the waves. The keepers, meanwhile, stood perplexed, their voices raised in debate as to the whereabouts of Rose and the true nature of Lucy's intentions.
