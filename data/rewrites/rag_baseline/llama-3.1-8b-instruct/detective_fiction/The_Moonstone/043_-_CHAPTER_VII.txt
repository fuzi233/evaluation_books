As I pushed open the door, the soft clinking of Rachel's fingers ceased on the piano keys. She slowly rose, her movements labored, as if the mere act of standing had exhausted her. Our gazes locked across the expanse of the room, a heavy silence settling between us like a shroud. The faint scent of candle wax and old wood wafted through the air, mingling with the musky aroma of Rachel's perfume, transporting me to memories of long-forgotten afternoons spent in her company.

My sudden appearance had likely startled her, and I hastened to bridge the distance between us. "Rachel!" I called out, my voice barely above a whisper, as if afraid of shattering the fragile atmosphere. The sound of my voice awakened something within her, and her limbs relaxed, her color returning to her cheeks. She began to move towards me, her eyes fixed on mine with a mixture of wariness and longing. Her footsteps were hesitant, as if drawn by some unseen force. The warm flush on her skin deepened, and the light in her eyes flickered, like a flame dancing in the breeze. I forgot the turmoil that had brought me to her doorstep; the weight of my shame, the stain on my reputation. All that mattered was the woman I loved, stumbling towards me with uncertain steps.

Her proximity set my heart racing, and I felt the familiar rush of desire. I reached out, my arms closing around her, and covered her face with kisses. For an instant, I thought I detected a spark of reciprocation, a fleeting moment when she might have forgotten everything. Almost immediately, her expression changed, and she pushed me away with a force that left me reeling. Merciless anger blazed in her eyes, and her lips curled into a sneer, as if she gazed upon a stranger who had committed some unspeakable offense. "You coward!" she spat, her voice laced with venom. "You wretched, heartless coward!"

Those words cut deeper than any blade. I felt the sting of her contempt, the bitter taste of shame. I remembered the days when she might have expressed her displeasure with a gentle rebuke, but now her words were a razor-sharp lash, slicing through my defenses. I sought to apologize, to plead for forgiveness, but my words felt hollow, inadequate. "I remember the time, Rachel," I began, "when you might have told me of my offense in a gentler way..."
