The night was a tempest of silence, a troubled darkness that bled into the waking day. Fear did not merely accompany their sleep; it inhabited it, a silent, terrible presence that made the house itself tremble. To the children, it was a phantom wind that shook the walls without a sound, a rising tide that filled the garret to the rafters yet made no splash, and a swarm of cats that clawed at their nightgowns before vanishing into the howl of a distant ancestor. They slept and woke in a cycle of dry, warm terror, unaware that while they dreamed of monsters, a far more ancient and profound judgment was unfolding in the shadows of the room.

I, however, was witness to the truth. When Mara departed with the children, the veil of the night lifted slightly. In the corner, cowering in mortal dread, lay the white leopardess, a creature of instinct terrified by the unnatural stillness. The Princess, Lilith, sat upon the settle, a statue of sullen defiance, while the room flickered with cloudy presences that seemed to breathe with the approaching doom.

When Mara returned, she drew the settle to the center of the hearth, placing herself opposite me. Between us, a small fire burned, but the air was thick with a dread sense of impending judgment. I felt the heaving of the earth, the shifting of the soul, a vibration that was neither physical nor spiritual, but the very fabric of reality straining under the weight of what was to come.

Midnight arrived, and the muffled woman rose. She unwound the swathes of cloth that hid her face, letting them drop to the floor, and revealed the visage of the Princess. It was a face of heartbreaking beauty, white and sorrowful, yet devoid of unhappiness—a serene vessel for a storm. Great tears streamed down her cheeks, wiped away by her robe, leaving her expression severe with pity.

"Will you turn away from the wicked things you have been doing so long?" Mara asked, her voice soft as a whisper.

Lilith remained silent. Mara repeated the question, then a third time. Finally, the seeming corpse stirred. Her mouth opened, and though no sound emerged, words formed in the air, direct and terrible.

"I will not," Lilith declared. "I will be myself and not another!"

"You are another now, not yourself!" Mara countered gently. "Will you not be your real self?"

"I will be what I mean myself now."

"If you were restored, would you not make amends for the misery you have caused?"

"I would do after my nature."

"You do not know it: your nature is good, and you do evil!"

"I will do as my Self pleases."

"You will do as the Shadow inclines you?"

"I will do what I will to do."

"You have killed your daughter, Lilith!"

"I have killed thousands. She is my own!"

"She was never yours as you are another's."

"I am not another's; I am my own, and my daughter is mine."

"Then, alas, your hour is come!"

"I care not. I am what I am; no one can take from me myself!"

"You are not the Self you imagine."

"So long as I feel myself what it pleases me to think myself, I care not. My own thought makes me me. Another shall not make me!"

"But another has made you, and can compel you to see what you have made yourself. You will not much longer have satisfaction in the thought of yourself. At this moment you are aware of the coming change!"

"No one ever made me! I defy that Power to unmake me from a free woman! You are his slave, and I defy you! You may torture me, but you shall not compel me!"

"There is a light that goes deeper than the will," Mara said, her voice steady. "That light can change your will, can make it truly yours and not another's. Into the created can pour itself the creating will, and so redeem it!"

"That light shall not enter me: I hate it! Begone, slave!"

"I am no slave, for I love that light. There is no slave but the creature that wills against its creator. Who is a slave but she who cries, 'I am free,' yet cannot cease to exist!"

"You speak foolishness from a cowering heart! I hold myself against you! What I choose to be, you cannot change!"

"I am sorry: you must suffer!"

"But be free!"

"She alone is free who would make free; she loves not freedom who would enslave. Every life you sought to subdue, you have enslaved. You are the slave of every slave you have made—such a slave that you do not know it! See your own self!"

Mara stepped back. Suddenly, the house was possessed by a soundless presence, a roaring flame that the children had mistaken for a wind. From the shadows, a silvery creature, like a slowworm of living fire, crawled across the floor. It moved with deliberate slowness toward the settle, its heat glowing vivid as incandescent silver. It stopped at the Princess's feet, then climbed the robe, inch by inch, until it vanished into the folds of her bosom.

The Princess's face remained stonily calm, her eyelids closed as over dead eyes. Minutes passed in agonizing stillness. Then, on her parched skin, drops of dew appeared, swelling into seed-pearls that poured down in streams. I lunged to crush the creature, but Mara interposed, drawing aside the robe. There was no serpent, no scorch mark. The fire had passed through the center of the black spot, piercing through the joints and marrow straight to the heart.

"She is seeing herself!" Mara whispered, pulling me back.

The Princess arched her body, springing to her feet. Horror twisted her features, and her hair stood out, emitting sparks before falling again to pour sweat onto the floor. She was far away, Mara explained, trapped in the hell of self-consciousness. The central fire of the universe was radiating into her the knowledge of good and evil. She saw the good she was not, and the evil she was. She knew she was the fire burning her, but she did not know that the Light of Life was the heart of that fire.

At last, she collapsed onto the floor. Mara stood over her, tears falling from her own eyes onto the woman who had never wept.

"Will you change your way?" Mara asked.

"Why did he make me such?" Lilith gasped, her voice breaking. "I would have made myself so different! I am glad it was he that made me and not I myself! He alone is to blame! Never would I have made such a worthless thing! He meant me such that I might know it and be miserable! I will not be made any longer!"

"Unmake yourself, then," Mara said.

"Alas, I cannot! The tyrant keeps me being! I curse him! Now let him kill me!"

The words jetted out like a dying fountain.

"Had he not made you," Mara said gently, "you could not even hate him. But he did not make you such. You have made yourself what you are. Be of better cheer: he can remake you."

"I will not be remade!"

"He will not change you; he will only restore you to what you were."

"I will not be aught of his making."

"Are you not willing to have that set right which you have set wrong?"

Silence stretched, the suffering abating slightly.

"If you are willing, put yourself again on the settle."

"I will not," she answered, forcing the words through clenched teeth.

A wind woke inside the house, blowing without sound or impact. Cold water rose, smooth and silent, lifting her gently until she floated onto the settle, unable to resist. Then it sank away.

The internal strife resumed, fiercer than before. Lilith's soul lay naked to the torture of pure light. She moaned, sighed, and murmured in a strange, shadowy language that touched upon the disparted manuscripts of Adam and forces I did not recognize. Her queendom was divided; she exulted over her enemies and wept for friends she hated.

"Those are not the tears of repentance!" Mara observed. "Self-loathing is not sorrow. Yet it is good, for it marks a step in the way home."

"Will you restore that which you have wrongfully taken?" Mara asked again.

"I have taken nothing," Lilith spat, pain twisting her face. "My power to take manifested my right."

Mara left her. Gradually, an invisible darkness enveloped us, a positive Nothingness that bordered on absolute Death. It was not the absence of everything, but the presence of void. Lilith dashed herself from the settle to the floor with a bitter cry—the recoil of Being from Annihilation.

"For pity's sake," she shrieked, "tear my heart out, but let me live!"

Then, perfect calm fell upon us, as of a summer night. The cup of her suffering had overflowed, and a hand had emptied it. She rose, standing erect with the air of a conqueror. She had met her spiritual foes and driven them back. She raised her withered arm in a pæan of unholy triumph—until her eyes fixed in a ghastly stare.

Before her, cast from an unseen mirror, stood the reflection of herself, and beside it, a form of splendent beauty. She trembled and sank to the floor. She knew the one: what God had intended her to be. She knew the other: what she had made herself.

For the rest of the night, she lay motionless. With the gray dawn, she rose, turning to Mara with a prideful humility.

"You have conquered," she said. "Let me go into the wilderness and bewail myself."

Mara saw the submission was neither feigned nor real. She returned.

"Begin, then, and set right in the place of wrong."

"I know not how," Lilith replied, fear in her eyes.

"Open thy hand, and let that which is in it go."

A fierce refusal struggled within her, but she kept it imprisoned.

"I cannot," she said. "I have no longer the power. Open it for me."

She held out the offending hand. It was more a paw than a hand, clenched tight.

"You must open it yourself," Mara said quietly.

"I have told you I cannot!"

"You can if you will. At worst you could beat it open! Gather your strength."

"I will not try what I know impossible. It would be the part of a fool!"

"Which you have been playing all your life! Oh, you are hard to teach!"

Defiance flared on her face. She turned her back on Mara. "I know what you have been tormenting me for! You have not succeeded! I will yet be mistress of myself! I am still queen of Hell!"

Then came the most fearful thing of all. It was Life in Death—life dead, yet existent. Lilith had glimpsed it before, but never fully until now. As she stood rigid, a sense of cold crept out of her being, pervading it. The lamp of life and the eternal fire seemed to die together.

Something was taking place in her that we could not feel, yet we felt its misery. The source of life had withdrawn itself. All that remained was the dregs of her dead and corrupted existence. She was a live death, a conscious corpse whose coffin would never break. She had killed her life, and she knew it. She must DEATH IT forever. She had tried to unmake herself and failed; she was a dead life that could not cease. She must BE.

In her face, I saw dismay beyond manifestation. The light in her was darkness, shining after its own kind. She was what God could not have created. She had usurped her share in self-creation, and her part had undone His. She saw what she had made, and behold, it was not good. Her right hand was clenched upon existent Nothing—her inheritance.

But with God, all things are possible. He can save even the rich.

Without changing her look, Lilith walked toward Mara. She felt her coming and rose to meet her.

"I yield," the princess said. "I cannot hold out. I am defeated. Not the less, I cannot open my hand."

"Have you tried?"

"I am trying now with all my might."

"I will take you to my father. You have wronged him worst of the created, therefore he best of the created can help you."

"How can HE help me?"

"He will forgive you."

"Ah, if he would but help me to cease! Not even that am I capable of! I have no power over myself; I am a slave! Let me die."

"A slave thou art that shall one day be a child!" Mara answered. "Verily, thou shalt die, but not as thou thinkest. Thou shalt die out of death into life. Now is the Life for, that never was against thee!"

Like her mother, in whom lay the motherhood of all the world, Mara put her arms around Lilith and kissed her on the forehead. The fiery-cold misery went out of her eyes, and their fountains filled. She was lifted and borne to her own bed in a corner of the room, laid softly upon it, and covered with caressing hands.

Lilith lay and wept. The Lady of Sorrow went to the door and opened it. Morn, with the Spring in her arms, waited outside. Softly they stole in, bringing a gentle wind that rippled the unknown, upwaking sea of her eternal life. It flowed about her, healing the desert places of her heart. When Mara returned to sit by her bed, her tears were flowing softer than the rain, and soon she was fast asleep.
