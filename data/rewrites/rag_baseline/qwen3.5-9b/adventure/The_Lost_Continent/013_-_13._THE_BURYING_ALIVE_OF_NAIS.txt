The decree regarding Nais was executed with the velocity characteristic of Phorenice's desires. To the Empress, the continent existed solely as an extension of her will; consequently, no expenditure was deemed excessive. Though the edict technically assigned the construction of the stone throne to me, the labor was delegated to the collective workforce. Heralds announced the mandate in every district, summoning masons, laborers, stonecutters, sculptors, and engineers from their current tasks. Architects selected a lead figure to direct operations, while subordinates ensured compliance. Materials within the city limits were seized without compensation, forcing the demolition of numerous structures to accommodate the colossal blocks chosen for the project.

Despite the ongoing siege by rebel forces battering the city walls, the building guilds operated with relentless efficiency. Their skill, augmented by Phorenice's technological innovations, displayed a steady progression. While they lacked the capacity to move the primordial monoliths placed by the early Gods for the Sun Circle, their application of rams, trucks, and cranes allowed them to manipulate masses of staggering proportion.

The foundation consisted of seven tiers of stone, each knee-high and stepped on the front face. The uppermost layer contained a cavity designed to house Nais's body, surmounted by the massive block forming the throne's seat. Throughout the night, illuminated by relayed torchlight, successive teams of stonecutters, masons, and sweating laborers hauled and dressed the stone. Engineers constructed lifting mechanisms, verifying the precise placement of every block. Whips cracked; men collapsed from exhaustion, only to be replaced instantly by others. Delay was intolerable when Phorenice spoke.

By dawn, as the square filled with spectators, the debris and scaffolding were removed, along with the corpses of approximately thirty workers who had perished from accidents or exertion. The throne stood magnificent, adorned with intricate carvings. Its base rose more than two man-heights above the ground, composed of courses weighing no less than twenty men each. The upper section, double that weight, featured a royal snake encircling the chair, its hooded head looming overhead. Suspended high by hydraulic rams, awaiting its final descent, the structure awaited its purpose.

Summoned by the chamberlains, I emerged from the royal pyramid the following morning. Nobles arrived with banner-bearers and trumpeters; the middle classes displayed their finest attire; even destitute figures, ribs visible beneath dusty skin, wore turbans and colored wrappings to signify the day's festivity. Trumpets heralded my arrival, and the crowd shouted welcomes. Accompanied by the retreating march of gorgeous chamberlains, I proceeded to a scarlet awning, taking my seat upon the cushions beneath it.

Phorenice then appeared, my intended bride, fresh from sleep and radiant in her splendor. Carried in an open litter of gold and ivory by fantastical savages from Europe, her refined features were accentuated by the contrast with their brutish appearance. The crowd exhaled in delight. It is evident that not a single man in the throng envied me her choice, nor could anyone present conceive that I was not overjoyed to secure such a lovely wife.

Internally, however, I summoned the iron of my training to mask my countenance. This was a ceremony, albeit a ghastly one, requiring a grand demeanor to prevent any caviling. Having endured much, I refused to shatter the scheme with visible agony—a shaking hand or a twitching cheek. My resolve hardened: I would commit the living body of my love to the hollow beneath the stone with the same calm indifference as a priest burying a stranger. Phorenice, I knew, would not betray our secret. Before all Atlantis, we shared a singular understanding.

To my surprise, the proceedings lacked mockery or flippancy. Everything was solemn and imposing. While the vulgarity of the crowd missed the depth of the tragedy biting at my heart, the external shape of the double sacrifice witnessed that day would surely remain etched in their memories, even if the inner meaning eluded them. On this occasion, the many-mooded Empress commanded rigid exactness and pomp, adhering strictly to the older rituals.

Borne by her European attendants to the scarlet awning, I handed her to the ground. Seating herself on the cushions, she beckoned me, intertwining our fingers as was customary between Atlantean rulers and their consorts. A squad of soldiers marched forward, parting to reveal Nais in their midst. She wore a metal collar around her neck, secured by chains held by two guards, preventing her from running into the escort's spears and meeting a swift death, a common fate for those sentenced to prolonged suffering.

Notably, Nais retained her clothing. Custom dictated that garments belonged to the guards, but since she was not stripped, someone must have bribed them—a gesture I attributed to Ylga, feeling a flicker of gratitude. The soldiers brought her to the edge of the pavilion's shade. She prostrated herself before the Empress, avoiding rough handling. Her face was pale, devoid of fear or defiance, her eyes calm and natural. She remembered her duty to Atlantis. Watching her, I felt a surge of love and pride.

Outwardly, I remained impassive as stone. Knowing Phorenice's gaze was fixed upon my face, I ensured nothing escaped my expression that I would not have her witness.

"Nais," the Empress said, "you have eaten from my platter as a fan-girl and drunk from my cup. What was yours, I gave you. You should have possessed more than gratitude; you should have known the length of the Empress's arm and the heaviness of her hand. Yet, you possess neither. Moreover, you attempted to seize a matter reserved exclusively for the Empress. You were offered pardon on terms and rejected it. You were foolish. However, today I am inclined toward clemency. Soon, seated on that carved granite throne Deucalion has built for me, I shall take my Lord Deucalion as husband. Speak plainly: say you are sorry, name a man you would choose, and I will remember the brightness of this occasion. You shall be pardoned and wed before we rise from these cushions."

"I will not wed," she replied quietly.

"Consider for the last time, Nais, the alternative. You will be taken, warm, quick, and beautiful as you stand now, and laid in the hollow beneath the throne-stone. Deucalion, who is to be my husband, will place you in that terrible bed as a symbol that all of Phorenice's enemies shall perish. Then he will release the rams and lower the upper stone into place, and the world shall see your face no more. Look at the bright sky, Nais, fill your chest with sweet, warm air, and consider what this death signifies. Believe me, girl, I do not wish to make an example of you unless you force me."

"I will not wed," the prisoner repeated softly.

The Empress released my arm and reclined against the cushions. "If the girl presumes on our old familiarity, or believes I jest, show her now, Deucalion, that I do not."

"The Empress is far from jesting," I stated. "I will perform this act because it is the Empress's wish, and because her command dictates that a symbol remain forever as an example for others. Lead your prisoner to the place."

The soldiers wheeled. The two guards preparing to drag Nais up the steps loosened their grip momentarily, but she walked willingly, her color unchanged. I rose, bowed to the Empress, and followed.

Before the gaze of ten thousand eyes, we displayed no emotion. Not only for Atlantis's sake, but because both Nais and I possessed a certain nicety and pride. We were not like Phorenice, flaunting endearments before others.

Once I ordered the guards to unhinge the collar restraining Nais's neck, I clasped my arms around her. Showing the roughness expected of one determined to prevent escape or undue struggle, a thrill surged through me so potent I nearly fainted. Only by supreme strain of will did I maintain the ceremonial facade. I, who had never embraced a woman without the arm of roughness, now held pressed to me one I loved with infinite tenderness. The revelation of how love links with love threatened my undoing. Outwardly, Nais made no sign, lying half-strangled in my arms as any woman does when borne away by a spoiler.

I trod with her to the uppermost step, the vast throne-stone overhanging us. Ensuring visibility for those watching from the pyramid slopes and surrounding rooftops, despite being beyond Phorenice's sight, I employed brutal force to drag her across the level and deposit her into the hollow. Remarkably, the girl offered no resistance whatsoever.

To prevent her from struggling out and gaining an easy death upon the stone's descent, brazen clamps fitted into grooves above the hollow. I installed these one by one, performing this butcher's work with one hand while fiercely holding her down with the other. Gods! Agony dripped from me onto the thirsty stone. I could not suppress it.

Clamping and locking the final two bars, I withdrew my brute's hand from her throat.

Hateful fingermarks appeared as bloodless furrows in the whiteness of her skin. Against all reason, even for the fate of Atlantis, I could not help lowering my glance to her face. But she was stronger. She gave me no last look. Her eyes remained steadfastly fixed on the cruel stone above. Thus, I left her, knowing it was best not to linger.
