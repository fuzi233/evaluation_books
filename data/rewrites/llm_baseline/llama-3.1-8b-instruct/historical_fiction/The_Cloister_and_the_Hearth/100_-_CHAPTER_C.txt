As the seasons passed, the once bustling streets of the tailor's shop grew quiet, its inhabitants slowly drifting apart like leaves on an autumn breeze. Eli and Catherine, those stalwart figures, remained steadfast, their years accumulating like the layers of a rich bouillon, until they had become a distant memory for Gerard and Margaret. Even Giles, that jester of a man, had wandered off to the courts of Bavaria, where he danced with kings and queens until his bones had grown brittle and his laughter had turned to a faint echo of its former self.

But amidst the fading embers of his family, Cornelis remained, a constant presence, waiting patiently for the day when he would inherit the family business. His mother, now a robust woman of eighty-six, clung to him tightly, her love and devotion only growing stronger as the years went by. As Cornelis lay on his deathbed, his voice barely above a whisper, he spoke of his plans to transform the shop and its surroundings, to make it a testament to the family's legacy. His mother's eyes shone with tears as she replied, "Yes, my darling," and in that instant, Cornelis's spirit slipped away, leaving behind a trail of memories and the promise of a new beginning.

Denys, his heart heavy with grief, returned to Burgundy, where he spent his days spinning tales of his adventures and railing against the whims of fate. It was there that he received an unexpected inheritance, a windfall that changed the course of his life forever. Years later, he would be remembered as a crusty old soldier, his stories of battle and bravery etched in the hearts of those who knew him.

Meanwhile, Jerome, disillusioned with the laxity of the north, had retreated to Italy, where he rose through the ranks to become a respected abbot. With an iron fist, he governed his monastery, earning the reverence and hatred of his followers in equal measure. Yet, on the night of his passing, a strange smile played on his lips, a smile that hinted at a deeper understanding, a connection to something greater than himself. As he lay surrounded by the symbols of his faith – the Vulgate and the crucifix – it seemed that, in that moment, he was not entirely alone.
