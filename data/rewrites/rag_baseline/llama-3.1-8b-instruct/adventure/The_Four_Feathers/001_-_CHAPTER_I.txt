As I stepped onto the sun-drenched flagstones of Broad Place, the warm scent of blooming flowers and fresh earth enveloped me, carrying me away to a realm beyond the confines of my crippled leg. The old red-brick house, perched on the southern slope of the Surrey hills, glowed like a gemstone amidst the dark expanse of pines, radiating an air of serenity. Lieutenant Sutch, a seasoned veteran with a limp, navigated the grand hall, where the portraits of the Fevershams stretched towards the ceiling like sentinels of a bygone era. As he emerged onto the terrace, the rolling hills of Sussex unfolded before him, bathed in the golden light of late afternoon.

General Feversham, a wiry man with a gaunt face and piercing blue eyes, stood upright like a statue, his gaze fixed on the distant downs. "How's the leg?" he asked, his voice devoid of warmth, as he rose from his chair. Though his body remained alert, his eyes betrayed a certain emptiness, a barrenness that seemed to hint at a life unfulfilled.

"It gave me trouble during the winter," Sutch replied, his tone matter-of-fact. General Feversham nodded, and for a brief moment, both men stood in silence, the only sound being the gentle hum of bees and the rustle of leaves. Below, the landscape unfolded like a canvas of emerald and gold, with the distant plume of a train weaving through the trees like a silver snake.

As they stood there, the past began to seep into Sutch's consciousness. He recalled the countless hours spent in the company of General Feversham, their conversations often revolving around the war, the Redan, and the fallen comrades. Yet, there was something about Feversham that continued to elude him – a quality that had drawn Muriel Graham, a woman of extraordinary intellect and beauty, to this man so vastly different from her in temperament.

"I thought I'd find you here," Sutch said finally, breaking the silence.

Feversham's expression remained impassive, but his words betrayed a hint of nostalgia. "It was my wife's favorite spot. She would sit here for hours, lost in the vastness of the landscape." His voice was as flat as the surrounding hills, but Sutch detected a glimmer of emotion beneath the surface.
