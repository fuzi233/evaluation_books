I felt, with an instinctive urgency that allowed me no time for reflection, that I must veil my recent experiences from my Wife. It was not born of fear that she would betray my secret, but rather a conviction that the narrative of my adventures would remain utterly unintelligible to any woman within the confines of Flatland. Thus, I endeavored to soothe her suspicions with a fabrication conjured for the occasion: that I had accidentally tumbled through the cellar's trapdoor and lay there, stunned. In our land, where the Southward attraction is so feeble, even this tale appeared extraordinary and nearly impossible to believe to a woman; yet my Wife, whose discernment far surpasses the average of her sex, perceived immediately that I was abnormally agitated. She did not argue the matter but insisted I was ill and required repose. Glad of an excuse to retreat to my chamber and ponder the event in solitude, I withdrew.

Once alone, a drowsiness seized me. Before my eyes finally closed, I strived to reconstruct the Third Dimension, specifically the process by which a Cube is formed through the motion of a Square. The image was not as lucid as I desired, yet I recalled the crucial phrase: "Upward, and yet not Northward." Determined to hold these words as the key that, if firmly grasped, could not fail to unlock the solution, I began to repeat them mechanically, like a charm: "Upward, yet not Northward." Soon, I sank into a sound, refreshing sleep.

During my slumber, I dreamed. I found myself once more beside the Sphere, whose radiant hue signaled that his former wrath had transmuted into perfect placability. Together, we drifted toward a bright yet infinitesimally small Point, drawing my Master's attention. As we neared, a faint humming emanated from it—a sound akin to a Spaceland bluebottle, yet infinitely less resonant, so subtle that even amidst the absolute stillness of the vacuum through which we soared, the vibration reached our ears only when we halted our flight at a distance of less than twenty human diagonals.

"Look yonder," my Guide intoned. "In Flatland thou hast lived; of Lineland thou hast received a vision; thou hast soared with me to the heights of Spaceland; now, to complete the range of thy experience, I conduct thee downward to the lowest depth of existence, even to the realm of Pointland, the Abyss of No Dimensions."

"Behold yon miserable creature," he continued. "That Point is a Being like ourselves, yet confined to the non-dimensional Gulf. He is himself his own World, his own Universe; of anything other than himself he can form no conception. He knows not Length, nor Breadth, nor Height, for he has had no experience of them; he possesses no cognizance even of the number Two, nor does he harbor a thought of Plurality. For he is himself his One and All, being truly Nothing. Yet observe his perfect self-contentment, and hence learn this lesson: to be self-satisfied is to be vile and ignorant, whereas to aspire is superior to being blindly and impotently happy."

"Now listen."

He paused, and from the tiny buzzing entity arose a low, monotonous, yet distinct tinkling, reminiscent of a Spaceland phonograph. From this source emerged the words: "Infinite beatitude of existence! It is; and there is none else beside It."

"What," I asked, "does the puny creature mean by 'It'?"

"He means himself," replied the Sphere. "Have you not observed before that infants and childish souls, unable to distinguish themselves from the world, speak of themselves in the Third Person? But hush!"

"It fills all Space," the little soliloquizing creature continued, "and whatever It fills, It is. Whatever It thinks, that It utters; and whatever It utters, that It hears; and It itself is Thinker, Utterer, Hearer, Thought, Word, Audition; it is the One, and yet the All in All. Ah, the happiness! Ah, the happiness of Being!"

"Can you not startle the little thing out of its complacency?" I urged. "Reveal to it what it truly is, just as you revealed it to me; expose the narrow limitations of Pointland and lead it upward to something higher."

"That is no easy task," my Master answered. "Try yourself."

Raising my voice to its utmost limit, I addressed the Point thus: "Silence, silence, contemptible Creature! You claim to be the All in All, yet you are the Nothing: your so-called Universe is but a mere speck in a Line, and a Line is but a mere shadow compared to—"

"Hush, hush, you have spoken enough," interrupted the Sphere. "Now listen, and mark the effect of your harangue upon the King of Pointland."

The lustre of the Monarch, who beamed more brightly than ever upon hearing my words, clearly indicated that he retained his complacency. Hardly had I ceased speaking when he resumed his strain.

"Ah, the joy! Ah, the joy of Thought! What can It not achieve by thinking! Its own Thought returning to Itself, suggestive of Its disparagement, thereby enhancing Its happiness! Sweet rebellion stirred up to result in triumph! Ah, the divine creative power of the All in One! Ah, the joy, the joy of Being!"

"You see," said my Teacher, "how little your words have accomplished. So far as the Monarch comprehends them at all, he accepts them as his own—for he cannot conceive of any entity other than himself—and takes pride in the variety of 'Its Thought' as proof of creative power. Let us leave this God of Pointland to the ignorant fruition of his omnipresence and omniscience; nothing that you or I can do can rescue him from his self-satisfaction."

After this, as we floated gently back to Flatland, I heard the mild voice of my Companion pointing out the moral of my vision, stimulating me to aspire and to teach others to aspire. He had initially confessed to being angered by my ambition to soar beyond the Third Dimension; yet, since then, he had gained fresh insight and was no longer too proud to acknowledge his error to a pupil. Proceeding further, he initiated me into mysteries even higher than those I had witnessed, demonstrating how to construct Extra-Solids through the motion of Solids, and Double Extra-Solids through the motion of Extra-Solids—all strictly according to analogy, by methods so simple and accessible that they are patent even to the Female Sex.
