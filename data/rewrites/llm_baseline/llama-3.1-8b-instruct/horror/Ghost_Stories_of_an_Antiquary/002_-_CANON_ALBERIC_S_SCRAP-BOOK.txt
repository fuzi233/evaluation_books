In the quaint, sun-kissed town of St Bertrand de Comminges, perched on the southern slope of the Pyrenees, the air was alive with the whispers of history. It was here, in the spring of 1883, that an Englishman named Dennistoun arrived, drawn by the allure of the ancient church that dominated the hill. A scholar from Cambridge, Dennistoun had set out from Toulouse with two companions, intending to explore the region's rich cultural heritage. However, as they disembarked in St Bertrand, he decided to precede them, eager to delve deeper into the mysteries of the church.

The verger, a wizened old man with a face as dry as the soil beneath their feet, was summoned by the proprietress of the Chapeau Rouge inn. Dennistoun watched with fascination as the sacristan's eyes darted about, his posture betraying a perpetual sense of unease. It was as if he expected some unseen presence to pounce upon him at any moment. The Englishman couldn't help but wonder if the man was haunted by a fixed delusion or oppressed by the weight of a troubled conscience. Perhaps, Dennistoun thought, it was simply the constant scrutiny of his wife that had worn him down.

As the hours passed, Dennistoun immersed himself in his note-book and camera, capturing the intricate details of the church's architecture and artistry. The sacristan, however, remained steadfastly by his side, flitting between the ornate stalls and the dusty relics that adorned the walls. The air was heavy with the scent of aged stone and the faint tang of incense, transporting Dennistoun to a bygone era. Yet, as the minutes ticked by, he grew increasingly uneasy, sensing that the sacristan's presence was not merely a courtesy, but a necessary one.

"Shall I send you home?" Dennistoun asked finally, his words laced with a hint of exasperation. "I'm quite capable of finishing my notes without your assistance. You can lock me in, if you wish."

The sacristan's reaction was immediate and intense, his eyes widening in alarm as he protested, "Leave monsieur alone in the church? Impossible! I'll stay with you, of course.
