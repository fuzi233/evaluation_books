As the sun began to set on the fourteenth of April, Romola found herself once more within the ancient walls of Florence. She had been unable to rest in Pistoja, where rumors of the Trial by Fire had created more questions than answers. The uncertainty had driven her to Prato, and now, despite her trepidation, she felt an inexplicable pull towards the city she had left behind. It was then that she encountered her godfather's confessor, a wise and aged monk from the monastery of San Spirito. He shared with her the tragic news of Savonarola's arrest and the devastating loss of her husband, Tito. The Augustinian monk had witnessed the tumultuous scene in the Piazza, where Tito had attempted to escape the mob by leaping into the Arno, only to be brutally murdered on the riverbank by a man consumed by hatred.

As Romola listened to the tale, she understood the catastrophe in a way that few others did. The monk spoke of Savonarola with a mixture of disdain and skepticism, characteristic of the Black Brethren, who saw the fiery preacher as a deceiver of the people. However, Romola's heart was heavy with grief, and she struggled to focus on the details of the story.

That evening, she arrived in Florence, and as she settled into Monna Brigida's humble abode, the older woman welcomed her with a mix of joy and concern. Romola's cousin, once a devout follower of the Dominican friar, had since abandoned her faith and donned false hair, a symbol of her return to the world. As Monna Brigida regaled Romola with stories of her adventures, Romola's mind remained preoccupied with the weight of her losses. The tragedy of her husband's death and Savonarola's alleged betrayal left her struggling to grasp the smaller details of the world around her.

As Monna Brigida's words spilled forth like a rich Tuscan broth, Romola's thoughts turned to her primary concerns: finding Savonarola and learning the fate of Tessa and their children.
