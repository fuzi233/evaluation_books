—**Mrs. Hemans**

When John Thornton left the house that morning, the world seemed too bright, too loud, too cruel. His blood still pounded with the sting of rejection, his head throbbed as if struck, and the clamor of Milton’s streets grated like a jeer. He longed for silence, for the quiet of a child’s unrestrained weeping—something honest, something raw.

He told himself he hated Margaret Hale. The lie split his chest like a crack of lightning, for beneath the storm of his anger, the truth burned fiercer: he loved her. Loved her even as she scorned him, even as she stood regal and untouchable, flashing defiance at him that morning. *She would not bend. She would not have him.*

An omnibus rumbled past, bound for the countryside. He boarded it without thought, letting the wheels carry him away from the soot and smoke of Milton. Fields unfurled beyond the windows, green and indifferent. He walked where the others walked, strode briskly until his mind numbed.

Memory returned in sharp fragments: the fool he had made of himself, the way he had bared his heart only to have it crushed. And yet—her arms had been around him yesterday. Her breath had warmed his shoulder. For one fleeting moment, she had been his.

By afternoon, he was no wiser. Only more certain. Margaret was unlike any woman who had ever lived—would ever live. She did not love him. She never would. But that changed nothing. He would love her still, defiantly, hopelessly.

Back in Milton, duty awaited. Magistrates to consult, Irish laborers to secure, the lingering chaos of the riot to manage. And then—home. To his mother.

---

Hannah Thornton had waited all day. Needle in hand, linen spread before her, she counted stitches and tablecloths to quiet the gnawing dread. John would return triumphant, engaged, lost to her. She unpicked old initials from damask, her fingers stiff with resentment.

When the door finally opened, she did not look up. Let him speak first. Let her hear the worst at once.

His footsteps paused. The silence stretched. Then—

Her heart clenched. She rose, gripping his shoulders, forcing him to meet her gaze.

*"A girl’s love is smoke,"* she hissed. *"It shifts with the wind. But mine? Mine is iron."*

He flinched. *"Don’t speak against her."*

*"I hate her,"* Hannah growled. *"For your misery. For daring to refuse you."*

*"Then you make me love her more,"* he said, weary. *"Let us never speak of her again."*

She nodded, sharp as a blade. *"Gladly."*

And so they did not. The strike, the warrants, the cold mechanics of business filled the space between them. Strangers might have thought them indifferent. But the fire of their silence burned hotter than any words.
