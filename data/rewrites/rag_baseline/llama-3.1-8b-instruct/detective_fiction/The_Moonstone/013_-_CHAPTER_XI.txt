The air had long since dissipated from the grand estate, leaving behind a faint scent of burnt sugar and citrus, reminiscent of the night's festivities. I lingered in the dimly lit hallway, watching as Samuel expertly mixed a final round of brandy and soda water at the side-table. My lady and Miss Rachel emerged from the drawing room, accompanied by the two gentlemen, their faces aglow with the warmth of the evening. Mr. Godfrey accepted a glass, his eyes scanning the room with a practiced air, while Mr. Franklin, looking drained, opted for a seat in the corner, his eyes fixed on some distant point beyond the fireplace.

As my lady bid the guests farewell, her gaze fell upon the Diamond, now nestled in the folds of Miss Rachel's dress. A faint smile played on her lips as she turned to her daughter. "Rachel, where will you keep the Diamond tonight?" she asked, her voice tinged with a mixture of curiosity and concern.

Miss Rachel, in high spirits and prone to flights of fancy, launched into a series of fanciful declarations. At first, she claimed ignorance of the Diamond's whereabouts, only to quickly assert that it would be placed on her dressing table, alongside her other treasures. However, her thoughts soon turned to the Diamond's alleged ability to shine with an otherworldly light, and she proposed moving it to an Indian cabinet in her sitting room, where it could be admired by its native counterparts. Her mother intervened, gently pointing out the cabinet's lack of a lock, and Miss Rachel's response – "Good heavens, mamma, is this an hotel? Are there thieves in the house?" – sent a ripple of amusement through the room.

As the evening drew to a close, my lady bid the gentlemen goodnight, her eyes lingering on her daughter with a mixture of affection and concern. "Why not let me keep the Diamond for you tonight, Rachel?" she suggested, her voice softening. Miss Rachel's response was akin to a child refusing to part with a beloved toy, and my lady knew better than to press the issue further. "Come into my room, Rachel, first thing tomorrow morning," she said, her words laced with a hint of unease. With that, she turned to leave, lost in thought, her expression a map of the turmoil brewing within.
