As the clock struck half past five, Newland Archer made his way to the unassuming stucco house on West Twenty-third Street, its wisteria-draped balcony a gentle contrast to the cast-iron framework. The house, rented from the eccentric Medora, seemed an unusual choice for the Countess Olenska, whose refined sensibilities might have been better suited to a more elegant neighborhood. Yet, as he stood before the door, Archer felt a spark of curiosity ignite within him.

The area surrounding the house was a far cry from the aristocratic enclaves he was accustomed to. Dressmakers and bird-stuffers inhabited the nearby buildings, their lives a world away from the rarefied atmosphere of the city's elite. Further down the street, a dilapidated wooden house stood at the end of a winding path, where the writer and journalist Winsett resided. Though Winsett rarely received visitors, Archer recalled the writer's words, spoken during a chance encounter: "The humanities are not confined to grandeur." The notion lingered, leaving Archer to ponder the relationship between art and environment.

Madame Olenska's dwelling, though similarly modest, boasted a few well-placed touches of paint, which lent it a hint of warmth. Archer's thoughts turned to the Polish Count, whose reputation for extravagance and deception had preceded him. He wondered if the Count had indeed stripped the Countess of her fortune, as well as her innocence.

The previous day had been a trying one for Archer. A luncheon with the Wellands had been followed by a grueling round of family visits, during which he had struggled to make his case for hastening their wedding. Mrs. Welland's stern reminders of the numerous gifts they would need to purchase – "twelve dozen of everything, hand-embroidered" – had left him feeling stifled. As they traveled from one doorstep to another in their family landau, Archer felt like a caged animal, longing to break free from the suffocating expectations that surrounded him.

Upon parting from May, he was left with a sense of melancholy. The Wellands' decision to postpone the wedding until the following autumn weighed heavily on his mind, and he couldn't shake the feeling that he was trapped in a life not of his choosing. When Mrs. Welland announced their next destination – the Chiverses and the Dallases – Archer felt a pang of resignation.
