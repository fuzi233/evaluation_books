I lingered, watching the last shimmer of the white leopardess vanish into the shadows before turning to follow the librarian—though every instinct rebelled. *Sleep?* Here, among the dead, while the world hummed with unseen urgency? Time slipped past like smoke. The sun bled into the horizon, and the air grew thick with the creeping chill of the tomb. My pulse faltered as the librarian’s form blurred into the gloom, his footsteps fading.

Then came the raven.

Its wings cut the silence, slow and deliberate. Fireflies winked in the void; the moon, pallid and reluctant, dragged itself above the land.

*"You are weary, Mr. Vane,"* the raven observed, perching on a jagged stone. *"You’ll need the horse by morning."*

It whistled—a sound like wind through bone.

A shadow blotted the moon. Hooves drummed, distant at first, then thunderous. The beast erupted from the lunar face itself—a specter of sinew and gleaming bone, its mane a storm of silver, its tail lashing like a whip. Nineteen hands tall, its hide stretched taut over a frame built for ruin. Death’s own mount.

Yet I *wanted* it.

Greed, raw and clawing, coiled in my chest. I ran my hands over its ribs, each one sharp beneath satin skin. The moon’s reflection pooled in its flanks. I whispered to it, breathed into its nostrils—and it answered with a hot gust, its eyes twin pits of blue fire over smoldering coals.

*"Good,"* croaked the raven. *"He’ll bear you better if he knows you."*

"Let me ride him now," I demanded.

I hauled myself onto its back, fingers twisted in its mane. The horse stood motionless.

*"Why delay?"* the raven asked.

"The leopardess—" I swallowed. "I *must* follow her."

But the horse beneath me was power incarnate. I *ached* to let it run.

Adam’s voice cut through the dark, cold as a blade. *"You’ve done nothing of worth, Mr. Vane. Do you know why?"*

"Because I’m a fool."

*"Dead men,"* he said, *"cannot wake the dead."*

The words meant nothing. I dug my heels in.

The horse *moved*.

Night became a blur. The wind screamed; the earth vanished beneath us. The moon shuddered in its arc—then *rolled*, tumbling like a dropped coin. Darkness swallowed us mid-leap.

The horse fell.

I staggered up, hands skimming its lifeless bulk. No breath. No pulse. Just a carcass.

I sat atop the corpse, face in my hands, as the world turned to ash around me.
