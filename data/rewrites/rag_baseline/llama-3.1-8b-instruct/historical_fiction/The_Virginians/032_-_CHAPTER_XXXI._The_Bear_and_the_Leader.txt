The silence in the room was oppressive, a heavy blanket draped over the polished wood of the Tunbridge Wells Assembly Rooms. Madame de Bernstein sat in a plush chair, her rheumatic joints creaking softly as she shifted her weight. Her gaze drifted between the faces of the assembly, the murmurs of conversation a gentle hum in the background. She was a woman of experience, one who had weathered many a storm and seen the best and worst of human nature. And yet, despite her advanced years, she remained convinced of her own acuity, her instincts sharp as a knife.

Her nephew, Harry Warrington, had been the subject of much speculation among the assembly. Whispers of his alleged dalliances with the ladies had reached even the ears of the Baroness, who had taken it upon herself to monitor his behavior. But Harry was a cunning young man, one who knew how to present himself to the world. His gallantry and charm had won over many a heart, including those of the ladies who had attended his gatherings at the Wells. Madame de Bernstein's own skepticism had begun to wane, her vigilance slackening as she grew older and her energies waned.

As she watched the assembly, Madame de Bernstein's thoughts turned to the younger generation. The ladies of today were a far cry from the fierce, dragon-like women of her youth. They were softer, more pliable, their passions and desires more easily swayed. And the men, oh the men, were no better. They chased after fleeting pleasures, their appetites insatiable, their morals lax. In her day, a woman's virtue had been a shield, a protection against the ravages of a cruel world. But now, it seemed, virtue was a luxury few could afford.

The assembly's chatter grew louder, the music and laughter mingling with the scent of perfume and candle wax. Madame de Bernstein's thoughts turned inward, her mind wandering back to the stories of old, when dragons ruled the land and virtue was a beacon of light in a dark world. She smiled to herself, remembering the tales of her youth, when knights in shining armor rode forth to vanquish the forces of evil. How quaint, how naive, those stories seemed now. The world had changed, and so had the people in it.
