As Gerard settled into his modest lodgings on the west bank of the Tiber, the scent of freshly baked bread wafted through the air, mingling with the sounds of merchants hawking their wares. Every morning, Gerard would venture out in search of work, carrying a specimen of his writing to each shop he visited. However, the reception was lukewarm, with most traders dismissing his Latin script as too thick and his ink too dark. "Can you not write Greek?" they'd ask, their tone dismissive. Gerard would reply that while he could indeed write in Greek, his Latin was far more refined.

Undeterred, Gerard spent his evenings pouring over a borrowed Greek manuscript, the creaking of the parchment beneath his fingers a soothing accompaniment to the clinking of pots and pans coming from the kitchen below. In a fortnight, his Greek script improved significantly, and he began working tirelessly until noon, reserving the afternoons for hunting down potential clients. When he presented a superior Greek specimen, the traders informed him that both Greek and Latin were unfashionable, the city flooded with manuscripts from across Europe. Gerard should have arrived a year earlier, they said.

To alleviate his disappointment, Gerard treated himself to a beautiful psaltery, its strings humming a gentle melody as he played in the evenings. His landlady, a stout woman with a warm smile, often chatted with him as she worked in the kitchen, preparing meals for the other lodgers. One day, she invited Gerard to join her for dinner, and as they sat together, she turned the conversation to his writing. "Those sly traders," she whispered, "have writers in their pay, who receive a handsome sum for their work, while they pay paltry wages to those like you. No wonder they're cold towards you, my dear. You write too well." Gerard asked her to explain, and she leaned in closer, her voice barely above a whisper. "Your excellent work might put their own out of business."

Gerard sighed, his heart heavy with the weight of her words. "Alas, dear lady, you see the worst in people, and yet you're so kind and generous yourself." The landlady chuckled, her eyes twinkling. "Ah, my dear, the Romans are a cunning lot. I'm just a simple Siennese woman, thank the Virgin."
