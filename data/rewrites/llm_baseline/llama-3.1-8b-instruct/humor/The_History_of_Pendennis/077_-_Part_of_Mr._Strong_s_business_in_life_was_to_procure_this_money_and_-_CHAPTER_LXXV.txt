As the evening train to London rumbled into Tunbridge station, Pen paced the platform with a mix of frustration and desperation. It felt like an eternity had passed since he'd last checked his pocket watch – six hours, he calculated, though it couldn't possibly have been that long. The train finally pulled in, and as passengers disembarked, a harried gentleman forgot his carpet-bag in the luggage rack, hastily rushing to reclaim it. Meanwhile, Pen hastened towards a nearby cab, where he requested the driver take him to Jermyn Street at top speed. The cabbie, a seasoned hansom man, nodded in gratitude at the tip thrust into his hand, and Pen sprinted up the stairs to Lady Rockminster's apartments.

Laura sat alone in the drawing room, lost in the pages of a book by the soft glow of a lamp. Her pale complexion seemed almost translucent in the dim light. As Pen entered, she looked up, her eyes locking onto his with an intensity that spoke volumes. The delicate balance of life's great moments often hinges on the briefest of interactions – a glance, a touch, a whispered word. We may enter with Lady Rockminster as she rises from her after-dinner slumber and makes her way to her sitting room.

Upon entering, Lady Rockminster's first words are a gentle reprimand: "Upon my word, young people!" Her attendant casts a curious gaze over her shoulder, no doubt sensing the charged atmosphere within. And indeed, the young couple is poised in a familiar tableau – one that every young lady has either witnessed, heard tales of, or perhaps even dared to hope for. Pen, upon entering the room, swiftly approaches Laura, her hand extended in greeting. Seizing it just as she begins to rise from her chair, he falls to his knees, his voice trembling as he declares, "I've seen her. She's chosen Harry Foker – and now, Laura?"

The gentle pressure of her hand, the warmth in her eyes, and the faint tremble of her lips convey a response that transcends words. Pen's head drops into her lap as he sobs out a heartfelt plea: "Come and bless us, dear mother."
