As the morning sun rose over the sleepy village, casting a warm glow over the dusty streets of Whinbury, Moore stirred in his small cottage, the scent of freshly brewed coffee wafting through the air. His sister, Hortense, had already begun preparing breakfast, the soft clinking of dishes and the gentle hum of the stove filling the kitchen. Moore rose, his movements economical and deliberate, and made his way to the dining area, where a steaming cup of café au lait awaited him. He took a seat at the table, the tartines cut into neat triangles, and began to eat in silence, his eyes scanning the room as if searching for something.

His thoughts, however, were elsewhere, lost in the labyrinthine world of commerce and finance that had been his domain for years. He had risen before dawn to attend to some pressing matter, the details of which he kept locked away, even from his sister's curious gaze. As he finished his meal, he rose and made his way to his counting-house, a small, cluttered room filled with ledgers, papers, and the faint scent of ink.

Henry, Joe Scott's young apprentice, was already there, sorting through the day's correspondence and newspapers. Moore took his seat behind the desk, breaking the seals on the documents and scanning their contents with a critical eye. The letters were short, but their tone was unmistakable – a mixture of disappointment and frustration that hung in the air like a challenge. Moore's nostrils flared, and for a moment, his eyes seemed to flash with defiance, as if daring the universe to present him with more of the same.

He chose a pen, stripped away the quill, and began to write, his movements swift and decisive. The answers flew onto the page, each sentence a precise and calculated response to the demands of the day. When he finished, he sealed the letters and set them aside, his gaze drifting towards the window, where the sunlight streaming in highlighted the dust motes dancing in the air.

The breeze carried the acrid scent of the nearby mill, its chimney puffing out great plumes of soot that stained the sky a deep, foreboding grey. Moore's eyes narrowed, his mind still fixed on the tasks ahead, but his body seemed to relax, his shoulders easing into the chair as he settled back to read the newspaper. The headlines seemed dull, the stories unremarkable, and he found himself glancing at his watch, the ticking a steady beat in the silence
