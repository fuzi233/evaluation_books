Consciousness returned not as a sudden dawn, but as a slow, rhythmic rising from the deep water of the Country of Silence. I was lying on a bed, a sensation so foreign and yet so necessary that I understood, vaguely, that I would never need it again, nor ascend from the silence below. From the abyss beneath the world, the Underground Organs began to throb—a deep, resonant thunder that sounded like music played beyond the reach of death. It rolled outward, a chanting of multitudes singing across far mountains, shifting between the low, mournful breath of a distant wind and the clear, ancient melody of the Song of Honour. I knew, in a dreamlike state, that millions in those depths were celebrating this miracle of joy. Yet the vision remained faint, half-hidden behind closed eyelids that refused to open fully, as if I were drifting eternally on strange, unreal waters.

Then came the scent of reality: sweet, lovely odours drifting from the great Fields where flowers grew perpetually along the lift's pathways. The lift itself was ascending, carrying me through miles of stone. Perhaps I stirred slightly, for the voice of the Master Doctor reached me, low and gentle, bidding me rest. All was well with the Maid. Afterward, I drifted into a haze, existing in a suspended state of half-life and half-sleep, wondering without anxiety whether I was truly dead.

Days followed, quiet and empty of thought. The Master Doctor would lean over me constantly, his gaze sharp and probing. Finally, after intervals that seemed endless, another figure bent over me. It was the dear, lovely face of Mine Own. Her eyes spoke love directly into my soul, though her expression remained calm and hushed. In that moment, I began to live again in my body. My hands fumbled weakly; she caught them, holding them tight, and life flowed from her to me. She was silent, gentle, and in that stillness, contentment took root, leading naturally to a peaceful slumber.

When the day finally came for me to rise, the attendants carried me to one of the Quiet Gardens of the Pyramid. They placed me there and stepped back, leaving me seemingly alone. Then, someone emerged from behind a bush, looking at me with a momentary hesitation, a shy uncertainty. Yet the love shining in her eyes rendered that shyness insignificant. Truly, it was Mine Own. I had never seen Naani dressed as a maid before, and she appeared more dainty, more exquisite, than I could have imagined. Suddenly, I attempted to rise to meet her, overcome by a natural foolishness, but she ran quickly to stop me. She sat beside me, resting my head against her breast, offering her lips freely. In that single instant, she was both maid and mother to me.

She commanded me to remain still, and we sat in an utter, dumb happiness until the attendants returned. The Master of the Doctors was among them, and I saw a genuine satisfaction in his face. From that day forward, Mine Own visited me daily. My recovery accelerated with astonishing speed, for Love healed me. Soon, I was permitted to descend to the Fields, but only via private paths, for the Multitudes might otherwise swarm to follow me; I needed solitude. Mine Own accompanied me. The Master Monstruwacan and the Master of the Doctors had agreed, appointing an Officer of Marriage to wed us quietly and simply. I was too weak for a public ceremony, which would occur later, when the Millions bestowed upon us a Guard of Honour stretching eight miles high from the summit to the base of the Mighty Pyramid. That grand ceremonial was reserved for the future, a tribute the Peoples could not deny giving to their hero.

For now, Mine Own was my constant companion, my wife, my strength, restoring me to perfect health. We were in the Love Days—the most beautiful season, provided the Love was true. We wandered the mighty Fields at will, walking the Love Paths near the villages. These villages were vast, truly countries in themselves. We hid our names to avoid the natural curiosity and kindness of strangers, needing to remain utterly together and quiet. We chose sleeping spots where the floral beauty was most wondrous, carrying provisions, though we ate whenever we reached a village.

Mine Own kept her promise a hundredfold, preparing great, hearty meals and teasing me relentlessly about my gluttony, kissing me to ensure I never found words in my defense. She was everything my heart and spirit desired, accompanying me with Love and entering my spirit with pure Joy. Once, we descended briefly into the Country of Silence, though we stayed only a short time before my memories returned. In later wanderings, we explored that realm often, accompanied by Memory, the holiness of great thoughts, and a Love that holds all things.

As we left the Country of Silence, I told Mine Own of a terrible realization: when she had been suspended by the Horrid Force of the House, I had suffered dreadful pain, fearing I would never wake to discover her kissing me while I slept. Mine Own blushed with lovely shame, unaware that I had been conscious of her sweetness during her supposed death. She accepted my agony, knowing she had died before the Earth-Force's vapour freed her spirit. The Doctors explained that she had been stunned, her spirit frozen, her entire being suspended. Only the great life-force of the Earth-Current had awakened her spirit, allowing her body to resume flowing blood and life. The Doctors had searched the old records extensively, finding perhaps one similar instance in antiquity, but nothing comparable throughout the ages.

While wandering and resting in the Fields, I shared stories with her, learning that she too had acquired strange knowledge during her illness, though not much, for she had been utterly helpless, forced from her bed while I lay still. The Master Doctor had ordered this separation to prevent my certain death, hoping to awaken my spirit. I marveled at the depth of my love, knowing she had held my hands bravely and gently despite having scarce power to stand. I offered a small prayer of praise for Mine Own.

Thus, I arrive at my conclusion, with one final account. This occurred shortly after our second, public marriage. One day, My Wife—Mine Own—took me with sweet cunning to the Hall of Honour. Upon arrival, I saw the Peoples standing in silence, waiting expectantly. She led me to the center of the hall. Suddenly, I understood her purpose. Standing in the Place of Honour was a statue of a man in broken armour, carrying a maid forever. I fell silent. How can one comprehend the magnitude of this honour? Such accolades were granted only to the Great Dead. I was but a young man, far removed from greatness, except that I loved with all my heart and spirit, rendering death a trivial thing compared to Love.

Love makes the heart sweet and brave, granting understanding in humility and wonder. Mine Own wept with joy and honest pride beside me. A profound silence of sympathetic love filled the great Hall. Those present allowed us to depart in quiet dignity. Walking away with Mine Own, close beside me, I realized I had gained Honour, yet learned that Honour is merely the ash of Life unless tempered by Love. To possess Love is to possess all, for True Love mothers Honour and Faithfulness; these three construct the House of Joy.
