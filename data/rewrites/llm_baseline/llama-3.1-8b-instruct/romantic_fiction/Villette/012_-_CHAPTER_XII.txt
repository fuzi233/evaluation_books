As I wandered through the tranquil gardens behind Madame Beck's residence on the Rue Fossette, the softening veil of time lent a serene quality to the surroundings. Despite being nestled in the heart of the city, the garden's verdant charm was a haven from the urban bustle. In the fading light of day, the once-majestic convent trees now stood as sentinels, their gnarled branches etched against the evening sky like nature's own calligraphy. Their fruit-laden limbs, now bare and twisted, still held a mystical allure, as if the very essence of the convent's history clung to them.

A local legend spoke of a spectral nun, said to appear on certain nights, her black habit and white veil a harbinger of foreboding. Some claimed to have glimpsed her form flitting between the trees, her presence a reminder of a dark chapter in the convent's past. It was said that a young woman, accused of transgressing her vows, had been entombed alive beneath the garden's soil, her spirit trapped in a perpetual cycle of sorrow. The notion, though unsubstantiated, had taken root in the collective imagination, fueling whispers of a ghostly presence that haunted the garden's shadows.

Beyond the realm of superstition, however, the garden held a quiet beauty. As a student at Madame Beck's school, I often stole away to the gardens during the summer months, drawn to the stillness and the sweet scents that wafted on the breeze. The gravel paths sparkled like polished stone, and the sun-bright nasturtiums that clambered up the ancient trees seemed to dance in the fading light. A grand berceau, its canopy a haven from the sun, cast dappled shadows across the grass, while a smaller, more secluded arbor, nestled in the vines that wrapped around the high wall, was a haven for jasmine and ivy to entwine.

In the midst of the day, when the schoolchildren's laughter and shouts filled the air, the garden might seem a common, well-trodden space. But as the sun dipped below the horizon, casting the world in a warm, golden light, the garden transformed. The air grew heavy with the scent of blooming flowers, and the soft chime of St. Jean Baptiste's bells carried on the breeze, a gentle summons to pause and savor the beauty of the moment.
