Where is the woman who cannot play a part when love demands it? Where is she who will not rise to the occasion, and act with grace and courage, to save the man she holds most dear? In moments like these, even the simplest of women find strength within them—nature itself steps in to teach her how to deceive even the wisest of men.

No sooner had the men left the room than Margaret slipped from her bed, still clad in her skirt, petticoat, stockings, and nightdress. She moved silently to the long chest where she had lain, lifted its lid—along with the blankets and coverings—and set it against the wall. Then, on silent feet, she glided to the door and listened. The footsteps faded through her father’s chamber and down the stairs. All was quiet.

But this chest held a secret known only to her and Gerard. A section of the floorboard had once been broken, and when Gerard was called upon to repair it, he lacked materials. So he cleverly cut away a portion just large enough to fit the chest beneath the boards, using the wood to mend the rest of the floor. As a result, the chest rested on the rafters a full foot below the surface, making it twice as deep as it appeared.

Margaret knelt, her heart pounding, and gave thanks to Heaven. Then she crept back to the chest, leaned over it, and whispered softly, “Gerard!”

No answer.

She spoke again, a little louder: “Gerard, it’s safe—thank God! You may come up now—but be careful!”

Still no reply.

Her hand trembled as she placed it on his shoulder. “Gerard!”

Nothing.

“Oh, what has happened?” she cried, her voice breaking. Her hands flew over his face, his chest, searching for breath, for life. She gripped his shoulders, shook him, lifted him—but he slipped from her grasp like a weightless thing, falling back without resistance, as though he were no longer flesh and blood.

A cold dread seized her. The lid had been closed. She had lain upon it. The men had been in the room for some time. With a strength born of desperation, she tore him from the chest, cradling him in her arms. She carried him to the window, flung it open wide, and laid him out into the cool night air. Moonlight bathed his pale form. His face was gray as ash. His body limp. Lifeless.

She pressed her fingers to his heart. It did not beat. Not a flicker. Horror struck her like lightning. He was dead. She had suffocated him with her own weight.

The mind cannot instantly accept such a horror—so sudden, so terrible. Just minutes ago, Gerard had been alive, hidden beneath her. How could he now be gone?

She called to him, pleading with every name of love she could summon—names never spoken before, too tender for words. She kissed his lips, stroked his hair, begged him to speak, to look at her, to show that he still lived. But there was no response. No sign. Only silence.

Then, trembling uncontrollably, she began to whisper to his ashen face—words both foolish and heartbreaking. “Oh, Gerard… I’m so sorry you’re dead. I’m so sorry I killed you. Forgive me for not letting them take you—it would have been better than this. Oh, Gerard… I’m so, so sorry.”

Suddenly, madness took hold. “No! No! This can’t be true—or there is no God! It’s monstrous! How can my Gerard be dead? How could I have killed him? I love him. Oh, God, You know how I love him! He doesn’t know. I never told him. If he knew my heart, he’d speak to me—he wouldn’t stay so deaf to poor Margaret. This must be a trick—to make me scream and betray him. But no—I love him too much for that. I’ll choke first!”

And with that, she clutched her own throat, fighting the urge to cry out in terror and despair. “If only he’d say one word… Oh, Gerard! Don’t die without speaking. Have mercy on me—scold me, curse me, but speak! If you’re angry, scold me! I deserve it—the fool who killed the man she loved more than herself. Ah! I am a murderess—the worst in all the world! Help! Help! I’ve murdered him! Ah! Ah! Ah! Ah! Ah!”

She tore at her hair, shrieking again and again—wild, piercing cries that echoed through the night like a death knell. They reached the ears of Dierich Brower and his men, who sprang to their feet, staring at one another in alarm.
