To reconstruct the architecture of Sola's narrative, one must first descend into the claustrophobic theater of the palace dining hall, where the air itself seemed thick with the weight of unspoken conspiracies. I drew Sola forth, guiding her past the rigid formalities demanded by the Green Men, until she finally unveiled the harrowing chronicle of Dejah Thoris's abduction. Her account began seven days prior, a timeline marked by the silent agony of a princess whose mind had fractured under the shadow of Zat Arras. Though I had not witnessed the private audience between the Princess and the usurper, the tremor in her posture betrayed a catastrophe of profound magnitude. When I discovered her attempting to flee the palace in the dead of night, the destination was obvious; she sought the Sacred Iss and the Valley Dor, fleeing the poison of the court.

We mobilized instantly, rallying a dozen of her most devoted guardians. Their loyalty was absolute, a fierce counterweight to the tyranny encroaching upon us. We tracked her wandering spirit through the labyrinthine corridors, eventually overtaking her near the outer gates. Beside her rode Woola, the faithful hound, the only constant amidst the chaos. When she perceived our approach, she feigned a rage born of desperation, ordering us to retreat, but for the first time, we defied her command. Seeing that she would not allow herself to embark on this final, solitary pilgrimage, her mask shattered. Tears streamed down her face as she embraced us, and together we vanished into the southern night.

Our journey was a blur of speed and peril. By the fifth dawn, we had traversed vast distances southward when the horizon erupted with the sight of a massive fleet of battleships turning north. They spotted us before we could conceal ourselves, and soon we were encircled by a horde of Black Martians. The Princess's guard fought with a noble ferocity, holding the line until their numbers were extinguished. In the aftermath, only Dejah Thoris and I remained alive. Realizing the trap, she attempted suicide, but a Black pirate snatched her dagger, and we were both bound, our hands rendered useless.

The fleet pressed onward, a armada of twenty great battleships supported by swift cruisers. That evening, a scout cruiser returned from ahead, bearing a new prize: a young Red woman captured in the hills beneath the nose of a trio of Red Martian battleships. Overheard fragments of conversation revealed the Black pirates' obsession—they were hunting a specific group of fugitives who had evaded them days prior. This new captive, however, held a significance that transcended mere intelligence. The commander interrogated her with intense fervor, treating her capture as a matter of paramount importance.

She was Thuvia, Princess of Ptarth, a beauty of striking grace. She recounted to Dejah Thoris a voluntary pilgrimage undertaken years ago from her father's court. When Thuvia learned the identity of her fellow captive, she fell to her knees, kissing the fettered hands of the Princess of Helium. She confessed that that very morning she had been with John Carter, Prince of Helium, and Carthoris, his son. Dejah Thoris, initially skeptical, listened as Thuvia detailed the extraordinary adventures shared among them in the Land of the First Born. The evidence was irrefutable; only John Carter could have performed such deeds. As Thuvia spoke of her love for the Prince and his unwavering devotion to the woman he chose, Dejah Thoris broke down in tears, cursing Zat Arras and the cruel fate that had torn her from Helium just days before her beloved lord's expected return.

"I do not blame you for loving him, Thuvia," Dejah Thoris sobbed, finding solace in the purity of the confession. "And that your affection is genuine, I can believe from the candor of your avowal."

Yet, the fleet's trajectory shifted. Approaching Helium, they realized John Carter had escaped, and they turned south once more. Soon, a guard entered our compartment and seized me, dragging me to the deck. "There is no place in the Land of the First Born for a green one," he sneered, delivering a blow that sent me tumbling from the heights. He intended to kill me, to rid the vessel of my presence forever. But fortune, in its capricious mercy, intervened. The ship moved slowly, skimming the surface of the terrain. As I plunged into the darkness below, expecting impact with the earth, I landed softly upon a mass of vegetation merely twenty feet beneath the hull. The keel had grazed the ground; I had survived the fall.

Spent and bruised, I lay through the night atop the highest peak of a lofty range. At sunrise, the panorama unfolded: a vast expanse of sea-bottom and distant hills stretched far below. The fleet, hovering precariously close to the surface during the previous night, had pitched me to what they believed was certain death. A few miles west lay a great waterway belonging to Helium. There, I secured a thoat, and the rest of the tale is known.

Silence reigned for many minutes, broken only by the internal thunder of my own heart. *Dejah Thoris in the clutches of the First Born!* The thought chilled me, but swiftly, the ancient fire of unconquerable confidence roared back to life. I sprang to my feet, shoulders thrown back, sword raised high, and vowed a solemn oath to reach her, rescue her, and exact vengeance. From a hundred scabbards, a hundred swords leaped forth; a hundred fighting-men surged onto the table-top, pledging their lives and fortunes to the expedition. Plans crystallized instantly. I thanked each loyal friend, leaving Carthoris to host them, and withdrew to my audience chamber with Kantos Kan, Tars Tarkas, Xodar, and Hor Vastus.

Here, in the dim glow of candlelight, we mapped the path to victory. Xodar posited that Issus would likely spare both Dejah Thoris and Thuvia for a year, providing a window of comparative safety and a fixed location for our search. The logistics of the fleet were entrusted to Kantos Kan and Xodar. The latter, having overseen the refitting of captured battleships for decades, directed the installation of water-propellers and auxiliary gearing essential for navigating Omean. Six months of absolute secrecy were required to prepare the armada, a duration deemed necessary to evade Zat Arras's prying eyes.

Kantos Kan voiced a grim prophecy regarding the usurper. "I doubt," he murmured, "if he would even welcome Dejah Thoris' return. With you and Carthoris removed from the equation, the path to the title of Jeddak lies clear before him. As long as he reigns supreme, neither of you is safe."

"There is a way," cried Hor Vastus, his eyes alight with a dangerous brilliance, "to thwart him effectively and forever."

"What?" I asked.

He smiled enigmatically. "I shall whisper it here, but someday I shall stand upon the dome of the Temple of Reward and shout it to the cheering multitudes below."

"Speak," I urged.

"John Carter, Jeddak of Helium," Hor Vastus whispered, the name hanging heavy in the air.

Light ignited in the eyes of my companions, grim smiles of anticipation stretching their faces as they turned to me expectantly. But I shook my head. "No, my friends," I replied, suppressing the surge of ambition. "Not yet. We must wait until Tardos Mors and Mors Kajak are gone, returning no more. If I remain, I will ensure the people of Helium choose their next Jeddak fairly. Whomever they select shall hold the loyalty of my sword, and I shall not seek honor for myself. Until then, Tardos Mors is Jeddak, and Zat Arras is his representative."

Hor Vastus nodded, but his gaze drifted to the window overlooking the gardens. "What was that?" he whispered urgently.

Before the words could fully form, he was already springing to the balcony. "There he goes!" he shouted, pointing frantically downward. "The guards! Below! The guards!"

We rushed to the windows, witnessing a figure dart across a patch of grass and vanish into the dense shrubbery. "He was on the balcony when I first saw him," Hor Vastus cried. "Follow him!"

We descended into the gardens, scouring the grounds with the entire guard for hours, yet the night marauder left no trace.

"What do you make of it, Kantos Kan?" Tars Tarkas asked.

"A spy sent by Zat Arras," the elder replied calmly. "It was always his method."

"He will have something interesting to report to his master," Hor Vastus chuckled darkly. "I hope he heard only our references to a new Jeddak."

"If he overheard our plans to rescue Dejah Thoris," I said, my voice hardening, "it will mean civil war. He will attempt to thwart us, and I will not be thwarted. I would turn against Tardos Mors himself if necessity demanded it. If it plunges all Helium into bloodshed, I shall proceed regardless. Nothing short of death shall stop me. Should I fall, my friends, will you swear to prosecute the search and bring her back safely to her grandfather's court?"

Upon the hilts of their swords, each man swore the oath.
