Waiting.

As the Tuscan sun climbed higher in the sky, its warm rays struggled to penetrate the gloom that had settled over Florence. The days passed, each one a slow, agonizing tick of the clock, without bringing either the letter from Baldassarre that Romola longed for or the news of the conspiracy that the Government was so desperate to uncover. Yet, even as she waited, other events unfolded, weaving a tapestry of trials and tribulations that bound her closer to Fra Girolamo.

The Plague spread its dark wings over the city, casting a shadow of fear and despair over every household. Romola watched as the afflicted huddled in their homes, praying for deliverance, while the priests and monks, once proud and confident, now moved with hesitant steps, their voices barely above a whisper as they intoned the prayers of absolution. And amidst this backdrop of suffering, the Excommunication of Savonarola was solemnly announced from the pulpit of the Duomo.

Romola felt an overwhelming urge to witness the scene, to bear witness to the turmoil that had taken hold of the city. She knew that the Excommunication would only strengthen her resolve to stand by the Frate, to follow him into the darkness, no matter the cost. As she pushed through the crowds, she felt the weight of the people's gaze upon her, their faces twisted in a mixture of fear and defiance. The air was heavy with tension, the very atmosphere thick with the weight of unspoken questions: where did duty end and resistance begin?

In the Duomo, the once-mighty voice of the priest was reduced to a mere whisper, a monotone recitation of words that had lost their power to inspire. The flickering candles cast eerie shadows on the stone walls, and the sound of the bells was like a mournful sigh. Romola's heart ached as she saw the faces of the faithful, their eyes downcast, their spirits broken. The Frate's words, once a beacon of hope, now seemed a defiant challenge to the established order.

And yet, despite the overwhelming fear and uncertainty that surrounded her, Romola felt a deep sense of purpose. She knew that she stood on the cusp of a great decision, one that would determine the course of her life forever.
