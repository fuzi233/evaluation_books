As Margaret stepped into the warm glow of their household, a faint scent of wood smoke lingered in the air, carried on the whispers of the past. On the table, two letters lay waiting – one addressed to her mother, its creased surface bearing the weight of her father's anxious eyes, and another, silken and rustling, adorned with the faint impressions of foreign lands. Margaret's fingers brushed against the latter, her aunt Shaw's words dancing across the page like the gentle lapping of waves against a distant shore. As she read, her father burst forth from the study, his footsteps heavy with the weight of worry.

"So your mother has retired early," he said, his voice a low rumble, "and on such a stormy day, no less. I'm afraid the doctor's visit may have unsettled her. What did he say, child?" Margaret's hesitation was a whispered secret, one that only her father's keen ear seemed to catch. His face grew graver, his eyes narrowing as he searched for reassurance.

"He believes she's not seriously ill, just in need of care," Margaret replied, her voice a soft melody. "He was kind, and promised to return soon to check on his remedies." Her father's gaze lingered, his mind already racing with the possibilities of what might lie ahead. The shadows in the room seemed to deepen, as if darkness itself was gathering, waiting to pounce.

"Only care, you say? No change of air? No warning that this city's damp air might be doing her harm?" His words trailed off, lost in the fog of his own fears. Margaret's eyes met his, and in that moment, she saw the weight of his anxiety, the unspoken worries that plagued him like a restless spirit. He tried to brush it aside, to distract himself with the mundane tasks of the day, but his thoughts kept returning, like a river flowing back to its source.

As they sat down to dinner, Margaret handed him the letter from her aunt Shaw, who had fled the heat of Naples for the cooler climes of Sorrento. "She's settled into a new life," Margaret said, her voice barely above a whisper. "But I think she misses the familiar rhythms of home." Her father's eyes strayed, his mind wandering to the letter from the doctor, and the unspoken fears that lingered within.
