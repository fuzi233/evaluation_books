For years, the story had echoed through the Great Redoubt, vibrating through the stone of the thousandth floor and drifting down to the deepest tiers of the underground cities. It was a tale of a second sanctuary, a hidden refuge in the desolation of the Night Lands where the last millions of humanity had gathered to fight to the end. I had heard it everywhere during my travels, which began at my seventeenth year and lasted for three years and two hundred and twenty-five days. In those days, a child spent only one day in each city, a rigid training in the vast machinery of survival.

We traveled constantly, meeting countless souls whom I loved deeply, yet whom I could never see again, for life offered no space for lingering. Duty to the Redoubt demanded it. Yet, amidst the millions and the fleeting years, the story of the other Refuge remained constant. Libraries held volumes asserting its existence with confidence, records from ages past that seemed undeniable then but were now read with skepticism by scholars who doubted even as they studied.

I, however, possessed no such doubt. From the moment Master Monstruwacan spoke of it to me, I knew it was true. He and I shared a bond rare between mentor and student; though he was fully grown and I merely a youth, our friendship was absolute. At twenty-one, he appointed me to the Tower of Observation at the Pyramid's apex. This was the highest honor in the Redoubt, a post that satisfied the natural human curiosity for astronomy, even if the stars were forever hidden.

My appointment was justified by a singular gift: Night-Hearing. While a fanciful term, it described a rare ability possessed by none in the entire Pyramid except myself. I could hear the invisible vibrations of the aether, taking messages from the eternal darkness without instruments, often clearer than the machines themselves. To me, the voice of Mirdath the Beautiful, a love lost to an aeon ago, seemed to whisper from my memory-dreams, singing sweetly within my soul.

One day, at the fifteenth hour, the beginning of Sleep-Time, I stood pondering this ancient love. As a young man might, I imagined that lost beauty whispering into my ears. Suddenly, I thrilled as if struck. Out of the everlasting night came a whisper, thrumming against my subtle hearing.

Four long years had passed since that awakening when I was seventeen. Now, from the world-darkness of that lost life, the whisper arrived. Knowing it instantly, yet trained in wisdom, I did not answer with a name. Instead, I sent the Master-Word through the night, projecting it with my brain-elements. I knew that if the caller were truly Mirdath, she would possess the power to hear without instruments. If it were a false calling from the Evil Forces, the House of Silence, or cunning monsters, they would be unable to speak the Master-Word—a truth proven through the Everlasting.

Trembling, striving not to tense my spirit, I felt the throb of the Master-Word circle my essence. With all the sweetness in my heart, I called out mentally: "Mirdath! Mirdath! Mirdath!"

At that instant, Master Monstruwacan entered the observation chamber. Seeing my face, he stood utterly still. Though he lacked Night-Hearing, he was wise and respected my gift. He had just returned from the Receiving Instrument, expecting perhaps a faint signal too weak for the machine, and searched for me to listen.

I recounted my story, my thoughts, and that awakening. He listened with sympathy and a troubled heart, for in that age, sanity was defined differently; men could discuss matters now deemed madness, thanks to refined mental arts and strange experiments. As I spoke, I listened with my spirit. Save for a faint, happy laughter wrapping around me, I heard nothing else that day.

This connection sparked a lifelong debate with the learned men of the Redoubt. They doubted the veracity of the old stories—the Days of Light, the Sun—while my memories told tales that enchanted their hearts but angered their skeptical brains. Master Monstruwacan, however, always listened, often so absorbed in my stories drawn from Memory-Dreams that he forgot his instruments and duties. When he roused himself, he would chide gently, and the lesser Monstruwacans would flee guiltily to their posts, looking muddled and hungry for more.

Even those outside the Tower, the skeptics who hungered for truth, would listen from dawn to the fifteenth hour. A faction formed among the learned who upheld my tales as verity, and belief slowly grew. Master Monstruwacan believed from the start, a quality I cherished.

My stories traveled downward through a thousand cities. Eventually, even the ploughboys in the Underground Fields, a hundred miles deep, knew of my tales. These Fields were the mightiest work of the world, a tapering pyramid of country lands extending a hundred miles from base to top, sheathed in grey metal to protect against monsters. Lit by the Earth-Current, they were gardens of life, where millions walked and loved. Here, amidst the rustle of grain and the laughter of poppies, the memory of my dear one and that faint calling lingered.

A law in the Pyramid forbade males from adventuring into the Night Land before twenty-two, and females never. Those who ventured after receiving lectures on the horrors of the Dark Land wore a capsule implanted in their arm; if trapped, biting it would ensure their spirit's safety in death. I had considered this adventure, listening for the quiet calling, sending the Master-Word twice without certainty, but often whispering "Mirdath" into the darkness, sometimes sensing a weak, distant answer.

Then, on the thirteenth hour, the aether thrummed violently. I signaled silence, and the Tower fell still. The gentle thrilling broke into a clear, low calling in my brain: my own name, the old-earth name of this day. It struck me with the fright of fresh awakening memories. Immediately, I sent the Master-Word. A silence followed, then a beat afar off, and finally, the sure throbbing of the answer. Before this, I knew Mirdath had called; now, I had certainty.

Using the instruments, I said "Mirdath," and a swift, beautiful answer stole from the dark—an old love-name she had used only for me. I signaled the men to continue their records, for the communication was established. Master Monstruwacan stood quietly nearby, ready to note anything needed.

For a space of wonder, I spoke with the girl in the darkness. She knew my name and the old love-name, identifying herself as Mirdath. Yet, sorrow touched me as I questioned her further. Her name was not truly Mirdath; it was Naani. She had not known my name, but in the library of her refuge, there was a story of a man named by my name, called by that sweet love-name. When she first called, she had heard the cry of "Mirdath" returning to her, reminding her of the ancient tale. She had answered as the maid in the book might have.

The romance of my memory-love vanished, replaced by a strange trouble. Yet, I marveled that any book could hold a story so similar to mine, forgetting that the history of all love is written with one pen.

In that hour of quaint, foolish pain, a new thrill came. The girl wondered why my voice was not deeper. In the olden days of the Present Age, my voice had been very deep. I explained that the man in the book was said to have a deep tone, but she insisted otherwise. We argued over this triviality, yet it was a miracle: a human voice crossing the night to the Great Redoubt, perhaps breaking a million years of silence. News spread swiftly through the cities; the Hour-Slips were full of excitement.

Naani's voice verified our ancient records. Somewhere in the Outer Lands lay a second Redoubt, a small, three-sided pyramid built by weary humans escaping monster attacks. Its founder had once lived in the Great Redoubt but was exiled for irresponsibility. Tamed by fear, he planned this new refuge, aided by four million wanderers who found the Earth-Current in a great valley. They tapped the current, built the Pyramid, and maintained contact with the Great Redoubt for ages.

But the Earth-Current failed. Communication ceased, and a million years of silence followed. Populations dwindled, and for a hundred thousand years, no one was Sensitive enough to hear. Then, the Current renewed its power; aging slowed, happiness returned, and children were born anew.

Naani, the daughter of the Master Monstruwacan of the Lesser Redoubt, proved to be Sensitive. She perceived odd vibrations and urged her father to repair the ancient, rusted instruments. They built a new device, and Naani was given the right to call first. She called vaguely, and the Master-Word answered solemnly. She cried out that she was answered, and the people wept and prayed. She spoke the Master-Word, and "Mirdath! Mirdath!" rang back, stunning her into silence. When she tried to reply, the instrument failed.

For a long time, she heard the call of "Mirdath" and the beat of the Master-Word but could not answer. Stirred by the voice, she recalled an ancient book about a man and a maid named Mirdath. Filled with the awakening of those silent ages, she read the tale repeatedly, falling in love with its beauty. When the instrument was fixed, she called the man's name from the book, leading to the connection I experienced.

Often, I heard faint, sweet laughter and gentle stirrings of the aether. These came from Naani, using her brain-elements unwittingly, eager to answer my calls across the blackness.

After explaining the Lesser Refuge, Naani spoke of their plight. Food was scarce; though they had been unaware until the Earth-Current reawakened, they now tasted the lack of vitality in their food. We advised them that the soil had lost its life and taught them ways to restore it. They were eager to act, freshly alive after so long a time of half-life.

News of our reconnection spread rapidly through the Great Redoubt. Libraries filled with those seeking old records, and I was pestered with questions, nearly denied sleep. Yet, I was not spoiled; I had my work, and I was always busy listening and speaking through the darkness.

A fresh matter arose soon after. Tales of the Lesser Redoubt's dire need and calls for help came frequently. Yet, when I sent the Master-Word, there was no answering. I feared the Monsters and Forces of Evil knew. Sometimes, the Master-Word answered, confirming they had caught the beat, but then they contradicted previous messages cunningly. We realized the evil things were testing us, tempting us from safety. They knew of our speech but could not speak the Master-Word, leaving us a sure test of truth.

This awakening among the Monsters was extraordinary. Instruments recorded greater powers working in the darkness, and the Monstruwacans kept a strict watch. From the Country Whence Comes The Great Laughter, a heart-shaking thunder rolled from the unknown East. The Pit of the Red Smoke filled the Deep Valley, hiding the towers on the far side. Giants were seen around the Kilns to the East, belching great fires whose meaning we could not fathom.

From the Mountain Of The Voice, rising to the South-East, I heard the calling of the Voice for the first time. Shrill, peculiar, and horrible, it sounded like a giant-woman hungering strangely, shouting unknown words across the night. By all these signs, the Land was awakened.

Other tricks followed. Once, a call thrilled in the aether claiming humans had escaped the Lesser Redoubt, faint for food and seeking succor. But when we sent the Master-Word, the creatures without could not reply. This was a relief, proving it was a trap.

Constantly, I spoke with Naani, teaching her to send thoughts through the night with her brain-elements, warning her not to overuse the power lest it exhaust her body and mind. Despite this, her messages were weak unless she used the instrument, likely due to a lack of health force, though her Night-Hearing was keen, just less than mine.

Through many conversations and constant exchanges of thoughts and doings, we drew near in spirit, feeling a previous acquaintance in our hearts. This thrilled me strangely.
