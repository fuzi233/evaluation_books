Lady Beatrice sat in a chamber draped with the warm hues of animal skins, her posture rigid, her hands half-clenched as if grasping at air invisible to others. Her countenance shifted rapidly between pallor and crimson flush, betraying a turmoil that mirrored the restless tapping of her foot. Suddenly, a servant pulled aside the heavy curtains. Beatrice's brow flared, and she motioned for entry. "Admit him," she commanded, snatching a small black mask from the table and pressing it over her face. In an instant, the blood drained from her cheeks, leaving a stark, terrifying contrast between the jet-black veil and her marble-like skin.

The intruder bowed deeply, a man cut from the common mold—short-haired, round-faced, dressed plainly yet decently. His features were unremarkable save for a pair of eyes that seemed perpetually unsettled. After a flurry of curtsies, he stood opposite her, awaiting instruction.

"Have they told you why you are summoned?" Beatrice asked.

"Yes, Signora."

"Do those who sent you concur regarding the sum you shall receive?"

"Yes, Signora. It is the full price, which secures the greater vendetta. Unless, by your benevolence, you wish to accept the lesser."

"I do not comprehend you."

"The lesser vendetta claims only the body," the man explained gently. "We observe him emerging from a church or striking him down in some unsuspecting moment. But the greater vendetta watches for him fresh from unrepented sin, slaying both his soul and his flesh. Yet, alas, such vendettas are less frequent today than they were a few years past."

"Silence his tongue," Beatrice whispered fiercely, her voice trembling with resolve. "Let his treasonous heart cease beating. But his soul... with that I bear no grudge."

"So be it, Signora," the bravo replied. "My employer knows not the man's name, nor his dwelling. From whom shall I glean these details?"

"From myself."

Anxiety flickered in the assassin's gaze. "Pray, let me entreat caution and precision in this matter."

"Fear me not," Beatrice said, leaning forward. "He is a youth of tall stature, with auburn hair and dark blue eyes, possessing a face so honest it might deceive a saint. He resides in the Via Claudia, at the corner house occupied by the glovers. Within live but three men: himself, a painter of short build and swarthy complexion, and a slender lad. My betrayer is a stranger, handsome and taller than you."

The bravo leaned in, listening intently. "Is that sufficient?"

"Hold," Beatrice interrupted. "Does he frequent any secluded haunt where I may direct your blow?"

"My spies report that recently he has wandered the banks of the Tiber after dusk," she continued, her voice rising. "Presumably to meet his light lover, who dares call herself my rival. Slay him there! Let my rival return to find nothing but a corpse—the smooth, heartless, insolent traitor!"

"Be composed, Signora. He will betray no other ladies."

"I know not that! He wears a sword and wields it well. He is young and resolute."

"Neither weapon nor spirit shall avail him."

"Do you truly trust in your aim? What manner of weapons do you employ?"

The bravo produced a steel gauntlet. "We strike with such ferocity that protection is essential. Consider this our mallet." He unbuttoned his doublet to reveal armor beneath, then laid a glittering stiletto upon the table with a flourish.

Beatrice shivered initially, then seized the blade in her pale hand, testing its edge against her finger.

"Beware, madam," the bravo warned.

"What? Is it laced with poison?"

"Saints forbid! We do not steal lives through drugs. We claim them with steel alone. However, it was freshly honed, and I hesitated to risk the Signora's fair skin."

"His skin is as white as mine," she murmured, a fleeting glimmer of pity crossing her face before vanishing. "But his heart is blacker than soot. Tell me, is it not right to remove a traitor who slandered me?"

"That judgment belongs to your confessor, Signora. I am but a tool in noble hands, much like this stiletto."

Beatrice did not heed his words. "Oh, how I once loved him! To the very end! And now I hate him. A fool! He will learn to toy with princes, to spurn them while fawning, preferring the scum of the streets to royalty, turning them into a byword." She lifted her masked face. "Why do you linger? Hasten to execute my vengeance."

"It is customary to remit half the payment beforehand, Signora."

"Ay, I forgot. Your revenge is purchased. Here is more than half," she declared, pushing a heavy leather sack across the table. "Come for the balance when the deed is done."

"You shall see me again, Signora."

With another bow, he departed.

Left alone, Beatrice remained seated, still masked, her arms stretched stiffly before her, her knuckles white as she gripped the wood. So sat the Sphinx of legend; so sat a tigress trapped by instinct. Yet, a cold chill crept over her now that the killer had vanished, giving rise to moody doubts, the harbingers of futile regret. *Gerard and Margaret* were already lost to time. This, indeed, was the true medieval world: proud, amorous, vengeful, generous, foolish, cunning, impulsive, unprincipled—and ignorant as dust.

Power was the curse of such a being. Had she been forced to commit her crimes with her own hands, her weak nerves would have tempered the fury of her passions, making her bark far worse than her bite. But power granted a feeble, furious woman access to male instruments. The result was as dreadful as the union itself unnatural. In this specific instance, it had sharpened an assassin's dagger for a poor, forlorn wretch who was merely contemplating suicide.
