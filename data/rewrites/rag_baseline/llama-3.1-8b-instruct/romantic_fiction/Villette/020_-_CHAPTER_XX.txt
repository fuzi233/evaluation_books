As I stood before the mirror, my godmother, Mrs. Bretton, surveyed me with an air of quiet determination. "It's time for a new dress," she declared, her voice firm yet gentle, as if she had already anticipated my resistance. I had grown accustomed to her paternal guidance, which had shaped my daily life since my arrival in Villette. The King's policies, though well-intentioned, had created a complex web of social and economic dynamics that had isolated our royal household from the outside world. My godmother's actions, however, seemed to be driven by a desire to reconnect me with the city's vibrant cultural scene.

She summoned a dressmaker, who measured me with precision, as if crafting a puzzle piece into place. "I want to follow my own taste," Mrs. Bretton announced, her words dripping with conviction. Two days later, a stunning pink dress arrived, its delicate fabric and bright hue sending a shiver down my spine. I felt like a stranger in my own skin, unsure how to reconcile this unfamiliar garment with my own sense of self.

My godmother's resolve, however, remained unwavering. "You will wear it tonight," she said, her tone leaving no room for debate. I reluctantly surrendered to her will, knowing that I would be accompanying her and Graham to a grand concert at the principal musical society. The event promised to be a spectacle, with the most advanced pupils of the Conservatoire performing alongside a lottery benefiting the poor. The King, Queen, and Prince of Labassecour would also be in attendance, adding an air of gravitas to the evening.

As I was led downstairs, I felt a sense of trepidation wash over me. The pink dress, softened by the drapery of black lace, seemed to whisper sweet nothings in my ear, urging me to conform to societal expectations. But Graham's kind smile and satisfied nod, as he handed me a bouquet of flowers, reassured me that I needn't worry about appearances. In his eyes, I saw a reflection of my own desires, and my fears began to dissipate.

As we drove through the city, the sights and sounds of Villette unfolded before us like a tapestry. The brightly lit streets, the lively chatter of the pedestrians, and the twinkling stars above created a symphony of wonder.
